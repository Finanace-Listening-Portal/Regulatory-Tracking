Child's career plans changed? Here's how to keep the education fund on track

When your child says they want to become a doctor, parents start planning for medical-school fees. A few years later, the dream may become law, design, technology or a degree overseas. The problem is that education costs can change along with the career plan. A savings strategy that was enough for one course may fall short when the goal changes.

The answer is not to keep starting new investments every time your child changes their mind. Instead, build a flexible education fund and review it as the goal becomes clearer.

Start with today's cost, not yesterday's estimate

Begin by finding out what the chosen course costs today, including tuition, accommodation, books, travel and other unavoidable expenses. Then estimate what the same education could cost when your child actually needs the money. SEBI's financial-goal planner asks investors to account for inflation when calculating future education costs rather than using today's figure unchanged.

Don't lock yourself into one career assumption

A flexible portfolio can be more useful than saving for one very specific degree. For a goal that is still many years away, parents may consider a combination of growth-oriented and more stable investments, depending on their risk tolerance. As the education date gets closer, gradually moving money needed soon into relatively stable investments can reduce the risk of a market fall just before fees are due.

Keep a separate emergency fund

An education corpus should not double up as the family's emergency fund. A job loss, medical expense or major home repair can arrive just when education fees are due. Keeping emergency savings separately means you are less likely to withdraw from the child's fund or take expensive debt to manage an unrelated crisis. SEBI's financial planning guidance also stresses the importance of maintaining funds for emergencies and unexpected expenses.

Review the fund when the dream changes

You don't need to overhaul the portfolio every time your child mentions a new career. Instead, review the plan when there is a genuine change in the likely course, college, location or admission timeline. Recalculate the future cost and compare it with the corpus you are building. If the gap has widened, increase your monthly contribution gradually rather than waiting until the admission year.

Use suitable savings products for the goal

For eligible girls, Sukanya Samriddhi can form part of an education savings strategy. India Post currently permits withdrawals of up to 50 percent of the balance at the end of the preceding financial year for higher education after the girl turns 18 or passes Class 10,

subject to the scheme's conditions and documentation. PPF can also be opened in a minor's name, with a current annual contribution ceiling of Rs 1.5 lakh.

Don't sacrifice your retirement

This is the boundary parents should not cross. Funding a child's education is important, but draining EPF, PPF or retirement investments can create a problem that is much harder to fix later. Your child may have borrowing options for higher education, but parents have fewer ways to replace lost retirement years.

A child's career dream does not have to determine the entire investment strategy. Build a flexible corpus, factor in education inflation, review the target when the course or college changes and gradually reduce risk as the admission date approaches. Keep emergencies and retirement savings separate from the education fund. The goal is to give your child room to choose a career while ensuring that the cost of that choice does not derail the family's broader financial plan.
