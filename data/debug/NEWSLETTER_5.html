India-Peru FTA talks enter final stretch, Peru hopes to sign deal in 2027

India and Peru’s negotiations for a free trade agreement have entered their final stretch, with Peru awaiting New Delhi’s response to a counterproposal and hoping to sign the deal in 2027, Trade Minister Rogers Valencia said on Friday.

“Negotiating with India is a fairly lengthy process,” Valencia told Reuters in an interview. “However, a counterproposal has now been sent to India, we are finalizing this process, and we hope the treaty with India can be signed in 2027.”

India has emerged as the second-largest destination for Peruvian exports this year, overtaking the United States, driven largely by gold shipments, according to official data from Peru’s trade ministry.

Peruvian exports to India totalled $6.184 billion between January and July, up 152% from the same period a year earlier. Shipments to the United States stood at $5.971 billion, up 21%.

India and Peru began their first round of FTA negotiations in 2017.

Peru expects its exports to exceed $100 billion this year, setting a new record compared with around $91 billion in 2025, Valencia said. China remains Peru’s largest export market, driven mainly by copper and other minerals.

Peru Seeks Talks With US On Tariffs

Valencia also discussed Peru’s trade relationship with the United States a day after US Secretary of State Marco Rubio visited the country.

He said he had raised concerns over higher US duties imposed on Peruvian exports in July, saying the tariffs could affect the country’s exports this year.

“The conversation was very frank and very open, and we noticed a great deal of receptiveness from Rubio,” Valencia said.

Peru has traded largely tariff-free with the US under a 2009 agreement, but the new duties have affected exports including blueberries, textiles, grapes and avocados.

Valencia said he expects to travel to the United States later this month with President Keiko Fujimori for the United Nations General Assembly, where Peru hopes to advance discussions with Washington on tariffs and bilateral trade relations.
