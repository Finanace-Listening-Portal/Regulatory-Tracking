Houthis warn US of ‘painful response’ if it joins Saudi war in Yemen after deadly Riyadh airport attack

A Houthi official on Monday warned the United States that it would face a “painful response” if it joined Saudi Arabia's military campaign against the Iran-aligned group in Yemen, as the two sides exchanged strikes and Riyadh reportedly sought greater military assistance from Washington following a series of deadly attacks.

“If the US becomes involved in the aggression against Yemen, we will not deliberate over the decision to respond. Rather, there will be a swift and painful response targeting the US and all its interests in the region,” said Houthi political bureau member Mohammed Al Bukhaiti, according to news agency AFP.

He also warned employees and workers at Saudi oil facilities and airports to stay away from the sites, saying they had become “legitimate targets”.

Bukhaiti said the campaign against Saudi targets was intended to send a message to Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman that he could no longer wage military action against Yemen from a position of safety.

The warning comes amid escalating hostilities between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia, with the group claiming fresh attacks on Saudi targets on Monday, including Riyadh's international airport, while the Saudi-led coalition reported intercepting ballistic missiles launched towards the capital.

Houthi attack on Monday

Explosions were heard across Riyadh on Monday night as Saudi Arabia's military coalition said it intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched by the Houthis towards the capital. The group, meanwhile, claimed responsibility for a missile strike targeting King Khalid International Airport and said it had also attacked the Khamis Mushait military base in southern Saudi Arabia with a drone.

“Our armed forces were able to target (King) Khalid International Airport in the Saudi enemy's capital Riyadh this evening with a ballistic missile and the hit was precise,” Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said, reported AFP.

The Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted the missiles, while Saudi state broadcaster Al Ekhbariya reported that the military was responding to drone and missile threats. At least three explosions were heard in Riyadh, although there was no immediate claim of responsibility for the blasts. Civil Defence officials subsequently said the danger had passed.

Operations at King Khalid International Airport remained suspended on Monday, while employees in Riyadh's financial district were instructed to work from home. Saudi's civil aviation authority said the suspension at King Khalid International Airport has been extended to verify the safety of airport facilities and operational readiness.

According to Reuters, a Notice to Airmen---an official advisory notifying flight and airport crews of disruptions---shows Riyadh’s airport will remain closed until 21:00 GMT on Saturday.

The atmosphere in the Saudi capital remained tense after mobile phones received a government alert warning of a “hostile aerial threat”. According to AFP, it was the first time the alert system had been used in Riyadh in weeks, with no official explanation for its absence during earlier attacks.

The Houthis also reported casualties from Saudi-led airstrikes in Yemen. The Houthi-run health ministry said strikes on a government complex and a house in the northern province of Al-Jawf killed nine people, including five children and a woman, and wounded seven others.

Houthi officials also said Saudi Arabia had carried out fresh strikes on the Yemeni capital, Sanaa, killing one person and injuring 12.

Amid the escalating violence, the US Embassy in Saudi Arabia directed government employees to obtain its authorisation before using any airport in the kingdom and urged American citizens to exercise “extreme caution”.

Meanwhile, Etihad Airways announced on Monday that it was cancelling all flights to and from Riyadh until the following week, while suspending services to Dammam and Jeddah. Bahrain's Gulf Air also cancelled its Riyadh flights until Saturday. US carrier Delta said it would delay the launch of its planned direct service between Atlanta and Riyadh, scheduled for October 23.

Houthi attacks on Oct 10 

The Houthi warning to Washington follows a deadly attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday that killed 13 people and wounded hundreds.

US President Donald Trump said the attack was “not acceptable” and that Washington was considering joining Saudi Arabia's air campaign against the Houthis. He said he would speak to Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman about the possibility of US military involvement. Trump had reportedly refused earlier Saudi requests for direct military assistance.

Saturday's strike was described as the deadliest attack on Saudi soil in more than a decade. Footage released by Saudi authorities showed Muslim pilgrims in religious attire waiting in departure lounges before an explosion tore through the facility, sending flaming debris crashing down.

'Serious' response

Meanwhille, on Monday, Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman said the recent attacks had tested the kingdom's infrastructure, institutions, people and resilience, signalling that Riyadh would respond firmly to the threat.

“The events of recent weeks are serious, our response must be equally serious,” he said at a conference in Riyadh.

Saudi Arabia has launched an investigation into how its air defence systems failed to intercept the barrage of missiles and drones fired by the Houthis, including Saturday's attack on the airport, regional officials quoted by the Associated Press said on Monday.

The officials said preliminary findings suggested the Houthis had launched multiple drones before firing cruise and ballistic missiles towards King Khalid International Airport.

The investigation is also examining why air defence systems did not issue an earlier warning of the attack, during which the missiles and drones reportedly travelled more than 1,000 kilometres (620 miles) to reach Riyadh.

Saudi officials seek greater US assistance

As the conflict with the Houthis escalates, Saudi Arabia is seeking more direct US involvement in its campaign in Yemen. As per the Associated Press, Washington has provided intelligence sharing, target identification and planning support for the Saudi-led offensive but has so far held back from conducting its own strikes against the group.

Any US intervention could prompt the Houthis to threaten shipping through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait at the southern entrance to the Red Sea, potentially disrupting a critical maritime route for global trade and oil shipments.

The threat comes as commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has also been disrupted by the wider US-Iran conflict and repeated attacks on vessels in the strategic waterway. Before the war, roughly one-fifth of the world's oil supply passed through Hormuz, making the security of both chokepoints critical to global energy markets.

The Houthis seized key areas along Yemen's Red Sea coast last month, including territory near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. Forces aligned with Yemen's internationally recognised government, backed by the Saudi-led coalition, have since been trying to recapture those areas.

A direct US role in the Saudi campaign could therefore widen the conflict and put additional pressure on maritime traffic through both the Red Sea and the Persian Gulf.

Gulf nations call for peace

Amid the escalating confrontation, an Emirati diplomat on Monday called for coordinated action among Gulf states to defend the region against attacks by Iran and allied groups, reported the Associated Press.

Anwar Gargash, diplomatic adviser to the UAE leadership, said Iran and its allies had wrongly assessed that Gulf Arab countries were the “weakest link” in the US-led war against Iran, during which Tehran and allied groups repeatedly attacked countries on the other side of the Gulf.

Iran, meanwhile, expressed concern over the fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis and said it was prepared to help facilitate a resolution.

“We are severely concerned about developments in the Arabian Peninsula,” Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei said at his weekly briefing on Monday. “We have many times announced readiness for applying our good offices in order to facilitate talks between the two sides.”
