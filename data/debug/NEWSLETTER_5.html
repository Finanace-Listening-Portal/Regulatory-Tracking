APJ Abdul Kalam Birth Anniversary: 30+ essay and speech ideas for students to honour ‘Missile Man Of India’ on World Students’ Day

World Students’ Day 2026 falls on Thursday, October 15, marking the birth anniversary of former President of India Dr APJ Abdul Kalam. Schools, colleges and educational institutions observe the occasion to celebrate his contributions to science and education and his enduring message that young people can help shape the country’s future. Students can mark the day through essays, speeches, debates and other activities focused on education, innovation, leadership and national development.

Kalam, who served as India’s 11th President from 2002 to 2007, remained closely associated with students throughout his life. An aerospace scientist and teacher, he encouraged young people to develop curiosity, think independently and work towards their goals. His life and work continue to provide themes for school assemblies, classroom discussions and student competitions.

Although World Students’ Day is widely associated with Kalam’s birth anniversary in India, it is not an officially designated United Nations observance. The day provides an opportunity for students to reflect on the value of education, scientific thinking, integrity and social responsibility.

World Students’ Day 2026: Why Is APJ Abdul Kalam’s Birth Anniversary Celebrated?

Born on October 15, 1931, in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam played an important role in India’s space and missile programmes before becoming President. His scientific career included work with the Defence Research and Development Organisation and the Indian Space Research Organisation. He also contributed to the development of India’s first Satellite Launch Vehicle and the country’s guided missile programme.

Kalam received the Bharat Ratna, India’s highest civilian honour, in 1997. Widely known as the ‘Missile Man of India’ and the ‘People’s President’, he also wrote books such as Wings of Fire,  India 2020 and Ignited Minds. These works reflect his belief in education, technology and the potential of young Indians.

After his presidential term ended in 2007, Kalam returned to teaching and continued interacting with students. He died on July 27, 2015, at the age of 83 after collapsing while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong.

World Students’ Day offers schools and colleges a chance to remember his legacy while encouraging students to connect their education with real-world challenges. Activities can focus on equal access to education, scientific curiosity, environmental protection, responsible technology use and the role of young people in building an inclusive society.

Schools can also organise poster-making competitions, quizzes, debates, science exhibitions, reading sessions and group discussions to mark the occasion. Such activities encourage participation and help students explore the relationship between education, creativity and public service.

World Students’ Day 2026 is an opportunity to celebrate Kalam’s legacy while recognising the role of students in India’s future. His life demonstrates how education, scientific curiosity and a commitment to serving society can work together.
