Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Shivhare Associates Vs ACIT (ITAT Agra) Only the Profit Element in Suppressed Sales Is Taxable After Rejection of Books: ITAT Agra Summary: The Agra Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held that only the profit element embedded in suppressed sales could be brought to tax in the case of a liquor retailer whose books of account had been rejected. Following its earlier orders in the assessee’s own case, the Tribunal directed the Assessing Officer (AO) to apply a 2.7% net profit rate on estimated sales, before deducting interest and salary to partners, subject to the returned income as the minimum. It also restored a separate addition under Section 68 to the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) [CIT(A)] for fresh adjudication. Facts of the Case Shivhare Associates, a partnership firm, carried on the retail business of country liquor, foreign liquor and beer under a contract awarded by the Madhya Pradesh Government. For AY 2015–16, the lead year, the firm filed its return declaring ₹38,29,260 as total income. During scrutiny, the AO called for the cash book, ledger, purchase and sale bills, expense vouchers and stock records to verify the trading results. The AO recorded that the firm failed to produce its books despite two opportunities and that its representatives stated that sale bills had neither been maintained nor issued. The AO also noted a discrepancy between purchases reflected in the tax collection at source data and purchases reported by the firm. On these grounds, the AO rejected the declared trading results. AO Treated the Entire Estimated Sales Difference as Profit The AO computed sales at the minimum selling price (MSP) by adding a 10% mark-up to the purchase cost. This yielded estimated sales of ₹38,36,10,049 against reported sales of ₹37,12,60,422. The AO treated the difference of ₹1,23,49,627 as additional profit and added it to the firm’s income. The assessee contended that there was no positive evidence of sales suppression and that it had been compelled to sell below the MSP. The CIT(A) found the explanation unsupported by documentary evidence and sustained the addition. Before the Tribunal, the assessee relied on its own earlier cases and sought estimation of net profit at 2.7% of estimated sales. ITAT Sustained Rejection of Books and Applied a 2.7% Net Profit Rate The Tribunal agreed that the AO was justified in rejecting the books for AY 2015–16. It then considered how the firm’s taxable profit should be estimated. In the assessee’s own cases for AYs 2010–11 and 2011–12, the Agra Bench had applied a 2.7% net profit rate in the same liquor business. The earlier proceedings drew on the Madhya Pradesh High Court decisions in CIT Vs. Balchand Ajit Kumar and Manmohan Sadani Vs. CIT on taxing the profit element in undisclosed sales. The Bench acknowledged that the grounds for rejecting the books differed across years. It found that the firm continued to carry on the same business and that the Revenue had shown no material distinction warranting a different approach to estimating profit. The firm’s declared net profit rates before partner interest and salary were 1.65%, 2.41% and 1.87% for the three years under appeal. The Tribunal considered these figures alongside the margins disclosed in the earlier years. The Bench also declined the Revenue’s proposal to adopt a 5% net profit rate. One precedent cited by the Revenue involved estimating turnover as a multiple of licence fees. Another involved material concerning liquor sales above the maximum retail price. The Tribunal found those circumstances distinguishable from the facts before it. The AO was accordingly directed to apply 2.7% to estimated sales for all three years, compute profit before deducting interest and salary to partners, and ensure that the resulting income did not fall below the returned income. Section 68 Addition of ₹1.21 Crore Restored to CIT(A) For AY 2015–16, the AO had also added ₹1,20,94,794 in connection with two unsecured loans. This comprised a loan of ₹10 lakh from Mohini Gupta, a loan of ₹1 crore from Radhey Shyam Rathore and ₹10,94,794 credited as interest to Rathore’s account. The assessee referred to receipt of the funds through banking channels. The CIT(A) sustained the addition because the evidence furnished did not establish the identity and creditworthiness of the lenders and the genuineness of the transactions. The Tribunal noted that the assessee had yet to produce the necessary supporting documents, including at the Tribunal stage. In the interest of justice, the Bench gave the firm a further opportunity to substantiate the loans. It set aside the CIT(A)’s findings on this issue and restored the matter to the CIT(A) for fresh adjudication after giving the assessee a reasonable opportunity of being heard. The Section 68 ground was allowed for statistical purposes, and all three appeals were partly allowed. Cases Discussed: Kachwala Gems Vs JCIT (Supreme Court); (2007) 288 ITR 10. For A.Y. 2010-11, the AO after rejecting the books of accounts of the assessee u/s 145 (3) of the Act had computed the suppressed sales by taking the average of minimum selling price and maximum retail price which came to Rs. 18,46,03,933/-. On appeal, the learned CIT(A) agreed with the findings of the AO that the rejection of books of account was justified, but referred to of the decision of the jurisdictional Hon’ble Madhya Pradesh High Court in the case of CIT vs Balchand Ajit Kumar 263 ITR 610 and Manmohan Sadani vs. CIT 304 ITR 52, which held that total sales cannot be regarded for as the profit of the assessee and on the contrary it is the net profit which was to adopted in such cases. The facts and circumstances of this case are in pari materia to the facts and circumstance of the cases of Laxmi Narain Shivhare and others in ITA No. 233/Agr/2014 and ITA No. 285/Agr/2014 where the assessees were a liquor contractor dealing in country liquor, Indian made foreign liquor (IMFL) and Beer. While rejecting the finding of the AO in respect of computation of suppressed sales as income of the assessee, the ld. CIT(A) has adopted N.P. rate at 3% on the sales turnover estimated by AO, inter alia observing as under: “3.3 I have gone through the assessment order, written submissions, case laws relied upon by the appellant and argument put forth during the course of appellate proceedings carefully. It is seen that the appellant, inspite of having been granted more than sufficient opportunities failed to produce sales bills/vouchers before the A.O for verification. In view of the facts discussed by the A.O in the assessment order, I am of the considered view that the action of the A.O rejecting the book results u/s 145(3) and estimating the income is in accordance with the provisions of law. Hence the action of the A.O estimating the income is held to be justified. However, in view of the following facts and decisions of jurisdictional High Court and various benches of ITAT, the estimation of income made by A.O appears to be on higher side. 3.4 The appellant is a liquor contractor and bound to pay the prescribed fee (Duty & basic excise duty) to the Govt, to take the delivery of the material which has to be sold before the end of license period. I find force in the argument of the appellant that if the appellant does not sell the material as per the market trend then by the end of the license period he may be in possession of unsold stock and face huge losses. As the appellant does not have any right to get back the prescribed fee paid to the Govt, in respect of the unsold stock, therefore, he has to sell the goods even at discounted rates. 3.5 The Hon’ble M.P. High Court (jurisdictional High Court) in the case of CIT Vs Balchand Ajit Kumar 263 ITR 610 M.P has held that the total sales cannot be regarded as the profit of the assessee. In the case of Manmohan Sadani Vs CIT 304 ITR 52 M.P the Hon’ble jurisdictional High Court has held that total sales cannot be regarded as profit of the assessee, on the contrary it is a net profit rate which has to be adopted in such cases. In view of the findings of the Hon’ble M.P High Court(jurisdictional High Court) in the above cited two cases I am of the considered opinion that the entire suppressed/undisclosed sale estimated by A.O cannot be taxed as ‘Income’ of the appellant. Only the net profit part can be taxed. 3.6 On the facts similar to the facts of the appellant, the ITAT, Coordinate Bench of Hyderabad, ITAT (ITA No.391/Hyd/2009) in the case of Manjeet Singh Bagga Vs. ITO has held that estimation of profit@3% of the purchases made by the appellant(Liquor Contractor) would meet end of justice. In the case of Kanak Durga Wines Hyderabad Vs ITO Hyderabad bench-B of the ITAT (ITA No.591/Hyd./2011) has estimated the net profit of the assessee(Liquor Contractor) @3% of the purchases or stock put for sale during the year under consideration. In the case of G.Sudarshan Hyderabad Vs ITO (ITA No.l26/Hyd./2012) Hyderabad bench-A of the ITAT directed the Assessing Officer to estimate the net profit of the assessee at 3% of the purchases or stock put for sale during the year, subject to the condition that the income shall not go below the returned income. 3.7 After going through the various decisions of jurisdictional High Court and ITAT discussed above, I am of the considered opinion that the entire suppressed/undisclosed sale of the appellant cannot be taxed as income. Only the net profit part can be taxed as ‘income . After taking into account, the argument put forth by the appellant in the written submissions and decisions relied upon by the appellant. 1 am of the considered view that it would be fair and reasonable to estimate the net profit of the appellant @3% of the total purchases cost debited by the assessee. It is seen from the assessment order that the total purchases cost debited by the appellant is Rs.14,83,37,420/-. The amount of net profit on this purchase cost @3% would be Rs.44,51,623/-. The appellant has declared total income of Rs.14,41,940/- from liquor business. Hence the addition of Rs.30,09,683/-(44,5I,623-14,41,940) is confirmed and balance addition of Rs.2,68,50,342/- is hereby deleted. …………………… 6. The rejection of books of account was affirmed by the ld. CIT(A) for A.Y. 2010-11, but the learned CIT(A) relying upon the decision of the jurisdictional Hon’ble Madhya Pradesh High Court in the case of CIT vs Balchand Ajit Kumar (supra) and Manmohan Sadani vs. CIT (supra)held that entire suppressed income cannot be taxed and only the net profit was to adopted in such cases as profits. The ld. CIT(A) reduced the same to 3% of the total sales, which, as discussed above, was reduced to 2.7% by the Tribunal. There are no distinguishing facts brought by the ld. CIT(A) or by the ld. CIT/DR to the finding of this Bench of the Tribunal in the case of the assessee for A.Y. 2010-11 and 2011-12 that entire suppressed/ undisclosed sales cannot be taxed as profit and only the net profit can be taxed. As discussed above, the assessee is doing the same business over the years and the books of account are being rejected since A.Y. 2010-11 and net profit being estimated.For this year also,we agree with the reasons given by the AO for the rejection of the books of accounts of the assessee.We are also conscious of the fact that there is a different reason for the rejection of books of accounts for A.Y. 2015-16, but ultimately, the issue is regarding as to whether the entire suppressed/undisclosed sales are to be taxed or an estimated net profit on suchsuppressed/undisclosed sales is to be taxed. This issue is squarely covered by the order of this Bench of the Tribunal in the case of the assessee for A.Y. 2010-11 and 2011-12 wherein, it directed the AO to apply net profit rate of 2.7% of the estimated sales before deducting interest and salary to partners subject to the minimum returned income. In this regard, we are also supported by the chart submitted by the assessee wherein the net profit has been declared by the assessee @ 1.65%, 2.41% and 1.87% before deducting interest and salary for A.Y. 2012-13, 2013-14 and 2015-16 wherein similar net profit @ 1.94% and 1.09%was consideredby this Bench of the Tribunal to be reasonably declared by the assessee for A.Y. 2010-11 and 2011-12 in para no. 12 of its order dated 16.05.2018.Therefore, we do not agree with the Ld. CIT(A) in upholding the order of the AO to tax the entire suppressed/undisclosed sales of Rs. 1,23,49,627/- as the income of the assessee. 7.5 In this regard, the submission of the ld. DR and the case laws relied upon by him for considering that the net profit @ 5% of the sales has been carefully considered by us but are not found to be acceptable. In the decision of Hon’ble Madhya Pradesh High Court in Badri Prasad Bhagwandas & Co. v. CIT (supra) relied upon by the Sr. DR, we notice that in the said case, the total sales were estimated by considering the sales of the assessee at 2.5 times of the license fees whereas in the present case, the sales have not been estimated in the said manner. Therefore, the facts are distinguishable in the case of the assessee. Similarly, in the case of ITO, Suryapet v. Sri Beeravelli Venkat Reddy (supra)relied upon by the Sr. DR, we notice that the Tribunal noted that it is well settled law that the best guide for estimation of income after rejecting the books of account is either the past history of the assessee or any other comparable case. Further, in the said case, the ld. DR had filed a written submission wherein it was referred that it was found in the latest ACB Investigation in the liquor trade that the sales were made much above MRP by forming cartels of the liquor traders, whereas no such finding is there on record in the case of the present assessee or with respect to the liquor trade carried out in the State of Madhya Pradesh. Therefore, again, the facts are distinguishable in the present case. 7.6 Therefore, in view of the above facts and following the order of this Bench in the case of the assessee for A.Y. 2010-11 and 2011-12 as referred above, we direct the AO to apply net profit rate of 2.7% of the estimated sales before deducting interest and salary to partners subject to the minimum returned income.
