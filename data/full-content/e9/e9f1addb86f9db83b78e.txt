Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Hical India Private Limited Vs Commissioner of Central Excise (CESTAT Bangalore) Summary: The Bangalore Bench of the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal allowed three appeals concerning eligibility for CENVAT credit on Kodak-branded film rolls supplied free of cost for bundling with cameras manufactured under a contract manufacturing arrangement. The appellant manufactured reloadable cameras for Kodak India Private Limited and cleared them together with film rolls in combination packs. The Central Excise Department denied credit on the ground that the film rolls were not used in or in relation to the manufacture of cameras and therefore did not qualify as inputs under the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004. The Tribunal rejected this interpretation and held that film rolls supplied with cameras constituted accessories eligible for input credit. The dispute related to the period from December 2005 to March 2010. Under an OEM agreement dated 5 December 2005, the appellant agreed to assemble KB 10 cameras and bundle them with Kodak-branded film rolls supplied without charge. The cameras were classifiable under Central Excise Tariff Heading 9006 51 00 and assessed to duty on the basis of maximum retail price under Section 4A of the Central Excise Act, 1944. The appellant contended that the value of the film rolls was included in the assessable value of the combination packs and that packing the cameras with film rolls constituted an activity connected with manufacture and marketability. The appellant relied on Kodak India Ltd. v. CCE, 2002 (147) ELT 886 (Tribunal, Bangalore), in which film rolls bundled with cameras were recognised as accessories eligible for credit. It also cited State of Uttar Pradesh v. Kores India Limited, AIR 1977 SC 132, concerning the meaning of accessories, and CCE v. Prime Health Care Products, 2011 (272) ELT 54 (Gujarat High Court), concerning toothbrushes packed with toothpaste. The Revenue maintained that film rolls were not inputs used in manufacturing cameras and supported the orders denying credit. The Tribunal observed that the earlier Kodak India Ltd. decision had already addressed the eligibility of film rolls supplied with cameras. It held that film rolls constituted accessories to cameras and therefore qualified as inputs under Rule 2(k) of the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004. The Tribunal further found that the value of film rolls formed part of the maximum retail price of the combination pack. Applying the Gujarat High Court’s decision in Prime Health Care Products, it concluded that packing accessories with manufactured goods to create a marketable combination pack supported credit eligibility. Accordingly, the Tribunal set aside the orders denying CENVAT credit, together with the consequential demands of duty, interest and penalties, and allowed all three appeals with consequential relief in accordance with law. FULL TEXT OF THE ORIGINAL JUDGMENT/ORDER OF CESTAT BANGALORE The instant appeals have been preferred by the assessee, M/s. Hical Technologies Pvt. Ltd, which involve common issue pertaining to CENVAT credit and hence taken up together for disposal by this common order. Periodical Show Cause Notices were issued for denying CENVAT credit starting from December 2005 to March 2010, which were adjudicated by the Ld. Commissioner, Bangalore, confirming demand of Central Excise duty consequent to disallowance of CENVAT credit, together with imposition of interest and penalty which is under challenge in these appeals. 2. Briefly stated, the facts of the case are that the appellant is engaged in the manufacture of camera (re-loadable) under contract manufacturing agreement entered with M/s Kodak India Pvt. Ltd. (‘Kodak’) The said goods are classifiable under Central Excise Tariff heading 9006 51 00 on which duty is assessed on the basis of MRP in terms of the provisions contained in Section 4A of the Central Excise Act, 1944. The appellant has availed CENVAT credit on the ‘film rolls’, which is supplied free of cost by Kodak for bundling with the camera manufactured by the appellant, so as to clear the said goods as ‘combi- pack’ containing camera and film roll. The availment of CENVAT credit on the ‘film rolls’ is the subject matter of dispute in the present case. 3. The appellant has entered into the subject agreement dated 05.12.2005, named as OEM Agreement, with Kodak. As per para 1.1 of the said agreement, the appellant has agreed to assemble KB 10 Cameras for Kodak and bundle pack the camera with Kodak branded film. As per the definition of term ‘bundle’ stated in the agreement, it means the KB 10 camera bundled with Kodak supplied film. The appellant has agreed to assemble KB 10 cameras for Kodak and bundle the camera with Kodak branded film that would be provided free of cost to appellant. The Ld. Commissioner has disallowed the credit on the ground that the film-rolls are not used in or in relation to manufacture of cameras and hence, not ‘input’ as defined in the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004 (Credit Rules) and therefore, not eligible for credit. 4. Shri K.S.Ravishankar, learned Advocate along with Shri N.Anand and Shri B. Venugopal, Advocates appeared for the appellant and Shri P.Gopalkumar, learned Jt.Commissioner(AR) appeared for the Revenue. 5. The learned Advocate appearing for the appellant submitted that the value of film-roll is very well included in the value of manufactured goods cleared by them in combo-pack to Kodak. He also submitted that the camera is specified in the Third Schedule to Central Excise Tariff Act and is a deemed manufacture, which includes any process, in relation to goods specified in the Third Schedule to the Act, involving packing or repacking of goods in a unit container or labelling or relabeling including declaration or alteration of MRP or any process to render the product marketable. He accordingly submitted that the activity of packing of camera with the film roll in a bundle / combi-pack would tantamount to manufacture. He also submitted that film-roll would qualify as ‘accessory’ to camera, which is very much eligible as ‘input’ as defined in Rule 2(k) of the Credit Rules. He relied on the Tribunal’s decision in the case of Kodak India Ltd vs. CCE 2002 (147) ELT 886 (Tri-Bang) and Hon’ble Supreme Court’s decision in the case of State of Uttar Pradesh vs. Kores India Limited AIR 1977 SC 132 wherein it has been held that typewriter ribbon was held to be ‘accessory’ of typewriter just as aviation petrol for a vehicle. He also relied upon the following decisions:- a. CCE vs. Prime Health Care Products [2011 (272) ELT 54 (Guj)] b. Indian Refrigeration Co Ltd. vs. CCE [2017-TIOL-3764 –TRI-MAD] c. Shervan Industrial Syndicate Ltd vs. CCE [2017-TIOL-TRI-ALL] d. Jindal Photo Ltd vs. CCE 2013 [TIOL-907-TRI-AHM] e. CCE vs. Dynavista Industries Pvt Ltd [2015-TIOL-1760-TRI-MAD] f. G. S. Enterprises vs. CCE [2014 (313) ELT 340 (Tri-Del)] 6. The learned Jt.Commissioner(AR) for the Revenue reiterated the findings of the learned Commissioner and supported the impugned orders and prayed that appeal be dismissed as being devoid of merit. 7. Heard both sides and perused the appeal records. 8. The issue to be decided in the instant case is whether the appellant is eligible for CENVAT credit of duty paid by Kodak, on Kodak branded film rolls supplied free of cost, to be packed in a combo pack along with the camera which is manufactured by the appellant for Kodak under the subject manufacturing agreement. 9. We find that the identical issue pertaining to available of credit, in the hands of camera manufacturer, on film rolls has been decided by this Tribunal in the case of Kodak India Ltd (Supra), under the erstwhile provisions contained in Central Excise Rules, wherein it has been held that film rolls cleared with camera will constitute ‘accessories’ to the camera and eligible for credit. The relevant portion of the decision is reproduced below:- “….A perusal of this rule indicates, the most important change brought about by this rule is to allow credit on specified items by bringing them in the ambit of inputs, such as goods manufactured for captive consumption, paints, goods used for generation of electricity, accessories, packing materials, by listing them in an unambiguous manner. This rule, starts with a non-obstante clause and therefore, if any, of the inputs as specified under Rule 57-B are brought and used, then such inputs and credit thereon would be available without recourse to the provisions of Rule 57A, since the notifications issued under Rule 57-A also form part of this Rule. The clarifying provisions did not, in this rule prevail over Rule 57A, but also over notifications issued thereunder. Therefore, it would be incorrect to deny credit on an input listed and covered under Rule 57B(1) on ground of any interpretation of exclusion otherwise. The rule does not however define an „accessory‟. The word used at Sl. No. (VI) is „accessories‟ in plural. Since the word accessory has not been defined and the word used in the rule is in plural, we cannot find any reason for the department to have an authority to determine or/and order, as to how many items of an accessory, could be allowed under this rule. The department is accepting and was allowing one film roll as an „accessory‟. Therefore, if the item is an „accessory‟, they would be inputs, even when supplied in multiples. We do not find any specific reason arrived at in the Commissioner (Appeals), impugned order, as to why, he has not considered more than one accessory, a film roll in this case, to be ineligible since the rule do not lay down or stipulate the number of accessories, except that the value of such accessories should be included in the assessable value. We find therefore no legal impediment in considering more than one film roll, as in this case, to be covered under Rule 57B(I)VI as a permissible inputs….” 10. We have perused the finding made by the learned Commissioner in para 49 of the impugned order, wherein he has sought to distinguish the ratio of the above Tribunal decision in the case of Kodak India Ltd (Supra) on the ground that the only condition for allowing the credit under the erstwhile Rule 57B was inclusion of their cost in the assessable value of the final product. We are of the view that the Ld. Commissioner erred in not appreciating that the film-rolls were very much considered as ‘accessories’ to a camera and once the same is considered as ‘accessories’, it qualifies as ‘input’ as defined in Rule 2(k) of the Credit Rules, as applicable during the impugned period and becomes duly eligible for credit. Moreover, when the film roll is being clubbed with the camera and both packed in a combi-pack, the MRP of the combi-pack will obviously include the value of film roll and eligible for credit. 11. Further, the Hon’ble Gujarat High Court in Prime Health Care Products (Supra) as relied by the appellant, has held that credit is eligible on bought out toothbrush when cleared in a combo-pack with the tooth paste manufactured by the assessee inasmuch as the activities of repacking and relabeling of combo-pack reaches to the final stage of marketability, will amount to manufacture, on which central excise duty is being paid by the assessee. The ratio of the said decision is squarely applicable to the present case also. 12. In view of the above findings, we do not find any merit in the impugned orders denying CENVAT credit, the same are thus set aside and the demand of duty, interest and penalty are set aside. The appeals are thus allowed with consequential relief as per law. (Operative part of the order was pronounced in the open Court.)
