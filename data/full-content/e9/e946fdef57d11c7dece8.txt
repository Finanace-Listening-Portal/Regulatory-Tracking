Summary: This practical guide examines the compliance requirements for board and committee meetings of SME listed companies under the Companies Act, 2013, Secretarial Standards and applicable SEBI Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements (LODR) and Prohibition of Insider Trading (PIT) Regulations. It explains the distinction between the corporate governance exemptions available to SME listed entities and their continuing statutory obligations, including board composition, independent directors, committees and secretarial audit. The article outlines an annual meeting calendar aligned with half-yearly and annual financial results, and provides detailed checklists for pre-meeting planning, notices, agenda papers, stock exchange intimations, trading window closure and maintenance of the Structured Digital Database. It addresses meeting conduct, quorum, disclosure of interests, committee recommendations and recording of resolutions. Particular attention is given to the applicable 30-minute and three-hour outcome disclosure timelines and the subsequent compliance requirements concerning minutes, statutory filings and reopening of the trading window. The guide also discusses the applicability of related party transaction requirements to qualifying SME listed entities, committee composition and responsibilities, and the duties of promoters, executive directors, chief financial officers and independent directors. An agenda-wise compliance map covers financial results, dividends, fund-raising, appointments, related party transactions and material events. The article concludes with common compliance lapses, potential penalties and practical measures for maintaining a complete meeting record. The framework is intended to assist directors and compliance professionals in coordinating statutory and exchange-related obligations throughout the meeting cycle. Introduction A board meeting of an SME listed company is governed by the full Companies Act regime plus those parts of SEBI LODR and the PIT Regulations that are not switched off for SME entities. SME listed companies are exempt from LODR Regs 17–27, but not from the Companies Act: Independent Directors, a woman director, Audit Committee, NRC, four board meetings a year and a secretarial audit still apply. Most lapses seen in secretarial audits are not about the meeting itself, but about the clock around it: a late Reg 29 intimation, an outcome filed after 30 minutes (or after 3 hours for a post-market meeting), a trading window that was never closed, or minutes signed on day 45. Listing on BSE SME or NSE Emerge turns a founder-run company into one answerable to public shareholders, the exchange and SEBI. The board room is where that change is most visible, and every person in it carries a different part of the responsibility: founders and promoters, executive directors, Independent Directors, the CFO and the Company Secretary and Compliance Officer. The same discipline applies to committee meetings: many board decisions of a listed company are valid only if the right committee has considered them first. This guide is a working checklist arranged as Before → During → After the meeting, followed by committee meetings, a role-wise checklist, an agenda-wise compliance map and the points most often missed. Legal framework at a glance Applies to SME listed companies Switched off for SMEs (Reg 15(2)(b)) Companies Act, 2013 – meetings, committees, RPT, minutes LODR Regs 17, 17A – board composition, meetings Board Meetings Rules, 2014 – Audit Committee and NRC (Rule 6) LODR Regs 18–22 – LODR committees, vigil mechanism Secretarial Standards SS-1, SS-2 – notice, quorum, conduct, minutes LODR Regs 24, 24A – subsidiaries, secretarial compliance report LODR Regs 6, 7, 29, 30, 32, 33, 42 – compliance officer, intimation, outcome, results LODR Regs 25–27 – Independent Directors, corporate governance report SEBI PIT Regulations, 2015 – UPSI, trading window, SDD Reg 46(2)(b)–(i), (t) – certain website items Secretarial audit, S. 204 – MR-3 for every listed company Schedule V Paras C, D, E – corporate governance disclosures Exception – LODR Reg 23 (RPT) applies from 1 April 2025 if paid-up equity exceeds ₹10 crore or net worth exceeds ₹25 crore Material RPT for SMEs = ₹50 crore or 10% of annual consolidated turnover, whichever is lower Two SME-specific points shape the whole calendar. Financial results are half-yearly, not quarterly. And Reg 23 now catches the larger SMEs, for which a material RPT is one exceeding ₹50 crore or 10% of annual consolidated turnover, whichever is lower; once applicable, it continues until both thresholds are unmet for three consecutive financial years. The annual board and committee calendar Four board meetings are enough for most SME companies if they are placed against the results and AGM deadlines. A suggested calendar for a 31 March year-end company is below; adjust to your year, but keep every gap within 120 days. Meeting window Board agenda (core items) Committees to meet first April – May (annual results within 60 days, i.e. by 30 May) Audited annual results; first-meeting-of-FY items (MBP-1, DIR-8, ID declarations); appointment of internal and secretarial auditors; dividend recommendation Audit Committee (results, auditor’s report, RPT review, internal audit); CSR Committee (annual action plan) July – August Board’s report, financial statements for AGM, AGM notice, record date, reappointments, secretarial audit report (MR-3) Audit Committee; NRC; Stakeholders’ Relationship Committee October – November (half-year results within 45 days, i.e. by 14 November) Unaudited half-yearly results with limited review; review of compliance and investor complaints Audit Committee (results, RPT status, vigil mechanism) January – February Next year’s budget; review of policies (RPT, Reg 30 materiality, codes of conduct, PIT code); board evaluation; succession planning NRC (evaluation); separate meeting of Independent Directors; Audit Committee (internal audit, internal financial controls) An additional meeting is called whenever an event needs board approval in between – fund-raising, allotment, a KMP change, an acquisition or an urgent RPT. Part A – Pre-meeting requisites The meeting is won or lost before it starts: every item below must be closed before the notice goes out or, at the latest, before the Chairperson calls the meeting to order. A1. Annual planning (start of the financial year) Board calendar approved: minimum 4 meetings in the FY, gap between two meetings not more than 120 days (S. 173(1)), mapped to the results deadlines (45 days from half-year end; 60 days for annual audited results). Board composition verified: at least one-third Independent Directors (S. 149(4)), at least one woman director, MD/CEO/WTD + CS + CFO in place (S. 203), CS designated as Compliance Officer (LODR Reg 6). Reg 23 applicability tested on last year’s audited numbers – if newly triggered, comply within six months. A2. Agenda and papers (T-15 to T-7 days) Agenda finalised with the Chairperson / MD; each item mapped to its approving authority (Board, committee, shareholders) and its disclosure trigger (Reg 29 / 30 / 33). Items that must first go to a committee (results, RPTs, auditor appointment, KMP appointment and remuneration) routed to the Audit Committee / NRC before the Board. Notes to agenda for every item: purpose, background, legal provisions, draft resolution, and whether any director is interested (SS-1). For results: draft results, limited review / audit report from the peer-reviewed statutory auditor, CEO/CFO certificate under Reg 33(2)(a), statement of assets and liabilities and cash flow. At the first meeting of the FY: MBP-1 (S. 184(1)) and DIR-8 (S. 164(2)) from every director; ID declarations under S. 149(7); IDs’ registration in the IICA databank confirmed. A3. Notice (at least 7 clear days) Notice with date, time, venue (and VC option) sent to every director – by hand, speed post, registered post, courier or e-mail – at least 7 days before the meeting (S. 173(3), SS-1), with agenda and notes. UPSI papers (results, fund-raising, M&A) circulated separately and password-protected; SS-1 allows UPSI notes at a shorter period only with the consent of a majority of directors, including at least one Independent Director. Shorter notice used only for urgent business, with at least one Independent Director present; otherwise decisions are final only on ratification by at least one Independent Director (S. 173(3) proviso). Proof of dispatch retained (e-mail log, courier receipts) – the first thing a secretarial auditor asks for. A4. Stock exchange and insider trading (T-2 working days and earlier) Reg 29 prior intimation to BSE SME / NSE Emerge at least 2 working days in advance, excluding the date of intimation and the date of the meeting, for financial results, dividend, buyback, bonus, fund-raising, voluntary delisting and alteration of securities. The intimation must state the meeting date and be uploaded on the company’s website. Trading window closed for Designated Persons and their immediate relatives from the end of the half-year till 48 hours after results are made public (PIT Schedule B); closure notice sent to DPs and the exchange. Every person receiving UPSI for the meeting (auditors, valuers, merchant bankers, advisers) entered in the Structured Digital Database before sharing, with confidentiality undertakings on file. A5. Logistics and quorum readiness (T-1 day) Expected attendance confirmed; quorum = one-third of total strength or 2 directors, whichever is higher (S. 174(1)); interested directors are not counted for that item. VC set up under Rule 3: secure link, roll-call script, recording arrangement, confirmation that no unauthorised person is present. Draft outcome letter and results filing pre-prepared (PDF and XBRL), with only the time of conclusion left blank – this is what makes the 30-minute deadline achievable. Statutory auditor’s attendance arranged if results are being approved; attendance register, draft minutes of the previous meeting and statutory registers ready. Part B – During the meeting The Compliance Officer is the Board’s conscience in the room: the job is to keep the meeting valid, the record accurate and the UPSI contained. B1. Opening the meeting Note the exact start time; elect a Chairperson for the meeting if the regular Chairperson is absent. Roll call, especially for VC participants: each director states name and location, confirms that they can see and hear everyone, and that no one else is present. Quorum confirmed at the start and for every item – it must be present throughout (SS-1); re-check when an interested director leaves. Leave of absence recorded for directors who sought it; draft minutes of the previous meeting noted / confirmed and signed. B2. Conducting each item Items taken in agenda order unless the Chairperson decides otherwise; an item not on the agenda only with the Chairperson’s permission and the consent of a majority of directors present, including at least one Independent Director (SS-1). Interest disclosed and recorded; the interested director does not participate in the discussion or vote (S. 184(2)) and is not counted in quorum for that item. Where Reg 23 applies, the Audit Committee’s prior approval confirmed for every RPT placed, and omnibus limits respected. Resolutions read out in their final wording; any amendment in the room captured verbatim; dissent or abstention of any director recorded by name. Matters needing shareholders’ approval (S. 180, S. 186 above limits, material RPTs, preferential issue) are recommended, not approved – check the resolution says so. Items under S. 179(3) (borrowing, investments, loans, buyback, issue of securities, approval of financial statements) passed only at a meeting, never by circulation. B3. UPSI and confidentiality Only directors, KMP and necessary invitees remain for UPSI items; invitees leave after their item. No screen-sharing or forwarding of draft results outside the room or link; VC chat disabled; any new insider who joins mid-meeting noted for the Structured Digital Database. B4. Closing Note the exact time of conclusion – the Reg 30(6) clock starts here: 30 minutes, or 3 hours if the meeting closed after market hours and more than 3 hours before the next day’s opening. Part C – Post-meeting compliance Most penalties on SME companies arise in the 30 days after the meeting. Start with the most time-critical filing. C1. The 30-minute / 3-hour rule for outcome and results Every decision taken at a board meeting, including financial results, must reach the exchange within 30 minutes of the meeting’s closure (Reg 30(6) read with Schedule III, Part A, Para A(4)). Since the LODR (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2024, effective 31 December 2024, the window is 3 hours if the meeting closes after normal trading hours of that day and more than 3 hours before normal trading hours begin on the next trading day. Normal trading hours for equity are 9:15 am to 3:30 pm. Board meeting closes at File by Rule applied 1:00 pm (market open) 1:30 pm During trading hours – 30 minutes 3:20 pm (market still open) 3:50 pm Closed before trading hours ended – 30 minutes, even though the deadline falls after hours 5:00 pm 8:00 pm After trading hours, more than 3 hours before next opening – 3 hours 7:00 am on a trading day 7:30 am Not after that day’s trading hours, and less than 3 hours to opening – 30 minutes The tightest deadline is the outcome and results filing; it is achievable only if the draft is prepared on T-1. Meeting over more than one day: results are disclosed within 30 minutes or 3 hours, as applicable, of the closure of that day’s session. Record the time: the outcome letter must state the start and end time of the meeting – the only evidence of which clock applied – and the minutes must match it. Filed with the results, where applicable: the half-yearly statement of deviation / variation in use of issue proceeds (Reg 32), reviewed by the Audit Committee; and, for SMEs covered by Reg 23, the half-yearly RPT disclosure under Reg 23(9). It is the evidence base for the secretarial audit under S. 204, which continues to apply to SME listed companies even though Reg 24A does not. Part D – Committee meetings LODR Regs 18 to 21 do not apply to SME listed entities, but the Companies Act committees do – and a board decision taken without the required committee recommendation is open to challenge. Treat each committee meeting as a mini board meeting: SS-1 applies to it in full. Committee When mandatory Composition Core role Audit Committee (S. 177, Rule 6) Every listed public company Minimum 3 directors; Independent Directors in majority; majority of members, including the Chairperson, able to read and understand financial statements Results and financial statements, auditors, RPT approval, loans and investments, valuations, internal financial controls, vigil mechanism, use of issue proceeds Nomination & Remuneration Committee (S. 178) Every listed public company 3 or more non-executive directors, at least half Independent; the company’s Chairperson may be a member but not chair the NRC Director and KMP appointments, remuneration policy, evaluation criteria Stakeholders’ Relationship Committee (S. 178(5)) More than 1,000 security holders at any time in the year Chaired by a non-executive director Investor grievances, transfers, transmission, dividends CSR Committee (S. 135) Net worth ₹500 crore+, turnover ₹1,000 crore+ or net profit ₹5 crore+ in the previous year 3 or more directors, at least one Independent; not required if CSR spend is up to ₹50 lakh CSR policy, annual action plan, spend and unspent amounts Meeting of Independent Directors (Sch. IV, Para VII) Every company with Independent Directors Independent Directors only Review of non-independent directors, the Chairperson and the Board; quality and timeliness of information D1. Before the committee meeting Terms of reference approved by the Board, in line with S. 177(4) / S. 178, with quorum stated (if silent, SS-1: one-third of members or 2, whichever is higher). Notice, agenda and notes at least 7 days before; meeting scheduled before the board meeting it feeds into – the same day if necessary. Audit Committee papers: draft results, auditor’s report, RPT list with arm’s length justification, statement of use of issue proceeds, internal audit report and vigil mechanism complaints. D2. During and after the committee meeting Recommendations recorded as “recommended to the Board” – the Board then decides; auditor’s observations and members’ questions minuted as the evidence of oversight. Recommendations placed at the very next board meeting; if the Board does not accept an Audit Committee recommendation, the reasons go into the Board’s report (S. 177(8)). Committee minutes drafted within 15 days, signed within 30 days and placed before the Board at its next meeting (SS-1). Chairpersons of the Audit Committee, NRC and SRC (or an authorised member) attend the AGM (Ss. 177, 178(7), SS-2). Composition restored promptly when a member resigns, so no meeting is held by an irregularly constituted committee. Part E – Role-wise checklist Compliance in an SME is rarely lost by the Company Secretary alone; it is usually lost when a director or promoter does not know what is expected of them. E1. Founders and promoters Treat every dealing between the company and you, your relatives or your other entities as a related party transaction: Audit Committee approval, board approval under S. 188, shareholder approval where material – and step out of the room for it. Do not trade, gift or pledge shares while the trading window is closed; report trades above ₹10 lakh in a quarter within 2 trading days (PIT Reg 7(2)); disclose pledges on promoter shares under SAST Reg 31. Keep UPSI out of family groups, investor chats and lender conversations until it is public. E2. Executive directors (MD / WTD) and the CFO Management papers to the CS in time for the 7-day notice; draft results ready for the Audit Committee at least a week before the board meeting. Sign the results only after the Audit Committee review and the auditor’s report are on record, and sign the CEO/CFO certificate under Reg 33(2)(a) only after reviewing the final numbers. Brief the board on material events between meetings – Reg 30 disclosures cannot wait for the next meeting. E3. Independent Directors Attend: your presence validates shorter-notice meetings and items not on the agenda. Read the papers, ask questions and ensure dissent is minuted – an Independent Director is liable only for acts within their knowledge, attributable through board processes, and with their consent or connivance (S. 149(12)). Scrutinise RPTs and loans to group entities closely – this is where SME governance most often fails. decide disclosure 12 hrs (internal) / 24 hrs (external); 30 min or 3 hrs if decided at the meeting Common lapses seen in practice “SME is exempt” is over-read. Only Regs 17–27 and parts of Reg 46 and Schedule V are switched off. The Companies Act still requires Independent Directors, a woman director, Audit Committee, NRC and secretarial audit. Two board meetings are not enough. Half-yearly results need only two results meetings, but S. 173 still requires four meetings with a 120-day gap. Trading window for half-years. Closure runs from the end of the half-year, not from the date of the notice. Structured Digital Database. Entries must be made before UPSI is shared, time-stamped and non-editable. Late or loose minutes. Minutes signed after 30 days, or kept on unnumbered loose sheets, are a recurring audit qualification. Peer-reviewed auditor. Reg 33 results must carry a report from an auditor holding a valid ICAI peer review certificate. Website not updated. Intimations, outcomes, results and policies must be on the website as well as the exchange portal. Separate ID meeting held on paper. It must be an actual meeting without non-independent directors and management. Board evaluation. The annual evaluation of the Board, committees and directors (Ss. 134(3)(p), 178(2)) must be done and reported. Conclusion A well-run SME board or committee meeting is a 45-day process, not a 2-hour event: plan the calendar at the start of the year, pre-draft the disclosures before the directors sit down, and close the paperwork within 30 days. When founders, directors and the Compliance Officer each own their part of that clock, the Board is protected, the promoters are protected, and the company’s standing with the exchange and its investors grows with every meeting. The points to carry away are few. SME listed companies are exempt from LODR Regs 17–27, but not from the Companies Act. Reg 23 applies to SMEs with paid-up equity above ₹10 crore or net worth above ₹25 crore. Board meeting intimation under Reg 29 is due 2 working days in advance for results and corporate actions. Outcome and results must be filed within 30 minutes of the meeting, or 3 hours if it ends after market hours and more than 3 hours before the next day’s opening. Minutes and MGT-14 fall due within 30 days and the trading window reopens 48 hours after results – most SME penalties arise in this 30-day tail. ****** **This document is for educational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice.
