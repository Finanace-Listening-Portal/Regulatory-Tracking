Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Bondada Engineering Limited Vs Office of The Assistant Commissioner of Central Tax ( Karnataka High Court) Summary: The Karnataka High Court allowed in part a writ petition challenging orders arising from proceedings concerning Input Tax Credit (ITC) under the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. The petitioner had challenged the order-in-original dated 31 March 2022, passed under Section 73(9) of the CGST Act, the order-in-appeal dated 19 April 2023, and the review order/authorization dated 30 September 2022. The petitioner sought quashing of these orders and a direction for fresh assessment of ITC pursuant to the show-cause notice dated 3 December 2020. The dispute arose after the petitioner submitted a reply to the show-cause notice, following which the original adjudicating authority passed its order. The petitioner subsequently preferred an appeal before the Appellate Authority, which was dismissed for non-prosecution. After hearing both parties and examining the record, the High Court observed that the Appellate Authority had dismissed the appeal for non-prosecution, although it had also assigned other reasons in its order. The Court held that a justice-oriented approach was necessary in the circumstances and considered it appropriate to provide the petitioner with another opportunity to prosecute the appeal on merits. Accordingly, the Court found sufficient grounds to interfere with the appellate order dated 19 April 2023. Rather than deciding the underlying Input Tax Credit dispute itself, the Court directed that the matter be reconsidered by the Appellate Authority afresh in accordance with law. Consequently, the High Court partly allowed the writ petition and set aside the order-in-appeal dated 19 April 2023, remitting the matter to respondent No. 2, the Appellate Authority, for fresh consideration. The petitioner was directed to appear before the Appellate Authority on the date specified in the order, without awaiting any further notice. The Court expressly kept all rival contentions open and clarified that it had expressed no opinion on the merits or demerits of the parties’ respective claims. Thus, the relief granted was confined to restoration of the appellate proceedings and an opportunity for reconsideration in accordance with law, without adjudicating the substantive ITC dispute. FULL TEXT OF THE ORIGINAL JUDGMENT/ORDER In this petition, the petitioner has sought for the following reliefs: “(i) Issue a WRIT OF CERTIORARI and quash the following impugned orders a. order-in-original No. 24/2022 dated 31.03.2022 (Annexure D) Issued by R1. b. order-in-appeal number 149/ADC-Al/GST/2023 dated 19.04.2023 (Annexure F9 Issued by R2. c. review order/authorization dated 30.09.2022 in proceedings No. GEXCOM/REV/GST/115/2022 (Annexure J) Issued by R3. and remand the matter back to the Respondent No. 1 for a fresh consideration in accordance with law. ii. Issue a WRIT OF MANDAMUS to direct the 1st Respondent to commence a fresh assessment of Input Tax Credit initiated pursuant to the show cause notice No. IV/16/231/2019-20/AED6 dated 03.12.2020 (Annexure B) Issued by R1. iii. Issue any other writ, order or direction to which the Petitioner is found entitled to in the present facts and circumstances. 2. Heard learned counsel for the petitioner and learned counsel for the respondents. Perused the material on record. 3. A perusal of the material on record will indicate that pursuant to the show-cause notice issued by respondent No.1 to the petitioner dated 03.12.2020, the petitioner submitted a reply, pursuant to which, respondent No.1 passed the impugned order dated 31.03.2022 under Section 73(9) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (for short ‘2017 Act’). Aggrieved by the same, the petitioner filed an appeal, which was dismissed for non-prosecution by respondent No.2-Appellate Authority and as such, the petitioner is before this Court by way of present petition. 4. A perusal of the impugned order at Annexure-F dated 19.04.2023 will indicate that respondent No.2-Appellate Authority has proceeded to dismiss the appeal for non-prosecution. Though respondent No.2-Appellate Authority has assigned various other reasons for dismissing the appeal, having regard to the fact that the appeal was dismissed for non-prosecution, it would be necessary to adopt a justice oriented approach and in order to provide one more opportunity to the appellant/petitioner to prosecute the appeal on merits, I deem it just and appropriate to set aside the impugned order at Annexure-F dated 19.04.2023 and remit the matter back to the Appellate Authority for re-consideration afresh in accordance with law. 5. In the result, I proceed to pass the following: ORDER (i) Writ petition is allowed in part; (ii) Order-in-appeal at Annexure-F dated 19.04.2023 is set aside; the matter is remitted back to respondent No.2-Appellate Authority for reconsideration afresh in accordance with law; (iii) Petitioner to appear before respondent No.2-Appellate Authority on 04.2025 without awaiting further notice from respondent No.2. (iv) All rival contentions of the parties are left open and no opinion is expressed on merits/demerits of the rival contentions.
