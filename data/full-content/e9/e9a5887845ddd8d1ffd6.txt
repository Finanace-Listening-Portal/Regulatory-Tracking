Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Vs Vodafone Idea Limited (ITAT Mumbai) Summary: The Mumbai Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), comprising Accountant Member S. Rifaur Rahman and Judicial Member Rahul Chaudhary, decided three connected appeals involving Vodafone India Ltd., subsequently merged into Vodafone Idea Ltd., for Assessment Years 2011–12 and 2012–13. The appeals arose from assessment proceedings involving disallowances under Sections 14A, 36(1)(iii), 40(a)(ia) and 37(1), deduction under Section 80-IA, and transfer pricing adjustments concerning advertisement, marketing and promotion (AMP) expenditure, brand royalty, technology support charges and reimbursement of employee expenses. For Assessment Year 2011–12, the Assessing Officer passed the final assessment order on 28 January 2016, determining total income at ₹478,20,94,710 against returned income of ₹235,15,11,079. The Tribunal examined the disputed additions in light of the assessment records, directions of the Dispute Resolution Panel, judicial precedents and earlier decisions involving the assessee. The first major issue concerned a disallowance of ₹357,23,70,000 under Section 14A read with Rule 8D. The Assessing Officer had made the disallowance notwithstanding the assessee’s contention that no exempt income was earned during the relevant previous year. The Tribunal relied on the Bombay High Court’s decision in PCIT v. Red Chillies Entertainment Pvt. Ltd. and earlier Tribunal orders in Vodafone’s own cases, holding that disallowance under Section 14A could not be sustained in the absence of exempt income. It accordingly deleted the entire addition. The Tribunal also considered the disallowance of ₹19,35,01,258 under Section 36(1)(iii) relating to interest on capital work-in-progress. It observed that acquisition of network assets to improve telecommunications services within the existing Mumbai circle did not necessarily amount to extension of business. Following its earlier decision for Assessment Years 2006–07 and 2007–08, the Tribunal deleted the interest disallowance. The Tribunal next examined the disallowance of ₹30,95,03,786 relating to roaming charges under Section 40(a)(ia). The Assessing Officer had treated payments made to other telecom operators as liable to tax deduction under Section 194J, reasoning that human intervention was involved in establishing, monitoring and maintaining telecommunications infrastructure. Vodafone argued that the actual roaming process was automated and did not involve human intervention in switching calls between networks. The Tribunal relied on its earlier orders for Assessment Years 2006–07, 2007–08 and 2009–10, which had followed Vodafone East Ltd. v. ACIT. It held that roaming charges did not attract the TDS obligations invoked under Sections 194C or 194J. Consequently, the disallowance was deleted, and the corresponding grounds raised by the Revenue were rejected. Another substantial dispute involved ₹47,17,99,596 in discounts extended to distributors of prepaid SIM cards, recharge vouchers and talk time. The Assessing Officer regarded these discounts as commission requiring deduction of tax under Section 194H and made a consequential disallowance under Section 40(a)(ia). Vodafone maintained that distributors purchased prepaid products under principal-to-principal arrangements and that no commission was paid or credited to them. The Tribunal examined earlier decisions involving Vodafone and Idea Cellular, including the Karnataka High Court’s ruling in Bharti Airtel Ltd. v. DCIT, the Bombay High Court’s decision concerning Reliance Communications Infrastructure Ltd., and the Rajasthan High Court’s decision in Hindustan Coca Cola Beverages (P.) Ltd. v. CIT. Following the findings on the nature of distributor transactions in earlier proceedings, it held that the discounts were not commission liable to TDS under Section 194H and deleted the disallowance. The Tribunal also considered the assessee’s deduction claims under Section 80-IA, including the eligibility of income connected with its telecommunications undertaking. The dispute involved the treatment of income arising from the eligible business and the extent to which such income qualified for deduction. The Tribunal examined earlier orders in the assessee’s own cases and the judicial authorities discussed in the proceedings. It granted relief in accordance with the applicable findings concerning the eligible undertaking and the treatment of other income. The Tribunal separately examined the transfer pricing adjustment of ₹22,01,14,350 relating to AMP expenditure, considering whether the expenditure constituted an international transaction with an associated enterprise warranting a separate arm’s length adjustment. Applying the relevant judicial principles and earlier findings, it deleted the AMP adjustment. On the remaining transfer pricing issues, the Tribunal examined adjustments of ₹7,97,68,155 concerning Vodafone brand royalty, ₹1,31,43,772 relating to technology support charges and ₹93,12,637 concerning reimbursement of salary and related expenses. The royalty dispute involved payments to Vodafone Ireland Marketing Limited for the right to use the Vodafone trademark and trade name. The Tribunal found that certain matters required further examination of agreements, supporting documents and the nature of the transactions. Accordingly, the relevant issues were restored to the Assessing Officer or Transfer Pricing Officer for fresh adjudication after providing the assessee a reasonable opportunity of being heard. For Assessment Year 2012–13, the Tribunal similarly restored the brand royalty issue and the ₹11,23,53,754 adjustment relating to reimbursement of expenses, directing examination of whether the expenditure concerned deputed employees and whether the reimbursements were at arm’s length. It also issued appropriate directions concerning tax credit and other consequential matters. The Tribunal further considered the Revenue’s challenge relating to ₹2,05,35,800 paid to the Department of Telecommunications, which had been disallowed under Section 37(1). Following the findings applicable to the assessee’s earlier proceedings, it rejected the Revenue’s challenge. Other issues concerning interest computation, tax deducted at source, book-profit computation and consequential adjustments were addressed through appropriate directions. The grounds challenging initiation of penalty proceedings under Section 271(1)(c) were dismissed as premature. By its order pronounced on 17 May 2024, the ITAT partly allowed Vodafone’s appeal for Assessment Year 2011–12, dismissed the Revenue’s appeal for that year and allowed Vodafone’s appeal for Assessment Year 2012–13 for statistical purposes. The ruling granted substantial relief on disallowances relating to exempt income, interest expenditure, roaming charges, prepaid distributor discounts and AMP expenditure, while directing fresh examination of specified transfer pricing adjustments. Cases Discussed PCIT v. Red Chillies Entertainment Pvt. Ltd. — [2020] 116 taxmann.com 770 (Bombay High Court); disallowance under Section 14A cannot be made where no exempt income is earned. Vodafone East Ltd. v. ACIT — 156 ITD 337 (ITAT Kolkata); roaming charges paid to other telecom operators do not attract TDS under Sections 194C or 194J. Bharti Airtel Ltd. v. DCIT — 372 ITR 33 (Karnataka High Court); treatment of prepaid SIM card and recharge voucher discounts under Section 194H. PCIT v. Reliance Communications Infrastructure Ltd. — Income Tax Appeal No. 702 of 2017 (Bombay High Court); no TDS under Section 194H on transactions conducted on a principal-to-principal basis. DCIT v. Core Healthcare Ltd. — 252 ITR 61 (Gujarat High Court); deductibility of interest on borrowed capital under Section 36(1)(iii). ITW Signode India Ltd. v. DCIT — 110 TTJ 170 (ITAT Hyderabad); interest deduction relating to borrowed funds. Hindustan Coca Cola Beverages (P.) Ltd. v. CIT — 402 ITR 539 (Rajasthan High Court); TDS obligations concerning distributor arrangements. CIT (TDS), Jaipur v. Idea Cellular Ltd. — Income Tax Appeal No. 90/2018, decided on 12 April 2018 (Rajasthan High Court); treatment of distributor discounts under Section 194H. Idea Cellular Ltd. v. DCIT (TDS) — ITA Nos. 1041, 1042, 1953–1955/Pun/2013 and connected appeals, decided on 4 January 2017 (ITAT Pune); principal-to-principal relationship between telecom operators and distributors.
