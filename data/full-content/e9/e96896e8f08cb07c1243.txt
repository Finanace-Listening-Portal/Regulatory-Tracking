Advt The central bank said the Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026, were issued on January 13 and came into effect on October 1, replacing the Foreign Exchange Management (Export of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2015.The RBI also issued the Second Amendment Regulations to the new trade framework on November 2, with retrospective effect from October 1, 2026.Clarifications on export declarations and reportingThe RBI has clarified that resident individuals exporting services are not required to separately furnish an EDF. The necessary details provided to their AD bank for realising inward remittances and crediting their bank accounts will suffice.For service exporters other than resident individuals, EDF filing is required only when the value of each inward remittance exceeds Rs 10 lakh, or its equivalent in foreign currency.The central bank has also clarified that the EDF must be filed before the inward remittance is credited to the exporter's account in cases where the specified threshold is exceeded.For software exports, the SOFTEX form has been discontinued from October 1. Software exporters operating from the Domestic Tariff Area (DTA) can now file the EDF with any AD bank or the Software Technology Parks of India (STPI), while exporters operating from Special Economic Zones (SEZs) can file it with the Development Commissioner of the respective SEZ. Advt The RBI has prescribed different timelines for EDF submission by service exporters. For software exports, the form must be submitted within 30 days from the end of the month in which the invoice is raised. For services other than software, the deadline is within 30 days from the end of the invoice month or on or before receipt of payment.For advance payments received against service exports, including software, the EDF must be submitted within 30 days from the end of the month in which the invoice is raised.The FAQs also clarify that AD banks can extend the prescribed EDF submission timelines if they are satisfied that the exporter's request is reasonable. Advt Further, the requirement for obtaining EDF or Guaranteed Remittance (GR) waiver letters has been discontinued. Where exported goods have no realisable value, exporters can declare zero value in the appropriate field in the EDF. AD banks are no longer authorised to issue EDF/GR waiver letters from October 1.Banks to continue reporting transactions in E/IDPMSThe RBI has clarified that while certain service exporters and importers are not required to furnish separate declarations, AD banks will continue to have reporting obligations under the new framework.Banks are required to report inward and outward remittance details, as applicable, in E/IDPMS for export and import of services undertaken by resident individuals and other persons up to the specified threshold. Advt For import of services, the central bank has clarified that the existing process will continue for resident individuals and other persons where the invoice or contract value is up to Rs 10 lakh. The information already being provided by such importers to AD banks for processing outward remittances will suffice.For service imports exceeding Rs 10 lakh by persons other than resident individuals, the reporting requirements under the revised framework will apply.The RBI has also clarified the scope of simplified reconciliation in E/IDPMS for transactions of up to Rs 10 lakh. The facility will apply only where the original shipping bill or bill of entry value, in the case of goods, or invoice value, in the case of services, is up to Rs 10 lakh.It will not apply to transactions where the original bill or invoice value exceeds Rs 10 lakh, even if the outstanding amount subsequently falls below the threshold.
