Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); PLUSH Vs ITO (ITAT Surat) Partner Disputes Constitute Sufficient Cause: ITAT Condones 1,279-Day Delay, Remands Assessment and Penalty The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Surat Bench, condoned delays of 1,279 days in the quantum appeal and 536 days in the penalty appeal, accepting that disputes between partners and related court proceedings had prevented the assessee-firm from attending to its tax matters. In PLUSH vs. ITO, the Tribunal restored both matters to the Assessing Officer (AO) for fresh adjudication, with directions to consider additional evidence and provide an opportunity of hearing. The Bench comprised Judicial Member Suchitra Kamble and Accountant Member B.M. Biyani. The decision emphasises that an explanation supported by evidence and remaining uncontroverted by the Revenue may establish sufficient cause for delayed filing. Facts of the Case The assessee, a partnership firm, did not file its income-tax return for AY 2017-18. A notice dated 29 December 2017 requiring it to file a return remained unanswered. Subsequent notices under section 142(1) and a show-cause notice under section 144 also went uncomplied with. Consequently, the AO passed an ex parte assessment order dated 3 October 2019, determining total income at ₹8,54,93,600, comprising cash deposits and credit entries in the firm’s bank account. The AO subsequently imposed a penalty of ₹66,04,380 under section 271AAC(1) through an ex parte order dated 20 October 2021. A retired partner, Parul Nilesh Shah, later received a departmental communication seeking payment of outstanding demands against the firm. Following enquiries, separate appeals were filed before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) against the assessment and penalty orders in May 2023. The CIT(A) dismissed both appeals primarily because of the filing delays, while also upholding the AO’s orders on merits. Explanation for the Delays The assessee explained that Parul Nilesh Shah had retired from the firm with effect from 1 April 2017 and was unaware of the assessment proceedings. According to the explanation furnished in Form No. 35, multiple legal disputes had affected the firm after the retirement. Consequently, nobody took responsibility for responding to tax notices. Upon receiving a demand communication dated 23 February 2023, the retired partner attempted to contact the person understood to be managing the firm’s affairs, but could not reach him. The assessee also submitted a corroborative affidavit from a present partner, Mahesh Kumar Seventilal Shah. The affidavit explained the disruption caused by disputes and confirmed support for pursuing the appeals. It also ratified the steps already taken on behalf of the firm. The assessee submitted that these circumstances explained both the earlier non-compliance and the subsequent delays in approaching the appellate authority. Additional Evidence Sought to Be Produced The assessee filed an application under Rule 29 of the Income-tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963, seeking admission of additional evidence. The documents included a Gujarat High Court petition concerning quashing of an FIR, VAT return acknowledgements, and income-tax returns and audit records for earlier assessment years. The assessee argued that these records supported the existence and continuity of its business activities. They were also relevant to its challenge concerning the addition under section 69A and application of section 115BBE. It explained that disputes and lack of effective access to the firm’s records had prevented production of these documents before the lower authorities. Revenue’s Submissions The Revenue supported the orders passed by the lower authorities. However, considering the circumstances affecting the firm, its representative did not object to remanding the matters. The Revenue requested that the Tribunal issue strict directions requiring the assessee to participate in the fresh proceedings. Tribunal’s Findings and Decision The Tribunal observed that the explanation concerning partner disputes and court proceedings was supported by an affidavit and had not been controverted by the Revenue. It therefore held that the assessee had been prevented by sufficient cause from filing its appeals within time. Relying on the Supreme Court’s decision in Collector, Land Acquisition vs. Mst. Katiji and Others, the Tribunal adopted a justice-oriented approach, giving preference to substantial justice over technical considerations. Accordingly, it condoned both delays and restored the assessment and penalty matters to the AO. The AO was directed to provide a hearing, consider the additional evidence and any further evidence submitted, and pass fresh orders uninfluenced by the earlier orders. Outcome and Significance Both appeals were allowed for statistical purposes. The Tribunal did not finally delete the assessed income or penalty; their sustainability remains subject to fresh adjudication. The relief also carried a clear obligation: the assessee must remain vigilant, participate in hearings and avoid unnecessary adjournments. Failure to cooperate would permit the AO to proceed according to law. Cases Discussed: Collector, Land Acquisition Vs Mst. Katiji and Others — Supreme Court; 1987 AIR 1353; 1987 2 SCC 387 (as cited in the judgment). FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT SURAT The captioned two (2) appeals filed by assessee involve connected/inter- related issues. Therefore, they were heard together and are being disposed of by this common order for the sake of convenience, brevity, clarity and to avoid repetition of facts. The details of these appeals are as under: (i) ITA 608/SRT/2025 – Quantum-Appeal: This is a Quantum-Appeal directed against the order of first appeal dated 02.04.2025 passed by learned Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals)-NFAC, Delhi [“Ld. CIT(A)”], which in turn arises out of the assessment-order dated 03.10.2019 passed by ITO, Ward-7, Vapi [“Ld. AO”] u/s 144 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 [“Act”] for assessment-year [“AY”] 2017-18. (ii) ITA 609/SRT/2025 – Penalty-Appeal: This is a Penalty-Appeal directed against the order of first appeal dated 02.04.2025 passed by Ld. CIT(A), which in turn arises out of the penalty-order dated 20.10.2021 passed by Ld. AO u/s 271AAC(1) of the Act for AY 2017- 18. 2. Briefly stated, the facts of the case are such that the assessee is a partnership firm. For AY 2017-18 under consideration, the assessee-firm was having two partners, namely (i) Shri Parul Nilesh Shah and (2) Shri Manish Bhagwanbhai Suthar. However, Shri Parul Nilesh Shah resigned from firm w.e.f. 01.04.2017. For AY 2017-18, the assessee-firm did not file any return u/s 139. The Ld. Therefore, Ld. AR prayed that in the interest of justice, the delays occurred in filing appeals before Ld. CIT(A) be condoned and these matters be remanded back to the file of Ld. AO for adjudication afresh after hearing assessee. He acknowledged that the assessee shall definitely make a proper and effective representation before Ld. AO. 7. Ld. DR for revenue though dutifully supported the orders of lower authorities but considering the facts and circumstances prevailing in assessee-firm, did not raise any objection against remanding back. He, however, requested the bench to give stricter direction to asseseee to ensure participation in set aside proceedings. 8. Upon careful consideration of the facts and material on record, we note that both assessment-order and penalty-order were passed by Ld. AO as ex-parte on account of non-compliances with the notices issued and, thereafter, the appeals before Ld. CIT(A) were also filed belatedly. The assessee has attributed both the non-compliance before Ld. AO and the filing delays before Ld. CIT(A) to the disputes between the partners and certain legal matters in courts, as explained in Form No. 35 and supported by the affidavit of a present partner. This explanation has not been controverted by revenue and Ld. DR has not raised any objection to the matter being restored. In these circumstances, we find that the assessee was prevented by “sufficient cause” from filing appeals before Ld. CIT(A) within time. It is a settled position by Hon’ble Supreme Court in Collector, Land Acquisition Vs Mst. Katiji and others 1987 AIR 1353, 1987 2 SCC 387 that whenever “substantial justice” and “technical considerations” are opposed to each other, the cause of “substantial justice” must be preferred by adopting a justice-oriented approach. Thus, taking into account the facts of case and the decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court, we take a judicious view, condone the delays occurred in filing appeals before Ld. CIT(A). 9. Further, considering the above submissions of parties; having regard to the principle of natural justice and also bearing in mind that no prejudice would be caused to revenue if the present matters are restored at the level of Ld. AO, we restore these matters to the file of Ld. AO for adjudication afresh, at the risk and responsibility of assessee. The Ld. AO shall give necessary opportunity of hearing to assessee and pass appropriate orders uninfluenced by his earlier orders. The Ld. AO shall also consider the additional evidences filed by assessee before us and further evidences, if any, the assessee may file to Ld. AO. The assessee is also directed to remain vigilant and ensure participation in the hearings as may be fixed by Ld. AO without seeking unnecessary adjournments failing which the Ld. AO shall be at liberty to proceed in accordance with law. Ordered accordingly. 10. In result, both of these appeals are allowed for statistical purposes. Order pronounced in open court on 30/10/2026.
