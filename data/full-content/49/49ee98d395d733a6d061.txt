(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal filed by N.V. International against the Gauhati High Court’s refusal to condone a delay of 189 days in filing an appeal under Section 37 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. The applicant, a registered partnership firm based in New Delhi, and one of its partners had sought to challenge the rejection of their commercial claim against the State of Assam by the Arbitral Tribunal. The District Judge, Kamrup (M), Guwahati, had passed the order dated 30 May 2016, which was communicated to the applicants’ counsel in the first week of June 2016. However, the appeal was not filed within the prescribed limitation period, resulting in an application seeking condonation of the 189-day delay. The applicants attributed the delay to the prolonged illness of their representative, who had allegedly been advised complete bed rest, and the death of the brother of applicant No. 2 on 14 November 2016. They submitted that these circumstances prevented timely action despite receiving information about the dismissal order. They further stated that a new counsel was engaged on 20 February 2017 and that approximately 15 days were required to prepare the appeal because of the voluminous records. The Gauhati High Court, however, found these explanations inadequate. No supporting particulars were furnished regarding the nature of the representative’s illness or how the death of the partner’s brother prevented the firm from filing the appeal. The High Court also questioned why a commercial partnership firm depended upon a single employee to initiate appellate proceedings. The Gauhati High Court reiterated that the relevant consideration in an application for condonation of delay is the sufficiency of the explanation rather than merely the length of the delay. Since the applicants failed to establish sufficient cause for the delay of 189 days, the High Court rejected the condonation application and dismissed the interlocutory application without any order as to costs. The matter subsequently reached the Supreme Court, which granted leave and dismissed the appeal in terms of its signed reportable judgment. The supplied Supreme Court order does not reproduce the reasoning contained in that reportable judgment. Accordingly, no additional substantive findings or limitation principles are attributed to the Supreme Court beyond the dismissal expressly recorded in the supplied order.
