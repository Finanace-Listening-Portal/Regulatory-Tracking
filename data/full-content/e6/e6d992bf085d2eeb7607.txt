Shah Commerce Education Limited on BSE and NSE on November 18, 2026, without a conventional Initial Public Offering (IPO), demonstrates an innovative corporate restructuring route under Indian securities laws. Through an NCLT-sanctioned Composite Scheme of Arrangement, Veranda Learning Solutions Limited demerged its commerce education business into J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited. Existing shareholders received one equity share of the resulting company for every share held in Veranda Learning Solutions Limited, resulting in the allotment of 9,65,06,610 equity shares without any additional investment. The transaction was facilitated by SEBI’s relaxation under Rule 19(7) of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957, permitting direct listing without a public offer, subject to prescribed conditions. With 67.97% public shareholding, the resulting company comfortably satisfied the minimum 25% public shareholding requirement. The restructuring highlights the interplay between Sections 230–232 of the Companies Act, 2013, SEBI LODR Regulations and SCRR provisions. It offers a practical blueprint for listed companies seeking to unlock shareholder value, establish independent business identities and achieve stock exchange listings through demergers rather than conventional IPOs. Introduction – An Interesting Corporate Restructuring Transaction The Indian capital markets witnessed a notable corporate restructuring milestone with the listing of J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited on the BSE and National Stock Exchange (NSE) on November 18, 2026. What makes this transaction particularly noteworthy for corporate restructuring professionals is the regulatory route adopted to achieve a separate stock exchange listing. Instead of pursuing a conventional Initial Public Offering (IPO), the commerce education business of Veranda Learning Solutions Limited was carved out through a Composite Scheme of Arrangement, transferred to a separate resulting company, and subsequently listed on both national stock exchanges. The existing shareholders of Veranda Learning Solutions Limited received shares in the resulting company in accordance with the approved share entitlement ratio, without making any additional payment. This transaction serves as an instructive case study on how a distinct business vertical housed within an existing listed company can be demerged and independently listed through an NCLT-sanctioned scheme, demonstrating the practical interplay between the Companies Act, 2013, the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, and the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 (SCRR). 2. Background – Creating a Separate Commerce Education Business J.K. Shah Classes has long held an established presence in the commerce and professional education segment. Previously, this commerce education vertical operated within the broader business portfolio of Veranda Learning Solutions Limited, a listed education enterprise housing several prominent brands, including: J.K. Shah Classes BB Virtuals Navkar Digital Institute Tapasya College of Commerce Logic School of Management To unlock value and establish operational focus, the group decided to separate the commerce education business from the remaining operations of Veranda Learning Solutions Limited. The strategic objective was to build a dedicated, independently listed commerce education entity with its own management bandwidth, operational flexibility, and tailored capital allocation. To implement this restructuring, J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited was incorporated as an unlisted public limited company on August 13, 2025, and subsequently acted as the Resulting Company under the Composite Scheme of Arrangement. 3. Understanding the Structure of the Composite Scheme The transaction was executed through a Composite Scheme of Arrangement amongst: 1. Veranda Learning Solutions Limited – Demerged Company and Amalgamated Company 2. Veranda XL Learning Solutions Private Limited – Amalgamating Company 3. J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited – Resulting Company The scheme comprised four principal steps: Step 1 – Amalgamation Veranda XL Learning Solutions Private Limited was amalgamated with Veranda Learning Solutions Limited. Step 2 – Demerger of the Commerce Education Business The commerce education business of Veranda Learning Solutions Limited—engaged in test preparation for CA and allied professional courses and examinations—was transferred to J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited on a going-concern basis. Step 3 – Cancellation of Existing Share Capital The entire pre-scheme paid-up share capital of J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited (1,000 equity shares of ₹10 each held by Veranda Learning Solutions Limited) was reduced and cancelled as an integral part of the scheme under Sections 230–232 read with Section 66 of the Companies Act, 2013, eliminating the need for a separate capital reduction procedure. Step 4 – Allotment of Shares to Existing Shareholders Shareholders of Veranda Learning Solutions Limited as on October 6, 2026 (the Record Date) became entitled to receive 1 fully paid-up equity share of ₹10 each in J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited for every 1 fully paid-up equity share held in Veranda Learning Solutions Limited. Accordingly, 9,65,06,610 equity shares were allotted on October 7, 2026. 4. The Regulatory Masterstroke – Rule 19(7) of the SCRR, 1957 Ordinarily, an unlisted company seeking a stock exchange listing must undertake a public issue and satisfy the minimum public offer stipulations prescribed under the SCRR and SEBI ICDR Regulations. However, the securities framework provides an explicit alternative route for scheme-based restructurings. Rule 19(7) of the SCRR, 1957 empowers SEBI, at its discretion or on the recommendation of a recognised stock exchange, to waive or relax the strict enforcement of any or all of the listing requirements prescribed under the Rules. The operational framework governing this relaxation is laid down in SEBI Master Circular No. SEBI/HO/CFD/POD-2/P/CIR/2023/93 dated June 20, 2023 (Master Circular on (i) Scheme of Arrangement by Listed Entities and (ii) Relaxation under Sub-rule (7) of Rule 19 of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957). Specifically, Part II, Paragraph A(1) of the Master Circular expressly recognises the listing of equity shares of an unlisted transferee/resulting company on a recognised stock exchange without making an initial public offer, subject to strict compliance with prescribed conditions, including: 1. NCLT-Sanctioned Scheme: The shares sought to be listed must be allotted by an unlisted transferee/resulting company to the shareholders of a listed transferor/demerged company pursuant to a scheme of reconstruction or amalgamation sanctioned by the NCLT under Sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act, 2013. 2. Minimum 25% Public Shareholding: At least 25% of the post-scheme paid-up share capital of the resulting company must comprise shares allotted to the public shareholders of the listed transferor company. 3. Restriction on Outside Issuance: The resulting company must not issue or reissue shares outside the approved scheme. 4. Warrants and Agreements: Prescribed conditions regarding outstanding warrants, convertible instruments, and shareholder agreements must be complied with. 5. Lock-in Continuity: Shares issued in lieu of locked-in shares of the transferor company remain subject to the corresponding remaining lock-in period. Additionally, the resulting company must obtain stock exchange observation/no-objection letters prior to filing with the NCLT, complete allotment post-sanction, and submit the listing application within prescribed timelines. Thus, Rule 19(7) is not an informal concession, but a structured, codified regulatory route. 5. Specific Application in the J.K. Shah Transaction The statutory public announcement issued by J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited on November 6, 2026 confirms the exact regulatory milestones of this listing: NCLT Sanction and Effective Date The Composite Scheme was sanctioned by the NCLT, Chennai Bench, vide order dated August 20, 2026, and became effective on August 31, 2026. SEBI Relaxation Under Rule 19(7) SEBI granted relaxation under Rule 19(7) of the SCRR from the strict enforcement of Rule 19(2)(b) (as amended by the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Amendment Rules, 2026, Notification G.S.R. 184(E) dated March 13, 2026, which governs minimum public offer thresholds via an offer document). Listed Capital and Public Shareholding 9,65,06,610 equity shares of ₹10 each allotted pursuant to the scheme were admitted to trading on BSE and NSE on November 18, 2026. The post-scheme shareholding pattern disclosed in the announcement reflects 67.97% public shareholding and 32.03% promoter and promoter group shareholding—comfortably exceeding the mandatory 25% public shareholding eligibility threshold stipulated under the SEBI Master Circular. 6. Interplay of SEBI LODR Regulations and SCRR Provisions A common misconception in corporate restructuring is that NCLT sanction alone guarantees stock exchange listing. In reality, scheme approval and listing relaxation are distinct regulatory tracks governed by separate provisions: 1. Sections 230–232 (read with Section 66), Companies Act, 2013 – Sanction of the Composite Scheme and integral cancellation of pre-scheme share capital by the NCLT. 2. Regulation 11, SEBI LODR – Ensures that schemes of arrangement do not violate, override, or circumscribe securities laws or stock exchange requirements. 3. Regulation 37, SEBI LODR – Mandates submission of the draft scheme to stock exchanges to obtain observation/no-objection letters prior to approaching the NCLT. 4. Regulation 94, SEBI LODR – Governs stock exchange obligations in forwarding draft schemes (Reg. 94(2)) and post-sanction Rule 19(7) relaxation recommendations (Reg. 94(5)) to SEBI. 5. Rule 19(2)(b), SCRR, 1957 – Prescribes minimum public offer and allotment thresholds for listing via a public offer document. 6. Rule 19(7), SCRR, 1957 – Empowers SEBI to relax strict enforcement of Rule 19(2)(b) to allow scheme-based listing without an IPO. In this transaction, BSE and NSE issued their observation letters on January 19, 2026 and January 20, 2026, respectively, followed by NCLT sanction on August 20, 2026, and in-principle listing approvals in October 2026. 7. Why Was a Conventional IPO Not Required? In a conventional IPO, an unlisted company raises capital or provides an exit through a fresh issue or an offer for sale (OFS) to new public investors, necessitating a Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP), merchant banker due diligence, book-building, and SEBI ICDR offer compliance. In the J.K. Shah restructuring, no fresh capital was raised from the public, nor was an offer for sale conducted. Instead, shares of the resulting company were mirror-allotted directly to the existing shareholders of Veranda Learning Solutions Limited pursuant to the demerger. Because a broad public shareholding base (67.97%) already existed by virtue of the parent company’s public shareholders, SEBI’s relaxation under Rule 19(7) enabled direct listing without duplicating public offer formalities. 8. Strategic Advantages for Multi-Business Listed Entities For conglomerates or listed companies housing diverse verticals (such as education, technology, and financial services) with differing capital intensities and growth trajectories, a demerger coupled with a Rule 19(7) listing offers compelling advantages: Independent Corporate Identity Establishes a dedicated Board, leadership team, and governance structure tailored to the specific industry. Direct Shareholder Value Unlocking Existing shareholders receive direct equity participation in the demerged entity without cash outlay. Independent Price Discovery Enables analysts and institutional investors to value the pure-play business on its own financial metrics rather than at a conglomerate discount. Tailored Capital Allocation and M&A Flexibility Allows the resulting company to independently raise growth capital, pursue strategic acquisitions, or enter partnerships suited to its sector. 9. Post-Listing Compliance – Listing Is Only the Beginning Achieving a scheme-based listing does not exempt the resulting company from the full rigour of listed-entity governance. From the date of listing, the company must ensure ongoing compliance with: Minimum Public Shareholding (MPS) Continuous adherence to at least 25% public shareholding under Rule 19A of the SCRR, 1957 and Regulation 38 of SEBI LODR. Board and Committee Governance Proper constitution and functioning of the Audit Committee, Nomination and Remuneration Committee, and Stakeholders Relationship Committee. Disclosure and Reporting Requirements Quarterly/annual financial results, shareholding patterns, Related Party Transaction (RPT) approvals, and timely Regulation 30 material event disclosures. Insider Trading Controls Maintenance of a Structured Digital Database (SDD), Code of Conduct, and trading window closures under the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015. 10. Practical Takeaways for Company Secretaries and Advisors 1. Evaluate the Rule 19(7) Route Early An IPO is not the sole gateway to the bourses; a qualifying demerger can achieve pure-play listing with greater capital efficiency. 2. Model Post-Scheme Shareholding at the Structuring Stage Ensure that public shareholding in the resulting entity comfortably clears the 25% Master Circular threshold after factoring in share entitlement ratios and cross-holdings. 3. Treat Regulatory Milestones Separately NCLT sanction, stock exchange observation letters, SEBI Rule 19(7) relaxation, and final trading approvals are distinct legal checkpoints—plan transaction timelines accordingly. 4. Build Governance Readiness Pre-Listing Secretarial systems, SDD infrastructure, and committee charters must be operational well before the listing date. 11. Conclusion The listing of J.K. Shah Commerce Education Limited illustrates how a well-architected Composite Scheme of Arrangement can do far more than separate business operations—it can create an independent listed enterprise, unlock shareholder value, and establish direct capital market access without a conventional IPO. As Indian corporate groups increasingly look to unlock value across specialized verticals, the interplay of Sections 230–232 of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule 19(7) of the SCRR will remain one of the most effective tools in the corporate restructuring playbook.
