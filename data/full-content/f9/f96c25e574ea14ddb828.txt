Glass Emporium (GSTAT Agra) Summary: The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), State Bench Agra, held that transportation of goods in an entirely different vehicle from the one declared in the e-way bill, without updating Part-B in accordance with Rule 138(5) of the CGST Rules, 2017, could not be treated as a minor technical discrepancy where the taxpayer failed to substantiate its explanation regarding vehicle breakdown. The Tribunal allowed the Department’s appeal and restored the penalty of Rs.6,38,046 imposed under Section 129(3) of the UPGST Act, 2017, against S.R. Glass Emporium. On 17 June 2023, the Mobile Squad, Firozabad, intercepted a goods vehicle bearing registration number DL-01 MA-7905 at Tundla Toll Plaza, District Agra. The vehicle was carrying fancy lights, LED lights and other electrical equipment dispatched by M/s S.R. LED Lights, New Delhi, to M/s S.R. Glass Emporium, Firozabad. The consignment was accompanied by a tax invoice, e-way bill and transport bilty dated 16 June 2023. However, the e-way bill mentioned vehicle registration number DL-01 MA-9513, which was entirely different from the registration number of the intercepted vehicle. The proper officer initiated proceedings under Section 129 of the CGST/UPGST Act read with Rule 138 of the CGST Rules. The taxpayer contended that the original vehicle had broken down during transit at night and that the transporter had arranged another vehicle to ensure timely delivery of the goods. According to the taxpayer, the failure to update the vehicle particulars in the e-way bill arose from a bona fide mistake. It was also submitted that no discrepancy had been found in the goods being transported. The Department maintained that notwithstanding the absence of discrepancies in the goods, transportation in an entirely different vehicle without updating the e-way bill constituted a violation of the statutory requirements. The proper officer rejected the taxpayer’s explanation and imposed a penalty of Rs.6,38,046 under Section 129(3). The taxpayer’s appeal under Section 107 was subsequently allowed by the First Appellate Authority through an order dated 14 October 2024, setting aside the original order and consequential penalty. Aggrieved by this decision, the Department preferred a second appeal before the Tribunal under Section 112(1). Before the Tribunal, the Department argued that a complete mismatch between the vehicle declared in the e-way bill and the vehicle actually transporting the goods affected the identity, traceability and legality of the movement of goods. It contended that compliance with Rule 138 was mandatory and that statutory penalties could be imposed irrespective of the existence of an intention to evade tax. The taxpayer, on the other hand, relied upon the Allahabad High Court decision in Rakesh Plastic Furniture and Crockery Emporium, Aliganj Vs State of U.P. and Two Others, wherein penalty proceedings arising from transportation in a different vehicle had been quashed in the absence of intention to evade tax. The Department relied upon the three-judge Bench decision of the Supreme Court in Union of India Vs Dharmendra Textile Processors, concerning the nature of statutory penalties under fiscal legislation. The Tribunal referred to the principle that mens rea was not an essential ingredient for attracting civil penalties unless expressly incorporated in the statutory provision. Considering the relevant fiscal provisions to be pari materia, the Tribunal expressed the view that the ratio of the Supreme Court judgment was applicable to the present dispute. Examining the taxpayer’s explanation regarding the alleged vehicle breakdown, the Tribunal noted that the driver had furnished an affidavit dated 6 September 2023 during adjudication proceedings, whereas the vehicle had been intercepted on 17 June 2023. No contemporaneous intimation regarding the alleged breakdown or transshipment had been made on the common portal. The Department argued that the explanation was an afterthought and that no supporting evidence, such as repair bills, toll receipts, FASTag deductions or breakdown assistance records, had been produced. The Tribunal observed that Rule 138(5) expressly mandated updating the particulars of the new vehicle in Part-B of the e-way bill where goods were transferred from one vehicle to another. The requirement was intended to prevent misuse of a single e-way bill for transporting multiple consignments through changes of vehicles during transit. The Tribunal considered compliance with this requirement mandatory and distinguished the complete mismatch of vehicle numbers in the present case from minor typographical errors or other established bona fide mistakes. The Tribunal found that neither the driver nor the taxpayer had contemporaneously raised the plea of vehicle breakdown and that the driver’s affidavit had surfaced only at a later stage of adjudication. In the absence of corroborative documentary evidence, the alleged breakdown could not be accepted merely on the basis of the belated affidavit. The Tribunal observed that acceptance of unsupported explanations raised months after interception would undermine the statutory monitoring mechanism governing Part-B of e-way bills. The taxpayer also relied upon CBIC Circular No.64/38/2018-GST dated 14 September 2018, providing relaxation in respect of specified minor discrepancies in documents accompanying goods in movement. The Tribunal held that the circular did not assist the taxpayer because the case involved an entirely different vehicle and not merely a minor error in recording its registration number. Consequently, the Tribunal held that the First Appellate Authority had erred in accepting the belated affidavit without examining the absence of corroborative evidence and in extending the benefit of doubt despite violation of Rule 138(5). The Department’s second appeal was allowed, the appellate order dated 14 October 2024 was set aside, and the original order imposing penalty of Rs.6,38,046 was restored. Union of India Vs Dharmendra Textile Processors (Supreme Court); (2008) 13 SCC 369; (2008) 306 ITR 277. FULL TEXT OF THE GST APPELLATE TRIBUNAL ORDER 1. Feeling aggrieved by the order dated- 14.10.2024, passed by Sh. Rakesh Verma, Additional Commissioner, Gr. II, Appeal in second appeal no. AD090923074755H/2024, whereby penalty a sum of Rs. 6,38,046/- imposed by proper officer u/s 129(3) of UPGST has been set aside, this second appeal u/s 112(1) of CGST/UPGST, Act, 2017 has been preferred by state tax department. For convenience hereinafter in the order appellant and respondent will be addressed as department and taxpayer, respectively. 2. Facts giving rise to this appeal, and shorn of unnecessary details, are that on 17.06.2023 at around 07:30 A.M, mobile squad Firozabad, headed by Assistant Commissioner had stopped and intercepted the goods vehicle DL 01 MA 7905, at Tundla, toll plaza, with in the limit of District, Agra. Driver of the vehicle revealed that goods pertaining to Fancy lights, led lights and other electric equipments were dispatched by M/s S.R led lights, tilak Nagar, New Delhi, GSTIN 07AEJFS6815E1ZW to another firm M/s S.R Glass emporium, Suhag Nagar, Firozabad, U.P, with GSTIN 09AHEPG6383K1ZY, covered with invoice no. 182 dated 16.06.2023 and e-way bill no. 721346563102 dated- 16.06.2023. As per documents possessed by the driver, Ludhiana Srinagar Transport company had issued bilty no. 10329 dated 16.06.2023. As per further case of the department, goods were being transported with an e-way bill of Vehicle no. DL-01 MA- 9513, while actual vehicle which was stopped and intercepted was bearing registration no. DL-01 MA-7905. In this way, vehicle number was entirely different from the vehicle mentioned in e-way bill. Learned intercepting officer arrived at the conclusion that the vehicle in which goods were being transported at the relevant time was entirely different form the vehicle declared in e-way bill and issued SCN for imposing penalty on the taxpayer u/s 129 of CGST/UPGST, Act read with rule 138 of CGST, Act, 2017. 3. Taxpayer has taken the stand that there was a transit break down in declared vehicle on the way in the night. In order to supply the goods within the stipulated time on an emergency call made by driver transporter managed another vehicle and goods were shifted to that arranged one. As per further version of the taxpayer due to bonafide mistake e-way bill could not be updated regarding shifting of the goods to different vehicle from declared one in e-way bill. It has further contended by the taxpayer that otherwise goods were found in order and there was no discrepancy was found. 4. Department’s version is only that even there was no discrepancy in goods transported, there was a clear violation of section 129(1) of CGST/UPGST, Act, coupled with rule 138 of CGST Rules 2017 which attracts provision enunciated in section 129(3). Learned proper officer by rejecting the submissions made by taxpayer passed order in original resulted imposition of penalty on taxpayer. 5. Taxpayer challenged the order in original before first appellate authority via an appeal preferred u/s 107 of CGST/UPGTS, Act. After hearing both sides, learned first appellate authority has allowed the appeal and set aside the order in original as well as consequential demand of penalty. 6. Being aggrieved by the order in appeal passed by first appellate authority, department has come before this tribunal preferring the second appeal u/s 112(1) of CGST/UPGST, Act. Learned departmental representative appearing for state tax department has submitted that order under appeal is not sustainable in law as first appellate authority has passed the same without proper appreciation of the facts and circumstances of the case and settled legal position. On the above, he submitted that appeal be allowed and by setting aside the order in appeal, order in original be restored. 7. Per contra, learned authorised representative for taxpayer has supported the findings recorded by first appellate authority and submitted for dismissal of appeal. 8. We have heard the submissions made by learned Authorised representatives for respective parties and perused the record carefully. Point for determination may be formulated as under – Whether transporting the goods in a vehicle which is entirely different form the declared vehicle in e-way bill is a substantial breach of rule 138 of CGST rules 2017, which attracts detention and seizure of goods and imposing penalty on taxpayer concerned? FINDINGS WITH REASON- 9. Learned departmental representative has submitted that complete mismatch of vehicle number from the vehicle declared in e-way bill vis-à-vis the actual vehicle used for transportation is a discrepancy directly affects the identity, transability and legality of movement of goods under GST regime. He further submitted that compliance of rule 138 is mandatory in nature and non-compliance thereof attracts penalty u/s 129 irrespective of intent to evade tax from taxpayer. 10. It is not in dispute that as per entries of invoice concerned, e-way bill and consignment note the goods were dispatched from S.R Led lights, Tilak Nagar Market, New Delhi to S.R Glass emporium, Suhagnagar, U.P, through vehicle DL 01 MA 9513 on 16.06.2023 and when vehicle was intercepted on 17.06.2023 in the early morning it was entirely different bearing no. DL 01 MA 7905 thus, bone of contention in the matter as to whether due to breakdown in the vehicle declared the goods were shifted to another vehicle on the way and accordingly e-way bill was not updated, inadvertently or there was any intention to evade tax therein on the part of taxpayer? 11. Learned authorised representative of taxpayer has cited a case law Rakesh Plastic Furniture and crockery emporium, Aliganj vs State of U.P and two others reported at 2025(88) Taxlok.com 011(Allahabad), wherein Hon’ble Allahabad High court has quashed the penalty proceedings launched u/s 129(3) of CGST, Act, where goods were transported in a different vehicle then mention in the e-way bill. Hon’ble High Court has observed that if there is no intention to evade tax on the part of taxpayer, mere technical lapse of not updating the vehicle number by the transporter could not justify the penalty on taxpayer. 12. Learned departmental representative sh. Mukesh Kumar too, has also cited ratio of Hon’ble Supreme Court of India titled Union of India vs Dharmendra textile processors (Supra), in which by answering a reference a three-judge bench of Hon’ble Supreme Court established foundational principles regarding statutory penalty provisions in fiscal legislation. It was held by Hon’ble Apex court that statutory penalties imposed for infractions under Revenue statues (such as section 11AC of Central Excise Act, 1944 or Section 271(1) (c) of Income tax Act, 1961 constitutes civil liability meant to remedy the loss of Revenue. It is further held that mens rea is not an essential element for attracting civil penalties under fiscal statues unless specifically incorporated in to the text of the provision. It is also held by Hon’ble Apex court that courts cannot supply words or read mens rea in to a tax provision where the statue is clear and unambiguous and court must give effect to the plain language of the statues as written by the legislation. Though, the case Dharmendra (Supra) had arisen out of the provisions of Central excise Act, 1944 and case in hand has arisen out of the provisions of Central goods and services Act, 2017 and similarly enacted UP Goods and Services Tax 2017 but in both the fiscal laws the relevant provisions in respect of evasion of tax are in pari materia and thus in our considered opinion ratio laid down by Hon’ble Supreme Court in Dharmendra (Supra) would directly be applicable to the case at hand. 13. Defense of taxpayer before adjudicating officer was that due to midway break down in the vehicle goods were shifted to another vehicle which was intercepted later. In this regard, driver of the vehicle has submitted his affidavit dated- 06.09.2023, before adjudicating authority first in time and no prior or contemporaneous intimation of any vehicle break down or transshipment was made on the common portal under rule 138(5) of CGST rules 2017. Learned DR has contended that compliance with rule 138(5) is mandatory before shifting goods to another vehicle and in this matter broke down defense is a clear after thought fabricated after three months, unsupported by any contemporaneous evidence, such as repair bills, toll receipts or fast tag deduction of the first vehicle or break down assistance record was not produced. 14. Rule 138(5) CGST rules, 2017 explicitly mandates that where goods are transferred from one vehicle to another, the consigner, recipient or transporter shall, before such transfer and further movement of goods update the details of new vehicle in part B of e-way bill on common portal. The language of the rule is preemptory, designed specifically to prevent the mischief of multiple consignments being transported under the cover of a single e-way bill by merely switching vehicles midnight transit. 15. In the present matter at the time of interception on 17.06.2023 at around 07:30 A.M and within three months neither the driver nor the taxpayer raised any plea of vehicle breakdown, the self-serving affidavit of the driver surfaced three month later in adjudicating process. The taxpayer failed to place on record any corroborative documentary evidence to substantiate a genuine break down theory. While judicial precedents grant relief in cases of minor typographical errors in vehicle numbers and other bonafide mistakes, the present case involves an entirely different vehicle without any contemporaneous explanation or portal updation. If unverified claims of vehicle breakdown raised months after interception are accepted, the entire monitoring mechanism of part B of the e-way bill would be rendered otiose and unenforceable. 16. Taxpayer has also taken a defense of CBIC circular no. 64/38/2018-GST date 14.09.2018, which only applies to specified minor errors in vehicle number mismatch while admittedly case in hand based on transit shifting of goods to another vehicle due to breakdown in earlier vehicle which leave the place of origin of goods. In this way, above circular would not help the taxpayer in any way. 17. Thus, in view of above discussion of facts and legal position the first appellate authority clearly errored in accepting the belated affidavit at its face value without scrutiniging the complete absence of corroborative evidence and in extending benefit of doubt where the statutory mandate of rule 138(5) was blatantly violated. 18. Consequently, this second appeal filed by department has substance and deserves to be allowed. Therefore, appeal is allowed, order under appeal dated – 14.10.2024 passed by first appellate authority is set aside. Consequently, order in original passed by proper officer is restored. 19. There is no pending interim application in the appeal. 20. Order is pronounced in open court on this day of 19th of November, 2026. 21. Copy of order be communicated to all concerned, as per rules.
