Articles 2 min read Commission caps may fuel premium inflation, reverse savings for policyholders: Thomas Devasia Former IRDAI member Thomas Devasia argues that reducing distributor payouts alone will not address the underlying cost structures in insurance, and that savings could eventually be recovered from customers through higher premiums. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Oct 15, 2026 at 06:13 PM IST var news_msid = 134968373; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India’s (IRDAI) proposal to recalibrate insurance commissions could fail to deliver lasting affordability for policyholders, with premium increases potentially offsetting the savings from lower distributor payouts, Thomas Devasia, Former Member (Non-Life), IRDAI said in an interview with ETBFSI.“The cap on commissions and thereby any reduction in the cost to the customer is a transient advantage which will get pulled back from the broad customer base via premium inflation. We have seen that slowly play out in the context of GST withdrawal in individual life and health insurance contracts,” he said. Advt Devasia argued that the regulator’s approach risks addressing distribution costs without adequately examining how insurers price products, allocate expenses and compete for business.“The entire set of proposals need a full rethink — open forums and inputs of competent professionals not representing particular interest are the better way forward,” he said.Enforcement, not just commission caps, is the underlying issueDevasia said the present situation reflects gaps in the implementation of existing regulations, including the expense-of-management framework and product-pricing rules. He argued that the regulator should first ensure that existing requirements are followed in substance rather than introducing a sweeping restructuring of distribution economics.He also questioned whether the proposed expense-of-management (EoM) reductions adequately account for differences between insurers. Companies relying heavily on intermediaries have a different cost structure from those with substantial in-house operations, digital distribution or direct sales teams.“Firstly, insurers’ EoM are not comparable. An insurer with substantial in-house operations, digital or direct sales team, with minimal outsourcing to intermediaries, is not comparable with another dependent totally on intermediaries,” Devasia said.He added that insurers catering to smaller-ticket customers in Tier-2, Tier-3 and rural markets could naturally have higher expense ratios than companies focused on large-ticket business. Advt The consultation paper proposes reducing EoM limits for life insurers to 12.5% of premium and for general insurers to 20% over five years. Devasia said he could not assess whether the proposed glide path was appropriate because the underlying operating profiles and supporting information had not been shared.Customers ultimately bear the cost of distributionDevasia argued that every rupee spent across the insurance system is ultimately borne by customers, whether through premiums or other product economics. He questioned the commercial incentive for insurers to expand into low-ticket segments if the economics of acquiring and servicing those customers become less attractive. Advt “Till date, the cost willingly offered to intermediaries by insurers for acquiring bulk business is compensated by the retail segment. Insurance penetration will be helped by digital networks if consumers are supported by distribution entities,” he said.He added that the regulator should assess the economics of distribution alongside insurers’ broader business strategies, rather than assuming that lower commissions would automatically improve affordability.The consultation paper, released on September 23, proposes product- and channel-specific commission limits, stricter disclosure requirements, cost audits and stronger safeguards against mis-selling. The proposals remain under consultation, with stakeholder comments due by October 25.
