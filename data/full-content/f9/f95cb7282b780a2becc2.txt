Articles 4 min read Bajaj Finance to launch new term-loan variants from Nov 1 as RBI weighs revolving credit ban Since the RBI proposed a ban on revolving credit by NBFCs in its August 6 draft circular, Bajaj Finance, with an estimated 15 per cent of consolidated AUM in such products, is preparing new term-loan variants from November 1. A complete ban would have a transient impact on AUM and marginally compress NIM and profitability, Vice Chairman & MD Rajeev Jain said. Advt “It's a draft, as you are aware, comments have been submitted by everybody. The product that we hope that RBI would find a middle path which benefits, which creates sustainability on one hand, protects customer centricity on the other and helps innovation from a way forward standpoint for non-banks,” Jain said.Jain said the impact on Bajaj Finance would depend on the final regulatory framework. If the RBI were to impose a complete ban, he said the company could see a temporary impact on AUM and marginal pressure on NIM and profitability.“Let's assume for a moment that RBI decides that it is a complete ban. There will be transient impact on AUM and a marginal compression in NIM and profitability. We will abide by what RBI has to state and work along,” he said.Bajaj Finance is also preparing changes to its product structure ahead of the final decision.“We are already launching a set of different variants of term loans on 1st of November which do not allow, of course, withdrawal but have some more attractive features to ensure that we can find a middle path in the term loan structure,” Jain said.“Both in short, we would like to act as a firm rather than wait. Least it does is it creates greater diversification and resilience; most it does is augments growth. Either way, I would say we are in a better place than just probably wait,” he added.Bajaj Finance has not disclosed the proportion of its AUM that could fall within the proposed definition of revolving credit. IIFL Capital, however, estimates that such products account for around 15 per cent of the lender’s consolidated AUM and around 20 per cent of its standalone AUM. Advt The move follows the RBI’s August 6 draft amendments to its directions governing credit facilities extended by NBFCs. The draft proposes that NBFCs should offer only credit products in the nature of term loans and should not offer revolving credit products. The proposed restriction, however, would not apply to NBFCs authorised by the RBI to issue credit cards.Under the draft, a term loan would have a fixed principal amount, with the sanctioned amount disbursed in one or more installments and repaid according to a predetermined schedule. Once the principal is repaid, either partly or fully, the sanctioned limit cannot be restored or replenished. Bullet repayment structures would continue to be permitted. The RBI had invited comments on the draft until August 28 and proposed that the amendments would come into force immediately once notified. Advt The RBI is still examining its proposed restrictions on revolving credit after receiving extensive industry feedback. Deputy Governor S C Murmu had said earlier that the central bank was assessing the risks at both individual NBFC and systemic levels before taking a final call.The proposal had an immediate impact on NBFC stocks. Bajaj Finance shares fell as much as 5.55 per cent intraday on August 7 to Rs 1,086, from the previous close of Rs 1,149.90.Bajaj Finance currently expects AUM growth of 26–27 per cent in FY27, while its medium-term guidance for profit growth remains at 23–25 per cent.
