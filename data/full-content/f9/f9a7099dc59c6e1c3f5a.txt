LIC held 4.35% of ICICI Bank as of June 2026, while insurance companies collectively held 8.24% and mutual funds 29.60%. The approval is enabling and does not mean LIC will immediately raise its holding to 9.99%. Advt The development follows RBI approval for LIC to raise its stake in HDFC Bank to up to 9.99%. LIC held 4.11% of HDFC Bank as of August 14. Its holdings in several other banks were already close to the regulatory ceiling, including 8.83% in Punjab National Bank, 8.47% in State Bank of India, 7.87% in Axis Bank and 7.83% in Bank of India as of August 20, according to Prime Database data cited in reports.Expanding roleThe recent approvals also come against a wider increase in the ability of financial institutions to hold sizeable stakes in banks. ICICI Prudential Asset Management Company received separate RBI approvals on September 8 to acquire up to 9.95% in Kotak Mahindra Bank, CSB Bank, DCB Bank and AU Small Finance Bank. The approvals give the asset manager one year to complete the acquisitions.The ICICI Prudential AMC approvals, however, should not be interpreted as a move towards control of the lenders. The proposed holdings are portfolio investments through schemes and funds managed by the asset manager, and the investments are described as passive rather than aimed at influencing bank management.The significance of the recent approvals therefore lies in the growing scope for large domestic financial institutions to build substantial, but regulated, positions in banks. Under the RBI’s shareholding framework, investors seeking to acquire 5% or more of a bank require prior approval, while the prescribed ceiling for non-promoter shareholders is generally 10%, subject to the applicable regulatory framework. Advt For LIC, the approvals provide additional investment flexibility across the banking sector rather than giving it management control over the lenders. Any increase in its ICICI Bank holding will depend on its investment decision and compliance with applicable regulations.The emerging pattern is therefore less about insurers becoming strategic owners of private banks and more about large domestic financial institutions receiving regulatory clearance to approach the upper end of permitted bank shareholding. LIC’s approvals for ICICI Bank and HDFC Bank, alongside ICICI Prudential AMC’s approvals across four other lenders, show how institutional ownership can increasingly sit close to the regulatory threshold without crossing into promoter or controlling ownership.
