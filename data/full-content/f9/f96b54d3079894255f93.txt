Articles 2 min read No interim relief for Tata Trusts: Tata Sons board meeting to be held as scheduled, says HC The Bombay High Court has denied interim relief to Tata Trusts regarding the upcoming Tata Sons board meeting, scheduled for November 30. The court affirmed that this meeting will occur as planned. Tata Trusts are contesting the reappointment of N Chandrasekaran as chairperson, citing the need for approval from their nominee directors, highlighting the ongoing power struggle within Tata Sons. Let the meeting go on as scheduled. and everything will be subject to further order," the court observed.During Friday's hearing, senior advocate Harish Salve, appearing for Tata Sons, opposed the request to defer the meeting, saying the company had to take a decision in view of a Reserve Bank of India directive. The RBI has set December 12 as the deadline for Tata Sons to inform it of its decision on complying with the central bank's listing requirements. Advt Salve argued that any resolution passed at the board meeting would remain subject to the high court's orders. However, senior advocate Abhishek Manu Singhvi, representing the SDTT, contended that the RBI directive was being used by Tata Sons to create a sense of urgency and push through the meeting.The trusts had sought a stay or postponement of the November 30 meeting. They also requested an order restraining Chandrasekaran from exercising his casting vote as executive chairman if there was a split among the nominee directors on the board.The court directed Tata Sons and the other respondents, including six of its directors, to file reply affidavits to the two suits and scheduled the next hearing for January 18, 2027.The Sir Dorabji Tata Trust (SDTT) and the RD Tata Trust (RDTT), which are among the seven charitable trusts controlling the Tata group, filed the petitions challenging Chandrasekaran's reappointment. The SDTT has sought Rs 50 crore in damages, while the RDTT has claimed Rs 15 crore, both with 18 per cent annual interest.The trusts have argued that the September 17 board resolution reappointing Chandrasekaran was invalid as it did not secure the affirmative votes required from their nominee directors under Article 121 of Tata Sons' Articles of Association.Tata Trusts, the largest shareholder in Tata Sons, has maintained that the reappointment required the approval of both its nominee directors. One of them is Trusts chairman Noel Tata. Advt Tata Sons, which owns prominent businesses and brands including Jaguar Land Rover and Tetley Tea, reappointed Chandrasekaran in September despite opposition from Noel Tata. The dispute has now moved to the courts, with the two trusts questioning the validity of the decision.
