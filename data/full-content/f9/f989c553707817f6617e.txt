Summary: A compliance calendar helps businesses, professionals and tax advisers track recurring and event-based obligations under income-tax, GST, company law and other applicable regulations. For FY 2026-27, an effective calendar should identify the relevant taxpayer or entity, statutory provision, return or form, reporting period, original due date, applicable extension, responsible person and evidence of completion. A general calendar cannot establish that every listed obligation applies to every business. GST filing frequency, TDS obligations, audit requirements and MCA forms depend on registration, turnover, entity status and the nature of transactions. Statutory deadlines must be distinguished from conditional relaxations, official extensions and portal advisories. Tax teams should reconcile underlying ledgers, obtain approvals, complete payment and filing, and retain acknowledgements and challans. This article explains how to create a reliable compliance calendar, monitor changes, control filing risks and document completion without relying on unverified dates. A missed tax payment or return may trigger interest, late fees, penalties, notices or restrictions on particular claims, depending on the governing provision. Different filings may also have different tax periods and due dates even when they are prepared from the same accounting records. Identify Applicable Compliance Obligations Before Setting Dates Income-Tax Returns, Audit and Advance Tax Determine the assessee category, applicable tax regime, relevant tax year and whether audit, transfer-pricing reporting, advance-tax instalments or other statements are required. The due date for one category should not automatically be applied to another. Confirm the applicable provisions and subsequent official extensions before publishing or using a due date. TDS and TCS Payments and Statements Map deductions or collections by transaction type, provision, payment period and statement form. Assign separate checks for deposit of tax, periodic statements, correction statements and certificates. Reconcile deductions with the ledger, challans and reported transactions. GST Returns, Payments and Reconciliations Identify each GST registration and its applicable filing arrangement. Track relevant GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, QRMP, composition, annual-return and reconciliation obligations only where applicable. Verify turnover thresholds, notifications, state-specific changes and GST portal advisories. Reconcile outward supplies, input tax credit, electronic ledgers and payment records before filing. MCA and Companies Act Compliance For companies and LLPs, map annual filings, financial-statement submissions, returns, event-based forms and governance obligations according to the entity’s status. Refer to the applicable law and current MCA filing guidance. A portal facility or FAQ should not be treated as an amendment to a statutory deadline unless supported by the competent authority. Other Applicable Regulatory Filings Depending on the business, separate calendars may be required for PF, ESI, professional tax, FEMA/RBI, SEBI and sector-specific requirements. State-based and event-triggered obligations should not be inserted as universally applicable dates. Identify the exact category of taxpayers, forms, reporting periods and jurisdictions covered by the extension. Do not assume that an extension for a return automatically extends the associated payment or audit obligation. Check Primary Sources and Effective Dates Before finalising a calendar, check the relevant income-tax, GST, MCA or other regulator’s official publications and portals. Preserve a dated copy of the notification or circular relied upon. A news report or portal banner may flag a change but should not replace the governing legal instrument. Monitor Technical Problems Separately Where a filing portal is unavailable, preserve screenshots, timestamps, correspondence and grievance ticket records. Technical difficulty does not itself establish an extension or immunity from consequences. Month-End and Quarter-End Compliance Controls Reconcile Books and Return Data Match sales, purchases, tax deductions, credits and liabilities to the underlying books. Identify unrecorded transactions, mismatches and adjustments before return preparation. Separate Preparation, Review and Authorisation Assign a preparer and independent reviewer, particularly for high-value or time-sensitive filings. Confirm that the person filing has valid credentials, authority and any required digital signature certificate. Verify Submission and Payment A prepared return, uploaded form or initiated payment is not necessarily a completed compliance. Check the final submission status, tax debit or challan confirmation and acknowledgement number. Common Compliance Calendar Errors and Their Consequences Applying a due date to an entity or taxpayer category to which it does not apply. Confusing the due date for a return with the deadline for paying tax. Using an extension without checking its precise scope or effective date. Failing to monitor changes affecting state-specific or event-based obligations. Assuming a successful upload proves final submission or acceptance. Omitting reconciliations and approvals to meet a filing deadline. Failing to retain challans, acknowledgements and supporting calculations. Interest, late fees, penalties and other consequences depend on the specific law, nature of default and applicable relief. They should be evaluated obligation by obligation rather than described as a single universal penalty. Illustrative Compliance Tracking Example Assume a company has monthly GST returns, periodic TDS statements and annual MCA filings. Its calendar should contain separate entries for each obligation, with the relevant period, applicable due date, assigned preparer and reviewer, and documentary evidence. If an official notification changes one filing deadline, update only the affected entries after confirming the notification’s scope. Preserve the original deadline and change history. This illustration explains the control process; it does not establish any statutory filing date. Records and Audit Trail to Preserve Maintain supporting ledgers, reconciliations, calculations, approval emails, payment challans, return acknowledgements, filing references, digital-signature records, extension notifications and portal correspondence. The file should allow an independent reviewer to reconstruct the position as of the filing date. For related control practices, see audit-trail guidance and documentation for financial and assurance reviews. Practical Compliance Calendar Checklist Confirm the legal entity, registrations and applicable compliance categories. Identify the relevant financial year, tax year and reporting period. Verify each due date from current authoritative sources. Record original dates and extensions separately. Allocate responsibility for preparation, review and filing. Set internal cut-offs earlier than statutory deadlines. Reconcile books, returns, tax payments and supporting statements. Check digital-signature validity and filing permissions. Save challans, acknowledgements and other proof of completion. Review pending items and official date changes regularly. Frequently Asked Questions on the FY 2026-27 Compliance Calendar 1. Is a published compliance calendar legally binding? No. The relevant statute, rules and valid notifications determine legal obligations and due dates. A published calendar is only a reference tool. 2. Do all businesses have the same GST filing deadlines? No. Filing requirements and timelines depend on the taxpayer’s registration, scheme, filing frequency and applicable notifications. 3. Does an income-tax return extension automatically extend tax-payment deadlines? Not necessarily. Each extension must be examined for the particular obligation and taxpayer category covered. 4. How should a company track MCA filings? Maintain an entity-specific schedule covering annual and event-based forms, relevant approvals, filing status and acknowledgements. 5. What if a government portal fails near the deadline? Record the error and raise a grievance promptly. Do not assume that the failure automatically changes the statutory deadline. 6. What is the strongest evidence that a return was filed? The relevant portal-generated acknowledgement or accepted filing status, supported by payment confirmation and the underlying records. 7. How often should a compliance calendar be reviewed? Review it regularly and whenever a relevant notification, transaction, registration change or regulatory development occurs. Key Takeaway A reliable FY 2026-27 compliance calendar is entity-specific, source-verified and evidence-driven. Its value lies not merely in displaying dates but in connecting each obligation to the governing law, responsible personnel, reconciled information and proof of timely completion.
