RBI/2026-27/243, substituted 31 August 2026 for 30 September 2026. As at 13 October 2026, that temporary relaxation has ended. The current deposit quotation therefore needs to be tested against the restored applicable ceiling and bank policy, rather than using a promotional June–August rate as if it remains available. See also TaxGuru: RBI commercial-bank amendment of 25 August 2026. The normal ceiling framework for FCNR(B) rates depends on the relevant currency reference rate or swap benchmark plus the prescribed spread, differentiated by tenor. The published directions identify one year to less than three years and three to five years as separate categories. The precise ceiling can vary by currency and month because the benchmark changes. This article deliberately does not quote an unverified “current highest FCNR rate”: a bank-specific rate and the applicable benchmark date are necessary to calculate a valid comparison. RBI FCNR(B) Swap Facility Is a Bank-Level Arrangement RBI introduced a 2026 USD/INR swap facility for qualifying fresh FCNR(B) deposits raised by authorised dealer Category-I banks. This is a liquidity and foreign-currency risk-management arrangement between eligible banks and RBI; it does not make the retail depositor a party to an RBI derivative. Public RBI material and TaxGuru’s reproduction of the June 8 circular describe the framework, including qualifying deposit tenors and swap tenor matching. The facility should not be marketed as a guaranteed extra coupon payable to every NRI. See also TaxGuru: RBI FCNR(B) swap facility circular. The depositor’s contractual rights are governed by the deposit receipt, currency, interest rate, maturity, early-withdrawal and repayment instructions. Even if the bank has accessed a central-bank swap arrangement, the depositor should not assume a guaranteed exchange rate for converting the maturity proceeds into Indian rupees. Any personal hedge or currency conversion is a separate transaction under bank and FEMA rules. Interest Calculation, Renewal and Premature Withdrawal Interest and compounding RBI’s FCNR(B) deposit framework uses a 360-day year for calculating interest. Interest is calculated and paid at 180-day intervals and for the remaining actual days, with the option to receive it on maturity with compounding. Consequently, the quoted annual rate, payout election and actual days affect maturity proceeds. Banks may present annualised yields differently; the receipt and calculation statement are the authoritative contract evidence. Renewal after maturity RBI directions address overdue renewal and the applicable interest for the interval between original maturity and renewal. In the established framework, renewal within a short prescribed window has special interest treatment, while longer delays can be treated as a fresh deposit for overdue-period purposes. The customer should not assume automatic continuation of a temporary 2026 ceiling-relaxation rate after the eligible period. On maturity, verify currency of credit, renewal tenor, withholding declaration and whether the bank has applied the correct current quote. Early withdrawal and interest loss RBI requires banks to permit premature withdrawal on request under the scheme, subject to its rules. Where the deposit is withdrawn before the minimum one-year period, no interest is payable under the relevant deposit-interest framework. Bank policy may also impose a disclosed penalty or recover swap cost in applicable cases. A depositor needing liquidity within one year should distinguish return of principal in the deposit currency from any INR conversion loss caused by exchange-rate movements. The Income-tax Act, 2025 carries forward the corresponding qualifying exemption framework from 1 April 2026. The important test is the depositor’s income-tax residential status for the relevant tax year, the bank and deposit type, and compliance with the statutory conditions; an FCNR(B) label alone is insufficient. A person who returns to India for permanent settlement may be permitted under RBI rules to retain the FCNR(B) deposit to maturity at the contracted interest rate, subject to the bank’s conditions. That FEMA/banking permission is not a perpetual income-tax exemption. Once the depositor becomes resident and ordinarily resident for income-tax purposes, the tax treatment of subsequent interest may change. Residency under FEMA and residency under the Income-tax Act are separate tests and may change on different dates. Interest may also be taxable in the depositor’s overseas country of tax residence, depending on local law and treaty relief. Any Indian exemption does not itself establish an exemption abroad. Documentary evidence should include overseas tax residency, account opening, interest certificate, bank withholding statement and the year-wise residency computation. FEMA Funding, Repatriation, Nomination and DICGC FCNR(B) funding is ordinarily through inward remittance via normal banking channels or transfer from an eligible NRE/FCNR account. Repatriation of eligible principal and interest is generally permitted under the scheme. Conversion from one permitted currency to another can create a spread or conversion cost and should not be treated as an investment return. Banks may permit loans against eligible deposits subject to RBI conditions, margins and prohibited end uses. Joint operation and nomination follow the applicable banking and FEMA rules. Deposit insurance through DICGC generally applies to eligible deposits with insured banks subject to the statutory aggregate cap of ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity, including principal and accrued interest. A foreign-currency deposit is not fully protected simply because it is denominated in USD or split into multiple receipts. The insured amount is subject to statutory rules and conversion methodology, and amounts above the limit carry bank-credit exposure. Currency Risk: Deposit Currency Is Not the Spending Currency A USD deposit can preserve a nominal USD principal while its INR value changes. For illustration, a USD 10,000 principal equals ₹9,00,000 at ₹90/USD and ₹9,50,000 at ₹95/USD before spreads and charges. This ₹50,000 difference is a currency translation effect, not an extra USD interest payment. Conversely, a person whose future expense is in euros may face USD/EUR risk even without converting into INR. No exchange-rate movement is assumed or forecast by this illustration. FCNR(B) differs from currency futures or options. A bank deposit does not generally require the depositor to post daily mark-to-market margin, while an exchange-traded derivative involves contract specifications, margin, settlement and separate regulatory permissions. Residents seeking to trade currencies must use routes authorised under FEMA and RBI; an FCNR(B) account is not a substitute for exchange-traded derivatives compliance. Accounting, Reporting and Audit Considerations Where an Indian reporting entity has a permitted foreign-currency monetary deposit or related receivable, AS 11 or Ind AS 21 generally governs initial recognition at the transaction-date exchange rate and subsequent translation of monetary items at the closing rate, subject to applicable exceptions. Interest accrual follows the entity’s financial-reporting framework and contract terms. A bank deposit and an associated hedge are separate financial instruments; hedge accounting under Ind AS 109 requires eligibility and formal documentation rather than being automatic. For individuals, Indian tax return foreign-asset reporting depends on residency, ownership and the scope of the applicable Schedule FA. An FCNR(B) account with an Indian bank is not automatically a foreign bank account merely because it is denominated in a foreign currency. However, offshore bank accounts, securities and other overseas assets may trigger separate disclosures for residents required to file Schedule FA.. CRS/FATCA reporting and overseas tax compliance also warrant review. NRIs should preserve documents establishing the source and repatriability of funds.
