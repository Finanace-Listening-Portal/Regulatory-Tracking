Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Dr. Agarwal’s Eye Hospital Limited (NCLT Chennai) Summary: The NCLT Chennai, Division Bench I, sanctioned the scheme of amalgamation of Dr. Agarwal’s Eye Hospital Limited, the transferor company, with Dr. Agarwal’s Health Care Limited, the transferee company, under Sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act, 2013. The scheme sought to consolidate the companies’ businesses, improve operational and financial efficiencies, integrate capital allocation, simplify their corporate structure and create shareholder value. Following the first-motion order dated 5 May 2026, shareholder and creditor meetings were held on 2 July 2026. The second-motion petition was filed on 8 July 2026, followed by notices to statutory authorities and newspaper publications. The Tribunal examined the reports of the Regional Director, Registrar of Companies, Income Tax Department and Official Liquidator, together with the petitioners’ responses. Regarding differences between secured-creditor certificates and charges appearing on the MCA portal, the petitioners explained that certain charges arose after the certificate’s cut-off date of 31 December 2025 and that another charge concerned a corporate guarantee rather than a secured borrowing. Taking account of these explanations and undertakings to honour liabilities, the Tribunal considered further directions unnecessary. It also recorded undertakings concerning continuing liability for offences and adjustment of authorised-capital fees. The Income Tax Department did not object to the amalgamation subject to preservation of its statutory rights and the transferee company’s responsibility for existing and future tax liabilities. The transferee undertook to intimate the relevant tax authorities, make necessary PAN and address-database changes, comply with lawful proceedings and refrain from using the amalgamation as a technical weapon to challenge such proceedings. The Tribunal expressly preserved the Department’s liberty to undertake appropriate recovery proceedings in accordance with law. These observations did not grant any exemption from taxes, stamp duty or other statutory charges. The scheme provided for issuance of 23 fully paid equity shares of the transferee company, of ₹1 each, for every two fully paid equity shares of the transferor company, of ₹10 each. The statutory auditors certified the scheme’s accounting treatment as compliant with applicable Indian Accounting Standards; the scheme prescribed the pooling of interest method under Appendix C of Ind AS 103. The Tribunal retained 1 April 2026 as the appointed date. It found the scheme beneficial to the companies and not detrimental to shareholders, while clarifying that sanction would not prevent lawful action for any statutory violation. The operative directions transferred the transferor’s business, assets, debts, liabilities, duties and obligations to the transferee; provided for the prescribed share allotment; continued pending proceedings against or by the transferee; and protected employee continuity without interruption of service. The companies were directed to deliver a certified copy of the order to the Registrar of Companies within thirty days of receipt. The order also provided for dissolution of the transferor without winding up, filing of revised constitutional documents, payment of differential authorised-capital fees where applicable, and payment of ₹35,000 plus applicable GST to the Official Liquidator towards the investigating auditor’s fees. The petition was disposed of subject to these directions.
