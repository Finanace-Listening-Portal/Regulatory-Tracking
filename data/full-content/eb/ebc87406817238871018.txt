A draft red herring prospectus is a disclosure document at a preparatory stage, not evidence that an IPO has opened for applications. As of the 7 November 2026 editorial check, the reviewed official index did not establish a final issue price, lot size, subscription window or listing date for this issuer. Readers should not treat social-media price bands or grey-market premiums as verified issuer announcements. This article is a filing-stage guide rather than an issue-subscription recommendation. The official SEBI filing index is the primary starting point for locating the company’s actual DRHP. Figures and transaction terms must be extracted from that signed document and any later amendments; they are deliberately not supplied here where the underlying figures have not been independently checked. This distinction matters because a DRHP may be revised, supplemented or withdrawn before an offer proceeds. How to Read the Proposed Offer Structure An investor should first identify whether the proposed offer contains fresh shares, an offer for sale by existing shareholders, or both. Fresh issue proceeds generally accrue to the issuer before expenses and can support the objects stated in the prospectus. An offer for sale transfers ownership between selling shareholders and investors, with proceeds generally going to the sellers rather than the company. The economic consequences are materially different even when both components appear in the same IPO. Check the capital structure before and after the proposed issue, promoter holdings, potential dilution, weighted-average acquisition cost disclosures where applicable, outstanding convertible instruments and any pre-IPO placements. Do not infer a proposed fundraising amount from an unverified media estimate. Only a later price-band announcement and final offer documents can establish certain pricing and application details. Business Model and Revenue Quality Checks For a consumer-facing food business, the key diligence questions include whether revenue arises from owned outlets, franchising, licensing, delivery platforms, distribution, or another model actually disclosed in the DRHP. Investors should not assume the issuer operates any particular format merely from its name. Examine segment disclosures, concentration of major customers or aggregators, store or unit economics where provided, seasonality, supplier dependence, geographic concentration and the degree of related-party involvement. Revenue growth alone cannot establish quality. Compare operating cash flow with reported profit, track receivable and inventory movements, and reconcile gross margins to product mix and procurement costs. If the business operates through multiple subsidiaries, understand how consolidated numbers differ from standalone figures. Financial statements should be read with accounting policies, audit qualifications, contingent liabilities and notes, rather than using isolated headline figures. Financial Statements: The Minimum Investor Checklist A credible analysis should review at least the periods presented in the actual DRHP and identify changes in revenue from operations, EBITDA or other clearly defined operating measures, profit after tax, finance costs, net worth, total borrowings and cash generated from operations. Ratios must be calculated using consistently defined numerators and denominators. Negative cash flow during rapid expansion may have a different explanation from negative cash flow caused by persistent operating losses, but neither should be dismissed without evidence. Read the statement of cash flows and working-capital discussion alongside the balance sheet. Check inventory ageing and impairment, lease liabilities, guarantees, statutory dues, tax disputes, related-party balances and capital expenditure commitments. If the document reports pro forma numbers, clearly separate them from audited historical results. Without verified DRHP financial tables, numerical conclusions about the issuer’s profitability or valuation would be speculative. Risk Factors That Deserve Priority The DRHP’s risk-factor chapter should be examined for company-specific operational dependencies, litigation, regulatory permits, brand or intellectual-property issues, supplier concentration, competition, cyber and data risks, key-person dependence, and the use of issue proceeds. These are diligence categories, not claims that Hunger Pangs Limited necessarily faces each of them. The issuer’s own disclosure should determine which are material. Look for changes between the first draft and any later red herring prospectus, especially in litigation, indebtedness, financial performance, promoter arrangements and objects of the issue. Assess the risk of execution delays and cost overruns when proceeds are earmarked for projects. An IPO can be legally compliant and still be unattractive at a particular valuation; regulatory filing is not an endorsement of expected returns. Price Discovery, Allotment and Post-Listing Risk An IPO investment decision requires the final price band, bid lot, category allocations, offer period, basis of allotment and listing arrangements. These should come from the RHP, exchange notices and registrar communications once available. Until then, an investor cannot compute a reliable minimum application amount or offer valuation. Grey-market quotations are unofficial and can be manipulated, illiquid or misleading. Once a final offer is announced, compare the implied valuation with disclosed earnings, cash flow, debt, growth prospects and reasonably comparable listed companies. Account for differences in scale, business mix and accounting policies. Subscription statistics measure demand during the offer, not intrinsic value or guaranteed listing gains. Investors should also consider concentration limits and the possibility of losses after listing. Practical Steps Before Any Application Locate the exact issuer record on SEBI’s public-issues page, download the latest DRHP and check whether a later RHP or prospectus supersedes it. Record the document date and version. Read the objects of the issue, capital structure, promoter details, financial statements, audit reports and material litigation sections. Then verify the actual opening dates, bid lot and price band through the issuer, stock exchanges and registrar once announced. Use only authorised ASBA or UPI application routes supported for the relevant investor category. Never transfer money to a person claiming to guarantee allotment. Retain application acknowledgements and check mandate status. If the offer has not opened, no legitimate subscription action is required merely because a DRHP has appeared on a regulatory website.
