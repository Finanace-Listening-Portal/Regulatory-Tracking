Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Vikram Muradia Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Section 80GGC Deduction Cannot Be Disallowed on Search Findings Omitted From the Show-Cause Notice Delhi ITAT Deletes Disallowance of ₹7,66,005 The Delhi ITAT deleted a disallowance of ₹7,66,005 under section 80GGC of the Income-tax Act, 1961 because the Assessing Officer relied on search findings that had not been put to the assessee in the show-cause notice. The notice questioned the evidence supporting donations to two political parties. The final assessment introduced findings from searches on certain political parties as a basis for denying the deduction. The Tribunal held that the assessee should have been given a reasonable opportunity to respond to that material before it was used against him. Scrutiny of Donations to Two Political Parties The assessee filed his return on 13 September 2024, declaring total income of ₹12,29,160. He claimed a deduction under section 80GGC for donations of ₹3,81,003 to Aam Janta Party and ₹3,85,002 to Prabal Bharat Party. The return was selected for scrutiny because of the size of the deduction claimed under the old tax regime. Both recipients were registered unrecognised political parties. In the show-cause notice dated 12 January 2026, the Assessing Officer stated that the parties’ contribution reports in Form 24A for FY 2023–24 could not be found on the Election Commission’s website. The notice also referred to the absence of evidence of political activity and the parties’ failure to respond to notices under section 133(6). On that basis, the assessee was asked to explain why the entire deduction should not be disallowed. Donor Furnished Bank Records, Receipts and Registration Evidence The assessee furnished bank statements evidencing the payments, donation receipts and evidence of registration with the Election Commission of India. The payments had been made by cheque from a disclosed bank account, and the Revenue did not dispute the source of the funds. The Tribunal observed that filing Form 24A was a matter of compliance by the political parties. A donor could not control whether a recipient furnished its contribution report to the Election Commission. The Bench considered that omission in light of the documents the assessee had produced to substantiate his own payments. Assessment Order Relied on Material Outside the Notice The Assessing Officer passed the assessment order on 16 February 2026 under section 143(3) read with section 144B. In denying the deduction, the order relied on findings from searches conducted on certain political parties. The show-cause notice had made no reference to those searches or their findings. The CIT(A) sustained the disallowance, and the assessee appealed to the Tribunal. The fact that the notice proposed disallowing the full ₹7,66,005 did not resolve the procedural defect. The assessee had answered the objections communicated to him. The adverse search findings raised a further basis for disallowance, and the assessment order was the first occasion on which the Assessing Officer invoked that basis against him. Authorities on the Scope of a Show-Cause Notice The Tribunal relied on Commissioner of Customs v. Toyo Engineering India Ltd., Civil Appeal No. 2532 of 2001, decided on 31 August 2006, for the principle that the Department cannot travel beyond the grounds stated in the show-cause notice. It also referred to Vivek Jaisingh Asher v. ITO (2024) 299 Taxman 222 (Bom.), where the Bombay High Court emphasised that a notice must identify the basis of the proposed action with enough precision for the assessee to respond effectively. The Tribunal cited Margita Infra v. National e-Assessment Centre, Delhi (2023) 458 ITR 101 (Guj.) on procedural safeguards in faceless assessments. It also followed the reasoning of the Delhi coordinate Bench in Robust Resorts & Hospitality LLP v. DCIT, ITA No. 4312/Del/2024, which examined a material difference between the case put to the assessee and the basis adopted in the final order. Tribunal Deletes the Disallowance Applying these authorities, the Tribunal held that the deficiency in the show-cause notice vitiated the disallowance. It deleted the entire ₹7,66,005 disallowance under section 80GGC and allowed the assessee’s appeal. The operative relief rested on the Assessing Officer’s reliance on adverse material that the assessee had been given no opportunity to address. Author’s Comments The practical point is to compare the grounds and material disclosed in the show-cause notice with those actually used in the assessment order. Here, the proposed figure and the statutory provision remained the same throughout. What changed was the evidentiary basis for the disallowance. A reply addressing missing contribution reports and unanswered verification notices could not address search findings that had never been disclosed. The decision also distinguishes the evidence a donor can furnish from compliance that lies with the recipient. The assessee produced his own payment records, receipts and registration evidence. The political parties controlled their Form 24A filings. The Tribunal’s observations on that distinction supported its analysis, while the decisive ground for deletion was the use of undisclosed search findings. Now the short question that arises for my consideration is as to whether the learned AO could travel beyond what is stated by him in the show cause notice and frame an assessment by making a disallowance under section 80 GGC of the Act. This issue is no longer res integra in view of the decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Commissioner of Customs vs Toyo Engineering India limited reported in 2006 (8) TMI 184 – Supreme Court in Appeal No. 2532 of 2001 dated 31/8/2006. The relevant operative portion of the said judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court is reproduced below:- “16. Learned counsel for the Revenue tried to raise some of the submissions which were not allowed to be raised by the Tribunal before us, as well. We agree with the Tribunal that the revenue could not be allowed to raise these submissions for the first time in the second appeal before the Tribunal. Neither adjudicating authority nor the appellate authority had denied the facility of the project import to the respondent on any of these grounds. These grounds did not find mention in the show cause notice as well. The Department cannot be travel beyond the show cause notice. Even in the grounds of appeals these points have not been taken.” 6. Further the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of Vivek Jaisingh Asher vs ITO reported in 299 taxman 222 (Bom) vide judgment dated 16/4/2024 had observed on the importance of show cause notice as under:- “9. The courts have time and again held that issuance of show cause notice is not an empty formality. Its purpose is to give reasonable opportunity to the affected persons to effectively deal with the allegations in the show cause notice. In our view, even the show cause notice dated 23rd August 2022 is defective in as much as even though it had reference to Section 56(2)(x) of the Act, it did not mention whether the Assessing Officer proposed to treat the stamp duty value as deemed income of assessee under clause (a) or clause(b) of Section 56(2)(x) of the Act. This is because both are separate provisions and under either of these two clauses the stamp duty value could be treated as deemed income. By not specifying whether Section 56(2)(x)(a) or Section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act was applicable, the A.O. first of all has not given reasonable opportunity of showing cause to the assessee. Assessee would be totally unaware of the grounds which had prompted the A.O. to arrive at a prima facie conclusion and issue show cause notice. The power that the A.O. had was required to be executed properly. Moreover in the assessment order dated 29th September 2022 that is impugned in the petition, the A.O. has chosen to give Section 56(2)(x), a go by and treat the stamp duty value of the flat at Rs.11,68,99,000/- as from unexplained source under Section 69 of the Act. There is no reference to Section 56(2)(x) of the Act in the operative part of the order dated 29th September 2022. 10. In the circumstances, the impugned order dated 29th September 2022 cannot be sustained. The allegations in the affidavit in reply that assessee has claimed tenancy rights as colourable device in order to get an exemption under the provisions of the Act and evade the tax liability also cannot be accepted because if the A.O. had evidence to that effect the same should have been stated in the show cause notice dated 23rd August 2022.” 7. Further the Hon’ble Gujarat High Court in the case of Margita Infra vs National e Assessment Centre, Delhi reported in 458 ITR 101 (Guj) on the importance of issuance of show cause notice and contents thereon and its relevance for framing the assessment had observed as under:- “8. The Orissa High Court in the case of Sribasta Kumar Versus Union of India and another in WP © No. 29279 of 2021, was examining the issue where principal ground on which the assessment order had been challenged that was, that the mandatory requirement under section 144B of the Act has not been complied with prior to issuance of the assessment order, wherein the Court held thus: “5. I also find that the co-ordinate bench of Delhi Tribunal in the case of Robust Resorts & Hospitality LLP vs DCIT in ITA No. 4312/Del/2024 dated 12/11/2025 had observed in this regard as under:- “3. Now the primary requirement for application of section 69C of the Act is that assessee should incur expenses out of unexplained source of income. The when it is found not to be wholly and exclusively for business. Where purchases and source of expense are not doubted and only doubt was regarding genuineness of expense, disallowance may be made under section 37(1) of the Act, by Assessing Officer and section 69C of the Act may not become applicable. Thus where the Id. AO has show caused the assessee for justifying expenses alleged to be bogus making addition u/s 69C of the Act by invoking deeming income provision, is not justified and violates principles of natural justice made applicable under the Act, by way of mandatory notices, u/s 143(2) or 142(1) of the Act. The principles of procedural fairness and burden of proof require assessee being given due notice of prospective action and specially if there is any legal burden on assessee to be discharged specifically. Thus we are inclined to sustain this ground No. 8 and allow the appeal.” 9. Respectfully following the aforesaid judicial precedents, I have no hesitation to delete the disallowance made under section 80 GGC of the Act for deficiency in the show cause notice issued by the learned AO. Accordingly, the grounds raised by the assessee are allowed. 10. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 18-Nov-2026.
