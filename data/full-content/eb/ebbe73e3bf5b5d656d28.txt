Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Pakman International Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Sales Already Included in Turnover Cannot Be Taxed Again as Accommodation Entries on an Uncorroborated Third-Party Statement Tribunal Deletes ₹30.49 Lakh Addition for Sales Already Disclosed The Delhi ITAT deleted an addition of ₹30,48,609 made on the allegation that payments received from a customer represented accommodation entries. The assessee had recorded the sales in its profit and loss account and included them in its turnover. The Assessing Officer relied on a statement made by the customer during a search, without identifying any defect in the assessee’s supporting documents or allowing cross-examination. The Tribunal held that a separate addition of the same trading receipts would result in double addition. Reassessment Followed a Search Statement by the Customer The assessee firm filed its original return for AY 2014-15 on 26 November 2014, declaring total income of ₹7,67,498. The Assessing Officer issued a notice under section 148 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 on 30 March 2021. After the time specified in the notice expired, the Assessing Officer issued a further notice under section 142(1) calling for a return. In a submission dated 10 December 2021, the firm stated that it had filed its return on 30 November 2021 and requested the reasons recorded for reopening. The Assessing Officer referred to the absence of a return on the e-filing portal and also recorded that the return had been filed beyond the time allowed in the section 148 notice. He accordingly proceeded on the footing that there was no return in response to that notice and did not furnish the reasons requested by the firm. The Assessing Officer completed the reassessment on 21 March 2022 under section 147 read with section 144, determining total income of ₹38,16,107. He added ₹30,48,609 in respect of sales to Atul Tyagi of Disha Enterprises, relying on Tyagi’s statement during a search that he had provided accommodation entries to various concerns. The CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, upheld the addition by order dated 23 June 2026. Sales Records and the Customer’s Statement Had to Be Examined Together The Tribunal found that the assessee had furnished details before the Assessing Officer to substantiate the sales to Disha Enterprises. The lower authorities had identified no deficiency or falsity in those documents. The addition rested on the customer’s statement, while the documentary evidence relating to the assessee’s own transactions had been disregarded. The Tribunal also noted that the assessee had been given no opportunity to cross-examine Atul Tyagi. The statement concerned Tyagi’s involvement in providing accommodation entries to various concerns. The question in this appeal was whether the receipts from this customer for these particular sales could be treated as accommodation entries in the assessee’s hands. The Tribunal found the statement insufficient on the record before it, particularly when the sales evidence remained unrebutted. Reassessment Computation Revealed the Duplication Although the Assessing Officer treated the firm as having filed no return in response to the reopening notice, the Tribunal noted that the final computation began with the returned income of ₹7,67,498. The trading receipts of ₹30,48,609 were already accounted for in arriving at the returned result. The Assessing Officer then added the same amount separately. This computational point gave the Tribunal a further ground for deletion. Once the sales were included in the turnover used to compute the returned income, the same receipts could not be brought to tax again as a separate item on the evidence available. The Tribunal deleted the entire addition and allowed the appeal. The assessee had also raised grounds concerning the validity of reopening, the absence of a section 143(2) notice, and credit for ₹2,40,000 of advance tax with consequential interest relief. The order’s operative reasoning addressed the ₹30,48,609 addition. It contains no separate finding on those other grounds. Author’s Comments Two questions shaped the result. The first was evidentiary. A customer’s admission about his wider activities may prompt an enquiry, but the assessment must examine what the statement establishes about the specific transaction and address the seller’s supporting records. Here, the absence of cross-examination mattered because the statement was the basis for rejecting the sales despite the documents on record. The second question was computational. The relevant comparison was between the turnover reported in the profit and loss account and the amount added separately in reassessment. Because the computation started with the returned income, the disclosed sales were already reflected in the trading result. Adding the full receipts again duplicated their inclusion. The case shows why a reconciliation from the customer’s account to the turnover and then to the reassessment computation is important when defending an addition relating to trading receipts. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. This appeal in ITA No. 7952/Del/2026 for AY 2014-15 arises out of the order of the ld. National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. CIT(A)’ in short] dated 23.06.2026 against the order of the assessment passed u/s 147 r.w.s. 144 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as the ‘the Act’) dated 21.03.2022 by the Assessing Officer, NFAC Assessment Unit, Delhi (hereinafter referred to as the ‘ld. AO’). 2. The assessee has raised the following grounds of appeal: – “1. The Ld. AO erred in law and on facts in initiating the reassessment proceedings under section 148 without complying with mandatory conditions and framing the assessment under section 147 r.w.s 144 and the Ld. CIT (A) erred in not quashing the assessment in the facts and circumstances of the case. 2. The Ld. AO erred in law and on facts in framing the assessment under section 147 r.w.s 144 r.w.s 144B without issuing jurisdictional notice u/s 143(2) of the Act and the Ld. CIT (A) erred in not quashing the assessment in the facts and circumstances of the case. 3. The Ld. AO erred in law and on facts in making the addition of Rs. 30,48,609/- under the head Income from Other Sources and the Ld. CIT (A) erred in law and on facts in sustaining the addition, in the facts and circumstances of the case. 4. The Ld. AO erred in law and on facts in not allowing credit of advance tax paid by the assessee at Rs. 2,40,000/- though verifiable from Form 26AS and also allowed in intimation u/s 143(1) and the Ld. CIT (A) erred in law and on facts in not giving credit for advance tax paid, in the facts and circumstances of the case. The corresponding reduction in interest u/s 234A, B & C may also be allowed accordingly. 5. The Ld. AO erred in law and on facts in not following the principles of natural justice while conducting the proceedings and the Ld. CIT (A) erred in law and on facts in confirming the action of Ld. AO, in the facts and circumstances of the case. 6. The appellant craves leave to amend OR alter all OR any of the aforesaid grounds of appeal and amend, alter OR add any other ground of appeal.” 3. I have heard the rival submissions and perused the materials available on record. The return of income for the assessment year 2014 15 was filed by the assessee firm on 26/11/2014 declaring total income of ₹7,67,498. The case of the assessee was sought to be reopened under section 147 of the Act vide notice under section 148 of the act dated 30-3-2021. The assessee did not file return of income within the time prescribed in the notice under section 148 of the Act. As no return was filed by the assessee, notice under section 142(1) of the Act was issued to the assessee calling for return of income in response to notice under section 148 of the Act. The assessee vide submission dated 10-12-2021 stated that it had filed the return of income on 30/11/2021 and sought for reasons recorded for reopening the assessment. The learned AO noted that from the efiling portal it was found that assessee had not filed any return of income and accordingly did not furnish the reasons for reopening as sought for by the assessee. The learned AO having noted this fact in page number 2 first paragraph, on the same breath, also noted that assessee has filed the return of income much after the time prescribed in the notice under section 148 of the Act and accordingly the case was treated as if the assessee had not filed any return of income under section 148 of the Act. Accordingly the reassessment was completed under section 144 read with section 147 of the Act determining total income of the assessee at ₹38,16,107 after making addition of ₹30,48,609 on account of sales made by the assessee to Shri Atul Tyagi on the ground that Shri Atul Tyagi in the course of his search had accepted that he was indulged in providing accommodation entries to various concerns. This action of the learned AO was upheld by the learned CITA. 4. At the outset, I find that the assessee had made sales to Shri Atul Tyagi of M/s Disha Enterprises and had disclosed the sales as his income in the profit and loss account. Pursuant to these sales, the assessee had received a sum of Rs 30,48,609 from the said party which is a trading receipt already offered to tax. Hence there is no question of treating the same as an accommodation entry and taxing it again. Further I find that the learned AO having ignored the income tax return filed by the assessee had actually taken due cognizance of the same in the final computation of income as he starts the determination of total income from the income returned by the assessee in the sum of Rs 7,67,498. The assessee on his part had duly furnished all the details to prove the veracity of the sales made to Disha Enterprises (Shri Atul Tyagi) before the learned AO. None of these documents were found to be either deficient or false by the lower authorities. The entire addition has been made merely by placing reliance on the statement of Shri Atul Tyagi by completely ignoring the documentary evidences placed on record. Even the cross examination of Shri Atul Tyagi was not provided to the assessee. 5. In view of the aforesaid observations, I hold that this is not a fit case for making any addition of ₹30,48,609 in the hands of the assessee. The assessee had furnished all the requisite details to prove the veracity of the sales made to Disha Enterprises (Shri Atul Tyagi) and had duly included the same in the total turnover of the assessee. Hence making a separate addition again in the sum of ₹30,48,609 would only result in double addition. The addition deserves to be deleted on this count also. The ground raised by the assessee are allowed. 6. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed.
