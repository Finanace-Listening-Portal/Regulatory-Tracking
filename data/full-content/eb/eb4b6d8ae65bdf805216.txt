Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Expedia Lodging Partner Services Sarl Vs ACIT (ITAT elhi) Summary: The Delhi Bench “D” of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal partly allowed the appeal of Expedia Lodging Partner Services Sarl for AY 2018–19. It rejected the challenge to approval for reassessment under section 151 but allowed the substantive ground that the assessee had no dependent agent permanent establishment (DAPE) in India. The appeal arose from the final assessment order dated 17 January 2025 under section 147 read with section 144C(13), pursuant to DRP directions dated 19 December 2024. The Swiss company provided strategic lodging services to overseas group companies, including global lodging-supply strategy, technology platforms and analysis of lodging opportunities for point-of-sale websites operated by other overseas Expedia entities. The assessee challenged the approval granted by CIT (International Taxation), Delhi–1 on 28 April 2022 for the section 148A(d) order and section 148 notice dated 29 April 2022. It argued that more than three years had elapsed from the end of AY 2018–19 and approval was therefore required from the higher authority specified in section 151(ii). It also contended that the exclusions and extensions under section 149 did not apply to section 151 before the Finance Act, 2023 amendment, effective from 1 April 2023. The Tribunal admitted the additional legal ground after noting reliance on National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. and Jute Corporation of India Ltd. The assessee relied on Bhagwan Sahai Sharma, Agnello Oswin Dias and Vodafone Idea; the Revenue defended the approval and relied on the statutory exclusion of the response period and supporting decisions. The Tribunal held that the section 148A(b) notice dated 27 March 2022 had been issued within three years. The original compliance date was 4 April 2022, subsequently extended to 22 April 2022; the assessee replied on 21 April 2022. Applying the third proviso to section 149, the Tribunal excluded the period from 1 April to 22 April 2022. It then applied the fourth proviso’s seven-day extension and held that the order and notice dated 29 April 2022 fell within the extended period. Following the jurisdictional Delhi High Court decisions in Abhinav Jain, Ram Balram Buildhome and BKR Capital, it upheld the CIT’s approval under section 151(i). It distinguished Bhagwan Sahai Sharma on the facts and observed that the implications of the third and fourth provisos had not been brought to the Bombay High Court’s notice in the authorities relied upon by the assessee. Ground 3 and additional ground 3.1 were dismissed. On merits, the assessee had earned gross commissions of ₹23,71,82,704 from Indian lodging suppliers and claimed that these were not taxable in India because it had no PE. The Assessing Officer treated Expedia Online Travel Services India Private Limited (EXP-IN) as its DAPE, applied an average global net-profit rate of 6.3%, worked out net profit of ₹1,49,42,510 and attributed 60%, or ₹89,65,506, to India. The assessee disputed EXP-IN’s alleged contract-negotiation and conclusion role, maintained that it provided support services on a principal-to-principal basis, and challenged profit attribution under Rule 10 without an appropriate FAR analysis or regard to arm’s-length remuneration. The Tribunal found the business model and material facts identical to AY 2022–23, for which its order in ITA No. 1886/Del/2025, also dated 21 September 2026, had already held that no DAPE existed. It applied that decision mutatis mutandis and allowed ground 6. Profit attribution under ground 7 consequently became academic. Interest under sections 234A and 234B and fee under section 234F were consequential, while the penalty ground was premature. The appeal was partly allowed. Brief facts of the case are that the assessee company is incorporated and governed by the laws of Switzerland on 3rd Dec, 2009. The assessee is engaged in the business of providing “Strategic Lodging Services” to its companies outside lndia which primarily include formulating, managing and guiding global strategies for optimizing lodging supply; developing technology platforms, tools and systems; gathering, analyzing and summarizing data and information related to such lodging opportunities for inclusion on Point of Sale websites (PoS), such websites among others are owned, managed and operated by the other Expedia group entities outside lndia. 4. In the instant appeal the assessee has raised additional ground under Rule 11 of the Income-tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963, with regard to issuance of notice under section 148 having been passed without obtaining approval from any specified authority under section 151 of the Act and has relied on the decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of National Thermal Power Co. The learned Counsel stated that for the instant year the order under section 148A(d) of the Act dated 29.04.2022 were passed after obtaining approval of CIT (International Taxation), Delhi–1 on 28.04.2022 instead of Pr.CCIT and therefore, it was vehemently argued that there was no valid approval for passing order under section 148A(d) of the Act. In this regard, it was submitted that the provisions u/s 151 of the Act as substituted by Finance Act 2021 provides that the specified authority for approving u/s 148 Order is as follows: 4.1. Principal Commissioner, Principal Director, Commissioner, or Director where three years or less have elapsed from the end of the relevant assessment year, 4.2. Principal Chief Commissioner or Principal Director General, or Chief Commissioner or Director General when there is no such Principal, where more than three years have elapsed from the end of the relevant assessment year. 7. It is pertinent to mention that the CIT (IT), Delhi-1 granted purported approval on 28 Apr 2022 (i.e.. more than three years from the end of AY 2018-19 or 31 Mar 2022) which was not permissible as per Section 151 (ii) of the Act. 8. It is further submitted that for the Subject Year, there was no extension for granting approval for passing 148 Order. Further, the exclusions/extensions of time limit provided u/s 149 of the Act are not applicable on time limits for granting approval u/s 151 of the Act. 9. The Ld AR brought our attention towards the fact that a specific provision was inserted u/s 151 of the Act by Finance Act 2023 wherein it was provided that the exclusions/extensions of time limit provided u/s 149 of the Act shall also apply on time limits for granting approval u/s 151 of the Act. Such amendment was applicable from 1 Apr 2023 onwards. The relevant extract of memorandum to Finance Bill 2023 is reproduced hereunder: “At the same time, to give further clarity with regards to the specified authority a proviso is proposed to be inserted in the section 151 to provide that while computing the period of three years for the purposes of determining the specified authority the period which has been excluded or extended as per the provisos in section 149 of the Act from the time limit for issuance of notice under section 148 of the Act shall be taken into account,……These amendments will take effect from the Ist day of April, 2023.” 10. It is submitted that Section 151 of the Act (before Finance Act 2023 amendment) clearly provides that where more than three years have lapsed from the end of the relevant assessment year, the specified authority should be Principal Chief Commissioner of Principal Director General (or Chief Commissioner or Director General where there is no Principal Chief Commissioner or Principal Director General). In the present case, approval was granted by CIT (IT), Delhi-1 on 28 Apr 2022 (Le., more than three years from the end of the relevant assessment year which is not in accordance with section 151 of the Act). This procedural defect is not merely technical but jurisdictional in nature, rendering the reassessment proceedings null and void. The reassessment proceedings. therefore, stand vitiated in their entirety. 11. The substantive jurisdictional issue for our adjudication is whether the order u/s 148A(d) and notice u/s 148 dated 29.04.2022 was issued without obtaining prior approval of correct specified authority u/s 151 of the Act. It is the case of the assessee that CIT(IT) Delhi had granted approval on 28.04.2022 i.e., after 3 years from the end of AY 2018-19 (i.e., after March 31, 2022) which was not permissible u/s 151(ii) of the Act. On the other hand, the case of the Revenue is that the assessee was issued show cause notice under section 148A(b) of the Act on 27.03.2022 for A.Y. 2018-19 i.e., within 3 years from the end of assessment year 2018-19. As the assessee was granted extended date for compliance upto 22.04.2022, the period from 01.04.2022 to 22.04.2022 is to be excluded as per the 3rd proviso to section 149 of the Act. Since the reassessment is within 3 years, the approval taken from CIT is correctly obtained from specified authority u/s 151(i) of the Act. 16. To appreciate the issue at hand, we deem it appropriate to reproduce the statutory provision of law u/s 149 as substituted by Finance Act 2021 w.e.f. 01.04.2021, existing at the relevant point of time. [Time limit for notice. 149. (1) No notice under section 148 shall be issued for the relevant assessment year,— (a) if three years have elapsed from the end of the relevant assessment year, unless the case falls under clause (b); (b) if three years, but not more than ten years, have elapsed from the end of the relevant assessment year unless the Assessing Officer has in his possession books of account or other documents or evidence which reveal that the income chargeable to tax, represented in the form of asset, which has escaped assessment amounts to or is likely to amount to fifty lakh rupees or more for that year: Provided that no notice under section 148 shall be issued at any time in a case for the relevant assessment year beginning on or before 1st day of April, 2021, if such notice could not have been issued at that time on account of being beyond the time limit specified under the provisions of clause (b) of sub-section (1) of this section, as they stood immediately before the commencement of the Finance Act, 2021: Provided further that the provisions of this sub-section shall not apply in a case, where a notice under section 153A, or section 153C read with section 153A, is required to be issued in relation to a search initiated under section 132 or books of account, other documents or any assets requisitioned under section 132A, on or before the 31st day of March, 2021: Provided also that for the purposes of computing the period of limitation as per this section, the time or extended time allowed to the assessee, as per show-cause notice issued under clause (b) of section 148A or the period during which the proceeding under section 148A is stayed by an order or injunction of any court, shall be excluded: Provided also that where immediately after the exclusion of the period referred to in the immediately preceding proviso, the period of limitation available to the Assessing Officer for passing an order under clause (d) of section 148A is less than seven days, such remaining period shall be extended to seven days and the period of limitation under this sub-section shall be deemed to be extended accordingly. Explanation.—For the purposes of clause (b) of this sub-section, “asset” shall include immovable property, being land or building or both, shares and securities, loans and advances, deposits in bank account. (2) The provisions of sub-section (1) as to the issue of notice shall be subject to the provisions of section 151.] 17. In the instant case, the AO issued show cause notice u/s 148A(b) on 27.03.2022 which is within 3 years from the end of AY 2018-19. The AO sought for compliance by 04.04.2022 wherein no reply was submitted. The AO allowed extension for the date of compliance upto 22.04.2022 wherein the assessee submitted reply on 21.04.2022. Thus as per 3rd proviso of section 149, the period from 01.04.2022 upto 22.04.2022 shall stand excluded from computing the period of limitation. Thereafter, as per the 4th proviso of section 149, the AO issued order u/s 148A(d) and notice u/s 148 on 29.04.2022 i.e., within the extended period of seven days. In such factual matrix, as the notice was issued within 3 years from the end of relevant assessment year, the AO, therefore, has correctly obtained approval of CIT(IT), Delhi on 28.04.2022 as per section 151(i) of the Act. 18. In exactly similar facts, in the case of Abhinav Jain V ITO (2026) 185 taxmann.com 461(Delhi), the hon’ble Delhi Court has held that where time to file reply to notice u/s 149(1) is extended, by virtue of 3rd proviso to section 149, the time allowed to assessee to file reply had to be excluded from computing the period of limitation. In that case, the show cause notice u/s 148(b) was issued on 22.03.2022 for AY 2018-19, granting time till 28.03.2022 to file reply. Subsequently, the AO issued two corrigenda on 31.03.2022 and 01.04.2022, extending the time to submit reply till 02.04.2022. The hon’ble Court held that by application of 3rd proviso to section 149(1), the time till 02.04.2022 (11 days) allowed by the AO for furnishing of reply, has to be excluded. Therefore, the order u/s 148A(d) and notice u/s 148 passed on 07.04.2022 shall be within limitation. The hon’ble Court further held as under: 51. In view of our above conclusion, the plea of Mr. Jain that the order under Section 148A(d) and notice under Section 148 have been passed and issued beyond three years, PCCIT/CCIT are competent to take decision to issue notice would not survive as the PCIT and CIT are competent to issue notice within three years, which has been done in this case. It was similarly held in Ram Balram Buildhome Pvt Ltd V ITO W.P.(C) 16232/2024 dated 30.01.2025 by the hon’ble Delhi Court. Likewise the hon’ble Delhi Court in the case of BKR Capital Pvt. Ltd Versus Income Tax Officer, Ward 4.1., Delhi, W.P.(C) 19738/2025, held that the AO acted within jurisdiction and that the period consumed in granting opportunity under section 148A(b) stands excluded as per 5 th proviso of section 149 of the Act of 2024 (corresponding to 4 th proviso of section 149 of Act of 2021). 19. The case of Bhagwan Sahai Sharma vs. DCIT (supra) relied by the assessee is clearly distinguishable in facts. In that case the AY involved is AY 2017-18 and the original notice u/s 148 was issued on 29.06.2021. In accordance with the hon’ble Supreme Court decision in Ashish Agarwal, the AO supplied information to the assessee on 02.06.2022 which was complied with on 25.06.2022. Thereafter the AO issued notice u/s 148 on 29.07.2022. In such facts and context, the hon’ble Court held that since the notice was issued beyond the period of three years from the end of relevant assessment year and the notice u/s 148 issued was without prior approval of PCCIT u/s 151(ii), the same is therefore invalid. Besides, in this decision, the hon’ble court did not discuss the implication of 3rd and 4th proviso of section 149 of the Act. 20. As far as the decision of the Bombay High Court in case of Agnello Oswin Dias v. ACIT (supra), Siemens Financial Services (P.) Ltd. v. Dy. CIT [2023] 457 ITR 647 (Bom.), and Vodafone Idea Limited v. DCIT (supra) is concerned, we find that the implication of 3rd and 4th proviso of section 149 of the Act, was not brought to the notice of the hon’ble Court. In any case, we are bound by the hon’ble jurisdictional Delhi Court and following its decision rendered in Abhinav Jain V ITO; Ram Balram Buildhome Pvt Ltd V ITO and BKR Capital Pvt. Ltd Versus Income Tax Officer, Ward 4.1., Delhi, discussed above, we hold that the AO has obtained the approval for issuance of notice u/s 148 from CIT(IT)- Delhi who is the correct specified authority u/s 151(i) of the Act. The ground 3 and the additional ground 3.1 is accordingly dismissed. 21. Coming to the merits of the case, we find that the facts and circumstances of AY 2018-19 is identical to the facts and circumstances of AY 2022-23. In the instant year, the assessee has same business model and has earned Gross revenue (ie., commissions from Indian lodging suppliers) of Rs 237,182,704 and claimed the same to be non- taxable in India as there is no PE in India. 22. The AO however, as in AY 2022-23, has adopted the same reasoning and logic to hold that the India AE, namely EXP-IN was a DAPE in India. Accordingly, the AO has adopted the global net profit rate (i.e., average of global net profit rate for Dec 2017 and Dec 2018) at 6.3%, working out net profit of Rs 1,49,42,510/-. The Ld AO then adopted attribution rate of 60% to income earned in India and computed the Income attributable to India at Rs. 89,65,506/-. 23. The ITAT has already held in AY 2022-23, vide its order in ITA 1886/Del/2025 dated 21.09.2026 that the assessee has no DAPE in India. As there is no change in facts and circumstances in the instant year, the decision in AY 2022-23 shall apply mutatis mutandis in the impugned year also. Ground 6 is accordingly allowed. 24. Ground 7 regarding attribution of income has become academic as we have held that there is no DAPE in India. 25. Ground 8, 9 regarding interest u/s 234A, 234B and fee u/s 234F is consequential and ground 10 regarding penalty is premature. 26. In the result, assessee’s appeal in ITA 1943/Del/2025 is partly allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 21.09.2026.
