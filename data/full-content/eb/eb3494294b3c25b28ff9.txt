Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Al-Ameen Educational Trust Vs CIT (Supreme Court of India) Summary: The dispute involving Al-Ameen Educational Trust concerned the imposition of penalty under Section 271D of the Income Tax Act, 1961, for accepting loans and deposits in cash in violation of Section 269SS during assessment years 2005-06 and 2006-07. The Trust had received substantial cash advances from staff members and a loan from the daughter of its President. The Revenue imposed penalties for the cash transactions, while the assessee contended that the transactions were genuine, the funds were required for construction activities, certain amounts were subsequently repaid through cheques, and there was no intention to evade tax. The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, by majority, accepted the explanation as reasonable cause under Section 273B and deleted the penalties. Read Kerala HC Judgment in this case: Cash Loans from Staff Attract Section 271D Penalty Without Reasonable Cause: Kerala HC The Kerala High Court reversed the Tribunal’s majority decision, holding that the assessee had failed to discharge the statutory burden of establishing reasonable cause under Section 273B. The Court observed that the genuineness of cash transactions, their subsequent deposit in bank accounts, repayment through cheques, and absence of tax evasion did not automatically justify non-compliance with Section 269SS. Mere ignorance of law or a general assertion of urgent financial requirements could not constitute reasonable cause, particularly for an educational trust capable of obtaining professional advice. Relying on Assistant Director of Inspection (Investigation) v. Kum. A.B. Shanthi, (2002) 255 ITR 258 (SC), and other judicial precedents, the High Court emphasised that statutory penalties could be avoided only upon satisfactory proof of reasonable cause. The High Court consequently answered the substantial questions of law in favour of the Revenue and held that the Tribunal had erred in deleting the penalties. It affirmed the order of the Tribunal’s Administrative Member, including the direction to examine afresh the circumstances surrounding the cash loan of Rs.49,00,000 received from Zeenath, the daughter of the Trust’s President. The Court clarified that accepting cash deposits from staff members without demonstrating any genuine impediment to using prescribed banking channels could not justify relief under Section 273B. Accordingly, the penalties relating to such transactions were sustained, subject to the directed reconsideration of the separate loan transaction. Subsequently, when the matter reached the Supreme Court of India, the Trust sought permission to withdraw its Special Leave Petitions because it proposed to avail the benefits of the Direct Tax Vivad Se Vishwas Act, 2020. The Supreme Court permitted unconditional withdrawal and dismissed the petitions as withdrawn, granting liberty to submit an application under the settlement scheme. It expressly directed that any such application should be considered independently, in accordance with law and on its own merits, without treating the Court’s order as expressing an opinion in favour of either party. Thus, the Supreme Court did not adjudicate the substantive penalty dispute or affirm the Kerala High Court’s findings on merits. The High Court judgment nevertheless remains significant in explaining the strict requirements of Sections 269SS, 271D and 273B concerning cash loans, statutory penalties and the burden of proving reasonable cause. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER The issue involved in these petitions is imposition of penalty under Section 271D of the Income Tax Act for violation of the provisions of Section 269SS of the Act. Today, when the present petitions are taken up for further hearing, Shri C.U. Singh, learned senior counsel appearing for the petitioner seeks permission to withdraw the Special Leave Petitions as the petitioner proposes to avail the benefit under the Direct Tax Vivad Se Vishwas Act, 2020. In that view of the matter, the Special Leave Petitions stand dismissed as withdrawn unconditionally with the above- mentioned liberty. As and when any application is made, the same be considered in accordance with law and on its own merits for which we have not expressed anything in favour of any party.
