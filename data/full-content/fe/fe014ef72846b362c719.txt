Its UPI market share remains below pre-restriction levels, despite a recovery in transaction volumes and users.Paytm has moved a step closer to reviving its digital wallet business after the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) granted in-principle authorisation to its wholly owned subsidiary, Paytm Payments Services Ltd (PPSL), to issue and operate prepaid payment instruments (PPIs), including wallets. The approval could help the fintech restore a payment option it lost after regulatory restrictions on Paytm Payments Bank in 2024, but it cannot restart wallet issuance until it completes the prescribed audit and obtains the central bank's final authorisation. Advt The RBI's letter, dated November 13, grants the authorisation under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, subject to compliance with its Master Directions on Prepaid Payment Instruments. PPSL must undertake a system audit, including a cybersecurity audit, through an eligible independent auditor and submit the report to the RBI within six months. Failure to meet the deadline will result in the automatic lapse of the in-principle authorisation, after which final approval will not be considered. The company must also obtain prior RBI approval for changes in its shareholding or ownership.The approval marks an important step in separating Paytm's wallet operations from its former banking arm. The wallet business was operated through Paytm Payments Bank, which was barred from accepting fresh deposits and wallet top-ups in January and February 2024 following regulatory action. The restrictions covered deposits and credits into existing customer accounts, prepaid instruments and wallets, effectively preventing customers from adding money to their Paytm wallets.The RBI cancelled the payments bank's licence on April 24, 2026, citing non-compliance with licensing conditions and regulatory requirements. The Delhi High Court subsequently ordered the bank's winding up in July. The new authorisation would allow PPSL, a separate subsidiary of One 97 Communications, to operate the prepaid payment instrument business independently of the payments bank.The altered groundHowever, Paytm is returning to a digital payments market in which its competitive position has weakened. Its share of UPI transaction volume stood at 8.1% in September 2026, compared with 11.8% in January 2024, before the restrictions on Paytm Payments Bank took effect. Its share had fallen to 6.7% in March 2025, before recovering gradually. Advt Paytm processed 1.94 billion UPI transactions in September 2026, compared with 1.06 billion in September 2024. The value of transactions increased to Rs 2.05 lakh crore from Rs 1.13 lakh crore over the same period. Its share of UPI transaction value rose from around 5.5% to nearly 7%. The growth indicates a recovery in its payments business, although it has not regained its earlier share of the market.The company has also rebuilt its customer and merchant base. Its average monthly transacting users increased to 8.5 crore in the September 2026 quarter, up from 7.5 crore a year earlier. Merchants on subscription plans, including devices, rose to 1.64 crore at the end of September, against 1.37 crore a year earlier. Its gross merchandise value increased 26% year on year to Rs 7.1 lakh crore during the quarter. Advt Competition remains intense, with PhonePe and Google Pay continuing to dominate UPI transaction volumes. Newer players have also gained ground. Navi accounted for nearly 5% of UPI transactions in September 2026, while super.money had a share of around 2%. The expansion of these platforms has made it harder for Paytm to regain market share even as its transaction volumes grow.For Paytm, the wallet licence could complement its UPI business and restore another payment option for consumers. Prepaid wallets can serve specific purposes, including gifting, transit and payments where users prefer to maintain a stored balance. The company has also previously indicated that a wallet would make its consumer payments offering more complete. Advt The immediate priority is to complete the prescribed audit and secure final RBI authorisation. Paytm will then need to persuade customers to reload their wallets and use them regularly in a market where UPI has become the dominant mode of digital payments. The regulatory approval removes an important hurdle, but reclaiming its lost market position will depend on customer adoption, merchant acceptance and the commercial relevance of wallets.
