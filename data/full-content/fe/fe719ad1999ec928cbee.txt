Summary: PGIM India Midcap Fund, formerly PGIM India Midcap Opportunities Fund, is an open-ended equity scheme that seeks long-term capital appreciation by allocating 65%–100% to mid-cap equity and related instruments. It uses the Nifty Midcap 150 Total Return Index as its benchmark and carries a Very High riskometer rating, reflecting exposure to volatility, liquidity constraints and portfolio concentration. The October 2026 portfolio disclosure shows holdings as of month-end, and the total expense ratio varies between Direct and Regular plans. The disclosed exit load is 0.50% on units redeemed or switched out within 90 days of allotment and nil thereafter, with the 90-day period assessed separately for each SIP instalment. A switch-out generally counts as a redemption for capital gains purposes, and the tax treatment depends on each lot’s holding period and the applicable equity-oriented fund and securities transaction tax conditions. The mandate concentrates the portfolio in mid-cap equities Official allocation ranges The official SID provides an indicative allocation of 65% to 100% in equity and equity-related instruments of mid-cap companies, 0% to 35% in other equity and equity-related instruments, and 0% to 35% in debt and money-market instruments including cash and cash equivalents. The minimum mid-cap allocation is the defining feature of the category. It limits the manager’s ability to move most assets into large-cap shares or cash during stressed conditions and therefore preserves material mid-cap market, valuation and liquidity exposure. Temporary defensive positions and derivatives, if used, remain subject to the SID, SEBI rules and disclosed limits. Investment objective is an aim, not an assurance The scheme seeks long-term capital appreciation by predominantly investing in mid-cap companies, but the SID expressly does not guarantee achievement of the objective. Mid-sized issuers may have narrower product ranges, weaker balance-sheet flexibility, lower trading liquidity or higher promoter dependence than established large-cap companies. The portfolio can also suffer from sector concentration and valuation compression. A benchmark-relative comparison does not eliminate the possibility of an absolute loss, and a favourable historical period does not establish future returns. Reading the October 2026 portfolio without turning it into a ranking Use the official monthly disclosure as a dated snapshot PGIM India’s disclosure directory lists an October 2026 monthly portfolio for PGIM India Midcap Fund and an October 2026 factsheet. Those documents are the appropriate current primary sources for company weights, sector weights, cash, derivatives and portfolio statistics. Holdings change after the disclosure date through purchases, sales, redemptions and price movement, so a monthly file is not a live portfolio. This article deliberately avoids reproducing a long ranking of holdings because the purpose is to explain the mandate and review method, not to imply that the disclosed companies will remain the largest positions. Concentration and liquidity require separate checks A portfolio review should identify the weight of the largest holdings, sector concentration, number of issuers, cash position and any derivatives, then compare those figures with the category mandate. Mid-cap liquidity can deteriorate when many funds or investors seek to exit the same shares. Reported net asset value reflects valuation rules, but the price realized by the fund in a stressed sale may differ from the last quoted price. A high number of holdings does not automatically mean low concentration if a few positions or sectors dominate economic exposure. TER, plans, exit load and redemption timing Total expense ratio is plan-specific and variable The total expense ratio is charged to the scheme and reduces NAV; it is not billed as a separate invoice to the investor. Direct and Regular plans have separate expense ratios because the Regular plan includes distribution-related expenses and commission. PGIM India’s product page displayed a 0.74% expense ratio as of 10 September 2026 in the page’s plan context, but that historical website figure should not be treated as the controlling TER for 24 November 2026. The latest daily TER disclosure and October 2026 factsheet should be checked for the exact plan before comparison. Even a small TER difference compounds over a long holding period, but it should not be assessed without risk, portfolio and service context. Exit load applies by redemption timing The product material states an exit load of 0.50% when units are redeemed or switched out within 90 days of allotment and nil thereafter. The current SID, KIM and addenda control, and load is generally assessed on each purchase or SIP instalment according to its allotment date. A switch from this scheme to another scheme is normally a redemption from the source scheme for load and tax analysis. The load reduces redemption proceeds; it is different from TER and from income tax. Liquidity is available, but NAV and payment timelines matter The SID describes an open-ended structure and a redemption payment timeline of three business days under the stated framework, subject to regulatory exceptions and operational conditions. The applicable NAV depends on valid application, cut-off time and realization of funds where relevant. Redemption proceeds can be delayed in exceptional circumstances permitted by regulation. Investors should keep the transaction acknowledgement, bank details, folio information and account statement and should not assume that placing an instruction guarantees same-day cash. Riskometer and investor-level risks behind a Very High label A Very High riskometer classification signals that substantial loss and volatility are possible, but it is not a numeric probability or a prediction. The scheme faces equity-market risk, mid-cap liquidity risk, issuer and sector concentration, valuation risk, derivatives risk, operational risk and potential tracking divergence from the benchmark. Debt and cash holdings add interest-rate, credit and counterparty considerations. The investor also bears behavioural risk: redeeming after a sharp fall or investing solely on recent return rankings can crystallize losses or create unsuitable concentration. Suitability depends on time horizon, cash-flow needs, other assets, tax position and capacity to withstand drawdowns. Indian tax treatment of redemptions and switches Equity-oriented fund holding period and rates When the scheme qualifies as an equity-oriented fund and the statutory securities-transaction-tax conditions are satisfied, units held for not more than 12 months are generally short-term capital assets. For transfers on or after 23 July 2024, section 111A provides a 20% rate for qualifying short-term capital gains. Qualifying long-term gains under section 112A are generally taxed at 12.5% to the extent aggregate covered gains exceed ₹1.25 lakh in the financial year, without indexation, plus applicable surcharge and cess. The taxpayer must verify acquisition date, transaction type and current law rather than applying a rate solely from a platform tax report. SIP lots and switches are tested separately Every SIP instalment creates a separate unit lot with its own allotment date and cost. A partial redemption is ordinarily matched under the applicable unit-accounting method, and the holding period may differ across lots. A switch-out is normally a transfer from the source scheme and can trigger capital-gain tax even when the money remains within the same fund house. STT, exit load and capital-gain tax are distinct. Tax harvesting, loss set-off, grandfathering of older equity units, residential status and return disclosure require taxpayer-specific review.
