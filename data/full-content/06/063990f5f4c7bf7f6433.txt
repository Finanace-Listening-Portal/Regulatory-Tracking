Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Torrent Gas Ltd Vs Commissioner (GSTAT Lucknow) Summary: GSTAT Lucknow dismissed Torrent Gas Limited’s appeal against rejection of its ₹7,264 refund claim for GST paid under the reverse charge mechanism on road-restoration charges paid to the Public Works Department, Uttar Pradesh. The amount comprised CGST of ₹3,632 and SGST of ₹3,632, discharged through Form GSTR-3B for October 2020. The company, which supplies natural gas through a pipeline network, had obtained permission to cut public roads for laying its pipelines and paid the associated restoration charges. Its refund application dated 26 February 2024 under Section 54 of the CGST Act was rejected on 6 May 2024; the first appellate authority upheld that rejection by Order-in-Appeal No. 438/GST/ADC/Alld/2025 dated 8 August 2025. The appellant contended that the charges compensated damage to public property and lacked the reciprocal supply necessary for taxation under Section 7(1). It relied on Bai Mamubai Trust v. Suchitra, service-tax decisions concerning compensation, and Circular No. 178/10/2022-GST. It alternatively claimed that road repair and maintenance related to the constitutional functions of Panchayats and Municipalities under Articles 243G and 243W, attracting the non-supply treatment under Notification No. 14/2017-Central Tax (Rate) or exemption under entries 4 and 5 of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate). It argued that neither an “original works” restriction nor a “noble cause” test appeared in the relevant provisions, and that repair remained a public function even when undertaken following a business entity’s request. The Tribunal held that the transaction arose from a voluntary, planned administrative arrangement, rather than a unilateral wrongful act, broken contract or award of civil damages. PWD performed a distinct physical repair service after the company obtained permission to excavate the road, with charges calculated by reference to the restoration work and excavation volume. That relationship established commercial reciprocity and consideration under Section 2(31), making the activity a supply of service under Section 7(1). The Tribunal therefore distinguished the compensation precedents and held that Circular No. 178/10/2022-GST did not exclude these fees from tax. On the exemption claim, the Tribunal distinguished routine road upkeep undertaken for the general public from targeted repairs performed for a company’s commercial pipeline project. It relied on the exclusion for services provided to business entities in entry 6(d) of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), and held that Notification No. 14/2017 did not protect the transaction in its particular commercial setting. Although road restoration generally forms part of road maintenance, the decisive factors were the recipient and the fee-for-service context, rather than whether the work was original construction or restoration. The broader interpretation of “in relation to” urged through Doypack Systems could not, in the Tribunal’s view, override the statutory restrictions; removing the lower authority’s expression “noble cause” would not alter the result. The Tribunal also rejected reliance on favourable refunds granted to the company’s registrations in Rajasthan and Punjab, holding that orders of proper officers in other States did not bind the Bench. The consistency argument associated with CIT v. Excel Industries Ltd. therefore failed. On alleged double taxation, it found no clear proof that PWD had issued a GST invoice or deposited tax under forward charge specifically for this road-cutting permit. It distinguished the authorities cited on that issue and held that the recipient’s liability under Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate) could not be displaced by an abstract claim of revenue neutrality or duplicate tax payment. The objections concerning a vague show cause notice, travel beyond the notice, absence of DIN, lack of independent reasoning and breach of natural justice also failed. The Tribunal found that the notice conveyed the factual and legal basis of the proposed rejection and that the appellant’s detailed reply dated 30 April 2024 showed its understanding of the case. It further relied on the authenticated electronic GST-portal workflow and the appellant’s participation to hold that absence of a physical DIN did not, by itself, invalidate these proceedings. Referring also to the Adani Total Gas ruling mentioned in the order, it upheld the RCM payment as valid, sustained rejection of the refund under Section 54 and dismissed Appeal No. LCK/40/2026. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF GSTAT LUCKNOW 1. Brief Facts of the Case. The present Appeal No. LCK/40/2026 has been filed against Order-in-Appeal No. 438/GST/ADC/Alld/2025 dated 08.08.2025 (Reference No. ZD090925047269S dated 04.09.2025) passed by the Additional Commissioner, (Appeal), CGST & Central Excise, Allahabad, whereby claimed refund of amounting to ₹7,264/- rejected by the proper officer under Section 54 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 was upheld. 1.1 M/s TORRENT GAS LIMITED (hereinafter referred to as the ” appellant “) having it principal place of business at 3RD FLOOR, HIG 1 B, SAI PLACE, CHANDRAVIHAR, LAKHANPUR, Kanpur Nagar, Uttar Pradesh, 208002. The Appellant is engaged in the business of supplying of natural gas through a gas pipeline network having GST registration number 09AAGCT7889P1Z1. 1.2 The Appellant received permission from the Public Works Department, Uttar Pradesh (PWD) to lay a gas pipeline network, which was subject to the payment of various charges for undertaking road restoration activities. The Appellant made a payment to the PWD during the month of October 2020. 1.3. The Appellant paid an amount equivalent to GST under the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) as notified by Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate), amounting to ₹7,264/- (CGST – ₹3,632/- and SGST – ₹3,632/-) in Form GSTR-3B filed for the period of October 2020. 1.4 The Appellant subsequently filed an application for a refund of the amount paid, equivalent to GST amounting to ₹7,264/-, in Form GST RFD-01 under Section 54 of the CGST Act on February 26, 2024, under Reference/Acknowledgment No. AA0902241945821. 1.5 A Show Cause Notice (SCN) in Form GST RFD-08 under Reference No. ZF0904204226897 was issued to the Appellant on 12 April 2024, requesting certain documents and clarifications. The Appellant filed a point-wise reply against the SCN on April 30, 2024. 1.6 The Adjudicating Authority issued Order-in-Original No. ZJ0905240092909 on 06.05.2024, rejecting the refund claim of ₹7,264/-. The Adjudicating Authority held that road was already been constructed for the use of general public and by constructing the road, Municipal Authorities/Panchayats have done the original work entrusted to them under Article 243G/Article 243W of the Constitution. On perusal of Eleventh and Twelfth schedule of the Constitution, it is evident that the work entrusted to Municipal Authorities/Panchayats is for noble cause largely for the community. While doing so the Government Authorities do not charge any consideration to any business entity. 1.7 In the instant case, the Appellant has requested the Government Authorities to open/dug out/cut the already constructed road not for any noble cause but only for furtherance of their business with sole motive to earn profit. Road restoration is required each time whenever road is opened/dug out/cut by the business entities for furtherance of their business. Such type of restoration work would not result in performing of the sovereign function as stipulated in Article 243W or 243G of the Constitution. In the instant case, the sovereign function has already been done by constructing the road by PWD. 1.8 Aggrieved by the aforesaid OIO, the Appellant filed an appeal in Form GST APL-01 under Section 107 of the Act on 05.08.2024, under Reference No AD090824009581Z. Accordingly, a personal hearing through virtual mode was attended by the authorized representative of the Appellant on 26.06.2025. 1.9 The Appellate Authority issued Order-in-Appeal No. 438/ GST / ADC / Alld / 2025 on 08.08.2025, upholding the rejection of the refund claim. The Appellate Authority sustained the rejection on the grounds that the road restoration work does not qualify for exemption under Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) or Notification No. 14/2017-Central Tax (Rate). It ruled that the Appellant is the recipient of the service of road restoration provided by the Government Authorities and is therefore liable to pay tax under the reverse charge mechanism as envisaged under Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate). A summary of the OIA was subsequently issued in Form GST APL-04 under Reference No. ZD090925047269S on 04.09.2025. 2. Grounds of appeal 2.1 The appellant has challenged the Order-in-Appeal dated 06.08.2025, contending that the order in appeal is against the law, without the weight of evidence and is totally unsustainable. 2.2 In his appeal, the appellant contests the refund rejected by OIA on various grounds which is as under; 2.3 ‘Road restoration charges’ being compensatory in nature, cannot be considered being towards any ‘supply’ under GST: In order to constitute a ‘supply’ under Section 7(1) of the CGST Act, there should be a supply of goods or services for consideration. The ‘road restoration charges’ recovered by PWD are not towards any ‘supply’ of goods or services but towards the compensation for the damage incurred on public property, i.e. roads. The payment of ‘road restoration charges’ is a condition and not a consideration for any service, as supported by the judgment of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in Bai Mamubai Trust v. Suchitra, which held that wrongful unilateral acts resulting in the payment of damages do not encompass the doctrine of supply. Furthermore, Circular No. 178/10/2022-GST clarifies that GST shall not be levied on liquidated damages intended solely to compensate for injury, loss, or damage. In his claim, the appellant cited the case laws viz. Bai Mamubai Trust v. Suchitra wd/o. Sadhu Koraga Shetty [TS-736-HC-2019(BOM)-NT] (Hon’ble Bombay High Court), South Eastern Coalfields Ltd. v. Similarly, entries 4 and 5 of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) exempt services by a governmental authority in relation to these functions. Road maintenance, repair, and communication are covered under Entry 13 of the Eleventh Schedule and Entry 4 of the Twelfth Schedule of the Constitution. The expression ‘in relation to’ expands the scope to include ancillary functions such as road restoration. In his claim, the appellant cited the case laws viz. Doypack Systems (P.) The broad expression ‘in relation to’ encompasses all and any activity relating to the function of maintaining roads with the objective of keeping them in good condition for public welfare. The ‘Road Restoration Charges’ are not liquidated damages or unilateral compensation as claimed by the Appellant. It is a calculated fee paid under a planned administrative arrangement where the PWD/NHAI performs a custom physical asset-repair service to restore the dug-up roads to their baseline state. Hence, it constitutes a taxable ‘Supply of Service’ under Section 7(1) read with Section 2(31) of the CGST Act, 2017. 3.3. While routine road upkeep for the general public is a sovereign function under Articles 243G and 243W of the Constitution, custom request-driven road cutting executed specifically to facilitate a private entity’s profit-driven business rollout loses all sovereign tax immunity. 3.4. The transaction is entirely hit by the restrictive proviso of Sl. No. 6, Point (d) of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), which categorically excludes any service provided by a Government or Local Authority to a ‘Business Entity’ from general tax exemptions. Proviso is as below; 6 Chapter 99 Services by the Central Government, State Government, Union territory or local authority excluding the following services— (a) services by the Department of Posts by way of speed post, express parcel post, life insurance, and agency services provided to a person other than the Central Government, State Government, Union territory; (b) services in relation to an aircraft or a vessel, inside or outside the precincts of a port or an airport; (c) transport of goods or passengers; or (d) any service, other than services covered under entries (a) to (c) above, provided to business entities. Nil Nil 3.5. Since the transaction is a taxable supply provided by the Government to a registered business entity, the statutory burden to discharge GST under the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) rests strictly on the recipient company as per Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate). Proviso is as below; 5 Services supplied by the Central Government, State Government, Union territory or local authority to a business entity excluding, – (1) renting of immovable property, and (2) services specified below- (i) services by the Department of Posts by way of speed post, express parcel post, life insurance, and agency services provided to a person other than Central Government, State Government or Union territory or local authority; (ii) services in relation to an aircraft or a vessel, inside or outside the precincts of a port or an airport; (iii) transport of goods or passengers. Central Government, State Government, Union territory or local authority Any business entity located in the taxable territory. 3.6. The entire refund workflow was executed through the authenticated, electronic GST portal where all notices (RFD-08), replies, and orders (OIO) are digitally logged. The complete participation of the Appellant leaves no room for any breach of natural justice. 3.7. In view of the above, the Respondent prays that the Order-in-Appeal No. 438/GST/ADC/AIld/2025 dated 08.08.2025 may kindly be upheld, and the present appeal may be dismissed. 4. Findings and Discussion 4.1. We have carefully examined the impugned Order-in-Appeal No. 438/GST/ADC/ALLD/2025 dated 08.08.2025, the grounds raised in the present appeal, the order dated 06.05.2024 passed by the Assistant Commissioner, CGST & Central Excise, Division-III, Kanpur, and the judicial precedents relied upon by the appellant and the material available on record. 4.2. Now we take up the matter for discussion and find that the Appellant argues that Section 9 of the CGST Act requires a “supply” for consideration. Appellant contend that road restoration fees paid to the Public Works Department (PWD) are purely compensatory damages to repair public roads, lacking the contractual reciprocity required to constitute a supply. Appellant rely on the Hon’ble Bombay High Court ruling in Bai Mamubai Trust v. Suchitra and Circular No. 178/10/2022-GST dated 03.08.2022 to state that liquidated damages are not taxable. In this regard, we find this argument legally flawed. Section 2(31) of the CGST Act, 2017 defines “consideration” broadly, encompassing any payment made “in respect of, in response to, or for the inducement of, the supply of services.” Here, the transaction is completely distinct from a unilateral wrongful act or an award of post-injury civil damages as seen in Bai Mamubai Trust. The Appellant entered a planned administrative arrangement where they voluntarily applied to the PWD for permission to cut open constructed public roads. The PWD performs a distinct, physical asset repair service to return the dug-up roads to their public baseline state, explicitly calculating the fee based on the structural volume of the work required. This establishes an unassailable element of bilateral transactional reciprocity. The payment is not passive compensation for a contractual breach under Circular No. 178/10/2022-GST dated 03.08.2022; it is a direct fee for service. 4.3. The appellant’s reliance on various case laws as quoted in his grounds of appeal is completely misplaced and factually distinguishable. Those rulings only apply when there is a broken contract or a court order, and no real service is given. In the present matrix, there is no breach of contract. Rather, it is a voluntary and prior planned administrative arrangement. The taxpayer appliesfor a specialized utility permit to execute road cutting, and the PWD/Local Authority performs a positive, specific infrastructure restoration activity on a fee-for-service basis. The recovery of charges calculated on the basis of physical excavation volume establishes direct commercial reciprocity, falling squarely within the ambit of ‘Consideration’ under Section 2(31) and ‘Supply of Service’ under Section 7(1) of the CGST Act, 2017. 4.4. Further, we find that the Appellant contend that roads and bridges are subjects listed under the Eleventh Schedule (Entry 13) and Twelfth Schedule (Entry 4) of the Constitution. Therefore, they argue that under Section 7(2) (b) and Notification No. 14/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017, such activities performed by public authorities cannot be treated as a supply: They further claim exemption under Entry 4 and 5 of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate). We agree that the routine upkeep of public roads is an architectural function of local government. However, Notification No. 14/2017-Central Tax (Rate) excludes activities only when the government is engaged as a public authority performing sovereign duties for the collective populace. When the PWD fixes standard potholes out of public funds, it acts as a sovereign authority. In this matrix, the PWD executed custom, request-driven physical patch repairs directly connected to a private commercial entity’s business pipeline rollout. When a government department executes targeted physical tasks for a commercial corporation to facilitate its business layout, it steps out of its generic sovereign capacity. Therefore, the ‘statutory shield of a “non-supply” under Article 243G/243W or general public exemption entries cannot be legally claimed by the Appellant. 4.4.1. The appellant’s reliance on Doypack Systems (P.) Ltd. vs. Union of India, as well as other cited precedents, is entirely misplaced and untenable. The principle of expansive interpretation laid down in Doypack Systems (P.) Ltd. vs. Union of India cannot be stretched to override the explicit statutory provisos of the GST exemption schedules. While the phrase ‘in relation to’ is broad, it is a settled principle of fiscal interpretation that an exemption notification must be construed strictly according to its text. The functions under Article 243G and 243W are intended for public welfare activities provided to the common populace. In this case, the service is custom-delivered to a specific corporate entity to facilitate its commercial pipeline layout. An expansive interpretation cannot be used to introduce an immunity that the legislature explicitly restricted under the statutory framework. 4.5. The Appellant argues that the phrase “in relation to” used in Notification No. 12/2017 and 14/2017 has an expansive meaning, as held in Doypack Systems (P.) Ltd. v. Union of India. Appellant argued the lower authorities erred by narrowing the exemption exclusively to the “original construction” of public roads. In this regard, we find that the core reason for taxability is not determined by whether the engineering work is “original” or “restored.” The taxability is governed by the specific nature of the recipient and the transactional context. As settled under fiscal interpretation norms, terms like “in relation to” cannot be stretched to defeat the explicit legislative proviso of a tax schedule. Although road restoration is generally part of road maintenance, this repair was specifically carried out for a business on a fee-for-service basis. Therefore, it falls under the tax net and does not qualify as a normal public utility exemption. 4.6. The Appellant contests the Adjudicating Authority’s use of the subjective term “noble cause” to deny the exemption, stating that constitutional entries governing public roads do not contain any qualifying administrative words such as “noble cause”. In this regard, we find that while the phrasing “noble cause” used by the lower authorities is an interpretive expression, its functional judicial meaning points directly to a “public interest utility” vs. a “private commercial layout”. The statutory entries under the Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules are designed to protect pro bono public services provided by the state to the community without a target business consideration. Since the Appellant paid the charges to cut public property for private profit distribution, the underlying object is purely transactional and business-driven. Therefore, removing the subjective phrase “noble cause” does not change the taxable nature of the transaction. 4.7. The Appellant states that the GST framework contains no clauses that alter the tax position of a government activity simply because it was executed upon the individual proposal or application of a business entity. In this regard, we find that the GST law explicitly differentiates between services provided by the government to common citizens versus services provided to commercial entities. Sl. No. 6, Point (d) of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) explicitly establishes that any service provided by the Central Government, State Government, or a local authority to a business entity is completely excluded from general tax immunity. 4.8. The Appellant highlights that under completely identical facts, its parallel registrations under the state jurisdictions of Rajasthan and Punjab were granted full refunds by their proper officers, relying on CIT vs. Excel Industries Ltd. along with other case laws to argue against an inconsistent approach by the department; however, we find this reliance completely misplaced and unsustainable in the present cross-jurisdictional context. It is a well-settled principle of judicial review and a recognized judicial norm that a summary refund sanction order passed by an original ‘Proper Officer’ in one provincial state jurisdiction does not operate as a binding legal precedent or res judicata over an independent legal framework or a higher Appellate Authority of another state. If an administrative officer in a parallel state has allowed a refund by overlooking the restrictive statutory criteria and parameters of SI. No. 6 of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), such an erroneous order possesses no binding value over this Bench, which remains strictly bound to enforce and apply the law as textually enacted by the legislature. 4.9. The Appellant submits that if a tax has been deposited once into the government treasury for a continuous transaction, forcing a service recipient to pay tax via RCM creates an unlawful dual tax incidence, relying on Elkos Pens Limited and Navyug Alloys Pvt. Ltd.; however, we find that this argument and their reliance on these judgments are factually and contextually misplaced. There is no clear, unequivocal proof on record to demonstrate that the Public Works Department (PWD) raised a standard or formal GST tax invoice under the Forward Charge Mechanism (FCM), or deposited a parallel tax entry into the exchequer specifically for this individual road cutting permit. Furthermore, under the strict textual mandate of Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate), any taxable service supplied by the Government or a Local Authority to a registered business entity must strictly be discharged via the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) by the recipient. Because the law explicitly mandates this specific mechanism, the statutory framework enacted by the legislature cannot be bypassed or diluted, and the Appellant cannot escape RCM compliance by raising abstract claims of revenue neutrality or double tax incidence. 4.10. The Appellant contends that the absence of a Document Identification Number (DIN) on the initial notice and the Order-in-Original (OIO) invalidates the proceedings, while also raising technical objections invoking Saci Allied Products Ltd. and CCE vs. Brindavan Beverages regarding the alleged vagueness of the Show Cause Notice (SCN) and breach of natural justice. In this regard, we find these contentions unsustainable. The record indicates that the SCN clearly detailed the factual and legal dynamics surrounding the non-applicability of Notification No. 14/2017 to request-driven commercial road cutting, and the Appellant fully understood the department’s inquiry, as evidenced by their exhaustive, point-wise legal response filed on 30.04.2024, leaving no room for a plea of surprise or prejudice. Furthermore, concerning the omission of a physical DIN on the lower authority’s notice and OIO, we find that the entire refund and dispute workflow was systematically processed, logged, transacted, and made available through the prescribed, authenticated, and official electronic GST portal. Since the application, issuance of notice, submission of replies, processing of the claim, and passing of the final order are all part of an integrated electronic process, there remains no doubt regarding the identity, source, integrity, or authenticity of the documents issued by the proper officer. Because the taxpayer fully participated in these transparent digital portal proceedings, the mere technical absence of a physical DIN does not by itself invalidate the statutory adjudication, violate the principles of natural justice, or extinguish the underlying tax liability. 4.11. We find that the lower authority accurately applied Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017. When a government department like PWD provides services or infrastructure permissions to a registered business entity for consideration, the statutory burden to pay GST is strictly placed on the recipient company under RCM. The Adjudicating authority correctly relied on the established legal parameters of identical disputes, such as the Assistant/Deputy Commissioner, CGST, Division-VII, Ahmedabad, North Commissionerate vs M/s Adani Total Gas Limited ruling, to hold that such request-driven commercial operations are taxable. Furthermore, the case laws cited by the appellant lack legal force as they are factually distinguishable from the situation in this case. Here, the appellant is a private/public limited company that caused damage to the public road for the furtherance of its business and paid consideration to the government authorities in lieu of the services provided by them. Consequently, these charges paid to the PWD and NHAI are not covered under the exemption Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) or Notification No. 14/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017 issued by the CBIC, New Delhi. Therefore, the GST amount of Rs. 7,264/- paid by the Appellant under RCM via Form GSTR-3B return for October 2020 was a valid statutory payment. The First Appellate Authority committed no error in confirming the rejection of the refund application under Section 54 of CGST Act 2017. In view of the above, we find that the OIA is fully sustainable, contains no loopholes, and requires no judicial interference from this Bench. Order In view of the foregoing discussion and findings, the Appeal No. LCK/40/2026 filed by the Appellant (M/s Torrent Gas Limited) is hereby dismissed. The Order-in-Appeal No. 438/GST/ADC/Alld/2025 dated 08.08.2025, is upheld.
