Articles 2 min read India's Q2 GDP growth seen at 7.6 pc led by investments: Ind-Ra PTI Published On Nov 26, 2026 at 05:47 PM IST var news_msid = 136191558; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook New Delhi, The Indian economy is likely to grow 7.6 per cent in the second quarter of the current fiscal year, driven primarily by investment growth, India Ratings and Research (Ind-Ra) said on Thursday.The September quarter growth in FY27 compares with 8.1 per cent expansion recorded in Q2 of FY26 and 7.8 per cent rise in the April-June quarter.Ind-Ra also raised India's GDP growth estimate for full FY27 to 7.2 per cent, up from 7.1 per cent earlier. Indian economy expanded 7.8 per cent in FY26.The current fiscal year will mark the sixth consecutive year of over 7 per cent growth, potentially reaching 7.8 per cent on average during FY22-27, said Ind-Ra Chief Economist Devendra Kumar Pant. Advt "Investment remains the main growth driver (average growth FY22-FY27: 10 per cent), supported by private consumption (7.7 per cent), with investment growth outpacing consumption growth," Pant added.The government is slated to release GDP growth data for July-September on November 30.Last month, the RBI forecast a 7.2 per cent growth for the second quarter, and the finance ministry's Monthly Economic Review sees growth at 7.35 per cent.The RBI has projected a 7.1 per cent GDP growth for the full 2026-27 fiscal year.Ind-Ra said investment would be the growth driver in Q2, with gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) estimated to have grown 10.5 per cent year-on-year in Q2 FY27.Both production and import investment indicators remained strong in Q2 FY27.The agency estimated stable consumption growth in Q2, with private final consumption expenditure (PFCE) slowing down marginally to 6.8 per cent in Q2 FY27 (Q1 FY27: 7.1 per cent).Consumption indicators such as GST collection, credit growth, automobile production, and durable goods production point to stable consumption.However, negative growth in indicators such as consumer non-durable production, consumption of petroleum products, and negative real wage growth of agricultural labour and urban minimum wages (government-notified wage in construction activities) are burdening consumption. Advt "The consumption outlook for 2H FY27 is likely to come under some pressure due to fading GST impact, higher interest rate/monetary tightening, and El-Nino impact on agriculture," Ind-Ra said.
