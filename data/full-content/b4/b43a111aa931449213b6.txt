Articles 4 min read RBI gives Fairfax two years to decide on CSB Bank; merger with IDBI or stake sale: Prem Watsa RBI has given Fairfax two years to decide on the future of CSB Bank, with a merger with IDBI Bank or a stake sale on the table, Fairfax Chairman Prem Watsa said. Watsa, who has backed India for over three decades, said banking could be the next-best investment after real estate as an economy undergoes rapid growth. Shrishti Sharma & Amol Dethe ETBFSI Published On Oct 15, 2026 at 07:28 PM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 134969822; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Canadian billionaire Prem Watsa-led Fairfax Financial Holdings has two years to decide whether to merge its existing banking asset CSB Bank with IDBI Bank or exit its stake in the lender, following its proposed acquisition of a 60.72 per cent stake in IDBI Bank.Fairfax currently holds around 40 per cent in CSB Bank. Watsa, speaking on a global conference call following the government's approval of Fairfax's IDBI acquisition, said the group has two options for CSB — merge the lender with IDBI Bank or sell its stake, and will take the decision within the two-year window.“We have two licenses, and the RBI has given us two years to make a decision as to what we do. So, we're going to look at all of that, and we'll make a decision at the time. And we'll let you know when we make the decision,” Watsa said during a global conference call following Fairfax's approval to acquire a 60.72 per cent stake in IDBI Bank. Advt Asked specifically about CSB Bank, Watsa said Fairfax has two options. “Merge or sell? Those are the two options we have. Merge or sell? We've got two years to make the decision. So we'll think through all of that very carefully,” he said.The issue stems from Fairfax now having two banking licences in India following its acquisition of IDBI Bank. RBI's ownership framework requires promoter holdings in a banking company to eventually come down to 26 per cent after 15 years, while higher holdings can continue during the interim period subject to licensing conditions and an RBI-approved dilution plan.Fairfax's decision comes after its nearly eight-year association with CSB Bank, which began with its investment in the erstwhile Catholic Syrian Bank in 2018. The lender has since significantly expanded its balance sheet, with deposits rising from Rs 14,691 crore in FY18 to Rs 36,861 crore in FY25, while net advances increased from Rs 9,337 crore to Rs 31,842 crore during the same period.The bank's deposits have therefore grown around 2.5 times since Fairfax's entry, while net advances have expanded more than threefold. CSB has also moved from a loss-making position in FY18 to reporting a net profit of Rs 593.8 crore in FY25. Advt Watsa's comments on CSB come as Fairfax prepares to build a larger financial-services presence in India through IDBI Bank, Go Digit and IIFL Capital. Fairfax plans to acquire 60.72 per cent of IDBI Bank from the government and LIC, and has proposed ICICI veteran NS Kannan as chairman of the bank, subject to RBI approval, while incumbent CEO Rakesh Sharma is expected to continue in his role.Watsa said Fairfax's decision on CSB will be guided by its broader conviction that banking remains a compelling long-term investment in India.“India is perhaps the best country to invest,” Watsa said, pointing to Fairfax's more than three decades of investments in the country. Advt “What people forget is India, by the end of this year, and the Commerce Minister mentioned this in Toronto, there's going to be free trade agreements. Free trade agreements with Europe, with the UK, with Japan, and on and on and on. Canada is almost done also. So what does that mean? That means you can manufacture in India at a very low cost and sell it all over the world,” he said.“On the other hand, companies can also sell into India. So our thinking is that in the next 10 years, India is going to have unbelievable growth because of that opening up the economy,” Watsa said.He said Fairfax sees the country's growth translating into a much larger opportunity for banks and other financial-services companies. Advt “The big opportunity is the growth in an emerging market. An emerging market goes from USD 3–4 trillion in GDP to USD 31 trillion in 20 years. You just think of what happens to the middle class, which I would define as 250–300 million people. That’s like the population of the United States, and it doubles in 10 years. Imagine the bank accounts, the insurance that’s needed, the housing that’s needed. I mean, it’s just unbelievable — every product that you can think of,” Watsa said.He also pointed to the potential for IDBI to become a significantly larger banking franchise as India's economy expands.“The growth possibility in India, we think, could shock everybody,” Watsa said, while pointing out that IDBI had moved from being a government-owned bank to a private-sector bank.
