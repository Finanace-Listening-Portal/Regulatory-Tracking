Photo: X @KajariaCeramicRam Prasad Sahu Mumbai 3 min read Last Updated : Sep 30 2026 | 7:28 PM IST @media (min-width:1100px) and (max-width:1280px) { .storymeta .meta-info { flex: 0 0 45%; } } Listen to This ArticleStrong growth guidance for 2026-27 (FY27), a revival in the project segment, and ongoing capital expenditure are positive for the country’s largest manufacturer of ceramic and vitrified floor and wall tiles, Kajaria Ceramics. While volumes were subdued in the first quarter (April-June/Q1) of FY27, the company expects a recovery, with margins expected to sustain at current levels. Though higher gas costs and supply constraints on account of the Iran war hit the entire tile industry, the impact has been greater on smaller, unorganised players. This has helped branded and organised players bridge the price gap with the unorganised sector and gain market share. Better prospects for the second half (H2) of FY27 led to upgrades and earnings revisions for Kajaria. The stock is trading at 29x its 2027-28 (FY28) earnings estimates and at a premium to its smaller listed peers. The company’s revenue in Q1 rose 20 per cent despite subdued tile volume growth of 6 per cent, with production affected by issues related to gas availability. Sales growth was largely driven by better realisations, with tile realisations rising 11 per cent and bathware realisations 16 per cent. Operating profit surged 39 per cent, while net profit rose 56 per cent year-on-year (Y-o-Y), well ahead of estimates. Operating profit margin expanded 266 basis points Y-o-Y to 19.6 per cent, compared with 19.2 per cent in the fourth quarter (January-March/Q4) of 2025-26, due to steady employee costs and other expenses. The management is eyeing more than 20 per cent growth in tile revenue, driven equally by volume and value growth. While Q1 saw subdued volume growth, the company expects healthy signs of recovery in H2FY27. Operating profit margin is expected to sustain itself in the 18-19 per cent range. The company has announced two brownfield expansions at Srikalahasti in Andhra Pradesh and Gailpur in Rajasthan, which should boost overall capacity. The management also highlighted its increased focus on the project segment and said it had received orders from two large projects during the quarter. Following a strong Q1 and robust guidance, Motilal Oswal Research has raised its earnings estimates by 18 per cent for FY27 and 10 per cent for FY28 on account of better realisations and margin estimates. Expecting a recovery in sales volumes, high margins, and strong cash flows, analyst Ashish Poddar of the brokerage has retained a ‘buy’ rating on the company and revised its target price to ₹1,500. A strong comeback by Morbi, Gujarat, the ceramic capital of India, could intensify competition and crimp Kajaria’s financial performance. This remains the downside risk to his call. While there were supply issues earlier, the availability of propane gas in Morbi has improved materially, with most factories now operational, easing supply constraints. Exports remain subdued amid elevated freight costs. While some manufacturers have cut prices and passed on the benefit of lower propane prices, branded players continue to see resilient volumes and have largely retained their 14-16 per cent price hikes. 360 One Capital Research prefers Kajaria within the tile segment and expects a strong upcoming third quarter (October-December/Q3). After several quarters, the management has become more confident about growth, guiding for double-digit volume growth. While valuations remain premium at 29x FY28 earnings per share estimates, analysts Amit Srivastava and Varun Julasaria of the brokerage believe the earnings recovery and improving growth trajectory can sustain the premium valuation.
