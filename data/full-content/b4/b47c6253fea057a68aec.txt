The brokerage expects the proposed equity capital raise to provide a benefit to NIM and has raised its FY27 and FY28 earnings estimates by about 1% and 4%, respectively. Advt Bajaj Finance reported a 29% year-on-year rise in second-quarter profit after tax to about Rs6,370 crore, while net interest income rose 24% to about Rs13,330 crore. Non-interest income increased 11% to about Rs2,640 crore, while operating expenses rose 23% to about Rs5,270 crore. Pre-provision operating profit increased 21% to Rs10,700 crore.The lender’s assets under management grew 26.5% year on year and 6.9% sequentially to Rs5.85 lakh crore, prompting it to raise its FY27 AUM growth guidance to 26-27%. Growth was broad based, with gold loans rising 14% sequentially, rural sales finance 7%, consumer sales finance about 6%, loans against securities about 15%, microfinance about 8% and car loans 6%. The company retained its long-term AUM growth guidance of 23-25%.Credit costs remained broadly stable at an annualised 1.56% in the quarter, compared with 1.54% in the previous quarter and 2.05% a year earlier. The quarter included a prudent management overlay of Rs340 crore for macroeconomic risks. Excluding the overlay, credit costs declined 12% year on year to Rs1,830 crore, translating into about 1.32%.Asset quality saw a minor deterioration. Gross NPA rose about 10 basis points sequentially to 1.06%, while net NPA increased about 3 basis points to 0.42%. Stage 2 and Stage 3 assets increased by about Rs1,600 crore during the quarter, including a Rs1,050 crore increase in Stage 2 gold loans linked to short-tenor schemes that are no longer being offered. Advt The company does not expect the transitional increase in Stage 2 gold loans to affect credit costs. It discontinued the short-tenor schemes in August and is reverting to longer-tenor products of 12 months, with options for monthly or quarterly interest payments. About 55% of the elevated Stage 2 balance has already been resolved, with no material credit cost or auction impact expected.Bajaj Finance is closely monitoring early-vintage performance and tightening originations where three-month-on-book metrics indicate emerging stress. Self-employed unsecured borrowers are viewed as more vulnerable to inflation and elevated oil prices, with stress testing prompting additional Stage 1 and Stage 2 provisions for non-salaried unsecured borrowers amid risks from El Niño, monsoons and oil prices. Advt Growth prospectsThe company expects MSME growth, which has lagged other segments, to recover to double-digit levels in the second half of FY27. It targets 60-62 million new loans and 18-20 million customer additions to its franchise in FY27, after acquiring about 4.4 million new customers in the second quarter.The proposed Rs17,500 crore equity capital raise, which is expected to be completed by December 2026, is expected to provide a benefit to NIM and support the company as organic margins come under pressure. The company is also evaluating options to mitigate the impact of proposed changes in insurance commissions on fee income and margins. Advt Bajaj Finance has submitted its feedback on the RBI’s proposed restrictions on revolving credit and expects a balanced framework. Management said a complete ban could temporarily affect AUM, NIM and profitability.The combination of strong loan growth, operating efficiencies, improving asset quality and declining credit costs is expected to support a stronger earnings trajectory despite margin pressure. The brokerage expects Bajaj Finance’s PAT to compound at about 30% annually between FY26 and FY28, with return on assets and return on equity reaching 4.1% and 20%, respectively, in FY28. It retained its buy rating with a target price of Rs1,250, based on four times September 2028 estimated book value per share.
