The pressure comes even as the insurer expects premium growth to sustain momentum in the second half of FY27, with health insurance remaining its biggest growth driveThe insurer’s underwriting loss widened to Rs 320 crore in the second quarter of FY27 from Rs 180 crore a year earlier. The combined ratio rose from 105.1% to 107%, against the brokerage’s estimate of 104.8%. Excluding catastrophe losses of Rs 118 crore, the ratio stood at 105.3%, according to Motilal Oswal Financial Services. Advt The overall claims ratio increased to 72.7% from 72.1% a year earlier, while the commission ratio rose to 20.4% from 19.1%. The expense ratio declined by 10 basis points to 13.9%, but this improvement was insufficient to offset the pressure from claims and commissions. Higher catastrophe frequency contributed to the increase in the claims ratio, while commission costs rose with strong retail business growth.Health insurance, including personal accident cover, remained the principal driver of premium growth, with net earned premiums in the segment rising 48% year on year. Overall net earned premiums increased 17% to Rs 6,650 crore, supported by 9% growth in motor and 22% growth in marine insurance. Fire insurance premiums declined 30%.The health claims trend will be a key monitorable as the insurer seeks to sustain growth in the second half of FY27. Management intends to maintain the group health loss ratio in the mid-90s and the retail indemnity loss ratio at 65-70%. It also aims to keep the overall motor loss ratio at 65-67%. The brokerage expects net earned premium growth to sustain momentum in the second half, with gross premium growth ahead of the industry.Motor insuranceMotor insurance performance remains mixed. The motor own-damage loss ratio deteriorated by 210 basis points year on year, while the motor third-party loss ratio improved by 930 basis points. Any subsequent reserving or reserve release following the Supreme Court judgment remains a key monitorable. The final distribution regulations could also materially affect growth if implemented as proposed. Advt Net profit rose 15% year on year to Rs 950 crore, beating the brokerage’s estimate by 35%, as stronger investment income offset weak underwriting performance. Investment income from policyholders’ accounts and shareholders’ accounts was each 31% above estimates. The quarterly investment yield increased to 2.47% from 2.19% a year earlier on an investment book of Rs 64,300 crore.The brokerage raised its profit estimates for FY27 and FY28 by 7% and 5%, respectively, following the stronger investment performance, while retaining its Neutral rating and a target price of Rs 1,730. Claims experience, commission costs and the ability to sustain premium growth will remain key factors for underwriting performance. Third-party premium rate adjustments, IFRS implementation and risk-based solvency requirements are additional monitorables.
