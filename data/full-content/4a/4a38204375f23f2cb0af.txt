Summary: MCA General Circular No. 05/2026 dated 24 October 2026 permits specified FY 2025-26 financial-statement forms to be filed up to 31 December 2026 and annual-return forms up to 31 January 2027 without additional fees. The relief is form-specific and year-specific: it does not extend the statutory time for holding an annual general meeting, cure an AGM default, cover an unlisted e-form, or reset the original statutory filing date. A company should therefore preserve its original due-date computation, choose the correct AOC-4 and MGT-7 variant, complete the accounts and annual return on the correct corporate record, and treat the circular as a temporary additional-fee window rather than a general compliance amnesty. Paragraph 2 creates two defined windows. Financial statements in the listed AOC-4 family may be filed up to 31 December 2026, while annual returns in MGT-7 or MGT-7A may be filed up to 31 January 2027. Within the relevant window, the circular removes additional fees. It does not say that normal filing fees are waived, and it does not place every company-law form inside the relief. Section 137 generally links filing of adopted financial statements to the AGM, while section 92 governs the annual return. The circular permits filing during a specified period without additional fees; it does not rewrite those statutory triggers. This distinction matters if the filing is later examined, if a linked form depends on the original event date, or if the company misses the circular’s outer date. The AOC-4 window: form selection still determines a valid filing The financial-statement relief covers five specified forms for FY 2025-26. A stand-alone company that is not in the XBRL or NBFC Ind AS class does not choose a different form merely because more time is available. A company with subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures must separately determine whether consolidated financial statements and AOC-4 (CFS), or the relevant XBRL or NBFC Ind AS variant, are required. The circular grants time; it does not alter accounting applicability, consolidation requirements, signing, audit, Board approval or adoption. Preserve the chain from approved accounts to uploaded form The final filing pack should trace the figures in the webform and attachments to the Board-approved and, where applicable, AGM-adopted financial statements. Reconcile the balance sheet, statement of profit and loss, cash-flow statement, audit qualifications, related-party disclosures, CSR information and consolidated figures before upload. If a revised set of financial statements is legally required, do not overwrite signed records informally; obtain company-specific advice on the statutory route. The MGT-7 and MGT-7A window: use the correct company classification The circular places MGT-7 and MGT-7A within the no-additional-fee window up to 31 January 2027 for FY 2025-26. The choice between them is not elective. MGT-7A is the abridged annual return for the class for which the governing rules prescribe it, including an eligible one person company or small company. Classification must be tested for the relevant financial year and filing date against the current Companies Act definitions and rules, including exclusions from the small-company definition. A company that no longer qualifies should not use the abridged form merely because it used that form in an earlier year. Reconcile the annual return to statutory registers and the financial statements Before filing, reconcile members, share capital, transfers, beneficial ownership records, indebtedness, directors and key managerial personnel, meetings, remuneration, penalties, compounding and certification data. The closing share capital and turnover information should agree with the approved financial statements and the company’s register of members. Differences should be explained and corrected at source rather than hidden in the filing. Preserve the final generated form, DSC validation, payment record and service request number. AGM obligations remain outside the filing relief Paragraph 3 of the circular expressly says that it must not be construed as extending the statutory time for holding annual general meetings. A company that failed to hold its AGM within the applicable section 96 timeline therefore cannot treat the AOC-4 or MGT-7 filing window as retrospective meeting relief. The factual position must be recorded accurately: the date on which the meeting was held, any Registrar extension obtained before expiry where legally available, and any default that requires separate action. Meeting minutes and filing dates answer different legal questions The AGM record proves convening, quorum, business transacted and adoption. The ROC filing acknowledgement proves submission of a form. One does not replace the other. If accounts were not adopted, the company should examine the special filing treatment prescribed by section 137 and the form instructions rather than entering an assumed adoption date. Similarly, payment of a filing fee does not by itself regularise a meeting default or inaccurate disclosure. Forms and periods that should not be pulled into the circular Circular 05/2026 names only the FY 2025-26 AOC-4 family and MGT-7/MGT-7A. It should not be used as authority for LLP Form 8, MSME Form I, ADT-1, DIR-12, MGT-14, PAS-6, CRA-2, beneficial-ownership filings, event-based forms, earlier financial years or a different statutory return. CRA-4 obtained separate relief under a later circular and must be assessed under that instrument. Treating the two circulars as a single universal waiver would obscure the different form lists and legal conditions. Build a relief register rather than changing every due date A defensible tracker keeps the original statutory due date, the circular relied upon, the last relief date, normal fee, additional-fee treatment, filing status and evidence link in separate fields. This prevents the compliance calendar from being rewritten as though the original due date never existed. It also makes it clear which items remain urgent because they are outside the circular. What happens after 31 December 2026 or 31 January 2027 Paragraph 4 provides the critical consequence. A filing made after the circular’s applicable window attracts all fees, including additional fees, under the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014 from the date on which that filing was actually due under the Act. The additional-fee clock is therefore not stated to begin on 1 January 2027 for AOC-4 or 1 February 2027 for MGT-7. A company planning to use the relief should retain enough time for form preparation, professional certification where required, DSC association, payment and resubmission. File before the outer date with a contingency period for resubmission, then preserve the SRN, challan, acknowledgement and final form. Frequently Asked Questions on MCA Circular 05/2026 1. Is 31 December 2026 a new AGM deadline? No. The circular expressly denies an extension of the statutory time for holding the AGM. It provides an additional-fee relief window for specified financial-statement filings. 2. Are normal filing fees also waived? The circular states that the listed forms may be filed without additional fees. It does not state that the normal filing fee is waived. 3. Can an eligible small company use MGT-7 instead of MGT-7A? The correct form must be determined under the current Act and rules. The relief window does not convert form selection into an option; classification and exclusions must be checked for the company. 4. Does the relief cover an AOC-4 for an earlier financial year? No general earlier-year relief appears in the circular. Its express scope is the listed financial-statement and annual-return forms for FY 2025-26. 5. Is MSME Form I covered up to 31 January 2027? No. MSME Form I is not one of the forms named in General Circular 05/2026. Its own law and any separate official relief must be checked. 6. From which date is additional fee counted if the outer date is missed? Paragraph 4 says applicable fees, including additional fees, arise from the date on which the filing was actually due under the Act, not merely from the end of the circular’s relief window. 7. Does an SRN prove that filing is complete? Not necessarily. Confirm payment, submission status, DSC validation and any resubmission or rejection. Preserve the final accepted form and acknowledgement. Key Takeaways AOC-4 variants named in Circular 05/2026 may be filed for FY 2025-26 up to 31 December 2026 without additional fees. MGT-7 and MGT-7A may be filed for FY 2025-26 up to 31 January 2027 without additional fees. The circular does not extend AGM time, waive normal fees, cure defaults or cover unlisted forms. Keep the original statutory due date in the compliance register because post-window fees run from that date.
