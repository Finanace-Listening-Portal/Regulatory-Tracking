Articles 4 min read Embedded finance can widen credit access, but housing loans will remain high-touch: NBFC leaders Digital distribution, vernacular technology and co-lending partnerships are creating new opportunities for NBFCs to expand credit access. However, executives at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026 said housing finance will continue to require human engagement, while better land records, property valuation and technology integration remain critical to scaling lending. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Updated On Oct 9, 2026 at 08:27 PM IST var news_msid = 134836901; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Embedded finance and digital distribution are opening new avenues for India’s lending industry to reach underserved borrowers, particularly in smaller towns. However, while technology can simplify onboarding and accelerate credit processing, complex products such as housing finance will continue to depend on customer trust, reliable property records and human engagement.Industry leaders at a trialogue chat at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026, moderated by Sneha Jha, Editor – Special Projects, ETCIO, said the next phase of lending growth would depend on expanding digital distribution without compromising underwriting discipline, customer experience and governance. Advt Digital distribution must balance scale with customer accessFor IIFL Finance, expanding retail credit requires a combination of physical distribution, technology investment and standardised lending processes.Mayank Sharma, President, IIFL Finance, said technology has become essential to delivering consistent customer experiences across metropolitan cities and smaller towns. However, digital distribution is not merely about increasing the number of platforms through which financial products are offered. Lenders must also ensure that credit checks, regulatory controls and monitoring remain consistent across customer journeys.IIFL Finance combines local employees, vernacular-language interfaces and simplified onboarding to reach customers with limited familiarity with formal financial services. Sharma said more than 40% of the company's customers are women, while its network of over 3,500 branches supports local engagement and distribution.The combination of physical presence and technology allows lenders to serve borrowers who may not have extensive digital financial histories or experience with formal credit.Deepak Patkar, MD & CEO, SMFG India Home Finance, cautioned that housing finance presents different challenges from shorter-duration retail lending products. Buying a home is a deeply personal and financially significant decision, involving long-term repayment commitments, extensive documentation and collateral assessments. Advt While digital platforms and technology can improve distribution, verification and processing, the customer-facing relationship is unlikely to disappear entirely.Patkar said digitisation of land records would be a major step towards making housing finance more efficient. Technology can reduce paperwork and improve verification, but borrowers taking on long-term financial commitments will continue to value direct engagement and confidence in their lender.Co-lending needs more than technology integrationAs lending ecosystems become increasingly interconnected, co-lending can help NBFCs expand credit distribution. But Patkar said partnerships must deliver a clear business advantage rather than add operational complexity. Advt One challenge is integrating the technology systems of participating lenders, which may use different loan origination and loan management platforms. Middleware and system interoperability are therefore essential to making partnerships work efficiently.Beyond technology, co-lending creates value when institutions combine complementary strengths, including different funding structures and costs of capital, to reach borrower segments they may struggle to serve independently.If both partners continue targeting the same customer segments, the arrangement risks becoming little more than an alternative funding mechanism, Patkar said. Advt He also stressed the importance of operational alignment. In housing finance, where credit assessment and customer servicing require significant judgement, written agreements and standardised policies cannot substitute for a practical understanding of how partners make decisions and manage customer relationships.Cybersecurity and governance are equally important as lenders become more dependent on interconnected systems. Patkar said SMFG India Home Finance has invested in technology and information security in line with the governance expectations of its parent, Japan's Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group.For NBFCs, digital expansion must therefore be accompanied by resilient technology infrastructure, continuous monitoring and protection of sensitive customer information.Property records remain a major hurdle for housing financeDespite the potential of digital distribution, property verification, valuation and land-related documentation remain significant barriers to scaling housing finance.Patkar cited India's mortgage-to-GDP ratio of approximately 11–12%, compared with levels exceeding 40% in several other economies, as an indication of the sector's long-term growth potential.Realising this opportunity, he said, will require simpler property ownership verification, clearer land classification and more reliable valuation processes. These challenges can be particularly pronounced in smaller towns, where property prices and documentation may be less standardised.Patkar also called for greater transparency around loan eligibility, saying customers should be able to understand their approximate borrowing capacity based on their financial profiles without navigating lengthy preliminary processes.Simpler eligibility checks, reliable collateral valuation and reforms to land-related laws and processes could improve the home loan experience and help lenders reach more borrowers.Credit availability will shape the next phase of growthWhile technology and distribution can make lending more efficient, Sharma said the availability of capital will remain critical to meeting India's growing financing needs.Demand for credit continues across retail borrowers, small businesses and MSMEs, particularly in smaller towns where access to formal capital remains limited. Sharma called for greater participation from banks, public sector financial institutions and policymakers in facilitating credit availability while maintaining asset quality.Patkar similarly said India's relatively low mortgage penetration leaves considerable room for housing finance to expand over the coming decades. However, unlocking this opportunity will require improvements in property verification, regulation and customer accessibility alongside technology adoption.The discussion underscored that embedded finance will depend on more than expanding digital distribution networks. For NBFCs, sustainable growth will require accessible credit, interoperable technology, complementary partnerships and robust governance, while preserving customer trust and underwriting discipline.
