Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Thomas Cook (India) Limited & Others (NCLT Mumbai) Summary : The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Mumbai Bench-I, considered an application under Sections 230–232, 61 and 66 of the Companies Act, 2013 concerning a composite scheme among Thomas Cook (India) Limited (TCIL), Sterling Holiday Resorts Limited (SHRL), TC Visa Services (India) Limited (TCVSL), Jardin Travel Solutions Limited (JTSL), and Borderless Travel Services Limited (BTSL), together with their respective shareholders. The applicant companies requested directions concerning shareholder and creditor meetings and statutory notices. The order, dated 4 November 2026, deals with the first stage of the arrangement procedure; it does not sanction the scheme itself. The scheme envisages demerger of TCIL’s resort undertaking into SHRL as a going concern, issuance of 81 fully paid equity shares of SHRL with face value ₹10 each for every 100 fully paid equity shares of TCIL with face value ₹1 each, and consolidation of every four TCIL equity shares of ₹1 into one share of ₹4. It further proposes amalgamation of the three wholly owned and presently non-operative companies—TCVSL, JTSL and BTSL—with TCIL, without issuance of TCIL shares for those amalgamations, followed by reduction of the face value of TCIL shares from ₹4 to ₹3. These are scheme proposals and have not been finally approved in this order. The applicants explained that segregating the resort business would permit SHRL to operate as a separately listed hospitality business and allow TCIL to concentrate on its remaining operations. They also described the consolidation and reduction of TCIL share capital as measures aimed at reorganisation and an efficient capital structure, while absorption of the three dormant subsidiaries was expected to reduce administrative costs. The boards of all five companies approved the scheme on 20 March 2026. TCIL stated that it had received stock-exchange observation or no-objection letters from BSE on 31 August 2026 and NSE on 1 September 2026 in terms of Regulation 37 of the SEBI Listing Regulations. On the meeting requirements, the Tribunal found that the equity shareholders of SHRL, TCVSL, JTSL and BTSL had given consent affidavits, permitting dispensation of their meetings. TCIL had six secured creditors, and secured creditor approvals representing 100% of its secured liabilities by value had been obtained; consequently, the Tribunal also dispensed with that meeting. The remaining four companies had no secured creditors. Although the unsecured creditors of the four subsidiaries had not submitted consent affidavits, the Tribunal observed that the scheme did not contemplate any compromise with them, that their rights would remain unaffected, and that sufficient net worth would remain available for creditor obligations. It accordingly dispensed with their meetings but directed each of those companies to issue scheme notices to every unsecured creditor with an outstanding amount of ₹10 lakh or more. For listed TCIL, however, the Tribunal found no consent affidavits from equity shareholders or unsecured creditors. It therefore directed TCIL to hold separate meetings of those classes within 60 days of uploading the order on the Tribunal’s website, using video conferencing or other audiovisual means, with remote e-voting and voting during the meetings. Notices containing the scheme and explanatory statement must be circulated at least 21 clear days in advance, and advertisements must appear in Financial Express and Loksatta. The order specifies Form CAA-2 notices, appointment of a chairperson and scrutinizer, quorum and proxy arrangements, a pre-meeting compliance affidavit and a report of results within 30 days after the meetings. The Tribunal additionally directed statutory notice under Section 230(5) to the Regional Director, Registrar of Companies, income-tax authorities, Official Liquidator, GST Department, SEBI, both stock exchanges and other relevant sectoral regulators. The notices must disclose the 30-day objection-response period described in the order. Applicants must provide contingent liability information and details of litigation materially affecting the proposal, to the extent not already furnished. The application was allowed on these procedural terms; subsequent shareholder and creditor consideration and any final sanction remain separate matters. FULL TEXT OF THE NCLT JUDGMENT/ORDER 1. Overview of the Scheme: “The Scheme is divided into following parts: 1. PART I deals with the definitions of capitalized terms used in this Scheme, date of taking effect, operative date, sequencing of the Scheme, and the share capital of the Parties; 2. PART II deals with the demerger of the Demerged Undertaking of TCIL into SHRL on a going concern basis, issuance of the Resulting Company New Shares by SHRL to the Eligible Shareholders in consideration thereof and matters incidental thereto; 3. PART III deals with the consolidation of four equity shares of TCIL of face value of INR 1 each into one share of face value of INR 4 each and matters incidental thereto; 4. PART IV deals with the amalgamation of TCVSL, JTSL and BTSL with the Transferee Company; the consequent dissolution without being wound up of the Transferor Companies and matters incidental thereto; and 5. PART V deals with reduction and reorganization of share capital by reduction in face value of TCIL’s equity shares from INR 4 per share to INR 3 per share; and 6. PART VI deals with the general terms and conditions that would be applicable to this Scheme.” 18. The rationale for the scheme is as follows: 1. TCIL, is a leading travel company in the country and has been offering inter alia broad spectrum of travel related services to its customers. TCIL owns and/or operates multiple resorts across India providing retreats in scenic locations amidst natural surroundings. 2. It also has resorts which offer a host of curated services across multiple attractive market segments including: i. Adventure Holidays with experiences such as trekking, kayaking, rafting & zip lining, ii. Educational trips including survival skills, self-defence, nature-appreciation etc. for resilience/ adaptability for students and corporates; iii. Corporate Getaways for team building activities, wellness & leadership development programs etc. 3. SHRL, a wholly owned subsidiary of TCIL has a wide range of experience of running and operating resort business, catering to a different demographic and offering more unique and curated experiences. 4. TCIL and SHRL’s Management believes that the Resort Business of TCIL would reap more benefit from being owned and operated by SHRL in light of their vast expertise in the hospitality industry. 5. Further, SHRL’s Management is of the view that SHRL has matured over the years and is well poised to chart its own growth path and operate as a separate listed entity in the fast-growing hospitality industry. 6. Accordingly, the Management of TCIL is of the view that for better focus and maximum result, it is better to divest the Resort Business into SHRL and subsequently list SHRL. 7. The main objective of the proposed restructuring, by way of a composite scheme, is to re-organize and segregate the different business verticals and focus on core businesses, in compliance with relevant regulations. In this manner, the two listed entities can each pursue growth opportunities that are more aligned with their own industry dynamics, leading to improved operational efficiencies and profitability. 8. The Management of TCIL believes that the proposed consolidation of the face value of its equity shares will result in a significant improvement in certain key financial ratios. This enhancement in ratios is expected to better reflect the true market value of the TCIL’s shares, aligning the share price more appropriately with its intrinsic value. 9. Such consolidation of share capital is also anticipated to convey stronger financial stability and valuation metrics, thereby presenting a more accurate picture of the Company’s financial strength and long-term prospects. 10. TCVSL, JTSL and BTSL are dormant and non-operative companies, incurring unnecessary compliance and administrative costs. Their absorption into TCIL will streamline the corporate structure and reduce overheads. This will enhance efficiency and allow management to focus on core business activities. 11. The Management of TCIL believes that the proposed reduction of share capital is required to right size the balance sheet and capital structure of TCIL such that TCIL has a more efficient capital structure and the shareholders also benefit in terms of improved earnings per share. 12. Such proposed reduction of the equity share capital of TCIL would not have any adverse effect on the creditors of TCIL or TCIL’s ability to fulfil its commitments or meet its obligations in the ordinary course of business as there is no payout resulting from the proposed capital reduction.” 19.
