Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Ridcor Infra Projects Limited Vs ITO (ITAT Jaipur) No Penalty U/s 271DA for Breach of Section 269ST Where Good & Sufficient Reasons Are Proved The Jaipur Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal deleted a penalty of ₹13,00,825 under section 271DA where a highway infrastructure company accepted cash from a defaulting toll contractor to protect its contractual revenue. The Tribunal found that the contractor’s persistent defaults, the company’s documented recovery efforts and the accounting of the receipts established good and sufficient reasons for the contravention of section 269ST. The ruling illustrates how the statutory proviso operates even when the cash receipt breaches the prescribed limit. How the Cash Receipts Arose Ridcor Infra Projects Limited was engaged in highway development in Rajasthan on a Design, Build, Finance, Operate and Transfer basis, with toll collection. During FY 2017–18, it auctioned toll collection rights to contractors against fixed weekly payments. Choudhary Builders was one such contractor. The assessee was a wholly owned subsidiary of RIDCOR Limited, a 50:50 joint venture between the Government of Rajasthan and Infrastructure Leasing & Financial Services Limited. Between 24 May and 21 August 2017, the assessee received six cash payments from the contractor, aggregating to ₹13,00,825. Four receipts were exactly ₹2 lakh each, while the other two were ₹2,60,000 and ₹2,40,825. The Assessing Officer identified the receipts from the audited financial records, rejected the explanation for accepting cash and levied a penalty equal to the entire amount. The CIT(A) confirmed the penalty, and the assessee appealed to the Tribunal. The Cash Restriction and the Statutory Safeguard Section 269ST restricts receipt of ₹2 lakh or more through modes other than those permitted by the provision. The threshold applies to amounts received in aggregate from a person in a day, in respect of a single transaction, or in respect of transactions relating to one event or occasion from a person. A cash receipt of exactly ₹2 lakh therefore reaches the statutory threshold. Section 271DA(1) prescribes a penalty equal to the amount received in contravention. Its proviso expressly bars the penalty if the recipient proves that there were “good and sufficient reasons for the contravention.” The burden rests on the recipient to establish why the prohibited mode of receipt was used in the circumstances of the case. Why the Company Accepted Cash The company explained that the contractor had repeatedly defaulted on its weekly contractual payments despite reminders. To safeguard its revenue, the company required the contractor to deposit actual toll collections into its designated bank account. It submitted that the receipts were recorded in its regular books and that tax had been paid. The Revenue maintained that the established violation of section 269ST warranted confirmation of the penalty. The company produced a letter dated 8 December 2017 recording that the contractor had failed to deposit any amount for the preceding 30 days despite continued follow-up. The contract was terminated with effect from 9 December 2017. The Tribunal treated this correspondence as evidence of the company’s efforts to recover contractual dues. The assessee also cited Hindustan Steel Ltd. v. State of Orissa, Lilavati Kirtilal Mehta Medical Trust and Delta Farm Services in support of its case. Why the Tribunal Deleted the Penalty The Tribunal read section 269ST and section 271DA together with the proviso. It found that the company accepted cash after the contractor began defaulting and did so to protect itself against the risk of losing contractual revenue. The entries appeared in the audited books, which were themselves the source of the Assessing Officer’s information. The Assessing Officer had made no allegation that these receipts resulted in tax evasion. The Bench also referred to the Delhi Tribunal’s order in All Fresh Supply Management Pvt. Ltd. v. ACIT, ITA No. 1955/Del/2023, dated 21 May 2024, as having dealt with an identical issue. On the facts before it, the Jaipur Bench held that the company had established sufficient reasons for accepting the cash. It accordingly deleted the entire penalty and allowed the appeal. Author’s Comments The ruling is significant because it gives independent effect to the proviso to section 271DA(1). Once a contravention is established, the authority must still examine the recipient’s explanation and the evidence supporting it. Here, the audited books established how the receipts were accounted for, while the contractor’s defaults and the recovery correspondence explained why cash was accepted. Taken together, these facts supported the company’s claim of business necessity. For a similar dispute, the strongest evidence will ordinarily be material that connects the need to accept cash with the receipts in question. The contract, payment schedule, default notices and bank deposit records can show what was due, when the payer defaulted and why the recipient adopted that course. The value of this decision lies in its application of the statutory safeguard to a documented commercial necessity. The restriction under section 269ST continues to govern the mode of receipt, while relief from penalty depends on the reasons proved in each case. Cases Discussed Hindustan Steel Ltd. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT JAIPUR The Appellant, Ridcor Infra Projects Limited (hereinafter referred to as the ‘assessee’) by filing the present appeal, sought to set aside the impugned order dated 26.11.2025 passed by the National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [hereinafter referred to as the ‘CIT(A)’] qua assessment year 2018-19 on the grounds inter alia that:- “ 1. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred in confirming the invocation of section 269ST r.w.s. 271DA of the Income Tax Act, 1961 in the case of the appellant since the said sections are not applicable in the current facts of the case. Hence, the impugned order deserves to be quashed. 2. Without prejudice to ground no. 1 above and without any admission, the Ld. CIT(A) has erred, in law and on facts, in confirming the penalty under Section 271DA of the Income Tax Act, 1961 without appreciating that the case of the appellant falls within the protective ambit of the Proviso to Section 271DA(1) of the Act. 3. Without prejudice to ground no. 1 above and without any admission, the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in confirming the penalty under Section 271DA of the Income Tax Act, 1961 by ignoring the legislative intent of Chapter XX-B, which is aimed at preventing tax evasion. As the impugned transaction does not defeat this objective, the levy of penalty is contrary to the purpose of the statute and therefore liable to be deleted. 4. The Appellant reserves the right to add, alter, amend, or withdraw any ground of appeal and to urge any further legal or factual contention at the time of hearing.” 2. Briefly stated, facts necessary for consideration and adjudication of the issues at hand are: Assessee company Ridcor Infra Projects Limited is into highway construction in the State of Rajasthan, as per Design, Build, Finance, Operate and Transfer (Toll) basis. In financial year 2017-18, the assessee company had auctioned toll collection to various entities on fixed payment basis to be paid on weekly basis. Chouldhary Builders was one such entity, who had involved in toll collection as per audit report of the assessee company for the year under consideration. It is noticed that the assessee company has received an amount exceeding the limit specified in section 269ST of the Act, in aggregate from a person in a day or in respect of a single transaction or in respect of transaction relating to one event or occasion from a person and as such received amount in cash, the details of which is extracted for ready perusal as under:- S. N. Name of payer PAN Nature of transaction Amount received (₹) Date of receipt 1 Choudhary Builders FYXPS3776K Contractual toll amount receivable 2,60,000 24.05.2017 2 Choudhary Builders FYXPS3776K Contractual toll amount receivable 2,00,000 29.05.2017 3 Choudhary Builders FYXPS3776K Contractual toll amount receivable 2,00,000 31.05.2017 4 Choudhary Builders FYXPS3776K Contractual toll amount receivable 2,00,000 21.06.2017 5 Choudhary Builders FYXPS3776K Contractual toll amount receivable 2,40,825 22.06.2017 6 Choudhary Builders FYXPS3776K Contractual toll amount receivable 2,00,000 21.08.2017 Total 13,00,825 Declining the contention raised by the assessee, the AO proceeded to hold that the assessee company has failed to prove that there were good and sufficient reasons for contravention of section 269ST of the Act and thereby levied the penalty of Rs. 13,00,825/- u/s 271DA of the Act. 3. Assessee carried the matter before the Ld. CIT(A) by way of filing appeal, who has confirmed the penalty levied by the AO by dismissing the appeal. Being aggrieved with the impugned order passed by the Ld. CIT(A), assessee has come up before the Tribunal by way of filing the present appeal. 4. We have heard Ld. ARs for the assessee and Ld. DR for the Revenue and perused the record available on file. 5. Undisputedly during the period 24.05.2017 to 21.08.2017 i.e. during the year under consideration, the assessee has received total amount of Rs. 13,00,825/- by way of cash deposit in tranches of more than Rs. 2,00,000/-. It is also not in dispute that the entire amount has been received in cash by the assessee from a toll contractor, who has been engaged for toll collection right on fixed payment basis by making fixed payment amount to the company on weekly basis, as per memorandum of understanding. It is also not in dispute that the assessee is a wholly owned subsidiary of RIDCOR Limited, which is 50:50 joint venture between the Government of Rajasthan and Infrastructure Leasing & Financial Services Limited (IL & FS). 6. In the backdrop of the aforesaid undisputed facts Ld. AR for the assessee company contended that the entire cash has been deposited in the designated bank account of the assessee as a “business expediency” to protect the loss of revenue; that all the transactions are duly recorded in the regular books of accounts; that despite repeated communications and reminders to the contractor, they continued to commit default in making payment of contractual payment; that due to persistent default the assessee company has terminated the contract with the contractor w.e.f. 09.12.2017; that legislative intent of section 269ST of the Act is to curb the generation, circulation and use of unaccounted cash and to promote digital transactions; that provisions contained in section 269ST and 271DA of the Act are intended to curb the generation and circulation of black money and to discourage cash transactions to facilitate tax evasion and in case of the assessee the entire cash transactions are duly recorded in the books of accounts, on which tax has already been paid; that assessee has sufficient reasons to accept the payment in cash as the toll contractor was continuously making default for payment of money and cash has been accepted on the basis of daily collection; that section 271DA(1) of the Act contains an express statutory safeguard which provides that no penalty shall be imposable if such person proves that there were good and sufficient reasons for the contravention and relied upon the decision rendered by the Supreme Court in case of Hindustan Steel Ltd. vs. State of Orisa reported in 83 ITR 26 (SC) and the orders passed by the Coordinate Bench of Tribunal in case of ACIT vs. M/s Lilavati Kirtilal Mehta Medical Trust in ITA No. 1568/Mum/2021 vide order dated 30.08.2022 and in case of Delta Farm Services vs. ITO in ITA No. 235 & 236/Del/2023 vide order dated 21.02.2025. 7. However on the other hand, Ld. DR for the Revenue by relying upon the order passed by the Ld. CIT(A) contended that when violation of section 269ST of the Act is proved the order passed by the Ld. CIT(A) is to be confirmed. 8. First of all in the face of the undisputed facts that the Assessing Officer has took cognizance of accepting the cash deposit by the assessee to the tune of Rs. 2,00,000/- and above during the year under consideration from the audited financials of the assessee company, which lead to the inference that the cash deposit has not been made or accepted to channelize the black money. Secondly it is also not the case of the Assessing Officer that on account of the cash receipt to the tune of Rs. 13,00,825/-, the assessee has evaded taxes to paid the Revenue department. 9. Moreover the assessee company is joint venture between the Government of Rajasthan and Infrastructure Leasing & Financial Services Limited on 50:50 basis and the entire business is being conducted on the basis of duly audited books of accounts. 10. The assessee company has come up with the explanation that since the toll collector was continuously making default in making payment and in order to safeguard the financial interest of the assessee company, they were made to deposit the actual toll collection to be deposited in the bank account of the assessee company to avoid any possible loss of revenue. When the Chouldhary Builders, a toll collector was collecting the toll collection from various entities, he was made to deposit the cash to avoid loss of revenue. 11. Moreover assessee company has brought on record communication between the assessee company and Choudhary Builders, which are in the form of letter dated 08.12.2017 stating therein that despite continuous persuasion he has not deposited any amount since last 30 days and terminated the contract w.e.f. 09.12.2017. These fact goes to prove that the assessee has made sufficient efforts to get the amount due under contract. Circumstances brought on record by the assessee company shows that they had “sufficient cause” to accept the cash payment to avoid financial losses to the state owned public Sector undertaking. 12. Harmonious construction of section 269ST and 271DA of the Act with proviso leads to the legislative intent that provisions have been brought into force to curtail the black money by promoting banking transactions and to stop the tax evasion. When these provisions are further interpreted in the light of the proviso “that penalty shall not be imposable, if such persons proves that they were good and sufficient reasons for the contravention”. 13. So in the given circumstances the assessee company has brought on record sufficient reasons to accept the cash money in contravention of section 269ST of the Act by recording each and every entry in the books of accounts, which were dully audited and the cash amount was only accepted when the toll contractor started defaulting in payment of dues as per contract. So when the assessee has not entered into the transaction to avoid the payment of taxes and transaction was made in bonafide belief that the toll contractor will runaway with the dues to be paid by him as per memorandum of understanding, they had accepted the cash amount. 14. Identical issues have been dealt with by the Coordinate Bench in case of All Fresh Supply Management Pvt. Ltd. vs. ACIT in ITA No. 1955/Del/2023 vided order dated 21.05.2024. 15. So in view of what has been discussed above, we find sufficient reasons as explained by the assessee to accept the cash amount and as such the penalty levied by the Assessing Officer and confirmed by the Ld. CIT(A) is ordered to be deleted. 16. Resultantly, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 18-11-2026.
