} } Listen to This ArticleIndia’s largest private-sector lender, HDFC Bank, mobilised over ₹1.5 trillion in deposits in the July-September quarter (Q2FY27), with its period-end deposits rising 4.9 per cent sequentially and nearly 19 per cent year-on-year (Y-o-Y) to ₹33.27 trillion, according to the bank’s quarterly business update. The bank had mobilised ₹65,000 crore in deposits in Q1FY27, compared to ₹2.45 trillion in Q4FY26. Average Casa (current account savings account) deposits grew 10.7 per cent Y-o-Y to ₹9.71 trillion against ₹8.77 trillion in the year-ago period. The bank said it has mobilised ₹1.10 trillion or $11.5 billion through foreign currency non-resident (bank) or FCNR(B) deposits under the Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI’s) concessional swap window, which was open from June to August 31. The bank said its overseas branches extended loan facilities worth $5.7 billion against these deposits. In addition, standby letters of credit issued to other banks in respect of loans against these deposits amounted to $3.1 billion. HDFC Bank’s period-end gross advances in Q2FY27 grew 16.3 per cent Y-o-Y and 5.2 per cent sequentially to ₹32.19 trillion. Axis Bank, India’s third largest private sector lender, said it’s deposits grew 5.8 per cent sequentially and 20.7 per cent year-on-year (YoY) to Rs 14.52 trillion as of September 30, while gross advances rose 8.8 per cent quarter-on-quarter (QoQ) and 22.7 per cent YoY to Rs 13.85 trillion. The private sector lender mobilised $10.62 billion (Rs 1.02 trillion) in FCNR(B) deposits under the Reserve Bank of India’s swap facility introduced earlier this year. The FCNR(B) mobilisation contributed to the sharp rise in the bank’s overall deposits during the quarter. A portion of the FCNR(B) inflows was used to reduce non-retail term deposits, which declined 8.6 per cent QoQ, the Axis Bank said. Term deposits nevertheless rose 27.3 per cent YoY and 8.4 per cent QoQ to Rs 9.23 trillion as of September-end. CASA deposits grew 10.6 per cent YoY and 1.5 per cent QoQ to Rs 5.30 trillion. Axis’ advances growth was also aided by loans extended by its international branches to customers as a leveraging facility for placing FCNR(B) deposits. These loans stood at $4.57 billion as of September 30. The bank also had standby letters of credit worth $4.69 billion (Rs 449 billion) issued in favour of other banks in respect of loans against FCNR(B) deposits. Another private sector lender Kotak Mahindra Bank said its period-end deposits grew 23.2 per cent Y-o-Y and 13.7 per cent sequentially to ₹6.51 trillion in Q2FY27. The bank mobilised $5.78 billion through FCNR(B) deposits during the quarter. Its international branches extended advances aggregating $1.68 billion (around ₹16,110 crore) against these FCNR(B) deposits, converted at the prevailing exchange rate as of September 30. The bank’s period-end advances grew 24.7 per cent Y-o-Y and 12.7 per cent sequentially to ₹5.77 trillion. IndusInd Bank’s net advances grew 11.2 per cent Year-on-Year (YoY) to Rs 3.62 trillion as of September 30, 2026, outpacing the 10.1 per cent growth in deposits to Rs 4.29 trillion. Indusind’s Casa ratio declined to 28 per cent, from 30.7 per cent a year earlier and 29.4 per cent in June. IndusInd Bank mobilised $3.51 billion of FCNR(B) deposits under the RBI’s deposit swap facility, with outstanding loans of about Rs 16,564 crore extended from its GIFT City branch against these deposits as of September-end. RBL Bank’s total deposits rose 34 per cent year-on-year (Y-o-Y) to ₹1.56 trillion in the quarter ended September 30 (Q2FY27), while gross advances grew 40 per cent to ₹1.43 trillion.
