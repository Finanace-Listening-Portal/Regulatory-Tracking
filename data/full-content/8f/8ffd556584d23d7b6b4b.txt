The lender is exercising greater caution in drought affected markets, particularly Maharashtra, Karnataka and Telangana, where its relatively high exposure could leave borrower cash flows vulnerable to weaker agricultural output.The moderation comes despite healthy lending momentum. Disbursements grew 26% year on year and 8% sequentially in the September quarter, while AUM expanded 17% year on year and 5% quarter on quarter, ahead of expectations, according to Axis Securities. Management clarified that the lower growth guidance reflects geographic caution rather than tighter credit filters or lower loan to value ratios. Advt The El Niño risk comes against a backdrop of rising credit costs and emerging asset quality concerns. Shriram Finance's calculated credit costs increased to 250 basis points in Q2 FY27 from 203 basis points in the previous quarter. The brokerage expects near term asset quality pressures to keep credit costs elevated in the second half, although management expects the current stress to be temporary and credit costs to gradually return towards the steady state level of around 2%.Gross non performing assets remained broadly stable at 4.65% in Q2, compared with 4.64% in the previous quarter, while net NPAs eased to 2.31% from 2.33%. Stage 2 assets, which indicate loans showing signs of increased credit risk, rose to 7.04% from 6.96% sequentially. Asset quality trends were broadly stable across most segments, except gold loans and two wheelers.The lender's portfolio continued to expand across key segments. Commercial vehicle loans grew 19% year on year and 4% sequentially, while passenger vehicle loans increased 20% year on year and 4% sequentially. The micro, small and medium enterprises portfolio grew 14% year on year and 10% sequentially. Gold loans recorded particularly strong growth of 68% year on year and 19% sequentially.Strong Q2 performanceProfitability remained strong in the quarter. Net interest income rose 40% year on year and 9% sequentially, supported by a 48 basis point sequential improvement in net interest margin to 9.52%. Pre provision operating profit grew 53% year on year and 12% sequentially, while net profit increased 59% year on year and 7% sequentially. Operating expenses remained contained, with the cost to income ratio at 22.9%. Advt However, margins are expected to normalise over time. Management expects net interest margins to remain around current levels for another couple of quarters before gradually easing to 9% over the medium term and around 8.5% over the longer term. Potential increases in incremental borrowing costs and a higher share of new vehicle financing, which yields 200-400 basis points less than used vehicle loans, could weigh on margins.Despite the risks, Axis Securities expects Shriram Finance to deliver around 17% compound annual AUM growth over FY26-29. It maintains a Buy recommendation but has lowered its target price to Rs 1,085 per share from Rs 1,230, while revising its valuation basis to 2 times estimated September 2028 book value from 2.4 times FY28 estimated book value. The brokerage expects strong margins and controlled operating costs to help offset the temporary impact of higher credit costs, supporting a return on assets of 4.1-4.2% and return on equity of 14-16% over FY27-29.
