Summary: The insurance free-look period is an important consumer-protection mechanism that allows policyholders to review newly issued insurance policies and seek cancellation within the prescribed period. Under the IRDAI regulatory framework, including the Insurance Products Regulations, 2024, and the June 2024 Master Circular on Life Insurance Products, the free-look period generally extends to 30 days from receipt of the policy document, subject to applicable conditions. Policyholders should carefully examine the policy schedule, premium obligations, sum assured, exclusions, riders, waiting periods, surrender values, benefit illustrations and customer information sheet to identify discrepancies between the issued policy and representations made during sale. Free-look cancellation does not necessarily guarantee a full premium refund, as insurers may deduct proportionate risk premium, medical examination expenses and stamp duty where permitted. The date of receipt is particularly important, making delivery records, acknowledgements and correspondence essential evidence. Policyholders should submit cancellation requests through acknowledged channels, preserve supporting documents and seek itemised refund calculations. The article distinguishes free-look cancellation from surrender, which may result in substantially lower payouts and different contractual consequences. It also highlights the need to examine income-tax exemptions, GST implications and replacement insurance coverage before making decisions. Common disputes include delayed policy delivery, undisclosed riders, misleading benefit illustrations, digital acceptance issues and unexplained deductions. Where grievances arise, policyholders should approach the insurer’s grievance-redressal mechanism. Overall, the article emphasises timely policy verification, documentary evidence, regulatory compliance and informed financial decision-making to protect policyholders against avoidable losses and disputes arising from insurance cancellation or mis-selling.
