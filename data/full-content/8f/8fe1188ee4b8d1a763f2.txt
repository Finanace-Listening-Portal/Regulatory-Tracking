Bombay High Court Judgment In a common judgment covering six assessee appeals, two Revenue appeals and Writ Petition No. 772 of 1999, the Bombay High Court considered whether assessments under Section 143(3) of the Income-tax Act could be made in the names of Reliance Polyethylene Limited (RPEL) and Reliance Polypropylene Limited (RPPL) after both companies ceased to exist on merging with Reliance Industries Limited (RIL). The High Court approved the merger on 11 January 1995 with effect from 1 January 1995. On 20 January 2025, the Court allowed RIL to raise the additional substantial question of law at the final hearing because it went to the Assessing Officer’s jurisdiction. RIL argued that the Assessing Officer knew of the amalgamation before passing the assessments. The Revenue opposed the late jurisdictional objection and admission of additional evidence. It relied on RIL’s assumption of the former companies’ liabilities, the use of their names in appeal forms and RIL’s participation throughout the proceedings. Applying Section 260A(7) and Order XLI Rule 27(1)(b) of the Code of Civil Procedure, the High Court admitted the undisputed documents exchanged between the parties because they were necessary to determine the Revenue’s prior knowledge. For AY 1994–95, the Assessing Officer had issued a Section 143(1) intimation on 17 December 1996 adjusting RIL’s refund of approximately ₹45 crore against demands that included RPEL and RPPL. The same officer nevertheless assessed RPEL at ₹36,96,04,710 and RPPL at ₹40,28,92,970 in their own names on 27 March 1997. For AY 1995–96, the assessment orders dated 27 February 1998 expressly recorded the merger. For AY 1993–94, RIL’s letter of 9 October 1995 and its computation notes filed on 30 November 1995 showed that the Revenue knew of the merger before the assessments dated 18 March 1996. The High Court applied Maruti Suzuki India Ltd. and held that assessments issued in the names of the former companies despite prior knowledge of the merger were void from the outset. RIL’s participation did not cure the jurisdictional defect. The Court distinguished Mahagun Realtors (P) Ltd., where the taxpayer had continued to hold itself out as the former company and had not disclosed the amalgamation in the relevant return. The High Court allowed RIL’s appeals and interim applications for AYs 1993–94 to 1995–96 and quashed the assessments and consequential appellate proceedings. The Revenue’s cross-appeals for AY 1994–95 and RIL’s writ petition became infructuous, and any interim order in the writ petition stood vacated. The Court left the merits of the additions undecided and clarified that the Revenue could initiate fresh proceedings against RIL in accordance with law. Cases Discussed PCIT v.
