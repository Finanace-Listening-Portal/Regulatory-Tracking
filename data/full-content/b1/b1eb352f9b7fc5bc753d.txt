Summary: Form PAS-6 is a half-yearly reconciliation report matching a company’s share capital with securities held in dematerialised form. The obligation to file this form does not arise merely because a company holds an International Securities Identification Number (ISIN). It depends on the applicability of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014. Rule 9A(8) requires unlisted public companies to file PAS-6, while Rule 9B(5), read with Rule 9B(2), extends the relevant requirements to private companies other than small companies and government companies. Following the revision of small company thresholds effective from 1 December 2025, private companies with paid-up capital not exceeding ₹10 crore and turnover not exceeding ₹100 crore may qualify as small companies, subject to statutory exclusions. Consequently, a private company that previously obtained an ISIN while classified as non-small may subsequently qualify for the small company exemption. The author opines that retaining an ISIN does not independently create a continuing PAS-6 filing obligation after the company falls outside Rule 9B. However, outstanding filings relating to periods when the company was covered by the requirements must still be addressed. Companies should examine their classification using audited financial statements, document changes in status and review their compliance obligations annually. The precise treatment of transitional reporting periods remains a matter requiring careful interpretation because no directly applicable judicial ruling or MCA clarification has been identified in the supplied material. Legal Position The Companies Act does not itself ask for PAS-6. The duty sits in Rule 9A(8), and it reaches a private company only through Rule 9B(5). Rule 9B(5) works only for a company that Rule 9B(2) covers, and Rule 9B(2) leaves out small companies. So the chain breaks the moment a company becomes small. The ISIN stays valid, but demat is no longer compulsory for that company, so keeping the ISIN becomes a choice. The author opines that PAS-6 follows the rule and not the code, as the table below shows. The author opines that a company should therefore confirm its position as on 31 March using the new limits. No court ruling or MCA clarification directly on this point was found, so the position is not judicially settled. Exemptions/Relaxations The relief is the small company carve-out in Rule 9B(2) itself. It is not available to holding companies, subsidiaries, Section 8 companies or special Act companies, because the law never treats them as small. They continue to file. Practical Interpretation Test the company’s status as on 31 March on audited figures, and check that it is not a holding, subsidiary or Section 8 company. File PAS-6 for every half year in which the company was still non-small. Do not leave old periods pending. Record the reclassification in a board minute, and decide whether to keep or surrender the ISIN through the depository and the RTA. Review the status every year. If the company crosses the limits again, it gets 18 months from the close of that financial year to comply with Rule 9B. The Rules carry no separate penalty for PAS-6. A default is usually dealt with under the general penalty in Section 450. Please verify the current amounts in the amended Act before advising. Example ABC Private Limited has paid-up capital of ₹6 crore and turnover of ₹60 crore. It was non-small under the old limits, took an ISIN and filed PAS-6. On the audited accounts as on 31 March 2026, it is small and is not a subsidiary. PAS-6 for the half years when it was non-small must be filed. After that, PAS-6 is not mandatory, even if the ISIN is retained. Conclusion The author opines that a small company need not file PAS-6 only because it holds an ISIN. Whether the company holds an ISIN or not makes no difference. PAS-6 is mandatory only for a non-small company.
