If a party is not satisfied with the order, the law provides a remedy of appeal in appropriate cases before the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT). NCLAT is an important institution in the corporate legal system of India. It hears appeals in matters covered by the Companies Act, 2013, the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC), and other laws which confer appellate jurisdiction upon it. Constitution and Jurisdiction of NCLAT NCLAT was constituted under Section 410 of the Companies Act, 2013. It started functioning on 1 June 2016 for hearing appeals against orders of the NCLT. Its work is not limited to company law disputes. Under Section 61 of the IBC, an appeal against an order of the NCLT in its capacity as the Adjudicating Authority under the relevant provisions of the Code may be filed before NCLAT. It also hears specified appeals relating to orders of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India. NCLAT also has appellate jurisdiction in matters concerning certain decisions and orders of the Competition Commission of India and the National Financial Reporting Authority, as provided by the relevant statutes. Therefore, before filing an appeal, it is necessary to identify the law under which the original order was passed and whether that law provides an appeal to NCLAT. Appeal Against an NCLT Order A party should not assume that every order of the NCLT can be challenged in the same manner. The right of appeal, the grounds available and the time limit depend upon the applicable law and the nature of the order. For example, Section 421 of the Companies Act, 2013 deals with appeals against orders of the NCLT under that Act. Section 61 of the IBC provides the appellate remedy in insolvency matters covered by that provision. The limitation period is particularly important. Under Section 421(3) of the Companies Act, an appeal is generally required to be filed within 45 days from receipt of the order, with a limited power to condone delay for a further period not exceeding 45 days on sufficient cause being shown. Under Section 61(2) of the IBC, the normal period is 30 days, and the Tribunal may allow a further period not exceeding 15 days if sufficient cause is established. These periods are different. An advocate handling an appeal must therefore check the correct statutory provision rather than apply one limitation period to every NCLAT matter. What Should Be Examined Before Filing? In my view, proper preparation of an appeal is as important as identifying the legal grounds. The appellant should carefully read the impugned order and identify the findings which are being challenged. The grounds of appeal should explain the error in the order. A general statement that the order is wrong may not be sufficient. The appeal should identify the relevant facts, the statutory provision involved, the submissions made before the NCLT and the reason why the finding is disputed. The record should also be complete. The impugned order, relevant pleadings, applications, replies and supporting documents should be arranged properly. Where a particular document is important to the dispute, its relevance should be clearly explained. The advocate must also examine whether the proposed grounds are available under the applicable statute. This is particularly important in insolvency matters, where the IBC prescribes specific grounds for certain appeals, including appeals against approved resolution plans. Importance of Timely Decisions Insolvency proceedings involve several parties, including financial creditors, operational creditors, the corporate debtor, resolution professionals and prospective resolution applicants. Delay can affect the value of assets and the possibility of resolving the company’s financial difficulties. For this reason, limitation and timely filing should not be treated as minor procedural matters. Parties should obtain the order, take legal advice and prepare the appeal without unnecessary delay. At the same time, the appeal must be decided in accordance with the applicable law and the material placed before the Tribunal. Speed is important, but a fair opportunity to present the case is also necessary. Role of Advocates Advocates appearing before NCLAT have an important responsibility. They must understand the applicable statute, identify the real issue in dispute and present the case in a clear manner. Unnecessary grounds and lengthy arguments that do not address the impugned order can make the matter difficult to follow. A well-prepared appeal should help the Tribunal understand what went wrong in the original decision and what relief the appellant is seeking. The opposing party must also have a fair opportunity to respond. Conclusion NCLAT plays an important role in the appellate system governing company law and insolvency matters in India. It provides a statutory forum for challenging specified orders and examining legal errors within its jurisdiction. For a successful appeal, the correct statutory provision, limitation period, grounds of challenge and supporting documents must be examined carefully. Every order should be assessed on its own facts and the law applicable to it. In my view, proper preparation and timely filing can help avoid procedural difficulties and enable the real dispute to be placed before the Tribunal. The purpose of an appeal is not merely to challenge an order, but to demonstrate clearly why the order requires interference under the law.
