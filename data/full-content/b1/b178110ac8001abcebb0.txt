Summary: The weekly regulatory roundup for 9–15 November 2026 covers important notifications, circulars, advance rulings and judicial decisions relating to Income Tax, GST, Customs, DGFT, SEBI, IBBI and RBI. The Supreme Court clarified that CBDT-prescribed gold possession limits do not establish lawful acquisition in criminal proceedings. Significant GST advance rulings address 18% tax on plastic nursery pots, RWA maintenance exceeding Rs 7,500, PVC-coated polyester fabric, textile shopping bags and paper carry bags. Other rulings clarify GST treatment of wooden handicrafts, utility reimbursements under pure-agent conditions, solar electricity, wheeling charges and input tax credit eligibility. GSTAT also issued Standard Operating Procedures for filing replies and transferring appeals involving identical questions of law across different benches. On the Customs front, important developments include extension of the ATA Carnet period, revised tariff values for gold, silver and other commodities, anti-dumping duty exemptions subject to IGCR compliance and temporary import-permit relaxation for non-insecticidal chemicals. DGFT prohibited exports of lithium-ion battery black mass, extended halal certification timelines for meat exports to Egypt, continued rice export inspection exemptions and introduced Certificate of Origin procedures under the India–New Zealand FTA. SEBI revised FATCA and CRS reporting requirements, while NCLAT decisions addressed corporate guarantees and pre-admission insolvency settlements. IBBI issued disciplinary and RTI-related orders, and RBI introduced consolidated Domestic Money Transfer Directions, 2026. The roundup also highlights a Supreme Court judgment concerning individual ownership of inherited property and Hindu Undivided Family rights. Income Tax SC, Gold Seizure Limits Do Not Establish Lawful Acquisition: Case of State of Bihar vs Phulpari Kumari, SC Judgement Dated 2nd November 2026. The apex court held that Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) circulars permitting individuals to hold gold up to specified limits (such as 500 grams for married women) do not automatically explain the lawful source of that gold in criminal trials. (Link: SC Judgement Dated 02/11/2026) B. GST AAR, Plastic Nursery Pots and Seedling Trays Attract 18% GST: Case of Solar Compact Private Limited, AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 8th November 2026. AAR ruled plastic nursery pots and seedling trays supplied by the party fall under HSN 3926 and attract an 18% GST rate. (Link: AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 08/11/2026) AAR, Actual Utility Reimbursements Excludable subject to Pure-Agent Conditions: Case of A to Z Food Ventures LLP, AAR Telangana Ruling Dated 30th October 2026. The applicant, operating cafes in Hyderabad, leased commercial space. Alongside monthly rent, the lease required paying actual electricity and water charges tracked via separate meters. AAR ruled that electricity and water charges reimbursed by A to Z Food Ventures LLP to its landlord are excludible from GST if all “pure agent” conditions under Rule 33 of the CGST Rules are met. While electrical energy and water are inherently GST-exempt (Notification No. 2/2017), utility reimbursements recovered by a landlord avoid GST only when collected strictly on actuals without markup, separately indicated on invoices, and supported by third-party bills under Rule 33. (Link: AAR Telangana Ruling Dated 30/10/2026) AAR, GST on RWA Maintenance Above Rs 7,500 Taxable in Full at 18% Rate: Case of Sattva Magnus Apartment Owners Mutually Aided Cooperative Maintenance Society Limited, AAR Telangana Ruling Dated 30th October 2026. AAR ruled that if a society’s aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 lakhs and the monthly maintenance contribution per member crosses Rs 7,500, Goods and Services Tax (GST) at 18% applies to the entire maintenance amount, not just the value exceeding Rs 7,500 per month. (Link: AAR Telangana Ruling Dated 30/10/2026) AAR, 5% GST on Wooden Pooja Mandirs, 18% on Cradles and Wall Clocks: Case of Wakato Bussan Enterprises (Sri Sakthikumar Ramakrishna), AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 2nd October 2026. AAR ruled that handcrafted wooden pooja mandirs, idols, frames, and flower vases attract 5% GST, while wooden baby cradles and wooden wall clocks attract 18% GST. The applicant sought a flat 5% concessional GST rate across all six products under Chapter 44. (Link: AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 02/10/2026) AAR, Supplier cannot seek Advance Ruling on Tenant Debit Note ITC: Case of Bell Medicare, AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 25th September 2026. AAR ruled a supplier cannot seek an advance ruling on whether its customer (tenant) is eligible to claim Input Tax Credit (ITC). It clarified that an applicant can only seek rulings regarding supplies undertaken by them and ITC claimed on their own inward supplies. A supplier cannot ask for a ruling on a recipient’s right to claim ITC on the supplier’s outward supplies. (Link: AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 25/09/2026) AAR, Solar Power Exempt from GST, No Reverse Charge on Wheeling Charges: Case of Kanthaammal Padmanaba Modern Rice Mill, AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 25th September 2026. The party has installed a 3 MW solar power plant to generate and supply electricity to designated institutional customers via an intra-state open-access arrangement. Under an Energy Wheeling Agreement with state power utilities, the generated solar energy is injected into the grid and wheeled to end customers. For these grid services, the utilities levy wheeling, transmission, distribution, carrying, and operation and maintenance (O&M) charges. AAR ruled that solar electricity supplied to institutional customers is exempt from GST, and that no reverse charge liability applies to utility wheeling and transmission charges. (Link: AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 25/09/2026) AAR, PVC-Coated Polyester Fabric Classified under HSN 3921 at 18% GST: Case of SRF Limited, AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 25th September 2026. AAR ruled that the product Double-sided PVC coated polyester woven fabric (supplied in roll form or rectangular sheets) is classified under HSN 3921. The GST Rate of 18% is applicable. (Link: AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 25/09/2026) AAR, Reusable Textile Shopping Bags Classified under HSN 4202 2990, Attract 18% GST: Case of PAS Exports, AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 23rd September 2026. The applicant manufactured textile carry bags supplied to textile shops, supermarkets, corporate customers and other businesses. It claimed that its products were manufactured using waste cloth materials comprising polyester, cotton and viscose. AAR held that textile carry bags manufactured by the party from a blend of polyester, cotton and viscose are classifiable under HSN 4202 2990 as shopping bags and attract GST at 18%. (Link: AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 23/09/2026) AAR, Paper Carry Bags Ineligible for 5% GST under Entry 322: Case of Adhav Packs Private Limited, AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 23rd September 2026. AAR held that 18% GST applies to paper bags and paper carry bags manufactured by the party. These products are classified under HSN 4819 40 (“Other sacks and bags”) and are ineligible for the concessional 5% GST rate under Entry 322 of Schedule I to Notification No. 09/2025 (Rate). (Link: AAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 23/09/2026) GSTAT Reply Filing SOP, Step-by-Step Procedure for Respondents to File Replies on Portal: The Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the process for filing replies by respondents to appeals instituted against them. The procedure explains the electronic filing process through the GSTAT portal, enabling respondents to identify the relevant appeal, upload their reply documents, complete the prescribed submission steps and obtain confirmation of successful filing. (Link: GSTAT SOP Dated 29/09/2026) GSTAT SOP for Transfer of Appeals Across Different Benches Involving Identical Question of Law: The Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlining the process for filing a Transfer Appeal where multiple appeals involving an identical question of law have been filed before different GSTAT Benches across India. The procedure enables taxpayers having multiple GSTINs linked to same Permanent Account Number (PAN) to select relevant appeals and initiate a single Transfer Appeal through GSTAT application. (Link: GSTAT SOP Dated 29/09/2026) C. Central Excise No Notification/ Circular during the week. D. Custom Duty Customs, Allows Extension of Four-Month ATA Carnet Period: The notification amends earlier notification 4/2018 dated 18th January 2018. It introduces an additional proviso to condition (vi), empowering the Principal Chief Commissioner of Customs or Chief Commissioner of Customs, as applicable, to extend the prescribed four-month period where satisfied that such extension is necessary in the public interest. The extension may be granted for such further period as the competent authority considers appropriate, subject to the overall validity of the ATA Carnet. (Link: Customs Notification 36/2026 Dated 13/11/2026) Customs, Fixation of Tariff Value of Edible Oils, Brass Scrap, Areca Nut, Gold and Silver: CBDT notified the Tariff Values of Edible Oils, Brass Scrap, Areca Nut, Gold and Silver, which shall come into force w.e.f. 14th November 2026. The tariff value for crude palm oil is set at USD 1141 per metric ton, while gold and silver have tariff values of USD 1245 per 10 grams and USD 1663 per kilogram, respectively. The tariff value for areca nuts is fixed at USD 10433 per metric ton. (Link: Customs Notification 91/2026 (NT) Dated 13/11/2026) Customs, Anti-Dumping Duty Exemptions are Subject to IGCR Rules: The notification amends earlier notification 18/2026 dated 27th July 2026, which deals with anti-dumping duty on Low Ash Metallurgical Coke. It inserts a new proviso into paragraph 2 which stipulates that the specific duty exclusions under clauses (a) and (c) are valid only if the importer strictly complies with the procedures prescribed under the Customs (Import of Goods at Concessional Rate of Duty or for Specified End Use) Rules 2022 (IGCR). (Link: Customs Notification 28/2026 (ADD) Dated 13/11/2026) Customs, Allows 90-Day Import Permit Relief for Non-Insecticidal Chemicals: CBIC has introduced an interim facilitation measure for the clearance of scheduled substances imported for non-insecticidal purposes, including acrylonitrile. The relaxation is allowed for 90 day, and temporarily permits eligible consignments to be released against submission of an end-use bond instead of insisting on an import permit at clearance. (Link: Customs Instructions 23/2026 Dated 10/11/2026) E. Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) DGFT, Prohibits Export of Lithium-Ion Battery Black Mass: The notification amends export policy of waste and scrap of primary cells, primary batteries and electric accumulators, including lithium-ion battery black mass, falling under ITC (HS) Code 85491400. The export classification has been changed from ‘Free’ to ‘Prohibited’ with immediate effect. It covers unsorted waste and scrap of primary cells, primary batteries and electric accumulators, including spent cells, batteries and accumulators, which do not contain lead, cadmium or mercury. (Link: DGFT Notification 49/2026 Dated 10/11/2026) DGFT, Extends Halal Certification Deadline for Meat Exports to Egypt to 12 Months: The notification extends the transition period for implementing mandatory India Conformity Assessment Scheme (i-CAS)-Halal certification for specified meat and meat products exported to Egypt. The deadline is extended by an additional 3 months, moving the transition period from 9 months to 12 months from the date of the original notification, to allow more time for completing the onboarding and accreditation processes of Egyptian Halal certification bodies. (Link: DGFT Notification 48/2026 Dated 09/11/2026) DGFT, Extends Timeline for Rice Export Inspection Exemption: The notification amends the export policy conditions for Basmati and Non-Basmati rice under HSN 1006 of Schedule-II. It extends the rice export inspection exemption up to 1st April 2027. (Link: DGFT Notification 47/2026 Dated 09/11/2026) DGFT, Notifies India–New Zealand FTA Certificate of Origin Authorities: The public notice operationalise origin certification under the India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement (FTA). It amends Paragraph 2.88(a) of the Handbook of Procedures (HBP) by inserting the India–New Zealand FTA (Serial No. xvii) into India recognised trade agreements. It also updates Appendix 2B of Foreign Trade Policy (FTP) to notify agencies authorised to issue Preferential Certificates of Origin. Designated issuers include DGFT regional offices, the Export Inspection Council, commodity boards (like APEDA, MPEDA, and Spices Board), and seven Special Economic Zone authorities. (Link: DGFT Public Notice 40/2026 Dated 13/11/2026) DGFT, Electronic filing and Issuance of Preferential CoO under India-NZ FTA: The trade notice announced the facility for electronic filing and issuance of Preferential Certificates of Origin (CoO) for exports to New Zealand under the India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement (India-NZ FTA), which comes into force on 20th October 2026. The facility is available through the Trade Connect ePlatform, providing a unified digital platform for applications and issuance of electronic Certificates of Origin (eCOO) by authorised issuing agencies. Exporters can select the India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement under Certificate of Origin service and choose an authorised issuing agency. (Link: DGFT Trade Notice 34/2026 Dated 13/11/2026) F. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) SEBI, Revises FATCA and CRS Reporting for Intermediaries Under Income Tax Rules: The circular revises Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) and Common Reporting Standard (CRS) reporting requirements for all registered intermediaries, stock exchanges, and depositories. Under the updated framework, Automatic Exchange of Information reporting moves to Section 508 and Rules 238–240, replacing Form 61B with Form 166. Reporting Financial Institutions must integrate these updated checks into client onboarding and KYC processes, uploading complete self-certification XML records directly to KYC Registration Agencies. (Link: SEBI Circular Dated 11/11/2026) G. Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) No Notification/ Circular during the week. H. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) NCLAT, Hypothecation Deed can Create Corporate Guarantee: Case of Bindu Kapoor vs Mamta Binani, NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 6th November 2026. The appellate tribunal held that a company described as a hypothecator may qualify as a corporate guarantor where the financing documents impose an obligation to discharge the borrower’s debt upon default. The substance of the contractual obligations, rather than the document title, determines the position. (Link: NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 06/11/2026) NCLAT, Pre-Admission Settlement Bars CIRP Admission under Section 9 of IBC: Case of Jagmal vs Mangal Sales Corporation through its Proprietor, NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 30th June 2026. The appellate tribunal ruled that when a full and final debt settlement is reached between parties before the formal pronouncement of an admission order under Section 9 of the IBC, the Adjudicating Authority must defer the order and allow the petition to be withdrawn. (Link: NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 30/06/2026) IBBI, Suspends IRP Sh. Venkata Shivkumar IP over Rs 70 Lakh Transfer and Liquidation Lapses: The irregularities include transferring Rs 70 lakh of the Corporate Debtor into a personal fixed deposit after replacement as Liquidator, and misleading investigators regarding the replacement order upload date. He is accused of excluding the Rs 1,087.98 crore property from the liquidation estate and conducting valuations without appointing two registered valuers per asset class. The irregularities also include submitting a Section 230 scheme without the required 75% secured creditor consent, failing to preserve records and cooperate with investigator, and omitting relationship disclosures for engaged marketing consultants. The DC suspends the IRP registration for 3 years. (Link: IBBI DC Order Dated 09/11/2026) IBBI, Directs Disclosure of Delegation of Powers under RTI, Upholds Privacy Exemption for Officers: The First Appellate Authority (FAA) ordered the CPIO to furnish the requested Delegation of Powers document immediately. It upheld the Central Public Information Officer’s (CPIO) refusal to disclose the officers’ identities. It ruled that this constitutes personal information exempt under Section 8(1)(j), as no larger public interest was established. (Link: IBBI FAA Order Dated 10/11/2026) I. Reserve Bank of India (RBI) RBI Domestic Money Transfer Directions 2026: The directions consolidate the domestic money transfer framework into a single rulebook while repealing earlier circulars. The non-account holders at a bank branch can send up to Rs 50,000 in cash to a beneficiary’s bank account via NEFT. The cash transfers through Business Correspondents (BCs) or ATMs are capped at Rs 5,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 per month per remitter. The beneficiaries receiving cash payouts are limited to Rs 10,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 per month. The card-to-card transfers are excluded from these directions. The remitters must register a verified mobile number and submit a self-certified Officially Valid Document (OVD). (Link: RBI Master Directions 476/2026 Dated 11/11/2026) J. Miscellaneous SC, Property Inherited through Partition of Grandfather Estate Is Individual, Not HUF: Case of J Rajesh (Minor) vs M Kumaran (Dead) Through LRs, SC Judgement Dated 6th November 2026. The apex court held that a grandson does not have a birthright or an automatic half-share in property that devolved upon his living father through the partition of a grandfather estate. The property inherited by a son under Section 8 of the Hindu Succession Act 1956 is held in his individual capacity and does not become Hindu Undivided Family property. Consequently, a grandson cannot claim a share by birth in such property during his father lifetime. (Link: SC Judgement Dated 06/11/2026) ***** Compiled by:- CMA Yash Paul Bhola, MBA, FCMA.
