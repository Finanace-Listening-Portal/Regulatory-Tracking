Deteriorating asset quality indicators, amid a drought-like situation emerging in some key states, remain a concern; however, the stock has corrected 14 per cent over the past month, analysts at JM Financial Institutional Securities said. Shriram Finance is also relatively better placed given its lower insurance distribution income versus other frontline NBFCs. Also, the recent equity infusion coupled with recent rating upgrades to AAA should cushion margins in the current rate hike cycle. Improving disbursement momentum and controlled cost ratios provide further comfort, the brokerage firm said. “Moderation in credit growth guidance to 15-17 per cent and higher credit cost amid volatile monsoon remains a concern, however, recent correction in valuation offers upside potential,” ICICI Securities said in a note. According to analysts at Motilal Oswal Financial Services, Shriram Finance indicated that credit costs could rise temporarily by 30-40bp over the next one or two quarters due to seasonal stress in affected geographies, but they are still expected to remain below 2.3 per cent for FY27. Elevated stress is mainly attributable to deficient rainfall and seasonal pressures in select regions, with no evidence of widespread portfolio deterioration. Lower agricultural output could weigh on rural incomes, consumption, and borrower cash flows in the coming quarters. The asset quality outlook for the company remains cautious amid these near-term headwinds. “The company delivered a mixed quarter, with PPoP ((Pre-Provision Operating Profit) beating estimates on the back of higher NIM and lower opex, while earnings were in line owing to higher credit costs. AUM growth at 17 per cent YoY was stronger than our estimate; however, credit costs increased amid higher slippages and write-offs, primarily reflecting drought-like conditions across multiple states in India.
