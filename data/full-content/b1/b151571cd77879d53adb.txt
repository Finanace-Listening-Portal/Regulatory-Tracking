Articles 2 min read SBI says 3,897 customer records exposed on dark web SBI was alerted on October 16 by its threat intelligence service provider, Defence Cyber Agency and CERT-In about suspected customer data exposure on the dark web; the bank activated cyber incident response protocols and brought in an independent CERT-In-empanelled agency to investigate. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Oct 21, 2026 at 12:01 PM IST var news_msid = 135084257; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Country’s largest lender State Bank of India (SBI) has identified 3,897 customer records containing personally identifiable information (PII) in a preliminary analysis of data available on the dark web, after receiving alerts from its threat intelligence service provider, the Defence Cyber Agency and CERT-In.The bank received the communications on October 16 regarding an alert concerning the suspected exposure of its customer data on the dark web, SBI said in a regulatory disclosure on Wednesday.During its preliminary analysis of approximately 375,000 records available on the dark web, the bank identified 3,897 customer records containing PII. Advt The exposed information reportedly included customers’ registered mobile numbers, dates of birth, names, gender, email addresses and residential addresses. It also included branch and CIF identifiers, lead IDs, and metadata associated with loan or lead records and other related files, SBI said.SBI, however, said a detailed investigation was required to establish the authenticity and completeness of the exposed data, determine the potential source and extent of the exposure, and assess the associated risks to customers and the bank.“The Bank promptly activated its cyber incident response and containment protocols,” SBI said.The lender has also engaged an independent CERT-In-empanelled cybersecurity agency to conduct a comprehensive assessment and investigate the nature and extent of the alleged compromise.The bank said the incident did not have any material impact on its core business operations, financial performance or business continuity.“The Bank’s core business functions continue to operate normally without disruptions,” SBI said, adding that a detailed assessment was currently underway and appropriate remedial and preventive measures were being implemented as required.Three months earlier state-owned Bank of Baroda had disclosed that an employee’s email account had been compromised, resulting in unauthorised access to certain data, which was followed by claims that nearly 1 TB of customer and internal data had surfaced on the dark web. The alleged dataset was reported to include customer details, identification documents, loan papers and internal audit records, although the bank did not confirm the extent or nature of the alleged data leak.
