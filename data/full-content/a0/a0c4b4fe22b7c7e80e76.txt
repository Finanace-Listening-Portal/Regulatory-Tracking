Summary: Importers of pre-packaged commodities into India must examine their responsibilities under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, along with applicable Customs and product-specific requirements. LMPC registration under Rule 27 records the particulars of manufacturers, packers and importers, but it does not constitute approval of individual products or their labels. Importers must separately review mandatory declarations relating to the responsible business, country of origin, generic product name, net quantity, maximum retail price, consumer-care information and other applicable particulars. The prescribed registration application fee is ₹500, and departmental guidance specifies a 90-day application timeline from commencement of the relevant activity. The departmental FAQs also address exemptions, e-commerce declarations and labelling obligations. Important 2026 changes include the continuing validity of Rule 27 certificates until cancellation, annual updating of changed particulars, identification of the responsible company director and arrangements for affixing declarations on imported packages. An October 2026 advisory clarifies the applicable Customs-warehouse arrangements and the treatment of qualifying AEO Tier-2 and Tier-3 importers. Additional developments concern origin-related symbols on cosmetics and toiletries and country-of-origin filters for e-commerce listings from 1 July 2027. This guide explains registration, documentation, exemptions, labelling, procedural requirements and practical precautions to help importers plan compliant shipments. Along with obtaining an Importer Exporter Code and completing Customs formalities, businesses must assess their obligations under Legal Metrology law. These requirements concern importer registration, accurate quantity declarations, pricing information and other details provided to consumers. Legal Metrology Packaged Commodities registration, commonly called LMPC registration, is relevant to businesses importing covered pre-packaged commodities for sale, distribution or delivery. However, obtaining the certificate is only one part of compliance. The packages themselves must also carry the applicable declarations. Understanding registration and labelling together helps importers plan shipments, coordinate with overseas suppliers and reduce avoidable corrections. What Is LMPC Registration? LMPC stands for Legal Metrology Packaged Commodities. The term “LMPC certificate” commonly refers to registration under Rule 27 of the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011. This registration records the relevant business particulars, including the applicant’s name, complete address and commodities. Although businesses frequently call it an LMPC licence, it is a registration under the packaged-commodity rules. An LMPC certificate does not certify product quality, safety or performance. It also does not automatically establish that every package imported by the registered business complies with the labelling requirements. Why Is LMPC Compliance Important for Importers? Legal Metrology requirements help consumers understand what they are purchasing. Information about quantity, price, origin and the responsible business allows buyers to compare products and raise complaints when necessary. For importers, compliance also affects shipment preparation and product distribution. Missing or incorrect declarations can create queries, require corrective work and delay business operations. The most practical approach is to assess the requirements before approving packaging or placing the purchase order. Changing artwork before production is generally easier than correcting an entire shipment. Legal Provisions Governing LMPC Registration The main legislation is the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, read with the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, subsequent amendments and applicable directions. Different provisions address different responsibilities. Rule 27 concerns registration of manufacturers, packers and importers. Other provisions govern declarations, their placement and presentation, retail sale and wholesale packages. Separate requirements apply to prescribed weighing and measuring instruments. Importer registration for packaged commodities should therefore be distinguished from registration and model approval connected with weights and measures. What Is a Pre-Packaged Commodity? A pre-packaged commodity generally means a commodity placed in a package without the purchaser being present, with a predetermined quantity. The package does not necessarily have to be sealed. The assessment depends on how the product is packed and supplied. A product arriving inside a carton does not, by itself, establish the full legal treatment. Businesses should distinguish an individual retail package from an outer transport carton. They should also identify whether the goods are intended for retail consumers, intermediaries or a qualifying industrial or institutional use. Who Should Assess LMPC Importer Registration? Importers of Retail-Packaged Goods Businesses importing products already packed for retail sale should examine LMPC applicability before shipment. Examples may include packaged household goods, consumer accessories, stationery and other retail products. The precise requirements depend on the goods, packaging and intended supply arrangement. E-Commerce Importers Selling through an online marketplace or an independent website does not automatically remove packaged-commodity obligations. Importers should review both the physical package and the online product information. A correct product listing does not remedy a defective package, and compliant packaging does not automatically make an online listing compliant. Own-Brand Importers Businesses sourcing products from overseas manufacturers under their own brand should identify the manufacturer, importer and other relevant parties accurately. Before approving artwork, confirm that the information reflects the actual arrangement. A brand name or logo should not replace the identity details required on the package. Importers Repacking Goods in India A business importing goods and repacking them domestically should assess whether its activities also involve packer-related responsibilities. The review should identify where repacking occurs, who performs it and which entity approves the final retail package. These facts help determine the appropriate registration and operating arrangements. Are Any Products or Supplies Exempt? Industrial and Institutional Supplies The rules provide specific treatment for packages intended for qualifying industrial or institutional consumers. Relevant conditions concern the purchaser, supply arrangement, intended use and the applicable “not for retail sale” declaration. Printing “not for retail sale” on a package does not automatically create an exemption. The underlying supply must meet the relevant legal conditions. Large Packages and Other Exclusions Certain provisions contain exclusions based on package quantities, uses or specified commodities. These exclusions must be read in their proper context. An exclusion from retail-package declarations should not automatically be interpreted as an exemption from all requirements, including registration. Wholesale packages also have separate provisions. Businesses claiming an exemption should retain supporting records, such as product specifications, purchase orders and intended-use documents. When Should an Importer Apply? Departmental guidance describes the Rule 27 application timeline as within 90 days of commencing the relevant pre-packing or importing activity. This timeline should not be treated as permission to import, distribute or sell packages carrying incomplete declarations. Registration and package compliance are separate obligations. Importers should begin their assessment before the first shipment. This provides time to identify the competent authority, prepare documents and arrange compliant packaging. Which Authority Handles LMPC Registration? Rule 27 refers to registration through the Director or Controller of Legal Metrology, as applicable. The correct application route should be confirmed for the business and its operations. Central and state systems can have different submission arrangements and document requirements. Where a business uses multiple premises, it should establish how those locations must be disclosed. Avoid assuming that a registration obtained through any available portal automatically covers every activity or location. Documents and Information Required Business Identity Documents Prepare the documents relevant to the applicant’s legal constitution. Depending on the applicable checklist, these may include incorporation or partnership records, PAN, GST information and identification of authorised persons. Names and addresses should be consistent across the records. Any difference should be resolved or clearly explained before submission. Import and Premises Information Keep the IEC and relevant premises information ready where required by the application process. If importing, storage and repacking occur at different locations, explain the arrangement accurately. The information submitted should reflect the business’s actual operations. Product Particulars Prepare clear descriptions of the commodities, packaging formats, quantities and countries of origin. A product register can help connect registration details with import documents and package artwork. It should be updated when suppliers or specifications change. Responsible Director Information The May 2026 amendment added the name of the company director responsible for violations under the Act and rules to the Rule 27 application particulars. Companies should examine this requirement alongside their internal authorisations and relevant nomination arrangements. The information should be accurate and supported by appropriate records. Step-by-Step LMPC Registration Process Step 1: Assess the Products and Business Activities Identify the goods, packaging and intended customers. Determine whether the business imports finished retail packages, wholesale supplies or goods that will be repacked. Record the basis of any proposed exemption rather than relying only on a supplier’s description. Step 2: Confirm the Competent Authority Identify the correct authority and obtain the current application instructions. This helps prevent preparation around an outdated form or a registration category intended for a different activity. Step 3: Prepare the Application File Compile business records, premises details, commodity information and applicable declarations. Review the file as a whole. Documents should agree with each other and with the proposed import arrangement. Step 4: Submit the Application and Fee Complete the prescribed application and pay through the accepted channel. Retain a copy of the submission, payment acknowledgement and correspondence. These records will help with queries and later changes. Step 5: Respond to Observations Provide complete explanations and supporting documents where clarification is requested. If a document is corrected, check whether the change affects other particulars. Avoid leaving different versions of the business information in the application file. Step 6: Verify the Certificate After registration, review the recorded name, address and commodity particulars. Establish an internal process for reviewing changes and maintaining the registration information. The certificate should remain consistent with actual operations. LMPC Registration Fees and Processing Time The prescribed Rule 27 application fee is ₹500. The rules also prescribe ₹100 for an alteration to the registration certificate. These amounts are separate from professional charges or other applicable service costs. A quotation should clearly distinguish statutory fees from the cost of assistance. Processing time depends on the application route, completeness of documents and observations raised. Businesses should avoid treating an estimated service timeline as a guaranteed government approval period. Mandatory Declarations on Imported Retail Packages Manufacturer, Packer and Importer Details Covered packages generally require the relevant identity and address information, subject to product-specific provisions. For imported products, the Indian importer’s details should accurately identify the responsible business. Check the information against the registration and commercial records before printing. Country of Origin Imported products require the applicable country-of-origin, manufacture or assembly declaration. Obtain reliable information from the supplier. The country from which goods are shipped should not automatically be treated as their country of origin. Common or Generic Product Name The package should identify what the commodity is. A brand name, promotional phrase or model number may not adequately explain the product. For a package containing several products, review the requirements concerning the name and number or quantity of its contents. Net Quantity Net quantity should be declared in the applicable standard unit of weight, measure or number. The quantity of the commodity must be distinguished from its packaging. Gross shipping weight should not be copied onto the retail label as net quantity. Maximum Retail Price Covered retail packages generally require the MRP inclusive of all taxes. Businesses should establish a controlled process for approving the declaration. Any later price change should be assessed against the applicable rules rather than made informally. Manufacturing and Shelf-Life Information The applicable manufacturing-date declaration should be reviewed alongside any product-specific requirements. Where relevant, shelf-life information such as the best-before or use-by date must also be considered. Food, cosmetics and other regulated products can require a combined review under more than one law. Consumer-Care Information The package should provide the applicable consumer-care details so that buyers can contact the responsible business. Check that the telephone number, email address and address information are accurate and monitored. Printing a contact detail that is no longer used undermines its purpose. Dimensions and Unit Sale Price Depending on the product and applicable provisions, dimensions and unit sale price may also be required. The review should consider the package’s actual contents and presentation. A standard label used across unrelated products may overlook these requirements. Presentation of Label Information Required declarations must be presented in the prescribed manner. Placement, visibility and lettering requirements are part of compliance. Importers should review the physical print sample rather than approving only an enlarged digital image. Text that appears clear on a computer may be unreadable on the final package. Maintain an approved artwork version for each product. This helps prevent suppliers from reusing an outdated design. Can Labels Be Affixed After Import? The rules permit labels on imported packages for making required declarations, but businesses must comply with the conditions governing where and when the labels are affixed. The October 2026 advisory distinguishes eligible AEO Tier-2 and Tier-3 importers from other importers. Eligible AEO importers may use approved importer premises where permitted under applicable Customs procedures. For other importers, declarations must be completed in the specified Customs or importer bonded warehouses before clearance for home consumption. Labels must be securely affixed and comply with the restrictions concerning original declarations and alterations. Importers should establish the permitted labelling arrangement with their logistics and Customs teams before dispatch. Validity and Annual Updating Requirements The Third Amendment Rules, 2026, state that Rule 27 registration certificates remain valid until cancelled. The amendment also introduces annual updating requirements for companies and firms concerning changed particulars, with portal arrangements for information including address, products and their numbers, and country of origin. Continuing validity does not remove the responsibility to keep information accurate. Assign a person to review the registration details against actual business operations.This registration should not be confused with the separate registration regime for importing weights and measures, which has different requirements. Other Important 2026 Developments Cosmetics and Toiletries Labelling The September 2026 amendment introduced origin-related dot declarations for soap, shampoo, toothpaste and other cosmetics and toiletries. It specifies a red or brown dot, as applicable, for products of non-vegetarian origin and a green dot for products of vegetarian origin at the top of the principal display panel. Importers in these categories should include the amendment in their artwork review alongside the applicable product-specific legislation. Country-of-Origin Filters for E-Commerce The April 2026 amendment specifies 1 July 2027 for the searchable and sortable country-of-origin filter requirement for imported-product listings. Businesses should distinguish this future requirement from existing obligations concerning declarations on digital listings. Product-origin data should be organised in advance so that website and marketplace teams can implement the requirement. Does LMPC Replace Other Import Approvals? LMPC registration does not replace other applicable approvals. Depending on the goods, an importer may also need to assess IEC, BIS requirements, food or cosmetics permissions, environmental obligations and Customs conditions. For example, an electronic product may require several separate assessments. An LMPC certificate alone does not establish that the product meets safety standards, environmental requirements or import-policy conditions. Common Mistakes Importers Should Avoid Treating Registration as Label Approval: Registration records the business particulars. It does not automatically approve every package used by the importer. Maintain a separate product-by-product labelling review. Copying Foreign Packaging Without Review: An overseas supplier’s existing packaging may not contain the declarations required for the Indian market. Provide written specifications and approve the final artwork before production. Assuming All B2B Supplies Are Exempt: Selling to another business does not automatically establish an industrial or institutional exemption. Check the purchaser, intended use and supply conditions. Confusing Different Registrations: Packaged-commodity registration and registration for importing weighing or measuring instruments serve different purposes. Select the application route according to the goods and activities involved. Leaving Corrections Until the Shipment Arrives Late corrections can create logistical complications, particularly where labelling must occur in a permitted location. Plan the artwork, labels and warehouse arrangements before dispatch. Conclusion Legal Metrology registration is an important responsibility for businesses importing packaged commodities into India. However, obtaining an LMPC certificate is only one part of compliance. Importers must also assess product classification, verify applicable declarations and ensure that packages carry accurate information about quantity, price, origin and the responsible business. Reviewing these requirements before shipment helps identify packaging errors early, coordinate corrections with overseas suppliers and reduce avoidable difficulties during clearance and distribution across the market. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ’s) Q1. What does LMPC stand for? Ans. LMPC stands for Legal Metrology Packaged Commodities. The term is commonly used for registration and compliance under the Packaged Commodities Rules. Q2. Is an LMPC certificate a product-quality certificate? Ans. No. Rule 27 registration records relevant business particulars. Product safety and quality approvals are separate requirements where applicable. Q3. Does an IEC replace LMPC registration? Ans. No. IEC and LMPC registration serve different purposes. Importers must assess the requirements separately. Q4. Is LMPC relevant to online sales? Ans. Yes. Online selling does not automatically remove packaged-commodity obligations. Physical labels and online listings should both be reviewed. Q5. Can import labels be affixed in India? Ans. Permitted arrangements exist, but location, timing and Customs conditions matter. Importers should confirm the appropriate process before shipment. Q6. Does Rule 27 registration expire after five years? Ans. The May 2026 amendment states that registration certificates remain valid until cancelled. Applicable updating requirements continue. Q7. Are all industrial supplies exempt? Ans. No. The supply must satisfy the relevant definitions and conditions. A business customer’s name on an invoice is not sufficient by itself. Q8. What is the prescribed registration fee? Ans. The Rule 27 application fee is ₹500. Professional and other applicable service charges should be identified separately. Q9. Can one label format be used for every product? Ans. Not necessarily. Product quantity, contents, dimensions, shelf life and other legislation can affect the declarations required. Q10. What should an importer do before ordering goods? Ans. Assess applicability, confirm registration arrangements, review the final package artwork and establish where any permitted Indian labelling will take place.
