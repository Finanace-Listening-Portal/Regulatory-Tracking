Summary: Star Health Super Star is an individual health insurance product offered by Star Health and Allied Insurance Company Limited under IRDAI UIN SHAHLIP27049V022627. The policy offers hospitalisation and daycare treatment coverage, modern-treatment benefits, annual health check-ups, restoration of sum insured and cumulative enhancement of cover, subject to the selected configuration and policy conditions. The insurer’s product information identifies a 30-day initial waiting period, a 24-month waiting period for specified diseases and procedures, and a 36-month waiting period for pre-existing diseases. The plan also advertises features such as unlimited restoration, Freeze Your Age, premium waiver and optional Super Star Bonus, but their applicability depends on eligibility, selection and contractual conditions. Premiums vary according to age, location, sum insured, family composition, medical history, tenure and optional benefits. Buyers should verify the policy wording, prospectus, customer information sheet and final schedule before purchase, particularly regarding exclusions, waiting-period reductions, maternity cover, co-payments, sublimits and claim documentation. The insurer states that eligible individual and family-floater health insurance policies have nil GST from 22 September 2025, while group health insurance continues to receive different tax treatment. Eligible taxpayers using the old income-tax regime may also claim a deduction under Section 80D, subject to statutory limits, payment conditions and the applicable assessment year. Understanding the distinction between advertised features and contractual entitlements is essential when comparing premiums, assessing coverage and filing claims. Policyholders should retain proposal disclosures, premium receipts and claim records, and seek written clarification from the insurer wherever the marketing material and policy wording appear inconsistent. Waiting periods determine when coverage begins Three separate clocks The initial waiting period is 30 days from commencement, with the usual accident exception stated by the insurer. Specified diseases and procedures carry a 24-month waiting period, while declared and accepted pre-existing diseases carry a 36-month waiting period. These clocks do different work. A condition may be outside the initial 30-day bar yet still fall under the specified-disease or pre-existing-disease restriction. Continuous coverage, portability or migration credit may affect served waiting periods only to the extent allowed by regulation and recognised in the policy record. Optional reductions do not erase every restriction The insurer presents optional waiting-period reductions and a Quick Shield feature for identified conditions from day 31. Such options should be confirmed in the quotation and schedule. Paying for one reduction does not automatically reduce another waiting period, cure non-disclosure or convert an exclusion into coverage. Maternity is also presented as an optional cover, with waiting-period variants and limits; the current FAQ refers to a maximum of four deliveries. The precise sublimit, eligible expenses and waiting-period election should be taken from the selected option. Exclusions and benefit limits that can change the claim result The insurer’s current summary identifies exclusions including obesity or weight-control treatment, dietary supplements unless forming part of admissible treatment, substance-abuse treatment, hazardous or adventure sports, cosmetic surgery unless an allowed exception applies, unproven treatment and intentional self-injury. Vaccination is generally listed as excluded, though a separately worded health-check-up benefit may include a limited vaccination component. Cashless and reimbursement claims: records drive the decision Cashless route For planned treatment at a network hospital, the insured should seek pre-authorisation within the policy timeline through the hospital insurance desk. Emergency admission should be notified promptly. The insurer or its authorised claims service examines coverage, medical necessity and available limits before approving, querying or declining cashless treatment. A cashless denial does not invariably decide the final claim; reimbursement may still be submitted where permitted, with complete records. Conversely, pre-authorisation remains subject to the final bill, discharge summary and policy conditions. Reimbursement route and claim documentation A reimbursement file commonly requires the claim form, identity and policy details, admission advice, discharge summary, diagnostic reports, prescriptions, itemised bills, payment receipts and bank information. The exact checklist and deadlines in the policy wording control. The claimant should retain copies, obtain an acknowledgement and answer queries with traceable documents. If a claim is denied or partly disallowed, IRDAI guidance requires the insurer to state reasons and refer to the relevant policy conditions. GST and income-tax treatment in 2026 GST on the premium Star Health’s current GST FAQ states that individual health policies, family-floater plans and senior-citizen health policies are subject to nil GST from 22 September 2025, while group health insurance continues to attract 18% GST. The tax treatment should be verified on the actual invoice because product character, risk-commencement date, group status and later law changes can matter. The GST exemption changes the premium tax component; it does not enlarge the sum insured, remove exclusions or change the claim procedure. Section 80D is regime- and fact-sensitive For taxpayers using the old tax regime, qualifying health-insurance premium may be deductible under section 80D, subject to the statutory limits, eligible relationships and payment-mode conditions. Broadly, the familiar annual limit is ₹25,000 for self, spouse and dependent children, increased to ₹50,000 where the insured is a senior citizen; a separate parent limit can apply on the same logic. Preventive health check-up is restricted to ₹5,000 within, not over and above, the applicable ceiling. Premium ordinarily must be paid other than in cash, while the preventive-check-up component has a cash exception. The deduction is generally unavailable under the default new regime. Policyholders should use the law and return form applicable to their assessment year and retain the premium receipt, insurer name and policy number.
