Summary: SEBI’s circular dated 5 November 2026 revises the accredited-investor framework with immediate effect. A manager of an alternative investment fund, the asset management company of a specialised investment fund, or a portfolio manager may determine an investor’s accredited status at onboarding or during the relationship after verifying the prescribed eligibility. The manager-led route sits alongside certificates issued by recognised Accreditation Agencies. Accreditation generally remains valid for three years; an investor accredited when entering a particular scheme continues to be treated as accredited for that scheme’s life. The circular also introduces securities-market exposure thresholds, clarifies documentary evidence and special treatment for limited liability partnerships and persons resident outside India, and preserves anti-money-laundering, customer-due-diligence and beneficial-ownership duties. This guide converts those changes into an evidence-led workflow for managers, distributors, compliance teams and investors without treating accreditation as a risk rating or investment recommendation. The revised framework allows the relevant manager to determine whether an investor qualifies as an accredited investor. For an alternative investment fund, that role is performed by the AIF manager; for a specialised investment fund, by the asset management company; and for portfolio management services, by the portfolio manager. The manager must verify the applicable financial or professional criterion before recording the status. This is an additional operational route, not a licence to accept a self-declaration without evidence. Recognised Accreditation Agencies continue to issue accreditation certificates. A manager should document which route was used, who reviewed the file, what criterion was met, the measurement date, the evidence obtained and the start and end of validity. Product eligibility, minimum investment, suitability, risk disclosure and contractual consent remain separate questions. A manager should calculate the validity from the date established under the selected route and should not substitute the date on which an investor later subscribes. The client master should preserve the original verification date, expiry date, qualifying criterion and supporting-document dates. Alerts should be generated early enough to complete renewal before the status is used for a new product or scheme. A Fresh Review May Be Needed Across Groups When an investor is onboarded by a manager that is not part of the same Group as the manager that earlier accredited the investor, a fresh accreditation is required under the revised framework. The receiving manager should not infer portability merely because an investor has participated in another AIF, SIF or portfolio. The file should identify the prior accrediting entity, test the Group relationship and record why an existing accreditation can or cannot be relied upon. Status for an Existing Scheme An investor accredited at onboarding for a scheme continues to be treated as accredited for that scheme’s life. This continuity prevents a later expiry from automatically unsettling participation already established under the scheme. It does not mean that the same expired status can be used for a new scheme, a fresh portfolio mandate or another manager. Systems should separate scheme-level continuity from the general accreditation-expiry field. Financial Criteria Now Include Securities-Market Exposure The circular introduces securities-market exposure as an alternative financial criterion. For an individual, Hindu undivided family, family trust or sole proprietorship, the stated exposure threshold is ₹5 crore. For a body corporate or other trust, it is ₹20 crore. The classification of the applicant matters: the exposure of a sole proprietorship is assessed under the individual-category threshold, while an LLP has its own partner-level rule. Exposure should be measured on evidence that identifies the investor, valuation date, securities included and aggregation method. A single demat screenshot is not automatically a complete exposure statement. The reviewer should reconcile multiple accounts, joint holdings and entity-level holdings, apply the circular’s scope, and retain a calculation explaining exclusions or adjustments. The framework does not convert market value into guaranteed wealth, nor does it establish that the investor understands a particular product. Net worth may be supported by audited financial statements or a chartered accountant certificate that is not older than six months. The reviewer should verify that the certificate identifies the applicant, measurement date, components included, liabilities deducted and professional credentials. A generic solvency statement that does not address the prescribed criterion should not be treated as equivalent evidence. Exposure Evidence Securities-market exposure may be evidenced by an electronic consolidated account statement summary not older than six months or by a chartered accountant certificate showing the exposure during the preceding six months. The file should preserve the complete source document and a reviewer-prepared reconciliation, rather than only a cropped total. Where prices or account balances move materially, the accreditation conclusion should be tied to the date and method supported by the circular. Existing Certificates Accreditation certificates already issued by recognised Accreditation Agencies continue until their stated expiry. The transition should therefore preserve existing certificate validity rather than forcing immediate reissuance. Managers should nevertheless authenticate the certificate, capture its expiry and check whether it is being used for a scheme or relationship permitted by the revised portability and Group rules. PROI Treatment Does Not Remove AML and FEMA Checks The circular treats a person resident outside India as deemed accredited and does not require the usual certificate or manager-led accreditation process. The manager must record the basis for that status. Residence for this purpose should be established under the applicable law and documents; nationality, mailing address or foreign telephone number alone is not a reliable substitute. The deemed status is confined to accreditation and does not decide whether a proposed investment is permitted under FEMA, sectoral conditions or product rules. Anti-money-laundering, customer-due-diligence, beneficial-ownership, sanctions and source-of-funds obligations continue. A deemed accredited investor may still require enhanced due diligence. The compliance record should therefore contain both the accreditation-status basis and the separate KYC/AML risk assessment, with clear approvals and escalations. Treating accreditation as a low-risk flag would defeat the circular’s safeguards. Record Retention and Auditability Managers should retain accreditation records for five years from the date of accreditation or five years after the investor ceases association, whichever is later. The record should be reproducible: application, evidence, calculations, verification notes, approvals, validity, Group analysis, scheme association, renewal and exceptions should be linked. Access should be restricted because the file contains sensitive tax, wealth and investment information. A defensible control reconciles the accreditation register to investor and scheme masters. Periodic testing should identify expired status used for new onboarding, missing partner files for LLPs, evidence older than the permitted period, unsupported PROI classifications and accreditation relied on across unrelated Groups. Corrections should preserve the original decision and create an auditable amendment rather than overwriting history. Implementation Actions for Managers Update onboarding forms to identify the accreditation route and qualifying criterion. Configure three-year expiry, scheme-life continuity and Group-based fresh-review rules separately. Build applicant-category logic for ₹5 crore and ₹20 crore exposure thresholds and the LLP partner test. Standardise income, net-worth and exposure evidence with six-month currency checks where applicable. Create a dedicated PROI status record without weakening KYC, AML, beneficial-owner or FEMA controls. Preserve existing agency certificates until expiry and authenticate every certificate relied upon. Reconcile the accreditation register to schemes and retain the record for the applicable five-year period. Frequently Asked Questions When did SEBI’s revised accredited-investor framework take effect? The circular dated 5 November 2026 states that the revised framework is effective immediately. Can an AIF manager itself verify accredited status? Yes. The relevant AIF manager, SIF asset management company or portfolio manager may determine status after verifying the prescribed criterion and retaining evidence. How long is accreditation generally valid? The revised validity period is three years, subject to scheme-life continuity and the rules for fresh accreditation in another Group. Does accreditation expiring end an investor’s existing scheme participation? An investor accredited at onboarding continues to be treated as accredited for that particular scheme’s life, but the expired status should not be reused automatically for a new scheme. What securities-market exposure threshold applies to an individual? The circular states ₹5 crore for an individual and the other applicant categories grouped with individuals. How is an LLP tested? Each partner of the LLP must independently satisfy the applicable accreditation criterion; an aggregate LLP balance alone is insufficient. Must a person resident outside India obtain a certificate? The circular treats a PROI as deemed accredited, but the manager must document the basis and continue all applicable KYC, AML, FEMA and product controls. Does accredited status mean an investment is suitable? No. Accreditation is an eligibility status and does not replace suitability, risk disclosure, product eligibility or professional advice. Key Takeaways Manager-led verification is permitted, but evidence and accountability remain with the relevant manager. Accreditation generally lasts three years, while an existing scheme receives scheme-life continuity. Exposure thresholds are ₹5 crore for the individual group and ₹20 crore for bodies corporate and other trusts. LLPs require partner-level eligibility; PROI status is deemed but must be documented.
