Articles 2 min read Spike in oil prices partly reverses rupee rally, forward premiums surge The Indian rupee has faced depreciation as a result of escalating oil prices combined with pressures from surrounding currencies. With Brent crude exceeding $96 per barrel, worries about India's import costs and inflation have intensified. Recent days have seen heightened hedging activity among importers, leading analysts to forecast potential testing of the rupee's support levels. Additionally, banks are urging central bank intervention to manage the growing dollar surplus. That shift had encouraged some investors to rotate funds towards India, supporting the rupee even as broader regional sentiment weakened.Bankers said the rupee's recent rally had also prompted importers to step up hedging activity.The cost of hedging through the forward market has risen sharply. The one-year USD/INR forward premium climbed to its highest level since January 2022 on Thursday and has increased by more than 70 basis points in just 10 days.Banks have sought the central bank's intervention in managing a dollar surplus which has been pushing up overnight FX swap rates and rupee forward premiums, three bankers said.
