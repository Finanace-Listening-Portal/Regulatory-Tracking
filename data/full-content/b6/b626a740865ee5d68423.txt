Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Rameshbhai Bhikhabhai Bajaniya Vs ITO (ITAT Ahmedabad) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Ahmedabad Bench, in Rameshbhai Bhikhabhai Bajaniya Vs ITO, partly allowed an assessee’s appeal relating to Assessment Year 2017-18 by sustaining an addition of ₹47,24,000 under Section 68 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, while directing the Assessing Officer to apply normal income-tax rates instead of the higher rate prescribed under the amended Section 115BBE. The Bench comprising Dr. BRR Kumar, Vice President, and Shri Tapas Ram Misra, Judicial Member, pronounced its order on 16 November 2026. The appeal arose from the order dated 31 October 2025 passed by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi. The dispute originated from cash deposits made in the assessee’s ICICI Bank account during the demonetisation period. The Assessing Officer treated deposits aggregating to ₹47,24,000 as unexplained cash credits. The assessee maintained that the deposits were supported by earlier cash withdrawals and business transactions. Before the Tribunal, the authorised representative produced a cash book for 1 April 2016 to 31 December 2016, sample sale and purchase invoices, and an ICICI Bank certificate recording withdrawals. The material showed withdrawals aggregating to ₹57,83,840 during April–June 2016, along with further withdrawals of ₹4,42,000 and ₹4,00,000 in October 2016. A significant circumstance was that the assessee deposited ₹45,00,000 in cash on 11 November 2016 and transferred an identical amount through RTGS on 12 November 2016. The Assessing Officer questioned the commercial substance of the transactions, including discrepancies in sale proceeds, the nature of purchase and sale bills, and the non-availability of sellers at the addresses furnished. The assessee also failed to identify the recipient of the ₹45 lakh RTGS transfer. The Tribunal observed that the declared business turnover was ₹31,26,700, whereas total bank credits amounted to ₹1,24,51,786. It found the explanation for retaining substantial cash for several months, making additional withdrawals in October and subsequently transferring the deposited money to an unidentified person unsatisfactory. The assessee separately contended that, having declared income under the presumptive taxation provisions of Section 44AD, he was not required to maintain books of account and therefore Section 68 could not be invoked. Reliance was placed on the Ahmedabad Tribunal’s decision in Narendra Chandubhai Rafaliya Vs ITO, where a separate addition had been disapproved after the Assessing Officer accepted the declared presumptive business receipts. The Tribunal distinguished that decision on its facts, particularly because the bank credits in the present case substantially exceeded the declared turnover and the assessee had failed to establish the source and subsequent movement of the cash. On the question of books of account, the Tribunal held that the assessee’s business receipts exceeded ₹10 lakh and attracted the statutory obligation under Section 44AA(2). It reasoned that Section 44AD provides a presumptive method of computing business profits but does not, by itself, override the applicable obligation to maintain books of account. Relying on the Bombay High Court’s decision in Arunkumar J. Muchhala Vs CIT, the Tribunal observed that a taxpayer could not take advantage of his own failure to maintain records and thereby avoid explaining the nature and source of credits. Accordingly, the addition of ₹47,24,000 under Section 68 was upheld, and the assessee’s second ground of appeal was dismissed. The first ground, challenging the assessment proceedings, had already been dismissed as not pressed. However, the assessee succeeded on the additional ground concerning the applicable tax rate under Section 115BBE. The assessee argued that the higher 60% rate introduced by the Taxation Laws (Second Amendment) Act, 2016, could not be applied retrospectively to AY 2017-18. Reliance was placed on several coordinate-bench decisions and the Rajasthan High Court judgment in Deepak Maratha Vs Union of India, which held that the amended provision was applicable from FY 2017-18, relevant to AY 2018-19. Following those authorities, the Ahmedabad Tribunal held that applying the amended higher rate to AY 2017-18 would be impermissible. It directed the Assessing Officer to apply normal tax rates to the assessed income, including the addition under Section 68. The appeal was consequently partly allowed. The decision distinguishes two separate questions: whether an unexplained cash deposit can be sustained as an addition under Section 68, and which tax rate applies to that addition for the relevant assessment year. Although the assessee failed to establish the source of the disputed cash deposits, the Tribunal granted relief on the rate of taxation. The order therefore provides an important distinction between the sustainability of an income addition and the temporal applicability of an amended taxing provision. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT AHMEDABAD The present appeal has been filed by the Assessee against the order of the Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre (hereinafter referred to as “NFAC”), Delhi (hereinafter referred to as “CIT(A)”), dated 31.10.2025 passed under Section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as the “Act”) and relates to Assessment Year (A.Y.) 2017-18. 2. The assessee has raised the following grounds of appeal in the Form 36 and an additional ground of appeal filed on 19-08-2026. 1. That the learned CIT(Appeal)/National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi has erred in law and facts by not quashing the assessment order, (a) as the notice issued under section 143(2) of the Act is not format issued by the Hon’ble Central Board of Direct Taxes and beyond his Jurisdiction and (b) further the assessment procedure adopted by the learned AO is against the law and therefore the order passed by the ld.AO is required to be quashed and the ld.AO should be directed to accept the returned income. 2. That the learned CIT(Appeal)/National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi has erred in law and facts by confirming the addition of Rs.47,24,000/- u/s 68 r.w.s. 115BBE of the Act for cash deposit in ICICI Bank Account No. 062505000660 as unexplained cash credit and therefore the learned AO should be directed to delete the said addition while computing the total income. 3. That your appellant craves a leave to add, alter OR amend any grounds at the time of hearing. Additional Ground: 1 That the learned National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi has erred in law and facts by not adjudicating the ground in income tax rate, in relation to addition of income under section 115BBE of the Act, and therefore the learned AO should be directed to tax the income under section 115BBE, without applying amended section 115BBE of the Act of higher income tax rate. 3. A.R. Shri Prakash D Shah has filed a Synopsis of arguments wherein he has specified that Ground of appeal no. 1 is not pressed. This ground is thus dismissed as not pressed. 4. In the Ground no. 2 the assessee has challenged the confirmation of addition of Rs.47,24,000/- being cash deposits into the Bank account, under section 68 of the Act. The Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC has stated in the impugned order that in the course of appeal proceedings, the appellant submitted in general that A.O. was not correct in making addition u/s 68 of the Act. Except for questioning the validity of notice u/s 143(2) of the Act, the assessee, it is stated by the CIT(A), did not file any written submission. In the absence of any evidence with respect to cash deposits made during the demonetization period, the addition made by the A.O. was confirmed in the impugned order. 5. In the hearing before us, the Ld. A.R. submitted copies of the cash book for the period 01-04-2016 to 31-12-2016, sample sale invoices, sample purchase invoices and a Certificate from ICICI Bank with regard to the amounts withdrawn on various dates from 01-04-2016 to 25-10-2016. While the assessee has made a deposit of Rs.45,00,000/- in cash into the ICICI Bank account on 11-11-2016, there was a RTGS payment of Rs.45,00,000/- on the very next day, i.e., 12-11-2016. The majority of the amounts were withdrawn from 05-04-2016 to 13-06-2016. In the month of October 2016, there were cash withdrawal of Rs.4,42,000/- on 04-10-2016 and Rs.4,00,000/- on 25-10-2016. On the other hand, the cash withdrawal from 05-04-2016 to 23-06-2016 aggregates to Rs.57,83,840/-. 6. The assessee has strongly contended that these withdrawals sufficiently established that the assessee has enough cash in hand out of which the cash deposit was made into the bank account. From the transaction of cash deposit, withdrawal and payment made by the assessee by way of RTGS, the A.O. had found the transaction to be dubious and unexplained. The A.O. recorded the following finding while making the addition: “It is also seen from the bank statement that the assessee deposited Rs.45,00,000/- on 11/11/2016 and transferred the same by RTGS on 12/11/2016. In this regard, the assessee was asked vide show cause notice dated 10/12/2019 to furnish the name, address etc. of the parties to whom the following amounts were transferred by RTGS and the purpose of such transactions. 9. From the facts and circumstances of the case and the facts elaborately discussed in above paras, discrepancies in assessee’s statements regarding receipt of sale proceeds and facts that the assessee’s statement of sales a/c and receipts of the exact sale proceeds much before the dates of sales, nature of purchase/sales bills, non-availability of the sellers in the given address, suspected sale transaction, etc. it is established beyond doubt that the assessee has engaged in paper transaction only and no business of the nature declared by the assessee has ever been carried out. It appears from the above findings that the entire operation is to convert the unaccounted income of the assessee and other group entities. Therefore, assessee’s claim of the cash deposits out of the cash withdrawn from the bank, that too before six months is not at all believable and acceptable. This is coupled with the facts that the assessee appears to have never incurred any expenses for the alleged purchases, as seen from the bank statement furnished by the assessee. Therefore, the cash deposit of Rs.47,24,000/- made by the assessee during the demonetization period is treated as unexplained cash credit u/s.68 of the Act and hence added to the total income of the assessee.” 7. The assessee challenges to this addition, which is also sustained by the CIT(A) are on two grounds, i.e., (i) that the provisions of Section 68 were not applicable to the assessee because assessee did not maintain books of account and filed its return under the presumptive tax provisions of section 44AD of the Act. (ii) It had sufficient cash withdrawals in his hands to justify the cash available for making deposit in the ICICI bank account on 11-11-2016. The assessee, however, has not furnished any details regarding the person to whom RTGS payment of Rs. 45,00,000/- was made on the very next date i.e. on 12-11-2016. 8. In support of its first contention that it was under no obligation maintain books of account for the reason that it was filing return after determining income under the provisions of Section 44AD of the Act, the assessee has relied upon ITAT Ahmedabad Bench decision in the case of Narendra Chandubhai Rafaliya vs. ITO in ITA No. 103/Ahd/2019. The Co-ordinate Bench had held as under: “8. In view of the above rulings as applied to the assessee’s set of facts, we are of the considered view that once the Ld. Assessing Officer has accepted the assessee’s figure of receipts from job work declared in the return of income on the basis of which return has been filed by the assessee on presumptive basis under section 44AD of the Act, then the Ld. Assessing Officer is precluded from making separate additions u/s 68 of the Act to the returned income on the basis that the assessee has not been able to prove the fact of carrying out job work activity in absence of adequate supporting documents etc. without disturbing the figure declared by the assessee offered as job work receipts. Further, as regards cash deposits of Rs.12,57,700/- the assessee has filed confirmations along with identity proofs of lenders and the same has not been disputed by the assessing officer (refer page 4 of the assessment order). Accordingly, considering the fact that deposits by each of the individual lenders were less than Rs.20,000 coupled with the fact that the assessee has been able to establish their identity, we are of the view that source of cash deposits of Rs.12,57,700/- also stands substantiated. Accordingly, ground number 1 to 3 of the assessee’s appeal are allowed.” 9. We notice that the aforesaid case is very different on facts from the assessee’s case. The assessee, as noted by the Ld. A.O. had disclosed total turnover of Rs.31,26,700/- whereas total credits in the bank account of the assessee was Rs.1,24,51,786/-. This shows, prima facie, that in addition to the business turnover the assesse had received funds from other sources. Further, the assessee has been unable to explain why it held on to a large sum of cash i.e. 57,83,840/- withdrawn between 12-04-2016 to 23-06­2016 without spending/using the same and in spite of having so much of cash why did he have to make further cash withdrawal on 04-10-2016 and 25-10-2016. It does not stand to reason why the assessee would withdraw cash in excess of Rs. 57,00,000/-, hold the same in hand for over four months, make further withdrawal in October and then, after demonetization, deposit Rs.45,00,000/- allegedly out of that cash in hand and then transfer, by way of RTGS, an equal amount to an undisclosed person. The source of cash deposit in to the bank account of the assessee on 11-11-2016 therefore remains unexplained. 10. As regards the assessee’s contention that it was exempt from maintaining books of account and that Section 68 was not applicable because he did not maintain any books of account, we are of the view that this proposition does not survive in view of the clear statutory provisions. The assessee is engaged in a business and its gross receipts from business was admittedly in excess of Rs. 10,00,000/-. Under such circumstances, the assessee was obliged u/s 44AA(2) to keep and maintain such books of account and other documents as may enable the A.O. to compute the total income in accordance with the provisions of the Act. Section 44AD, which starts with a non-obstante provision with reference to Sections 28 to 43C (the computation provisions) does not lay down any exemption to an assessee from maintenance of books of account and merely states that an eligible assessee can opt to be taxed under that provision whereby it is deemed that the 8% of the gross turnover or receipts would be the profit and gains of such business chargeable to tax. Since the assessee carried a statutory obligation to maintain books of account, it cannot be allowed to take advantage of its own failure to maintain books of account and claim that Section 68 would not be applicable. This view finds support from the decision of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of Arunkumar J Muchhala vs. CIT; [2017] 399 ITR 256 (Bom.) The Hon’ble High Court held, at para 11 of the judgment, as under: “When Appellant is doing business, then it was incumbent on him to maintain proper books and/ or books of account. It may be in any form. Therefore, if he had not maintained it, then he cannot be allowed to take advantage of his own wrong. Burden lies on him to show from where he has received the amount and what is its nature. Unless this fact is explained he cannot claim or have deduction of the said amount from the income tax. Sec. 68 of I.T. Act provides that where the assessee offers no explanation about the nature and source of the credits in the books of account, all the amounts so credited or where the explanation offered by the assessee is not satisfactory in relation to the same then such credits may be charged to tax as income of the assessee for that particular previous year.” 11. The assessing officer has referred to this judgment in the assessment order and we are of the view that the assessing officer’s reliance upon this judgment is in order. In our view, therefore, the addition u/s 68 on account of failure on the part of the assessee to establish satisfactorily the source from which the deposit was made in cash into the account of the assessee is justified. 12. Ground of appeal no. 2 is dismissed. 13. The assessee has raised an additional ground of appeal contending that the provisions of Section 115BBE of the Act laying down the special tax rate of 60% would be applicable prospectively for Asst. Year 2018-19 and that the A.O. erred in applying this provision for taxing the addition u/s 68 at the special rate laid down under that section. The assessee has relied upon the decisions of ITAT Ahmedabad Bench in the case of Shri Naranbhai Samatbhai Bharvad vs. ITO (ITA No. 272/Ahd/2024); decision of ITAT Hyderabad in the case of Shri Rambabu Varma vs. ITO ( in ITA No. 1613/HYD/2025); decision of ITAT Nagpur Bench in the case of Shri Nirmal Kumar Aggarwal vs. ACIT (ITA No. 530/NAG/2025); decision of ITAT Chandigarh Bench in the case of Shri Santoshkumar Miglani vs. ACIT (ITA No. 973/CHANDI/2026); and decision of ITAT Delhi Bench in the case of Shri Madan Singh Vs. ITO (ITA No. 7824/DEL/2025). All the aforesaid decisions of the Co-ordinate Benches have followed the judgment of the Hon’ble Rajasthan High Court in the case of Deepak Maratha vs. Union of India [2026] 491 ITR 562 (Rajasthan) wherein the Hon’ble High Court held that the provision of Section 115BBE, as amended by Taxation Laws (Second Amendment) Act, 2016 with effect from 01-04-2017 would be applicable for the Financial Year 2017-18 relevant to Asst. Year 2018-19. Applying the said amended provisions to Asst. Year 2017-18 would amount to levying higher tax with retrospective effect. 14. Respectfully following the decision of the Hon’ble Rajasthan High Court and those of the Co-ordinate Benches cited above, we hold that it was incorrect on the part of assessing officer to apply the special rates u/s 115BBE to the assessee’s instant case which pertains to Asst. Year 2017-18. The Ld. CIT(A) erred in not directing the A.O to apply the normal rate of tax to the income added u/s 68 of the Act. We therefore allow the additional grounds of appeal raised by the assessee and direct the assessing officer to apply the normal rate of tax to be assessed income. 15. In the result, appeal of the assessee is partly allowed.
