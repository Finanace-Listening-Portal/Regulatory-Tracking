Vs Union of India (Delhi High Court) The Delhi High Court allowed the appeal filed by Vidhya Ram and another against the Railway Claims Tribunal’s rejection of compensation for the death of their son Mukesh. The Court first condoned a 210-day delay, taking account of the appellants’ poverty, illiteracy and difficulty obtaining legal assistance. It cited earlier Delhi High Court orders granting relief in comparable circumstances and emphasised the beneficial nature of the legislation. The claim concerned a journey on 13 March 2017 from Rampur Junction to Bareilly Junction. The appellants maintained that Mukesh had purchased a ticket which was lost in the accident, and that he fell from the train near Mirganj and Bareilly. The Tribunal had relied on discrepancies in witness testimony, the absence of the ticket, and the condition and location of the body to conclude that the deceased was neither a bona fide passenger nor the victim of an untoward incident. The High Court held that non-recovery of a ticket was not conclusive and that the Railways had not rebutted the claimant’s account through cogent evidence. It applied the principles discussed in Union of India v. Rina Devi and Sukhabhai Nanjibhai Makwana v. Union of India. The Court further held that the body being found in pieces on the track did not establish suicide or trespass. There was no eyewitness or sufficiently reliable evidence showing that Mukesh voluntarily entered the railway track. On a preponderance of probabilities, the death resulted from an accidental fall from a passenger train within Section 123(c)(2) of the Railways Act, 1989. The Court set aside the Tribunal’s judgment, remanded the case to determine compensation and directed disbursement within two months of receipt of its order, with the matter to be listed before the Tribunal on 30 November 2026. The present appeal has been filed under Section 23 of the Railway Claims Tribunal Act, 1987 against the judgment dated 21.03.2023 passed by the Railway Claims Tribunal, Principal Bench, Delhi (hereinafter referred to as the “Tribunal”) in Claim Application No. OA/II(u)/DLI/LKO/508/2021,whereby the claim application filed by the appellants seeking compensation on account of death of their son, Mukesh (hereinafter referred to as the “deceased”), came to be dismissed. 2. The facts, as averred, in a nutshell are that on 13.03.2017, the deceased undertook a journey from Rampur Jn. to Bareilly Jn. after purchasing a valid journey ticket, which was stated to be lost during the incident. It was stated that due to heavy rush and the resultant push and pull of passengers, he was standing near the gate of the compartment and, while the train was passing between Mirganj and Bareilly Junction, he fell from the train and sustained fatal injuries. 3. The Tribunal examined the evidence of the appellants, including the testimony of the appellant/Vidhya Ram as AW-1 and Harprasad as AW-2. It was found that the latter’s testimony was unreliable on account of the inconsistencies noticed in his cross –examination. The Tribunal also placed considerable reliance upon the panchnama and the DRM report, and particularly the fact that the body had been found in pieces. It consequently held that the deceased had not been proved to be a bona fide passenger and that the occurrence was not an “untoward incident” within the meaning of Section 123(c) of the Railways Act, 1989 (hereinafter referred to as the “Act”). 4. Learned counsel for the appellants, while assailing the impugned judgment, contended that the non – recovery of the journey ticket could not, by itself, have resulted in rejection of the claim, particularly when the appellants had consistently pleaded that the deceased had purchased a ticket and that the same was lost in the occurrence. Union ” It is further submitted that the respondent did not lead any evidence in support of the DRM report, despite having relied upon the statements and conclusions contained therein. As regards the manner of occurrence, learned counsel submits that there is no eyewitness or (2019) 3 SCC 572 4 other cogent evidence to establish that the deceased had voluntarily gone upon the railway track or had committed suicide. 5. Per contra, learned counsel for the respondent, on the other hand, submitted that the claim application did not disclose the number or name of the train or the time of commencement of the journey. It is further submitted that the evidence of AW- 2 was introduced after a considerable delay and was materially inconsistent. Reliance is also placed on the police proceedings, panchnama and post – mortem report to contend that the deceased was found on the railway track and had been run over by a train. 6. Having heard learned counsels for the parties, the first question which arises for consideration is whether the deceased has been shown to be a bona fide passenger. The fact that no journey ticket was recovered from the deceased cannot be treated as conclusive against the appellants. The Supreme Court has held that while the initial burden lies upon the claimant, mere absence of a ticket does not negative the claim of bona fide passenger and the matter has to be considered on the attending circumstances, after which the burden shifts upon the Railways. {Ref: Rina Devi (supra)} The same principle has recently been reiterated in Lata (supra) wherein the Supreme Court cautioned against adopting a technical approach while considering claims under the Act and held that the governing standard is that of preponderance of probabilities. 7. In the present case, the appellants had, from the inception, pleaded that the deceased had undertaken the journey after purchasing AW-2’s evidence regarding the particular train and the circumstances in which he saw the deceased is inconsistent and, to that extent, cannot be relied upon to establish the identity of the train. That, however, does not amount to proof that the deceased was travelling without a valid ticket. Significantly, after the applicants closed their evidence, the respondent also chose not to lead any evidence. The recent decision of the Supreme Court in “ Anr. v. Union of India Sukhabhai Nanjibhai Makwana& ” 5, is of relevance in this regard. The Apex Court reiterated that once the initial burden is discharged, the Railways must rebut the claimant’s version by cogent evidence and that absence of the ticket cannot, in isolation, defeat the claim. On a consideration of the evidence as a whole, this Court is of the view that the Tribunal gave undue weight to the non -recovery of the ticket and the discrepancies noticed in the testimony of AW-2. 8. The more substantial question is whether the death of the deceased occurred as a result of an “untoward incident”. The contemporaneou s record, in fact, establishes that the body was found on the railway track near Pole No.1331/12 and that the Station Master was informed about the same. The police proceedings thereafter recorded that the body had been cut into pieces. These circumstances establish the occurrence of a railway accident, but they do not, by themselves, establish how the deceased came to be on the track. The conclusion that a person was run over while crossing the track is different from a finding that the person first fell from a moving passenger train and thereafter came under the wheels. 9. The Tribunal, however, proceeded on the basis that the condition of the body, as recorded in the post -mortem report, corroborated the theory of suicide. The post – mortem report records multiple fractures and that the body was received in pieces. There is nothing in the medical evidence, as noticed by the Tribunal, which establishes that the deceased had deliberately placed himself on the track or that he had committ ed suicide. The inference drawn by the Tribunal thus travels beyond what the medical evidence itself establishes. Likewise, the panchnama recording that the body had been run over by a train does not disclose that any person had witnessed the deceased entering or remaining upon the track before the occurrence. This aspect assumes significance also because the respondent did not examine any of the persons whose statements were relied upon in the DRM report. The report, therefore, could not have been treated as conclusive proof of the manner in which the deceased met his death. 10. A similar view has been taken by this Court in cases where the Railways sought to infer trespassing or a “run over” merely from the place where the body was found or its position vis-à Guruvachan Singh v. Union of India -vis the railway track. In “” 6, this Court held that the place where a body is recovered cannot, by itself, determine the manner of the incident. More recently, in “Sukhmand of India ar Singh v. Union ”7, this Court reiterat ed that the position of the body on or near the track, in the absence of supporting ocular or other cogent evidence, 2026 SCC OnLine Del 2351 7 does not conclusively establish that the passenger was trespassing and was run over. The Court also recognised that the possibility of a pa ssenger first falling from a moving train and thereafter coming under its wheels cannot be ruled out merely from the condition or location of the body. 11. The present case stands on the same footing. There is no eyewitness to the occurrence. The contemporaneous record does not record anyone having seen the deceased voluntarily enter the railway track. Nor does the material relied upon by the respondent establish any circumstance from which suicide can reasonably be inferred. On the evidence available, the occu rrence is, on a preponderance of probabilities, one of accidental falling from a passenger train and consequently falls within Section 123(c)(2) of the Act. 12. In view of the above, the impugned judgment is set aside and the matter is remanded back to the Tribunal, which is requested to assess the amount of compensation payable to the appellant in accordance with law and direct the authorities concerned to disbur se the same within two months from the receipt of a copy of this order. For this purpose, the matter be listed before the Tribunal at the first instance on 30.11.2026. 13. The appeal is allowed and disposed of in the above terms. 14.
