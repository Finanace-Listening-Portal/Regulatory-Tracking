} } Listen to This ArticleThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on Thursday clarified that banks must maintain the 20 per cent foreign exchange risk reserve (FERR) in their cash reserve ratio (CRR) accounts, over and above the mandatory CRR balance, and keep records for the central bank to verify compliance during audits. The clarification, issued through frequently asked questions (FAQs), formalises an arrangement banks had worked out among themselves on Monday evening, when the new rules came into effect. The requirement is adding to hedging costs and pushing up forex forward premiums. “The only clarity that was probably missing was where to place the FERR money. All banks took a decision on Monday evening that we would keep it in our CRR balance. The RBI has now clarified that this is the account where the money has to be maintained, and it will check during audits that we have maintained 20 per cent in our CRR account,” said the treasury head at a private bank. The FERR is equivalent to 20 per cent of the rupee value of the notional amount of eligible foreign exchange derivative contracts. It applies to specified rupee-linked contracts where users buy foreign currency to hedge current account transactions and the notional value exceeds $2 million. Banks must also maintain the reserve for eligible contracts undertaken to hedge currency risks on their own assets and liabilities. Forward premiums have risen sharply as banks and customers unwind positions and adjust to the new rules.
