Summary: what the current V03 contract is designed to do ICICI Pru iProtect Care is a non-linked, non-participating individual pure-risk life insurance product of ICICI Life Insurance Limited, formerly ICICI Prudential Life Insurance Company Limited. The insurer’s download centre identifies UIN 105N209V03 as the version valid from 1 July 2026 “to till date”. Its core purpose is protection: the policy pays the contractual death benefit on death during the policy term, or accelerates that benefit when the life assured meets the specified terminal-illness definition. It is not presented as a savings product, and buyers should not expect a maturity corpus merely for surviving the term. The product page also describes a 12-month premium-break facility after specified conditions are met, an accelerated claim-intimation payment for qualifying large-cover policies, complimentary health-management services while the policy is in force, and optional rider covers. Each feature is conditional. The proposal, underwriting decision, benefit schedule, policy wording and rider wording—not a webpage illustration—determine what a particular policyholder owns. Death cover and terminal-illness acceleration Death benefit belongs to the contract, not to a sales example The life cover is the amount payable on death of the life assured during the policy term, subject to the policy remaining in force and the contractual exclusions. The product page describes 100% of the death benefit as payable on death or terminal illness, whichever occurs first. Because the terminal-illness payment accelerates the death benefit, it is not an extra second layer of cover. Once that accelerated benefit is paid, the policy terminates and its rights and benefits end. Terminal illness has a strict medical definition The insurer states that terminal illness requires a condition which, in the opinion of two independent medical practitioners specialising in its treatment, is highly likely to lead to death within six months. Diagnosis and confirmation must be by medical practitioners registered with the Indian Medical Association and approved by the insurer; the insurer may independently assess the case. The terminal-illness feature is stated not to apply where the policy is sourced through POS personnel. A serious diagnosis that does not satisfy this exact definition may therefore not trigger the benefit. Premium break: continued cover creates a deferred liability The premium-break feature allows an eligible policyholder to defer 12 months of premiums from a policy anniversary while risk cover continues. It is not a waiver. The deferred amount includes the base and rider premiums and may include underwriting extras, modal loadings, cesses and levies. If an insured event occurs during the break, the insurer deducts the deferred amount from the eligible claim. If no claim occurs, the deferred amount becomes payable with the next due premium within the applicable grace period. A policyholder expecting long-term payment difficulty should contact the insurer before a due date and obtain the exact contractual consequences in writing. Allowing a policy to lapse can affect claim eligibility; revival can require evidence of health, payment of arrears and acceptance under the policy terms. Accelerated claim-intimation payment is conditional For an in-force policy with life cover of at least ₹1 crore, the insurer describes an accelerated death-benefit payment of up to ₹3 lakh after death claim registration and required documents. It is not available for a death during the first three policy years from commencement of risk or from revival, whichever is later. The stated payment timing is within one working day from claim registration, subject to documents. The amount is part of—not additional to—the death benefit. If the claim is later found not payable, the claimant must refund the accelerated amount within seven days of communication. Where the selected payout is wholly or partly monthly income, the initial accelerated amount is capped at the lower of the first month’s income and ₹3 lakh. Nominees should therefore treat an early payment as provisional support and preserve the balance until claim adjudication is complete. Those are ceilings, not automatic entitlements. Rider term, premium, exclusions, waiting or survival conditions, and termination events must be checked in the issued rider schedule. A base-policy claim and a rider claim may require different evidence. Underwriting, disclosures, nomination and claims Proposal-stage disclosure is a continuing risk-control step Age, occupation, income, tobacco or nicotine use, health history, medication, investigations, family history, existing insurance and hazardous activities can affect underwriting. The proposer should answer every question completely and correct any mistake before issuance. An intermediary’s verbal view does not replace the signed declaration. The buyer should review the electronic proposal copy and immediately report omissions because inaccurate or incomplete material disclosure can lead to investigation or claim dispute under applicable law and policy terms. Claim file should connect identity, event and entitlement A nominee ordinarily needs claim forms, policy details, identity and bank evidence, death certificate and medical or event records. Accidental death can require police, post-mortem and investigation documents. Terminal illness requires the specialist evidence specified in the policy. The insurer may seek additional documents reasonably connected with adjudication. Nomination should be current; a change in family circumstances should prompt a review through the insurer’s official servicing channel. Escalation route if servicing or a claim stalls Start with ICICI Life’s official customer-service or grievance channel and retain the complaint reference. If the response is not satisfactory, use the insurer’s grievance-redressal hierarchy. Subject to eligibility and applicable rules, the complainant may approach IRDAI’s Bima Bharosa system and the Insurance Ombudsman. Each forum has jurisdiction, time and documentation requirements; lodging a complaint does not predetermine the merits. GST and income-tax treatment as at 2 November 2026 GST on an individual life policy The Government’s September 2025 GST changes exempted individual life insurance services, effective 22 September 2025. The official FAQ explains that the treatment of instalment premiums follows the payment-date rule. Accordingly, an individual policy premium paid on or after that effective date is generally within the exemption, while historic invoices and other insurance categories may follow their own position. Policyholders should verify the insurer’s actual invoice, particularly where a rider, group arrangement or another separately supplied service is involved. Deduction and receipt rules require policy-specific facts A premium can qualify for the life-insurance deduction only if the taxpayer uses the applicable deduction regime and satisfies the statutory conditions and limits. The premium-to-capital-sum-assured restrictions, eligible person, payment year and overall ceiling must all be checked. Death proceeds are generally treated differently from non-death receipts. For maturity or other non-death proceeds, issue date, premium ratio and statutory conditions can change exemption; tax may apply where those conditions fail. A tax result should never be inferred only from the product label or from the insurer’s marketing statement.
