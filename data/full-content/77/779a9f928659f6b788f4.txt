Summary: Non-Contributory Period (NCP) days are an important component of EPF and EPS compliance because they affect contribution calculations, Electronic Challan-cum-Return (ECR) reporting and an employee’s eventual pension eligibility. Under the Employees’ Provident Funds Scheme, 2026 and Employees’ Pension Scheme, 2026, contributions are calculated with reference to wages actually drawn or payable, subject to the applicable statutory wage ceiling. Following the increase in the PF wage ceiling to Rs 25,000 from 17 September 2026, employers must correctly determine EPF and EPS wages for employees having unpaid leave, loss of pay, partial-month employment or other non-contributory periods. Paid leave, paid holidays and paid weekly offs ordinarily do not constitute NCP days. While non-contributory periods are excluded when calculating average pensionable wages under the prescribed mechanism, they reduce pensionable service and may materially affect eligibility for monthly pension. Even incorrectly reported NCP days can potentially reduce rounded eligible service below the minimum ten-year requirement. The article explains the statutory provisions governing NCP days, practical payroll calculations, the distinction between pro rata wage ceilings and non-contributory periods, pensionable service computation and common ECR reporting errors. It also examines the treatment of joining and exit dates, unpaid half days, salary arrears and revised returns. Employers should maintain accurate attendance and wage records, document payroll methodologies, reconcile ECR submissions and promptly rectify reporting errors to prevent disputes during subsequent pension claims. They go on each member’s ECR line as a whole number. They lower EPF and EPS wages for the month, and EPFO later subtracts them from service when it works out pension. Over a career, a few wrong days can decide whether someone gets a monthly pension or only a withdrawal benefit. Where the Rules Come From Now The EPF Scheme, 1952 and the EPS, 1995 were replaced on 29 June 2026 by the EPF Scheme, 2026 (G.S.R. 525(E)) and the EPS, 2026 (G.S.R. 527(E)), made under the Code on Social Security, 2020. For NCP days the wording is close to the old schemes, but the paragraph numbers are new. Calculation of EPF Contributions on Actual Wages Para 18(4) of the EPF Scheme, 2026 says contributions are worked out on wages actually drawn or payable during the month. Three days of loss of pay carry no wages, so they carry no contribution. Para 18(3) caps contributions at the notified wage ceiling, now Rs 25,000 a month from 17 September 2026 under S.O. 5109(E), unless there is a joint option under para 9(4). Meaning of Non-Contributory Service Under EPS, 2026 The EPS, 2026 defines “non-contributory service” in para 2(1)(i) as the period of actual service for which no contribution to the Pension Fund was received or is receivable. “Actual service” in para 2(1)(a) runs from the date of joining to the date of exit. What Counts as an NCP Day? A day is NCP only if the member was in service and earned no wages for it. 1. Loss of pay, unpaid leave and unpaid absence are NCP days. 2. Paid leave of any kind, weekly offs, paid holidays and comp offs are not NCP days, because wages are paid for them. 3. A weekly off inside a run of unpaid leave follows your leave policy. If the policy pays it, it is not NCP. 4. Maternity leave depends on who pays. If the employee draws the benefit from ESIC, the employer pays no wages for those days, so they are NCP. Treatment of NCP Days Before Joining and After Exit Days before joining and after exit are not “actual service” under para 2(1)(a), so on a plain reading they are not non-contributory service either. EPFO’s ECR material does not say this for joiners and leavers, and practice varies. Our reading is to report 0 and let the joining and exit dates explain the lower wages. What “Pro Rata” in the EPS Actually Means Distinction Between Wage Ceiling Periods and NCP Days The words “pro rata” in paras 11 and 12 of the EPS, 2026 can be misread as an NCP rule. They are about wage ceiling periods: Rs 15,000 for service before 17 September 2026 and Rs 25,000 after. They split service by ceiling, not by NCP days. Impact of NCP Days on Average Pensionable Wages NCP days come into pensionable wages through para 11(2). Where the 60 months before exit include non-contributory periods, wages drawn are divided by the actual days for which wages were drawn and multiplied by 30. EPFO’s Pension Manual writes this as pension wages x 30 divided by (1825 minus NCP days). So NCP days do not pull down the average. They hit the service count instead. How NCP Days Cut Pensionable Service Calculation and Rounding of Pensionable Service EPFO’s Pension Manual puts it as pensionable service equals actual service minus non-contributory service, and converts NCP days into months by dividing by 30. Para 10(1) of the EPS, 2026 ties pensionable service to contributions received or receivable. Para 9(1) rounds contributory service to the nearest year: six months or more counts as a year, less is ignored. Para 12(1) needs at least 10 years of eligible service for a monthly pension. Below that, para 13 gives a withdrawal benefit. Example: How Incorrect NCP Reporting Can Affect Monthly Pension Eligibility Take a member who exits after 9 years 7 months of actual service. With no NCP days, that rounds up to 10 years. With 60 NCP days on record, EPFO deducts 2 months, service becomes 9 years 5 months and rounds down to 9. The member gets a withdrawal benefit, not a pension. If 30 of those days were paid weekly offs wrongly filed as NCP, the employer’s ECR made that difference. Common ECR Mistakes With NCP Days 1. NCP days taken from the leave register, so paid leave shows up as NCP. 2. A 26-day base for NCP. The ECR 2.0 rules say a month with no wages must show NCP equal to the days in that month. 3. Half days entered as decimals. The ECR does not accept half-day NCP, so you need a written rule for unpaid half days. 4. EPS wages filled for a member past superannuation or outside EPS. They should be 0. The portal also rejects EPS wages above EPF wages. 5. Part payment through two ECRs for one month, with part wages or part NCP in each. EPFO says each file must carry the correct wages and NCP. 6. A wrong LOP fixed by paying it back as arrears. The arrear ECR has no NCP field, so the day stays NCP on record. If the original return was wrong, look at the revised return in EPFO’s Re-Engineered ECR manual, which has its own conditions. 7. CP reported for days after exit, which on our reading cuts service that had already ended. EPF and EPS Contribution Due Dates and Long-Term Pension Risks Contributions are due within 15 days of the close of the month under para 20(1) of the EPF Scheme, 2026 and para 4(1) of the EPS, 2026. A wrong NCP figure rarely shows up then. It shows up years later in a pension claim, when the employee has long left.
