Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); International Society for Life Sciences Vs CIT (Exemption) (ITAT Jaipur) Pending Registration Applications Must Be Decided Before Renewal Rejection: Jaipur ITAT The Jaipur Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) has set aside orders rejecting applications for registration and approval under Sections 12A and 80G, holding that the Commissioner should first have decided the connected applications pending after an earlier Tribunal remand. In International Society for Life Sciences v. CIT (Exemption), Jaipur, ITA Nos. 1040 and 1041/JPR/2026, pronounced on 4 November 2026, the Tribunal held that the rejection orders were premature. It directed the CIT(E) to decide the earlier remanded matters first and thereafter examine the subsequent applications in accordance with law. Both appeals were allowed for statistical purposes. Background: Society Held Provisional Registration and Approval The appellant society, engaged in charitable activities, had been granted provisional registration under Section 12A(1)(ac)(vi) through an order dated 4 April 2022. It also received provisional approval under Section 80G on 28 June 2022. The society subsequently applied for regular registration under Section 12A(1)(ac)(iii) and regular approval under Section 80G. The CIT(E) rejected those applications through orders dated 30 December 2024. The Commissioner also cancelled the society’s existing provisional registration and provisional approval. The society challenged those decisions before the Tribunal. Earlier Rejection Orders Had Been Set Aside By an order dated 4 June 2025, the Tribunal set aside the earlier orders and directed the CIT(E) to decide the matters afresh after providing an adequate opportunity of hearing. Consequently, the applications concerning the society’s registration and approval returned to the Commissioner for reconsideration. Those proceedings remained pending when the society’s subsequent applications came to be examined. This procedural position was central to the present dispute. The society’s registration status was still awaiting fresh adjudication pursuant to the Tribunal’s earlier directions. Subsequent Form 10AB Applications Were Rejected On 29 September 2025, the society filed further applications in Form 10AB, seeking registration under Section 12A(1)(ac)(ii) and approval under the corresponding renewal provision of Section 80G(5). The CIT(E) issued a deficiency notice asking the society to justify its eligibility. The Commissioner subsequently rejected both applications on 22 February 2026, essentially because the society did not possess the existing registration or approval required for the category under which it had applied. The rejection referred to the earlier December 2024 orders and treated the Form 10AB applications as deficient or ineligible. The society appealed, arguing that this approach ignored the Tribunal’s earlier remand. Existing Registration Was the Basis of Rejection The CIT(E)’s reasoning was that the renewal route applied to an institution already holding the requisite registration or approval. Since the December 2024 orders had rejected regular registration and cancelled provisional registration, the Commissioner considered the society ineligible to use that route. However, those earlier rejection and cancellation orders had already been set aside for fresh consideration. The society therefore objected to its subsequent applications being rejected on the basis of a registration position that remained unresolved before the same authority. The Tribunal examined whether such rejection could be sustained while the foundational applications were still pending. Tribunal Found the Rejections Premature The ITAT noted that, when the impugned orders were passed on 22 February 2026, the earlier remanded applications had not been decided. It held that the CIT(E), acting as a quasi-judicial authority, was required to dispose of those pending matters first. Only after determining the society’s position in the earlier proceedings could the Commissioner properly examine the subsequent applications. The Tribunal therefore rejected the approach of immediately declaring the later applications deficient because the society lacked documents establishing the required registration. In the circumstances, the absence of those documents could not be assessed independently of the unresolved proceedings that would determine the underlying registration position. Connected Proceedings Required Proper Sequence The Tribunal emphasised the sequence in which the applications should be adjudicated. The earlier remand concerned the society’s registration and approval status. The later applications depended upon that status. Deciding the later matters first created an avoidable cycle of rejection and appeal while the connected foundational issue remained undecided. The Tribunal held that the earlier applications should receive priority, followed by consideration of the present applications. It also observed that this approach would serve the interests of justice by curtailing multiplicity of proceedings. Fresh Decision Directed After Hearing The Tribunal set aside both orders dated 22 February 2026 and directed fresh adjudication after providing the society an adequate opportunity of hearing. The CIT(E) must first decide the applications pending pursuant to the order dated 4 June 2025 and then decide the subsequent applications according to law. The decision does not itself grant registration under Section 12A or approval under Section 80G. It restores the applications for examination in the correct sequence. Its significance lies in requiring a pending foundational registration dispute to be resolved before dependent applications are rejected on that very ground.
