Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Vilas Polymer Private Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Hyderabad) Summary: The Hyderabad Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal considered six appeals filed by Vilas Polymer Private Limited against reassessment orders passed under Section 143(3) read with Section 147 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, for assessment years 2014-15 to 2019-20. The proceedings arose from search and seizure operations conducted on 4 January 2023 involving the Excel Group of companies, including Excel Rubber Private Limited, ACE Tyres Private Limited and Vilas Polymer Private Limited. During a separate search at the residence of the group’s senior accounts manager, Shri Ramesh Kumar Sanaka, the Income Tax Department recovered electronic records maintained in FOCUS 5.5 software and Excel worksheets containing details of unaccounted cash receipts and payments. The group’s managing director admitted unaccounted income aggregating to ₹107.63 crore, of which approximately ₹26.22 crore pertained to the assessee company. The assessee challenged the validity of the reassessment notices issued under Section 148, contending that the statutory conditions prescribed under Sections 148 and 149(1)(b) had not been satisfied. It argued that the principal incriminating material had been recovered from the personal laptop of Shri Sanaka during a search conducted under a separate warrant of authorisation, rather than from the assessee’s possession. According to the assessee, the Assessing Officer had failed to record the requisite satisfaction and obtain the necessary statutory approvals before relying on material seized from another person. It was further submitted that notices issued beyond three years required evidence showing escaped income of ₹50 lakh or more represented in the forms specified under Section 149(1)(b), and that the Assessing Officer had not properly established those jurisdictional conditions. The assessee also questioned the validity of the sanction under Section 151, alleging mechanical approval without adequate application of mind. The Tribunal examined the jurisdictional objections in the light of the separate search authorisations, panchnamas, statements recorded under Section 132(4), seized electronic records and reasons recorded for reopening the assessments. It placed substantial reliance on the decision of its coordinate Bench in ACE Tyres Private Limited, another Excel Group company involved in the same search proceedings, where reassessment notices issued on substantially identical facts had been quashed for failure to satisfy the mandatory conditions under Sections 148 and 149(1)(b). The Tribunal noted the distinction between material seized from an employee in his individual capacity and material seized directly from the assessee, and considered the statutory requirements governing the use of such information for reopening assessments. Following the coordinate Bench’s reasoning, it held that the reassessment proceedings for assessment years 2014-15 to 2018-19 suffered from jurisdictional defects. For assessment year 2019-20, the Tribunal separately considered the validity of fresh reassessment proceedings initiated after the earlier proceedings had been dropped. The Assessing Officer had initially issued a notice under Section 148 on 18 March 2023, pursuant to which the assessee filed its return on 15 November 2023. Subsequently, another notice under Section 148 was issued on 14 November 2024 without following the prescribed procedure under Section 148A(b) and Section 148A(d). The Tribunal held that the subsequent initiation of reassessment proceedings could not bypass the applicable statutory safeguards merely because earlier proceedings had been initiated and later dropped. Consequently, the Tribunal quashed the reassessment notice and consequential assessment order for assessment year 2019-20 as well. All six appeals were allowed on jurisdictional grounds, rendering adjudication of the substantive additions unnecessary. The ruling underscores that even where search proceedings reveal substantial alleged unaccounted transactions, reassessment notices must independently satisfy the mandatory statutory requirements governing jurisdiction, limitation, approval and procedure.
