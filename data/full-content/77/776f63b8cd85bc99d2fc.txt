Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Commissioner State Tax (Through Ram Kumar Yadav) Vs Jalaj Alia (GSTAT Agra) Summary: The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), State Bench Agra, held that penalty under Section 129(3) of the CGST/UPGST Act, 2017, was unsustainable in the circumstances of a case involving transportation of defective machinery for repair without generation of an e-way bill before commencement of movement. The Tribunal observed that the consignment was accompanied by valid supporting documents, the movement did not constitute a taxable supply of goods, and the Revenue had failed to establish any intention to evade tax. Accordingly, the Department’s appeal was dismissed and the order of the First Appellate Authority setting aside the penalty was upheld. The taxpayer, Sentro Agro Chemicals, Ganj Basoda, District Vidisha, Madhya Pradesh, had purchased machinery from M/s AD Packaging, Ahmedabad, under a tax invoice dated 24 February 2023. Subsequently, a technical defect developed in the machinery, necessitating its transportation from Vidisha to Faridabad, Haryana, for repair. The machinery was accompanied by the original purchase invoice, transport bilty dated 7 July 2023 and a delivery challan issued under Rule 55 of the CGST Rules, 2017. On 8 July 2023, the vehicle transporting the machinery was intercepted by the mobile squad in Lalitpur District, Uttar Pradesh. Although the driver produced the original invoice, bilty and delivery challan establishing the bona fide nature of the movement, an e-way bill had not been generated before commencement of transportation. The taxpayer immediately generated the e-way bill on the date of interception and produced it before the proper officer prior to issuance of the show cause notice under Section 129(3). However, the proper officer rejected the explanation that the omission arose from a bona fide misunderstanding of the e-way bill requirements applicable to non-supply transactions and imposed a penalty for violation of Rule 138. The First Appellate Authority subsequently set aside the penalty, prompting the Department to approach the Tribunal. The Tribunal observed that sending capital goods or machinery to another State solely for repair and return did not constitute a supply of goods under Section 7 of the CGST/UPGST Act, although repair charges subsequently levied by the repairer would separately constitute a taxable supply of services. Rule 55(1)(c) permitted transportation of goods for reasons other than supply under a delivery challan. Nevertheless, Rule 138 mandated generation of an e-way bill for interstate movement of goods exceeding Rs.50,000 even where the movement was for reasons other than supply. The Tribunal clarified that the applicability of the penal provisions under Section 129(3) had to be examined with reference to the statutory objective of preventing tax evasion. Relying on the Supreme Court decision in Assistant Commissioner, State Tax and Others Vs Satyam Shivam Papers Pvt. Ltd., the Tribunal observed that penalty under Section 129 could not be imposed mechanically in the absence of intention to evade tax, particularly where the movement itself did not attract tax liability. The Tribunal also referred to the Allahabad High Court decisions in Modern Traders, Axpress Logistics India Ltd. and Abhay Prakash Kataria concerning the sustainability of detention and penalty proceedings in circumstances involving procedural deficiencies and absence of tax evasion. The Tribunal found that the taxpayer had produced contemporaneous documents establishing ownership of the machinery, its movement for repair and its entrustment to a common carrier immediately upon interception. The subsequent generation of the e-way bill before issuance of the show cause notice further demonstrated the taxpayer’s bona fides. In these circumstances, the failure to generate the e-way bill before dispatch was treated as a venial and technical breach arising from a bona fide misconception regarding the applicability of e-way bill requirements. The Revenue had not produced reliable evidence indicating any intention to evade tax. Accordingly, the Tribunal answered both questions against the Department, dismissed its second appeal and upheld the order setting aside the penalty under Section 129(3). The decision did not dispense with the mandatory requirement of generating an e-way bill for qualifying non-supply movements but held the penalty unsustainable on the particular facts of the case. (Allahabad High Court); 22/09/2025. FULL TEXT OF THE GST APPELLATE TRIBUNAL ORDER 1. Under section 112(1) Central Goods and Services Tax, Act/ Uttar Pradesh Goods and Services Tax, Act (for short CGST/UPGST, Act), this appeal has been preferred by state tax department U.P, against order dated- 29.11.2023 passed by First appellate authority in appeal no. AD091123158952X/22-11-2023, whereby by allowing first appeal, Order in Original and consequential demand of penalty have been set aside. For convenience appellant and respondent will be addressed as department and taxpayer. 2. In brief, facts of the case are that taxpayer firm Sentro Agro Chemicals, Ganj Basoda, District-Vidisha, Madhya Pradesh, GSTIN 092300021406TMP, had purchased a machine in furtherance of its business on 24.02.2023 from M/s AD packaging Ahmedabad GSTIN 024ABSFA0300N1ZK, under tax invoice no. 87 dated 24.02.2023. subsequently, a technical defect developed in the said machine and for the purpose of repair such machine was dispatched form Vidisha (M.P) to Faridabad (Haryana) via vehicle no. UP 17 AT 1204 accompanied with original purchase tax invoice dated 24.02.2023, transport bilty dated- 07.07.2023 and delivery challan issued under rule 55 of CGST rules 2017. 3. As per, further case of department, on 08.07.2023 during transit through Uttar Pradesh the said vehicle was intercepted by proper officer working in mobile squad on the point situated in District Lalitpur (U.P). Upon inspection the driver produced the tax invoice bilty and delivery challan establishing the bonafide nature of movement of machine for repair. However, no e-way bill had been generated prior to the commencement of movement. Immediately upon interception and prior to issuance of the SCN u/s 129(3), the taxpayer generated the e-way bill on 08.07.2023 itself and produced it before the proper officer. 4. Rejecting the explanation of inadvertent omission due to lack of legal knowledge regarding e-way bill requirement for even non-supply repair movements, the proper officer after serving SCN an order u/s 129(3) imposing penalty for non-compliance of rule 138 CGST rules 2017 was passed. 5. On appeal by taxpayer u/s 107, the first appellate authority allowed the appeal and set aside the penalty order, hence the revenue has preferred the present second appeal before this Tribunal. 6. We have heard both sides and perused the record, the points for determination before us may be formulated as under- i) Whether the movement of machinery sent for repair- not involving a “supply” u/s 07 of CGST/UPGST, Act attracts penal consequences u/s 129(3) merely for non-generation of an e-way bill prior to movement of goods, despite being accompanied by a valid delivery challan, bilty and original purchase invoice? ii) Whether the order of first appellate authority setting aside the penalty, warrants interference in second appeal? FINDINGS WITH REASON- 7. According to section 7 of CGST/UPGST, Act sending a capital goods or machinery to another state solely for repair and return does not constitutes “supply” of goods (though the repair services charges levied later by the repairer are taxable separately as supply of service) under rule 55 (1) (C) of CGST rules 2017, transportation of goods for reasons other than by way of supply is validly covered by a delivery challan. In the present matter, the vehicle was duly accompanied by the deliver challan, bilty dated 07.07.2023 and the original purchase invoice dated- 24.02.2023. However, rule 138 mandates the generation of an e-way bill for interstate movement of goods exceeding Rs. 50,000/- even for reasons other than supply, such as repair, the invocation of harsh penal provisions u/s 129(3) must be evaluated against the core objective of the statues, namely preventing tax evasion. 8. Hon’ble the Supreme Court of India in case of Assistant Commissioner, State Tax and others vs Satyam Shivam Papers, pvt. Ltd. (2022) 14 SCC 157 affirmed that penalty u/s 129 can not be imposed mechanically in the absence of any intention to evade tax/ mens rea, particularly when there is no underlying tax liability on the movement itself. Apart from that, Hon’ble Allahabad High court has consistently held in catena of judgements including M/s Modern Trades Vs State of U.P writ tax no. 763/2018, M/s Axpress logistic India ltd. Vs Union of India 2018 (18) GSTL 14 Allahabad and M/s Abhay Prakash Kataria vs State of U.P writ tax decided on 22.09.2025, that where goods, such as machinery sent for repair, are accompanied by a valid delivery challan and transport documents, and the transaction is non-taxable with zero element of tax evasion, proceedings and penalty u/s 129(3) are unsustainable. In the present matter e-way bill was generated on 08.07.2023, i.e. the very day of interception and before the issuance of SCN demonstrates bonafide conduct rather than any clandestine attempt to evade revenue. Though, if the goods been moving without any supporting documents such as delivery challan or bilty an adverse inference of clandestine removal could have been drawn while here in the present matter every foundational document proving ownership movement for repair and entrustment to a common carrier were produced at the very threshold of interception, at the most the failure to generate e-way bill prior to dispatch was a venial and technical breach resulting from the taxpayers bonafide misconception that e-way bills apply only to taxable sales and movements thereof. In this way, the movement of the defective machine from Vidisha to Faridabad was genuinely for repairing purpose and did not involve any taxable supply of goods. The consignment was supported by contemporaneous and valid document namely original tax invoice, transport bilty and delivery challan. Revenue has completely failed to bring on record any reliable material or evidence indication an intention to evade tax on the part of taxpayer. Further, subsequent generation of e-way bill on 08.07.2023 prior to issuance of SCN shows bonafides of the taxpayer. In such facts and circumstances, first appellate authority is rightly appreciated the facts and settled judicial precedents in setting aside the penalty-imposed u/s 129(3) of CGST/UPGST, Act. Therefore, points for determination here are answered in negative and against department. 9. Consequently, in view of foregoing discussion the second appeal filed by the department is sans of merit and accordingly dismissed. Order in appeal is hereby upheld. 10. There is no pending interim application in the appeal. 11. Order is pronounced in open court on this day of 19th of November, 2026. 12. Copy of order be communicated to all concerned, as per rules.
