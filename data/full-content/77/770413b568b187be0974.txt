} } Listen to This ArticleThe Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA), chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, has approved the sale of a 60.72 per cent stake in IDBI Bank to Canada-based Fairfax Financial Holdings Ltd for Rs 52,878 crore. The transaction, once completed, will be the largest foreign direct investment (FDI) in India’s financial sector. The government will sell 30.48 per cent of its stake, while Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) will divest 30.24 per cent of its shareholding. This will leave the government with a residual stake of 15 per cent and LIC with 19 per cent. The CCEA has also delegated powers to the Alternative Mechanism, an empowered group of ministers headed by Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman, to sell the government’s residual 15 per cent stake through the open market in accordance with the standard operating procedure. LIC will be free to manage its residual shareholding in line with its internal procedures and regulatory requirements, the finance ministry said. Of the five parties that initially submitted expressions of interest, four qualified under the prescribed eligibility criteria and disqualification conditions. Two of them subsequently submitted techno-financial bids and became qualified bidders. The ministry did not disclose the names of the other bidders. The finance ministry said there was no predetermined benchmark for bidding in such transactions. Bidders independently value the enterprise and submit confidential bids in sealed envelopes.
