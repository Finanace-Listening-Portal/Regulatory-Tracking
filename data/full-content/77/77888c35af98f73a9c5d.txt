} } Listen to This ArticleRetail inflation in India rose to 6.09 per cent in October, breaching the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI's) upper tolerance limit of 6 per cent for the first time in two years, according to data released by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) on Thursday. The rise was largely driven by higher food prices. The Consumer Price Index (CPI)-based inflation stood at 5.54 per cent in September. The October reading is also the highest since the new CPI series with base year 2024 was introduced. The RBI's inflation target is 4 per cent, with a tolerance band of 2 per cent on either side. Food inflation rises to 8.94% in October Food inflation, measured by the Consumer Food Price Index (CFPI), rose to 8.94 per cent in October from 7.74 per cent in September. Among food items, onion prices recorded the highest inflation at 116.22 per cent in October, up from 108.13 per cent in September. Garlic inflation increased to 60.74 per cent from 54.62 per cent, while ginger prices rose 48.32 per cent. Lemon prices recorded inflation of 47.30 per cent, compared with 36.41 per cent in the previous month. Rural inflation higher than urban inflation Inflation remained higher in rural areas than in urban centres during October.
