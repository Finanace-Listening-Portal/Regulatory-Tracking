Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Radha Krishna Mariganti Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) Summary: The Hyderabad SMC Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), comprising Accountant Member Manjunatha G. and Judicial Member Ravish Sood, allowed two appeals filed by Radha Krishna Mariganti for Assessment Year 2015-16. The Tribunal quashed the reassessment proceedings involving an addition of Rs. 13,43,000 under Section 69A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, on the ground that the reassessment notice issued under Section 148 on 5 April 2022 was barred by limitation. Consequently, it also directed deletion of the penalty of Rs. 4,17,801 levied under Section 271(1)(c), holding that the foundation for levy of penalty ceased to exist once the underlying assessment order was quashed. The assessee, an individual, had not originally filed his return of income for AY 2015-16. Based on information available with the Income Tax Department concerning cash deposits in bank accounts and salary income earned during the relevant financial year, the Assessing Officer initiated reassessment proceedings under Section 147. In response to the notice issued under Section 148, the assessee filed his return of income declaring total income of Rs. 7,68,140. Thereafter, notices under Sections 143(2) and 142(1) were issued seeking explanations and supporting documents concerning various financial transactions, including cash deposits in bank accounts. During the reassessment proceedings, the assessee explained the sources of cash deposits with reference to salary income, maturity proceeds, insurance receipts, earlier cash withdrawals, gifts and other receipts. However, the Assessing Officer was not satisfied with the explanations furnished in respect of certain deposits aggregating to Rs. 13,43,000. Consequently, the amount was treated as unexplained money under Section 69A, and the assessment was completed on 18 March 2024 under Section 147 read with Sections 144 and 144B, determining total income at Rs. 21,11,140 against the returned income of Rs. 7,68,140. Aggrieved by the assessment order, the assessee preferred an appeal before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi. The assessee contended that the relevant documentary evidence, including bank statements, affidavits and explanations concerning the cash trail, had been furnished before the Assessing Officer but had not been properly appreciated. The assessee also sought condonation of delay in filing the appeal, explaining that confusion concerning the computation of tax demand and the non-availability of the auditor, who was abroad for personal reasons, had resulted in the delay. The CIT(A), however, concluded that the reasons furnished did not constitute sufficient and reasonable cause and declined to condone the delay of 181 days. Accordingly, the appeal was dismissed in limine without adjudicating the substantive issues on merits. Before the Tribunal, the assessee raised additional legal grounds challenging the validity of the reassessment notice dated 5 April 2022. The authorised representative submitted that the grounds went to the root of the matter and could be adjudicated on the basis of facts and documents already available on record without requiring any fresh investigation. Although the Revenue opposed admission of the additional grounds because they had not been raised before the Assessing Officer or the CIT(A), the Tribunal followed the Supreme Court judgment in National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. Vs CIT, reported in (1998) 229 ITR 383 (SC), and admitted the additional grounds for adjudication. On the jurisdictional issue, the assessee submitted that under the unamended provisions of Section 149, the six-year limitation period applicable to AY 2015-16 had expired on 31 March 2022. Since the notice under Section 148 was issued only on 5 April 2022, it was barred by limitation in view of the first proviso to Section 149. Reliance was placed upon the judgment of the jurisdictional Telangana High Court in Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Private Limited Vs DCIT, wherein it was held that the limitation applicable to earlier assessment years continued to be governed by the restriction prescribed under the first proviso to Section 149. The Revenue contended that the amended reassessment provisions effective from 1 April 2021 extended the limitation period to ten years in cases involving escaped income exceeding Rs. 50 lakh. It further argued that the first proviso to Section 149(1) applied only where the limitation period under the erstwhile provisions had expired before 1 April 2021. According to the Revenue, where the limitation period remained available on that date, the amended provisions could be invoked. Reliance was placed upon the Supreme Court judgment in Union of India Vs Rajeev Bansal and the High Court decisions in Salil Gulati Vs ACIT and Ramadoss Srikanthi Vs ACIT. The Tribunal observed that the reassessment notice dated 5 April 2022 had admittedly been issued beyond six years from the end of AY 2015-16. Examining the first proviso to Section 149 and the Supreme Court judgment in Rajeev Bansal, the Tribunal held that the extended limitation period under the amended reassessment provisions could not be applied retrospectively to revive a time-barred case. The Tribunal also followed the jurisdictional Telangana High Court judgment in Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Private Limited and the coordinate bench decision in Peda Subbarao Unnam Vs ITO, which recognised that initiation of proceedings under Section 148A within the limitation period could not validate a subsequent reassessment notice issued beyond the restriction prescribed under the first proviso to Section 149. Applying the aforesaid precedents, the Tribunal held that the reassessment notice dated 5 April 2022 was invalid and that the consequential assessment order was liable to be quashed. Since the assessee succeeded on the jurisdictional ground, the Tribunal did not adjudicate the remaining grounds concerning the addition under Section 69A or the merits of the explanations furnished in respect of cash deposits. In the connected appeal concerning the penalty of Rs. 4,17,801 imposed under Section 271(1)(c) through an order dated 19 September 2024, the Tribunal observed that the penalty was consequential in nature and arose from the addition made under Section 69A in the reassessment order. Since the assessment itself had been quashed on the ground of limitation, the very foundation for levy of penalty ceased to exist. Accordingly, the Tribunal set aside the order of the CIT(A) confirming the penalty and directed the Assessing Officer to delete it. Both appeals filed by the assessee were allowed. Cases Discussed: National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. Vs CIT (Supreme Court of India); [1998] 229 ITR 383 (SC) – Followed by the Tribunal for admitting additional legal grounds capable of adjudication on the basis of facts already available on record, without requiring further investigation. Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Private Limited Vs DCIT (Telangana High Court); Writ Petition No. 25121 of 2024; 17/11/2025; AY: 2017-18 – Binding jurisdictional High Court judgment followed regarding invalidity of a reassessment notice issued beyond the six-year limitation period, notwithstanding initiation of proceedings under Section 148A within that period. Union of India Vs Rajeev Bansal (Supreme Court of India); (2024) 469 ITR 46 (SC); 2024 SCC OnLine SC 2693 – Relied upon by the Revenue and considered by the Tribunal while interpreting the first proviso to Section 149(1) and the protection afforded against revival of time-barred assessments. Salil Gulati Vs ACIT (Delhi High Court); (2023) 455 ITR 24 (Delhi) – Relied upon by the Revenue in support of its interpretation concerning the applicability of the extended reassessment limitation period under the amended statutory provisions. Ramadoss Srikanthi Vs ACIT (Madras High Court); (2025) 481 ITR 126 (Madras) – Relied upon by the Revenue regarding the application of the amended limitation provisions governing reassessment notices. Peda Subbarao Unnam Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad); AY: 2015-16 – Coordinate bench decision followed regarding invalidity of reassessment notices issued under the amended regime after expiry of the applicable limitation period under the erstwhile provisions. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT HYDERABAD The appeals filed by the assessee are directed against the orders of the learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre [in short “NFAC”], Delhi, dated 13.02.2026 passed under sections 147 r.w.s 144 and section 271(1)(c) of the Act, respectively, pertaining to the assessment year 2015-16. 2. The grounds raised by the assessee read as under : “1. The order of the Ld.CIT(A) is erroneous and against the provisions of the IT Act. 2. The Ld.CIT(A) ought to have observed that the Very issuance of notice u/s. 148 barred by limitation and the proceedings initiated is void ab initio. 3. The Ld. CIT(A) erred in dismissing the appeal without adjudicating the case of the Appellant on merits. 4. The Ld.CIT(A) ought to have appreciate that the delay occurred due to a bonafide belief of the appellant that since the demand raised by the Ld.AO was less than 100, no further appeal was required. 5. The Ld.CIT(A) ought to have observed that there is no negligence on the part of the appellant for causing the delay in filing the appeal. Therefore the delay in filing should have condoned. 6. Without prejudice to the above, The Ld.CIT(A) ought to have appreciated that the Appellant has duly explained the source for cash deposits along with the evidences such as bank statements, affidavits and return of income to substantiate his claim. 3 7. The Ld.CIT(A) ought to have observed that the Appellant has submitted the complete cash trail along with the detailed explaination. Thus, the Addition made by the Ld.AO by invoking provisions of sec. 69A is not sustainable. 8. The Ld.CIT(A) ought to observed that Ld.AO has erred in considering the fact that the cash deposits were the resultants of the earlier cash withdrawals. Thus, the contention of the Ld. AO is not correct. 9. The Appellant craves to add/alter/leave/modify any other grounds at the time of hearing.” 3. The additional grounds raised by the assessee read as under : “10. The notice issued u/s. 148 dated 05.04.2022 by the Ld. AO, is barred by limitation in view of the First Proviso to Sec. 149 of The Income Tax Act, 1961. Hence the notice issued u/s. 148 is void and liable to be quashed. 11. The reassessment proceedings w/s. 147 stands vitiated as the notice u/s. 148 is barred by limitation in view of first proviso to Sec. 149. Hence the entire reassessment proceedings shall be treated as null and void.” 4. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee is an individual. The assessee did not file return of income for the A.Y. 2015-16. Based on information available with the department regarding cash deposits in bank account and salary income earned by the assessee during the relevant financial year, the case was reopened under Section 147 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Accordingly, notice under Section 148 of the Act was issued and served on the assessee. In response thereto, the assessee filed return of income declaring total income of Rs.7,68,140/-. Thereafter, notices under Sections 143(2) and 142(1) of the Act were issued calling upon the assessee to furnish details and explanations in respect of various transactions including cash deposits made in bank accounts. The assessee furnished details from time to time and explained the sources for cash deposits with supporting documents. 5. During the course of assessment proceedings, the A.O. examined the explanation furnished by the assessee with regard to cash deposits made in his bank accounts. Although the assessee explained the sources for such deposits with reference to salary income, maturity proceeds, insurance receipts, cash withdrawals, gifts and other receipts, the A.O. was not convinced with the explanation furnished in respect of certain cash deposits. According to the A.O., the assessee failed to satisfactorily establish the source for cash deposits aggregating to Rs.13,43,000/-. Therefore, the A.O. treated the said amount as unexplained money under Section 69A of the Act and completed assessment under Section 147 r.w.s. 144 r.w.s. 144B of the Act on 18.03.2024 determining total income at Rs.21,11,140/- as against the returned income of Rs.7,68,140/-. 6. Aggrieved by the assessment order, the assessee preferred appeal before the Ld. CIT(A). Before the Ld. CIT(A), the assessee challenged the addition made towards unexplained cash deposits and contended that all the relevant evidences explaining the sources for cash deposits were furnished before the A.O. The assessee further contended that the A.O. completed the assessment without properly appreciating the evidences and explanations furnished during the course of assessment proceedings. The assessee also filed a petition seeking condonation of delay in filing of appeal and explained that the delay occurred due to confusion arising out of the computation of demand and also on account of non-availability of the Auditor, who was out of the country for personal reasons. The assessee, therefore, requested the Ld. CIT(A) to condone the delay and admit the appeal for adjudication on merits. 7. The Ld. CIT(A) after considering the submissions of the assessee observed that the appeal was filed with a delay of 181 days. The Ld. CIT(A) further observed that the reasons explained by the assessee did not constitute sufficient and reasonable cause for condonation of delay. The Ld. CIT(A), after referring to various judicial precedents governing condonation of delay, held that the assessee failed to establish sufficient cause for the delay in filing of appeal. The Ld. CIT(A) further observed that the assessee had actively participated in the assessment proceedings and had furnished response to the show-cause notice issued by the A.O. Therefore, according to the Ld. CIT(A), the assessee had no valid ground to explain the delay in filing of appeal. Accordingly, the Ld. CIT(A) declined to condone the delay of 181 days and dismissed the appeal in limine without adjudicating the issues raised by the assessee on merits. 8. Aggrieved by the order of the Ld. CIT(A), the assessee is in appeal before the Tribunal. 9. The learned counsel for the assessee, Shri C. Maheswar Reddy, C.A., submitted that, the additional grounds raised by the assessee are purely legal in nature and go to the root of the matter challenging the validity of reassessment proceedings. The learned counsel for the assessee further submitted that the issue raised in the additional grounds can be adjudicated on the basis of facts already available on record and does not require any further investigation into facts. Therefore, he submitted that, the additional grounds raised by the assessee may be admitted for adjudication. In support of his contention, he relied upon the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. vs. CIT reported in [1998] 229 ITR 383 (SC). 10. On the other hand, the learned Sr. A.R. for the Revenue, Shri B. Laxmi Kanth, objected to the admission of additional grounds and submitted that the assessee has not raised these issues either before the A.O. or before the Ld. CIT(A). The learned Sr. A.R. further submitted that the reassessment proceedings have been validly initiated in accordance with law and therefore, the additional grounds raised by the assessee are not maintainable. Accordingly, he submitted that the additional grounds raised by the assessee should not be admitted for adjudication. 11. We have heard both the parties, perused the material available on record and had gone through the orders of the authorities below. There is no dispute with regard to the fact that the additional grounds raised by the assessee are purely legal in nature and go to the root of the matter challenging the validity of reassessment proceedings. Further, the issue raised in the additional grounds can be adjudicated on the basis of facts already available on record and does not require any further verification or investigation into facts. Therefore, following the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. vs. CIT (supra), we admit the additional grounds raised by the assessee for adjudication. 12. Since the issue raised in the additional grounds is purely legal in nature and goes to the root of the matter, therefore, we first take up the issue regarding validity of notice issued under section 148 of the Act. 13. The learned counsel for the assessee, Shri C. Maheswar Reddy, C.A., submitted that the notice issued under Section 148 of the Act dated 05.04.2022 is barred by limitation in view of the first proviso to Section 149 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The learned counsel for the assessee further submitted that the assessment year involved is A.Y. 2015-16 and therefore, as per the unamended provisions of Section 149 of the Act, the limitation for issuance of notice under Section 148 of the Act expired on 31.03.2022. The learned counsel further submitted that although the proceedings under the new regime were initiated by the Assessing Officer, the notice under Section 148 of the Act admittedly came to be issued only on 05.04.2022, which is beyond the limitation period prescribed under the provisions of the Act as it stood prior to amendment. Therefore, he submitted that the notice issued under Section 148 of the Act is invalid and consequently, the reassessment proceedings completed under Section 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act are liable to be quashed. In support of his contention, the learned counsel relied upon the judgment of the Hon’ble Telangana High Court in the case of Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Society and submitted that the Hon’ble High Court has held that the limitation for issuance of notice under Section 148 of the Act for assessment years prior to 01.04.2021 is governed by the first proviso to Section 149 of the Act and therefore, notice issued beyond the period prescribed under the erstwhile provisions is barred by limitation. Thus, the learned counsel for the assessee submitted that the notice issued by the Assessing Officer under Section 148 of the Act dated 05.04.2022 is invalid being barred by limitation and the same is liable to be quashed. 14. The learned Sr. A.R. for the Revenue, Shri B. Laxmi Kanth, on the other hand, submitted that, after amendment of the provisions relating to reopening with effect from 01.04.2021, the limitation for issuance of notice under Section 148 of the Act has been extended up to ten years in cases where the income escaping assessment exceeds Rs.50,00,000/- and therefore, the notice issued by the Assessing Officer is within the time limit. The learned Sr. A.R. further submitted that, the proviso to Section 149(1) of the Act is applicable only in cases where limitation had expired before 01.04.2021 and where limitation was still available as on 01.04.2021, the amended provisions would apply. In support of his contention, he relied upon the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Union of India vs. Rajeev Bansal reported in (2024) 469 ITR 46 (SC) as well as decisions of various High Courts including Salil Gulati vs. ACIT reported in (2023) 455 ITR 24 (Delhi) (HC) and Ramadoss Srikanthi vs. ACIT reported in (2025) 481 ITR 126 (Madras) (HC). 15. We have heard both parties, perused the material available on record and had gone through the orders of the authorities below. There is no dispute with regard to the fact that the notice under Section 148 of the Act, has been issued by the A.O. on 05.04.2022 for the assessment year 2015–16 which is beyond the period of six years from the end of the relevant assessment year. As per the unamended provisions of Section 149 of the Act, the notice under Section 148 of the Act, could have been issued within six years from the end of the relevant assessment year and accordingly, the limitation expired on 31.03.2022. 16. We further find that, though as per the amended provisions of Section 149 of the Act, the limitation for reopening of assessment has been extended up to ten years in cases where income escaping assessment exceeds Rs.50,00,000/-, however, the first proviso to Section 149 clearly provides that no notice under Section 148 shall be issued at any time in a case for the relevant assessment year beginning on or before 01.04.2021 if such notice could not have been issued at that time on account of being beyond the time limit specified under the provisions of the Act, as it stood prior to amendment. The scope and applicability of the said proviso has been considered and interpreted by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Union of India vs. Rajeev Bansal reported in (2024) 469 ITR 46 (SC), wherein it has been held that, the extended time limit under the amended provisions cannot be applied retrospectively to revive a time barred case. 17. We also find that, the Hon’ble Jurisdictional Telangana High Court in the case of Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Private Limited vs. DCIT (supra) has considered identical issue and held that, the notice issued under Section 148 of the Act, after expiry of six years from the end of the relevant assessment year is barred by limitation even though the proceedings under Section 148A of the Act, were initiated within the limitation period. Further, similar view has been taken by the coordinate bench of the Tribunal in the case of Peda Subbarao Unnam vs. ITO (supra), wherein it has been held that once the limitation under the old regime had expired, the notice issued under Section 148 of the Act, under the new regime is invalid. 18. In the present case, since the limitation for issuance of notice under Section 148 of the Act, had already expired on 31.03.2022, in our considered view, the notice issued by the Assessing Officer dated 05.04.2022 is clearly barred by limitation and consequently the assessment order passed under Section 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act, is bad in law and liable to be quashed. 19. Accordingly, we hold that, the notice issued under Section 148 of the Act, is invalid and the consequent reassessment order passed by the Assessing Officer is liable to be quashed. Since we have quashed the reassessment proceedings on legal issue, the other grounds raised by the assessee on merits become academic in nature and hence not adjudicated. 20. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed. ITA No.791/Hyd/2026 21. The present appeal filed by the assessee relates to the penalty levied by the A.O. under Section 271(1)(c) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 amounting to Rs.4,17,801/- on the addition made towards unexplained cash deposits in bank account under Section 69A of the Act, in the assessment completed under Section 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act for the A.Y. 2015-16. Aggrieved by the penalty order dated 19.09.2024, the assessee preferred appeal before the Ld. CIT(A). The Ld. CIT(A), after considering the facts of the case, upheld the action of the A.O. and confirmed the penalty levied under Section 271(1)(c) of the Act. Aggrieved by the penalty order dated 19.09.2024, the assessee preferred appeal before the Ld. CIT(A). The Ld. CIT(A), after considering the facts of the case, upheld the action of the A.O. and confirmed the penalty levied under Section 271(1)(c) of the Act. 22. Aggrieved by the order of the Ld. CIT(A), the assessee is in appeal before the Tribunal. 23. We have heard both parties, perused the material available on record and had gone through the orders of the authorities below. We find that, on identical set of facts for the assessment year 2015-2016 in ITA No.790/Hyd/2025, we have quashed the assessment order on the ground that the notice issued under Section 148 of the Act, dated 05.04.2022 is barred by limitation. Since the penalty levied under Section 271(1)(c) of the Act, is consequential in nature and arises out of the additions made in the said assessment order, in our considered view, the penalty levied by the A.O. under Section 271(1)(c) of the Act, cannot survive. Once the assessment order itself has been quashed, the very foundation for levy of penalty ceases to exist. Therefore, the penalty order passed by the A.O. has no legs to stand. Thus, we set aside the order of the Ld. CIT(A) and direct the A.O. to delete the penalty levied under Section 271(1)(c) of the Act. 24. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed. 25. To sum-up, both the appeals filed by the assessee are allowed. Order pronounced in the Open Court on 19th June, 2026.
