Articles 3 min read Canara Bank sees up to Rs 200 cr hit to distribution income under proposed IRDAI norms Canara Bank expects a Rs 120-145 crore annual hit to its life insurance distribution income if the proposed IRDAI norms are implemented, with the overall impact across its distribution business estimated at up to Rs 200 crore. The bank’s FY26 fee income stood at Rs 9,649 crore, while its disclosed bancassurance business was Rs 639.20 crore. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Updated On Oct 14, 2026 at 07:19 PM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 134944221; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Bengaluru based public sector lender Canara Bank expects the proposed IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory Development Authority of Infdia) distribution norms to have an overall impact of up to Rs 200 crore on its distribution income, with the largest impact coming from its life insurance distribution franchise, Managing Director & CEO Brajesh Singh told ETBFSI.The bank estimates that its life insurance distribution income could take a hit of around Rs 120-145 crore annually if the proposed norms are implemented in their current form.“The guideline is still a draft, but even if we take it hypothetically, when it is implemented, it will mostly affect our life insurance distribution franchise. We have calculated the impact based on the existing level of business, and the total dent would be around Rs 120-145 crore,” Singh said. Advt He added that when the impact across all the products distributed by the bank is taken together, the total dent would not exceed Rs 200 crore.“Taken together, across everything that we distribute, the dent will not be more than Rs 200 crore in any case,” Singh said.The estimate is based on the existing level of business and is an annual impact, Singh clarified.Elaborating on the life insurance impact, Singh said the calculation was based on the difference between the commission that the bank currently earns and the level envisaged under the proposed framework.“Whatever commission we were earning, suppose 15 per cent, the new guidance is, say, 10 per cent. So we have calculated the dip between the existing and proposed levels, and that comes to precisely Rs 145 crore,” he said.However, Singh said the impact would remain manageable for Canara Bank given the scale of its earnings.According to Canara Bank’s FY26 annual report, the bank’s fee income stood at Rs 9,649 crore, compared with Rs 8,866 crore in FY25, registering a growth of 8.83 per cent.Its disclosed bancassurance business stood at Rs 639.20 crore in FY26, compared with Rs 556.83 crore in FY25. Of this, life insurance distribution contributed Rs 457.79 crore, while non-life insurance contributed Rs 91.53 crore. The disclosure also includes mutual fund income and other distribution-related income.The Rs 120-145 crore estimated annual hit to life insurance distribution would therefore be equivalent to roughly 23-32 per cent of the bank’s FY26 life insurance distribution income, while the broader Rs 200 crore potential impact would be around 21 per cent of its total FY26 bancassurance business. Advt Against Canara Bank’s overall fee income of Rs 9,649 crore, the maximum Rs 200 crore impact would be around 2 per cent.How did Q2 FY27 fare for Canara Bank?Canara Bank's global business grew 15.88 per cent year-on-year to Rs 30.72 lakh crore in Q2 FY27. Global advances grew 19.46 per cent to Rs 13.75 lakh crore, while deposits rose 13.13 per cent to Rs 16.97 lakh crore. Net interest income increased 20.39 per cent to Rs 11,005 crore, while net profit rose 8.4 per cent to Rs 5,175 crore. Asset quality also improved, with gross NPA declining to 1.33 per cent and net NPA to 0.30 per cent.
