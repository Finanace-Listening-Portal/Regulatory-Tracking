} } Listen to This ArticleThe government’s approval on Wednesday to sell a controlling stake in IDBI Bank to a Fairfax Financial Holdings-backed entity marks a major change in the lender’s ownership, but customers do not need to close their accounts, withdraw deposits or alter loan repayments simply because the transaction has been approved. The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) on October 14 approved the sale of a 60.72% stake in IDBI Bank for ₹52,878 crore. The stake comprises 30.48% held by the Government of India and 30.24% held by Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC). After the transaction, the government is expected to retain 15% and LIC 19% of the bank. The deal remains subject to the required regulatory and completion processes, according to the Ministry of Finance’s announcement. Are your savings accounts and fixed deposits safe? The sale of a controlling stake does not, by itself, cancel existing savings accounts, current accounts or fixed deposits. Customers do not need to close their accounts merely because the bank is changing ownership. For customers, the immediate impact of the ownership change is expected to be limited. Madan Sabnavis, chief economist at Bank of Baroda, said that it would be “business as usual” for depositors and borrowers, while the bank's human-resource management could evolve after the transaction. Analysts say the acquisition could also give Fairfax a wider branch network, a larger deposit base and greater scope to expand lending. Eligible deposits continue to fall under the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) framework. The insurance limit is ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, including principal and interest, for deposits held in the same right and capacity. The limit applies across eligible savings, current, fixed and recurring deposit accounts at the same bank; it is not ₹5 lakh for every individual account. The DICGC explains the rules in its official FAQs and guide to deposit insurance. Customers with deposits exceeding ₹5 lakh should understand that this is the statutory deposit-insurance ceiling, not a guarantee that every amount above it will be protected in every circumstance. "" change in shareholding does not affect the safety of deposits or automatically alter interest rates, charges or loan terms. The bank will remain regulated by the RBI, and deposits continue to be insured by DICGC up to the prescribed limit. Over time, the new promoter may bring changes in digital banking, products, branch presence or service standards. Customers should rely only on official bank communications and review any notice before accepting revised terms or charges," said Alay Rizvi, Managing Partner, Acxord Juris. Will fixed deposit interest rates change? There is no automatic change to an existing fixed deposit's contracted interest rate simply because the bank's shares are being sold. Customers should continue to refer to the terms of their deposit. Rates offered on new fixed deposits or renewals could change in the future as the bank reviews its funding requirements, competition and business strategy. However, the government's transaction announcement does not specify any change to IDBI Bank's deposit rates. Customers whose fixed deposits are nearing maturity should compare the bank's latest rates before renewing. They should also check premature-withdrawal penalties before breaking an existing deposit. What happens to existing loans and EMIs? Existing home loans, personal loans, vehicle loans and other credit agreements do not automatically change because the bank's ownership changes. Borrowers should continue paying their EMIs according to their existing loan agreements. A change in shareholding does not, by itself, alter the repayment schedule or remove a borrower's obligations. For floating-rate loans, interest rates can still change in accordance with the loan agreement, the applicable benchmark and the reset schedule. Such changes should not be confused with an automatic consequence of the privatisation process. Customers should continue using official payment channels and verify any communication about changes to their loan accounts directly with the bank. Could branches, customer service or digital banking change? A new controlling shareholder could eventually review IDBI Bank's business strategy, technology investments, product offerings, branch network and customer-service operations. These are possible areas of change, not confirmed immediate changes announced as part of the transaction. The government has not specified a customer-facing transition plan announcing branch closures, new digital platforms or revised banking products in its official announcement. Customers should therefore continue using their existing branches, ATMs, mobile banking and internet banking services unless IDBI Bank announces otherwise. They should also be cautious of unsolicited messages claiming that the sale requires them to update account details, transfer money or disclose passwords and one-time passwords (OTPs). Will IDBI Bank change its name? The approved transaction concerns a change in ownership and management control. It does not automatically require customers to open new accounts or obtain new banking credentials. Any future change to the bank's name, branding or account-servicing arrangements would need to be communicated through official channels. Customers should not act on unverified messages claiming that privatisation requires them to transfer funds or share confidential banking information. Will customers get better banking services? That will depend on how the new owner manages the bank after the transaction is completed. A change in ownership can create opportunities for investment in technology, improved customer service and changes to the bank's lending and deposit strategy. These are potential outcomes, not guaranteed benefits. For customers, the measures that will matter most are the reliability of digital services, branch access, deposit rates, loan pricing and grievance redressal. "As with any other customer of a PSU, IDBI Bank's customers can expect more proactive engagement from their bank with the proposed change in ownership. IDBI historically has had a lot of customers and has lost some of them to the sheen of some of the private sector banks. The new ownership would look first to address the problems that made them leave and also proactively engage with the existing customers and offer them a better packaged service," said Nihas Basheer, Partner, Wadia Ghandy & Co. What happens to the government's and LIC's remaining stakes? After the sale, the government is expected to retain a 15% stake and LIC 19%, leaving them with a combined holding of 34%. The government has also authorised a mechanism to consider selling its remaining stake through the open market in accordance with the prescribed procedure. LIC can manage its remaining holding subject to its internal procedures and applicable regulatory directions, according to the Ministry of Finance's FAQs on the transaction. The distinction is important: IDBI Bank is moving towards private control, but the government and LIC are not expected to exit their shareholdings entirely as part of this transaction. What should IDBI Bank customers do now? Depositors: Check fixed deposit maturity dates and terms. Remember that DICGC protection is capped at ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, subject to eligibility rules. Borrowers: Continue paying EMIs as scheduled and follow the existing loan agreement. Account holders: Keep using existing banking channels unless the bank announces a change. All customers: Follow official bank communications and never share OTPs, passwords or confidential banking information in response to unsolicited messages.
