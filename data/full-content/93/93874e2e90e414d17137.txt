Higher funding costs and proposed insurance commission changes pose additional profitability risks.Several non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) have tightened underwriting and calibrated loan disbursements in districts facing drought-like conditions in Maharashtra, Karnataka and Telangana, as emerging repayment stress threatens to weigh on rural and agricultural loan portfolios. Vehicle financiers and microfinance lenders are particularly exposed, with early signs of rising delinquencies prompting concerns over credit costs and loan growth in the second half of FY27.Although headline asset quality across the sector remained stable in the September quarter, rising net slippages, Stage-2 assets and early-bucket delinquencies at select lenders indicate potential stress in rural and agriculture-linked portfolios. The concerns are particularly pronounced in farm equipment, construction equipment and commercial vehicle financing, as well as microfinance lending in drought-affected areas, according to JM Financial. Advt Vehicle financiers reported a sharp acceleration in disbursement growth to 31% year-on-year in Q2FY27 from 21% in the preceding quarter, led by Cholamandalam Investment and Finance Company, which recorded 47% growth. This lifted assets under management growth in the segment to 19% from 17%. However, early delinquencies increased at Shriram Finance, while stress in farm equipment, construction equipment and commercial vehicle portfolios in drought-hit Maharashtra, Telangana and Karnataka is expected to keep credit costs elevated.Credit costs among vehicle financiers increased to around 2% in Q2FY27 from 1.8% in Q1. The brokerage expects disbursement growth to moderate in the second half of the financial year as weaker rural incomes and a high base weigh on demand, with credit costs remaining elevated, particularly in farm equipment and construction and commercial vehicle segments.Microfinance stressMicrofinance lenders are also showing signs of emerging stress despite an improvement in reported bad-loan ratios. Gross Stage-3 assets across NBFC-MFIs declined to 2.3% in Q2FY27 from 2.8% in the preceding quarter, but credit costs increased to 3.7% from 3.2%, led by CreditAccess Grameen. The lender saw Stage-2 assets and portfolio at risk beyond 15 days inch up amid drought-related stress in Maharashtra, which accounts for around 22% of its assets under management.The broader NBFC sector continued to report strong growth, with assets under management across the 23 companies in the brokerage's coverage universe increasing around 21% year-on-year in Q2FY27, against 19% in Q1. Disbursement growth improved to 27% from 26%, while net interest income rose 26%. Profit after tax grew around 40%, supported by operating performance and broadly flat provisions. Advt Among diversified NBFCs, assets under management grew 26%, although disbursement growth moderated to 25% from 32% in the preceding quarter. Net slippages in the segment increased to 1.4% from 1.1%, led by Bajaj Finance, HDB Financial Services and Tata Capital, amid challenges in gold loans and commercial and construction equipment financing.Housing finance companies and MSME lenders also reported improved disbursement momentum. HFC disbursements grew 25%, while MSME and loan-against-property lenders recorded 33% growth. However, early delinquencies remained sticky at select MSME lenders, with Stage-2 assets rising at SBFC Finance and Fedbank Financial Services. Advt Higher funding costs and regulatory changes could add to pressure on NBFC profitability. Several housing finance companies raised lending rates by 10-25 basis points between October and November to pass on higher borrowing costs. The proposed changes to insurance commissions could also weigh on lenders with greater reliance on insurance distribution income.
