Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Royal Builders & Developers (GST AAR Karnataka) Summary: The Karnataka Authority for Advance Ruling, in In re Royal Builders & Developers, Order No. KAR.ADRG/48/2026 dated 15 October 2026, considered whether a developer undertaking a residential plotted-layout project under a Joint Development Agreement could obtain an advance ruling on its invoicing procedure and whether eligible input tax credit could be utilised against GST on development services supplied to landowners. The Authority declined to rule on invoicing procedure but allowed utilisation of eligible input tax credit, subject to statutory conditions. The applicant, a partnership firm, entered into a Joint Development Agreement with landowners for development of plots at Yelawala Village, Mysuru District. It undertook to develop infrastructure and amenities, secure statutory approvals and bear development expenditure, with completed plots to be divided between the developer and landowners in the agreed proportions. The original application raised seven questions concerning taxability, rates, activities, invoicing, non-payment, credit and sale of developed plots. By email dated 30 July 2026, the applicant restricted its request to invoicing and input tax credit. On invoicing, the applicant sought clarity on whether invoices should cover only development activities attributable to the landowners’ share and how invoice value and extent should be determined. The Authority distinguished determination of value of supply under Section 15 from procedural guidance on raising invoices. Since Section 97(2) specifies the permissible subjects of an advance ruling and does not empower the Authority to issue administrative advice on invoicing methodology, documentation or accounting, the invoicing question was held not maintainable. On input tax credit, the Authority observed that the applicant was making taxable outward supplies of development services to landowners under the forward-charge mechanism. Under Section 16 of the CGST Act, a registered person may avail credit for eligible business inputs, subject to prescribed conditions; Section 49 permits utilisation of electronic credit ledger balances towards output tax, while Section 17 imposes relevant restrictions. The applicant could therefore use eligible input tax credit to discharge GST on those taxable development services. The Authority further held that eligible credit does not require reversal merely because it is utilised against the development-service liability. Any ineligible input tax credit must, however, be reversed in accordance with the CGST Act and Rules. The ruling did not determine the specific invoice amount or prescribe the procedure for raising invoices, and the applicant’s other original questions were not decided. FINDINGS & DISCUSSION: 8. At the outset we would like to make it clear that the provisions of the CGST Act, 2017 and the KGST Act, 2017 are in pari materia and have the same Provisions in like matters and differ from each other only on a few specific provisions. Therefore, unless a mention is particularly made to such dissimilar provisions, a reference to the CGST Act, 2017 would also mean reference to the corresponding similar provisions in the KGST Act, 2017. 9. We have considered the submissions made by the applicant in their application for advance ruling. We have also considered the issues involved on which advance ruling is sought by the applicant, relevant facts, and the arguments made by the applicant and the submissions made by their learned representative during the time of hearing. 10. The Applicant has rephrased the questions and sought a ruling on:- i. the procedure for invoicing the landowners in respect of the development activities undertaken under the Joint Development Agreement (JDA), including the determination of the value and extent of such invoicing, and; ii. Whether the Applicant is entitled to avail and utilise the eligible input tax credit towards payment of GST on such development services and, if not, the manner and timing of reversal of input tax credit. 11.1 Issue No. (i) – Invoicing of Development Services i. The applicant has sought a ruling regarding the procedure of raising invoices on the landowner for the construction services provided by the developer under the Joint Development Agreement, including the determination of the invoice value and the extent to which invoices are required to be raised. The applicant has sought a ruling regarding the procedure of raising invoices on the landowner for the construction services fal Builders & Developers Page 7 of 11 including the determination of the invoice value and the extent to which invoices are required to be raised ii. At the outset, it is pertinent to examine whether the aforesaid question falls within the ambit of Section 97(2) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, which exhaustively specifies the questions on which an advance ruling can be sought. The matters specified therein are: a) classification of any goods or services or both; b) applicability of a notification issued under the provisions of the Act; c) determination of time and value of supply of goods or services or both; d) admissibility of input tax credit; e) determination of the liability to pay tax on any goods or services or both; f) whether the applicant is required to be registered; g) whether any particular act or thing amounts to or results in a supply of goods or services or both. iii. The present question does not seek a determination of the value of supply under Section 15 of the Act or the applicable valuation rules. Instead, it seeks clarification regarding the procedural aspects of invoicing, namely the manner in which invoices are to be issued, the amount to be reflected in the invoice, and the extent of invoicing to be undertaken between the developer and the landowner. iv. The Advance Ruling Authority is constituted to decide only the specific questions enumerated under Section 97(2) of the CGST Act, 2017. It is not empowered to issue advisory opinions or administrative guidance on accounting practices, documentation requirements, invoicing methodology, or the mechanics of compliance under the GST law. v. Although the applicant has referred to the amount to be mentioned in the invoice, the substance of the question pertains to the procedure of invoicing and not to the statutory determination of the value of supply under Section 15 read with the applicable valuation provisions. Similarly, the question does not seek determination of tax liability, classification, applicability of any notification, admissibility of input tax credit, registration requirement, or whether an activity constitutes a supply. vi. Accordingly, the question is outside the scope of Section 97(2) of the CGST Act, 2017 and is therefore not maintainable before the Advance Ruling Authority. – vii. It is well settled that the jurisdiction of the Advance Ruling Authority is confined strictly to the matters enumerated under Section 97(2), and the Authority cannot render rulings on procedural or administrative issues which are not covered by the said provision. 11.2 Issue No. (ii) – Availment, Utilisation and Reversal of Input Tax Credit i. We observe that the Applicant is making a taxable outward supply of development services to the landowners and is liable to discharge GST on such supply under the forward charge mechanism. ii. Section 16 of the CGST Act, 2017 entitles a registered person to avail input tax credit on goods or services used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business, subject to the conditions and restrictions prescribed therein. Further, Section 49 of the CGST Act permits utilisation of the balance available in the electronic credit ledger towards payment of output tax, subject to prescribed conditions. iii. Accordingly, the Applicant is entitled to avail and utilise the eligible input tax credit towards discharge of the GST liability on the taxable outward supply of development services, subject to fulfilment of the conditions prescribed under Section 16 and restriction contained in Section 17. iv. Since the eligible input tax credit is available for utilisation towards payment of output tax, the question of reversal of such eligible credit does Builders & Developers Page 9 of 1E not arise. However, any ineligible input tax credit shall be reversed in accordance with the provisions of the CGST Act, 2017 and the Rules made thereunder. 12. In view of the foregoing, we pass the following. RULING i. Question 1: Whether the Applicant is required to issue tax invoices to the landowners in respect of the development activities, including the determination of the invoiced amount and the extent of such invoicing? Answer: The question is not maintainable under Section 97(2) of the CGST Act, 2017, as it seeks clarification regarding the procedural aspects of invoicing and does not fall within any of the specified categories of questions on which an advance ruling can be sought. Accordingly, no ruling can be given on the said question. ii. Question: Whether the Applicant is entitled to avail and utilise the eligible input tax credit towards payment of GST on such development services and, if not, the manner and timing of reversal of input tax credit? Answer: The Applicant is entitled to avail and utilise eligible input tax credit towards payment of GST on the taxable outward supply of development services, subject to Sections 16 and 17 of the CGST Act, 2017. No reversal of eligible input tax credit is required. However, any ineligible input tax credit shall be reversed in accordance with the provisions of the CGST Act, 2017 and the Rules made thereunder.
