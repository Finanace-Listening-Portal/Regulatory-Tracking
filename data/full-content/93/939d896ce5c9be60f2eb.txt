Payments up to Rs 2,000 will remain free, while the new MDR does not apply to credit-linked UPI transactions, including RuPay credit cards linked to UPI, according to NPCI's framework. Advt The distinction could become important for banks and card issuers as UPI continues to take share from cards in merchant payments. UPI accounted for 77.3 per cent of merchant transactions in July 2026, up from 74.9 per cent a year earlier, while credit cards' share fell to 17.7 per cent from 19.8 per cent, according to Equirus data.Higher-value paymentsThe new MDR applies to only a small proportion of UPI merchant transactions. NPCI said payments of up to Rs 2,000 account for more than 95 per cent of P2M transaction volume, while the government estimates that around 96 per cent of P2M transactions will remain unaffected.The affected segment, however, is concentrated in higher-value payments. Industry estimates put transactions above Rs 2,000 at around 4 per cent of P2M volumes but about 67 per cent of their value. That makes the higher-value segment relevant for banks seeking to retain payment revenue as UPI expands.For a Rs 50,000 direct UPI merchant payment, for example, the new MDR would be Rs 200, while the charge would be capped at Rs 300 for transactions of Rs 75,000 or more. Credit cards already carry higher merchant acceptance costs than the new UPI MDR, but they also generate interchange income for issuers and offer rewards and a credit period to customers. Advt RuPay's unusual positionThe change is particularly significant for RuPay because its credit cards can be linked to UPI. The new framework keeps such credit-linked UPI transactions under the existing credit product rules rather than the new 0.4 per cent direct-account UPI MDR.This gives RuPay credit cards a different position from conventional UPI payments: customers can use the UPI interface and QR acceptance network while funding the transaction through credit. The Finance Ministry also said this week that the existing framework permits credit transactions on UPI only through RuPay credit cards. Advt NPCI has previously directed issuers to maintain parity in rewards, benefits and features on RuPay credit cards used through UPI, except where the issuer does not earn interchange.Cards under pressureThe development comes as the credit card market continues to grow in absolute terms but faces increasing competition from UPI. Credit cards outstanding rose to 122.86 million in July 2026 from 111.62 million a year earlier, while monthly credit card spending reached Rs 2.08 lakh crore, according to RBI data.At the same time, credit card transaction volumes are increasingly reflecting smaller-ticket spending. Transactions rose 24.1 per cent year-on-year to 601 million in July, while average transaction value fell 13.5 per cent to Rs 3,460. Industry analysis has linked part of the shift towards lower ticket sizes to the growing use of RuPay credit cards through UPI, although the available data does not establish that UPI alone drove the increase in card transaction volumes. Advt The new MDR therefore does not amount to a broad reversal of UPI's advantage. More than 95 per cent of P2M transactions will continue to face no MDR, and consumers will not be charged for UPI payments. Instead, the change alters the economics of a relatively small but high-value segment of merchant payments.For banks, the potential benefit could come from a combination of MDR income on eligible UPI transactions and greater use of credit products for larger purchases. But within that shift, RuPay is positioned differently from Visa and Mastercard because its credit cards can operate through UPI's merchant acceptance network.
