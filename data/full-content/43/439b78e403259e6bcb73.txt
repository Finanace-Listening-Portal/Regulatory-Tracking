Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Velusamy Meenal Vs ACIT (ITAT Chennai) Section 154 Rectification Cannot Substitute an Appeal Against Original Assessment: Chennai ITAT Summary: The Chennai Income Tax Appellate Tribunal has held that proceedings arising from a rectification application under Section 154 cannot serve as a substitute for an appeal against the original assessment order. In Velusamy Meenal v. ACIT, the Tribunal observed that the rectification application was an attempt to fill the gap created by non-filing of an appeal against the original assessment. After being informed that the assessee had subsequently filed a belated appeal against that assessment, it dismissed the rectification-related appeal without adjudicating the substantive assessment disputes. This article is based on the supplied order: C:UsersUserDesktoptaxguru officeviewOrder (1).pdf Facts of the Case The assessee filed a return under Section 139(4) on 27 March 2018, declaring total income of ₹1,03,50,580 for Assessment Year 2017–18. The case was selected for scrutiny to examine, among other matters, large cash deposits and differences in opening and closing written-down value. The Assessing Officer invoked Section 145(3) and estimated profit at 3.5% of total receipts of ₹60,77,38,284, resulting in assessed profit of ₹2,12,70,840. The original scrutiny assessment order was passed under Section 143(3) on 27 December 2019. Rectification Application and Subsequent Appeals The assessee filed an application under Section 154 before the Assessing Officer. That application was rejected. The assessee challenged the rejection before the CIT(A)/NFAC, which dismissed the appeal through an order dated 3 November 2023. The present appeal before the Tribunal arose from that rectification-related appellate order. It was filed with a delay of 853 days. Contentions Raised by the Assessee The assessee disputed the conclusion that the issues raised were debatable and outside the scope of rectification. The grounds referred to: Non-deduction of interest and bank chargesagainst interest assessable on fixed deposits. Computation of gross receipts without considering materials recovered by the Department and subcontract-related amounts. Rejection of books under Section 145(3). Adoption of 5% profit on total contract receipts. Alleged denial of an effective opportunity of hearing. These were the assessee’s contentions; the Tribunal did not decide their merits. Status of the Appeal Against the Original Assessment During the hearing, the authorised representative informed the Tribunal that an appeal against the original Section 143(3) assessment was pending before the CIT(A). The Tribunal noted that this differed from the position recorded in the CIT(A)’s rectification-related order, which stated that no appeal against the original assessment was pending. The assessee also filed an affidavit and petition seeking condonation of the 853-day delay in the present appeal. In that petition, the assessee disclosed that the original assessment had been challenged belatedly, and furnished the relevant acknowledgement. Tribunal’s Finding: Rectification Appeal Cannot Replace Assessment Appeal On these facts, the Tribunal characterised the Section 154 application as an attempt to cover the vacuum created by non-filing of an appeal against the original assessment order. It held that the present appeal could not legally act as a substitute for the appeal against that assessment. Since the authorised representative had furnished particulars of the separately filed assessment appeal, the Tribunal found no reason to entertain and adjudicate the present proceedings. It therefore dismissed the grounds without adjudicating the underlying issues. Scope of Section 154 Section 154 provides a mechanism for correcting a mistake apparent from the record. It is distinct from the appellate remedy against an assessment order. The principle emerging from this decision is that rectification proceedings cannot be used to replace the appropriate assessment appeal. The order should not, however, be read as holding that a rectification application is barred merely because an assessment appeal exists. The Tribunal’s conclusion rested on the particular procedural history and the attempt to pursue assessment grievances through the rectification-related appeal. What the Tribunal Did Not Decide The Tribunal did not uphold the 3.5% profit rate on merits, nor did it independently examine the correctness of rejecting the books or computing the contract receipts. Although it recorded the 853-day delay and the filing of a condonation petition, it did not provide a separate reasoned determination granting or refusing condonation. The operative outcome was dismissal of the rectification-related appeal without substantive adjudication. The decision did not determine the merits or admissibility of the separately filed appeal against the original assessment. Practical Takeaway The decision highlights the need to distinguish between correction of an apparent mistake and a substantive challenge to an assessment. Where the grievance concerns the rejection of books, estimation of profit or determination of receipts, taxpayers should preserve the appropriate appellate remedy and its limitation requirements. A Section 154 application cannot be relied upon to fill the gap left by failure to pursue that appeal. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT CHENNAI This is an appeal preferred by the assessee against the order of Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax (A) / NFAC, (hereinafter referred to as the ‘Ld. CIT(A)’), Delhi dated 03.11.2023 for the Assessment Year (hereinafter referred as the ‘AY’) 2017-18. GROUDS OF APPEAL 1. The order of the NFAC, Delhi dated 03.11.2023 vide DIN & Order No. ITBA/NFAC/S/250/2023-24/1057672198(1) for the above-mentioned Assessment Year is contrary to law, fact and in circumstances of the case 2. The NFAC, Delhi erred in sustaining the action of the Assessing Officer in rejecting the rectification petition filed under Section 154 of the Act on the premise of the disputed issue being a “debatable” / outside the purview of the scope of rectification proceedings without assigning proper reasons and justification 3. The NFAC, Delhi failed to appreciate that issue of non-deduction of interest and bank charges towards the interest assessable on fixed deposit as well as the computation of gross receipts without considering the quantum of materials recovered by the Department and sub-contract on the facts of the present case could not be reckoned as a “debatable issue” and ought to have appreciated that the assessment of 3.5% of the total contract receipts aggregating to Rs. 60,77,38,284/- to the tune of Rs. 2,12,70,840/- as income of the appellant should be reckoned as wholly unjustified and not sustainable in law. 4. The NFAC, Delhi failed to appreciate that the reporting of the appellant for the income earned during the assessment year under consideration was correct on various facets and ought to have appreciated that the arbitrary re-computation of taxable income should be reckoned as bad in law. 5. The NFAC, Delhi failed to appreciate that in any event rejection of the books of accounts in terms of Section 145(3) of the Act as well as the quantification of the income of the appellant at 3.5% of the total contract receipts was arbitrary and wholly unjustified and further ought to have appreciated that having note taken on record the nature of the business pursued by the appellant, the mechanical action in adopting the rate at 3.5% of the total contract receipts should accordingly be reckoned as nullity in law. 6. The NFAC, Delhi failed to appreciate that the findings in relation thereto forming part of the impugned order were wrong, erroneous, incorrect. Invalid, unjustified and not sustainable both on facts and in law. 7. The NFAC, Delhi failed to appreciate that the entire re-computation of taxable total income including the fixing the estimated income at 3.5% of the total contract receipts was wrong, erroneous, incorrect, invalid, unjustified and not sustainable both on facts and in law, 8. The NFAC, Delhi failed to appreciate that having not adhered to the prescription of faceless appellate regime, the consequential appellate order passed should be reckoned as bad in law. 9. The NFAC, Delhi failed to appreciate that there was no effective/proper opportunity given before passing the impugned order including non-granting of personal hearing and any order passed in violation of the principles of natural justice is nullity in law. 10. The Appellant craves leave to file additional grounds/arguments at the time of hearing. 2. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee filed his return of income on 27.03.2018 u/s. 139(4) of the Act, declaring total income at Rs.1,03,50,580/-. The case of the assessee was selected for scrutiny to examine the large cash deposit and difference in opening and closing WDV and cash deposits. Ultimately, the case of the assessee was assessed applying the provisions of section 145(3) of the Act and profit @ 3.5 % of the total receipts (Rs. 60,77,38,284/-) was assessed amounting to Rs. 2,12,70,840/-. The assessee being aggrieved with this order, preferred an appeal before the Ld. CIT(A) and his/her order is still pending for disposal. In the meantime, the assessee filed an application u/s. 154 of the Act before the AO and the same was rejected by the AO. Now the assessee again preferred an appeal before the Ld. CIT(A) (against the rejection of application filed u/s. 154 of the Act), who in turn dismissed the appeal of the assessee. The assessee being further aggrieved, preferred the present appeal before us with a delay of 853 days. 3. We have gone through the orders of the authorities below and submissions of the assessee along with grounds taken before us. During the arguments before the Bench, it was confirmed by the Ld. AR that appeal against the original order passed u/s. 143(3) of the Act vide dated 27.12.2019 is also pending for disposal before the Ld. CIT(A) (although as per the order of Ld. CIT(A), against rejection of application u/s. 154 of the Act, it is stated therein that there is no appeal pending against the original assessment order passed u/s. 143(3) of the Act). 4. Although, the Ld. AR today filed an affidavit along with a petition for condonation of delay of 853 days, in this petition the assessee tried to explain the delay of 853 days and also conveyed to the bench that he has challenged the original scrutiny assessment u/s. 143(3) of the Act belatedly vide acknowledgement no. 274814480121126. In view of the above facts on record, it is observed that the application filed by the assessee u/s. 154 of the Act was nothing but an attempt to cover the vacuum created by non-filing of appeal against the original assessment order and the present appeal can’t be the substitute for appeal to be filed against the original assessment order, legally also. But, as the Ld. AR himself has furnished the information about appeal filed against the original assessment order vide acknowledgement no. 274814480121126, we do not see any reason to entertain and adjudicate the present appeal for the reasons mentioned (supra). Resultantly, the grounds raised by the assessee are dismissed with no adjudication of the matter. 5. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is dismissed in the above terms. Order pronounced on the 16th day of November 2026, in Chennai.
