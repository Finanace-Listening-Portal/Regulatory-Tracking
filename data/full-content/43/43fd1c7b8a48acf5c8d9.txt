Vs ACIT (ITAT Surat) Section 80P Claim Requires Examination of Actual Receipts: Inter-Departmental Interest Sent Back for Verification Background of the Case The assessee, a cooperative society, filed its return declaring gross total income of ₹26,87,815 and claimed deduction of the entire amount under section 80P, resulting in nil taxable income. While processing the return under section 143(1), the deduction was partly disallowed to the extent of ₹9,72,930. This comprised interest income of ₹2,55,865 and rental income of ₹7,17,065. The society filed an application for rectification under section 154. By an order dated 14 August 2020, the Assessing Officer accepted the claim relating to rental income under section 80P(2)(e). However, he rejected the deduction claimed in respect of the interest income. The assessee’s appeal before the CIT(A) was unsuccessful. The society consequently approached the Tribunal, where the surviving dispute concerned the treatment of ₹2,55,865 described as interest income. Assessee’s Explanation Before the Tribunal Before the Tribunal, the assessee sought admission of additional evidence under Rule 29 of the Income-tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963. Referring particularly to a Chartered Accountant’s certificate, the assessee explained that two amounts included in the disputed interest income—₹58,968 and ₹93,089—represented inter-departmental interest within the same society. According to the assessee, one department recorded these amounts as income while another department recorded the corresponding expenditure. They therefore did not constitute interest income in the real sense at the level of the society. The two internal entries together amounted to ₹1,52,057. As regards the balance interest income, the assessee contended that it was eligible for deduction under section 80P(2)(a)(iii). The society submitted that these factual aspects concerning the nature of the receipts had not been examined by the lower authorities. It therefore requested restoration of the matter to the Assessing Officer for verification and fresh adjudication. The Revenue relied on the orders of the lower authorities but did not seriously oppose the proposed remand. Tribunal’s Findings The Tribunal observed that the central issue was the eligibility for deduction under section 80P in respect of the disputed interest income. It found that the additional evidence, particularly the Chartered Accountant’s certificate, had a direct and material bearing on the nature and character of the receipts. Accordingly, it admitted the evidence under Rule 29. The Tribunal noted the assessee’s specific contention that part of the amount represented inter-departmental interest and that the remaining interest qualified for deduction under section 80P(2)(a)(iii). Since these matters had neither been examined nor verified by the lower authorities, a fresh factual enquiry was necessary. The issue was therefore restored to the Assessing Officer with directions to examine the additional evidence, verify the nature and character of the receipts, and adjudicate the deduction claim in accordance with law after providing a reasonable opportunity of hearing. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. The Tribunal did not itself exclude the internal entries or finally grant deduction for the remaining interest income. Author’s Comments Before deciding whether interest income qualifies for deduction, it is necessary to establish whether the disputed amount represents income of the assessee at all. This case illustrates the importance of distinguishing an external receipt from an internal accounting allocation. Where departments form part of the same assessee, an interest charge between them may appear as income in one departmental account and expenditure in another. The society’s consolidated accounts and tax computation must therefore be examined to determine the actual effect. The description “interest income” in a departmental ledger does not, by itself, settle its tax treatment. However, the Chartered Accountant’s certificate is supporting evidence, not a substitute for reconciliation. On remand, the society should establish the corresponding debit and credit entries and demonstrate how they were treated in arriving at its gross total income. The remaining interest presents a separate question. The assessee invoked section 80P(2)(a)(iii), but the Tribunal did not determine whether the receipts satisfied that provision. Their source and connection with the qualifying activity will require examination. The decision should therefore be cited for admission of material additional evidence and the need to verify the actual character of receipts. It cannot be presented as a blanket ruling allowing deduction under section 80P for interest income earned by a cooperative society. Cases Discussed Totgars Co-operative Sale Society Ltd. Vs ITO (Supreme Court)– (2010) 322 ITR 283 (SC). Referred to in the assessee’s additional-evidence application. The application cited this decision while discussing the society’s claim for deduction. The Tribunal did not decide the present appeal by applying this precedent.
