Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Updated On Oct 28, 2026 at 07:41 PM IST var news_msid = 135267368; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Representative imageIndia’s retail inflation could peak at 6.3% in the December quarter of FY27, with food prices and rising input costs posing upside risks to the inflation trajectory, Morgan Stanley said in its latest report.The firm expects headline consumer price index (CPI) inflation to average 5.3% in FY27, while projecting the Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI) repo rate to rise to 6.25% by April 2027. It said inflation pressures were broadening beyond food, even as underlying demand-driven pressures remained relatively contained.Food inflation may rise to 8.2% in second half of FY27Headline CPI inflation accelerated to 5.5% year-on-year in September 2026 from 2.7% in January, while food inflation rose to 7.4% in September from 5.7% in August, the report said. Advt Food inflation is expected to rise to around 8.2% in the second half of FY27, compared with 5.3% in the first half. Deficient monsoon rainfall, potential losses in kharif output and uncertainty around rabi sowing could add to price pressures.The report noted that the southwest monsoon recorded a 12.6% deficit, its widest in 11 years. Preliminary government estimates pointed to a potential 3% year-on-year decline in kharif foodgrain production, while rabi sowing had started weakly, with overall acreage down 36.9% year-on-year as of October 23.Wholesale inflation raises consumer price risksWholesale price index (WPI) inflation accelerated to 11.4% in September, while core WPI inflation stood at 9.8%, reflecting elevated commodity prices and currency depreciation.The report said higher producer costs could eventually feed into consumer prices, typically with a lag of four to six months. Companies may initially absorb higher input costs through margins, but sustained pressures could lead to price increases.The rupee had weakened around 7.5% against the US dollar over the preceding 12 months. Morgan Stanley cited RBI sensitivity estimates suggesting that a 5% depreciation in the rupee could raise headline CPI inflation by 40 basis points.A 10% increase in oil prices could raise headline CPI inflation by 68 basis points, according to the report. Crude oil prices is expected to reach $105 per barrel in the December quarter of 2026 before moderating to an average of $94.3 per barrel in 2027. Advt RBI may raise repo rate to 6.25%Morgan Stanley expects the RBI to deliver three additional 25-basis-point rate hikes following its 25-basis-point increase in October, taking the cumulative tightening in the current cycle to 100 basis points and the terminal repo rate to 6.25% by April 2027.The firm described the expected rate increases as policy normalisation aimed at anchoring inflation expectations, restoring real rates and preserving macroeconomic stability.However, it said the risks were tilted towards a more prolonged or aggressive tightening cycle if food inflation remained persistent or crude oil prices stayed at $120–130 per barrel for an extended period. Advt Core inflation remains relatively containedCore CPI inflation, which excludes food and fuel, rose to 4.3% in September from 3.7% in January. However, core-core inflation, which also excludes jewellery, remained lower at 3.2%.Morgan Stanley attributed the relatively contained underlying pressures to subdued wage growth and constrained pricing power. Employee wage growth stood at 6.8% year-on-year in the September quarter, the report said.The firm also flagged rising household inflation expectations as a risk to disinflation, warning that persistent food prices and higher input costs could create second-round effects and slow the return of inflation towards the RBI’s 4% target. Advt For FY28, Morgan Stanley forecasts headline CPI inflation at 4.5%, but said the risks were tilted to the upside.
