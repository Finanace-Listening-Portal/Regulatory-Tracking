Key Takeaways Show all 28 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Summary: reading the current V18 savings contract PNB MetLife Guaranteed Future Plan is an individual, non-linked, non-participating savings life insurance plan. PNB MetLife India Insurance Company Limited’s active-plan register and current sales brochure identify UIN 117N124V18. The contract combines life cover with one of four benefit structures: Endowment, Income, Income plus Lumpsum, or Income plus Boosters. The word “guaranteed” applies to benefits calculated under the chosen option when the policy remains in force and the contractual conditions are met; it does not convert the policy into a bank deposit, make surrender value equal to premiums paid, or guarantee that every customer receives an advertised illustration. Age, premium, premium-payment term, policy term, payout frequency, deferment and underwriting change the issued cash flows. This guide separates current V18 features from promotional examples, explains death, lapse, reduced paid-up, surrender, revival and loan consequences, and applies current income-tax and GST rules. A prospective policyholder should use the signed benefit illustration and issued policy schedule for personal numbers, because a general article cannot calculate an individual internal rate of return or suitability. Current product identity and what UIN 117N124V18 proves The current active-plan register lists Guaranteed Future Plan under UIN 117N124V18, and the current brochure describes it as individual, non-linked and non-participating. Non-linked means there is no unit account whose value follows market prices. Non-participating means the contract does not share in insurer-declared bonuses. Instead, the issued schedule fixes the applicable guaranteed-addition, wealth-addition, income and booster parameters for the chosen configuration. The insurer’s registration number is 117; that registration and the UIN identify the insurer and approved product version, but neither is a regulator’s recommendation, a promise of investment performance or a substitute for underwriting. Version control matters. Older brochures and withdrawn versions of the same plan remain searchable online. A customer considering a new purchase should see 117N124V18 consistently on the proposal, benefit illustration, customer information sheet, policy schedule and brochure. If an issued document carries a different UIN, its own terms control. The current V18 brochure expressly states that it invalidates earlier printed versions for this particular plan and that the detailed policy document governs. Four benefit options create different cash-flow obligations Endowment and Income options Under the Endowment option, the maturity benefit is designed as a lump sum. The brochure explains that guaranteed additions accrue during the premium-payment term, while wealth additions for the Endowment structure accrue after completion of that term. The amount depends on entry age, premium, payment term and the issued factors. Under the Income option, accumulated guaranteed additions and premiums support scheduled guaranteed income during the payout period. The brochure permits income timing and frequency choices, but a monthly or half-yearly election is not simply annual income divided without adjustment: the current income-payout factors are 100% for annual, 97% multiplied by one-half for half-yearly, and 95% multiplied by one-twelfth for monthly payments. Income plus Lumpsum and Income plus Boosters Income plus Lumpsum combines scheduled income with a separate maturity lump sum based on the contract’s wealth-addition design. Income plus Boosters adds periodic booster amounts to the regular income schedule rather than a wealth-addition maturity amount. The brochure makes these components option-specific: wealth additions are available with Endowment and Income plus Lumpsum, while boosters belong only to Income plus Boosters. A sales conversation that combines features from different options would be misleading. The signed illustration must show one coherent option, premium term, policy term, deferment, income frequency and benefit schedule. Life cover, death benefit and option-specific conditions The plan carries life cover through the policy term while the policy is in force. The current brochure’s illustrations show death benefits that depend on the selected configuration and policy year. It also states that for the promotional limited-pay illustration the minimum life cover is eleven times annualised premium, while certain short premium-payment terms offer age-based or alternative death-benefit multiples. These headline multiples should not be copied into a personal calculation without the current schedule. The nominee receives the death benefit specified for the issued policy, subject to policy status, exclusions, valid nomination or succession, disclosure and claim evidence. Two exclusions require particular attention. For suicide within twelve months from commencement of risk or revival, the in-force policy provides the higher of 80% of total premiums paid or available surrender value, as set out in the brochure. For business sourced through a Point of Sales Person, a 90-day waiting period applies; for non-accidental death within that period, the brochure states that 100% of total premiums paid is payable. Accidental death during that waiting period is treated differently under the contract. These provisions are not general statements about every PNB MetLife policy and must be matched to the issued channel and wording. Premium discipline: grace, lapse and reduced paid-up status Grace period preserves cover only temporarily For limited-pay policies, the grace period is 15 days for monthly mode and 30 days for other modes. Risk cover continues during grace. If a valid death claim occurs before the overdue instalment is paid, the due and unpaid premium for that policy year may be deducted from the death benefit. The grace period is therefore a short contractual buffer, not a waiver of the premium obligation. The first full policy year changes non-forfeiture rights If the first full policy year’s premium is not paid, the limited-pay policy lapses after grace; risk cover and rider benefits cease, and no benefit is payable while lapsed. Once the policy has acquired surrender value, later non-payment can instead move it to reduced paid-up status. Paid-up status preserves reduced contractual benefits, but scheduled survival benefits are not payable in the same form. For income options, reduced amounts are accumulated under the brochure’s formula and paid at maturity. This is materially different from continuing the original income stream. Surrender, revival and policy loans Surrender value is formula-driven, not a premium refund For limited pay, Special Surrender Value is acquired after the first full policy year has been paid; the current brochure states that Guaranteed Surrender Value is acquired after at least two full policy years. A single-pay policy acquires both from commencement of risk. The amount payable is the higher of GSV and SSV. GSV uses the applicable factor on total premiums paid after deducting survival benefits already paid, subject to the formula. SSV is determined by the insurer and reviewed in accordance with IRDAI regulations. Payment of surrender value terminates the policy and associated cover. A policyholder should request a dated written quotation before surrendering and compare it with the reduced paid-up alternative. Revival restores benefits only after acceptance A lapsed or paid-up policy may be submitted for revival during the revival period, subject to arrears, interest, underwriting and written acceptance. The brochure describes the then-current revival interest as 8% compounded annually and ties review to the ten-year government-security yield formula. That rate is reviewable, so it should not be treated as permanent. A surrendered policy cannot be revived. On accepted revival, additions linked to premiums paid on revival can accrue as if the contract had continued, and past-due income may be handled in the manner specified in the brochure. Loan availability depends on acquired surrender value The plan permits a policy loan under the detailed terms. The brochure states that the then-current loan interest is 10% a year compounded annually and is reviewed using a government-security-linked formula. Outstanding principal and interest reduce amounts otherwise payable and can affect policy economics. The customer should obtain the maximum eligible amount, current interest rate, compounding basis and foreclosure consequences in writing. A loan is not an additional benefit or free liquidity; it is borrowing secured against the contract. Claims and grievance handling for a V18 policy A claimant should intimate the insurer promptly, quote the policy number and UIN, and use the current claim form and document list. Common evidence can include the death certificate, claimant identity and bank details, policy record, medical material and incident documents, but the exact list depends on the event. Keep acknowledgements for every submission. Nomination should be maintained under section 39 of the Insurance Act; assignment follows section 38, subject to the policy’s restrictions. Material facts in the proposal must be complete because section 45 governs when and how a life policy may be questioned for fraud or misstatement. The current brochure provides the insurer’s toll-free number 1800-425-6969 and service email [email protected]. It also identifies escalation to the Grievance Redressal Officer at [email protected] and, if unresolved and jurisdictionally eligible, the Insurance Ombudsman. A useful complaint records the issue, dates, clause relied upon, amount disputed, remedy requested and prior correspondence. IRDAI does not sell policies, announce bonuses or ask customers to invest premiums; suspicious calls should not be treated as official claim or service communications. Income tax: deduction and receipt exemption are conditional Premium deduction does not arise merely from the product name Life-insurance premium can form part of the eligible payments supporting the aggregate deduction under section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, subject to the statutory premium-to-capital-sum-assured condition, the overall ceiling and the taxpayer’s applicable tax regime. Ownership, the person insured and competing eligible investments matter. A brochure statement about “tax benefits” is therefore not a certificate that the full premium is deductible. Retain premium receipts, the policy schedule and benefit illustration and apply the law for the relevant tax year. Maturity, income and surrender receipts need a separate test For non-ULIP life policies issued on or after 1 April 2023, the current exemption framework applies an aggregate annual-premium threshold of ₹5 lakh across relevant policies, alongside the premium-to-sum-assured and other conditions. If the exemption fails, the taxable computation generally considers the receipt and qualifying premiums under the applicable income-from-other-sources rule; it is not correct simply to tax or exempt the gross cash flow without analysis. Death receipts have a separate statutory protection, subject to the governing law. Because this plan can distribute income over many years, keep an annual record of premiums and each benefit received and obtain tax advice for the relevant year. GST on individual life insurance from 22 September 2025 The GST Council’s 56th-meeting materials announced exemption for all individual life insurance policies, including term, ULIP and endowment policies, and the current V18 brochure says GST is not applicable to individual life insurance under prevailing provisions. Government guidance implemented the exemption from 22 September 2025. Accordingly, a qualifying individual-policy premium paid under the current rule is not charged GST. This does not make every insurer service or group policy exempt, does not decide income-tax treatment, and does not prevent a later legislative change. The invoice and payment date remain the practical evidence for the transaction. Frequently Asked Questions on Guaranteed Future Plan V18 1. Is V18 a market-linked policy? No. UIN 117N124V18 is non-linked and non-participating. Benefits follow the issued contract rather than a unit value or participating bonus. 2. Are the four payout options interchangeable? No. Endowment, Income, Income plus Lumpsum and Income plus Boosters have different components. The issued schedule and illustration for the chosen option control. 3. Does “guaranteed” mean premiums can be stopped without consequence? No. Full benefits depend on policy status and premium payment. Non-payment can cause lapse or reduced paid-up benefits, depending on whether non-forfeiture rights have been acquired. 4. When does a limited-pay policy acquire surrender value? The brochure says SSV is acquired after the first full policy year has been paid and GSV after at least two full policy years. The higher contractual amount is payable on surrender. 5. Can a surrendered policy be revived? No. A lapsed or paid-up policy may be considered for revival within the contractual period and subject to acceptance, but a surrendered policy cannot be revived. 6. Is the advertised internal rate of return available to everyone? No. The headline illustration is tied to stated age, premium, term, option, mode and in-force assumptions. Personal return must be calculated from the signed benefit illustration and actual cash-flow dates. 7. Are all maturity or income payments tax-free? No. Exemption depends on issue date, aggregate premiums, premium-to-sum-assured limits and other statutory conditions. Death receipts follow a separate rule. 8. Is GST currently payable on this individual policy premium? Under the prevailing exemption effective 22 September 2025, qualifying individual life insurance premiums are exempt. Check the invoice and any later change in law. Lapse, paid-up conversion, surrender, revival and loans materially change outcomes. Surrender pays the higher of contractual GSV and SSV, not automatically the premiums paid, and it terminates the policy.
