Table of Contents▸ In re Encube Ethicals Private Limited (CAAR Mumbai) Cases Discussed FULL TEXT OF THE ORIGINAL RULING (AS SUPPLIED) Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Encube Ethicals Private Limited (CAAR Mumbai) Summary: The Customs Authority for Advance Rulings (CAAR), Mumbai, considered two applications filed by Encube Ethicals Private Limited seeking clarification on the applicable Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) rate on imported bulk drugs and Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs). The applicant contended that its imports qualified for the 5% IGST rate under Sl. No. 226 of Schedule I to Notification No. 9/2025–Integrated Tax (Rate), dated 17 September 2025, although it had previously been paying IGST at 18%. The disputed products were classified under Chapters 28 and 29 of the Customs Tariff and were imported for pharmaceutical manufacturing, examination, testing, clinical research and bioequivalence studies. The applicant relied on Section 3(b) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, and the definition of bulk drugs under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013. It argued that the statutory meaning of drugs includes substances intended for use as components of medicines. The applicant also produced import licences under Forms 10, 11 and CT-17 to establish the pharmaceutical character of the goods. It maintained that the notification’s reference to goods falling under Chapter 30 or any Chapter demonstrated that the concessional entry was not confined to finished pharmaceutical formulations. The Authority examined the competing entries under Notification No. 9/2025, particularly Sl. No. 226 of Schedule I and Sl. Nos. 35 and 36 of Schedule II. It observed that the former specifically covered drugs and medicines, whereas the latter generally covered inorganic and organic chemicals under Chapters 28 and 29. Applying the principle that a specific entry prevails over a general entry, the Authority accepted that APIs satisfying the description of drugs could qualify under Sl. No. 226 despite their chemical tariff classification. The Authority also considered judicial precedents and advance rulings discussing whether bulk drugs fall within the meaning of drugs. These included decisions concerning Cipla, Burroughs Wellcome, Laurus Labs, Biocon, Sterling Biotech and Altis Finechem, together with the earlier CAAR ruling issued to Cipla Limited on 17 March 2026. The Authority distinguished the pharmaceutical nature and intended use of the imported APIs from their classification as chemical substances and considered the relevant statutory licences and supporting documentation. The Authority ruled that the subject bulk drugs/APIs were eligible for IGST at 5% under Sl. No. 226 of Schedule I to Notification No. 9/2025–Integrated Tax (Rate), provided the particular goods were not covered by the exclusions, including Sl. No. 113 of Notification No. 10/2025–Integrated Tax (Rate). The Customs authorities at the port of import could verify the goods, certificates of analysis, licences and other supporting documents. However, in the case of an exemption notification, the same must be construed strictly in favour of the Revenue. I observe that the IGST Notification in question is a taxing notification and not an exemption notification. The subject goods are intended to be used as active pharmaceutical ingredients in the manufacture of pharmaceutical formulations and in certain cases for examination, testing, clinical research, bioavailability or bioequivalence studies. Thus, the subject goods possess the character of Bulk Drugs/APIs and arc regulated as pharmaceutical substances. 7.10.2 Under Section 3(b) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, the definition of “drug” includes medicines and substances intended for use in the diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease or disorder in human beings or animals, as well as substances intended for use as components of a drug. Accordingly, Bulk Drugs/APIs intended for use as active ingredients in pharmaceutical formulations Call within the regulatory framework governing drugs, subject to the conditions prescribed under the said Act and the rules made thereunder. In the present case, the Applicant has submitted the relevant statutory licences/approvals issued under the Drugs and Cosmetics regulatory framework in support of the nature and intended use of the subject goods. 7.10.3 SI. No. 226 of Schedule Ito Notification No. 09/2025–Integrated Tax (Rate) dated 17.09.2025 prescribes an KIST rate of 5% for “All Drugs and medicines including their salts and esters and diagnostic test kits; formulations manufactured from bulk drugs”, and the entry covers goods falling under “30 or any Chapter”, subject to the exclusion specified therein. The entry is description-based and is not restricted merely to goods filling under Chapter 30. Further, the fact that such goods are imported in bulk form or as APIs does not, by itself, take them outside the scope of the specific description of “All Drugs and medicines”, where their pharmaceutical character and intended medicinal use are established from the• documents and regulatory approvals placed on record. 7.10.4 In view of the above discussion, I find that the subject Bulk Drugs/Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs), as described in the application and supported by the documents submitted by the Applicant. qualify as “Drugs and medicines” for the purpose of Sl.
