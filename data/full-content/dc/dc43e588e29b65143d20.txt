(Bombay High Court) Court Can Record an Assurance to Preserve Documents While Deferring Ad-Interim Disclosure The USD 15 Billion Damages Suit The Bombay High Court accepted Bank of Baroda’s assurance to preserve documents in its custody or control while adjourning the hearing of Dr. Bavaguthu Raghuram Shetty’s application for ad-interim disclosure. Justice Arif S. Doctor recorded the assurance pending further hearing. The order illustrates how documents sought in a civil suit can be preserved while the parties complete their pleadings and the Court considers whether immediate disclosure should be directed. Dr. Shetty, the founder of NMC Healthcare LLC, instituted the suit seeking USD 15 billion in damages from Bank of Baroda and the other defendants. According to the plaintiff, the alleged fraud involved persons associated with the bank and persons who held key managerial positions at NMC Healthcare. He attributed substantial financial losses to the defendants’ alleged acts and omissions. The documents listed in the schedules to his interim application were said to be in the bank’s possession. The plaintiff contended that those records would help establish the alleged fraud and the role of the respective defendants. Bank of Baroda Opposes Immediate Disclosure Bank of Baroda opposed ad-interim relief and sought time to file a reply. Its senior counsel submitted that, on the plaintiff’s own pleadings, the cause of action, if any, arose in 2020. The bank also informed the Court that it had filed a separate interim application that morning seeking deferment of the hearing. In support of that request, the bank referred to a judgment delivered in proceedings between the parties in Abu Dhabi, which, according to its counsel, held that the plaintiff was complicit in the fraud he alleged. The plaintiff’s counsel stated that the deferment application had yet to be served on his clients and that they had received no intimation of its filing. He then sought a narrower protective measure. If the Court was inclined to defer disclosure, counsel asked that the documents identified in the schedules be preserved until the next hearing. The object was to protect those documents against possible destruction, tampering or interpolation in the meantime. Court Accepts Assurance to Preserve Scheduled Documents The bank submitted that the documents already formed part of the Abu Dhabi court proceedings and disputed the plaintiff’s apprehension about their preservation. Its senior counsel nevertheless assured the Court that all documents forming part of the schedules to the interim application and within the bank’s custody or control would be preserved as they were. The High Court accepted that statement. The terms of the assurance define its scope. It covers the scheduled documents within Bank of Baroda’s custody or control and requires their preservation in their existing state. The plaintiff’s request to obtain disclosure of those documents remained pending. Thus, the immediate procedural concern about the documents’ continued availability was addressed through the assurance while the question of access awaited further hearing. Objection Under Rule 89 Kept Open Bank of Baroda also contended that the proceedings before the Bombay High Court were barred by Rule 89 of the Abu Dhabi Global Market Court of First Instance, Court Procedure Rules, 2016. The plaintiff disputed the applicability of Rule 89. The Court expressly deferred consideration of that objection until the bank filed its reply affidavit. The reference to the Abu Dhabi judgment arose in the bank’s submissions, and the effect of the foreign proceedings on the Bombay suit remained for consideration. The bank agreed to file its reply within three weeks, furnish an advance copy to the plaintiff and seek no extension. The plaintiff’s counsel stated that the rejoinder affidavit would be filed within five days thereafter. He also undertook to file an affidavit proving service on the other respondents, who, according to him, had been served but had yet to appear. The Court listed the matter for 17 December 2026 for consideration of ad-interim relief. Author’s Comments The practical significance of the order lies in the distinction between preservation and disclosure. Preservation secures the continued availability and integrity of documents. Disclosure concerns whether, and on what terms, those documents must be made available to the applicant. In this case, the plaintiff identified the records by reference to schedules, and the bank’s assurance was tied to those schedules and to documents within its custody or control. That specificity gave the assurance a defined scope even though the disclosure application remained to be heard. The order is procedural and ad-interim. Its immediate effect was to place the bank’s preservation assurance on the judicial record while allowing the parties to address the disclosure request and the Rule 89 objection through affidavits. The alleged fraud and the USD 15 billion damages claim remain matters for adjudication in the suit. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF BOMBAY HIGH COURT 1. Mr. Andhyarujina, Learned Senior Counsel appearing for the Plaintiff, has today pressed for ad-interim relief in terms of an order of disclosure by Defendant No. 1, i.e., the Bank of Baroda, in respect of documents more particularly set out in the Interim Application. 2. He submits that the present Suit has been filed seeking to recover by way of damages, USD 15 billion from the Defendants on account of what he submits had been a fraud that has been perpetrated upon the Plaintiff by the Defendants who are either the employees of Defendant No. 1 or, then employees/former employees who held key managerial positions at a company called NMC Healthcare LLC, of which the Plaintiff is the founder. He submits that on account of various acts of omission and commission on the part of the Defendants, the Plaintiff has suffered huge financial losses. 3. Mr. Andhyarujina has therefore sought an order of disclosure directing Defendant No. 1 to disclose various documents, more particularly described in the Schedules annexed to the Interim Application, which are stated to be in the possession of Defendant No. 1, which he submits would establish the fraud that has been perpetrated upon the Plaintiff and also the role of the Defendants in such fraud. 4. Dr. Saraf, Learned Senior Counsel appearing for Defendant No. 1, opposes the grant of any ad-interim relief and prays for time to file a reply. He submits that the cause of action, if any, arose in 2020, as pleaded in the Plaint. 5. Dr. Saraf further informs the Court that Defendant No. 1 has only this morning filed an Interim Application seeking deferment of the hearing of the present Application. He informs the Court that in certain proceedings which had been instituted between the parties in Abu Dhabi, a judgement has been delivered which holds that the Plaintiff is complicit in the fraud which the Plaintiff now claims to have been perpetrated. 6. Mr. Andhyarujina submits that neither has any Application been served upon his clients, nor have they been informed of its filing. He then submitted that, if this Court was not inclined to grant an order of disclosure at this stage, Defendant No. 1 then be directed to preserve all such documents as mentioned in the Schedules to the Interim Application until the next date of hearing to ensure that the same are not either destroyed, tampered with and/or interpolated. 7. Dr. Saraf submits that the documents in question already form part of the proceedings before the Abu Dhabi Court, and therefore the need for passing any order of preservation of those documents does not arise and that the apprehensions expressed by the Plaintiff in this regard are wholly baseless. He, however, assures the Court that all such documents forming part of the Schedule to the Interim Application and in the custody or control of Defendant No. 1 shall be preserved as they are. His statement is accepted. 8. Dr. Saraf also submits that the present proceedings are barred by Rule 89 of the Abu Dhabi Global Market Court of First Instance, Court Procedure Rules, 2016. Mr. Andhyarujina disputes this contention and submits that Rule 89 has no application to the present proceedings. This issue will be considered after the Reply-Affidavit is filed. 9. Dr. Saraf submits that Defendant No. 1 shall file its Reply-Affidavit within a period of three weeks from today, with an advance copy to the other side. He assures the Court that no extension of time will be sought for filing the Reply-Affidavit. 10. Mr. Andhyarujina submits that the Rejoinder-Affidavit shall also be filed within five days thereafter. Learned Counsel also undertakes to file an Affidavit of Service to prove service upon the other Respondents who he submits have been served but have today not appeared. 11. Stand over to 17th December 2026 for ad-interim relief.
