} } Listen to This ArticleIndia’s largest private sector lender, HDFC Bank, on Saturday reported a 4.7 per cent year-on-year increase in net profit to ₹19,510 crore in the July-September quarter of FY27 (Q2FY27), as a decline in other income and higher operating expenses and provisions partly offset growth in core interest income. Net interest income (NII), the difference between interest earned and interest expended, rose 9.7 per cent year-on-year to ₹34,620 crore, supported by 16.3 per cent growth in gross advances. Other income declined 3.6 per cent year-on-year to ₹13,830 crore, primarily reflecting a sharp fall in treasury trading gains. However, it rose nearly 8 per cent sequentially. Fees and commissions, the largest component of other income, increased to ₹9,690 crore from ₹8,840 crore a year earlier. Net interest margin (NIM) stood at 3.16 per cent of total assets and 3.32 per cent of interest-earning assets. Operating expenses increased to ₹18,970 crore from ₹17,980 crore in the corresponding quarter a year earlier. The cost-to-income ratio stood at 39.1 per cent. Provisions and contingencies for the quarter amounted to ₹3,570 crore, while the total credit cost ratio stood at 0.44 per cent. The bank’s gross advances stood at ₹32.2 trillion as of September 30, 2026, registering 16.3 per cent growth over a year earlier. Average advances under management, adjusted for transfers through inter-bank participation certificates, bills rediscounted and securitisation or assignment, rose 14.1 per cent year-on-year to ₹31.87 trillion. They were up 4.9 per cent sequentially. Retail loans grew 11.7 per cent year-on-year, while loans to small and mid-market enterprises increased 23.5 per cent. Corporate and other wholesale loans rose 16.4 per cent. Overseas advances accounted for 3.3 per cent of total advances. Total end-of-period deposits increased 18.8 per cent year-on-year to ₹33.27 trillion as of September 30, 2026. Within this, time deposits rose 22.8 per cent to ₹22.76 trillion, while current account and savings account (CASA) deposits grew 10.8 per cent. Savings account deposits stood at ₹7.16 trillion and current account deposits at ₹3.36 trillion. CASA deposits accounted for 31.6 per cent of total deposits as of September 30, 2026. The bank’s total balance sheet size increased to ₹45.78 trillion as of September 30, 2026, from ₹40.03 trillion a year earlier. The bank’s gross non-performing assets (GNPA) ratio improved to 1.11 per cent of gross advances as of September 30, 2026, from 1.17 per cent in the June quarter and 1.24 per cent a year earlier. Excluding non-performing assets in the agricultural segment, the GNPA ratio stood at 0.87 per cent, compared with 0.91 per cent in the June quarter and 0.99 per cent a year earlier. Net non-performing assets stood at 0.37 per cent of net advances as of September 30, 2026. “I think the topline has been extremely healthy. We have seen strong underlying business momentum, particularly in the retail and MSME segments, which are moving in the right direction. The margin trajectory should turn upwards from the next quarter onwards. Our asset quality also continues to be extremely strong, which has been our USP through various business cycles, and we believe this will remain so for a long time”, said Sashidhar Jagdishan, MD&CEO, HDFC Bank. On a consolidated basis, the bank reported net revenue of ₹78,690 crore and net profit of ₹20,700 crore for Q2FY27. Consolidated net profit for the first half of FY27 stood at ₹39,940 crore. Seperately, Jagdishan also introduced the MD&CEO designate of the bank Anup Bagchi, who will succeed Jagdishan on October 27. “We welcome Anup with open arms. I believe he will bring a fresh perspective, and we wish him all the very best in his tenure as the CEO of HDFC Bank,” he said. In his opening remarks, Bagchi said: “I am very grateful to the board for the trust it has placed in me to lead the bank. I look forward to working with the board, the chairman and the leadership team. I would also like to thank Shashi for his leadership over the past six years. He has steered the bank through the post-pandemic period, overseen the landmark merger and helped scale up the branch network and strengthen the bank’s core technology capabilities. Accomplishing all of this in six years has been a significant undertaking. Bagchi also thanked Kaizad Bharucha, deputy MD, HDFC Bank for his contribution as he has decided to exit the bank at the end of December this year. “I would also like to thank Kaizad. One of the most distinguishing strengths of HDFC Bank, and the HDFC group more broadly, has been its robust credit quality across cycles and regimes. Since the bank was established in 1995, the country and its banking institutions have gone through several phases of credit cycles. The fact that the institution has maintained such strong credit quality is a testament to Kaizad and the deeply committed team he has helped build”, Bagchi said. “The post-merger balance sheet and the broader transformation are still works in progress, but I believe much of that journey is behind us. There is still work to be done, and I am grateful to the board for giving me this opportunity. We will work together with the team to ensure that the bank continues to move from strength to strength”, Bagchi said.
