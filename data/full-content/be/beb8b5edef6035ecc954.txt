Articles 2 min read SEBI working to deepen capital markets, simplify regulations to support 2x economy: Chairman Pandey The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is working to deepen capital markets, simplify regulations and expand funding avenues as it looks to prepare the country's financial markets to support an economy twice their current size, Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on Friday. ANI Published On Nov 28, 2026 at 12:30 PM IST var news_msid = 136240310; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is working to deepen capital markets, simplify regulations and expand funding avenues as it looks to prepare the country's financial markets to support an economy twice their current size, Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on Friday.Addressing the 17th Capital Market Conference organised by ASSOCHAM, Pandey outlined the regulator's priorities, including reviewing disclosure requirements, improving corporate bond market liquidity, widening participation by global investors and adopting emerging technologies with adequate safeguards."A comprehensive review of the LODR framework is underway," he said, adding that it would remove disclosures duplicating government filings and replace multiple exchange penalties for the same violation with a single penalty. Advt SEBI is also reviewing regulations governing IPOs, further public offers, preferential issues, qualified institutional placements and SME listings. It has tightened scrutiny of SME listings and is moving towards risk-based supervision, with fewer routine inspections for compliant entities.The regulator has also introduced the SWAGAT-FI single-window mechanism to ease access for low-risk foreign portfolio investors (FPIs). The framework has registered 740 FPIs, accounting for around 17 per cent of FPI assets under custody.Highlighting its approach to different categories of investors, Pandey said, "We have matched regulations to the investor type. A sophisticated investor and a first-time saver need different protection."SEBI has made direct plans the default option on mutual fund digital platforms, reduced the minimum face value of bonds from Rs 1 lakh to Rs 10,000 and introduced a credit riskometer to help investors assess bond-related risks. It has also reduced the threshold for large-value alternative investment funds from Rs 70 crore to Rs 25 crore.On investor protection, he said, "A smartphone can open a trading account in minutes. It cannot explain risk." SEBI's 'Samaj Se Investing Simple' campaign has recorded around 37 crore social media views and reached 63 cities, with awareness initiatives conducted in 13 languages. Advt On technology adoption, Pandey said, "We have welcomed innovation but with safeguards." SEBI is exploring a domestically governed artificial intelligence model, alongside responsible AI guidelines and a sandbox for testing applications in a controlled environment.The regulator is also developing a corporate bond market-making framework with the Reserve Bank of India and the Ministry of Finance to improve liquidity and access to repo facilities.Its other initiatives include a pilot for tokenised corporate bonds and measures to strengthen market resilience, including faster recovery of critical operations and tighter cyber incident reporting requirements. Advt Outlining its priorities, Pandey said, "First, we will keep simplifying the rulebook" and "Second, we will deepen the markets that finance growth."He added, "Deepening the capital market requires widening the issuer base," urging industry associations to work with stock exchanges to familiarise more enterprises with public capital raising and listing responsibilities."Calibration is a continuing discipline. We will keep measuring, adjusting, and consulting," Pandey said, adding that building capital markets capable of financing the country's ambitions remained a shared responsibility of regulators and industry.
