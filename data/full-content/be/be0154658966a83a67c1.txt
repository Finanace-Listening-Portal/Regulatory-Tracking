Articles 3 min read Bank credit grows 19.4% in September; services, NBFC lending lead sectoral growth: RBI Bank credit grew 19.4 per cent year-on-year in September 2026, with services credit rising 28.3 per cent and industry credit growing 19.5 per cent, according to RBI data. Lending to NBFCs rose 41.8 per cent, while personal loan growth stood at 17.3 per cent. Lending to industry grew 19.5 per cent, while credit to agriculture and allied activities rose 19.5 per cent. Personal loan growth stood at 17.3 per cent.Total outstanding bank credit, including food and non-food credit, stood at around Rs 229.95 lakh crore as of September 30, 2026, registering annual growth of about 19.4 per cent. Advt Services credit, NBFC lending lead growthWithin services, bank credit to NBFCs increased 41.8 per cent year-on-year to Rs 22.53 lakh crore, making it one of the strongest drivers of overall services credit growth.Credit to public financial institutions increased 79.7 per cent, while lending to housing finance companies grew 19.7 per cent.Among other services segments, credit to computer software rose 45.8 per cent, while aviation credit increased 42.7 per cent. Trade credit grew 23.9 per cent and commercial real estate lending rose 23.3 per cent.Credit to shipping, however, grew at a slower pace of 9.4 per cent in September, compared with 32.5 per cent growth in the year-ago period.The strong growth in services credit also marks a sharp acceleration from August, when services credit had grown 24.3 per cent year-on-year. The RBI had then identified NBFCs, trade, professional services and commercial real estate as key contributors.Industry credit grows 19.5%; medium industries outperformBank credit to industry increased 19.5 per cent year-on-year to Rs 49.77 lakh crore in September.Within industry, lending to medium industries grew 33.2 per cent, while credit to micro and small industries increased 24.4 per cent. Credit to large industries grew 16 per cent.Among major industrial segments, credit to engineering grew 36 per cent, while lending to gems and jewellery increased 33 per cent and construction credit rose 30 per cent. Advt Credit to infrastructure grew 11.8 per cent, with power-sector lending increasing 22.9 per cent. However, lending to roads remained subdued, growing just 0.7 per cent, while telecom credit contracted 13.4 per cent.Credit to railways, excluding Indian Railways, also declined 22.5 per cent.The RBI's September release showed infrastructure, basic metal and metal products, engineering, chemicals, food processing and textiles as segments showing buoyant year-on-year credit growth.Agriculture, personal loans maintain double-digit growthCredit to agriculture and allied activities grew 19.5 per cent year-on-year, significantly higher than the 8.9 per cent growth recorded in the corresponding period last year. Outstanding agricultural credit stood at around Rs 28.19 lakh crore. Advt Personal loans grew 17.3 per cent, compared with 11.5 per cent a year earlier. Housing and vehicle loans continued to register resilient growth, while advances against fixed deposits accelerated.At the same time, growth in loans against gold jewellery and education loans moderated compared with a year earlier, although growth in both segments remained strong.Outstanding loans against gold jewellery increased 82.6 per cent year-on-year to Rs 57,719 crore in September, sharply lower than the 124.8 per cent growth recorded in the corresponding period last year. On a financial-year basis, gold loan outstanding rose 25.1 per cent between March and September, against 52.7 per cent growth in the same period last year. Advt The RBI has also flagged a classification-related factor in this segment, noting that since May 2024, a bank has changed the classification of a category of agricultural loan into “loans against gold jewellery” under the retail segment.
