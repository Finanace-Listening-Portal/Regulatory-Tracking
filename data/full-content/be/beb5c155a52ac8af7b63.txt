Advt YES Bank share now fallen nearly 17 per cent over the two trading sessions since the Supreme Court verdict. YES Bank disclosed the impact on capital adequacy a day after the Supreme Court on October 15 dismissed appeals filed by the bank, the Reserve Bank of India and the Union government against the Bombay High Court’s January 20, 2023 judgment.The Bombay High Court had set aside the write-down of the AT1 bonds aggregating Rs 8,415 crore, which had been undertaken in March 2020 in connection with the reconstruction of YES Bank. The Supreme Court’s order has upheld that judgment.According to YES Bank’s disclosure, the proforma impact of reinstatement of the AT1 bond principal amount on the CET1 ratio, including profits, is expected to be around 250 bps as of June 30, 2026. Meanwhile, Kotak Mahindra Asset Management Company suspended fresh subscriptions in Kotak Medium Term Fund and Kotak Credit Risk Fund, which hold YES Bank AT1 bonds that had been valued at zero following their March 2020 write-off. The AMC said it had taken the step proactively to safeguard investors’ interests and is reviewing the Supreme Court judgment to determine the appropriate course of action. Supreme Court orderThe Supreme Court on October 15 dismissed the appeals challenging the Bombay High Court order that had set aside the write-down of the AT1 bonds. The court also vacated any interim order in the matter. Advt The court further directed that the annual coupon rate on the AT1 bonds would remain unaffected by the outcome of the appeals and would have to be paid in accordance with the respective Information Memoranda.The dispute dates back to March 2020, when YES Bank was placed under reconstruction and the administrator wrote down AT1 bonds aggregating Rs 8,415 crore. The Bombay High Court subsequently set aside the administrator’s decision, a ruling that has now been upheld by the Supreme Court.YES Bank said it is examining the Supreme Court judgment in detail, including evaluating available legal options and suitable actions going forward.On capital management, the bank said it has access to appropriate capital management levers and retains the flexibility to undertake additional capital actions, as considered appropriate, to pursue its growth objectives.
