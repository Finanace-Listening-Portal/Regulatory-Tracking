Articles 4 min read AI could reshape monetary policy, financial stability; central banks need global cyber cooperation: BIS chief AI is rapidly reshaping the global economy, with investment in data centres and related infrastructure surging while productivity gains from generative AI are already visible across several tasks, Pablo Hernández de Cos, General Manager, Bank for International Settlements (BIS), said at Global Fintech Fest 2026. However, the AI boom also brings risks for financial stability, including rising leverage, opaque financing structures and AI-enabled cyberattacks, making global cooperation among central banks increasingly important, he said. Advt Hernández de Cos said the BIS is particularly focused on understanding how AI could alter demand, supply and financial markets, while also creating new risks through leverage, interconnected financing structures and cybersecurity threats.AI to create new monetary policy challengesAI has already triggered a major investment boom in data centres, cloud infrastructure and hardware. Industry participants expect global AI-related investment to rise from around $500 billion currently to between $3 trillion and $14 trillion by 2030.Hernández de Cos said the increasing reliance on leverage and debt to finance this investment warranted attention from policymakers.The long-term impact of AI on the natural rate of interest could also differ depending on how the technology evolves. In a scenario where AI drives self-reinforcing productivity and growth, higher productivity could raise the marginal product of capital and the natural rate of interest, potentially requiring tighter monetary policy to contain inflation.In contrast, if AI shifts income away from labour and creates a shortfall in aggregate demand, weaker investment and growth could require easier monetary conditions.“The objectives of monetary policy remain unchanged. What changes is the environment in which this policy is conducted,” he said. Advt The BIS chief also cautioned that the scale and speed of the current AI investment boom, combined with the weight of expected returns attached to it, warrants close monitoring.Cybersecurity needs global cooperationCybersecurity is another major concern for central banks as AI adoption accelerates.AI can strengthen the ability of financial institutions to defend against cyberattacks, but can simultaneously give attackers greater capabilities. Hernández de Cos said the asymmetry was particularly important because a single weak link could result in a significant negative outcome.For central banks, the response therefore needs to involve faster adoption of AI alongside stronger cyber defences. Advt Importantly, he said cybersecurity risks cannot be viewed only from a domestic perspective because financial and payment systems are highly interconnected across jurisdictions.“Whatever concerns we might have in terms of the implications of AI on cybersecurity, we have to take them with a global perspective,” he said, calling for greater international cooperation among central banks.BIS flags debt, circular financing risksThe growing reliance on debt and opaque financing arrangements to fund AI infrastructure could also create financial stability risks, Hernández de Cos said.He pointed to “circular financing”, where chip manufacturers and hyperscalers take equity stakes in AI companies, which in turn commit to purchasing chips and computing capacity. Such arrangements create interconnected exposures that can be difficult to observe and value. If returns from AI investment disappoint, a pullback in investment could turn the current capital expenditure boom into a bust, he said. Advt Hernández de Cos compared the risks with earlier technology-driven investment booms, including the canal mania of the 1830s, British railway mania of the 1840s and the dot-com surge of the late 1990s.He, however, stressed that he was not suggesting the AI boom would necessarily end in a similar correction.Central banks already exploring AI use casesCentral banks can themselves deploy AI across statistics, economic analysis, payment-system oversight and supervision, Hernández de Cos said.AI can help compile new and unstructured data to complement traditional statistics and can also support economic and financial analysis through nowcasting and forecasting.Payment systems are another key use case, particularly in fraud detection, where AI can be deployed to identify and prevent fraudulent activity.Supervision could also benefit significantly, given the large volumes of information that banking and financial supervisors need to assess. AI could improve both the efficiency and effectiveness of supervisory activities, he said.The BIS Innovation Hub is working with the central banking and supervisory community to strengthen the adoption of AI in these areas.On India, Hernández de Cos said the country was “in the middle” in terms of AI readiness, with adoption dependent not only on its economic structure but also on policies aimed at strengthening digital infrastructure and human capital.“Policies can improve the way AI adoption accelerates in one economy,” he said.Click here to read our coverage of the Global FinTech Fest 2026.
