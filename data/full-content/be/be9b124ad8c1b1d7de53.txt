While Motor Finance continued to contract, strong momentum in SME, corporate and housing finance supported overall growth. Excluding Motor Finance, AUM rose 32% year on year to Rs 2.84 lakh crore.The divergence was also evident in profitability trends. Consolidated net interest margin (NIM) contracted around 10 basis points sequentially to 4.95% as borrowing costs rose, even as net interest income increased 23% year on year to around Rs 3,700 crore. Consolidated profit after tax, after non-controlling interests, grew 51% to Rs 1,660 crore. Other income rose 27% to around Rs 980 crore and included a gain of Rs 186 crore from the derecognition of associates, according to Motilal Oswal Financial Services. Advt SME lending was a key growth driver, with AUM increasing 34% year on year. Management attributed the expansion to term loans and particularly strong momentum in supply chain financing. Corporate lending grew faster, at 54%, while retail AUM increased 17%. Tata Capital Housing Finance’s AUM rose 26% year on year and 6.4% sequentially to Rs 95,100 crore as of September 2026.Motor finance remained the principal weak spot. Its net loan book declined 17% year on year and 3% sequentially to Rs 23,600 crore. Disbursements, however, increased to Rs 2,400 crore in the September quarter from Rs 2,000 crore in the preceding quarter. Gross Stage 3 assets in Motor Finance rose 80 basis points sequentially to 8.7% from 7.9%, even as credit costs, slippages and the cost-to-income ratio improved.Management said the Motor Finance recovery was running two to three quarters behind expectations. The portfolio run-off moderated during the quarter, and the book is expected to stabilise in Q3FY27 and return to growth from Q4, supported by expansion across markets and dealerships.The composition of new lending could also influence future profitability. Disbursements in high-margin products, including personal loans, business loans, microfinance, affordable housing, micro loan against property, two-wheeler finance and vehicle finance, grew 40% year on year in Q2FY27. However, AUM in these products rose only 14%. Management expects the outstanding portfolio to catch up with disbursement growth over the next few quarters, potentially supporting margins. Advt The pressure on margins reflected higher funding costs. Tata Capital’s calculated cost of borrowings rose around 15 basis points sequentially to 7.4%, while its calculated yield remained broadly stable at 11.2%. The resulting spread narrowed 15 basis points to 3.8%. Tata Capital and its housing finance subsidiary raised their prime lending rates by 25 basis points and 20 basis points, respectively, effective November 1, 2026.With 66% of assets and 48% of liabilities linked to floating rates, the company expects to be relatively well positioned to navigate further repo rate increases. Management expects NIM plus fees to remain stable or improve marginally in Q3FY27, followed by a stronger recovery in Q4, supported by pricing actions and a better product mix.Asset quality stable Advt Asset quality remained broadly stable at the consolidated level. Gross and net non-performing asset ratios stood at 1.9% and 0.8%, respectively, unchanged sequentially, while gross NPAs excluding Motor Finance declined around 10 basis points. Consolidated credit costs fell to about 0.85% on an annualised basis from 0.95% in the preceding quarter and 1.3% a year earlier.Tata Capital reiterated its medium-term AUM growth guidance of 23-25% and expressed confidence in meeting its FY28 guidance for growth, cost-to-income, credit costs, return on assets and return on equity. The brokerage estimates AUM and profit after tax to grow at compound annual rates of 24% and 34%, respectively, between FY26 and FY28, with return on assets of around 2.2% and return on equity of 15% in FY28. Advt Despite the growth outlook, the brokerage retained its Neutral rating on the stock with a target price of Rs 340, based on 2.2 times estimated September 2028 book value. It said the current valuation adequately reflects the company’s near-term growth and earnings potential.
