Articles 2 min read RBI exploring gold tokenisation, flags risks around faster asset movement: ED P Vasudevan The Reserve Bank of India is exploring gold tokenisation as a potential way to address challenges in the existing model, RBI Executive Director P Vasudevan said. He also flagged concerns around faster asset movement, leverage, interoperability and financial stability. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 9, 2026 at 07:36 PM IST var news_msid = 133972333; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India is exploring gold tokenisation as one of the potential applications of tokenisation in financial assets, RBI Executive Director P Vasudevan said at the Global FinTech Fest 2026.“There was some discussion yesterday, we were looking at gold tokenisation is one more area that is being explored,” Vasudevan said, adding that tokenisation could potentially address some of the challenges in the existing model.Vasudevan was speaking on the potential of tokenisation to transform the way financial assets are issued, traded and settled. He said tokenisation can allow assets to be divided or fractionalised while also carrying information on identity and rights attached to them. Advt One of the benefits, according to Vasudevan, is programmability. Smart contracts could allow certain actions to happen automatically based on predefined conditions. For instance, if the value of an asset used as collateral falls below a particular threshold, an additional collateral trigger could be built into the system. Restrictions on where an asset can be transferred could also be programmed.However, Vasudevan also flagged the need to assess the risks that could emerge as tokenised assets and money move significantly faster through digital platforms.“If that program can be so fast, then I can leverage the same assets multiple times a day,” he said, pointing to the possibility of faster collateral movement increasing leverage. “If we increase the leverage, how do we actually add in more issues of risk and stability? You have to be looking at that.”He also cautioned that faster movement of money could create another risk if there are insufficient points in the system to stop or control such movement. “If money moves so fast, and if you’re not able to stop it somewhere, then that again could be an element” of risk, Vasudevan said.Beyond leverage and financial stability, the RBI executive director highlighted issues around data certainty, privacy, data movement, interoperability and cross-border transactions as tokenisation develops.He said that interoperability would be particularly important if multiple platforms begin offering tokenised assets. “If there are two or three platforms, how do we actually make them interoperable?” he said, adding that interoperability has been one of the strengths of India’s digital and data ecosystem and should not be lost as tokenisation develops. Advt Vasudevan also said tokenisation does not necessarily mean the disappearance of intermediaries. Instead, their role could evolve, with new forms of intermediaries such as token service providers and “tokenisation as a service” emerging in the ecosystem.He sees central bank digital currency as another component that could support tokenised financial assets, particularly by allowing the money leg and asset leg of a transaction to move together on the same platform. This could help enable delivery versus payment without the traditional settlement gap.Vasudevan said there is a need to weigh the benefits of tokenisation against the risks that could emerge as the technology scales.
