The shift of NBFC liabilities towards bank borrowings from other sources was driven by the RBI's February 2025 risk-weight relaxation and 125bps of repo cuts till December 2025, which made bank borrowing relatively cheaper, according to JM Financial. Advt At the company level, several names including PFL, LIC HF, HDB, L&T Finance and SBFC raised their bank-loan share sharply during FY25-Q1FY27, mostly at the expense of NCDs, while Poonawalla and ABCAP pivoted into NCDs instead. MFI names show the highest bank-loan dependence of any cohort. CP mix fell for most, while DA/co-lending/securitisation mix stayed broadly flat.With bank-loan share of NBFC liabilities rising again, a rate hike would now feed faster into the overall cost of funds across a larger share of the book than before. Bank loans linked to repo/EBLR typically reprice more quickly, while CPs and NCDs are fixed-rate until each rollover, usually within 3-12 months for CPs and 3-5 years for NCDs.Yields across T-bill, G-sec, corporate and NBFC buckets have risen around 20-90bps since June 2026. One-year MCLR has also turned up by around 30bps since Q4FY26 even as the repo rate has remained flat in CY26. The spread of the 10-year G-sec over the repo rate is running well above its 10-year average, signalling that the market is already pricing in around 50bps of rate hike ahead.The liability skewThe cost of NCDs and bank borrowings is expected to rise in FY27/FY28. NCDs account for around 2-48% of borrowings across the coverage universe, while 10-60% of outstanding NCDs are coming up for repricing in the remainder of FY27/FY28. Bank borrowings account for around 20-75% of borrowings for most of the covered NBFCs. Advt The T-bill-linked portion of bank borrowings will start getting repriced immediately given rising yields. The impact of increasing MCLR should be visible with a lag sometime in 2HFY27/FY28, while repo-linked bank borrowings would get repriced immediately after a repo rate hike. Recent rating upgrades for some companies could provide some respite, with Shriram, Piramal and PNBHF positioned to refinance cheaper.Negative impact on FY28F cost of funds due to yield/rate increase would be around 25-45bps against a repo rate hike of around 50bps for various NBFCs. The quantum would depend upon funding mix, composition of bank borrowings, maturity profile of bond borrowings and repricing risk on those bond borrowings. Tata Cap, LTF, Poonawalla, Aavas, Fusion and HFFC have higher sensitivity to rate/yield increases given their higher share of NCDs/securitisation/assignment in borrowings, higher share of T-bill/repo-linked bank borrowing and/or higher share of maturing NCDs in FY27/FY28 with higher refinance risk. Advt The impact of a rate hike would depend more on the asset-side composition of NBFC/HFCs, with asset composition more important than liability composition. HFCs, except Aptus, have around 70-100% of their loan books floating and should face a positive impact from repricing. On the other hand, VF/MFIs/MSME/Gold lenders with around 60-100% of their loan books fixed would be immune from upward yield repricing in the near term.Among diversified names, ABCAP, Piramal and Tata Cap have around 70-72% of their loan books floating, while L&T Finance, HDB and Poonawalla have around 73-76% of their books fixed. BAF has a balanced loan book, with the share of fixed book at around 53%. Advt The sensitivity analysis shows that, ceteris paribus, a 50bps rate hike could have a minus 20bps to plus 10bps impact on RoA and a minus 8% to plus 7% impact on EPS of NBFC/HFCs. All HFCs, more so prime HFCs such as LIC HF, PNB HF and BHFL, appear to be the biggest beneficiaries in terms of positive impact on FY28E NIM/RoAs. Aadhar/HFFCs seem to be better placed than Aavas/Aptus.All VFs see negative NIM/RoA impact, although SHFL can minimise the impact due to the recent rating upgrade and equity infusion. All MFIs, especially CREDAG/Fusion, and gold and MSME lenders except SBFC should also see negative NIM/RoA impact due to the higher share of fixed/floating loan/liability book.Within diversified NBFCs, ABCAP/Piramal should see positive impact while LTF/HDB should see negative impact on NIM/RoA. BAF/Tata Cap should also see minor negative impact on their NIM/RoA, less due to loan mix but more due to higher negative impact on their cost of funds.
