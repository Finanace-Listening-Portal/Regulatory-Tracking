Articles 3 min read RBI assessing risks at systemic level before making final call on revolving credit: DG Murmu The Reserve Bank of India is assessing the risks of allowing revolving credit at both individual NBFC and systemic levels before taking a final call on its proposal to restrict such facilities, Deputy Governor S C Murmu told ETBFSI. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 18, 2026 at 06:39 PM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 134336577; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India is assessing the risks of allowing revolving credit at both individual NBFC and systemic levels before taking a final call on its proposal to restrict such facilities, Deputy Governor S C Murmu told ETBFSI.“The issue is still open because we have received lots of feedback and we have not taken a final call yet in what form it will come. This is still an open question,” Murmu said.He said the central bank was examining the risks associated with allowing revolving credit not only for individual entities but also from a systemic perspective. “I will not be able to give you what is the probability that the revolving credit will be allowed or not. But one thing I can assure you is each and every feedback has been taken into account and also if possible, we will be also discussing with some of the participants to understand it better,” Murmu said. Advt “Because one thing what we are trying to do through this is basically we are looking at what are the risks if it is allowed, not only on the individual entity but also at systemic level,” he added.Indian Central Bank on August 6 proposed amendments to its NBFC credit facility directions that would restrict NBFCs to offering term loans and bar them from offering revolving credit products. Under the draft, a term loan is a fixed principal amount with a predetermined repayment schedule, while a revolving facility allows a borrower to draw, repay and redraw against a sanctioned limit. NBFCs authorised to issue credit cards were exempted from the proposed restriction.The proposal formalised a concern that the RBI had been communicating to NBFCs for nearly two years, with lenders and industry participants telling ETBFSI that the central bank had previously indicated that NBFCs should not offer such revolving facilities.RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra subsequently also said the draft was not intended as a new regulatory direction, stating that “it was never the regulatory intent to allow NBFCs to offer such products” and that the draft reiterated the central bank’s position. At the same time, he said the RBI would consider the feedback received from stakeholders and weigh the pros and cons before taking an appropriate view. Advt The industry has raised concerns that a blanket restriction could affect products beyond conventional flexi-loans, including supply-chain finance, working-capital facilities and other products where borrowers have the ability to draw against a sanctioned limit.FIDC, the RBI-recognised self-regulatory organisation for NBFCs, subsequently submitted feedback on the draft. The industry body sought changes to the proposed framework, including allowing the principal repaid ahead of schedule to be restored or replenished subject to safeguards.The RBI has also engaged directly with the industry on the proposal. Murmu held a meeting with NBFCs and FIDC earlier this month, where lenders shared their concerns over the proposed blanket restriction. The discussions included the impact on a wider set of lenders and products than initially expected, with supply-chain finance emerging as one of the areas where the industry has sought clarity.
