Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); State of Bihar Vs Phulpari Kumari (Supreme Court of India) Gold Within CBDT Limits Is No Shield Against Disproportionate Assets Prosecution: Supreme Court Possession of Gold and Explanation of Its Source Are Different Matters The Supreme Court has held that reliance on CBDT instructions concerning possession of gold does not, by itself, explain its source in a disproportionate assets prosecution. Merely showing that the quantity of gold falls within the limits mentioned in a circular or press release cannot justify quashing proceedings under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988. A Bench comprising Justice K. V. Viswanathan and Justice Arun Palli accordingly set aside the Patna High Court’s order quashing the prosecution against Phulpari Kumari. The Supreme Court restored the Special Judge’s order taking cognizance and directed that the case proceed from that stage. The decision draws a significant distinction between reliance on gold-holding limits and accounting for the acquisition of that gold. Background of the Case An FIR was registered on 11 May 2015 at the Vigilance Police Station, Patna, against the respondent and her husband concerning allegations of possession of assets disproportionate to their known sources of income. The offences invoked were under Section 13(1)(e), read with Section 13(2), of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988. After obtaining sanction, the investigating agency filed a chargesheet on 29 July 2020, only against the respondent. The Special Judge, Vigilance, Patna, took cognizance on 28 January 2021. The respondent challenged that order before the Patna High Court through Criminal Miscellaneous No. 56839 of 2021. Her principal argument rested on CBDT Instruction No. 1916 dated 11 May 1994, together with the Ministry of Finance’s press release dated 1 December 2016. Defence Based on the Gold Limits The respondent relied upon the quantities commonly associated with the CBDT instruction: 500 grams for a married woman, 250 grams for an unmarried woman and 100 grams for a male member. It was argued that, since possession of gold within those quantities was permitted, prosecution under the Prevention of Corruption Act could not be maintained on that basis. The State opposed this argument. Its contention was that the circulars did not answer the question whether the assets were disproportionate to known sources of income. Possessing gold and satisfactorily accounting for its source were separate issues. The High Court nevertheless accepted the respondent’s contention. It proceeded on the basis that the gold did not exceed 750 grams, which it treated as the permissible limit applicable to her. It consequently held that the Vigilance Department had erroneously seized the gold and erred in calculating the disproportionate assets. On that reasoning, the prosecution was quashed. Supreme Court Rejects the High Court’s Reasoning The Supreme Court described the High Court’s reasoning as “totally untenable.” It explained that a prosecution under the provisions invoked concerns allegations that the accused owns assets disproportionate to known sources of income. In that context, reference to a circular or press release concerning possession of gold does not itself account for the assets. The central principle is therefore clear: a quantity-based argument cannot substitute for an explanation of the source of acquisition. Even where an accused relies on the quantities mentioned in the CBDT material, the question whether the gold forms part of disproportionate assets requires examination under the applicable criminal law. The High Court could not treat those quantities as an automatic answer to the prosecution’s allegations. The Supreme Court thus rejected the use of the CBDT material as a sufficient basis, by itself, for terminating the proceedings. Prosecution Restored; Guilt Not Decided The Supreme Court allowed the State’s appeal and restored the order of cognizance dated 28 January 2021 in Special Case No. 20 of 2015, arising from Vigilance P.S. Case No. 37 of 2015. The judgment does not convict the respondent or conclusively establish that the gold represented disproportionate assets. Its immediate effect is to revive the prosecution so that the matter can proceed according to law. The respondent’s counsel submitted that other circumstances would demonstrate that the assets were not disproportionate to known sources of income. The Court preserved her liberty to initiate proceedings available in law before the Trial Court. It expressly directed that such proceedings be decided uninfluenced by the observations in either the High Court’s order or the Supreme Court’s present order. Author’s Comments The decision cautions against treating the familiar 500/250/100-gram quantities as a universal legal immunity. A person relying on administrative instructions must consider the particular issue for which those instructions are being invoked. Whether jewellery attracts seizure, whether its acquisition is satisfactorily explained, and whether it constitutes disproportionate assets are distinct questions. An answer to one does not automatically resolve the others. Equally, this short order should not be overstated as deciding every controversy concerning jewellery under income-tax law. The issue before the Supreme Court was specifically whether reliance on the CBDT material justified quashing a disproportionate assets prosecution. The ruling’s essential message is that gold within the cited limits does not, merely for that reason, become explained gold for purposes of the Prevention of Corruption Act. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER 1. Leave granted. 2. Heard Mr. Akshay Amritanshu, learned counsel for the appellant-State of Bihar and Mr. Ranjit Kumar, learned counsel for the respondent. 3. The present appeal calls in question the correctness of the order dated 14th May, 2026 passed by the High Court of Judicature at Patna in Criminal Miscellaneous No.56839 of 2021. By the said order, the High Court has quashed the order of cognizance dated 28th January, 2021 passed by the learned Special Judge, Vigilance, Patna in connection with Special Case No. 20 of 2015 arising out of Vigilance P.S. Case No. 37 of 2015 registered for the offences under Sections 13(2) read with Section 13(1)(e) of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (for short, “the Act’). 4. On 11th May, 2015, a First Information Report was registered at the Vigilance Police Station, Patna, in P.S. Case No. 37 of 2015 against the respondent under Section 13(1)(e) read with Section 13(2) of the Act as well as her husband for owning assets disproportionate to known sources of income. After obtaining sanction, on 29th July, 2020, a chargesheet was filed only against the respondent for the said offences. 5. On 28th January, 2021, the Court of Special Judge, Vigilance, Patna took the cognizance against the respondent. The respondent challenged the said order by filing Criminal Miscellaneous Petition No. 56839 of 2021, for quashing the order of cognizance. The point argued before the High Court was that under Instruction No.1916 dated 11th May, 1994and Press Release issued by the Ministry of Finance, Department of Revenue, Central Board of Direct Taxes dated 1st December, 2016, a married woman is entitled to possess 500 grams of gold, an unmarried lady is entitled to possess 250 grams of gold and a male is entitled to possess 100 grams of gold. It was argued that since the respondent is entitled to possess gold, there could be no prosecution under the Act. 6. The appellant-State vehemently opposed the said submission and contended that those circulars have no application for determining assets disproportionate to known sources of income and for carrying on prosecution under the Act. The High Court accepted the stand of the respondent and in paragraph 6 of the impugned order held as under: “6. In the present case, if the gold is calculated with reference to the aforesaid Circular, the petitioner does not possess more than 750 Grams which is the permissible limit of petitioner. Thus, the Vigilance Department has seized the gold erroneously and has committee error in calculating the weight of the gold jewellery of the petitioner and determining the proportionate assets of the petitioner.” The prosecution against the respondent was quashed. 7. Mr. Akshay Amritanshu, learned counsel for the appellant-State submits that entitlement to possess gold is one thing, accounting for it when called upon in a prosecution under Section 13(1)(e) read with Section 13(2) of the Act, is a wholly different matter. 8. Learned counsel for the respondent defended the impugned order and submits that there are other aspects which will show that the assets are not disproportionate to the known source of income. 9. Having heard learned counsel for the parties and perused the records, we find that the reasoning of the High Court is totally untenable. In a prosecution under Section 13(1)(e) read with Section 13(2) of the Act, the accused is called upon to explain allegations with regard to owning assets disproportionate to known sources of income. Merely showing certain press release and circulars permitting the holding of gold to a certain extent will not amount to accounting for the possession of the said assets. 10. In view of the above, we set aside the impugned order of the High Court and restore to file the order of cognizance dated 28th January, 2021 taken by the Court of Special Judge, Vigilance, Patna in Special Case No. 20 of 2015, arising from P.S. Case No. 37 of 2015. The case shall proceed from that stage. 11. At this stage, Mr. Ranjit Kumar, learned counsel for the respondent submits that there are other aspects which the respondent would like to argue. 12. The respondent is at liberty to initiate such proceedings as are available in law before the Trial Court, and needless to say that if those proceedings are resorted to, they will be decided uninfluenced by any of the observations in the order of the High Court or in the present order. 13. The Appeal is allowed in the above terms. 1. Leave granted. 2. The appeal is allowed in terms of the signed order. 3. Pending application shall stand disposed of.
