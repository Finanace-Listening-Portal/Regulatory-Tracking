(Karnataka High Court) Summary: The Karnataka High Court, Dharwad Bench, in M/s G R Enterprises Vs State of Karnataka & Ors., considered a writ petition arising from rejection of an appeal under Section 107 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 on limitation grounds. The petitioner, a proprietorship concern registered under the CGST Act and the Karnataka GST Act, had received a show-cause notice proposing cancellation of its GST registration. As the competent authority was dissatisfied with the petitioner’s reply, cancellation followed. The petitioner challenged the appellate order dated 18 April 2024 and the underlying order dated 16 March 2024, and sought a direction requiring the Appellate Authority to adjudicate the appeal on merits. The Court noted that a comparable question had already been considered in M/s R M Chougule and Associated Vs Assistant Commissioner of Commercial Tax & Ors., Writ Petition No. 101618 of 2025, decided on 29 October 2025. In that earlier decision, the Court had permitted an appeal against cancellation of registration to be filed within four weeks of receipt of the order and directed the Appellate Authority to decide it on merits without rejecting it on limitation grounds. The related approach to delayed GST appeals is discussed in Limitation Not a Ground to Reject GST Registration Cancellation Appeal: Karnataka HC. Justice M. Nagaprasanna reproduced the operative reasoning and directions from the earlier judgment and applied that decision to the present case. The High Court accordingly set aside the impugned appellate order, thereby requiring the Appellate Authority to consider the appeal on merits. The Court expressly left all other contentions open to be raised before the Appellate Authority and did not adjudicate the validity of the cancellation itself. The judgment is therefore concerned with restoration of the opportunity for appellate examination, rather than automatic reinstatement of GST registration. The cited earlier decision also referred to Jai Hind Enterprises Vs Assistant Commissioner of Commercial Taxes & Ors., W.P. No. 107549 of 2024, decided on 21 January 2025, and the Division Bench ruling in Yallappa Patil Vs Assistant Commissioner & Ors., W.A. No. 100608 of 2025. These decisions form the judicial background to the Court’s approach to delayed appeals. The importance of the present order is further illustrated by the subsequent proceedings involving the same taxpayer, reported in G.R. Enterprises Vs Commissioner of Commercial Taxes (GSTAT Bengaluru), where the Tribunal subsequently enforced the High Court’s direction for a merits hearing. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT 1. The petitioner is before this Court seeking the following prayer: a. “Issue Writ of Certiorari or quashing the impugned order bearing Appeal No.KGST/AP-01/2024-25, dated 18/04/2024, passed by respondent No.3 vide ANNEXURE – F, to the petition. b. Issue Writ of Certiorari or quashing the impugned order bearing No. Sankya.va.te.s.aa./LGSTO/Ho/2023-24/T.No.1954 and reference No.ZA2903240709390, dated 16/03/2024 passed by Respondent No.4 vide ANNEXURE- E, to the petition. c. Issue writ of Mandamus or directing the Respondent No. 3 to pass the order on merits of the case, in appeal filed by the petitioner. d. Pass such other Order or further Orders as this Hon’ble Court may deems fit in the facts and circumstances of this case, in the interest of justice and equity.” 2. Heard the learned counsel Sri.H.R.Kambiyavar, appearing for petitioner, learned AGA-Sri.T.Hanumareddy, appearing for respondents Nos.1, 3 and 4 and learned counsel Sri.M.B.Kanavi, appearing for respondent No.2. 3. The petitioner is a proprietorship concern registered under the Provisions of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the CGST Act’ for short) and the Karnataka Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the KGST Act’ for short). The issue relates to the issuance of a show-cause notice as to why the registration of the petitioner should not be cancelled. The cancellation ensued upon the authority not being satisfied with the petitioner’s reply. The petitioner then seeks to file an appeal invoking Section 107 of the CGST Act, which was rejected on the ground of limitation. 4. In identical circumstances, this Court has permitted appeals to be filed despite the delay in Writ Petition No.101618 of 2025 decided on 29.10.2025, wherein this Court has held as follows. 1. “Petitioner is before this Court seeking the following prayer: a. “Issue Writ of Certiorari or in the like nature of certiorari quashing the impugned order bearing no. Appeal No. GST-227/2024-25/B-491, dated 30/09/2024, passed by respondent no. 2. vide ANNEXURE-C, to the petition. b. Consequently or in the like nature of certiorari quashing the impugned order bearing no. ACCT/A-5/RECTIFICATION/2023-24 dated 21.11.2023, passed by respondent no. 1 vide ANNEXURE-A. c. Pass such other order of further orders as this Hon’ble Court may deems fit in the facts and circumstances of this case, in the interest of justice and equity.” 2. The learned counsel appearing for the petitioner submits that the petitioner notwithstanding the fact of payment of complete tax, the assessment is drawn against him, against which an appeal is preferred and the appeal is rejected on the sole ground of delay. 3. The learned counsel submits that the issue in lis stands answered by the judgment rendered by this Court in Writ Petition No.107549/2024 disposed of on 21.01.2025. 4. In the light of the issue standing answered by this Court and that of the Division Bench in Writ Appeal No.100608/2025, the petition deserves to succeed in the same direction that is issued while disposing of Writ Petition No.107549 of 2024. 5. For the aforesaid reasons, the following: ORDER i. Writ petition is allowed in part. ii. The petitioner is permitted to file an appeal against the cancellation of registration before the Appellate Authority within four weeks from the date of the receipt of this order. iii. In the event, the appeal is preferred within four weeks as aforesaid, it shall be considered on its merit and not reject the appeal on the ground of limitation. iv. In the event, the petitioner would not prefer an appeal within four weeks as permitted above, the benefit of the order rendered in the subject petition would not be available to the petitioner. Ordered accordingly.” 5. In the light of the Appellate Authority being directed to consider the appeal on its merits, the impugned order of the Appellate Authority is hereby set aside. 6. All other contentions are left open to be urged before the Appellate Authority.
