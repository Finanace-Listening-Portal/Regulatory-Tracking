Summary: Kotak Life’s e-Term is a pure-risk individual life insurance product offered in Life, Life Plus and Life Secure options. The official current product page identifies UIN 107N129V04 and describes alternative lump-sum and recurring death-benefit payout structures. Life Plus adds a conditional accidental death benefit, whereas Life Secure offers waiver of future premiums for qualifying total and permanent disability. Optional riders and the advertised special exit value are subject to their own eligibility and exclusions. The central purchase decision is whether the selected cover, term and premium schedule match a household’s protection need. This guide distinguishes the option-specific benefits, identifies disclosure and claims documentation controls, and explains why income-tax deductions and GST treatment should be checked against current law rather than assumed. It also flags channel restrictions, the risk of relying on an older brochure, and the need to verify the final policy schedule. The insurer’s current product page identifies UIN 107N129V04. The UIN matters because product brochures from earlier versions can still circulate online. A prospective policyholder should obtain the proposal illustration, latest approved wording and rider schedule for the exact UIN quoted, and reconcile these with the final policy schedule. Term insurance is primarily intended to replace income and support dependants following the insured person’s death during the covered period. It is not equivalent to a savings deposit. The coverage amount should be linked to outstanding loans, household expenses, education commitments, existing assets and the years for which dependants require support. Premium affordability over the entire chosen payment period is more important than an attractive introductory illustration. Life, Life Plus and Life Secure Options Under the Life option, the stated death benefit is 100% of the sum assured on death, subject to policy terms. Life Plus adds an accidental death benefit, subject to the insurer’s stated maximum of ₹1 crore for that additional benefit. Life Secure instead includes waiver of future outstanding premiums when the insured becomes totally and permanently disabled as defined in the contract. These benefits are not interchangeable: accidental death cover is not a general disability income benefit, and premium waiver is not a separate lump-sum disability payout. A customer should compare the exact definitions of accident, total and permanent disability, survival periods, exclusions and claim evidence. A benefit may depend on the chosen option being active at the time of the insured event. The Life Secure option is not available with single-premium payment, according to the product information. Do not assume a rider or optional benefit is automatically included merely because the marketing page mentions it. Immediate Versus Recurring Claim Payout The Immediate payout is described as a lump sum equal to 100% of the sum assured on death. The Level Recurring structure is described as 10% initially and 6% annually for 15 years. The Increasing Recurring structure begins with 10% initially and an annual payout starting at 6% of the sum assured, increasing by 10% per year for 15 years. These structures change the timing and nature of the family’s cash receipts, and should not be compared merely by adding undiscounted nominal instalments. A lump sum may be appropriate for immediate debt settlement and flexibility, but places investment and spending decisions on the nominee. Recurring payments can support regular household budgets but may be less adaptable to emergency expenses and inflation. Confirm whether the selected option can be changed, how claims are processed, and how payments to minors or multiple beneficiaries are handled under the governing wording. Eligibility, Premiums and Cover Selection The product page indicates a minimum entry age of 18 and a general maximum of 65, with a lower maximum for certain pay-till-60 configurations. The stated minimum basic sum assured is ₹51 lakh, while the maximum is subject to underwriting. Maturity age, policy term and premium payment term depend on the selected option and payment structure. Applicants must therefore use an individual benefit illustration instead of assuming one age limit or price applies universally. Premiums can vary with age, health, tobacco use, occupation, lifestyle, coverage amount, term, payment frequency and underwriting. The insurer describes regular, limited and single-pay structures, subject to option restrictions. Paying for a shorter period may increase instalments even if the total schedule differs. Compare total contractual outflow, not just the monthly instalment, and check how grace periods, lapse and revival work. Riders, Exit Value and Claims Documentation The insurer lists a Critical Illness Plus Benefit Rider covering specified critical illnesses subject to definitions, and a Permanent Disability Benefit Rider with stated payments equal to 120% of the rider sum assured over five years. The latter is subject to channel and option restrictions; the insurer says it is unavailable with Life Secure and through the online channel. A rider is a separate contractual promise with its own triggers, exclusions, premium and termination provisions. The advertised special exit value at age 60 is conditional rather than an unconditional return-of-premium promise. Check the eligibility window, minimum term, premium status and exclusions in the latest wording. For death claims, nominees should preserve the policy schedule, identification, death certificate, medical records where relevant and insurer correspondence. Prompt notification, accurate original proposal disclosures and consistent beneficiary details reduce avoidable disputes. Never conceal material medical or lifestyle information to obtain a lower premium. Tax and GST Position for Indian Policyholders The insurer states that GST is exempt on individual life insurance premiums from 22 September 2025. This should not be extended automatically to group insurance or unrelated products. For income-tax treatment, distinguish premium deduction eligibility from the treatment of claim proceeds, and identify whether the applicable law is the Income-tax Act, 1961 or the Income-tax Act, 2025 for the relevant period. The actual deduction can depend on the tax regime and statutory conditions; it is not appropriate to promise a deduction to every policyholder. Death benefits and other receipts may have different statutory treatment depending on their character and the policy terms. Tax advisers should examine the beneficiary, payment type, premium-to-cover ratio, policy date and applicable law. Keep premium receipts and insurer certificates, and avoid using a generic tax calculator as a substitute for the actual statutory provisions. Frequently Asked Questions Is Kotak e-Term an investment plan? No. It is described as an individual pure-risk life policy; any special exit value is conditional and should not be treated as a guaranteed investment return. Does Life Plus cover every accidental injury? No. Its additional benefit relates to qualifying accidental death and is governed by contractual definitions and exclusions. Does Life Secure pay a disability lump sum? Its stated core additional feature is waiver of future outstanding premiums upon qualifying total and permanent disability, not an automatic lump-sum payout. Can an online customer add the permanent disability rider? The insurer states that this rider is unavailable through the online channel; confirm the latest sales-channel rules. Can nominees choose between lump sum and instalments later? The payout basis is selected under the policy; confirm the applicable change provisions rather than assume post-claim flexibility. Are premiums always tax-deductible? No. Eligibility depends on the relevant law, tax regime and statutory conditions. Is the policy GST-free? The insurer describes exemption for individual life premiums from 22 September 2025; verify the exact product and period. Why check the UIN?
