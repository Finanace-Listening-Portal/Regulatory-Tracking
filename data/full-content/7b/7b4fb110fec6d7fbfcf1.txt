Madhusudhan Reddy (Supreme Court of India) Property Rights Surrendered Under a Court-Approved Family Settlement Cannot Be Revived Through a Subsequent Gift Deed Issue Before the Court Whether a person who had relinquished his claim over specified family properties under a compromise recognised by a High Court decree could subsequently gift those properties to another family member. The Supreme Court held that the subsequent gift could not stand against the earlier family settlement and court-approved compromise. It set aside the judgments of both courts below and decreed the suit seeking declaration of title, invalidation of the gift deed and consequential relief. Background of the Family Dispute The litigation originated in a family settlement dated 19 April 1959, concerning ancestral properties left behind by B. Venkat Reddy. Following the deaths of Venkat Reddy and his eldest son, Sudhakar Reddy, the settlement was entered into by Venkat Reddy’s widow, Manikyamma, Sudhakar Reddy’s widow, Anasuyamma, and the surviving sons, Madhusudhan Reddy and Janardhan Reddy. The settlement separately allocated substantial agricultural lands among them. Manikyamma received 53 acres and 10 guntas in Karkalpahad Village. Madhusudhan Reddy received only 1 acre and 30 guntas in Survey No. 15 in that village, besides lands elsewhere. Anasuyamma subsequently adopted Venkatraji Reddy in 1975. She and her adopted son instituted a partition suit in 1977. Earlier Partition Proceedings Recognised the Family Settlement By its judgment dated 21 April 1984, the Trial Court partly decreed the partition suit concerning certain house properties. However, it refused partition of the Schedule A properties because those properties were already covered by the 1959 family settlement. During the ensuing appeal, Anasuyamma, her adopted son and Madhusudhan Reddy entered into a further settlement dated 24 February 1986. Under that arrangement, Anasuyamma and Venkatraji Reddy relinquished their claim to a one-third share in the Himayatnagar house property, which Madhusudhan Reddy obtained absolutely. They also gave up their claim to a share in another house property. In return, Madhusudhan Reddy gave up his claim to a share in Manikyamma’s properties, surrendering those rights to Anasuyamma and Venkatraji Reddy. He also paid them ₹2.20 lakh. The compromise was recorded in the appellate proceedings, and the High Court disposed of the appeal in its light on 7 September 1990. Subsequent Gift Included Lands Outside the Donor’s Entitlement Despite that settlement, Madhusudhan Reddy executed a registered gift deed dated 24 April 2007 in favour of Siddhartha Reddy, Janardhan Reddy’s grandson. The gifted lands included properties in Karkalpahad Village which had not been allotted to Madhusudhan Reddy under the 1959 settlement. They belonged to the allocation made to Manikyamma, over which Madhusudhan Reddy had expressly surrendered his claim under the subsequent compromise. Anasuyamma, Venkatraji Reddy and a purchaser of part of the property therefore instituted a suit seeking declaration of title, a declaration that the gift deed was null and void, mutation of revenue records and permanent injunction. Trial Court Wrongly Disregarded the Compromise Decree The Trial Court dismissed the suit in April 2012. In doing so, it treated the 1990 compromise decree as having no evidentiary value. The Supreme Court expressed clear disapproval of this approach. By ignoring the compromise, the Trial Court failed to account for the very arrangement under which Madhusudhan Reddy had relinquished his claim over his mother’s properties while obtaining exclusive rights in the Himayatnagar house. The rights asserted through the later gift could not be examined by simply overlooking that binding history. High Court Failed to Examine the Foundation of the Parties’ Rights The Telangana High Court affirmed dismissal of the suit in December 2022. The Supreme Court found that, although the High Court extensively narrated the chronology and submissions, it failed to draw the correct conclusions from the 1959 settlement and the 1986 compromise. The High Court also observed that execution of the gift deed had never been disputed. The Supreme Court pointed out that the suit itself had been instituted challenging that gift deed. Further, the High Court’s reasoning concerning Manikyamma’s entitlement and death ignored both the earlier allocation of properties to her and Madhusudhan Reddy’s subsequent relinquishment. These were fundamental errors affecting the foundation of the decision. Decision The Supreme Court allowed the appeal, set aside the Trial Court and High Court judgments, and directed that the suit stand decreed as prayed for. Thus, the appellants obtained the declaratory and consequential relief sought against the subsequent gift deed. No costs were awarded. Author’s Comments The judgment demonstrates the importance of tracing title through the entire chain of family arrangements and judicial decrees. A registered gift deed cannot supply an entitlement which the donor never possessed or had already relinquished. It also highlights that a family compromise must be read as an integrated arrangement. A party cannot retain the property secured through the settlement while later dealing with properties over which the same settlement required surrender of his claim. Courts examining a subsequent transfer must first identify what rights remained with the transferor after the earlier settlement and decree. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT OF THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA 1. By judgment dated 09.04.2012, the learned Senior Civil Judge, Mahabubnagar, dismissed the suit. Surprisingly, the learned Judge opined therein that the compromise decree dated 07.09.1990 in CCCA No. 86 of 1984 had no evidentiary value. Holding so, the learned Judge completely ignored the compromise by Madhusudhan Reddy with Anasuyamma and Venkatraji Reddy and proceeded to hold against them. 12. Aggrieved by the dismissal of their suit, Venkatraji Reddy and Gunna Malinidevi filed Appeal Suit No. 372 of 2012 before the Telangana High Court at Hyderabad, which was dismissed by judgment dated 12.12.2022. Surprisingly, the learned Judge of the High Court, having set out at great length the chronology of events and the arguments advanced by the learned counsel, failed to draw the correct inferences from the past history, in terms of the family settlement of 1959 and the compromise of 1986, which had the seal of approval of the High Court, vide the judgment dated 07.09.1990 in CCCA No. 86 of 1994. Non-application of mind by the learned Judge is patent from the observation that ‘execution of the Gift Deed dated 24.04.2007 was never disputed’. In saying so, the learned Judge lost sight of the fact that O.S. No. 250 of 2007 was filed on 21.08.2007 challenging the said gift deed. Compounding her error, the learned Judge then observed that Manikyamma died during the pendency of the suit and was not entitled to any share and, therefore, the claim of Venkatraji Reddy that he was in enjoyment of the property allotted to her had no merit. This finding was based on the failure of the learned Judge to note the contents of the family settlement dated 19.04.1959, which was accepted by Madhusudan Reddy also, and the compromise dated 24.02.1986, whereby Madhusudhan Reddy gave up his claim for a share in the properties allotted to Manikyamma in the family settlement of 1959 and secured full rights in the house property at Himayatnagar, Hyderabad. 13. Founded on such fundamental errors, the judgments of the High Court and the Trial Court cannot be sustained. Notably, despite service of notice, none of the respondents in this appeal chose to appear. Significantly, by order dated 16.05.2023, this Court had directed status quo obtaining as on that date, with regard to the nature and possession over the lands in dispute, to be maintained. Despite such an order being passed against them, none of the respondents chose to come forward to contest the appeal. Their failure to do so speaks for itself. 14. The appeal is, accordingly, allowed setting aside the judgment and decree dated 12.12.2022 in AS No. 372 of 2012 and the judgment and decree dated 09.04.2012 in O.S. No. 250 of 2007.
