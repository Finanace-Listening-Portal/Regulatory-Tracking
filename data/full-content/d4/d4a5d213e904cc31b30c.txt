Articles 5 min read Banks face rising funding costs as credit growth outpaces deposits Banks are adding loans faster than deposits, pushing the incremental credit-deposit ratio to 90.1 per cent and raising the cost of mobilising fresh funds. The highest rate on certificate of deposit issuances has climbed to 8.04 per cent, while the weighted average call rate has risen to 5.69 per cent, pointing to increasing pressure on banks to finance credit expansion. The gap between year-on-year credit and deposit growth widened to 268 basis points in the fortnight ended October 31, 2026, while the incremental credit-deposit ratio rose to 90.1 per cent in FY27 so far. This means banks have deployed about Rs 90 of every Rs 100 of additional deposits mobilised during the financial year towards lending.The pressure is also visible in the cost of short-term borrowings. The highest rate on certificates of deposit (CDs) issued during the fortnight rose to 8.04 per cent from 6.92 per cent in the previous fortnight. Meanwhile, the weighted average call rate increased 13 basis points to 5.69 per cent, placing it 19 basis points above the 5.50 per cent policy repo rate. These indicators point to higher marginal costs for banks raising funds through money market instruments, although they do not establish the average cost of funds across the banking system. Advt Bank credit grew 19.5 per cent year on year to Rs 231.8 lakh crore as of October 31, compared with 18.2 per cent in the previous fortnight. Deposits expanded at a slower 16.9 per cent to Rs 282.5 lakh crore, marginally higher than the 16.7 per cent recorded on October 15. The difference between the two growth rates widened to 268 basis points from 151 basis points in the previous fortnight.The incremental credit-deposit ratio, which measures the proportion of additional deposits deployed as credit over a period, rose to 90.1 per cent in FY27 so far from 84 per cent as of October 15. The overall credit-deposit ratio also increased to 82.1 per cent from 81.6 per cent.The fortnightly movements underline the imbalance. Credit increased by Rs 4.40 lakh crore during the fortnight, while deposits rose by Rs 3.75 lakh crore. More significantly, across the two fortnights since September 30, credit increased by Rs 1.88 lakh crore even as deposits remained Rs 1.45 lakh crore below their September-end level. The latest fortnight's deposit increase followed a quarter-end decline of Rs 5.19 lakh crore.Fund raising via CDsBanks also raised more funds through CDs during the fortnight, with issuances increasing to Rs 52,000 crore from Rs 46,000 crore previously. The highest issuance rate rose to 8.04 per cent from 6.92 per cent. The spread between the lowest and highest CD rates widened to 254 basis points from 142 basis points. Outstanding CDs increased by Rs 8,000 crore to Rs 5.94 lakh crore. The increase in the highest issuance rate points to higher marginal borrowing costs for banks tapping this market, although it does not represent the average rate paid on all CD issuances. Advt The rise in the weighted average call rate to 5.69 per cent from 5.56 per cent also points to an increase in short-term money market borrowing costs. The rate was 19 basis points above the 5.50 per cent policy repo rate. Meanwhile, the banking system's net liquidity absorption by the Reserve Bank of India declined to Rs 1.63 lakh crore on October 31 from Rs 1.86 lakh crore on October 15 and Rs 4.86 lakh crore on September 30. Although the system remained in surplus, the decline indicates a reduction in the liquidity cushion available to banks.The funding pressure comes even as banks have been adding credit at a faster pace than in the previous year. Credit has increased by Rs 18.23 lakh crore in FY27 so far, against Rs 11.50 lakh crore during the corresponding period last year. However, the headline year-on-year growth rate also reflects a relatively low base, with credit having grown 11.3 per cent in the year-ago period. On a two-year basis, credit growth was 33 per cent, translating into an annualised rate of about 15.3 per cent. Advt The increase in funding costs could have implications for banks' margins if deposit rates and market borrowing costs rise faster than lending yields. The 25-basis-point repo rate increase on October 7 is expected to feed into external benchmark-linked loans first, followed by loans linked to the marginal cost of funds-based lending rate as banks' funding costs adjust. A single increase of this magnitude is unlikely to materially restrain credit demand, but sustained increases in lending rates could eventually weigh on borrowing appetite.For banks, the challenge is not simply to sustain loan growth but to finance it without putting pressure on margins. The widening credit-deposit growth gap, higher incremental credit-deposit ratio and increase in the highest CD issuance rate point to a rising cost of mobilising incremental funds. The extent to which this affects net interest margins will depend on how banks price fresh deposits and market borrowings, and how much of the increase in funding costs they can pass on to borrowers through lending rates.
