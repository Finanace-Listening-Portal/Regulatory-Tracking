Summary: State Bank of India is an Indian banking institution and an RBI-authorised dealer through which eligible customers may undertake permissible foreign-exchange transactions. Its retail outward-remittance channels include YONO SBI FxOUT and branch processing. SBI currently states that a retail customer can use YONO to remit up to USD 40,000 or equivalent per transaction per day in eight listed currencies, provided the account is KYC-compliant and PAN is updated. That channel limit is not the same as the Liberalised Remittance Scheme ceiling, and neither figure is permission for a prohibited purpose. This profile explains the legal entity and authorisation boundary, who may use the service, purposes and currencies, documents, exchange-rate and fee components, settlement and return risks, grievance routes, GST and current TCS treatment. It also distinguishes an ordinary permitted remittance from overseas margin or leveraged forex funding, which LRS does not permit. Charges and exchange rates can change and must be confirmed from SBI’s live quote and applicable schedule before authorisation. The article is factual, not an endorsement or a representation that every remittance will be accepted. RBI’s public information identifies authorised dealers as persons specifically authorised under section 10(1) of the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, and lists State Bank of India among banks authorised to deal in foreign exchange. SBI therefore acts as the authorised institution executing the remittance; it is not merely a lead generator for an unidentified overseas dealer. That authority remains limited by FEMA, RBI directions, sanctions screening, purpose codes, documentation and SBI’s internal controls. AD Category-I Status Is Not Unlimited Permission An authorised dealer bank may sell or remit foreign exchange only for a permissible current-account or capital-account transaction and after satisfying itself about the purpose and evidence. RBI authorisation does not convert a prohibited transaction into a permitted one, guarantee that a beneficiary bank will accept funds, or approve an overseas investment platform. SBI can seek more documents, decline a request or hold it for compliance review. The customer remains responsible for giving a true purpose, observing the applicable limit and making accurate tax declarations. Question Verified position Practical consequence Who executes? Permission for offshore margin, CFDs or leveraged forex LRS cannot be used for overseas margin or margin calls What SBI FxOUT Currently Offers SBI’s official Remittance From India page states that retail customers may use the YONO SBI application to send an overseas remittance up to USD 40,000 or equivalent per transaction per day. The listed currencies are US dollar, euro, pound sterling, Canadian dollar, Australian dollar, New Zealand dollar, Singapore dollar and UAE dirham. The customer account must be KYC-compliant and PAN must be updated. These are channel conditions published by SBI, not a promise of acceptance or a replacement for purpose-specific limits. Digital Limit, LRS Limit and Purpose Limit Are Different The USD 40,000 figure is a YONO transaction-and-day ceiling. RBI’s LRS permits a resident individual, including a minor through the guardian framework, to remit up to USD 250,000 in a financial year for permitted current or capital account transactions, subject to exclusions and special rules. A lower digital-channel limit can apply even when unused LRS capacity remains. Conversely, available channel capacity does not increase the LRS balance or override a transaction-specific restriction. Remittances for studies, medical treatment or emigration may require evidence and can involve rules beyond the ordinary annual ceiling. Typical Permitted Purposes Permitted purposes can include private visits, maintenance of close relatives, gifts, studies abroad, medical treatment, emigration, employment-related expenses and eligible investments. The declared purpose determines the RBI purpose code, documents, TCS treatment and whether the transaction is within LRS. SBI also provides branch-based routes and an education-payment arrangement involving Flywire, but the customer should identify who receives funds and what contractual terms apply before using any specialised channel. KYC, Beneficiary Data and Supporting Documents At minimum, the remitter should expect a KYC-compliant SBI account, updated PAN, sufficient cleared funds, a registered mobile or digital access for YONO, and accurate beneficiary bank information. SBI may request the LRS application-cum-declaration, Form A2 or its electronic equivalent, purpose code, beneficiary name and address, bank name and address, account number or IBAN, SWIFT or BIC, intermediary details and evidence supporting the purpose. Minor, guardian, joint-account, education, medical, gift, investment or emigration transactions can require additional records. Documents Should Match the Declared Purpose An education remittance may need an admission letter, fee demand, student identifier and loan evidence. Medical payments may need an estimate or hospital communication. Maintenance and gift transfers require a truthful relationship and purpose statement. Investment remittances require the customer to establish that the asset and route are permitted. Discrepancies in names, amounts, country, purpose or beneficiary details can cause queries, rejection or return. A customer should never select a convenient purpose code that does not describe the transaction. Exchange Rate, Bank Charges and Correspondent Deductions The total rupee debit is not determined by the interbank exchange rate alone. It may include SBI’s applicable exchange rate or spread, a remittance service charge, GST on the taxable value of foreign-currency conversion or banking service, SWIFT or communication expenses, and correspondent or beneficiary-bank deductions. SBI publishes service-charge material but schedules can be revised and the exact debit depends on amount, currency, channel, purpose and charge instruction. A reliable comparison therefore uses the final pre-authorisation quote, not a third-party rate display. What to Confirm Before Authorising The customer should confirm the rupee amount, foreign-currency amount, rate timestamp, SBI charge, GST, charge option, possible correspondent deductions and amount expected by the beneficiary. For a fixed invoice, an “our”, “shared” or beneficiary charge instruction can change the net credit, but availability depends on the corridor. Where SBI’s current public schedule does not give an all-in amount for the specific case, this profile treats costs as quote-dependent rather than inventing a flat fee. A status shown as processed by SBI does not necessarily mean final credit. Weekends, local holidays, time zones, intermediary reviews, sanctions checks, invalid bank data and beneficiary-bank requirements can extend the timeline. A Returned Payment May Not Equal the Original Rupee Debit If the beneficiary or correspondent bank rejects the payment, funds may return after foreign and domestic charges. If the foreign currency is converted back to rupees at a later rate, the refund can differ from the original debit. The customer should obtain a transaction reference, preserve the debit advice, wait for SBI’s trace where appropriate and provide any corrected information through the approved route. Cancellation is not assured once the payment has entered correspondent processing. LRS Restrictions and Resident Eligibility LRS is for resident individuals and covers permitted transactions within its conditions. It does not authorise remittance for lottery tickets, banned activities or any transaction otherwise prohibited by FEMA. RBI’s directions expressly exclude remittances in the nature of margins or margin calls to overseas exchanges or overseas counterparties. Funding an offshore leveraged forex or CFD account cannot be justified merely by describing it as an investment, and a foreign regulator’s licence does not establish Indian-resident eligibility. SBI Is a Remittance Provider, Not an Approval of the Recipient SBI’s ability to transmit funds does not certify the commercial merits, solvency or legality of an overseas recipient’s offering. Customers must independently verify the beneficiary, contract, refund rights and India-specific legal position. A remittance can also create foreign-asset, income-tax or FEMA reporting duties after the bank transfer is completed. TCS and GST for an October 2026 Remittance Under section 394 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, an authorised dealer collects tax on LRS remittances once the aggregate exceeds ₹10 lakh in the tax year. The current statutory table applies 2% for education or medical-treatment purposes and 20% for other purposes on the amount above the threshold, subject to the Act’s conditions. The Finance Act 2025 also removed TCS for an eligible education remittance funded by the specified education-loan category. TCS is generally a tax credit reflected through the taxpayer’s records; it is not the same as the remittance fee and does not by itself decide the final tax liability. Purpose and Cumulative Amount Drive Collection The bank needs PAN, the correct purpose and cumulative information to apply TCS. Splitting a remittance or using several authorised dealers does not lawfully reset the taxpayer-level threshold. GST is separate: it may apply to SBI’s charges and to the prescribed taxable value for foreign-exchange conversion. The actual invoice or debit advice should be used because GST valuation is not simply the foreign amount multiplied by the headline rate. Complaints and Escalation For a delayed, returned or incorrectly charged transfer, the customer should first raise the issue with the servicing branch, YONO or SBI contact channel using the transaction reference and documentary trail. SBI publishes contact-centre numbers, circle grievance cells and a Principal Nodal Officer route. Its current customer-service policy states that a customer dissatisfied with SBI’s resolution may approach the RBI Ombudsman through RBI’s Complaint Management System, subject to the scheme’s eligibility and prior-complaint requirements. A commercial dispute with the foreign beneficiary may fall outside the bank’s control. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Is SBI itself the authorised remittance institution? Yes. State Bank of India is the bank executing the transaction within its RBI-authorised-dealer scope; correspondent banks may handle the overseas leg. 2. What is the current YONO FxOUT ceiling? SBI states USD 40,000 or equivalent per transaction per day for eligible retail customers. This is distinct from the annual LRS limit. 3. Which currencies does YONO list? USD, EUR, GBP, CAD, AUD, NZD, SGD and AED are listed on SBI’s current remittance page. 4. Can LRS fund an overseas forex margin account? No. RBI directions do not permit LRS remittances in the nature of margins or margin calls to overseas exchanges or counterparties. 5. Why can the beneficiary receive less than the ordered amount? Correspondent or beneficiary-bank charges and the selected charge instruction can reduce the net credit. 6. Is a returned remittance refunded at the original exchange rate? Not necessarily. Charges and the conversion rate on return can make the rupee credit different from the original debit. 7. What TCS may apply in 2026? Subject to the statutory conditions, section 394 uses a ₹10 lakh threshold and rates of 2% for education or medical purposes and 20% for other LRS purposes on the excess. 8. Where can an unresolved SBI complaint be escalated? Use SBI’s published grievance hierarchy first; an eligible unresolved complaint may then be taken to the RBI Ombudsman through CMS. Key Takeaways SBI is the authorised bank executing its retail outward remittances, subject to FEMA and RBI controls. YONO FxOUT’s USD 40,000 daily transaction ceiling is not the same as the USD 250,000 annual LRS ceiling. The total cost combines the live exchange quote, bank charges, GST and possible correspondent deductions. LRS does not permit overseas margin or margin-call remittances for leveraged forex or CFDs. Purpose, documents and cumulative remittances determine processing and current TCS treatment.
