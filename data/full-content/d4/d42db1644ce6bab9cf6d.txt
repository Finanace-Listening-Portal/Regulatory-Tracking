Rising energy costs and uneven monsoon rainfall have intensified inflationary pressures. Advt With India's economic growth expected to remain strong, the RBI, which kick-started its rate-hiking cycle last month, has room to tighten policy further, analysts say.More than 90% of economists, 56 of 61, in a November 13-20 Reuters poll forecast the RBI to hike its repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.75% at the conclusion of its December 2-4 policy meeting.While three economists expected the central bank to leave rates unchanged, two predicted a larger 50-basis-point increase.A majority of economists, 34 of 46, also forecast the RBI to follow up with a final hike to 6% in February."The inflation outlook has deteriorated in the last few months ... the fact that India is growing much faster than we had expected - could be north of 8% easily - I think that gives the RBI more latitude to prioritise elevated inflation, which has now breached the upper end of the tolerance range," said Dhiraj Nim, senior economist at ANZ."There is a clear generalisation of pressures beyond transitory parts of CPI, which are energy and food."Despite a recent decline in crude oil, prices are nearly 30% above prewar levels, suggesting inflation will remain elevated in the near term.However, the rupee's turnaround from one of Asia's worst-performing currencies to one of its best-performing could give the RBI space to moderate the pace of tightening. The central bank has spent billions of dollars to defend the currency against the dollar this year.Still, a cumulative 75-basis-point increase would mark the shallowest tightening cycle in more than eight years. Advt "The chatter around aggressive rate hikes was only building up because the rupee was under significant pressure just a month ago. At this point, they (RBI) will be a little more comfortable," said Upasna Bharadwaj, chief economist at Kotak Mahindra Bank, who expects two more rate hikes.Bharadwaj added inflation would remain elevated and closer to the 6% mark for the next five to six months.While inflation was forecast to average 6% in the fourth quarter and come down in the subsequent quarters, it was expected to stay above the RBI's medium-term target of 4% this year and next - a view broadly unchanged from the October poll. Advt Elevated prices will weigh on household spending and slow economic activity over the coming quarters.The economy likely grew about 8% on average over the past five quarters, including the one just ended, but is expected to slow to 6.9% this quarter and 6.8% next, the survey showed."Growth is improving purely because of government expenditure and, to a certain extent, because of urban consumption ... But (from) this quarter onwards, I don't think these two stories will play as much," said Debopam Chaudhuri, chief economist at Piramal Group.
