Summary: EbixCash World Money Limited, formerly Centrum Direct Limited, is the exact legal entity identified on the provider’s customer-contact page for its foreign-exchange and remittance services. The Reserve Bank of India’s current Authorised Dealer Category-II directory links to the Mumbai regional list, while the provider identifies itself as an RBI AD-II. That status permits specified foreign-exchange and remittance activity under FEMA and RBI directions; it does not authorise unlisted leveraged forex, contracts for difference or unrestricted overseas margin funding. The available consumer services include buying and selling eligible foreign currency, prepaid travel-card activity and outward remittance for documented permitted purposes such as education, medical treatment, travel and maintenance of relatives. Customers must establish identity, residence, purpose and source of funds, and transaction documents vary by use. Exchange rates, spread, card charges, delivery fees, correspondent or beneficiary-bank deductions and taxes must be confirmed in the transaction quote. LRS remittances can attract TCS above the statutory threshold, while GST applies to the taxable money-changing service value. This factual profile explains the process, settlement, refund and complaint routes without endorsing the provider or comparing it with competitors. A franchisee’s permitted role can be narrower than the principal’s role. Customers using a branch, airport counter, app or partner site should therefore identify which entity is accepting the instruction, which entity issues any card, and which bank or correspondent settles the payment. The covered offering is travel money and permitted remittance, not speculative leveraged currency trading. An RBI AD-II authorisation does not by itself permit the company to sell overseas spot-margin accounts, CFDs or options to Indian residents. LRS must not be described as a route for remitting overseas margin or meeting margin calls. Anyone presented with such a product should stop and verify the venue, instrument, counterparty and RBI permission independently. Customer Services and the Purpose Test The provider’s current service pages describe foreign-currency notes, prepaid forex or travel cards, buying back unused currency and outward money transfer. They also list student remittances and other documented purposes such as medical treatment, travel, maintenance of close relatives, conference or training fees, visa and registration fees, employment or emigration expenses and certain business-related payments. Availability still depends on FEMA, the exact purpose code, customer status, sanctions screening, documentary proof, corridor, currency and transaction limit. Resident Individuals and LRS A resident individual can use the Liberalised Remittance Scheme up to the prevailing annual limit for permitted current- or capital-account transactions. The provider’s pages state the current headline limit as USD 250,000 per financial year, but that figure is an overall ceiling rather than an automatic entitlement. Prohibited purposes, sector-specific rules, prior-approval cases, aggregate use through other authorised dealers and documentation can reduce or prevent the requested remittance. A declaration must account for earlier LRS utilisation in the same financial year. Travellers, Students, Families and Businesses A traveller can buy eligible currency notes or load a travel card for a documented trip, subject to current cash, card and aggregate limits. A student or parent may remit tuition or living expenses against an admission letter, invoice or other university evidence. Medical remittance requires the treatment or hospital documentation relevant to the transaction. Maintenance remittance requires the beneficiary relationship and purpose. A business remittance needs the underlying contract, invoice, import or service evidence, tax documentation and permitted purpose code. The July 2026 licence announcement refers to expanded trade and family-remittance capability, but each transaction still requires purpose-specific review; a company should not rely on a retail FAQ for an import or export payment. A corporate instruction can require constitutional documents, board authority, beneficial-ownership records and proof of the underlying obligation. For an outward remittance, the customer should expect Form A2 or the authorised electronic equivalent, an LRS declaration if the transaction uses LRS, beneficiary name and address, bank and routing details, purpose code, tax information and the underlying invoice or demand. Incomplete or inconsistent names, expired documents, unexplained third-party payments, unsupported purpose, exhausted LRS capacity or compliance alerts can delay or stop processing. Submitting documents does not create a right to execution. Check the Entity on Every Receipt The quote, application, payment instruction and final advice should consistently identify the counterparty and transaction reference. Where an online marketplace or digital partner originates the request, the customer should distinguish the interface from the authorised entity that executes the exchange or remittance. For travel cards, the issuing entity and settlement bank should be confirmed from the cardholder agreement. This separation determines who holds money, who handles a reversal and where a complaint must be escalated. Rates, Spreads, Fees and Tax Should Be Read Separately A foreign-exchange quote is time-sensitive. The customer pays or receives a rate that can incorporate the provider’s spread relative to a reference or wholesale market. The website offers an estimate and states that a booked rate can be locked under stated payment conditions. That does not make the rate universal across branches, currencies, denominations, delivery methods or transaction times. A partial-payment booking can also expire or change if its conditions are not met. Documented Charges Versus Quote-Dependent Costs The provider FAQ states that EbixCash World Money does not levy wire-transfer charges beyond those displayed, while advising customers to check whether the beneficiary bank charges separately. Door-delivery charges and availability are expressly quote-dependent. Card issuance, reload, ATM, cross-currency, replacement, inactivity or cash-out charges depend on the current card terms and issuer schedule. The prudent comparison is the final INR paid, foreign currency delivered, all provider charges, taxes and possible overseas deductions—not the headline exchange rate alone. If the reference rate is unavailable, it provides a gross-amount method. The supplier may instead choose the prescribed slab-based deemed value for the financial year. GST is charged on the resulting taxable service value at the applicable rate, not simply described as tax on the entire foreign-currency principal. The actual invoice should show the method and amount used. LRS TCS From 1 April 2026 Section 394 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, as amended by the Finance Act, 2026, requires an authorised dealer to collect tax when aggregate LRS remittances exceed ₹10 lakh in the tax year. The current statutory table states 2% for education or medical treatment and 20% for other purposes, applied under the section’s threshold mechanics. No collection is made for an education remittance funded by a qualifying loan from the specified financial institution. TCS is a tax credit, not an additional foreign-exchange entitlement or the customer’s final income-tax liability. The dealer needs PAN and aggregate-use information, and the customer should reconcile the credit with the tax statement. Booking, Settlement, Cancellation and Refund Flow A typical transaction begins with service and purpose selection, an indicative quote, document upload or branch verification, final compliance approval and payment from an acceptable source. Currency notes are delivered or collected under the booked conditions. A travel card is issued or loaded under the issuer terms. For outward remittance, the authorised entity converts funds, creates the cross-border instruction and provides a reference or advice. Beneficiary credit still depends on cut-off time, working days, correspondent processing, beneficiary-bank checks and accurate account details. Published Timing Is a Service Target, Not an Absolute Promise The current provider FAQ says outward remittances initiated before 2:00 PM IST on a working day are processed the same day, while timing can vary by destination. Processing is not the same as final beneficiary credit. Currency availability, documentation defects, enhanced due diligence, holidays, bank queries, sanctions screening or rejected beneficiary details can extend the time. Customers should obtain the cut-off, expected value date and tracking reference for their corridor. Failed Transactions and Reversals A failed or cancelled transaction must be traced to the entity holding the funds. A reversal can use the prevailing buyback or cancellation terms rather than the original customer rate, and bank or correspondent charges may not be recoverable. Card encashment, lost-card replacement and unused-balance refund follow the card agreement. The customer should retain the booking confirmation, payment proof, cancellation instruction, final advice and refund calculation. A refund should not be promised as instantaneous merely because an interface shows a failed status. Complaints and Escalation The official grievance page asks customers to quote the transaction reference, service type and contact details. It gives [email protected] and the published helpline for initial complaints. Separate escalation addresses are shown for forex-card and remittance matters. The page says complaints are acknowledged within three working days and targeted for resolution within 30 days. Rejected or partly accepted complaints can be reviewed by an Internal Ombudsman where applicable. If the company does not respond within 30 days or the customer is dissatisfied with the final response, the grievance page points to the RBI Complaint Management System under the Integrated Ombudsman framework. The complaint record should include the legal entity, branch or digital channel, transaction reference, date, amount, currency, quoted and applied rate, fees, documents, beneficiary status and prior correspondence. A dispute with a card issuer or partner may require parallel escalation to the entity named in that product’s terms. Service Limits That Should Remain Explicit AD-II status is activity-specific and does not turn EbixCash World Money into a universal bank or an overseas leveraged-forex broker. LRS is restricted to resident individuals and permitted purposes; it is not permission for overseas margin or margin-call remittances. A quote can change until the stated rate-lock conditions are satisfied, and overseas banks can make separate deductions. Travel cards have issuer, network, ATM, merchant, currency-conversion, loss, replacement and refund terms distinct from currency notes. Submission of KYC documents does not override purpose restrictions, sanctions controls, limits or the authorised person’s compliance decision.
