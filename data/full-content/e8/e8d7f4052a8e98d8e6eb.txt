Articles 3 min read RBI norms may hit retail securitisation deals; institutional market largely insulated The RBI's revised securitisation framework is unlikely to materially affect the institutional market, which accounts for most of transactions, but could disrupt small-ticket deals distributed through online bond platforms, industry experts told ETBFSI. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Updated On Nov 25, 2026 at 09:28 AM IST var news_msid = 136145449; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India's revised securitisation norms could restrict retail investors' access to small-ticket, short-tenure debt transactions distributed through online bond platforms, even as the institutional market, which accounts for the bulk of securitisation activity, is expected to remain largely unaffected.Vinod Kothari, founder of Vinod Kothari & Consultants, said the revised framework would not materially affect most of the securitisation market, but would make certain small-ticket, short-tenure transactions distributed through OBPPs (Online Bond Platform Provider) unviable.These include securitisation of receivables from short-term rental transactions, such as furniture rentals and other leased assets, which were being distributed to retail investors through online platforms. Advt "Each of these small value transactions of small tenure, which were happening on OBPPs, retail participation, that is completely gone," Kothari said.Under the amendments issued on November 23, the RBI has mandated that the issuance, holding and subsequent transfer of securitisation notes be undertaken only in dematerialised form. It has also extended the minimum ticket size of Rs 1 crore to subsequent transfers, in addition to issuance, with the threshold referring to the investment made by an individual investor. The amendments allow securitisation notes with amortisation structures to be traded at their amortised value even if the ticket size falls below Rs 1 crore.Vivek Ramji Iyer, partner at Grant Thornton, however, supported the restrictions saying that the minimum ticket size would help ensure that participation is limited to investors with the financial understanding and sophistication required to assess such instruments."Retail investors don't understand these papers. They don't understand these products," Iyer said, adding that securitised instruments carry high risks and investors could potentially lose their principal.Broader market impactHowever, with Over 90-95% of securitisation transactions placed with institutional investors, impact on the broader market is likely to be limited. Advt Vineet Jain, senior director at CareEdge Ratings, said 90-95 per cent or more of securitisation transactions are currently placed with institutional investors, with only a small segment of the market likely to be affected by the revised norms."On overall securitisation market volume, it would not have much impact. But small entities which would do, let's say, deal size of maybe Rs 10 crore, Rs 15 crore, Rs 20 crore, which then get distributed to retail, those transactions will get impacted," Jain said.He added that the changes could affect a segment of the market that has emerged over the last couple of years, with some fintechs using online bond platforms to distribute pass-through certificates (PTCs) to retail investors. Advt RMBS threshold draws criticismKothari also expressed concerns over the Rs 1 crore minimum investment threshold for public offers of residential mortgage-backed securities (RMBS)."We are holding a strong view that residential mortgage-backed transactions should be capable of being sold to the public as well. They should be publicly offered," he said.He argued that the minimum investment threshold makes public issuance of such securities difficult, adding that "public offer of anything Rs 1 crore or above is completely counterintuitive."Kothari added that allowing public issuance of RMBS could help reduce the cost of housing finance by enabling funds to flow directly from retail investors rather than through intermediaries.
