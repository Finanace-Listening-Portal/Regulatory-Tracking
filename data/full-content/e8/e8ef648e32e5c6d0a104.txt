Summary: The supplied article explains the relationship between Narcotics Control Bureau investigations under the NDPS Act, 1985 and Enforcement Directorate proceedings under the PMLA, 2002. It distinguishes narcotics enforcement from money-laundering investigation, asset tracing, provisional attachment and prosecution, while also outlining ED’s responsibilities under FEMA and the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act. Its central proposition is that an NCB case does not automatically establish a PMLA offence: the relevant NDPS offence must qualify as a scheduled offence, identifiable proceeds of crime must arise from the alleged criminal activity, and the person’s involvement in the relevant laundering conduct must be examined. The FAQs provide six checkpoints covering the scheduled offence, criminal activity, identified property, financial trail, Section 3 conduct and the status of the underlying narcotics proceedings. They also discuss transaction chronology, financing illicit traffic under Section 27A, bank records, cash deposits, hawala records, crypto transactions, property purchases and digital evidence. Judicial discussions address equivalent-value attachment, prosecution of persons who are not accused in the scheduled offence, the consequences of acquittal, discharge or quashing, and procedural safeguards after cognizance of a PMLA complaint. Decisions including Vijay Madanlal Choudhary, Pavana Dibbur and R.K.M. Powergen are discussed alongside other authorities. The article emphasises examining each case’s factual foundation, the accused persons’ respective roles and the survival of the scheduled offence before applying the cited propositions. What is NCB è ED nexus for investigations and adjudications across the India? The NCB è ED nexus is primarily the “drug offence è Proceeds of Crime (PoC) è money laundering” connection. What are the legal points involved in NCB è ED nexus? (i) The NDPS offence and the PMLA offence are “not the same offence”. (ii) The NDPS case can constitute the underlying scheduled offence, while ED’s case concerns the subsequent handling, concealment, possession, acquisition or use of the Proceeds of Crime (PoC) from the “criminal activity” subject to the requirements of PMLA, 2002. (iii) The Supreme Court has emphasised that the existence of Proceeds of Crime (PoC) is essential for offence under the section 3 of PMLA, 2002 and that the Proceeds of Crime (PoC) is required to be derived or obtained from “criminal activity” relating to a PMLA scheduled offence. (iv) Conclusion The NCB establishes or investigates the “drug-crime side” under the NDPS Act, 1985 and the ED follows “money laundering side” under PMLA, 2002. 6. What are important points for defence in NCB and ED cases? (i) The NCB case does “not automatically” mean an ED/PMLA case. (ii) The ED still has to establish the statutory ingredients of PMLA, 2002 particularly the existence and linkage of Proceeds of Crime (PoC) and person’s involvement in the “criminal activity” contemplated under the section 3 of PMLA, 2002. (iii) This becomes particularly important where: (a) The NCB has seized drugs but “no identifiable financial Proceeds of Crime (PoC)” are established (b) The alleged Proceeds of Crime (PoC) belong to someone “other than the NDPS” accused (c) The property or transactions predate the alleged drug offence (d) There is “no demonstrated money trail” connecting the property to the scheduled offence (e) The person is merely a relative, employee, director, professional or business associate “without evidence” of handling Proceeds of Crime (PoC) (f) The NCB proceedings themselves change materially, such as discharge, quashing or acquittal. 7. What is chronology test for NCB è ED nexus? (i) Date of alleged NDPS activity under the NDPS Act, 1985 (a) Date of proceeds allegedly generated under the PMLA, 2002 (b) Date of money or property acquired under the PMLA, 2002 (c) Date of alleged laundering activity under the PMLA, 2002 (d) Date of NCB FIR or complaint under the NDPS Act, 1985 (e) Date of ED ECIR under the PMLA, 2002 (f) Date of PMLA search or attachment under the PMLA, 2002 (ii) A property or transaction that predates the alleged generation of Proceeds of Crime (PoC) can create an important factual or legal issue about whether it could actually be Proceeds of Crime (PoC) of that PMLA scheduled offence under the PMLA, 2002. (iii) The Supreme Court has itself emphasised the significance of the point at which the PMLA scheduled offence is completed and when the alleged Proceeds of Crime (PoC) under the PMLA, 2002 could have come into existence. 10. What is important of the sec. 27A of NDPS Act, 1985 for the ED’s investigations? (i) The section 27A is relevant for financing illicit traffic, harbouring offenders and is particularly significant in PMLA analysis because the alleged NDPS activity can involve the generation and movement of financial proceeds under the NDPS Act, 1985. (ii) Therefore, the ED’s case based on the sections 21, 23, 27A or 29 of the NDPS Act, 1985 is required: (a) To analyse over and above simple possession or use case under the PMLA, 2002. (b) The ED required to identify: Drug offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 Quantity or transaction under the NDPS Act, 1985 Criminal activity under the PMLA, 2002 Money generated under the PMLA, 2002 Recipient of Proceeds of Crime (PoC) under the PMLA, 2002 Bank, cash or asset under the PMLA, 2002 Money laundering activity under the PMLA, 2002 The Supreme Court and High Courts Judgments for the NCB è ED Nexus 11. What is Supreme Court case of Nirman Builders v. Union of India (2026)? (i) This Supreme Court case is relevant particularly to the value-of-property limb of the section 2(1)(u) of PMLA, 2002. (ii) The Supreme Court has reaffirmed that Proceeds of Crime (PoC) can include property equivalent in value to the property derived from the PMLA scheduled offence subject to the statutory conditions under the PMLA, 2002. (iii) This matter is relevant when the ED says that we cannot trace original drug money or property, therefore another property of equivalent value can be attached. (iv) That proposition has to be analysed separately from the threshold question: (a) Was there first a PMLA offence generating identifiable Proceeds of Crime (PoC) under the PMLA, 2002? (b) Therefore, latter remains foundational. 12. What is Supreme court case Pavana Dibbur v. ED (2023)? (i) The Supreme Court dealt with the relationship between the PMLA scheduled offence and the PMLA case under the PMLA, 2002. (ii) The Supreme Court held that even a person who was “not an accused” in the PMLA scheduled offence can take benefit when “all accused” in the scheduled offence are acquitted, or when the scheduled-offence proceedings under the PMLA, 2002 are discharged or quashed. (iii) This principle has subsequently been reiterated by the Supreme Court. (iv) Therefore, in an NCB–ED matter, one should closely monitor the fate of the NCB/NDPS proceedings under the NDPS Act, 1985. 13. What is different Supreme Court cases’ analysis for NCB è ED nexus? Union of India (2022)?) (i) The Supreme Court held that there must be a PMLA scheduled offence under PMLA, 2002 and also property derived or obtained from “criminal activity” relating to the scheduled offence both “before” there can be Proceeds of Crime (PoC). (ii) The Supreme Court held that the NDPS offence must be examined against the PMLA Schedule offence under the PMLA, 2002 and also the particular allegations in the NCB case “both”. (iii) The Supreme Court subsequently reiterated that the existence of PMLA scheduled offence under the PMLA, 2002 is a condition precedent for the existence of Proceeds of Crime (PoC). 15. Whether the NCB case is “automatically” the PMLA case? No (i) The Supreme court held in case of Vijay Madanlal v. Union of India (2022) that the NCB case under the NDPS Act, 1985 is “not automatically” PMLA case under the PMLA, 2002 (ii) The Supreme court held that the ED under the PMLA, 2002 is “not permitted” to say that the NCB case under the NDPS Act, 1985, therefore there are Proceeds of Crime (PoC) 16. What are important legal questions in Vijay Madanlal v. Union of India (2022)? (i) The existence of NCB offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 (a) What exact NDPS offence is alleged under the NDPS Act, 1985? (b) Is that offence a PMLA scheduled offence under the PMLA, 2002? (c) What “criminal activity” is alleged under the PMLA, 2002? (d) What property or money is said to have been generated from it under the PMLA, 2002? (e) What are the identifiable Proceeds of Crime (PoC) trail under PMLA, 2002? (f) How is the particular person connected with that property under PMLA, 2002? (g) What laundering activity is attributed to that person under sec. 3 of PMLA, 2002? (ii) (a) The Supreme Court has described money laundering as an independent offence concerning a process or activity connected with Proceeds of Crime (PoC) But (b) These Proceeds of Crime (PoC) are required to be originated from “criminal activity” relating to a PMLA scheduled offence under the PMLA, 2002. 17. What is Delhi High Court case of Anshad Badruddin v. ED (2026)? (i) This is a useful recent authority because it expressly relied on Pavana Dibbur v. ED (2023) and R.K.M. Powergen v. ED (2025). (ii) The Delhi High Court reiterated that: (a) A PMLA scheduled offence is foundational for ED investigations and adjudications under the PMLA, 2002 (b) Proceeds of Crime (PoC) is required to be related to that PMLA scheduled offence under the PMLA, 2002 (c) The ED’s jurisdiction is not unlimited under the PMLA, 2002 (iii) The PMLA cannot be invoked merely because there is some alleged “criminal activity” unrelated to a PMLA scheduled offence under the PMLA, 2002. 18. What is Delhi High Court case of Kanchana Rai v. ED (2026)? (i) The Delhi High Court discussed Vijay Madanlal v. Union of India (2022) and Pavana Dibbur v. ED (2023), including the distinction between: (a) A person who was not an accused in the predicate offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 but is alleged to have dealt with its Proceeds of Crime (PoC) under the PMLA, 2002 and (b) A situation where the predicate offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 itself has ceased to exist. (ii) The Delhi High Court emphasised that PMLA proceedings cannot simply continue indefinitely when the very foundation, the scheduled offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 has disappeared. 19. What is Delhi High Court case of Bhupesh Arora v. ED (2026)? (i) The Delhi High Court examined the requirement that the alleged Proceeds of Crime (PoC) required to be specifically connected to the PMLA scheduled offence under the PMLA, 2002. (ii) The Delhi High Court cautioned against treating a large volume of transactions as Proceeds of Crime (PoC) merely because those transactions appear within the broader investigation by the ED under the PMLA, 2002. (iii) This Delhi High Court case is potentially very useful when ED’s alleged Proceeds of Crime (PoC) figure is “based upon gross banking transactions” rather than a traceable amount under the PMLA, 2002. 20. What is Karnataka High Court case of Prathap N v. ED (2026)? (i) The Karnataka High Court applied Pavana Dibbur v. ED (2023) and dealt extensively with the consequences of the predicate proceedings ceasing to exist under the NDPS Act, 1985. (ii) The Karnataka High Court emphasised that when the predicate proceedings have been completely extinguished under the NDPS Act, 1985 and that position remains operative, the ED cannot simply keep the PMLA proceedings alive by treating the predicate offence as notionally continuing under the PMLA, 2002. 21. What is Punjab & Haryana Court case of Sukhpal Singh Khaira v. ED (2025)? (i) This case is presently one of the most directly relevant for a NCB/NDPS è ED/PMLA nexus. (ii) The predicate case involved: (a) The sections 21, 23, 27-A and 29 under the NDPS Act, 1985 (b) The heroin smuggling (c) An accused, Gurdev Singh, who was convicted (d) ED subsequently alleged that drug proceeds were provided to Sukhpal Khaira (e) The petitioner argued that his NDPS proceedings were stayed and therefore the PMLA trial should also be deferred. (iii) The High Court rejected the request to defer the PMLA proceedings. (iv) It distinguished Bharti Cement, because in Bharti Cement the same person was facing both the PMLA scheduled offence and PMLA proceedings under the PMLA, 2002 where in Sukhpal Singh Khaira it’s alleged to have generated the Proceeds of Crime (PoC) and the accused Gurdev Singh had already been convicted. (v) Why this case matters? (a) It’s demonstrating the distinction between NCB/NDPS prosecution against Sukhpal Singh Khaira is pending or stayed (b) The ED alleges Sukhpal Singh Khaira himself generated Proceeds of Crime (PoC) (c) The NDPS accused Gurdev Singh has already been convicted (d) The ED alleges Sukhpal Singh Khaira subsequently received or used Gurdev Singh’s Proceeds of Crime (PoC). (e) In the 2nd situation, Pavana Dibbur makes the ED case materially stronger because Sukhpal Singh Khaira does not need to have been an accused in the original NDPS offence. 22. What is Allahabad High Court case of Neeharika Singh v. ED (2025)? (i) The Allahabad High Court drew an important distinction when “all” predicate-offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 accused are: (a) Acquitted, or (b) Discharged, or (c) The entire predicate case is quashed (ii) Therefore, the Allahabad High Court held in this case that there is no surviving scheduled offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 and therefore no Proceeds of Crime (PoC) under the PMLA, 2002 (iii) However, the Allahabad High Court held that when “some accused” are: (a) Discharged (b) Acquitted, or (c) The proceedings against them are quashed But (iv) “Other accused are not discharge”, acquitted or the proceedings against them are quashed, the PMLA proceedings against the “other accused” will continue when there is independent material connecting him to the Proceeds of Crime (PoC) under the PMLA, 2002. (v) Conclusion: This Allahabad High Court case of Neeharika Singh v. ED (2025) is highly relevant to the NCB under the NDPS Act, 1985 when the case involving multiple accused. 23. What is Madras High Court case of R.K.M Powergen v. ED (2025)? This Madras High Court decision is worth retaining separately from 2022 decision because it applies the Supreme Court’s later PMLA jurisprudence to the question of: Whether the ED can continue proceedings when the alleged predicate offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 itself is absent? 24. What is Telangana High Court case of Bharti Cement Corporation v. ED (2022)? (i) This case is important because where: (a) The “same accused faces” the predicate offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 and the PMLA, 2002 (b) Both trials are pending (c) The outcome of the PMLA scheduled offence under the PMLA, 2002 would directly determine whether Proceeds of Crime (PoC) existed. (ii) The reasoning was that the PMLA Special Court should not finally pronounce upon the money-laundering charge “before” there is a definite finding regarding the PMLA scheduled offence generating the alleged Proceeds of Crime (PoC) under the PMLA, 2002. (iii) The Punjab & Haryana High Court in Sukhpal Singh Khaira expressly distinguished Bharti Cement on these facts. (iv) Practical significance: Do not cite Bharti Cement “without” 1st examining who is the accused in the NDPS case and who is the accused in the PMLA case. 25. What is Madras High Court case of Powergen Pvt. Ltd v. ED (2022)? Application to the ED under the PMLA, 2002 (i) The ED cannot use PMLA investigation itself to discover whether a predicate/scheduled offence in existed under the PMLA, 2002. (ii) The Supreme Court had dismissed the challenge while recording that, when there was no predicate offence under the Customs Act, the ED could not use PMLA powers to discover the existence of a predicate offence under the PMLA, 2002 described as “putting the cart before the horse.” (iii) This principle was again considered in R.K.M. Powergen Pvt. Ltd. v. ED (2025). Application to the NCB under the NDPS Act, 1985 (iv) This judgement gives you an important question: (a) Did the NCB case actually disclose a scheduled NDPS offence under the NDPS Act, 1985 capable of generating the alleged Proceeds of Crime (PoC) under the PMLA, 2002? When the answer is no, the ED cannot manufacture the predicate offence merely through its own PMLA investigation under the PMLA, 2002.
