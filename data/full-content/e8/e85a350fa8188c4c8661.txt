(Supreme Court of India) IBC Appeal Filed on 46th Day Is Time-Barred: Holiday Cannot Extend Condonable Period Introduction The Supreme Court, in Lulu International Shopping Malls Private Limited v. Gorinka Pratyusha and Others Etc., 2026 INSC 1224, decided on 16 November 2026, held that a holiday falling on the final day of the condonable period cannot extend the time for filing an insolvency appeal. The Court also reaffirmed that, although the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) lacks review powers under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, it can recall its orders on sufficient grounds through its inherent jurisdiction. Background of the Dispute The NCLT initiated the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against Manjeera Retail Holdings Private Limited in July 2023. Lulu International emerged as the successful resolution applicant, with its plan receiving 100% approval from the Committee of Creditors. The NCLT approved the plan on 26 March 2025. Lulu claimed that it subsequently invested ₹271.52 crore towards implementation. Certain purchasers of commercial units challenged the approval. They alleged that their payments had been recorded as customer advances instead of recognising them as financial creditors. They also complained that earlier directions to include their claims in the Information Memorandum had not been implemented. Appeals Were Filed Beyond the Permissible Period Under Section 61(2) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, an appeal must be filed within 30 days. The NCLAT may condone delay for a further period not exceeding 15 days, upon sufficient cause being shown. In this case, the ordinary limitation period expired on 25 April 2025, while the additional condonable period expired on 10 May 2025. The purchasers filed their appeals on 11 May 2025, the 46th day. Nevertheless, the NCLAT condoned the delay by applying Section 4 of the Limitation Act, 1963, because 10 May was a second Saturday and a holiday. Holiday Protection Does Not Cover the Additional 15 Days The Supreme Court applied Tata Steel Ltd. v. Raj Kumar Banerjee and Others, (2025) 9 SCC 483. That decision distinguishes the prescribed limitation period from the additional period during which a court or tribunal may exercise discretion to condone delay. For Section 4, the prescribed period means the statutory limitation period itself. It does not include the discretionary extension available upon establishing sufficient cause. Accordingly, the holiday-related protection could not extend the additional 15-day window under Section 61(2). The appeals filed on the 46th day were therefore beyond the permissible period. E-Filing Undermined the Explanation for Delay The Supreme Court also noted that the appeals were electronically filed on Sunday, 11 May 2025, which was itself a holiday. Consequently, the purchasers could equally have e-filed them on the preceding Saturday. The holiday did not establish sufficient cause for filing beyond the permissible window. This observation reinforced the Court’s conclusion, although the decisive legal point remained that Section 4 could not enlarge the condonable period. NCLAT Can Recall Orders Despite Having No Review Power Lulu sought recall of the orders condoning delay, relying upon Tata Steel. The NCLAT rejected those applications, treating the earlier orders as decisions passed after hearing both sides. The Supreme Court held that this approach failed to distinguish review from recall. Although the NCLAT has no statutory power of review under the IBC, Rule 11 of the NCLAT Rules, 2016 preserves its inherent jurisdiction to recall orders on sufficient grounds. The Court referred to the five-member NCLAT decision in Union Bank of India v. Dinkar T. Venkatasubramanian, subsequently confirmed by the Supreme Court, and the principles recognised in Greater Noida Industrial Development Authority v. Prabhjit Singh Soni. Binding Precedent Could Not Be Ignored The NCLAT had incorrectly considered Tata Steel to be subsequent to its delay-condonation order. In fact, Tata Steel was decided on 7 May 2025, whereas the NCLAT condoned delay on 10 October 2025. The Supreme Court emphasised that its interpretation of a statutory provision constitutes the law of the land. A contrary order cannot be protected merely because it was passed earlier, unless it has attained finality. The NCLAT was therefore unjustified in refusing to exercise its recall jurisdiction when the settled legal position was brought to its attention. Recall Applications Kept the Limitation Issue Alive The purchasers argued that the delay-condonation orders had not been separately appealed and had consequently become final. The Supreme Court rejected this contention because Lulu’s recall applications had kept the issue alive. Once rejection of those applications was challenged before the Supreme Court, the error could be corrected under Section 62 of the IBC. The Court allowed Lulu’s appeals, set aside the rejection of recall and the delay-condonation orders, and dismissed the purchasers’ NCLAT appeals on limitation. Practical Implications The judgment underscores the strict distinction between 30 days of limitation and 15 days of discretionary condonation. Parties should not assume that a holiday on the last condonable day permits filing afterwards. It also confirms that recall jurisdiction can address an unlawful order in appropriate circumstances. The purchasers’ substantive grievances were not decided; their appeals failed because they were filed beyond the permissible time. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT OF THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA 1. Dismissal of the recall applications filed by Lulu International Shopping Malls Private Limited, the appellant, by the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal1, Chennai, vide common order dated 10.02.2026, is the source for grievance in these appeals. 2. On 16.03.2026, this Court stayed the proceedings in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(INS) Nos. 388 and 389 of 2025 on the file of the NCLAT. 3. The National Company Law Tribunal, Hyderabad Bench2, initiated corporate insolvency resolution process against Manjeera Retail Holdings Private Limited, the corporate debtor, vide order dated 18.07.2023 in Company Petition (IB) No. 296/7/HDB/2022. The appellant emerged the successful resolution applicant therein, upon 100% approval of its resolution plan by the Committee of Creditors of the corporate debtor. Thereafter, its resolution plan was approved by the NCLT, vide order dated 26.03.2025 passed in IA(IBC)(Plan) No. 08 of 2024 in CP (IB) No. 296/7/HDB/2022. Pursuant thereto, the appellant claims to have invested ₹271.52 crore in the implementation of its resolution plan. 4. While so, the respondents, viz., Gorinka Pratyusha, Srinivas Kalluri and Rajani Kalluri, who claimed to have entered into agreements with the corporate debtor for purchase of commercial units in Manjeera Mall, that was to be constructed by the corporate debtor, filed appeals against the approval of the resolution plan. Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 388 of 2025 was filed by Gorinka Pratyusha and Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 389 of 2025 was filed by Srinivas and Rajani Kalluri. Their complaint therein was that Birendra Kumar Agarwal, the Resolution Professional of the corporate debtor, had recorded the payments made by them, towards full sale consideration for their units, as ‘advances from customers’ instead of acknowledging them as financial creditors. They contended that the orders of the NCLT in I.A. Nos. 384 and 492 of 2024 in the company petition, directing the Resolution Professional to include their claims in the Information Memorandum, were not given effect to. 5. However, the aforestated appeals filed by them were not within time, in terms of the limitation prescribed in Section 61(2) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 20163. Section 61(2) provided for an appeal being filed within 30 days of the date of the order with a condonable delay of 15 extra days and no more. In terms thereof, the appeals should have been filed within the 30 day-period that expired on 25.04.2025 or at least within the extendable 15 day-period that expired on 10.05.2025. However, both the appeals were filed by the respondents on 11.05.2025, i.e., on the 46th day. Their applications for condonation of delay on their part were, however, allowed by the NCLAT, vide common order dated 10.10.2025 passed in I.A. Nos. 1139 of 2025 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 388 of 2025 and I.A No. 1141 of 2025 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 389 of 2025, by applying Section 4 of the Limitation Act, 19634, on the ground that the 45th day, being the last day of the extendable 15-day period under Section 61(2), fell on a holiday, i.e., 10.05.2025, a second Saturday. 6. Aggrieved by such condonation of delay by the NCLAT, the appellant filed recall applications in I.A. No. 183 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 388 of 2025 and I.A. No.232 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 389 of 2025. The appellant also filed I.A. No. 184 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 388 of 2025 and I.A. No. 233 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 389 of 2025, seeking condonation of the delay of three days in filing the said recall applications. 7. The specific case of the appellant in its recall applications was that the decision of this Court in Tata Steel Ltd. vs. Raj Kumar Banerjee and others5 barred the application of Section 4 of the Act of 1963 when an appeal was filed beyond the prescribed period of 30 days under the IBC. This Court categorically held therein that the expression ‘prescribed period’ appearing in Section 4 of the Act of 1963 could not be construed to mean anything other than the ‘period of limitation’ and any period beyond that prescribed period, during which the Court or Tribunal has the discretion to allow a person to institute the proceedings, cannot be taken to be part of the ‘prescribed period’. Hence, as per the above decision, the benefit of exclusion of the period during which a Court or Tribunal is closed shall be available only when the appeal is filed within the ‘prescribed period of limitation’ and shall not be available in respect of the period extendable by the Court in exercise of its discretion. 8. Perusal of the impugned common order dated 10.02.2026 passed by the NCLAT reflects that the delay of 3 days on the part of the appellant in filing the recall applications was condoned but the recall applications came to be dismissed. Though the aforestated decision of this Court was specifically brought to its notice, the NCLAT opined that the order dated 10.10.2025 was passed by it on merits after hearing the counsel for the parties and it could not be recalled on the basis of fresh evidence and material. It was further observed that submission of a precedent, giving a ruling different from the order sought to be recalled, subsequent to the passing of the said order, cannot be a reason to recall such order as it would not fall within the ambit of exercising inherent powers to rectify an error apparent on the face of the record. According to the NCLAT, the only scope left for the appellant was to invoke remedies as were available to it in law. Holding so, the NCLAT rejected the appellant’s recall applications. 9. First and foremost, we may note that the NCLAT was in error in opining that the decision of this Court in Tata Steel Ltd. (supra) was subsequent to its order dated 10.10.2025, as Tata Steel Ltd. (supra) was decided on 07.05.2025, long before the order dated 10.10.2025 was passed by the NCLAT, condoning the delay in the filing of the appeals by the respondents. Further, when this Court clarifies the legal position in relation to a statutory provision, the same would be the law of the land and there is no question of an order contrary thereto passed earlier by a Court or Tribunal being saved, unless such order has attained finality. 10. It is no doubt true that the NCLAT does not have the power of review under the provisions of the IBC but it is vested with the power of recalling its orders by exercising its inherent jurisdiction under Rule 11 of the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal Rules, 2016. This was the import of the 5-Judge Bench decision of the NCLAT in Union Bank of India (erstwhile Corporation Bank) vs. Dinkar T Venkatasubramanian and others6, wherein it was held that, though it was not vested with power to review a judgment, however, in exercise of its inherent jurisdiction, the NCLAT can entertain an application for recall of a judgment on sufficient grounds. This 5-Judge Bench decision of the NCLAT was confirmed by this Court in Union Bank of India vs. Financial Creditors of Amtek Auto Ltd. and others7. This Court observed therein that it was in agreement with the view taken by the 5-Judge Bench of the NCLAT and found no reason to interfere with the said judgment. 11. More recently, in Greater Noida Industrial Development Authority vs. Prabhjit Singh Soni and another8, upon reviewing the case law on the subject, a 3-Judge Bench of this Court observed that the law which emerged from such decisions was that a Tribunal or a Court is invested with such ancillary and incidental powers as may be necessary to discharge its functions effectively for the purpose of doing justice between the parties and, in the absence of a statutory provision, in an appropriate case, it can recall its orders in exercise of such ancillary or incidental powers. 12. Therefore, the NCLAT was not justified in refusing to exercise its inherent jurisdiction even when apprised of the settled legal position with regard to application of Section 4 of the Act of 1963. More so, as the respondents did not even establish sufficient cause for condonation of the delay on their part in the filing of their appeals. Be it noted that 10.05.2025 was a second Saturday and the NCLAT was of the opinion that the same constituted sufficient grounds to prolong the extended limitation period to the 46th day. However, it is an admitted fact that the appeals were e-filed on the next day, i.e., on 11.05.2025, which was also a holiday, being Sunday. Therefore, such e-filing of the appeals could as well have been done on 10.05.2025 itself. In any event, the application of Section 4 of the Act of 1963 in the case on hand was not valid, given the legal position as set out in Tata Steel Ltd. (supra). 13. It has been contended by the learned senior counsel appearing for the respondents that the common order dated 10.10.2025 passed by the NCLAT, condoning the delay in the filing of the appeals, was not separately challenged and as the time to do so has expired, the said order is not liable to be interfered with. It is pointed out that there can be no merger of the said order with the order dismissing the recall applications and, therefore, condonation of delay by the NCLAT is deemed to have attained finality. However, this specious argument loses sight of the fact that the condonation of delay in the filing of the appeals did not attain finality, as the recall applications filed by the appellant in relation thereto kept the issue alive and when the NCLAT, in its wisdom, refused to exercise its inherent jurisdiction to recall such order and the same was subjected to appellate review before this Court, the same can be remedied by this Court in exercise of jurisdiction under Section 62 of the IBC. 14. On the above analysis, this Court finds that entertainment of the appeals by the NCLAT was illegal, as the appeals were filed beyond the time prescribed in Section 61(2) of the IBC. Further, the NCLAT erred in failing to apply the law laid down by this court in Tata Steel Ltd. (supra). 15. The appeals are accordingly allowed, setting aside the order dated 10.02.2026 passed by the NCLAT, Chennai, in the recall applications in IA No. 183 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 388 of 2025 and IA No. 232 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 389 of 2025. The recall applications shall stand allowed and the common order dated 10.10.2025 in IA No. 1139 of 2025 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 388 of 2025 and IA No. 1141 of 2025 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 389 of 2025 shall stand set aside. In consequence, Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) Nos. 388 and 389 of 2025 on the file of the NCLAT, Chennai, shall stand dismissed on the ground of limitation. Parties shall bear their own costs.
