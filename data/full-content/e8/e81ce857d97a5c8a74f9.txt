Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Capri Global Capital Limited Vs Silver Jubilee Motors Limited (NCLT Mumbai) Summary: The NCLT Mumbai Bench-VI admitted Capri Global Capital Limited’s application under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 against Silver Jubilee Motors Limited, rejecting objections based on a subsequent conditional one-time settlement, part-payments and loan statements describing the accounts as “Live”. The application, filed on 3 March 2026, claimed a default of Rs.2,66,04,388.99 as on 23 February 2026, with 3 April 2025 pleaded as the date of default. The debt arose from facilities of Rs.3.50 crore and Rs.82.25 lakh extended under agreements dated 31 August 2015 and 31 August 2016. Earlier CIRP proceedings initiated by Capri had been withdrawn following regularisation of overdue amounts. After renewed defaults, the creditor issued a fresh SARFAESI demand notice and pursued security enforcement. The order also records another creditor’s CIRP proceedings, subsequently withdrawn following settlement. The corporate debtor relied on an OTS approval dated 18 March 2026 for Rs.1.98 crore, entered into after the present petition was filed. It initially reported payments of Rs.90.50 lakh and subsequently Rs.98.50 lakh up to 28 April 2026. It argued that the settlement novated the original contractual obligations under Section 62 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, that acceptance of payments attracted waiver and estoppel, and that the remaining settlement liability had fallen below the Rs.1 crore threshold under Section 4. Relying on Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India, it submitted that insolvency jurisdiction should not be used for coercive recovery. Through a separate application under Section 60(5) read with Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016, it sought to introduce the creditor’s loan statements dated 28 May 2026 after the matter had been reserved for orders. Following Dena Bank (now Bank of Baroda) v. C. Shivakumar Reddy, the Tribunal permitted those relevant, pre-existing statements to be taken on record. On merits, the Tribunal found that the OTS expressly made timely payment essential and provided that any payment default would immediately nullify the settlement, withdraw the concessions and restore the loan accounts to their original position as on March 2026. No payment was made after 28 April 2026, and the settlement balance payable by 25 May 2026 remained unpaid. The agreed consequences therefore followed without any further cancellation notice. Acceptance of earlier payments did not establish an unequivocal waiver of the pending statutory remedy, an extension of time or revival of the failed OTS. The payments nevertheless had to be credited against the outstanding liability. The Tribunal held that the statutory threshold was satisfied when the petition was instituted on a claim of approximately Rs.2.66 crore. Subsequent part-payments could not retrospectively invalidate its institution, and the debtor could not rely on the concessional OTS amount after the settlement had failed under its own terms. It relied on B. Prashanth Hegde v. State Bank of India, M. Suresh Kumar Reddy v. Canara Bank and Devika Resources Pvt. Ltd. v. MAA Manasha Devi Alloys Pvt. Ltd. The description “Live” was merely an accounting or operational classification and did not negate documentary evidence of an earlier default or override the settlement’s express conditions. Parallel recovery or security enforcement proceedings likewise did not bar the insolvency remedy where its statutory requirements were established. Accordingly, the Bench admitted the petition, declared a moratorium and appointed Davinder Singh Saroya as Interim Resolution Professional. It directed an immediate public announcement, cooperation by the debtor’s officers and managers, quarterly progress reports and deposit of Rs.3 lakh towards initial CIRP costs. Importantly, the Tribunal did not crystallise the creditor’s claimed amount; collation of the outstanding liability was left to the IRP, with credit to be given for payments already received. CONTENTIONS OF THE CD 3.1. Affidavit-in-Reply dated 22.04.2026 was filed and affirmed by one Mr. Sachin Kunjir, who is stated to be the Accounts Head and authorized representative of the CD. 3.2. It is submitted that the remaining amount of INR 1,08,00,000/- is due by CD to FC on or before 25.05.2026. 3.9. The CD has already issued the Post-Dated Cheque (“PDC”) dated 25.05 2026 having cheque no. 858536 drawn on State Bank of India, Tilak Road, Pune. 3.10. The acceptance of payments under the OTS arrangement clearly establishes that the parties have acted upon the settlement, thereby giving it binding effect in law. Once the Financial Creditor has elected to enter into a negotiated settlement and has accepted part performance thereof, it cannot simultaneously pursue insolvency proceedings on the basis of the original debt, as the same stands restructured and substituted by the OTS terms. 3.11. It is a settled position of law that a One Time Settlement constitutes a fresh and independent contract, which has the effect of novating the original contractual relationship between the parties in terms of Section 62 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. Consequently, the original cause of action based on the earlier loan agreements ceases to survive, and the rights and obligations of the parties are thereafter governed solely by the terms of the settlement. 3.12. In the present case, once the Financial Creditor agreed to accept a reduced amount in full and final settlement under OTS and prescribed a revised payment schedule, the original financial debt stood altered, and any alleged default must necessarily be examined only in the context of the OTS terms and not the original loan documentation. 3.13. The IBC, 2016 is a beneficial legislation intended for resolution and revival of stressed assets, and not for coercive recovery of dues. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India, Writ Petition (Civil) No. 99 of 2018 dt. 25.01.2019 has categorically held that the Code is not a substitute for debt recovery proceedings and that settlement between parties must be encouraged at all stages. 3.14. The conduct of the FC in entering into an OTS arrangement and accepting payments thereunder clearly demonstrates its conscious decision to resolve the matter outside the rigours of insolvency proceedings. Having elected such a course, the FC is bound by the principles of election, waiver, and estoppel, and cannot be permitted to approbate and reprobate by simultaneously invoking CIRP. 3.15. It is further submitted that once a settlement has been arrived at and is being performed, the continuation of insolvency proceedings would defeat the very object of the Code as well as the said OTS, which prioritises resolution over liquidation. The Hon’ble NCLAT in various judgments has consistently held that where parties have settled disputes, insolvency proceedings ought not to be continued as a pressure tactic. 3.16. In the present case, the CD has demonstrated its bona fide intent and financial capacity by making substantial payments under the OTS and continuing to adhere to the settlement terms. The same clearly negates any allegation of inability to pay debts and instead reflects a genuine effort towards repayment of the debt and resolution of the debt. 3.17. The existence of a binding and operative OTS, coupled with substantial compliance thereof, effectively removes the substratum of the present Petition. The alleged default, if any, cannot be examined in isolation of the settlement terms, and therefore, the very foundation of the Section 7 application stands vitiated. 3.18. The documents already forming part of the record, including the OTS proposals, payment details, and previous proceedings between the parties, clearly establish a continuous course of settlement negotiations and execution thereof. 3.19. It is evident that the present Petition is not a fit case for admission under Section 7 of the Code, as the dispute between the parties stands effectively governed by the OTS arrangement and is in the process of being resolved & concluded. 3.20. It is prayed by the CD that this Tribunal may direct the petitioner to withdraw the instant petition and, failing to obey any such directions of the Ld. Bench by the Petitioner, the Ld. Bench may be pleased to dismiss the present Company Petition as not maintainable, recognize the binding nature of the One Time Settlement between the parties, and pass such further orders as this Hon’ble Tribunal may deem fit in the interest of justice and equity. 2.21 The CD has attached the following supporting documents along with the Reply: a) Copy of the NCLT Order / Report dt. 13.04.2026 in CP IB. 242/2026. b) Copy of the OTS proposal letter dated 12.03.2026 along with copies of post-dated cheques for INR 198 Lakhs given by the CD to FC. c) Copy of the OTS Settlement Approval Letter dated 18.03.2026. d) A copy of the details in chart format evidencing payments made till 16.04.2026 by CD to FC as per the OTS letter dated 18.03.2026. e) Copy of the Board Resolution and Vakalatnama. 4. The Hon’ble Supreme Court further held that, in the absence of any express provision prohibiting or prescribing a time limit for filing additional documents, the Adjudicating Authority is competent to permit such documents to be taken on record where the facts and circumstances so warrant. 5.11 Significantly, in the said decision, the Hon’ble Supreme Court noticed that the Financial Creditor had itself filed applications under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules seeking permission to place additional documents on record and that such applications were allowed by the Adjudicating Authority before deciding the Section 7 application. The Hon’ble Supreme Court found no illegality in the said course of action and affirmed the jurisdiction of the Adjudicating Authority to entertain such applications. 5.12 The CD has attached the following supporting documents along with the I.A.: a) Copies of the Loan Account Statements dated 28.05.2026. 5.13 It is noteworthy that there was no objection by the FC to take these documents on record. 6. ANALYSIS AND FINDINGS 6.1 We have heard the Ld. Counsels for the FC and the CD and have perused the records as placed before us. 6.2 The undisputed/admitted facts in this matter are: i. The debt arises from Facility Agreements dated 31.08.2015 and 31.08.2016, pursuant to which the FC had disbursed the loan facilities to the CD. The loan accounts were subsequently classified as NPAs. ii. The FC had earlier initiated CIRP against the CD, which was admitted on 12.01.2024 and was subsequently withdrawn under Section 12A pursuant to settlement between the parties. iii. The CD again committed default. A fresh Demand Notice under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act was issued on 28.01.2025 and received on 01.02.2025. The period of 60 days expired on 02.04.2025, and the FC has accordingly pleaded 03.04.2025 as the date of default. iv. The present Application was filed on 03.03.2026. Thereafter, on 18.03.2026, the parties entered into an OTS for Rs.1.98 crore, pursuant to which the CD made substantial payments which were received by the FC. v. The CD subsequently placed on record Loan Account Statements dated 28.05.2026, generated by the FC itself, in which the two subject loan accounts are reflected as “Live”. 6.3 The principal disputes which arise for determination are: i. Whether the subsequent OTS dated 18.03.2026 and payments made thereunder render the Section 7 Application non-maintainable. ii. Whether the reduction in the amount payable under the OTS to below Rs.1 crore takes the case below the threshold prescribed under Section 4 of the Code. iii. Whether the Loan Account Statements dated 28.05.2026 showing the accounts as “Live” negate the default pleaded by the FC. 6.4 By I.A. No. 3409 of 2026, the CD sought permission to place on record the Loan Account Statements dated 28.05.2026 pertaining to Loan Account Nos. 80600005455076 and 80600005456257. The documents sought to be introduced are stated to have been generated by the FC itself in the ordinary course of maintaining the said loan accounts and were generated prior to the matter being reserved for orders on 22.07.2026. 6.5 There is no dispute that the said documents relate directly to the very loan accounts which constitute the subject matter of the present Section 7 Petition. The CD is not seeking to introduce a new cause of action or to alter the nature of the proceedings, but seeks to place before the Tribunal contemporaneous account statements which, according to it, have a bearing upon the subsisting liability and the maintainability of the Petition. The fact that the documents were generated on 28.05.2026, i.e. well before the matter was reserved, is also material. The Application, therefore, cannot be regarded as an attempt to reopen the matter on the basis of material generated after reservation of the case. 6.6 The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Dena Bank (now Bank of Baroda) v. C. Shivakumar Reddy & Anr., (2021) 10 SCC 330, has held that there is no bar in law to the filing of additional documents in a proceeding under Section 7 of the Code, in the absence of any statutory prohibition or prescribed cut-off period. The Hon’ble Court recognised that the Adjudicating Authority is competent to permit relevant additional documents to be placed on record where the facts and circumstances so warrant. The said principle squarely applies in the present case. The statements sought to be produced originate from the FC itself and are directly connected with the issue this Tribunal must determine before admission of the Petition. Accordingly, we hold I.A. No. 3409 of 2026 to be maintainable. The Loan Account Statements dated 28.05.2026 are taken on record and are considered for adjudication of the present Petition. The evidentiary effect of the said statements, however, is considered hereinafter while examining whether the subsequent contractual events have any bearing upon the subsistence of the debt and default 6.7 Coming to the maintainability of the Section 7 Application, the first objection of the CD is founded upon the OTS dated 18.03.2026 and the payments made pursuant thereto. The OTS, however, came into existence after the filing of the present Section 7 Application on 03.03.2026. The jurisdiction under Section 7 was invoked on the basis of a financial debt which had already become due and payable and a default which had already occurred. The subsequent negotiations or settlement between the parties may have a bearing upon the amount ultimately recoverable or the manner in which the parties may choose to settle their inter se obligations, but they cannot retrospectively eradicate the fact that the statutory conditions for invoking Section 7 had already arisen, especially when the settlement reached between the parties has failed. 6.8 The position becomes clearer from the statutory scheme. Section 7(1) permits a financial creditor to initiate CIRP when a default has occurred. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in the recent judgment of B. Prashanth Hegde v. State Bank of India, 2026 INSC 155, has reiterated that the essential ingredients of a Section 7 application are the existence of a financial debt, occurrence of default and satisfaction of the statutory threshold. Therefore, the relevant enquiry is whether such debt and default existed so as to validly invoke the jurisdiction of this Adjudicating Authority. In the present case, the subsequent OTS was not the transaction pursuant to which the original default arose; rather, it was entered into after the default and after institution of the proceedings. The OTS, therefore, must be examined not as extinguishing the original default at inception, but as a subsequent contractual arrangement subject to stipulated conditions. 6.9 Further, the OTS itself does not amount to a complete discharge of the financial debt. The CD relies upon the payments made thereunder, but the OTS was admittedly not fully performed. Consequently, the subsequent payments constitute part-payments towards the liability and cannot be treated as extinguishing the entire debt or retrospectively nullifying the default which had already occurred. Even assuming that the OTS altered the terms on which the parties proposed to settle the outstanding liability, such subsequent contractual arrangement cannot, in the facts of the present case, be treated as defeating the cause of action which had already accrued to the FC on the date of filing, especially when the settlement reached between the parties has failed. More importantly, the OTS Approval Letter dated 18.03.2026 brought on record by the CD itself expressly provides for the consequence of failure to adhere to the stipulated payment schedule. The relevant clause reads as under: “However, in the event of any default in the payment schedule as mentioned hereinabove, Capri Global Capital Limited shall be at liberty to initiate/purpose/continue appropriate legal proceedings against you to recover the entire outstanding amount including charges and charges waived off and this letter shall be treated as null & void with immediate effect. It is to be noted that in case the settlement amount as agreed, is not paid on or before the stipulated date as mentioned hereinabove, the entire waiver as provided shall be withdrawn and the present settlement letter, so issued, shall be rendered null and void and your loan accounts shall be restored to its original position as on that month, i.e., March, 2026, as if no settlement is arrived. Kindly note that time is the essence of this settlement letter.” 6.10 The aforesaid stipulation is clear and unambiguous. The reduced settlement amount and the waiver/concession extended by the FC were conditional upon compliance with the payment schedule within the stipulated time. The parties expressly agreed that time was of the essence and that, upon default in the agreed payment schedule, the OTS would stand rendered “null and void with immediate effect”, the waiver would stand withdrawn and the loan accounts would be restored to their original position as on March 2026, as if no settlement had been arrived at. The contractual consequence of default is thus expressly incorporated in the OTS itself. The liberty granted to the FC to initiate, pursue or continue legal proceedings is a separate consequence and does not make the nullification of the settlement contingent upon the issuance of any further notice, declaration or cancellation letter. Once the stipulated condition was breached, the agreed consequence followed in terms of the OTS letter itself. 6.11 The CD had made substantial payments pursuant to the OTS. The material on record, however, shows that the last payment made by the CD was on 28.04.2026 and that no further payment was made thereafter. The OTS contemplated payment of the settlement amount in accordance with the stipulated schedule, including the balance amount payable on or before 25.05.2026. It is an admitted position that the entire settlement amount was not paid within the stipulated period. Consequently, the condition upon which the reduced settlement and waiver were granted was not fulfilled. In terms of the express condition contained in the OTS Approval Letter, the settlement stood rendered null and void with immediate effect, the waiver stood withdrawn, and the loan accounts stood liable to be restored to their original position as on March 2026. 6.12 The acceptance of payments pursuant to a subsequent OTS, particularly where the settlement remains incompletely performed, cannot by itself amount to an unequivocal abandonment of a statutory remedy which had already been validly invoked. There is no material to establish that the FC, by accepting the payments, expressly or unequivocally waived its right to pursue the pending Section 7 proceedings. On the contrary, the very fact that the OTS was subsequently entered into and remained only partially performed demonstrates that the liability continued to subsist. The payments made up to 28.04.2026 are required to be duly given credit in determining the amount actually outstanding; however, such payments cannot be construed as either a waiver of the default under the OTS or a revival of the settlement after its stipulated conditions were breached. In the absence of any material showing that the FC expressly agreed to extend the time or revive the OTS, the contractual consequences expressly agreed between the parties must prevail. 6.13 The CD contends that, after giving credit for the payments made under the OTS, the amount payable fell below Rs.1 crore and, therefore, the Application ceased to satisfy the threshold requirement. By Notification dated 24.03.2020, the minimum amount of default for initiation of proceedings under Part II was specified as Rs.1 crore. The Hon’ble Apex Court in B. Prashanth Hegde has expressly reiterated that, post the said notification, a default of Rs.1 crore or above constitutes the threshold for application of Part II of the Code. However, the contention of the CD proceeds on the assumption that the reduced liability under the OTS continued to remain operative notwithstanding the subsequent default. That assumption is contrary to the express terms of the OTS itself. Once the settlement stood rendered null and void and the waiver stood withdrawn in terms thereof, the CD cannot rely upon the concessional OTS amount for contending that the subsisting liability was below the statutory threshold. 6.14 The material consideration, therefore, is whether the statutory threshold was satisfied when the jurisdiction of this Adjudicating Authority was invoked. In the present case, the Section 7 Application was filed claiming an outstanding amount of approximately Rs.2.66 crore, substantially above the prescribed threshold, on the basis of the default pleaded by the FC. The subsequent reduction in the outstanding amount by reason of payments made after filing cannot retrospectively convert a validly instituted Section 7 Application into one which was below the statutory threshold at its inception. To hold otherwise would mean that the maintainability of a proceeding validly instituted under Section 7 could fluctuate merely on account of subsequent part-payments. 6.15 Significantly, the subsequent OTS itself was for an amount of Rs.1.98 crore. Thus, even based on the CD’s own case, the liability which was sought to be settled under the subsequent arrangement was substantially above the statutory threshold. The subsequent payments may reduce the quantum outstanding, but they do not erase the fact that the default giving rise to the Section 7 proceedings was above the threshold prescribed under Section 4. The decision in M. Suresh Kumar Reddy v. Canara Bank, (2023) 8 SCC 387, reinforces that once the Adjudicating Authority is satisfied that a financial debt is due and payable and that default has occurred, non-payment of even part of the debt constitutes default within Section 3(12), and the statutory consequence under Section 7 follows. 6.16 We also rely on the views of the Hon’ble NCLAT, New Delhi, in Devika Resources Pvt. Ltd. v. MAA Manasha Devi Alloys Pvt. Ltd., Comp. App. (AT) (Ins) No.938 of 2024 & I.A. No. 3418, 3419 of 2024 [(2025) ibclaw.in 354 NCLAT], for this issue, wherein it held the following: “18. It has been held in the aforesaid cases that the threshold has to be seen at the time of filing of the application and not at the time of the admission of the application.” Accordingly, the second issue is answered. 6.17 One of the objections of the CD is also founded upon the Loan Account Statements dated 28.05.2026, wherein the accounts have been described as “Live”. This circumstance, by itself, cannot negate the default which had already occurred. The expression “Live” is an accounting or operational classification of the account and cannot be treated as a legal determination that no default had occurred within the meaning of Section 3(12) of the Code. The question before this Adjudicating Authority is whether there was a debt due and payable and whether the CD failed to pay the same when it became due. It is not whether the account continued to remain operational in the books of the FC on a subsequent date. Further, the said statements dated 28.05.2026 cannot be read in isolation from the OTS Approval Letter dated 18.03.2026 and the subsequent failure to comply with the stipulated payment schedule. The contractual restoration of the loan accounts to their original position upon default is not negated merely because the FC’s accounting system continued to describe the accounts as “Live”. 6.18 The subsequent statements must, therefore, be read in their proper evidentiary context. They record the position of the loan accounts as on 28.05.2026, whereas the default relied upon by the FC had occurred prior thereto and the Section 7 Application had already been instituted. A later accounting description cannot retrospectively displace the contemporaneous documentary evidence of default, relied upon by the FC. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Dena Bank has also recognised the importance of documentary material and permitted additional documents to be considered in determining the existence of financial debt and default. Accordingly, the third issue stands answered. 6.19 The objection that the FC is merely seeking recovery of its dues also does not constitute a ground for rejection where the statutory ingredients of Section 7 stand satisfied. The existence of other recovery proceedings or security enforcement measures does not, in the absence of a statutory bar, extinguish the remedy available under the IBC. The nature of the proceeding under Section 7 is determined by the existence of financial debt and default, and not merely by the fact that the FC has also pursued other remedies. In the present case, the subsequent OTS does not alter the fact that the financial debt and default had already furnished the basis for invocation of Section 7. The subsequent contractual settlement, having failed in accordance with its own terms, cannot be relied upon to defeat the pending proceedings. 6.20 Thus, the subsequent OTS, the payments made thereunder, and the later description of the accounts as “Live” do not cancel out the fact that, as on the date of institution of the proceedings, the FC had established the existence of a financial debt and a default exceeding the statutory threshold. The subsequent payments can only be given credit in determining the amount outstanding, but cannot retrospectively invalidate the initiation of CIRP where the statutory requirements stood fulfilled on the date of filing. The position is further backed by the terms of the OTS itself, under which the failure to pay the settlement amount within the stipulated period resulted in withdrawal of the waiver and restoration of the loan accounts to their original position as on March 2026. Accordingly, the CD cannot simultaneously rely upon the concessional OTS amount for disputing the statutory threshold and disregard the very contractual condition under which such concession was granted. 6.21 In the facts of the present case, the OTS dated 18.03.2026 was a conditional settlement and not an unconditional extinguishment of the FC’s pre-existing liability. The CD made payments thereunder, the last such payment being on 28.04.2026, but failed to complete the stipulated payment schedule and made no further payment thereafter. The consequence expressly agreed under the OTS was that the settlement would stand rendered null and void with immediate effect, the waiver would stand withdrawn and the loan accounts would stand restored to their original position. The subsequent Loan Account Statements dated 28.05.2026 showing the accounts as “Live” do not alter this contractual consequence. The payments made by the CD are nevertheless liable to be duly accounted for and credited against the outstanding liability. 6.22 In view of the aforesaid discussion, the disputed facts are answered as under: i. The subsequent OTS dated 18.03.2026 and the payments made thereunder do not render the Section 7 Application non-maintainable. The OTS was entered into after institution of the proceedings and was conditional upon timely payment. Since the CD failed to comply with the stipulated payment schedule, the OTS stood rendered null and void in terms of its own express provisions, without the necessity of any further act or recourse for giving effect to such contractual consequence. ii. The reduction in the amount payable under the OTS to below Rs.1 crore does not take the case below the threshold prescribed under Section 4 of the Code. The OTS was a conditional concessional arrangement and, upon its failure, the waiver stood withdrawn and the loan accounts were contractually restored to their original position as on March 2026. In any event, the Section 7 Application was instituted on 03.03.2026 when the FC claimed an outstanding amount of approximately Rs.2.66 crore, which was substantially above the statutory threshold. iii. The Loan Account Statements dated 28.05.2026 showing the loan accounts as “Live” do not negate the default pleaded by the FC. Such description is an accounting/operational classification and cannot override the documentary evidence of default or the express terms of the OTS governing the consequences of non-payment. 6.23 Accordingly, the objections raised by the CD with regard to the subsequent OTS, reduction of the outstanding amount below Rs.1 crore and the subsequent “Live” status of the loan accounts are devoid of merit. The FC has established the existence of financial debt and default satisfying the requirements of Section 7 read with Sections 3(12) and 4 of the Code. Once such default is established, the scope of discretion available to the Adjudicating Authority at the admission stage is limited. The Application is complete in all respects. There is no disciplinary proceeding against the IRP. Accordingly, C.P. (IB) No. 242 of 2026 deserves to be admitted under Section 7 of the IBC, 2016. 6.24 We make it clear that at this stage we have not crystallised the amount as claimed in this Application; the same is left to be collated by the IRP.
