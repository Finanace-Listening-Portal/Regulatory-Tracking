Articles 2 min read RBI keeps further rate hikes on table as inflation pressures build The Reserve Bank of India’s Monetary Policy Committee minutes showed that the central bank kept the possibility of further rate hikes open, as members flagged rising inflation, broader price pressures and the risk of second-round effects. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Oct 21, 2026 at 07:42 PM IST var news_msid = 135094360; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India has left the door open to further rate hikes, with members of the Monetary Policy Committee flagging rising inflation, broadening price pressures and the possibility of second-round effects from supply shocks as risks that could warrant additional policy action.Per the RBI MPC minutes released on Wednesday, the duration and extent of the rate-hike cycle would be contingent on the actual growth-inflation developments and outlook, particularly underlying inflation, the broadening of price pressures, second-round effects of supply shocks and the impact of demand impulses. Advt Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta said a further increase in the policy rate could be required if higher input costs begin to pass through more significantly to output prices.“At some point, a larger pass-through of input costs to output prices may materialise,” Gupta said, adding that under such circumstances, “a further increase in the policy rate might be needed” to keep inflation and inflation expectations anchored.Gupta argued that the RBI should not wait for more definitive evidence of inflation becoming broad-based, given that monetary policy works with a lag. She said inflation was currently driven largely by external supply shocks, but the possibility of headline inflation reaching the upper tolerance limit warranted pre-emptive action.The MPC has projected CPI inflation at 6 per cent in Q3 FY27, before moderating to 5.7 per cent in Q4 and 5.6 per cent in Q1 FY28. The full-year inflation forecast was raised to 5.2 per cent from 5 per cent earlier.Governor Sanjay Malhotra also pointed to increasing signs of inflation generalisation. The share of commodities in the CPI basket recording inflation above 4 per cent rose to 37 per cent in August from 20 per cent in March, while core inflation increased to 4.2 per cent from 3.9 per cent in July. Core inflation excluding precious metals rose to 2.9 per cent in August.Malhotra said underlying inflation, measured by core inflation excluding precious metals, was also expected to move above target in the coming months, indicating a normalisation of inflation from the benign levels seen earlier. Advt The governor's assessment was echoed by other MPC members, who pointed to signs that supply-side pressures were increasingly feeding into broader price formation.Executive Director Indranil Bhattacharyya said there was increasing evidence of pass-through of input-cost pressures in segments such as restaurants and cooked meals. PMI surveys and producer-price indices also suggested some pent-up pass-through, with greater pass-through expected if demand conditions remain strong.Saugata Bhattacharya also argued that there was a strong economic case for monetary tightening, citing inflation risks and financial-market conditions. He said current and forecast real interest rates did not appear consistent with levels required to ensure price stability. Advt The next MPC meeting is scheduled for December 2 to 4, when policymakers will have more data on the trajectory of inflation and economic activity.
