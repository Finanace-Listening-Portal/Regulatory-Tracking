Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Karvy Data Management Services Limited Vs Employees State Insurance Corporation (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madras High Court, in Karvy Data Management Services Limited Vs Employees State Insurance Corporation, held that Employees’ State Insurance Corporation (ESIC) demands relating to the period preceding the commencement of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) could not be recovered from a corporate debtor following approval of its resolution plan under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC). Justice C. Saravanan disposed of two writ petitions challenging orders issued under the Employees’ State Insurance Act, 1948, including demands for contribution, interest, damages and consequential recovery proceedings. Applying the Supreme Court’s decision in Ghanashyam Mishra & Sons Private Limited Vs Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited, the Court held that pre-CIRP contribution and interest demands aggregating ₹33,14,018 were unsustainable. However, it permitted ESIC to recover damages and interest attributable to the post-CIRP period after separately quantifying the amounts payable. The petitioner, Karvy Data Management Services Limited, challenged orders issued by ESIC on 2 November 2025 and 4 November 2025, along with an attachment order dated 14 January 2026. The impugned proceedings concerned alleged statutory liabilities relating to the period from February 2011 to September 2024. These included ESI contributions under the statutory framework of the ESI Act, interest under Section 39(5), damages under Section 85B and recovery proceedings under the Act. The petitioner contended that the recovery proceedings were legally unsustainable because the company had already undergone insolvency resolution proceedings and its resolution plan had received approval from the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT). The Court recorded that CIRP had commenced against the petitioner on 15 September 2023 and that the NCLT approved the resolution plan on 13 December 2024 under Section 31 of the IBC. The disputed contribution demand amounted to ₹19,50,201, while the consequential interest demand was ₹13,63,817. The Court held that the aggregate demand of ₹33,14,018 attributable to the pre-CIRP period had to abate in light of Section 53 of the IBC and the principles laid down by the Supreme Court in Ghanashyam Mishra & Sons Private Limited Vs Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited and Others, reported in (2021) 9 SCC 657. Accordingly, ESIC could not enforce the pre-CIRP contribution and interest demands against the petitioner after approval of the resolution plan. A separate controversy concerned damages under Section 85B and interest under Section 39(5) of the ESI Act. The impugned orders referred to damages of ₹2,989 and interest of ₹7,063, covering periods partly preceding and partly following commencement of CIRP. The petitioner argued that the demands related to periods commencing as early as 1 February 2011 and were therefore affected by the five-year limitation contemplated under the second proviso to Section 45A of the ESI Act. According to the petitioner, the Corporation could not determine contributions for periods extending beyond the statutory limitation. ESIC opposed this contention and submitted that Section 85B of the ESI Act did not prescribe any limitation period for imposing damages. Reliance was placed on the Supreme Court’s judgment in Employees State Insurance Corporation Vs C.C. Santhakumar, reported in (2007) 1 SCC 584. The Corporation further argued that once statutory contributions became due and were determined, interest followed automatically and the employer could not avoid the resulting liability. The controversy therefore involved the interaction between statutory recovery provisions under the ESI Act, the limitation applicable to contribution determination and the effect of insolvency resolution proceedings under the IBC. After considering the submissions, the High Court distinguished liabilities attributable to the pre-CIRP period from those arising thereafter. It held that recovery of amounts relating to the pre-CIRP period was unsustainable in view of Section 53 of the IBC and the binding principles established in Ghanashyam Mishra. Regarding damages and interest for the post-CIRP period, the Court directed ESIC to quantify the amount attributable exclusively to that period before proceeding against the petitioner. The petitioner was directed to pay such quantified amount when demanded. The Court did not separately determine the petitioner’s limitation objection under Section 45A or conclusively adjudicate the competing submissions concerning limitation under Section 85B. The High Court accordingly disposed of both writ petitions, directing that ESIC could proceed only after quantifying the damages and interest payable for the post-CIRP period. No costs were awarded, and the connected miscellaneous petitions were closed. The decision highlights the distinction between statutory liabilities relating to periods preceding insolvency resolution and liabilities attributable to subsequent periods, while reaffirming the protection available against recovery of pre-CIRP claims following approval of a resolution plan under the IBC. Cases Discussed Ghanashyam Mishra & Sons Private Limited Vs Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited and Others (Supreme Court); Civil Appeal No. 8129 of 2019; 13/04/2021; (2021) 9 SCC 657. The Madras High Court relied upon this judgment to hold that pre-CIRP ESI contribution and interest demands aggregating ₹33,14,018 were unsustainable after approval of the resolution plan. The Supreme Court had explained the binding effect of an approved resolution plan and the extinguishment of claims not forming part of that plan. Employees State Insurance Corporation Vs C.C. Santhakumar (Supreme Court); Civil Appeal No. 4293 of 2002; 21/11/2006; (2007) 1 SCC 584. ESIC relied upon this decision in support of its submission that Section 85B did not prescribe a limitation period for levying damages. The Madras High Court recorded the reliance but disposed of the proceedings principally by distinguishing pre-CIRP and post-CIRP liabilities. The impugned orders have been issued to implement the order passed under Section 85-B towards damages, contribution under Section 45-A of the ESI Act, and interest under Section 39 (5) of the ESI Act, 1948. The dispute in these orders pertain to the period between February 2011 – September 2024. The relevant portion of the impugned order is extracted as below:- “ For the above reasons, I. Dy.Asst.Director, in exercise of the powers delegated to me for levy of Damages under Sec.85 (B) of the ESI Act, think fit and accordingly order that damages amounting to Rs.2989/- (Rupees Two Thousand Nine Hundred Eighty – Nine Only) for the wage periods from 01.02.2011 to 30.09.2024 be imposed and you as a Principal Employer are hereby ordered to pay the said damages within a period of 30 days from the date of this order, under intimation to this office failing which this shall caused to be recovered under the provisions of Sec.45-C to 45-I of the Act.” 3. There is no dispute that the petitioner company was proceeded under the provisions of the Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) and Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process was initiated on 15.09.2023 and a Resolution Plan was approved by the National Company Law Tribunal on 13.12.2024 under Section 31 of IBC, 2016. 4. As there is no dispute that part of the demand is for the period prior to CIRP proceedings, the demand of Rs.19,50,201 towards ESI contribution under Section 39 of the ESI Act and the consequential interest under Section 39 (5) for a sum of Rs.13,63,817/- is unsustainable. 5. In other words the total demand of Rs.33,14,018/- (Rs.19,50,201/- + Rs.13,63,817/-) towards the contribution and interest under Section 39 for the period prior to CIRP proceedings has to abate, in the light of Section 53 of the IBC, 2016 and in terms of ratio laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Ghanashyam Mishra & Sons Private Limited Vs Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited and others reported in 2021 (9) SCC 657. 6. The part of the demand towards damages under Section 85-B and interest under Section 39 (5) have been confirmed by the order dated 02.11.2025 and 04.11.2025 for a sum of Rs.2989/- and Rs.7063/- respectively, is also partly prior to the CIRP and partly towards the period thereafter. 7. According to the learned counsel for the petitioner, the impugned order pertains to period commencing from 01.02.2011 onwards, which are far beyond the period permissible under the second proviso to Section 45-A 8. A specific reference is made to the limitation under Section 45-A as per which no order shall be passed by the respondent Corporation in respect of the period beyond 5 years from the date on which contribution shall be payable. 9. On the other hand it is the contention of the learned counsel for the respondents that there is no limitation under Section 85-B for levy of damages. The learned counsel for the respondents in this connection has placed reliance of the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of ESIC Vs C.C.SanthaKumar reported in (2007) 1 SCC 584. 10. It is submitted that in view of the above there can be no limitation as far as the levy of damages under Section 85-B of the said Act. It is further submitted that once the contribution is due and determined, interest has to be paid automatically and liability cannot be avoided. 11. Having considered the submissions of the learned counsel for the petitioner and the learned counsel for the respondents and taking note of the fact that the amount involved insofar as the damages under Section 85-B of the said Act, for a sum of Rs.2989/- and interest under Section 39(5) for the period mentioned above for a sum of Rs.7360/- being meagre, I am inclined to dispose of these writ petitions by holding that the recovery of amounts due for the pre-CIRP period is unsustainable in view of the limitation under Section 53 of the IBC, 2016 and in the light of the ratio laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Ghanashyam Mishra referred supra. 12. As far as the payment of damages and interest for the period post-CIRP is concerned, the respondents are directed to proceed against the petitioner only after quantifying the amount due for the period post CIRP proceedings. The petitioner shall pay the same as and when such a demand is made. 13. With the above observation and directions these writ petitions are disposed of. No costs. Consequently connected miscellaneous petitions are also closed.
