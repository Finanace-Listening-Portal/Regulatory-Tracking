(Delhi High Court) Summary: Delhi High Court dismissed the Revenue’s appeals challenging the ITAT’s decision that assessments against Rolland Enterprises Ltd. were unsustainable for failure to issue draft assessment orders under Section 144C of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The assessee was incorporated under the laws of the British Virgin Islands and owned equipment relating to exploration and development of oil fields that was leased to Focus Energy Limited. Following search proceedings, assessments for AYs 2008-09 to 2011-12 were completed under Section 153C/144. The AO treated the assessee as resident in India under Section 6(3), holding that its control and management was situated in India. The CIT(A), however, found that control and management was with the Board of Directors, who were not residents of India. Before the ITAT, the assessee raised cross-objections that the mandatory procedure under Section 144C had not been followed because no draft assessment order was issued. The High Court held that the Revenue’s contention proceeded on the basis that the assessee was taxable as an Indian resident, but it was undisputed that the assessee was incorporated in the British Virgin Islands and was therefore a foreign company falling within the definition of “eligible assessee” under Section 144C(15)(b). Relying on Pr. Commissioner of Income Tax-7 v. Sumitomo Corporation India (P) Ltd., 2024 SCC OnLine Del 6125, the Court held that the AO was required to follow Section 144C. Finding no infirmity in the ITAT’s decision and no substantial question of law, the Revenue’s appeals were dismissed. Cases Discussed Pr. Commissioner of Income Tax-7 v. Sumitomo Corporation India (P) Ltd., 2024 SCC OnLine Del 6125 (Delhi High Court) — followed as covering the issue in favour of the assessee that compliance with the draft assessment order procedure prescribed under Section 144C is mandatory. It is stated that during the search proceedings certain documents containing information pertaining to the Assessee, were found and seized. Notices under Section 153C of the Act were issued to the Assessee, and assessments for the assessment years (AYs) 2008-09, 2009-10, 2010-11 and 2011-12 were completed under Section 153C/144 of the Act. 3.2 The Assessee is an entity incorporated under the laws of the British Virgin Islands. It is the Assessee’s case that it is an “eligible assessee” and the procedure as prescribed under the Act was not followed. According to the Assessee, it is an eligible assessee and therefore it was necessary for the AO to pass a draft assessment order and not to proceed straightaway to pass a final assessment order. The said issue is covered in favour of the Assessee by the decision of this Court inPr. Commissioner of Income Tax-7 v. Sumitomo Corporation India (P) Ltd.: 2024 SCC OnLine Del 6125. 6.The term “eligible assessee” is defined under Section 144C(15)(b) of the Act, which reads as under: “144C.Reference to dispute resolution panel. – ******** (15) For the purposes of this section,– (a) ********* (b) “eligible assessee” means,– (i) any person in whose case the variation referred to in sub-section (1) arises as a consequence of the order of the Transfer Pricing Officer passed under sub- section (3) of section 92CA; and (ii) any non-resident not being a company, or any foreign company.” 7. The first question projected by the Revenue proceeds on the basis that the Assessee is taxable as a resident of India. However, it is not disputed that the Assessee is an eligible assessee under Section 144C of the Act. There is no dispute that the Assessee is incorporated under the laws of British Virgin Islands. It is thus a foreign company. Since, the Assessee is an eligible assessee within a meaning of Clause (b) of Section 144C(15) of the Act, the AO was required to follow the procedure as prescribed under Section 144C of the Act. 8. We find no infirmity in the decision of the learned ITAT. Plainly, no substantial questions of law arise in the present appeals. The appeals are, accordingly, dismissed.
