Articles 5 min read UPI MDR could redraw fintech valuations as investors shift focus to monetisation, profitability: Investors Will UPI's new revenue stream bring investors back to payments, or will funding remain selective as fintechs face a sharper test on transaction mix, monetisation and profitability? Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Sep 17, 2026 at 02:42 PM IST var news_msid = 134307081; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook UPI MDR ?The introduction of MDR on select high-value UPI transactions could mark a shift in how investors assess India's payment fintechs, moving the conversation from transaction scale and merchant additions towards monetisation, unit economics and sustainable profitability. Investors highlighted the framework could improve confidence in the sector, but capital is unlikely to return indiscriminately.“The biggest impact of the MDR framework is likely to be on the quality of the payments business model rather than an immediate reversal of accumulated losses. For investors, the key change is that UPI now has a defined monetisation pathway, which improves visibility on unit economics and reduces one of the structural uncertainties around payments businesses,” said Sagar Agarvwal, Founder & Managing Partner, Beams Fintech Fund. Advt Surabhi Sanyukta, SVP - Investments, BlackSoil, said the revised framework is positive for investor confidence because it introduces a direct, transaction-linked revenue stream into a business that has operated under zero MDR for six years. However, she said the impact will differ significantly across companies depending on their exposure to eligible, higher-value P2M transactions.Investor lens shifts from UPI scale to monetisationThe change could be particularly relevant for listed payment fintechs, where the market has historically had to assess businesses operating at massive transaction scale but with limited direct revenue from the underlying payment rail.Sanyukta said the new framework could alter the parameters investors use to value listed fintechs.“It could also change how listed fintechs are valued, with greater focus on revenue per merchant, transaction mix and profitability rather than just transaction volumes or merchant count,” she said.That would put greater emphasis on the quality of a fintech's payment franchise. Companies with a larger share of high-value transactions and large merchants could have greater monetisation potential than those whose volumes are concentrated overwhelmingly below the MDR threshold.Ashish Fafadia, Partner, Blume Ventures said the fact that the industry was not budgeting for the MDR revenue makes the framework particularly significant for larger market leaders. Advt “The industry was never budgeting this and to that end it’s a windfall that will benefit larger market leaders much more than others due to the number of connector points they capture, merchants, partnerships and others,” he said.Agarvwal, however, cautioned that the creation of an MDR pool should not be equated with an equivalent increase in fintech profitability, since the revenue is distributed across the wider payments ecosystem and the framework applies only to a defined portion of transactions.“Over time, I would expect investors to place greater value on platforms that can convert this improved payments economics into higher-margin merchant and financial-services businesses,” he said. Advt ₹22,000 cr opportunity could strengthen investment caseThe potential size of the opportunity could nevertheless make a material difference to the investment narrative around payments.Agarvwal cited Bernstein estimates that a 40-basis-point MDR, if applied to around half of UPI transaction value, could create an annual revenue pool of about ₹22,000 crore by FY28.“The scale of the opportunity is meaningful. Bernstein estimates that a 40-basis-point MDR, if applied to around half of UPI transaction value, could create an annual revenue pool of about ₹22,000 crore by FY28,” Agarvwal said. Advt “That should not be read as ₹22,000 crore of incremental revenue for fintechs, because the pool will be distributed across banks, payment service providers and other ecosystem participants and the actual eligible transaction mix will determine realisation,” he added.Even a fraction of that pool, Agarvwal said, could materially alter the economics of an industry that has historically operated without merchant-level transaction revenue.For investors, this gives payments businesses greater visibility on the path from transaction activity to revenue. It could also make the distinction between a pure payment processor and a fintech with multiple monetisation layers more important.Funding could return, but investors will be far more selectiveThe impact could extend beyond listed companies to the private funding market, where payment fintechs have faced a more difficult fundraising environment as scale failed to consistently translate into payments revenue.Sanyukta said the biggest change for unlisted fintechs is greater visibility on monetisation.“For several years, UPI scale did not necessarily translate into payments revenue, which made investors increasingly selective and concentrated funding among a few scaled players. The new framework could improve funding prospects for differentiated businesses with meaningful exposure to higher-value P2M transactions,” she said.Investors, she added, are likely to examine the share of eligible transactions, revenue per merchant and the path to sustainable profitability more closely.Agarvwal similarly expects investor appetite to improve for businesses where payments can become a monetisation layer rather than merely a customer-acquisition tool.“For private fintechs, the more important change is that payments can once again become a credible monetisation layer rather than simply a customer-acquisition channel. That should improve investor appetite for businesses with meaningful payment volumes, strong merchant relationships and a clear path to monetisation,” he said.However, he does not expect a broad-based return of capital to the sector.“The sector has matured considerably, and investors will increasingly differentiate between businesses that merely process transactions and those that can use payments to build higher-value products across credit, financial services, commerce and merchant software,” Agarvwal said.But the investment thesis itself could become clearer.“The more important change is that payments now have a clearer path to recurring, transaction-linked revenue. I would expect capital to become more constructive towards differentiated payments businesses, but funding is likely to remain selective rather than broad-based,” Sanyukta said.Younger fintechs could see funding interest returnThe impact is also expected to extend to private payment fintechs, although investors are unlikely to treat MDR as a standalone investment thesis.Fafadia said the newer generation of payment fintechs could have a more favourable fundraising story as the sector gets a clearer monetisation pathway.“The newer players surely will have a favourable story to tell and there will be more interest for funding the younger startups,” he said.However, he added that investors would still examine the broader business model and revenue streams beyond MDR.“Investors will definitely be trying to understand the larger business model and the spokes of other revenue beyond share of MDR, partly because it’s too small and largely also because purely on the back of MDR, P&Ls will be negative still,” Fafadia said.
