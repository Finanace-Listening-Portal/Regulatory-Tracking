Articles 2 min read Budget 2027: MFIs seek one-year extension of credit guarantee scheme, dedicated funding and equity support Microfinance institutions have sought three funding-related measures in the upcoming Union Budget, including a one-year extension of the Rs 20,000 crore credit guarantee scheme, a dedicated fund and an equity funding mechanism to help smaller lenders access growth capital, people familiar with the discussions told ETBFSI. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Nov 19, 2026 at 06:13 PM IST var news_msid = 135991860; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman on November 18 chaired the fifth Pre Budget Consultation with stakeholders from the capital markets, banking, financial services and insurance sectors in New Delhi, ahead of the Union Budget 2027-28.The meeting was also attended by the Secretaries of the Department of Economic Affairs and the Department of Financial Services, and the Chief Economic Adviser to the Government of India.Microfinance institutions have sought extension of the existing credit guarantee scheme by another year, creating a separate dedicated fund to provide funding support to MFIs and introducing an equity funding mechanism to help lenders access growth capital, people familiar with the discussions said. Advt The first demand is for a one-year extension of the Credit Guarantee Scheme for Microfinance Institutions (CGS-MFI 2.0), which was introduced with a Rs 20,000-crore guarantee pool and subsequently extended by two months until August 31, 2026.ETBFSI had earlier reported that the scheme, aimed at improving credit flow to microfinance institutions through guarantee support to lenders, saw utilisation of only around Rs 8,500 crore of the Rs 20,000-crore corpus before its extended deadline.The industry has now sought another year's extension, particularly to ensure that smaller and mid-sized MFIs can benefit from the facility.The second proposal is to create a separate dedicated fund to provide funding support to microfinance institutions, which have faced difficulties accessing funds, particularly smaller and mid-sized players.Equity funding mechanism for growth capitalThe third recommendation relates to an equity funding mechanism that can help MFIs raise growth capital, particularly at a time when private equity interest in the sector has become more limited.The proposal envisages a mechanism similar to the existing India Microfinance Equity Fund (IMBF), which provides equity support to microfinance institutions. The industry has not proposed a specific corpus size for either the dedicated fund or the equity funding mechanism.
