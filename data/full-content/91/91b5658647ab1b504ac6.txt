} } Listen to This ArticleEven as clarity is awaited on the effective date of the proposed 40 basis points (bps) merchant discount rate (MDR) on person-to-merchant (P2M) transactions above ₹2,000 via Unified Payments Interface (UPI), banks — that are key players in the ecosystem — have indicated that the proceeds will be reinvested. This would help further expand the platform, acquire more merchants, and strengthen its security and technological capabilities, rather than add to their income or profitability. Last month, just two days before the scheduled implementation of the MDR on select UPI transactions on October 15, the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) deferred the levy until after Diwali. NPCI has not yet notified the new implementation date. The MDR is set at 0.40 per cent and capped at ₹300. Some categories of merchant transactions, including bill payments, fuel and education, among others, will attract a flat fee of ₹5. An MDR is a fee paid by a merchant to the bank and payment company processing a digital transaction. When asked how much revenue State Bank of India (SBI), the largest issuer bank and one of the biggest payment service providers (PSPs) in the UPI ecosystem, expects to earn from the proposed MDR on person-to-merchant (P2M) transactions above ₹2,000, chairman C S Setty said the levy should not be viewed as a revenue stream for the bank. “Let us wait for the final implementation of MDR. We should not look at MDR as a revenue item. It is more about the ability of the system to spend continuously and augment technology and security features. Even a small amount of MDR would be quite useful in strengthening the system,” Setty said at the bank’s Q2FY27 earnings press conference. Echoing similar views, Arnika Dixit, group head – wealth management, affluent banking, cards and payments at Axis Bank, said the bank was not in a position to provide specific guidance on the revenue it could earn from the proposed MDR, as its implementation had been deferred. “Whenever it is implemented, the bank is committed to using a part of the revenue to further grow the ecosystem by expanding its merchant footprint and meeting the infrastructure costs incurred in maintaining UPI,” Dixit said at the bank’s post earnings analyst call for Q2FY27. Axis Bank is the largest PSP in the country. A PSP bank is a bank that provides the technology and infrastructure to connect users and payment apps to the UPI network and facilitate transactions. For a ₹10,000 transaction, the merchant would pay ₹40. The acquiring bank would receive the ₹40 but pay ₹28 to the issuing bank, leaving it with ₹12. The issuing bank would receive ₹28 but pay ₹12 to the payer-side PSP, leaving it with ₹16. The PSP would receive ₹12 and pay ₹8 to the app provider, retaining ₹4. In other words, of the ₹40 generated as MDR, the acquiring bank retains ₹12, issuing bank ₹16, payer PSP ₹4 and app provider ₹8. In the UPI ecosystem, SBI is the largest issuer bank, followed by Bank of Baroda, HDFC Bank, Union Bank of India, Punjab National Bank, Canara Bank and Axis Bank, among others. Meanwhile, Axis Bank has a market-leading position in the UPI payer-PSP space, with a market share of around 38 per cent by volume. Brokerages have estimated that MDR will generate ₹15,000-20,000 crore in revenue for the payments ecosystem. In an interview with Business Standard, Debadatta Chand, managing director (MD) and chief executive officer (CEO) of Bank of Baroda, said the proposed MDR levy would generate some incremental revenue for the bank. He added that the proceeds would be reinvested in technology and strengthening UPI infrastructure to improve customer service and expanding the ecosystem. “We need to take UPI to the next stage, which will require investments in the ecosystem.
