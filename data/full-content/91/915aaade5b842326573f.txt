The outstanding gold loan portfolio surged 60.6 per cent year-on-year to Rs 23.3 lakh crore, reflecting rising demand, higher average borrowing amounts and increasing participation from borrowers in smaller cities, according to a report by CRIF Credit Information Services.The latest figures point to a market that is gradually moving beyond its traditional strongholds in southern India. While the southern states continue to account for the bulk of outstanding loans, Tier III and smaller urban centres are becoming increasingly influential in shaping the industry's growth.The expansion of gold-backed lending is becoming more visible outside established markets. Borrowers in Tier III and beyond locations increased at a three-year compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 16.1 per cent, reaching 3.7 crore between September 2023 and September 2026.Growth across the rest of India has also gathered pace, with the gold loan portfolio recording a substantial 96.4 per cent year-on-year increase in September 2026.Nevertheless, southern India remains the industry's largest market, contributing more than 70 per cent of the total outstanding portfolio. Tamil Nadu occupied the top position with Rs 6.9 lakh crore in gold loans, followed by Andhra Pradesh at Rs 3.7 lakh crore. Karnataka accounted for Rs 2.4 lakh crore, while Telangana and Kerala recorded Rs 2.1 lakh crore and Rs 1.7 lakh crore, respectively.Gold Loan Portfolio More Than Doubles In Two YearsThe scale of the industry's expansion becomes clearer when examining its overall portfolio. Outstanding gold loans climbed from Rs 10.7 lakh crore in September 2024 to Rs 23.3 lakh crore in September 2026, representing more than a twofold increase.The number of active gold loans rose 14 per cent year-on-year to 10.2 crore. However, the much faster growth in the overall portfolio suggests that higher average loan values have played a substantial role in expanding the market.Gold-backed borrowing is also gaining a larger share of India's retail credit landscape. It represented 12.6 per cent of the country's Rs 185.2 lakh crore retail loan portfolio, an increase of 3.3 percentage points compared with the previous year.Women's Participation And First-Time Borrowers IncreaseA broader borrower base is another important feature of the changing gold loan market. According to the report, the total number of gold loan borrowers rose from 3.7 crore in September 2023 to 5.6 crore in September 2026, translating into a three-year CAGR of 15.6 per cent.Women's participation expanded even more quickly. The number of women borrowers registered a CAGR of 16 per cent, reaching 2.4 crore over the same period. Women also represented 42.2 per cent of the outstanding gold loan portfolio, highlighting their growing presence in this segment.The industry is additionally attracting individuals with limited or no previous borrowing history. Approximately 45.7 lakh people who obtained gold loans during Q2 FY27 were classified as new-to-credit borrowers.Gold loan originations during Q2 FY27 indicate a growing preference for larger-ticket borrowing. The total value of loans originated increased 27.2 per cent year-on-year to Rs 8.15 lakh crore.However, the number of accounts moved in the opposite direction, declining 4.1 per cent to 369.4 lakh.
