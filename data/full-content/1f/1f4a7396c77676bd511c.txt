Summary : Carbon-credit accounting must begin by identifying the instrument. ICAI’s Guidance Note addresses self-generated Certified Emission Reductions under the Clean Development Mechanism; it does not automatically decide the accounting for every purchased credit, voluntary-market unit or credit issued under a different Indian or international scheme. Within its scope, a self-generated CER is not recognised merely because emissions have been reduced or verification work has started. Recognition occurs only when the CERs are credited by the UNFCCC so that they are unconditionally available to the generating entity, and only when the asset-recognition criteria are satisfied. The rights are then treated as inventory, measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value, with a narrow cost base linked to certification. For Tax Year 2026-27, section 194 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies a 10% rate to income from transfer of carbon credits and disallows any expenditure or allowance against that income. The statutory definition is tied to a one-tonne reduction validated by the UNFCCC. This guide keeps accounting recognition, operating-asset costs and the special tax computation in separate workpapers so that a fashionable label does not substitute for instrument-level analysis. The 2012 ICAI Guidance Note is explicit about its scope: self-generated CERs arising from a Clean Development Mechanism project. It does not deal with purchased CERs, use of credits in the entity’s own business, Joint Implementation or international emissions trading. A finance team should therefore obtain the registry statement, project documents, validation and verification record, serial numbers, transfer restrictions, contractual rights and applicable scheme rules before selecting an accounting policy. If the unit is not a UNFCCC-validated CER within the Guidance Note, management must identify the applicable reporting framework and develop a reasoned accounting policy using the relevant recognition, inventory, intangible-asset, provision and revenue principles. Describing every environmental certificate as inventory under the 2012 Guidance Note would overstate its scope. The accounting memo should state both why the guidance applies and where it stops. Certification is not a routine formality because the expected reductions may fail to produce issued units. Before crediting, the possible resource is treated through the contingent-asset logic rather than recognised as inventory. The Guidance Note identifies the recognition point as the stage when CERs are credited by the UNFCCC in a manner that makes them unconditionally available to the generating entity. Probability and reliable measurement still matter Crediting is necessary but the asset-recognition criteria must also be met. Future economic benefits should be probable, and cost or value must be measured reliably. Market evidence may include recent transactions, observable bids, binding offtake arrangements, transferability and registry restrictions. A model based only on an aspirational headline price is not adequate. The file should record the quantity, serial numbers, recognised date, available market evidence and management approval. Self-Generated CERs Are Inventory Under the ICAI Guidance Although a CER is not a physical good, the Guidance Note treats recognised self-generated CERs as inventory and applies AS 2. The balance sheet presentation should identify certified emission rights separately from raw materials, work in progress, finished goods and other inventory. The quantity ledger should reconcile opening units, units credited, transfers, cancellations or surrenders and closing units to the external registry. Cost is narrower than total project expenditure Many expenditures support an emissions-reduction project, but they do not all bring the CER inventory to its present location and condition. Research, project design, registration and general operating expenditure do not automatically become CER inventory cost. The Guidance Note identifies certification-related costs, such as relevant consultant fees and cash payments associated with obtaining the credited CERs, as the direct cost base. Tangible pollution-control or emissions-reduction equipment is analysed separately under the fixed-asset standard, while qualifying development expenditure for a distinct intangible asset follows the intangible-asset standard. Apply lower of cost and net realisable value At each reporting date, recognised CER inventory is measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value. Net realisable value is the estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business less estimated costs necessary to make the sale. The estimate should consider the specific vintage, registry, transfer eligibility, buyer market, taxes, brokerage and post-reporting-date evidence that confirms conditions existing at the reporting date. A later price increase does not justify an earlier optimistic carrying amount. Registry and accounting cut-off require special attention around the reporting date, especially when the sales contract is signed in one period but registry transfer or control occurs in another. Income-tax Act, 2025 Applies From 1 April 2026 For Tax Year 2026-27, section 194 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 governs the special rate. Table item 3 applies to any person whose total income includes income by way of transfer of carbon credits. The rate is 10%, and the Act states that no deduction for expenditure or allowance is permitted in computing that income. The remaining total income is taxed under the otherwise applicable provisions. Surcharge and cess, where applicable, require separate computation. The statutory definition is narrower than commercial usage Section 194 defines a carbon credit, for one unit, as the reduction of one tonne of carbon dioxide emissions or equivalent gases validated by the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change and tradable at the prevailing market price. The tax memo must therefore verify that the transferred unit fits the statutory definition. A credit from another registry or an emerging domestic scheme should not be placed under the 10% item merely because parties call it a carbon credit. If the definition is not met, the ordinary charging and computation provisions may need analysis. Accounting cost does not create a tax deduction under the special item Financial statements may recognise inventory cost and cost of sales. Section 194 nevertheless prohibits any expenditure or allowance in computing the income covered by its carbon-credit item. The tax workpaper should start with the accounting transaction and then show the statutory special-rate base, disallowed expenditure or allowance, and the treatment of other income. Book profit, taxable income and cash tax will therefore differ. Deferred-tax consequences depend on the reporting framework and facts and should be considered independently. Disclosure and Control File The Guidance Note calls for disclosure of the number of CERs held as inventory and the basis of valuation, the number under certification, and depreciation plus operating and maintenance costs of emission-reduction equipment expensed during the year. Management should additionally document significant judgments about applicability, recognition, market evidence and NRV where required by the broader financial-reporting framework. The tax note should separately identify the special-rate income and the no-deduction rule. A strong control file reconciles four populations: physical or registry activity, project monitoring data, accounting quantity and cost, and tax computation. Differences should be assigned and resolved. Registry access should use controlled credentials and independent review. Unit cancellations, retirement and surrender need documented authority because they may eliminate sale value without a cash transaction.
