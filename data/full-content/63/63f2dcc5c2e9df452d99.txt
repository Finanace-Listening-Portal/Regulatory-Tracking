Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Harpal Dhanda Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Purchases Cannot Be Rejected in Isolation When the Revenue Accepts the Sales & Stock Balances and Has Not Rejected the Books ITAT Deletes ₹3.55 Crore Bogus Purchase Addition as Sales and Stock Accepted Background of the Case The Delhi Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal allowed the assessee’s appeal against an addition of ₹3,55,63,965 for alleged bogus purchases. The Tribunal found that the supplier names and purchase amounts underlying the reopening were factually incorrect and that the Revenue had failed to reconcile the discrepancies demonstrated by the assessee. It also found that the Revenue had accepted the sales and stock balances and had left the books of account intact. In these circumstances, the Tribunal held that the purchases could not be rejected in isolation. The appeal arose from a reassessment order dated 22 March 2022, which the CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, upheld on 27 March 2026. The cause title identifies the assessment year as 2013–14, while the opening paragraph refers to 2015–16. The order therefore contains an internal discrepancy regarding the assessment year. Recorded Reasons Did Not Match the Purchase Records The Assessing Officer proceeded on the basis that the assessee had obtained accommodation entries aggregating to ₹3,55,63,965 from parties identified in 12 entries. The assessee produced documentary evidence showing that the purchases recorded from the parties in the list were only ₹2,12,33,120, spread across eight parties. The assessee’s written submission also pointed out that one supplier name appeared twice in the list. The difference was material. The recorded reasons attributed purchases to parties and in amounts that did not correspond with the assessee’s records. The assessee accordingly challenged the factual foundation of the reopening under section 148, besides disputing the addition on merits. The Revenue relied on the orders of the lower authorities. The Tribunal found that it could neither reconcile the differences in supplier names and amounts nor rebut the documentary evidence produced by the assessee. Accepted Sales and Stock Balances Cannot Be Ignored The assessee argued that the Revenue had accepted the books of account, sales and gross profit. The argument was that the sales could not have been made without the corresponding goods being available. The Tribunal found that the Revenue had nowhere disputed the sales and had accepted the opening and closing balances without rejecting the books of account. The Tribunal treated the purchases, stock and sales as connected entries. Once the Revenue accepted the sales and stock position reflected in the same books, it could not simply disown the purchases in isolation on the strength of allegations whose figures and party details remained unreconciled. The Revenue’s failure to answer the assessee’s evidence was significant because the addition rested on the very transactions that the assessee had disputed through its records. Assessee’s Argument Under Section 69C The assessee also contended that the purchases and corresponding expenditure were recorded in the books and that rejecting the purchases could not, by itself, justify an addition for unexplained expenditure under section 69C. The grounds of appeal refer to section 69C. Paragraph 8 of the order mentions section 69 when recording the addition and then refers to section 69C while summarising counsel’s argument. The Tribunal’s operative reasoning rested on the incorrect factual premises of the reopening, the Revenue’s failure to rebut the assessee’s evidence, and the acceptance of the sales and books. It did not lay down a separate rule that recording expenditure in the books, by itself, prevents an addition under section 69C. Supreme Court Decision Followed The Tribunal followed the Supreme Court’s decision in CIT v. Odeon Builders Pvt. Ltd. and allowed the assessee’s appeal. The result turned on the combined effect of two findings. The Revenue could not reconcile the alleged accommodation entries with the assessee’s actual purchase records, and its treatment of the purchases was inconsistent with its acceptance of the sales and stock balances appearing in the same books. The assessee had also raised grounds concerning change of opinion, disclosure in the original proceedings and the validity of approval. The Tribunal’s concluding findings focused on the factual discrepancies in the reopening and the treatment of the purchases. The order contains no separate findings on those other grounds. Author’s Comments The most useful aspect of this decision is the emphasis on party-wise reconciliation. In a bogus purchase dispute, a general assertion that purchases appear in the books may carry limited weight. Here, the assessee demonstrated a specific mismatch between the parties and amounts in the recorded reasons and those in the purchase records. That comparison gave the Tribunal a concrete basis to test the allegation and assess the Revenue’s response. The decision also shows why acceptance of sales and stock balances matters when the Revenue seeks to disallow the entire alleged purchase amount. Accepted sales establish that goods were available for sale. The identity of the supplier can still be examined on the evidence. In this case, however, the Revenue failed to reconcile the alleged supplier details or purchase values. The decision is strongest where the assessee can demonstrate both the defect in the underlying allegation and the consistency of the accepted trading records. Cases Discussed: Raychem RPG (P.) Ltd. Counsel to argued that the reason recorded by the AO on which he reopened the case u/s.148 of the Act is not correct even the amount and the name of the parties are not correctly brought in the reasons recorded by the AO. 7. It was further argued by the Ld. AR that the books of account, sales as well as amount of sales and gross profit of the appellant were duly accepted by the revenue. Accordingly, he argued that the appellant could not have made the sales without having made the purchases. 8. Therefore, it is not correct to call such purchases as bogus since sales have been accepted by the revenue of corresponding purchases, it should have been disallowed and lastly it was argued by the Ld.Counsel that the AO has made addition u/s.69 of the Act. It was argued that various judicial pronouncements have accepted the fact that once the purchase are reflected in the books of account and corresponding expenditure is also shown, the addition on the basis of the rejection of such purchases cannot be done u/s.69C of the Act. 9. We have heard both the parties and we have also considered the findings given by the authorities below. We have gone through the synopsis and other written submission filed by the Ld. Counsel of the assessee. We find that the facts on which the case was reopened by the AO are not correct as shown in the documentary evidence produced by the assessee as given above. It is also seen that the revenue has nowhere disputed the sales made by the assessee. If sales are not disputed and their opening and closing balance are accepted as the books of account has not been rejected by the revenue, then just part of the books of account i.e. purchases cannot be denied or rejected in isolation. 10. The revenue could not rebut this argument of the appellant. The revenue could also not reconcile the errors pointed out by the Ld. Counsel of the assessee regarding the names of parties from whom alleged bogus purchase were made or the amount of such purchases made. No rebuttal could be made by the revenue on these issues. 11. We have gone through the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court on these issues and respectfully following the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme court in the case of CIT Vs. Odean Builders (supra). Assessee’s appeal on this issue is allowed. 12. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 20.11.2026.
