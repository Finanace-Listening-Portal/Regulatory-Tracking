Summary : The Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules, 2025 introduced a purpose-specific test for whether one currency is exchangeable into another, an objective for estimating a spot exchange rate when it is not, and detailed disclosures about the restriction, rate, estimation process and risk. The amendment applies for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2025 and does not permit comparative information to be restated on initial application. The accounting question is not whether an official or published rate exists. Management must assess, at the measurement date and for the specified purpose, whether it can obtain more than an insignificant amount of the other currency within a normal administrative delay through a market or exchange mechanism that creates enforceable rights and obligations. If that threshold fails, the spot rate is estimated to reflect an orderly exchange transaction between market participants under prevailing economic conditions. This guide focuses on the evidence chain—purpose, amount, access channel, timing, enforceability, observable inputs, estimation governance, transition entry and disclosure—so that the conclusion is reproducible rather than driven by a preferred rate or a single post-period transaction. New paragraphs 8A and 8B require exchangeability to be assessed at a measurement date and for a specified purpose. Paragraph 19A supplies the estimation objective when a currency is not exchangeable. Paragraphs 57A and 57B require information that allows users to understand the financial effects, rate used, estimation process and related risks. Appendix A provides the application guidance. For a company with a 31 March year-end, the requirements are therefore relevant to financial statements for FY 2025-26 and continue into FY 2026-27. The amendment is narrower than a general foreign-currency accounting review but can affect monetary items, fair-valued non-monetary items, translation of foreign operations and presentation-currency translation. The analysis should identify the exact Ind AS 21 purpose before considering rate availability because a currency may be exchangeable for one purpose but not another. Directional test; do not assume reciprocity Exchangeability Is a Fact Pattern, Not a Rate-Screen Observation The existence of a quoted rate is not enough A published official rate, dealer quote or financial-data screen shows a price but not necessarily executable access. Management must determine whether the entity can obtain the other currency through a qualifying mechanism for the relevant purpose. A rate available only for a narrow class of transactions, subject to an unapproved allocation or through a channel without enforceable settlement rights may not establish exchangeability for the item being measured. Insignificant availability does not establish exchangeability Paragraph 8B treats a currency as not exchangeable when the entity can obtain no more than an insignificant amount of the other currency for the specified purpose at the measurement date. The assessment should compare obtainable amounts with the relevant need, not with an arbitrary entity-wide threshold. Management should document requested amount, executed amount, rejected or queued amount and restrictions that explain the gap. Splitting an order into small transactions should not be used to disguise the economic inability to obtain the currency. Direction and purpose can produce different answers The application guidance recognises that one currency may not be exchangeable into another even if the reverse direction is possible. Similarly, access for settling a permitted import may not establish access for dividend remittance, loan repayment or translation of a net investment. A matrix by currency pair, direction, purpose and date is more reliable than a single country-level flag. Estimating the Spot Rate When Exchangeability Fails Paragraph 19A requires an estimated rate that reflects the rate at which an orderly exchange transaction would occur at the measurement date between market participants under prevailing economic conditions. This is an objective, not a safe harbour. Management may use an observable exchange rate without adjustment when it meets that objective, or another estimation technique. The chosen input should be evaluated for timing, purpose, transaction characteristics, market depth and the effect of restrictions. Observable rates require a relevance bridge An observable rate might arise from a later transaction, another permitted purpose or a related market. The accounting paper should explain why the rate approximates the measurement-date orderly rate and whether an adjustment is necessary. A first subsequent exchange can provide evidence, but it should not automatically replace measurement-date conditions. Changes in controls, inflation, monetary policy, liquidity or market access between the dates may require adjustment or may make the later rate unsuitable. An estimation technique needs governed inputs and sensitivity Where no suitable observable rate exists, the model may use relevant market indicators and economic relationships, but inputs must be supportable and internally consistent. The file should identify the model owner, data sources, adjustments, calibration, approval and sensitivity to significant assumptions. The purpose is not to select the rate that produces the preferred earnings or reserve outcome. Independent review should challenge both the exchangeability conclusion and the rate because the two judgments can create a material effect. If the entity reports foreign-currency transactions in its functional currency and concludes at the initial-application date that the relevant currencies are not exchangeable, affected foreign-currency monetary items and non-monetary items measured at fair value are translated using the estimated spot rate at that date. The initial effect is recognised as an adjustment to opening retained earnings. When the entity uses a presentation currency different from its functional currency, or translates a foreign operation, affected assets and liabilities are translated at the estimated spot rate at the initial-application date. The transition rule routes the initial effect to the cumulative amount of translation differences in the relevant circumstances. Hyperinflationary functional currencies require separate attention to affected equity items. The transition journal should be tied to the population of balances, rate memorandum and the precise limb of paragraph 60M. Disclosures Must Explain Effects, Method and Risk Paragraph 57A requires information about the nature and financial effects of the currency’s lack of exchangeability, the spot rates used, the estimation process and the risks created by the restriction. A generic statement that management used an estimated rate is unlikely to explain the judgment. Disclosures should connect the restriction to affected transactions or foreign operations, identify material carrying amounts and rates, describe whether observable rates were used with or without adjustment, and explain significant inputs and assumptions. The note should be consistent with liquidity, going-concern, impairment, risk and foreign-operation disclosures elsewhere in the financial statements. If currency access constrains dividend remittance or debt service, the risk narrative should not be limited to translation mechanics. Conversely, the lack of exchangeability does not automatically prove impairment or going-concern uncertainty; those conclusions require their own standards and evidence. Audit File for a Purpose-Specific Exchangeability Conclusion Map every affected balance or foreign operation to a measurement date and specified purpose. Retain executable requests, settlement records, bank correspondence and legal-market restrictions. Measure obtainable amounts against the relevant need and document why any amount is significant or insignificant. Record the directional conclusion for each currency pair rather than assuming reciprocal access. Link the selected rate or model to the paragraph 19A orderly-transaction objective. Reconcile the transition entry or current-period measurement to the approved rate memorandum. Cross-check the financial-statement note against liquidity, risk, impairment and foreign-operation disclosures. Frequently Asked Questions When did the Ind AS 21 amendment become applicable? It applies for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2025 under the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules, 2025. Does an official exchange rate prove that a currency is exchangeable? No. The entity must be able to obtain more than an insignificant amount for the specified purpose within normal administrative delay through a qualifying mechanism. Can exchangeability differ by direction? Yes. Exchangeability from currency A into currency B does not automatically establish exchangeability from B into A. Can management use the first subsequent transaction rate? It may be relevant evidence, but it must satisfy the measurement-date objective after considering timing, purpose and changed market conditions. What happens to comparatives on first application? Paragraph 60M says comparative information is not restated. The specified initial effects are recorded in opening retained earnings or cumulative translation differences, depending on the fact pattern. What must be disclosed when a spot rate is estimated? Disclose the nature and financial effects of non-exchangeability, rates used, estimation process and related risks, with the Appendix A details relevant to the facts. Does non-exchangeability automatically create an impairment loss? No. It can affect measurement and risk, but impairment is assessed separately under the applicable Ind AS using its own evidence and requirements. Key Takeaways Assess exchangeability at each measurement date and for the specified accounting purpose. More than a quoted rate is required: access, amount, timing and enforceability must be evidenced. The estimated rate must meet the paragraph 19A orderly-exchange objective, not a preferred accounting result. Comparatives are not restated on initial application; the transition entry depends on the translation context.
