Advt “The weighted average lending rate (WALR) of SCBs on fresh rupee loans hardened by 8 bps... while it remained unchanged on outstanding loans. In contrast, the weighted average domestic term deposit rate (WADTDR) moderated by 28 bps and 2 bps for fresh deposits and outstanding deposits, respectively, due to a decline in bulk deposit rates on account of a surge in liquidity from mobilisation of FCNR(B) deposits,” the RBI said.The divergence between lending and deposit rates comes even as bank credit growth remained strong. Bank credit grew 18.1 per cent year-on-year as of September 15, 2026, compared with 10.4 per cent a year earlier. Credit growth was broad-based, with industrial credit growth more than doubling from a year earlier, supported by lending to large industries and continued buoyancy in MSME credit.Net inflows under non-resident deposits rose to USD 85.7 billion in August 2026, from just 1 billion a year earlier to USD 86.4 billion in net FCNR(B) inflows during the month, the RBI said.For April-August 2026, net inflows under non-resident deposits stood at USD 119.2 billion, compared with 5.6 billion in the corresponding period a year earlier. Indian central bank attributed the sharp increase to the surge in FCNR(B) deposits/The surge also changed the composition of non-resident deposits. The RBI said the share of FCNR(B) deposits in total outstanding non-resident deposits rose sharply in July and August, following the mobilisation under the special swap facility. Advt RBI data in the Bulletin showed outstanding FCNR(B) deposits at Rs 1.55 lakh crore at end-August 2026, up from Rs 68,284 crore at end-July. FCNR(B) flows during April-August stood at Rs 1.21 lakh crore, compared with Rs 620 crore in the year-earlier period.Liquidity impactThe FCNR(B) inflows also had a wider impact on domestic liquidity conditions. The RBI said the surge in liquidity surplus was mainly due to FCNR(B) swaps, prompting the central bank to conduct variable-rate reverse repo operations to manage liquidity.Money-market rates softened during the first half of FY27, with the RBI noting that liquidity conditions were largely supported by capital inflows under its swap facilities. Fresh certificates of deposit issuances also declined considerably, with the Bulletin attributing this partly to the mobilisation of FCNR(B) deposits and the resulting liquidity surplus. Advt Strong capital inflows supported by FCNR(B) deposits helped India’s balance of payments record a USD 45.4 billion surplus in August, according to the RBI.The central bank also noted that the rupee was supported by robust FCNR(B) deposit inflows in early September, although those gains were subsequently reversed as crude oil prices rose.
