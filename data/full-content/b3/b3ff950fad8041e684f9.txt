Articles 2 min read A month after RBI’s rate hike, banks start raising deposit rates as liquidity shrinks, Bank of India the latest to move Domestic banks are beginning to increase their deposit rates due to shrinking liquidity conditions in the market. Bank of India recently announced a rate hike on deposits for various maturities, including a special 500-day option. This decision reflects the growing cost of deposits in the banking system as demand for credit rises. Banking system liquidity has significantly decreased from its peak in early September. Joel Rebello ET Bureau Published On Nov 4, 2026 at 06:40 PM IST var news_msid = 135539762; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook About a month after the central bank raised policy rates for the first time in four years, domestic banks are starting to raise deposit rates-albeit marginally-as liquidity shrinks and demand for credit surges through the busy season.Bank of India (BoI) is the latest lender to raise its deposit rates which, unlike external benchmark-linked lending rates, would take time to head north despite the early-October start to the hardening cycle by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). On Monday, BoI increased its deposit rates for maturities between one and below three years by 10 to 35 basis points, becoming the second bank in the current cycle to hike rates. Advt One basis point is 0.01 percentage point.After the hike, one year deposit less than ₹3 crore will earn 6.60% interest, up from 6.50%, while one year to less than two years will earn 6.70% interest, up from 6.50%. The bank has also introduced a special 500-day deposit, which will earn 6.85%, from 6.50% earlier."The hike in deposit rates reflects higher cost of deposits in the system. Bulk deposit rates have gone up and the bank also has to adjust the liability profile in line with the higher interest rates," said a senior BoI executive. "On the whole, liquidity in the banking system has tightened as the advantages from the unprecedented inflows from the FCNR (B) scheme have started waning."Banking system liquidity has come off from a peak of more than ₹11 lakh crore early September to about ₹1.96 lakh crore currently as a result of bond sales and variable reverse repo auctions. The tightening in liquidity can be noticed in the higher rates banks are paying in the certificate of deposit (CD) market, which have increased to over 8% for the first time since early 2023, reflecting tighter systemic liquidity. Indian Bank had already hiked rates in select tenures late last month on two special term deposits of 555 days and 777 days.
