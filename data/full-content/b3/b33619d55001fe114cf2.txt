Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Jainam Rathod Vs State of Haryana & Another (Punjab and Haryana High Court) Summary: The Punjab and Haryana High Court, in Jainam Rathod Vs State of Haryana & Another, dismissed a second petition seeking regular bail under Section 439 read with Section 482 of the Code of Criminal Procedure in a prosecution initiated by the Serious Fraud Investigation Office (SFIO). The proceedings arose from Criminal Complaint CIS No. COMA/05/2019, CNR No. HRGR01-007022-2019, dated 18 May 2019, filed before the Sessions Judge-cum-Special Judge, Gurugram. The complaint alleged offences under Section 447 of the Companies Act, 2013, relating to corporate fraud, and Section 120B read with Sections 417, 418, 420 and 467 of the Indian Penal Code. The Court declined to reconsider its earlier decision rejecting bail, finding no sufficient change in circumstances to justify a different conclusion. The petitioner had previously approached the High Court through CRM-M No. 36693-2019, seeking the same relief. That petition was dismissed by a detailed order dated 13 November 2019, after consideration of the issues and submissions raised. In the second petition, the Court examined whether subsequent developments warranted reconsideration. It observed that, on the merits of the prosecution case, no ground had been demonstrated for departing from the conclusions reached in the earlier order. The petitioner’s principal additional argument concerned the prolonged period of incarceration. Counsel submitted that approximately two years had elapsed after dismissal of the first bail petition and that the petitioner had remained in continuous custody. It was further submitted that even the complainant had requested the trial court to segregate the cases of accused persons who were in custody, with a view to facilitating their trial, but that request had been rejected. According to the petitioner, these circumstances indicated that the trial was likely to take considerably more time, making continued detention unjustified. The High Court rejected this submission. It held that the mere passage of additional time in custody was insufficient, in the circumstances of the case, to justify bail. The Court attached significance to the serious proportions of the alleged criminal conduct and the fact that the earlier bail petition had already been adjudicated through a detailed order. Its reasoning was confined to the facts before it and should not be read as laying down an absolute proposition that prolonged incarceration can never justify bail. The petitioner also relied on subsequent orders of the Supreme Court granting interim bail to certain co-accused, including the principal accused. Counsel argued that the petitioner should receive similar treatment, particularly because his alleged role was comparatively limited. The High Court, however, distinguished the Supreme Court’s interim orders from final adjudications of bail entitlement. It noted that the Supreme Court had not finally granted bail to the accused persons concerned and was already seized of the matter for final consideration. Consequently, the High Court considered it inappropriate to reopen its own detailed final order merely on the strength of interim relief granted in related proceedings. Addressing the alternative prayer for interim bail, the Court found that the petitioner had not established a sufficient basis for claiming parity with the co-accused. It observed that the Supreme Court’s interim order did not disclose specific reasons enabling a meaningful comparison of the petitioner’s role with those of the persons granted temporary relief. Without an identifiable factual basis for such comparison, the High Court could not reach a definite conclusion that the petitioner was similarly situated. The Court therefore declined to grant interim bail merely because other accused persons had obtained temporary relief from the Supreme Court. Accordingly, the High Court dismissed both the second regular bail petition and the application for interim bail. The decision illustrates the importance of demonstrating material grounds for reconsideration when a successive bail application is filed after an earlier detailed rejection. In this case, the additional period of custody, anticipated delay in trial and interim bail granted to co-accused were held insufficient to warrant a different order, having regard to the serious allegations and the absence of a demonstrable basis for parity. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF PUNJAB AND HARYANA HIGH COURT This is a second petition filed by the petitioner under Section 439 read with Section 482 of the Code of Criminal Procedure seeking grant of bail pending trial in Criminal Complaint CIS No. COMA/05/2019, CNR No. HRGR01-007022-2019 dated 18.5.2019 filed by the Serious Fraud Investigation Office (respondent No. 2) before the Sessions Judge-cum-Special Judge, Gurugram under Section 447 of the Companies Act, 2013 and Section 120-B read with Sections 417, 418, 420 & 467 of the Indian Penal Code. A perusal of the record shows that the first petition i.e. CRM-M No.36693-2019 filed by the petitioner for the same purpose, was dismissed by this court vide order dated 13.11.2019; by passing a detailed order, dealing with all the aspects involved and argued in the matter. Now, the petitioner has filed the second petition for the same purpose. On merits, this Court does not find any ground to take a view different than the one already taken in the earlier orders. It has, additionally, been submitted by the counsel for the petitioner that after dismissal of the first bail petition, about two years have passed and the petitioner is in continuous custody since then, and also, that even the complainant had moved an application before the trial court for segregation of the cases of the persons like the petitioner, who are in custody, but even the said request has been declined by the trial court. Therefore, the trial is likely to take still longer time. Hence the petitioner should be granted bail. However, this court does not find this to be a ground sufficient to grant the bail. Mere passing of more time in custody cannot be a ground for grant of bail in this matter because of the serious proportions of the crime alleged to be involved in the present case. Although counsel for the petitioner has submitted that after the dismissal of earlier petitions, the Hon’ble Supreme Court has ordered release of co-accused on bail; including the main accused, therefore, the petitioner be also released on bail, however, the Hon’ble Supreme Court has not finally granted bail to any accused. The order relied upon by the counsel for the petitioner is only an interim order. Since this Court has already passed a detailed final order in the matter, therefore, this Court does not find it appropriate to re-open the matter only on the basis of an interim order passed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court, particularly, when the Hon’ble Supreme Court is already seized of the matter for final adjudication. The counsel for the petitioner has further argued that since the Hon’ble Supreme Court has released the main accused on interim bail, therefore, the petitioner; whose role is negligible as compared to the persons granted interim bail by the Supreme Court, be also granted interim bail. However, even this prayer of petitioner cannot be accepted for two reasons. Firstly, as mentioned above, the matter already stands decided on merits by a detailed judgment. So only for granting interim bail, the matter cannot be re-opened by this Court. Secondly, any specific reason; with reference to which the case of the petitioner could have been considered by this Court for assessing the parity of the case of the petitioner; on merits; with the case of co-accused who have been granted concession of interim bail, is not decipherable from the order of Hon’ble Supreme Court. Therefore, even for the interim bail, this Court is not in a position to come to any definite opinion qua merits of the case. Accordingly, finding no merits in the main petition and in the application for interim bail, the same are dismissed.
