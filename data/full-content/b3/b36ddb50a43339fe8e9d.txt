Summary : SEBI’s circular dated 30 October 2026 gives regulated entities additional time until 31 January 2027 for three connected digital-accessibility milestones: appointing an IAAP-certified accessibility auditor, conducting accessibility audits of digital platforms, and remediating the findings. The extension is not a general suspension of the accessibility framework. SEBI expressly states that all other provisions of the earlier circulars remain unchanged and must continue to be followed. Entities should therefore treat the revised date as a controlled completion window, not as permission to defer governance, platform inventory, vendor coordination, documentation or ongoing accessibility. This guide maps the extended items, identifies what remains outside the extension, and sets out an evidence-led completion workflow for stock exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories, registered intermediaries and the industry bodies and supervisory structures named in the circular. It also explains why an audit report alone is insufficient: management must demonstrate scope completeness, remediation ownership, retesting, exception handling and submission through the applicable reporting channel. It extends the deadline to 31 January 2027 for appointment of an IAAP auditor, conduct of the accessibility audit for digital platforms and remediation of findings from that audit. The three items form one practical chain. The auditor must be appropriately engaged, the full platform population must be tested, and identified barriers must be corrected and evidenced. The circular is addressed broadly to recognised stock exchanges, recognised clearing corporations, registered depositories, registered intermediaries, AMFI, APMI and BSE Limited in its supervisory roles for investment advisers and research analysts. An entity should confirm its reporting route under the earlier compliance guidelines instead of assuming that every report goes directly to SEBI. The extension changes the completion date for the named activities; it does not rewrite the allocation of reporting responsibility. The 30 October circular must be read with that sequence. The earlier framework required identification of investor-facing digital platforms, an accessibility professional, an initial audit, remediation, and continuing reporting. Because the latest circular says all other provisions remain unchanged, completed requirements should remain completed and supported by records. An entity should not withdraw, overwrite or silently replace earlier submissions merely because a later deadline has been granted for three activities. Platform Inventory Must Be Complete The audit population should cover every investor-facing digital platform within the applicable framework, not only the principal website. Depending on the entity, the population may include mobile applications, web portals, onboarding interfaces, transaction screens, investor-service modules, downloadable forms, authentication flows, complaint facilities and electronic documents. The inventory should record the owner, public URL or application identifier, business purpose, technology stack, vendor, release status and whether the platform processes time-sensitive investor actions. A Four-Stage Completion Plan Before 31 January 2027 Stage 1: Governance and Auditor Appointment Management should nominate an accountable executive and a cross-functional working group covering compliance, technology, product, legal, information security, procurement and customer service. The engagement letter with the IAAP-certified professional should define the platform list, testing standards, assistive-technology coverage, sampling, access to test environments, treatment of third-party components, severity grading, retesting and the form of the final report. Certification or competence evidence should be retained with the appointment record. Stage 2: Audit, Triage and Ownership Findings should be entered into a controlled register rather than left only in the auditor’s report. Each entry should identify the platform, page or journey, accessibility barrier, severity, affected user group, applicable requirement, responsible owner, target date and dependency. The register should distinguish a design defect from a content defect, a code defect, a third-party limitation and a document-accessibility failure. This classification makes remediation measurable and prevents repeated fixes that address symptoms rather than the source component. Stage 3: Remediation and Retesting Closing a development ticket is not proof that an accessibility issue has been resolved. The corrected version should be deployed to the relevant environment and retested using the method appropriate to the original finding. Evidence should include the version number, deployment date, screenshots where useful, automated test output, manual keyboard checks, screen-reader results and auditor confirmation for material findings. Regression testing is important where a shared component, design system or document template has been changed. Stage 4: Approval and Submission Before the deadline, the compliance owner should reconcile the platform inventory to the audit scope and the audit findings to the remediation register. Open matters should be explained, risk-assessed and routed through the escalation process. The final submission package should contain the prescribed report, management approval, relevant annexures and evidence of transmission to the correct authority. Signed submission and acknowledgement Draft report exists but no proof of filing Special Attention for Outsourced and Frequently Changing Platforms Outsourcing technology or content does not remove the regulated entity’s responsibility for compliance. Contracts with application developers, cloud providers, website vendors, registrars, KYC service providers and document-generation vendors should support audit access, correction timelines and evidence sharing. Where the vendor controls a common component used by several journeys, the entity should verify that the corrected component is deployed across every relevant instance. Vendor assurances should be tested against actual production behaviour. Digital platforms change continuously. A report based on an old build may not establish accessibility of the version available to investors on 31 January 2027. Release-management controls should flag accessibility-sensitive changes, require testing before deployment and preserve the build identifier reviewed by the auditor. Urgent patches after the audit should be screened for regression risk. New investor journeys launched during the extension window should be added to the inventory and assessed rather than deferred to the next annual cycle. Board and Senior Management Reporting A concise dashboard should report the number of in-scope platforms, audit completion, findings by severity, remediation progress, retest completion, vendor dependencies and submission readiness. Percent-complete measures must have clear denominators. For example, “90% remediated” is meaningful only if the total finding population is stable and retesting is included. Management should separately identify any high-impact barrier affecting login, onboarding, payment, order placement, redemption, complaint filing or access to mandatory disclosures. Frequently Asked Questions What is the revised SEBI deadline? The revised deadline is 31 January 2027 for appointment of the IAAP auditor, completion of the accessibility audit and remediation of audit findings. Does the circular postpone every digital-accessibility obligation? No. SEBI states that all other provisions of the referenced circulars remain unchanged and must be complied with. Can an entity audit only its main website? The scope should follow the earlier framework and the entity’s complete investor-facing platform inventory, which may include apps, portals, forms and digital documents. Is closing a technology ticket sufficient evidence? No. The correction should be deployed and retested, with evidence tied to the relevant platform version and original finding. Who should receive the compliance submission? The reporting route depends on the entity type and the mechanism prescribed in the earlier SEBI accessibility guidelines and relevant supervisory arrangements. How should third-party platform issues be handled? They should be included in the audit population, assigned to an accountable owner, contractually escalated and retested after remediation. What should management monitor until January 2027? Management should monitor auditor appointment, scope completeness, findings, remediation, retesting, unresolved dependencies and evidence of timely submission. Key Takeaways 31 January 2027 applies to the three activities expressly named in SEBI’s 30 October 2026 circular. Earlier completed requirements and all unaffected provisions remain operative. A complete platform inventory is the foundation for a defensible accessibility audit. Remediation requires deployment, retesting and evidence; a closed ticket alone is inadequate.
