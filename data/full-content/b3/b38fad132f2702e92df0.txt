Summary : YES Bank Limited’s official fixed-deposit page, checked on 7 November 2026, displays resident callable fixed-deposit rates effective 2 June 2026 for deposits below ₹3 crore. The highest regular rate in that table is 7.25% per annum for 18 months 1 day to less than 24 months; the eligible resident senior-citizen rate for the same band is 7.75%. Rates vary materially by exact tenure, with annualised yields separately displayed for cumulative treatment. Interest may be paid quarterly, reinvested with quarterly compounding or paid monthly at a discounted value. Premature withdrawal is permitted, but the bank recalculates interest using the rate available on the booking date for the period actually completed and applies the relevant penalty; under the current published penalty table, eligible senior citizens booking or renewing from 16 May 2022 are exempt from that penalty. Interest remains taxable under the Income-tax Act, 2025, and DICGC insurance is capped at ₹5 lakh for principal and interest together per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity. This is a neutral verification guide, not a recommendation or ranking. Payout Choice Changes Cash Flow and Yield Quarterly and Reinvestment Options The bank states that interest may be paid quarterly or compounded quarterly under the reinvestment option. The displayed annualised yield reflects compounding assumptions and should not be read as the nominal coupon. A cumulative maturity value depends on principal, exact tenure, day count, compounding, tax deduction and any premature closure. The receipt and maturity instruction are more reliable than a marketing headline. Monthly Interest Is Discounted Monthly payout is available at a discounted value because interest is paid before the standard quarterly interval. A depositor who needs periodic cash flow should compare the bank’s actual monthly payout quote with the cumulative option. Multiplying the nominal annual rate by principal and dividing by twelve will not necessarily match the credited amount. Short Tenures Use Simple Interest For deposits below six months and one day, the bank’s published terms state that interest is calculated at maturity as simple interest. This makes the annualised-yield column less useful for estimating a short-tenure cash amount. Booking date, maturity date and actual day count should be used. Premature Withdrawal Reprices the Deposit YES Bank permits premature withdrawal of a resident callable fixed deposit. Interest is recalculated at the rate that prevailed on the original booking date for the period for which the deposit actually remained with the bank, and then the applicable penalty is considered. The contracted long-tenure rate does not simply run until the closure date. The depositor should request a pre-closure quotation showing the completed tenure, base rate and penalty. Income-Tax Treatment for Tax Year 2026-27 Interest Remains Taxable Even Without TDS Fixed-deposit interest is taxable according to the depositor’s status and applicable head of income. TDS is only a collection mechanism. If the bank does not deduct because the statutory threshold is not crossed or a valid declaration applies, the interest may still be taxable and reportable. Cumulative deposits can create annual accrual and information-reporting entries before cash maturity. The bank interest certificate, Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement should be reconciled to the return or books. Section 393 Replaces the Old Section Reference For a credit or payment event on or after 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 governs withholding. Section 393 consolidates non-salary TDS. For interest other than securities paid by a banking company, the statutory threshold is ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen. The threshold is not a tax exemption. The applicable rate, PAN consequences and declaration process must be checked under the current Finance Act, rules and portal instructions. Some YES Bank webpage language still refers to legacy section numbers from the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income Tax Department’s transition FAQ states that old provisions govern events up to 31 March 2026, while transactions from 1 April 2026 use the 2025 Act and the relevant section 393 table item. Depositors and bank teams should follow the current statute rather than copying an obsolete section label into a 2026-27 record. DICGC Cover Is ₹5 Lakh and Bank-Wide YES Bank appears on the RBI list of scheduled private-sector banks and on the DICGC insured-bank list checked for this article. DICGC protection is limited to ₹5 lakh for principal and accrued interest together per depositor per bank in the same right and same capacity. Savings, current, recurring and fixed deposits across all branches of the same bank are aggregated for that capacity. Multiple receipts or channels do not multiply the limit. Different rights and capacities may receive separate treatment under DICGC rules, but the legal ownership pattern controls. A sole-proprietor balance is aggregated with the proprietor’s individual balance. Joint accounts with the same names in the same order are aggregated together. Insurance applies through the statutory mechanism upon a covered bank event; it is not immediate liquidity protection and does not cover amounts above the ceiling.
