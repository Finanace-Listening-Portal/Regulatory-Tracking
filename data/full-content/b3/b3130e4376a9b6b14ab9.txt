Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Kalyan Jewellers India Ltd Vs Commissioner of Central Tax and Central Excise (CESTAT Bangalore) Summary: The Bangalore Bench of the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT) allowed the appeal of Kalyan Jewellers India Ltd. against a service tax demand arising from advertisement cost sharing, employee deputation and alleged works contract services. The Tribunal set aside the Commissioner’s order dated 24 March 2023, holding that the extended limitation period could not be invoked on the facts of the case. The proceedings arose from a show cause notice issued on 30 December 2020 for transactions relating to April 2014 to June 2017. The assessee had recovered part of its advertising expenses and actual salaries of deputed employees from its associated company, Kalyan Jewellers Mini Stores Pvt. Ltd. The Department treated the receipts as consideration for business promotion and manpower supply, and also alleged liability under reverse charge for works contracts. On limitation, the Tribunal observed that the notice was founded on the assessee’s audited financial statements and accounting records, which had been subject to departmental audits. The accounting treatment of salary reimbursements did not, by itself, establish wilful suppression with intent to evade tax. Accordingly, the extended period was unavailable. On works contract services, the Tribunal found no evidence of an actual works contract. The assessee had furnished documents supporting labour payments for showroom renovation and interior work, while materials were procured by it. The works contract demand was therefore unsustainable on merits. Regarding advertisement expenditure, the Tribunal relied on decisions addressing common expenditure and shared advertising costs. It accepted that sharing expenses incurred on advertisements through third-party vendors did not, on these facts, establish a taxable business promotion service rendered by the appellant to its associate. The Tribunal took a different view on the merits of employee deputation. Applying the Supreme Court ruling in Northern Operating Systems, it considered the manpower supply demand justified in principle. Nevertheless, because the demand for April 2014 to June 2017 was raised through the notice of 30 December 2020 using the extended period, it was held unsustainable on limitation. The appeal was allowed with consequential relief, if any. Cases Discussed: Collector of Central Excise Vs. FULL TEXT OF THE CESTAT BANGALORE ORDER The issue in the present appeal is demand of service tax under the category of ‘Business Promotion Service’, ‘Manpower Supply Service’ and ‘Works contract service” under reverse charge mechanism (RCM). 2. The brief facts are alleging short payment of service tax, proceedings were initiated and show cause notice was issued on 30.12.2020. Thereafter Adjudication authority as per the impugned order confirmed the demands under various service categories along with interest and also imposed penalty under various provisions of Finance Act, 1994. Aggrieved by said order, present appeal is filed. 3. When the appeal came up for hearing, as regards demand under the category of ‘manpower service’, Learned Consultant submits that M/s. Kalyan Jewellers Mini Stores Private Limited (KJMSPL/ KJMS) is an associated concern of the Appellant. Learned Authorized Representative (AR) reiterated the finding in the impugned order and submits that M/s. Kalyan Jewellers Mini Stores Private Limited (KJMSPL/ KJMS) is an associated concern of the Appellant. KJMSPL deals exclusively in jewellery manufactured by the Appellant. Appellant supplied manpower to KJMSPL and the amounts received has not been appropriated as income in the Profit & Loss Account, instead the same has been credited to salary expenses. With effect from 01.07.2012, levy and collection of service tax is governed by Section 66B of the Finance Act, 1994. As per this Section, service tax shall be levied on the value of all services, other than those services specified in the negative list, provided or agreed to be provided in the taxable territory by one person to another and collected in such manner as may be prescribed. Section 65B (44) of the Act defines service as “any activity carried out by a person for another for consideration, and includes a declared service, the assessee has received certain amount as consideration for promoting their business”. As per Section 65B (51) of the Act, taxable service means any service on which service tax is leviable under Section 66B. It, therefore, appears that the activities of business promotion undertaken by the assessee by permitting their client to be benefited from their advertisement/ business promotion activities, falls within the ambit of “taxable service” in terms of Section 65B(51) of the Act. In the instant case, the appellant has received certain amount as consideration for promoting their customers’ business by transferring benefits of their advertisement/ promotion works. It appears that, by agreeing to utilise the benefits of their advertisement/ business promotion activity, the appellant is actually promoting the business of their client i.e. M/s Kalyan Jewellers Mini Stores Private Limited and adjudication authority rightly confirmed the demand. 12. As regards the demand confirmed by invoking extended period of limitation, we find that as per the show cause notice, it is admitted that it is based on the verification of audited balance sheet and profit and loss account of the company during the financial year. Appellant’s books of account was periodically audited by the Central Excise audit team and when pointed out that appellant had not discharged service tax on works contract services received by them under RCM during very same period, it was paid by them with interest. Further as per the submission made by the appellant they were of the bonafide belief and understanding that secondment of employees and cost sharing of advertisement expenses is not a taxable service as it is nothing but reimbursement of expenses. 18. The entire show cause notice is based on Appellant’s own records. When all the transactions are duly recorded by the Appellant and when information to issue a show cause notice is gathered from such records, no allegation can be made that Appellant had indulged in willful suppression of facts with an intent to evasion of payment of tax. The issue was considered by this Tribunal in the matter of M/s. Creative Micro System vs CC, Bangalore (2016 (206) E.L.T 922 (Tri. Bang) wherein it is held that when details are taken from the records of the assessee, there is no intention to suppress the facts. Accounting the amounts received in lieu of provision of services under the head salary cannot be considered as a willful suppression of fact and that as non-furnishing of details. In this regard, we find that as per the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the matter of M/s. Anand Nishikawa Co. Ltd Vs. Commissioner of Central Excise, Meerut -2005 (188) E.L.T 149 (S.C), mere failure to declare the facts does not amount to willful suppression of facts. Thus, the impugned order confirming demand by invoking extended period of limitation is unsustainable. 19. As regards issue on merit, we find that demand under the category of ‘works contract services’, there is no evidence on record to prove that the appellant had entered into any works contract service. Further the appellant had produced documents related to labour charges and there is no evidence to show that it is towards any works contract service. Thus, demand under the category of works contract services is also unsustainable. As regards the demand under advertisement, issue was considered by the Tribunal in the matter of M/s. Ruchi Strips (Supra) wherein it is held that:- “6. Regarding a tax liability under “Business Auxiliary Services”, I find that the appellant were getting certain considerations from their sister concern towards common pool expenses, dividend, refund of bond money etc. The appellant pleaded that there are common expenses for facilities like canteen, transportation within their group companies. There is no promotional activities involved in sharing the expenditure. I note that the impugned order simply recorded that all these services tantamount to provisions of services on behalf of the clients and accordingly, the appellants are liable to tax under “Business Auxiliary Services”. It is not clear as to what type of service is being provided by the appellant and on whose behalf. There is no third party involved at all, in the whole transaction. Certain common expenditure towards various facilities like canteen, transportation, etc. were shared among group companies. Such arrangement cannot be considered as activities taxable under “Business Auxiliary Services”. In terms of Section 65(19) of the Finance Act, 1994, there should be either promotion or marketing of service or goods and various other auxiliary support services. In the arrangement, as discussed above, for availing certain facilities, the appellant is sharing expenditure with other group companies. There is no promotion activities in such arrangement. The impugned order did not discuss the legal scope of tax entry applicable to the case in hand and the same is not sustainable”. 20. Further we find that Tribunal in the matter of GR Thangamaligai (supra) held that: “14. When we examine the admitted facts pertaining to the arrangement between the Appellant and WGC what is discernible is an understanding to collaborate and work jointly to promote the sale of 22 karat gold jewellery by means of advertisement in the media. The fact that the expenditure incurred is being shared between both the parties at a specified percentage as can be gleaned from the agreement fixing the contribution of WGC to the extent of 25% of the ‘Total Spends’ is indicative only of a cost sharing in a joint effort at promotion of such jewellery that is intended to pay dividend to both parties to the agreement. The fact that the Appellant is required to provide the invoice of the vendor who undertakes the activity of such promotion advertising in order for WGC to part with its contribution is indicative of the fact that the Appellant is not providing any service to WGC, rather the expenses incurred in such promotional advertising by engaging the services of a vendor who provides the service of advertisement, is merely being agreed to be shared, with the WGC requiring proof by nature of the invoice of the vendor to release its share of the contribution towards the common cause. Thus, the provision of a sponsorship service as per the definition reproduced above by the appellant to WGC, and that too for a consideration, is absent in the said arrangement. 15. In the aforesaid circumstances, we are of the considered opinion that the Appellant does not render any service, much less the alleged services of sponsorship, when they jointly work in terms of the said Co-Op Agreement. The amount received by the appellant is not a consideration for any service and is clearly the share of the expenses incurred for the promotional advertisement services availed from a third party. Our aforesaid view is also fortified by the decision in Hindustan Construction Company Ltd v. Commissioner of CGST, (2025) 27 Centax 299 (Tri-Bom) which is seen affirmed by the Honourable Supreme Court as reported in Commissioner of CGST, Navi Mumbai v. Hindustan Construction Company Ltd, (2025) 27 Centax 300 (SC)”. 21. Further in the matter of SK Cars India Pvt. Ltd (supra) it is held that: “4. The other amount which has been subjected to service tax is the reimbursement expenses received towards advertisement charges. The expenses for advertisement which were borne by the appellant was reimbursed by Sundaram finances as well as M/s. Maruti Udyog Pvt. Ltd. The scheme of joint advertisement was adopted in order to reduce the expenses in regard to the advertisement for the sale of cars and loan facility. These expenses were reimbursed by M/s. Maruti Udyog Pvt. Ltd. as well as M/s. Sundaram Finances Ltd. The appellant has not received any consideration and only the expenses were reimbursed. The judgment of the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Union of India v. M/s. Intercontinental Consultants and Technocrats Pvt. Ltd. 2018 (3) TMI 357 (SC) = 2018 (10) G.S.T.L. 401 (S.C.) was relied to argue that reimbursable expenses are not subject to levy of service tax prior to 2015. The decision in the case of Electronics Technology Parks v. CCE and Trivandrum 2021 (7) TMI 1095 = 2022 (56) G.S.T.L. 182 (Tri. – Bang.) was relied to support the above arguments. The Ld. Consultant prayed that the appeal may be allowed. 11. A further demand has been made under the category of Business Auxiliary Services on the reimbursable expenses received by the appellant from M/s. Maruti Udyog Ltd. and M/s. Sundaram Finance Ltd. As per the annexure to the Show Cause Notice is seen that these are nothing but reimbursement of expense for advertisement. The Honourable Supreme Court in the case of Intercontinental Technocrats Ltd. (supra) has held that the reimbursable expenses cannot be subject to levy of service tax. The decision relied by the Ld. AR for the Department has not considered the decision of the Hon’ble Apex Court and hence not applicable. 22. As regards demand under the category of ‘manpower service’, we find that though the appellant is claiming such receipts are reimbursable expenses which is not taxable and relied on catena of decisions to support their claims, we find that issue was considered by Hon’ble Supreme Court in the matter of CC, CE & ST, Bangalore (Adjudication) Vs. Northern Operating Systems Pvt. Ltd. (Supra) wherein it is categorically held that secondment of employees by overseas entity for purpose of completion of assessee’s job amounts to manpower supply. Following the ratio of the above judgment, adjudication authority rightly confirmed the demand under the category of manpower service. However, we find that in the said judgment, Hon’ble Supreme Court set aside the demand by invoking extended period of limitation. Since the demand in the present appeal under the category of ‘manpower service’ is confirmed, invoking extended period covering the period April 2014 to June 2017, whereas the Show Cause Notice (SCN) was issued on 30.12.2020, the said demand is also unsustainable on limitation. 23. In view of the above discussion, the impugned order is unsustainable and liable to be set aside. Accordingly, the impugned order is set aside, and the appeal is allowed with consequential relief, if any, in accordance with law.
