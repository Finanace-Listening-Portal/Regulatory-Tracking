Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Anoopama Shah Vs Bosch Home Comfort India Limited (NCLT Ahmedabad) Summary: The NCLT Ahmedabad allowed Anoopama Shah’s appeal for rectification of the register of members in respect of 250 shares under Folio No.007448. The opening pronouncement sheet identifies the company as Jonson Control-Hitachi Air Condition India Ltd, while the amended memo of parties in the detailed order identifies Respondent No.1 as Bosch Home Comfort India Limited, formerly Johnson Controls Hitachi Air Conditioning India Limited. The appellant stated that she had purchased the shares jointly with her husband during 1991–1994 and retained the original certificates bearing Nos.14639, 4781 and 34943. When she submitted them for dematerialisation in March 2019, the registrar and share transfer agent informed her that duplicate certificates had already been issued and the shares subsequently transferred and dematerialised. She alleged that a person in Jaipur had impersonated her, procured changes to the shareholder records using forged documents and obtained duplicate certificates, which were thereafter transferred to Bimal Kumar Shah. The company and its registrar and share transfer agent disputed liability, maintained that they had followed the applicable process, and raised objections concerning limitation, maintainability and the practical impossibility of restoring shares that had become fungible after dematerialisation. They also referred to the closure of the appellant’s SEBI SCORES complaint and the absence of criminal proceedings against the alleged impersonator. The Tribunal treated the relief principally as rectification under Section 59 of the Companies Act, 2013. It considered the statutory framework governing duplicate certificates and the power under Rule 70(5) of the NCLT Rules, 2016 to determine questions of title necessary for rectification. It also discussed Supreme Court and NCLAT decisions recognising rectification jurisdiction in cases involving impersonation, irregular duplicate certificates, disputed facts and subsequent dematerialisation. On the evidence, the Tribunal found that the appellant’s continued possession and production of the original certificates, the earlier rejection of a change-of-address request because of a signature discrepancy, and the circumstances surrounding the duplicate certificates established that the subsequent entry lacked sufficient cause. Respondent No.3 had never attended the proceedings. The registrar had not produced any document showing that she had identified the old address to be changed, the signatures did not match, and a report of loss made in 2018 referred to certificates allegedly lost in 2014. Duplicate certificates were sent to Jaipur without proof of the old address. The Tribunal concluded that the company and its registrar had no sufficient cause to remove the appellant’s name. The limitation objection did not survive at that stage because delay had already been condoned on 20.06.2024 and no stay of further proceedings had been shown in the pending challenge. The Tribunal directed the company to restore the appellant’s name for the specified 250 shares and required the registrar to take consequential steps in the depository records within 30 days of receipt of the order. The company was also directed to make consequential entries in its statutory records, with relief expressly confined to Folio No.007448. Considering the submission that the dematerialised shares had become untraceable, the Tribunal alternatively directed the company and registrar to pay the market value of the shares to which the appellant was entitled and the dividends as on the date of the order. The appeal was allowed to that extent without costs. The Registry was separately directed to send the order to the Principal Chief Commissioner of Income Tax, Jaipur, for necessary action concerning income, if not offered to tax, in the cases of Anoopamaben Shah and Bimal Kumar Shah. Register of Members in respect of 250 shares of Respondent No.1 Company. 8.2 The Appellant states that the 250 shares were purchased jointly with her late husband during 1991-1994 and stood recorded under Folio No.007448, with share certificates issued in their names. 8.3 The Appellant states that on 14.03.2019 she submitted the original share certificates to Respondent No.2 for dematerialisation, but the request was rejected on 09.04.2019 as duplicate certificates had already been issued and dematerialised. 8.4 According to the Appellant, an impersonator submitted requests for change of address and signature along with forged documents, including the death certificate of her late husband, pursuant to which duplicate certificates were issued. 8.5 The Appellant further submits that the shares were transmitted to the impersonator and thereafter transferred to Mr. Bimal Kumar Shah on 12.06.2018, who subsequently dematerialised the shares. 8.6 The Appellant contends that the Respondents proceeded despite a recorded signature mismatch and a change of address from Surat to Jaipur, without contacting her at the original registered address or properly verifying the documents. 8.7 The Appellant relies on the principle that a transfer founded upon forgery or fraud does not confer valid title and submits that her continued possession of the original share certificates supports her claim that she never intended to transfer the shares. 8.8 The Appellant submits that she acted after discovering the alleged fraud by corresponding with the Respondents, issuing legal notices and approaching SEBI, and further states that delay had already been condoned by order dated 20.06.2024. 8.9 The Appellant submits that Section 59 empowers the Tribunal to rectify the Register of Members where a name is entered or omitted without sufficient cause, and contends that dematerialisation or fungibility of shares does not defeat her claim for restoration or consequential relief. 8.10 The Appellant therefore prays for allowing the Appeal, declaring her as the rightful shareholder, nullifying the alleged fraudulent transfer, restoring her name in the Register of Members and granting appropriate consequential reliefs under the Companies Act, 2013. 9. The Respondent No.1 and 2 also filed Written Submissions along-with judgments on 11.02.2026 vide inward no. D-1260 & D-1262. The contentions of the Respondent No.1 & 2 are as follows: – 9.1 The Respondent Nos. 1 and 2 submit that the Appellant seeks declaration of her shareholding in Respondent No.1, cancellation of the alleged fraudulent transfer of 250 shares and restoration of her name in the register of members under Sections 57 and 58 of the Companies Act, 2013. 9.2 They submit that the Appellant was aware of the issuance of duplicate share certificates and subsequent transfer of the shares at least from 09.04.2019 and had also approached SEBI on 02.07.2021. The Appeal was filed on 09.09.2022. 9.3 It is submitted that the Appellant had claimed only 13 days’ delay, whereas according to the Respondents the cause of action arose much earlier and the statutory period applicable to the appeal under Section 58 was 60 days. The Respondents therefore contend that the Appeal was filed belatedly. 9.4 The Respondent No.1 states that it challenged the order dated 20.06.2024 condoning the delay by filing Company Appeal (AT) No.249 of 2024 before the NCLAT, Delhi. It therefore requested that the present proceedings not be decided on merits during pendency of that appeal. 9.5 The Respondent No.1 submits that Respondent No.2 was appointed as its Registrar and Share Transfer Agent and was entrusted with shareholder services, including maintenance of the register, transfer and transmission of shares, issue of duplicate certificates and dematerialisation. 9.6 The Respondents submit that the allegation of impersonation has not been followed by any FIR or criminal proceedings against Respondent No.3. They further submit that Respondent No.3 has not been effectively brought before the Tribunal to answer the principal allegation. 9.7 They submit that the Appellant’s suggestion that the Respondents should purchase equivalent shares from the market and compensate her was not part of the original prayer. According to them, the Respondents followed the applicable process and cannot be held responsible for an alleged third-party fraud. 9.8 The Respondents contend that although the Appeal refers to Sections 57, 58 and 59 of the Companies Act, 2013, the Appellant’s principal allegation is impersonation by Respondent No.3. They therefore submit that the reliefs claimed under Sections 58 and 59 are not applicable to the stated facts. 9.9 The Respondents set out that, in 2017, requests were received concerning change of address, change of signature, unpaid dividend and deletion of the joint holder’s name. The request for change of address was initially rejected because of a difference in the specimen signature. 9.10 Thereafter, upon receipt of the required documents, duplicate share certificates were issued and forwarded to the Appellant on 21.03.2018. On 02.05.2018, the Appellant submitted the certificate and transfer documents, following which a precautionary notice was issued to the transferor. 9.11 Subsequently, Mr. Bimal Kumar Shah submitted the original share certificate, transfer deed and supporting documents for transfer of the shares in his name. Notice dated 23.06.2018 was issued to the transferor and, no objection having been received, the shares were transferred in his name. 9.12 On 14.03.2019, Sykes and Ray Equities India Ltd. forwarded the share certificates for dematerialisation. Respondent No.2 informed it on 09.04.2019 that duplicate certificates had already been issued and the original certificates had been cancelled and retained. 9.13 The Respondents further submit that the Appellant was informed by letter dated 02.09.2020 that the duplicate certificates had ultimately been transferred to Mr. Bimal Kumar Shah and dematerialised. On her request dated 13.11.2020, the relevant documents were supplied to her on 04.03.2021. 9.14 The Respondents state that the Appellant thereafter issued a legal notice dated 25.05.2022, which was duly replied to. They also point out that her complaint before SEBI’s SCORES portal was closed after the Company’s reply, while the Respondents had offered cooperation for any action against the alleged impersonator. 9.15 Ultimately, Respondent Nos. 1 and 2 submit that they acted in accordance with the applicable process, discharged their duties and committed no breach of law or duty of care. They therefore seek that no relief be granted against either Respondent. 10. We have heard the Ld. Counsel for the Appellant and the Ld. Counsel for Respondent No.1 and Respondent No.2 at length. We have considered the Appeal, Affidavits-in-Reply, Rejoinders, Written Submissions and documents placed on record. We have also considered Annexures A to N to the Appeal, Annexures A to T filed by Respondent No.1, Annexures A to T filed by Respondent No.2 and the Additional Affidavit of the Appellant with Annexures A-1 to A-6. 11. Section 59 of the Companies Act, 2013 empowers the Tribunal to rectify the Register of Members where the name of any person is entered or omitted without sufficient cause and also permits rectification of the records of the depository. Section 46 and Rule 6 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014 govern the issue of duplicate share certificates, while Rule 70(5) of the NCLT Rules, 2016 permits determination of questions of title and other questions necessary for rectification. 12. In Ammonia Supplies Corporation (P) Ltd. v. Modern Plastic Containers Pvt. Ltd. & Ors., (1998) 7 SCC 105, the Hon’ble Supreme Court held that rectification jurisdiction extends to questions necessary for rectification, while the Tribunal has to determine whether the dispute is one falling within the scope of rectification. In Shashi Prakash Khemka (Dead) through LRs & Anr. v. NEPC Micon (Now NEPC India Ltd.) & Ors., (2019) ibclaw.in 06 SC, Civil Appeal Nos. 1965-1966 of 2014, decided on 18.01.2019, the Hon’ble Supreme Court recognised the jurisdiction of the Tribunal in matters of rectification under Section 59 and the corresponding exclusion of civil-court jurisdiction under Section 430. 13. In Adesh Kaur v. Eicher Motors Ltd. and Ors., (2018) ibclaw.in 227 SC., Civil Appeal Nos.19426-19427 of 2017, decided on 03.07.2018, the Hon’ble Supreme Court held that in an open-and-shut case of impersonation and fraudulent issue of duplicate shares, the Tribunal can exercise jurisdiction under Section 59 and direct rectification of the Register of Members and the concerned depository records. 14. In Chalasani Udaya Shankar and Ors. v. Lexus Technologies Pvt. Ltd. and Ors., (2024) ibclaw.in 231 SC, Civil Appeal Nos. 5735-5736 of 2023, decided on 09.09.2024, the Hon’ble Supreme Court held that the Tribunal is required to examine the material, evidence and factual circumstances to determine whether rectification is warranted. The Court further recognised that an open-and-shut case of fraud can be dealt with under Section 59. 15. In Gireesh Kumar Sanghi v. Sanghi Industries Ltd. & Ors., (2023) ibclaw.in 802 NCLAT, decided on 01.12.2023 the Hon’ble NCLAT held that once the legislature has created a complete bar of the jurisdiction of the Civil Court by enacting Section 430 of the Companies Act, 2013, there is no shred of doubt that the jurisdiction to decide the rectificatory jurisdiction under Section 59 of the Act shall be available to be exercised even where there are contested facts and disputed questions. 16. In Indusind Bank Ltd. and Anr. v. Neetu R. Menda, (2026) ibclaw.in 166 NCLAT, Company Appeal (AT) No.38 of 2024, decided on 10.02.2026, the Hon’ble NCLAT considered a case involving retention of original share certificates, issuance of duplicate certificates and subsequent dematerialisation and upheld rectification where the material on record established irregularity in the process. The said decision is relevant to the effect of subsequent dematerialisation on relief under Section 59. 17. Indisputably, the Appellant was the owner and holder of original share certificates bearing Certificate Nos.14639, 4781 and 34943 relating to 250 shares under Folio No.007448. Pages 20 to 30 of the Application being copies of share certificates of Amtrex Appliances Limited show that holders of certificates were Anoopmaben Manis Shah and Manis Kumar Saralal Shah. 18. The Respondent No. 1 and 2 submits that the shares were dematerialized in favour of Bimal Kumar Shah. 19. The Appellant contends that issuance of duplicate share certificates to Anoopmaben Shah (Respondent No.3) and transfer of shares to Bimal Kumar Shah is fraudulent. 20. We are asked to determine in appeal under section 59 of the Companies Act, 2013 whether the name of the Appellant that was entered in the register of members is omitted from the register of members without sufficient cause. For determining the same, the facts based on the information provided by the Appellant and Respondent No.2, at the cost of repletion, are stated below: – 20.1 The Respondent No.2 is the Registrar and Share Transfer Agent of Respondent No.1 and the transfer of shares has been carried out by it, for and on behalf, of Respondent No.1. 20.2 The Respondent No.2 claimed to have received a letter on 30.05.2017 from Anoopamaben Manish Shah for change of address, change of signature, and request for payment of unpaid dividends. A copy of death certificate of joint holder was also attached. This letter dated 30.05.2017 shows address of this applicant as of Jaipur and stated that dividend on 600 shares was not received due to change of address. An affidavit for change of signature and notary attested copy of death certificate of joint holder was attached. The death certificate was of Manis Kumar Shah and not of Manis Kumar Saralal Shah and date of death was 23.06.2005. 20.3 Respondent No.2 asked for sending the original share certificates for entire holding along with other documents vide letter of 19.06.2017. 20.4 The Applicant of Jaipur again wrote a letter of 30.06.2017 but did not provide the documents. 20.5 The request for change of address was rejected by Respondent No.2 vide letter dated 20.07.2017 because of signature difference between applicant signature and the one available with Respondent No.2. 20.6 Page 35 of the Respondent No.2 reply is a copy of electricity bill in the name of Anoopamaben Shah who is shown as daughter of Manis Shah. 20.7 The Respondent No.3 (Applicant from Jaipur) again made the same request vide letter of 23.10.2017. 20.8 The respondent No.2 vide letter of 01.11.2017 again asked for information. 20.9 Respondent No.3 again wrote letter on 07.11.2017 with the same contents indicating not receiving the dividends and for change of address. 20.10 The Respondent No. 2 vide letter dated 15.11.2017 aske the applicant to submit a copy of FIR/Police complaint for loss of shares. 20.11 The Applicant vide letter dated 23.02.2018 submitted a letter addressed to Jaipur Police regarding loss of shares indicating that while shifting office from Paradise Tower in 2014 she lost share certificates. This letter to police authorities was submitted on 24.01.2018. An indemnity bond was also submitted by Applicant from Jaipur and Bimal Kumar Shah and Neha Shah were the surety. 20.12 Respondent No.3 again requested for change of address, resend the dividends and issue of duplicate share certificate on the ground that she misplaced the same. 20.13 Duplicate share certificates were issued and sent to Jaipur address vide letter of 21.03.2018. 20.14 Respondent No.3 vide letter of 02.05.2018 sent those 600 duplicate shares with transfer deed indicating consideration of Rs 18 lakhs received from Bimal Kumar Shah of address 10/62, Mansarovar, Jaipur whereas the applicant address was of 10/61, Mansarovar, Jaipur. Confirmation of signature was of IDBI Bank. 20.15 Share transfer request was received by Respondent No.2 from Bimal Kumar Shah vide letter of 12.06.2018 and then shares were dematerialised. 20.16 On 14.03.2019, the Appellant requested for dematerialisation of shares. 20.17 Respondent No.2 vide letter of 09.04.2019 informed that duplicate share certificates were already issued, dematerialised and subsequently cancelled. 20.18 The Respondent No.2 vide letters dated 25.05.2019 and 06.06.2019 requested more information from the Appellant and this information was provided vide letter of 30.07.2020. 21. In the present case, the Appellant has produced the original share certificates bearing Certificate Nos.14639, 4781 and 34943 relating to 250 shares under Folio No.007448. The Additional Affidavit and Annexures A-1 to A-6 further contain documents concerning the identity of the Appellant and her husband and have been placed on record pursuant to the direction of this Tribunal. 22. The record shows that the earlier request concerning change of address was rejected on account of difference in signature, whereas subsequently duplicate share certificates were issued on the basis of documents furnished from Jaipur. The Appellant thereafter produced the original certificates, while disputing the identity, signatures and the document concerning the alleged death of her husband relied upon in the subsequent transaction. 23. On consideration of the documents as a whole, the continued possession and production of the original certificates, the earlier signature discrepancy, the circumstances in which duplicate certificates were issued and the subsequent transfer and dematerialisation establish that the subsequent entry was not supported by sufficient cause within the meaning of Section 59. We note that Respondent No.3 has never attended the proceedings. Further, the Respondent No.2 has not produced any document to show that Respondent No.3 at anytime indicated her old address that was to be changed. Further, signatures were not matching. The Report for loss of shares at Jaipur was made in 2018, after the Respondent No.2 asked for the same, whereas certificates are shown to have been lost in 2014. The Respondent No.2 sent the duplicate share certificates on Jaipur address without asking proof for old address. The Respondent No.2 and Respondent No.1 had no sufficient cause to remove the name of the Appellant from the Register. 24. The objection regarding limitation does not survive for determination at this stage. The delay in filing the present proceeding was condoned by this Tribunal vide Order dated 20.06.2024, and no order staying further proceedings has been shown to have been passed in the pending challenge thereto. The present relief is principally one of rectification under Section 59 of the Companies Act, 2013. 25. In view of the aforesaid observations and findings, we are of the considered view that the name of the Appellant was omitted from the Register of Members without sufficient cause and that the subsequent transfer and dematerialisation of the subject shares cannot be sustained. Accordingly, the Appeal deserves to be allowed to the extent of the 250 shares under Folio No.007448. 26. Accordingly, Respondent No.1 is directed to rectify its Register of Members by restoring the name of the Appellant, Ms. Anoopama Shah, in respect of 250 shares covered by Folio No.007448 and Share Certificate Nos.14639, 4781 and 34943. The Respondent No.2 shall take all consequential steps required for giving effect to such rectification in the relevant depository records, in accordance with law, within 30 days from the date of receipt of this Order. 27. Upon completion of the aforesaid rectification, Respondent No.1 shall make all consequential entries in its Register of Members and other statutory records maintained under the Companies Act, 2013 and the applicable Rules. The present directions are confined to the aforesaid 250 shares under Folio No.007448 and shall not extend to any other shares or folios. 28. The Respondent No.2 has submitted that the prayer is practically impossible to be granted because shares are dematerialised and floated in the market2, they have become fungible and have lost their identity and have become completely untraceable and provisions of Companies Act, 2013 do not permit the issue of additional shares. Considering this submission, the Respondent No.2 and Respondent No.1, as an alternative, shall pay the market value of all the shares the Appellant is entitled and the dividends as on the date of this order. 29. In view of the above, the prayers of the Appellant are allowed to the aforesaid extent. The Company Appeal No.57 of 2023 stands allowed accordingly. There shall be no order as to costs. 30.
