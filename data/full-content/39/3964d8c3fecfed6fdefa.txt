Summary : Franklin India Prima Fund was renamed Franklin India Mid Cap Fund with effect from 11 July 2025. It is the same underlying scheme, not a new launch or a separate successor portfolio. The current KIM describes an open-ended equity scheme predominantly investing in mid-cap stocks, and official July 2026 material identifies Nifty Midcap 150 as the benchmark and 1 December 1993 as the allotment date. The reviewed factsheet reported assets under management of ₹12,870.79 crore at 31 July 2026, a Regular Plan base expense ratio of 1.47 per cent, a Direct Plan base expense ratio of 0.81 per cent, minimum initial purchase of ₹5,000, additional purchase of ₹1,000 and SIP instalments from ₹500. Those are dated official figures, not live values. The published exit load is one per cent on redemption or switch-out within one year from allotment and nil thereafter. This article resolves the name, explains the mid-cap mandate, plan and option differences, costs, transactions, risk and Indian equity-oriented-fund tax without ranking the scheme or recommending investment. The official factsheet describes a minimum 65 per cent allocation to mid-cap stocks. The remainder may be invested within the SID limits in large-cap or small-cap equity, debt and money-market instruments, cash and permitted derivatives. The exact monthly weights change with market movements, subscriptions, redemptions and portfolio decisions, so a category label is not a substitute for the latest official portfolio disclosure. Nifty Midcap 150 is the stated benchmark. As a total-return index it incorporates distributions and offers a broad reference for mid-cap exposure. The scheme is actively managed and may diverge because of stock selection, sector weights, cash, derivatives, expenses and transaction timing. Performance should be compared for the same plan, option and period. A Regular Plan return and Direct Plan return differ because expense ratios differ even though the underlying portfolio is common. How to Read the Monthly Portfolio Review the largest holdings, combined top-ten weight, sector concentration, market-cap mix, cash, derivatives and changes across several months. Mid-cap labels can obscure very different liquidity and business profiles. A large asset base may increase trading impact when buying or selling less-liquid shares. A portfolio snapshot is historical from the moment it is published and should not be treated as a promise that the same security or weight remains current. Direct and Regular Plans, Growth and IDCW Direct and Regular Plans invest in the same scheme portfolio. The Direct Plan excludes distributor commission from its expense structure, while the Regular Plan includes distribution-related expense, so their NAVs and returns differ over time. Direct is not “risk free” and Regular does not guarantee advice quality. A reader should choose a channel based on service need and current expense disclosure rather than compare raw NAV values, because a lower NAV does not make a plan cheaper. Growth retains distributable surplus within NAV. IDCW can distribute surplus when declared, but it is not assured interest and the NAV falls to reflect the payout and applicable adjustments. IDCW is generally taxable in the investor’s hands, while Growth postpones investor cash flow until redemption. A switch between plans or options is normally processed as a redemption and new purchase, so exit load and capital-gains tax can arise even without a bank credit. TER, Exit Load and Other Costs The reviewed July 2026 factsheet reported a base expense ratio of 1.47 per cent for the Regular Plan and 0.81 per cent for the Direct Plan. TER is deducted from scheme assets and reflected in NAV; it is not a separate invoice. Rates can change and can include or exclude components depending on the disclosure format, so the AMC’s latest daily TER page should control. The difference between the two plans can compound over long periods but does not determine whether the scheme itself is suitable. The published exit load is one per cent when units are redeemed or switched out within one year from allotment and nil thereafter. Each SIP instalment has its own allotment date, and lot selection ordinarily follows operational rules such as first-in-first-out within a folio. Exit load is separate from capital-gains tax, securities transaction tax and any advisory or platform fee. Nil load after one year does not mean tax-free redemption. Riskometer disclosure is a regulatory classification, not a forecast of a particular loss or return. Mid-cap shares can face sharper volatility, thinner liquidity, valuation compression, business concentration, governance concerns and financing sensitivity. Portfolio concentration, sector cycles, manager decisions and redemption pressure can amplify those risks. Open-ended structure permits redemption requests but does not guarantee immediate liquidity at an expected NAV in exceptional conditions. Mid-cap market prices can fall faster than underlying business fundamentals change. Lower trading liquidity can increase market impact when the fund buys or sells. A long operating history does not protect current investors from drawdowns. A large scheme size can create capacity constraints in less-liquid holdings. Past performance, ratings and recent flows do not establish future return or suitability. Indian Tax Treatment for Equity-Oriented Fund Units When the scheme satisfies the statutory equity-oriented-fund conditions, units held for not more than twelve months are short-term assets. For qualifying transfers on or after 23 July 2024 on which the required securities transaction tax conditions are met, section 111A generally taxes short-term capital gains at 20 per cent. Qualifying long-term capital gains under section 112A are generally taxed at 12.5 per cent on aggregate covered gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh in the financial year. Surcharge and cess apply, and non-resident or special-status investors can have additional rules. A switch is normally a taxable transfer even when the proceeds remain within the AMC. SIP instalments create separate lots and holding periods. IDCW is generally taxable at the investor’s applicable rate and may be subject to TDS when statutory conditions are met. Capital-gain statements should retain units, date, cost, consideration, exit load and STT. The current scheme’s domestic-equity composition and statutory classification should still be verified for the relevant transaction rather than assumed forever from its category name.
