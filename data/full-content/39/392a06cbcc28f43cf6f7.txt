(Image Source: iStock)follow usfollow usIndia's retail inflation accelerated sharply to 6.09 per cent in October, crossing the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) upper tolerance threshold of 6 per cent for the first time in two years. The increase was primarily fuelled by a steep rise in food prices, which pushed food inflation to 8.94 per cent during the month. Data released by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) on Thursday showed that consumer price pressures intensified compared with September, when Consumer Price Index (CPI)-based inflation was recorded at 5.54 per cent.October's inflation reading is also the highest recorded since the introduction of the revised CPI series with 2024 as its base year. The RBI aims to keep inflation at 4 per cent, allowing for a margin of 2 percentage points on either side. The latest figures have therefore taken retail inflation beyond the central bank's prescribed comfort range.Food Prices Drive October Inflation HigherThe sharp increase in food costs emerged as the primary contributor to the rise in overall retail inflation. According to the Consumer Food Price Index (CFPI), food inflation climbed to 8.94 per cent in October, compared with 7.74 per cent in September.The pressure was visible across both rural and urban markets. Food inflation in rural areas reached 9.09 per cent, while the corresponding figure for urban India stood at 8.68 per cent.The latest figures indicate that households continued to face substantial price pressures, particularly when purchasing essential food commodities.Onion, Garlic And Ginger Among Biggest Price DriversSeveral kitchen staples recorded significant price increases during October, with onion witnessing the steepest inflation among the food items highlighted in the data.Onion inflation surged to 116.22 per cent, compared with 108.13 per cent in September. Garlic prices also remained elevated, with inflation rising from 54.62 per cent to 60.74 per cent over the same period.Ginger recorded price inflation of 48.32 per cent, while lemons became 47.30 per cent more expensive year-on-year. This was a considerable increase from the 36.41 per cent inflation recorded for lemons in September.Outside the food category, silver jewellery continued to experience considerable price pressures. Inflation in this segment stood at 52.39 per cent in October. However, this represented a moderation from the 87.82 per cent recorded in the preceding month.Not all food commodities witnessed rising prices. Several vegetables and other essential items continued to record deflation in October, offering some relief amid the broader increase in food costs.Potatoes registered the sharpest decline among the listed commodities, with prices recording deflation of 14.24 per cent during the month.Cauliflower prices fell by 10.57 per cent, while coconut recorded a decline of 5.60 per cent.
