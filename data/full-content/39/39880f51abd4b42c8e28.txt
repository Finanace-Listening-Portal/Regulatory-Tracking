The company announced an 11.7% increase in its net profit, reaching ₹505 crore, marking the fourth consecutive quarter of double-digit profit growth. Consolidated revenue for the quarter also saw an 11.6% rise, amounting to ₹3,563 crore. The company’s domestic FMCG business experienced a 10.7% growth, supported by a 5.5% increase in underlying volume. Notably, Dabur’s premium brands expanded at a rate four times faster than regular brands, now constituting 20% of the domestic business. The international segment also performed well, with a 20% growth in INR terms, driven by strong results in key markets such as Bangladesh, Egypt, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Turkey. Dabur’s CEO, Mr. Herjit S. Bhalla, highlighted the company’s strategic execution and brand strength as key contributors to the positive results. Despite facing challenges such as geopolitical uncertainties and high input costs, Dabur achieved broad-based growth with healthy underlying volumes. The company also noted a resurgence in General Trade, which grew by mid to high single digits, aided by initiatives under Project Saksham. In terms of category performance, the Home & Personal Care (HPC) segment grew by 10.9%, driven by robust performances in the shampoo and hair oils businesses, which saw high double-digit growth. The Healthcare segment grew by 6.9%, buoyed by strong performances in the Digestives, OTC, and Ethicals categories. Meanwhile, the Food & Beverages division recorded a 17.4% increase, with notable recovery in the beverages portfolio. In line with its payout policy, Dabur’s Board of Directors declared an interim dividend of 350% for the fiscal year 2026-27, amounting to ₹3.50 per share, totalling a payout of ₹620.88 crore. Market analysts view Dabur’s consistent growth and strategic focus on premiumisation and channel expansion as positive indicators for sustaining its growth trajectory in the coming quarters.
