Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) Section 154 Rectification Cannot Substitute an Appeal Against Original Assessment: Chennai ITAT Summary: The Delhi Income Tax Appellate Tribunal has held that a disallowed depreciation claim on the land component of a hotel property does not, by itself, establish concealment of income under Section 271(1)(c) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. In Sanya Hospitality Pvt. Ltd. v. DCIT, the Tribunal deleted the penalty of ₹73,37,806 in the lead appeal, even though the underlying depreciation disallowance had attained finality. Following the Supreme Court’s decision in CIT v. Reliance Petroproducts Pvt. Ltd., it reiterated that every addition or disallowance in assessment proceedings does not automatically warrant a penalty. The same reasoning was applied to the connected appeal for Assessment Year 2011–12, and both appeals were allowed. This article is based on the supplied order: C:UsersUserDesktoptaxguru officeviewOrder (3).pdf Facts of the Case As stated in the grounds reproduced in the order, the assessee company purchased a hotel complex comprising land and building on 1 July 2009 for a consolidated consideration of ₹1,97,39,24,155. The assessee claimed depreciation without excluding the land component from the depreciable value. During assessment, the land value was determined at ₹43,29,09,957, and the corresponding depreciation was disallowed. The Tribunal recorded the depreciation disallowance at ₹2,16,45,448 and noted that the quantum issue had attained finality. The Assessing Officer imposed a penalty of ₹73,37,806 through an order dated 30 September 2025. The CIT(A), Delhi-26, upheld the penalty by an order dated 9 January 2026. The assessee consequently approached the Tribunal. Assessee’s Contentions The assessee contended that the depreciation claim arose from the consolidated purchase of the hotel property, comprising land and building, and that the resulting disallowance did not justify a concealment penalty. It also submitted that, from Assessment Year 2013-14, the company had voluntarily excluded the land value from the block of depreciable assets and corrected the written-down value. According to the assessee, this demonstrated the absence of mala fide intention. These submissions formed part of the grounds of appeal. The Tribunal’s decisive reasoning rested on the legal distinction between an assessment disallowance and the requirements for imposing a concealment penalty. Revenue’s Argument The Revenue contended that the assessee could not legally claim depreciation on land. Since the depreciation disallowance had attained finality, the Revenue supported the conclusion of the lower authorities that the assessee had concealed particulars of income and that penalty under Section 271(1)(c) was justified. Tribunal’s Finding: Disallowance Does Not Automatically Establish Concealment After considering the rival submissions, the Tribunal found no reason to sustain the penalty. The Bench held that the mere claim of depreciation could not be regarded as an attempt to conceal particulars of income. It relied on the Supreme Court’s ruling in CIT Vs Reliance Petroproducts Pvt. Ltd., (2010) 322 ITR 158 (SC). The Tribunal explained that quantum and penalty proceedings require separate consideration. Every disallowance or addition in assessment proceedings does not automatically attract penalty. Consequently, the fact that the depreciation disallowance had attained finality did not, by itself, establish that the assessee had concealed income. Principle Applied from Reliance Petroproducts The Supreme Court’s decision in CIT Vs Reliance Petroproducts Pvt. Ltd. distinguishes a claim that is unsustainable in law from the furnishing of inaccurate particulars of income. The relevant principle is that mere rejection of a claim does not automatically justify penalty under Section 271(1)(c). The statutory ingredients of concealment or furnishing inaccurate particulars must be established. Applying that principle, the Tribunal held that the disallowed depreciation claim, by itself, was insufficient to sustain the impugned penalty. The same distinction between a wrong or unsuccessful claim and a penal default has been examined in other judicial decisions. Those decisions provide related legal context but were not separately cited by the Bench in the present order. Relief Granted in Both Assessment Years The Tribunal deleted the penalty of ₹73,37,806 in the lead appeal, ITA No. 1529/Del/2026, relating to Assessment Year 2010-11. It directed that the same order would apply to ITA No. 1530/Del/2026 for Assessment Year 2011-12 because the connected appeal raised an identical question of law and facts. Accordingly, both appeals were allowed. The order does not separately specify the penalty amount for Assessment Year 2011-12. Scope of the Decision The ruling does not permit depreciation on land or reverse the underlying disallowance. The quantum disallowance remained final. The relief is confined to penalty. The Tribunal’s conclusion was that disallowance of a depreciation claim does not, merely by itself, establish concealment of income. The decision must therefore be distinguished from cases where an incorrect claim is accompanied by independently established concealment, false particulars or other facts satisfying the statutory penalty requirements. Practical Takeaway The decision reinforces the distinction between an unsuccessful tax claim and liability to penalty. Even where a depreciation disallowance is accepted or has attained finality, the justification for penalty must be examined independently. The assessee’s explanation, particulars furnished and statutory requirements remain relevant. In the present case, the depreciation disallowance survived, but the concealment penalty did not. Cases Discussed CIT Vs Reliance Petroproducts Pvt. Ltd. (Supreme Court); (2010) 322 ITR 158 (SC). The Tribunal expressly relied upon this judgment for the proposition that every disallowance or addition in quantum assessment proceedings does not automatically attract penalty under Section 271(1)(c). The principle distinguishes an unsustainable claim from concealment or furnishing inaccurate particulars of income. Related TaxGuru discussion of the Supreme Court principle. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI These assessee’s twin appeals ITA 1529 & 1530/Del/2026 for assessment year 2010-11 and 2011-12 arise against the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) [in short, the “CIT(A)”], Delhi’s-26 orders, both dated 09.01.2026, having DINs and orders no. ITBA/APL/S/250/2025-26/1084611898(1) & 1084621605(1) , involving proceedings under section 271(1)(c) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’); respectively. Heard both the parties. Case file perused. 2. The assessee pleads the following substantive grounds in its instant “lead” appeal ITA 1529/Del/2026 in assessment year 2010-11: “Grounds of Appeal 1. That on the facts and in law, the Assessment order dt.30.09.2025 passed by deputy commissioner of Income Tax Central Circle 16, New Delhi, Income Tax Department (hereinafter referred to as “Ld. Assessing Officer” or “Ld. A.O.”] u/s 271(1)(C) of the Income Tax Act (hereinafter referred to as “the Act”] and upheld by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) CIT(A), Delhi-26, (hereinafter referred to as “Ld. CIT(A)”] vide order dt.09.01.2026 is bad in law & facts and is void ab initio. 2. Whether on facts and law the penalty order dated 30/09/2025 passed by Dy. Commissioner of Income Tax Central Circle-16 New Delhi (hereinafter referred to as “Ld. Assessing Officer” “Ld. A.O.”) imposing a penalty of Rs. 73,37,806/- is unjustified and against the facts of the case and learned commissioner of income tax appeal (A) Delhi-26 has grossly erred in upholding the penalty imposed. 3. That during the assessment year company purchased a hotel complex with land and building for a consolidated amount of Rs. 1,97,39,24,155/- on 01/07/2009 and depreciation for half year calculated and claimed the total depreciation to be carried forward and during assessment proceedings the value of land for was calculated at Rs. 43,29,09,957/- and depreciation on this of Rs. 2,16,45,449/- was reduced from carry forward of depreciation and thus there was no intention on the part of assessee and penalty imposed is unjustified and unwarranted and up held by CIT appeal. 4. That assessee company on its own volition from assessment year 2013-14 excluded the land value from the block and WDV was corrected from assessment 2013/14 on ward which proves that there was no malafide intention and penalty u/s 271(1)(c) has been wrongly imposed and confirmed by CIT appeal New Delhi 5. All the above grounds are independent and without prejudice to others. That the appellant carves the right to add, modify, amend and delete the grounds of appeal during the course of the hearing.” 3. Suffice to say, the solitary substantive issue between both the parties herein is that of correctness of the lower authorities’ action levying section 271(1)(c) penalty of Rs. 73,37,806/- in the Assessing Officer’s order dated 30.09.2025 as upheld in the CIT(A) lower appellate discission. 4. That being the case, we sought to ascertain the corresponding quantum addition between the parties as well as its finality as on date. We are informed that the same pertains to the assessee’s depreciation disallowance claim raised at its behest amounting to Rs.2,16,45,448/- representing land component which has attained finality. The Revenue vehemently submits given the fact that the assessee could not have raised such a claim on land, it has rightly been held to have concealed the particulars of income under section 271(1)(c) of the Act in both the lower proceedings. 5. We have given our thoughtful consideration to the assessee’s and the Revenue’s respective stands and we find no reason to sustain the impugned concealment penalty for the precise reason that such a deprecation claimed simplicitor could not be held as the assessee’s attempt to conceal its particulars of income in light of CIT vs. Reliance Petroproducts Pvt. Ltd. (2010) 322 ITR 158 (SC) having settled the law that quantum and penalty proceedings are parallel in nature wherein each and every disallowance/addition in course of the former does not ipso facto attract the latter provision. The impugned penalty of Rs.73,37,806/- is hereby deleted in very terms. The assessee succeeds in its “lead” appeal ITA 1529/Del/2026. Same order to follow in the assessee’s latter appeal ITA 1530/Del/2026 in AY: 2011-12 since raising identical question of law and facts, as the case may be. 6. These assessee’s twin appeals ITA 1529 & 1530/Del/2026 are allowed. A copy of this common order be placed in the respective case files.
