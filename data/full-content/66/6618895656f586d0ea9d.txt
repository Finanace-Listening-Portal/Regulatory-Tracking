(ITAT Mumbai) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Mumbai “G” Bench, in DCIT Vs Skyway Infra Projects Pvt. Ltd., adjudicated eight appeals filed by the Revenue and eight cross-objections filed by the assessee for Assessment Years 2013-14 to 2020-21. The consolidated order dated 28 February 2023 arose from a common appellate order passed by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)-53, Mumbai, on 27 July 2022. The assessee company was engaged in executing civil construction and infrastructure contracts for government and municipal authorities, including the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai. Following a search and seizure operation conducted on 6 November 2019 under Section 132 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, assessments were framed under Section 153A read with Section 143(3). The disputes principally concerned alleged bogus purchases, unexplained unsecured loans under Section 68, consequential interest expenditure, subcontract expenses, the validity and limitation of search assessments, and credit for tax deducted at source. The assessee challenged the validity of the assessment for AY 2013-14, contending that Section 153A permitted assessments only for six years preceding the year of search and that the relevant assessment year fell outside this period. The Tribunal examined the amendments introduced by the Finance Act, 2017, which permitted assessment of additional relevant assessment years, extending the period up to ten assessment years where the statutory conditions were satisfied. Considering that the search was conducted after 1 April 2017 and the income involved exceeded the prescribed threshold of Rs. 50 lakh, the Tribunal held that the Assessing Officer was empowered to issue notice under Section 153A for AY 2013-14. The assessee’s jurisdictional objection was therefore rejected. The assessee also alleged that the assessment order dated 30 September 2021 was barred by limitation because it was not visible on the Income-tax Department’s portal immediately after midnight. The Tribunal rejected this contention, observing that delayed uploading on the portal did not, by itself, establish that the assessment order had been passed beyond the limitation period, particularly in the absence of supporting evidence. A major dispute related to purchases from thirteen suppliers that the Assessing Officer treated as non-genuine on the basis of material gathered during search and survey proceedings. The investigation identified deficiencies in purchase invoices, delivery records, transportation documents, stock registers and other supporting evidence. Statements of certain suppliers and enquiries conducted at their business premises also raised doubts regarding the actual supply of materials. For AY 2013-14, the Assessing Officer disallowed purchases of Rs. 48,04,750. The CIT(A), after considering additional evidence and the remand report, deleted additions relating to certain suppliers and restricted the disallowance for the remaining suppliers to five per cent of the purchase amounts. The Revenue challenged the relief, while the assessee maintained that purchases were supported by invoices, banking transactions and the execution of municipal infrastructure contracts. The Tribunal examined the evidence, the suppliers’ responses and the conclusions of the lower authorities, and did not accept the CIT(A)’s treatment of the disputed purchases. The Tribunal also considered the assessee’s argument that additions could not be made under Section 153A in completed assessments without incriminating material discovered during the search. The assessee relied upon the principle governing unabated assessments and contended that the Assessing Officer had made additions without identifying relevant incriminating evidence. The Tribunal examined the material gathered during the search, including statements of suppliers, discrepancies in invoices, enquiries concerning business premises and documentary deficiencies. It distinguished the factual circumstances from cases where additions were made merely by revisiting material already available in completed assessments. The Tribunal found that the search investigation had revealed material relevant to the disputed purchases and unsecured loan transactions. Consequently, the assessee’s objection based on the alleged absence of incriminating material did not succeed. Another substantial issue concerned additions under creditworthiness-genuineness-loans. Section 68 relating to unsecured loans from various parties. The Assessing Officer questioned the identity and creditworthiness of the lenders and the genuineness of the transactions, relying upon enquiries conducted during the search, inspection reports and deficiencies in the supporting records. The CIT(A) deleted the additions after considering the documents produced by the assessee and the remand proceedings. The appellate authority also noted that no relevant loan entries existed in the assessee’s books for certain parties and that the assessee had discharged its evidentiary burden for other lenders. The Revenue challenged these findings, contending that the documents produced were insufficient to establish genuine loan transactions and that the field enquiries revealed serious doubts about the existence and financial capacity of the alleged creditors. On examining the unsecured loan additions, the Tribunal emphasised that creditworthiness-genuineness-loans. Section 68 requires the assessee to establish the identity of the creditor, the creditor’s creditworthiness and the genuineness of the transaction. It observed that merely furnishing PAN details, income-tax returns, ledger accounts and other primary documents would not necessarily discharge this burden where independent enquiries revealed contradictory facts. The Tribunal considered the investigation reports indicating that several alleged creditors were not found at their stated addresses and that the assessee had failed to produce adequate evidence establishing their financial capacity and the actual genuineness of the transactions. Relying on the principles governing unexplained cash credits, the Tribunal concluded that the assessee had not satisfactorily discharged the statutory burden. It therefore reversed the relief granted by the CIT(A) in respect of the disputed unsecured loan additions. The consequential dispute concerning interest claimed on such loans was also considered in the consolidated adjudication. The appeals further involved disallowances of subcontract expenditure claimed in connection with the assessee’s infrastructure projects. During the search, the Department questioned payments to subcontractors, including Ashok Construction and Rajni Developers, because of deficiencies in supporting documents and doubts concerning their business premises. The Assessing Officer disallowed the expenditure, while the CIT(A) deleted the disallowances after considering additional documents and the fact that the subcontractors appeared during remand proceedings. Before the Tribunal, the Revenue argued that establishing the identity of the subcontractors was insufficient without proving that the relevant services had actually been rendered. The Tribunal accepted that the appearance of the subcontractors helped establish their identity but held that adequate evidence of actual services, including supporting project and measurement records, remained necessary. It therefore restored the subcontract expenditure issue to the Assessing Officer for fresh examination after considering the assessee’s submissions and supporting evidence. In its final decision, the ITAT applied its findings for AY 2013-14 to the connected appeals and cross-objections for the subsequent assessment years wherever the underlying facts and grounds were common. It rejected the assessee’s challenges concerning the ten-year assessment period and limitation, reversed the CIT(A)’s relief on disputed purchases and creditworthiness-genuineness-proved. unsecured loans, and directed reconsideration of subcontract expenditure on the question of actual services rendered. The assessee’s grounds supporting relief on purchases, loans and related matters were rejected or treated as infructuous, while the ground concerning TDS credit was also dismissed as infructuous because it merely supported an undisputed direction for verification. Accordingly, the Tribunal dismissed the assessee’s cross-objections and partly allowed the Revenue’s appeals, including relief granted for statistical purposes on the remanded subcontract expenditure issue. The order highlights the importance of search-related evidence, independent verification of creditors and suppliers, and substantive proof of transactions beyond the production of basic documentary records.
