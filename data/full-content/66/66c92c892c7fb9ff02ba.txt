Vs DCIT (ITAT Hyderabad) Summary: The Hyderabad Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal allowed the appeal of Adama India Pvt. Ltd. for Assessment Year 2013-14, deleting two transfer pricing adjustments concerning management services received from overseas associated enterprises and interest imputed on outstanding receivables. The first dispute involved an adjustment of ₹2,84,58,356 arising from the Transfer Pricing Officer’s determination of the arm’s length price of management services at NIL. The assessee had aggregated these services with its manufacturing operations under the Transactional Net Margin Method, contending that the services were closely connected with its business activities. The TPO rejected this approach, questioned the commercial benefits derived from the services and concluded that the assessee had not adequately demonstrated the benefits received. The Tribunal observed that the assessee had submitted detailed supporting documents establishing the receipt of management services in its day-to-day operations. Relying on the Delhi High Court decisions in CIT Vs Cushman and Wakefield (India) Pvt. Ltd. and CIT Vs EKL Appliances Ltd., it held that determining the actual commercial benefits of international transactions was not within the TPO’s domain in the manner adopted by the lower authorities. Since the Revenue had not established that the supporting documents lacked genuineness, the Tribunal held that the NIL valuation and consequential adjustment were unsustainable. It accordingly deleted the adjustment of ₹2,84,58,356. The second dispute concerned an adjustment of ₹2,26,05,726 on overdue receivables from overseas associated enterprises. The TPO had used the State Bank of India’s prime lending rate of approximately 14.5% to calculate notional interest. The Tribunal held that an Indian domestic lending rate was not an appropriate benchmark for the international transactions under consideration. It further observed that the authorities had failed to adopt a prescribed most appropriate method and select appropriate comparables before making the adjustment. The Tribunal therefore directed deletion of the interest adjustment as well. Both substantive grounds succeeded, and the assessee’s appeal was allowed in full. Both the learned representatives reiterated their respective stands against and in favour of the impugned TP adjustment relating to receipt of management services in the nature of an intangible transaction. Case law CIT vs. Cushman and Wakefiled (India) Pvt.Ltd. (2014) 367 ITR 730 (Del) and CIT vs. EKL Appliances Ltd (2012) 345 ITR 241 (Del.) holds that it is not within the domain of the “TPO” to determine the actual benefits derived from the International transactions in issue which needs to be seen from assessee’s view point going by common business prudence only. We make it clear that the assessee has already filed its detailed paper book in the nature of supportive evidence indicating receipt of management services in day to day functioning from its overseas Associated Enterprises. There is no rebuttal coming from Revenue’s side that all the said details lack genuineness which could be taken as the benchmark for arriving at nil ‘ALP’ thereof. We thus hold that the learned lower authorities action making impugned ALP adjustment of Rs.2,84,58,356/- is not sustainable. The same is directed to be deleted. The assessee succeeds in its first substantive grievance therefore. 4. Next comes latter issue of interest on receivables addition of Rs.2,26,05,726/- qua the overdue credit period regarding international transactions between the assessee and its overseas Associated Enterprise learned CIT DR fails to rebut the clinching fact that the TPO as well as the CIT(A) have adopted prime lending rate of 14.5% as issued by the SBI which has no relevance to international transactions forming subject matter of ALP determination mechanism before us. We further make it clear that all the learned lower authorities have failed to adopt even the most appropriate method ‘MAM’ before adopting SBI lending than LIBOR rate as the interest on receivables benchmark. There is hardly any dispute that chapter X of the Act is a special provision wherein each and every adjustment qua an assessee’s international transaction could be made only after adopting the ‘MAM’ followed by selection of comparables in the very segment only. We thus direct the TPO to delete the impugned latter adjustment on interest on receivables amounting to Rs.2,26,05,726/- as well. The assessee succeeds in both of its substantive grievances. This assessee’s appeal is allowed in above terms. Pronounced in Open Court on 04th May, 2021.
