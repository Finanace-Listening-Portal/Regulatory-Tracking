Kadam and Sons Vs Commercial Tax Officer (Karnataka High Court) Summary: The Karnataka High Court, Dharwad Bench, in P.V. Kadam and Sons Vs Commercial Tax Officer, held that a GST appellate authority must comply with the statutory requirement of passing a reasoned order specifying the points for determination, its decisions and the reasons supporting those decisions. The petitioner, a civil contractor, challenged an appellate order dated 23 December 2025 dismissing its appeal for non-appearance. The underlying dispute concerned a demand of Rs.4,90,858/- for the period April 2018 to March 2019, arising from adjudication proceedings under the GST statutory framework. The Joint Commissioner of Commercial Taxes (Appeals), Belagavi, dismissed the appeal after recording that the petitioner had not attended the hearings or responded to the opportunities provided. Although the appellate authority stated that it had examined the grounds of appeal, available documents and information on the departmental portal, it found no reason to interfere with the original order. The petitioner argued that dismissal for non-appearance denied the opportunity of hearing guaranteed by the principles of natural justice and relied upon earlier Karnataka High Court decisions in Bondada Engineering Limited and Anand Alluminium Works, in which similar appellate dismissals had been set aside and the matters remanded. The High Court examined Sections 107(11) and 107(12) of the KGST Act, 2017, particularly the obligation to pass a written appellate order containing the points for determination, the decision on those points and the reasons for the decision. It found that these statutory requirements were lacking in the impugned appellate order. The Court further observed that a statutory appeal constitutes a substantive remedy available to the taxpayer on questions of fact and law, and denial of that statutory remedy amounts to denial of a substantial right. Accordingly, the Court allowed the writ petition, set aside the appellate order dated 23 December 2025 and remanded the matter to the appellate authority for reconsideration after providing a reasonable opportunity of hearing. The petitioner was directed to appear before the appellate authority on 26 October 2026, and the authority was requested to dispose of the appeal within two months from the first appearance. All rival contentions were expressly left open. The High Court did not determine the validity of the underlying tax demand. The original adjudication concerns the period 2018-19, although the final direction of the judgment refers to 2017-18. Cases Discussed 1. Bondada Engineering Limited Vs Office of the Assistant Commissioner of Central Tax and Others (Karnataka High Court) — W.P. No. 26900 of 2024; 20/03/2025. The Court relied upon the principle that dismissal of a statutory GST appeal for non-prosecution may warrant interference to provide another opportunity for adjudication on merits. The earlier appellate order was set aside and the matter remanded for reconsideration. 2. M/s. Anand Alluminium Works Vs The Assistant Commissioner of Central Tax and Others (Karnataka High Court) — W.P. No. 104589 of 2026; 25/09/2026; NC: 2026:KHC-D:16827 not applicable to this cited decision. The Court quoted this decision extensively concerning the mandatory requirements of Sections 107(11) and 107(12) of the KGST Act, 2017, and the obligation to provide reasonable opportunity before disposing of a statutory appeal. A perusal of the order passed by the appellate authority clearly shows that the appellant has not appeared before the Tribunal. As per Section 107(12) of the KGST Act, 2017, the appellate authority is required to follow the procedure contemplated in the said provision. Section 107(12) of the KGST Act, 2017 reads as under: “Section 107. Appeals to Appellate Authority.- (12)The order of the Appellate Authority disposing of the appeal shall be in writing and shall state the points for determination, the decision thereon and the reasons for such decision.” 7. The same is lacking in the order impugned herein that apart the appeal is a statutory remedy available to the petitioner both on facts and law and denial of statutory right amounts to denial of substantial right of the petitioner. In that view of the matter, this Court proceedings to pass the following : ORDER (i) The writ petition is allowed. (ii) The impugned order dated 31.03.2026 passed in Appeal No.GST-533/2023-24 is set aside. (iii) Matter stands remanded to the appellate authority to provide reasonable opportunity to the petitioner to make out his case and decide the matter strictly adhering to Section 107(11) and (12) of the KGST Act, 2017 and pass appropriate order in accordance with law. (iv) Since the parties are before this Court, the petitioner shall appear before the concerned appellate authority on 12.10.2026 and make his submission on the merits of the case and appellate authority to consider the same strictly in adherence to Section 107(11) and (12) of the KGST Act, 2017. (v) The appellate authority is requested to dispose of the matter considering the Tax period 2017-2018 and dispose of the appeal as expeditiously as possible within an outer limit of 2 months from the date of first appearance. (vi) All rival contentions are left open.” 5. As stated above, sub-Section (12) of 107 of the KGST Act, 2017 states that, the order of the Appellate Authority disposing of the appeal shall be in writing and shall state the points for determination, the decision thereon and the reasons for such decision. The same is lacking in the order impugned herein. That apart, the appeal is a statutory remedy available to the petitioner, both on facts & law and denial of statutory right amounts to denial of substantial right of the petitioner. In that view of the matter, this Court proceedings to pass the following: ORDER (1) The Writ Petition is allowed and remanded. (2) The impugned order dated 23.12.2025 passed in Appeal No.GST-341/2023-24/B-1440 vide Annexure-B, is set aside. (3) The matter stands remanded to the Appellate Authority to provide reasonable opportunity to the petitioner to make out its case and decide the matter strictly adhering to Sections 107(11) and (12) of the KGST Act, 2017 and pass appropriate order in accordance with law. (4) Since the parties are before this Court, the petitioner shall appear before the Appellate Authority on 26.10.2026 and make its submission on the merits of the case and the Appellate Authority to consider the same strictly adhering to Sections 107(11) and (12) of the KGST Act, 2017. (5) Considering the Tax period i.e., 2017-18, the Appellate Authority is requested to dispose of the appeal as expeditiously as possible, within an outer limit of 2 months from the date of first appearance. (6) All rival contentions of the parties are left open.
