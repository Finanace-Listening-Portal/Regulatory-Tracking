Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); ITO Vs ADG Online Solutions Private Limited (ITAT Delhi) Creditor’s Failure to File Return Does Not Establish Cessation of Liability: Delhi ITAT Deletes ₹3.80 Crore Addition Introduction The Delhi Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, in ITO v. ADG Online Solutions Private Limited, ITA No. 4612/Del/2026, Assessment Year 2021–22, decided on 13 November 2026, upheld deletion of an addition of ₹3,79,89,000 under section 41(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The decision reinforces an important distinction: doubts about a creditor’s tax compliance do not establish remission or cessation of the assessee’s liability. Continued recognition in the books, supporting commercial documents and subsequent payments were material circumstances supporting the assessee. Background of the Dispute The assessee had credited ₹3,79,89,000 in the account of M/s Skylark International. The Assessing Officer questioned the outstanding liability because the creditor had not filed its income-tax return, the agreement was executed on letterhead without registration, and the amount had remained unpaid for a substantial period. The Assessing Officer treated the entire credit balance as income under section 41(1). The CIT(A), however, deleted the addition after examining the documentary evidence, accounting treatment and subsequent payments. The Revenue challenged that relief before the Tribunal. Its principal contention was that subsequent payment could not establish the genuineness of a liability which remained unsupported by satisfactory confirmation and the creditor’s return of income. Non-Filing of Return Does Not Extinguish a Debt The CIT(A), whose reasoning was approved by the Tribunal, rejected the proposition that a creditor’s failure to file its return resulted in cessation of the liability. Whether a debt continues to exist depends upon the contractual and commercial relationship between the parties. The creditor’s income-tax filing status does not, by itself, determine whether the debtor remains obliged to pay. There was no finding that the assessee had written back the liability. Nor was there evidence that Skylark International had waived or remitted the amount. The outstanding balance continued to be reflected as payable in the assessee’s books. Consequently, the circumstances relied upon by the Assessing Officer did not establish the statutory event necessary for invoking section 41(1). Subsequent Payment Supported the Subsisting Liability An important fact was the payment of ₹41,73,000 in the next financial year, supported by bank statements. The appellate authority regarded this payment as acknowledgment of an existing obligation. The Assessing Officer had not controverted the payment with adverse evidence. The decision should nevertheless be read accurately: the specifically quantified subsequent payment was a part-payment, and the order does not establish that the entire disputed balance had been discharged. Its significance lay in supporting the assessee’s case that the liability continued to subsist. Taken together with the books and supporting records, the payment undermined the inference that the entire liability had ceased during the relevant year. Unregistered Agreement Was Not Sufficient to Reject the Transaction The agreement was also questioned because it had been executed on letterhead and was unregistered. The CIT(A) found that no compulsory registration requirement had been demonstrated on the facts of the case. Its unregistered character therefore did not automatically make the transaction non-genuine. This is a fact-specific finding. The decision does not dispense with registration where another applicable law requires it. It rejects the assumption that every commercial agreement must be registered before the related liability can be recognised. Accounting Treatment Also Required Examination The appellate order recorded that ₹1,62,25,000 represented prepaid expenditure, while ₹1,61,75,000 had been debited to the profit and loss account. The prepaid portion had been carried as an asset rather than claimed as expenditure. This was relevant because section 41(1) requires examination of whether an allowance or deduction had previously been made in respect of the relevant loss, expenditure or trading liability. The entire creditor balance could not automatically be brought to tax without that inquiry. 41 However, the figures reproduced in the order do not reconcile with the total credit of ₹3,79,89,000. They should therefore be treated as figures stated in the judgment, rather than presented as a mathematically verified reconciliation. For the expenditure charged to the profit and loss account, the assessee had furnished the agreement, ledger and TDS details. No material demonstrated that the services were fictitious. Tribunal’s Decision The Tribunal found the CIT(A)’s reasoning logical and declined to interfere. Accordingly, the Revenue’s appeal was dismissed, sustaining deletion of the entire addition. The appellate reasoning also referred to CIT v. Sugauli Sugar Works (P.) Ltd. [1999] 236 ITR 518 (SC) concerning the requirement of remission or cessation and the insufficiency of mere passage of time. indiankanoon.org Author’s Comments An unverified liability and a ceased liability are different propositions. Section 41(1) requires satisfaction of its specific conditions; suspicion about the underlying transaction cannot substitute for evidence of remission or cessation. One qualification is essential. Explanation 1 to section 41(1) expressly covers unilateral writing off of a liability in the assessee’s accounts. The reference to Sugauli Sugar Works should therefore not be read as granting immunity to every unilateral write-back under the applicable statutory provision. Here, the material finding was that no write-back had occurred. 41 The practical lesson is to preserve agreements, ledgers, service evidence and banking records. Such evidence enables the assessee to demonstrate that an outstanding commercial obligation remains payable despite the creditor’s independent compliance failures. Cases Discussed CIT v. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI This appeal is filed by the Department against the order dated 18-Feb-2026 passed by the Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC for the A.Y 2021-22. 2. The Department has raised the following grounds of appeal: 1. On facts and in law, the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in deleting the addition of Rs.3,79,89,000/-made by the Assessing Officer u/s 41(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 without properly appreciating the facts and circumstances of the case. 2. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred in holding that there was no cessation of liability, ignoring the fact that the assessee failed to substantiate the genuineness of the liability with credible evidence during the assessment proceedings. 3. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred in relying upon subsequent payment made by the assessee, without appreciating that the liability remained unverified and unsupported by confirmation OR return of income of the creditor. 4. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred in ignoring that the creditor, M/s Skylark International, had not filed its return of income, thereby casting serious doubt on the genuineness of the liability. 5. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred in not appreciating that the agreement produced by the assessee was not supported by credible evidence and was insufficient to establish the genuineness of the transaction. 6. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred in holding that provisions of section 41(1) are not applicable, without considering that the surrounding circumstances clearly indicated that the liability was not genuine and had effectively ceased. 7. The order of the Ld. CIT(A) is contrary to the facts on record and is not sustainable in law. 3. Although the revenue has taken seven grounds of appeal but the issue involved in this appeal is the deletion of addition of Rs.3,79,89,000/- made by the AO u/s.41(1) of the IT Act. 4. During proceedings before us the Ld. DR argued that vehemently against the order passed by the Ld. CIT(A) deleting the addition of Rs.37989000/- made by the AO. The Ld. DR argued that the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in considering that there was sucession of liability. He also ignored the fact that the assessee failed to substantiate the genuineness of liability with credible evidence during assessment proceedings. 5. The Ld. DR also argued that the Ld. CIT(A) has wrongly considered the subsequent payment made by the assessee without appreciating the fact that the liability itself remained unverified and un supported by conformation or return of income of the creditor. Thus, the Ld. DR also brought it on record that the creditor M/s. Skylark International, had not filed its return of income. 6. Per contra the Ld.Counsel of the assessee relied on the order of the Ld. CIT(A) and supported the facts on record in his order. 7. We have considered the findings given in the assessment order and by the Ld. CIT(A) in his appellate order on this issue. We find that the Ld. CIT(A) has given a very speaking order which is reproduced as under :- “6.2.4. I have carefully considered the assessment order and detailed submissions filed by the appellant along with documentary evidences. The addition of Rs. 3,79,89,000/- has been made by the Assessing Officer u/s 41(1) mainly on the ground that the appellant had credited the said amount in the name of M/s Skylark International and the said party had not filed return of income and therefore the genuineness of transaction could not be verified. The AO therefore treated the entire credit balance as cessation of liability. 6.2.5. On careful examination of the assessment order, it is observed that the Ld. AO has treated the outstanding balance payable to M/s Skylark International as ceased liability primarily on two grounds: firstly, that the vendor had not filed return of income; and secondly, that the agreement produced was executed on letterhead and not registered. Additionally, the Ld. AO observed that the liability had remained unpaid for a substantial period. 6.2.6. However, it is seen that the liability is reflected in the books of account of the appellant as outstanding and payable. There is no finding in the assessment order that the appellant has written back the liability in its accounts. There is also no evidence brought on record to show that the creditor has waived or remitted the amount. In absence of such material, it cannot be inferred that the liability has ceased. The appellant has also produced bank statements showing actual payments made to Skylark International in subsequent period, which clearly establishes that the liability has not ceased but has been discharged through banking channels. 6.2.7. The reasoning that non-filing of return by the vendor leads to cessation of liability is not sustainable. The tax compliance of a third party does not determine the subsistence of liability in the books of the appellant. The existence of liability depends upon contractual and commercial relationship between the parties and not upon filing status of income tax return by the creditor. The fact that subsequent payment of Rs. 41,73,000/- was made in the next financial year assumes significance. Such payment is a clear acknowledgment of subsisting liability. If the liability had ceased during the year under consideration, there would have been no occasion to discharge part of it subsequently. The Ld. AO has not controverted this fact with any adverse evidence. 6.2.8. Further, the mere fact that agreement was not registered does not automatically render the transaction non-genuine. There is no statutory mandate in the present facts requiring compulsory registration for recognition of liability. Commercial agreements executed between parties cannot be disregarded solely on technical grounds unless supported by contrary evidence. 6.2.9. A crucial aspect not appreciated in the assessment order is the correct accounting treatment of the entire amount of Rs. 3,79,89,000/-, which has been duly explained by the appellant. The break-up of the amount is as under: Total amount credited to Skylark International – Rs. 3,79,89,000/ Less: Amount booked as Pre-paid expense – Rs. 1,62,25,000/- Net amount debited to Profit & Loss Account – Rs. 1,61,75,000 It is therefore evident that a substantial portion of the amount has been treated as prepaid expenditure and carried as an asset in the balance sheet. This amount has not been claimed as deduction in the Profit & Loss Account. Once the amount is shown as an asset, it cannot be treated as income or cessation of liability. The Assessing Officer has treated the entire credit balance as income without appreciating this fundamental accounting treatment. 6.2.10. It is further noted that the balance amount of Rs. 1,61,75,000/- has been debited to the Profit & Loss Account as expenditure incurred towards services rendered by M/s Skylark International in the ordinary course of business. The appellant has furnished the service agreement, ledger account and TDS deduction details evidencing that the expenditure relates to digital marketing and software development services obtained for business purposes. The expenditure is recorded in the regular books of account, subjected to tax audit and tax has been deducted at source under section 194C. The Assessing Officer has not brought any material on record to demonstrate that the services were not rendered or that the expenditure was fictitious or excessive. Accordingly, the said expenditure is allowable as business expenditure. 6.2.11. Most importantly, the appellant has produced bank statements evidencing actual payments made to M/s Skylark International in subsequent period, clearly establishing that the liability has not ceased but has been discharged through banking channels. Once the liability has been actually paid, the very basis of addition under section 41(1) ceases to exist. 6.2.12. It is also noted that no independent inquiry appears to have been conducted by the Ld. AO to establish that the liability was fictitious or had ceased. No material has been brought on record demonstrating that the creditor denied the transaction or that the liability had become legally unenforceable during the relevant year. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of CIT v. Sugauli Sugar Works (P) Ltd. has clearly held that mere unilateral entry or mere lapse of time does not amount to cessation of liability unless there is evidence of remission. Applying the ratio of the said decision, it is evident that in absence of material establishing remission or cessation, section 41(1) cannot be invoked. 6.2.13. Considering the totality of facts and circumstances, it is held that the essential condition of “remission or cessation” has not been established by the Ld. AO. The addition appears to have been made on suspicion rather than on concrete evidence. The statutory requirements of section 41(1) are not satisfied in the present case. Accordingly, the addition of Rs. 3,79,89,000/- made under section 41(1) is deleted.” 8. After considering the arguments made by both the parties during proceedings before us and the findings given by the Ld. CIT(A) in his order we find that the findings given by the Ld. CIT(A) is very logical. Therefore, in our considered view there is no need to make any interference on this issue in the findings given by the Ld. CIT(A). Accordingly, revenue’s appeal on this issue is dismissed. 9. In the result, the appeal filed by the revenue is dismissed.
