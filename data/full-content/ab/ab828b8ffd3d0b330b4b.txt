EconomyFinMin Nirmala Sitharaman Chairs 30th FSDC Meeting In Delhi; RBI Governor, SEBI Chief AttendEdited by: Priya RaghuvanshiUpdated: Oct 23, 2026, 15:15 ISTFinance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman chaired the 30th FSDC meeting in Delhi, attended by the RBI Governor and key financial sector regulators.Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman (Image Source: X/Finance Ministry)follow usfollow usUnion Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman chaired the 30th meeting of the Financial Stability and Development Council (FSDC) in New Delhi on Friday, bringing together the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Governor, financial sector regulators and senior government officials. The meeting brought representatives from key financial institutions and government departments together to discuss matters concerning financial stability, regulatory coordination and the development of India's financial sector.According to a social media post by the Ministry of Finance, "Union Minister for Finance and Corporate Affairs Smt. Nirmala Sitharaman chairs the 30th meeting of the Financial Stability and Development Council #FSDC, in New Delhi, today."RBI Governor and senior officials from several Finance Ministry departments were among those present at the meeting. These included secretaries from the Department of Economic Affairs, Department of Revenue and Department of Financial Services.The Chief Economic Adviser to the Government of India also attended the meeting, along with the chairpersons of India's major financial sector regulatory bodies.These included the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI), International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA), Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA) and Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI).Officials from the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) and the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) were also present, adding representatives from the technology and cybersecurity sectors to the discussions.What Is The Financial Stability And Development Council?The FSDC is India's highest-level institutional forum for deliberations on financial stability, financial sector development and cooperation among regulatory authorities.Apart from monitoring financial stability, the council's responsibilities cover financial inclusion and financial literacy. It also deals with macroprudential supervision, which involves assessing risks that could affect the financial system as a whole, including those arising from large financial conglomerates.The council provides a platform for different financial regulators and government departments to coordinate their approaches to issues affecting the broader financial system.When Was FSDC Set Up And Who Chairs It?The government established the FSDC through a notification issued on December 30, 2010. The Union Finance Minister serves as its chairperson, while its membership includes the RBI Governor, senior Finance Ministry officials and heads of key financial sector regulators.The council also has a subcommittee chaired by the RBI Governor. This body examines issues raised by its members relating to financial stability, regulatory coordination and the development of financial markets and institutions.Based on the issues under consideration, the council and its subcommittee deliberate on possible measures and recommend appropriate steps where necessary.Why Is The FSDC Important?The FSDC plays a central role in bringing India's financial regulators and government authorities onto a common platform. Its mandate spans risks to financial stability, coordination between regulators and measures to strengthen the financial sector.The presence of senior officials from the Finance Ministry, RBI, regulatory institutions, MeitY and CERT-In at the latest meeting reflects the range of institutions involved in financial oversight and related areas.The council's structure allows authorities to deliberate on interconnected issues that may affect financial markets, institutions and the wider economy.
