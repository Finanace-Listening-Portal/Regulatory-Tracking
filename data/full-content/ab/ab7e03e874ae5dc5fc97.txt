Summary: Canara Bank’s Canara Tax Saver Scheme is a named five-year term deposit offered through its Fixed Deposit and Kamadhenu Deposit streams. The bank publishes a ₹100 minimum in multiples of ₹100, a ₹1,50,000 maximum per person, no pledge or loan facility, and no premature closure except on the depositor’s death under applicable rules. Canara Bank’s domestic retail term-deposit card for deposits below ₹3 crore, effective 17 March 2026 and checked on 23 November 2026, states 6.25% per annum for the five-year to ten-year bucket and an additional 0.50 percentage point for an eligible senior citizen, producing 6.75% per annum. Deposits made in tax year 2026-27 require section 123 and Schedule XV of the Income-tax Act, 2025 analysis; the deduction shares a ₹1.5 lakh aggregate ceiling, is unavailable under section 202s concessional regime, and does not exempt interest. Eligible deposits are subject to DICGC’s ₹5 lakh aggregate limit per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity. No A new qualifying deposit must independently satisfy then-current law For tax year 2026-27, apply section 123 and Schedule XV The bank page’s section 80C wording reflects the earlier Act The Canara Bank product page describes the deduction under section 80C of the Income-tax Act, 1961. From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 governs tax year 2026-27. section 123 allows an individual or Hindu undivided family a deduction for qualifying sums enumerated in Schedule XV, subject to the Schedule’s conditions and an aggregate ceiling of ₹1,50,000. The official transition FAQ confirms that section 123 structurally retains the specified-savings deduction previously associated with section 80C. The ₹1,50,000 amount is not a separate limit for this one certificate. Life-insurance premium, provident-fund contribution, tuition fees and other qualifying payments can use the same aggregate ceiling. A deposit of ₹1,50,000 does not create an additional deduction if the taxpayer has already exhausted the ceiling. Conversely, the scheme’s maximum does not require the customer to deposit the full amount; the deduction is limited to the qualifying amount actually paid or deposited, subject to the law. The concessional regime changes the deduction result The Income Tax Department’s transition FAQ states that section 123 deduction is not available when the assessee is taxed under the new concessional regime in section 202. A depositor can still hold the product as a bank deposit, but the tax-saver label does not override the chosen computation regime. The tax file should compare regimes using total income and all permitted items rather than treating the FD’s gross interest rate as a tax benefit. A cumulative Kamadhenu deposit can accrue and compound interest without paying cash to the savings account, so the depositor should obtain Canara Bank’s annual interest certificate and reconcile the amount with AIS and Form 26AS. Waiting until maturity can create omissions across several tax years. Section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 sets the bank-interest withholding threshold at ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen, subject to the provision’s conditions and aggregation. Canara Bank uses core banking, so the customer should review interest across branches and deposits rather than only this certificate. Whether TDS is deducted does not decide taxability: interest below the threshold can still be taxable, while TDS above it is credit against the final liability rather than a separate tax. Any Form 15G, Form 15H, lower-deduction certificate or other withholding position must be valid under current law and the depositor’s facts. Failure to deduct does not exempt income. Excess deduction should be reconciled to the tax credit and corresponding income. Do not quote a guaranteed maturity amount from the card alone A maturity figure depends on the booking date, interest stream, compounding convention, payout instructions, tax withholding and any death-claim event. The official card provides rates and approximate annualised yield, not one universal maturity value. A depositor should rely on the bank-generated receipt or illustration for the selected option and retain it without treating the result as a return forecast for a later booking. DICGC cover aggregates every eligible Canara Bank balance DICGC’s official guide states that eligible savings, fixed, current and recurring deposits are insured up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank in the same right and same capacity, including principal and accrued interest. The limit is not ₹5 lakh for each tax-saver certificate, account or branch. A customer with savings balances and other Canara Bank deposits must aggregate them when assessing the insured amount. For example, a depositor with ₹1,50,000 in the tax-saver scheme and ₹3,70,000 of other eligible Canara Bank principal and accrued interest in the same capacity has ₹5,20,000 in aggregate; the ordinary DICGC ceiling remains ₹5,00,000. Genuine different capacities and separately insured banks are analysed under DICGC’s rules. Insurance is not a promise of immediate withdrawal, protection against rate changes or cover above the statutory ceiling.
