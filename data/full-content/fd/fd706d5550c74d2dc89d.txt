Consultation Paper on Recommendations pursuant to Review of provisions of SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018 related to Initial Public Offer by Small and Medium Enterprises October 14, 2026 www.taxguru.in 1. Objective The objective of this consultation paper is to seek comments / views / suggestions from the public and other stakeholders on the recommendations pursuant to review of provisions of SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018 (SEBI ICDR Regulations or ICDR Regulations) related to Initial Public Offer (IPO) by Small and Medium Enterprises (SME). 2. Background Over time, the SME segment has evolved significantly, with a substantial increase in the number of issuers accessing the platform, funds raised through SME IPOs and the number of investors participating in such issues. In view of these developments, SEBI constituted a Working Group for review of the provisions relating to IPOs by SMEs under the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018 (Working Group), with the objective of undertaking a comprehensive review of the existing SME framework, including its eligibility criteria, issue and allocation framework, trading and market-making arrangements, migration framework and other related provisions. The Working Group held deliberations on various aspects of the framework and submitted its recommendations to SEBI. The Report of the Working Group, containing its detailed recommendations and rationale, may be accessed at :- https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/oct- 2026/WG%20Report%20for%20Review%20of%20SME%20Provisions_p.pdf The recommendations of the Working Group were thereafter placed before the Primary Market Advisory Committee (PMAC) for its consideration and deliberation. Pursuant to the deliberations of the Working Group and PMAC, subsequent internal deliberations were undertaken by SEBI and certain aspects of the recommendations were further considered and refined. Accordingly, the proposals set out in this Consultation Paper reflect the Working Group recommendations, PMAC deliberations and subsequent internal deliberations. 3. Need for Review of the Framework The SME regulatory framework was designed with the objective of providing smaller enterprises access to public capital while incorporating safeguards commensurate with the relatively smaller size, scale and investor base of such issuers. However, the SME Platform has undergone significant evolution since the framework was introduced. The increase in the number of issuers, capital raised, investor participation, market capitalization and migration of entities listed on SME Platform to the Main Board indicates that the segment now encompasses enterprises with a wider range of size, maturity and market profile. In this context, certain provisions of the existing SME regulatory framework may warrant reconsideration from the perspective of proportionality, ease of compliance, cost of fund raising, investor participation and consistency with the Main Board framework. Certain SME Platform specific requirements, including those relating to minimum application and allotment, trading lots, underwriting, compulsory market making, migration and other issue- related requirements, may have acquired different relevance in light of the evolution of the market. At the same time, the review also needs to recognize that issuers accessing SME Platform may continue to have characteristics that distinguish them from issuers accessing Main Board and, therefore, requirements necessary for investor protection and orderly functioning of the SME Platform may need to be retained. Accordingly, the present review seeks to examine the SME regulatory framework holistically, with the objective of creating a regulatory architecture that is proportionate to the nature and scale of issuers accessing SME Platform, facilitates wider access to and participation in the capital market, reduces avoidable regulatory and financial burden, and at the same time preserves appropriate safeguards for investors. The proposals set out in the subsequent sections accordingly seek to rationalise SME Platform specific requirements where warranted, align provisions with the Main Board framework where the underlying regulatory objective is similar, and introduce greater flexibility where the existing framework may www.taxguru.in impose unnecessary constraints, while retaining requirements that are considered necessary from the perspective of investor protection and market integrity. www.taxguru.in 5. Proposals 5.1. Review of Eligibility Framework 5.1.1. Shift from Post-Issue Paid-up Capital to Post-Issue Market Capitalization 5.1.1.1. Existing Provision Regulation 229(1) and Regulation 229(2) of the ICDR Regulations presently provides for eligibility to access the SME Platform based on the post-issue paid-up capital of the issuer. An issuer having post-issue paid-up capital of up to ₹10 crore is eligible to make an IPO under the SME framework, while an issuer having post-issue paid-up capital of more than ₹10 crore and up to ₹25 crore may also issue specified securities in accordance with Chapter IX of the ICDR Regulations. 5.1.1.2. Need for Review The paid-up capital represents the nominal share capital of an issuer and may not adequately reflect the economic size, scale, valuation or growth potential of an enterprise. An analysis of 735 Main Board and SME IPOs undertaken during the last three financial years, where the paid-up capital at the time of IPO was more than ₹10 crore, also indicated a significant disconnect between paid-up capital and market capitalization whereby issuers with relatively low paid-up capital could have substantially higher market capitalization. Consequently, an issuer may have post-issue paid-up capital exceeding the prescribed SME threshold and be mandatorily required to access the Main Board, even though its business operations, financial scale and overall enterprise size may be more appropriately suited to the SME Platform. Further, reliance on paid-up capital may, in certain cases, require Mainboard issuers to undertake capital restructuring, including bonus issuance through capitalization of reserves, solely to meet the prescribed paid-up capital threshold. Such restructuring may entail additional costs, taxation implications and compliance requirements, without necessarily representing any corresponding increase in the underlying economic size or scale of the enterprise. 5.1.1.3. Recommendation of the Working Group 5.1.1.3.1. An issuer having post issue market capitalization up to ₹1,000 crore shall be required to access only SME Platform; 5.1.1.3.2. An issuer having post issue market capitalization above ₹1,000 crore and up to ₹4,000 crore may also access SME Platform. 5.1.1.3.3. Eligibility determined at the stage of filing of the Pre-filed draft offer document draft offer document, as the case may be, should remain subject to the post issue market capitalization applicable at the subsequent stage of filing the Red Herring Prospectus in case of book-built issue or Prospectus in case of fixed price issue. Accordingly, where an issuer has initially filed a Pre-filed Draft Red Herring www.taxguru.in Prospectus/ Draft Red Herring Prospectus (“DRHP”) for a Main Board IPO but, at the stage of filing the Red Herring Prospectus in case of book-built issue or Prospectus in case of fixed price issue, its estimated post issue market Capitalization falls to ₹1,000 crore or below, the issuer would be required to re- file the draft offer document under the SME framework. 5.1.1.3.4. Conversely, where an issuer has initially filed its DRHP under the SME framework and its estimated post-issue market Capitalization subsequently exceeds ₹1,000 crore at the stage of filing the Red Herring Prospectus in case of book-built issue or Prospectus in case of fixed price issue, the issuer may continue to proceed under the SME framework, subject to upper threshold of ₹4,000 crore 5.1.1.3.5. However, where an issuer has initially filed its DRHP under the SME framework and its post-issue market capitalization subsequently exceeds ₹4,000 crore at the stage of filing the Red Herring Prospectus in case of book-built issue or Prospectus in case of fixed price issue, the issuer would be required to re-file the Pre-filed DRHP/ DRHP under the Mainboard IPO Framework. 5.1.1.4. Rationale The proposed shift seeks to ensure that the eligibility framework is based on a parameter that more closely reflects the economic scale and market value of an enterprise, rather than its nominal capital structure. Market capitalization reflects the value attributed by investors to an enterprise and, accordingly, captures differences in scale and valuation even where enterprises may have similar levels of paid-up capital. This is particularly relevant for enterprises operating under capital-efficient or asset-light business models, where substantial economic value and growth potential may not be accompanied by a proportionate increase in paid-up capital. The Working Group further analysed the market capitalization of entities presently listed on the SME Platform based on prevailing market prices. The analysis indicated that while a substantial proportion of SME-listed entities have market capitalization below ₹1,000 crore, there is also a meaningful segment which has attained higher market capitalization. Accordingly, ₹1,000 crore was considered an appropriate principal benchmark, with a flexibility band extending up to ₹4,000 crore. 5.1.1.5. Existing Provision Regulation 229(4) of the ICDR Regulations prescribes certain conditions for an issuer which has been formed through conversion from a proprietorship concern or partnership firm or LLP, including requirements relating to the period of existence of the issuer following such conversion and the financial track record to be demonstrated by the issuer. The existing provision is primarily framed around conversion of an existing business into a company and requires the issuer to have completed at least one full financial year after such conversion before accessing the SME Platform. 5.1.2.2. Need for Review The requirement of completion of one full financial year may result in materially different effective waiting periods depending upon the date of conversion. For instance, an issuer converted on April 1 may become eligible after approximately 12 months, whereas an issuer converted immediately thereafter may effectively have to wait for almost 24 months. Such variation may not necessarily be commensurate with the underlying objective of ensuring a reasonable period of operations in corporate form. Further, the existing provision is primarily linked to statutory conversion and may not cover situations where substantially similar economic outcomes are achieved through other forms of business reorganization. An issuer may, for instance, acquire, transfer or otherwise take over a business previously carried on by a proprietorship concern, partnership firm or LLP and subsequently derive a significant portion of its revenue from such business. This may result in similarly placed business reorganizations being subject to different regulatory treatment depending upon the legal form through which the reorganization is undertaken. 5.1.2.3. Recommendations of the Working Group 5.1.2.3.1. Replace the existing requirement of completion of one full financial year with four consecutive completed quarters from the date of conversion or from the latest effective date of the qualifying business reorganization, as applicable; 5.1.2.3.2. Require such issuer to have filed at least one annual audited financial statement or part thereof prepared after the conversion or business reorganization with the Registrar of Companies, in accordance with The Companies Act, 2013, before filing the Draft Offer Document; 5.1.2.3.3. Extend the applicability of the provision beyond statutory conversion to cases where, pursuant to a business reorganization, one or more businesses previously carried on by a proprietorship concern, partnership firm or limited liability partnership are carried on by the issuer, where the issuer derives more than fifty per cent of its revenue, as disclosed for the latest financial year in the Draft Offer Document, from such business or businesses. www.taxguru.in 5.1.2.3.4. Introduce an appropriate clarificatory Explanation defining “business reorganization”, i.e. "business reorganization" includes acquisition, transfer, vesting, succession, a scheme of arrangement or any other arrangement whereby one or more businesses previously carried on by a proprietorship concern, partnership firm or limited liability partnership are carried on by the issuer.”; 5.1.2.3.5. Permit the historical operating track record of the proprietorship concern, partnership firm or LLP to be reckoned for determining compliance with the eligibility requirements under Chapter IX in cases covered by the provision. 5.1.2.4. Rationale The proposed framework retains the investor-protection objective of Regulation 229(4), while providing a more uniform and certain seasoning requirement. Replacing one full financial year with four consecutive completed quarters would ensure a consistent period of operation in corporate form, irrespective of the date of conversion or business reorganisation. Extending the provision to qualifying business reorganisations would ensure consistent treatment of substantially similar transactions and address potential regulatory arbitrage. The 50% revenue threshold would ensure that the provision applies where the reorganised business constitutes a substantial part of the issuer's operations. The requirement for post-reorganisation audited financial information, while permitting the historical operating track record of the predecessor business to be reckoned, would balance investor protection with recognition of the continuity of the underlying business. 5.1.2.5. Existing Provisions The existing framework requires the issuer to have minimum operating profits (Earning Before Interest and Taxes) of ₹1 crore from operations in at least two of the three preceding financial years. 5.1.3.2. Need for Review The existing threshold of ₹1 crore of operating profit may no longer adequately reflect the scale and financial capacity expected of an issuer accessing the public capital market. Further, the requirement is based on achievement of the prescribed threshold in at least two individual financial years, which may not appropriately capture the issuer's overall operating performance where profitability varies across financial years. 5.1.3.3. Recommendation of the Working Group 5.1.3.3.1. Existing requirement based on achievement of a specified profit threshold in individual financial years may be replaced with a requirement that the issuer have an Average Operating Profit of not less than ₹3 crore per year during the preceding three financial years, calculated based on the restated and consolidated financial statements. 5.1.3.4. Rationale The proposed revision would align the profitability criterion more closely with the Main Board framework and ensure that the threshold remains meaningful for issuers accessing the public capital market. Assessing profitability on an average basis over the preceding three financial years would provide a better measure of the issuer’s overall operating performance, rather than being dependent on the profitability achieved in individual financial years. The proposed ₹3 crore average operating profit requirement would also serve as an additional investor-protection safeguard, by ensuring that issuers accessing the SME Platform demonstrate a reasonable and sustained level of operating profitability. 5.1.3.5. Background The existing SME IPO framework prescribes financial eligibility requirements for issuers seeking to access the public capital market. Under the proposed framework, Regulation 229(6) requires an issuer to have an average operating profit of not less than ₹3 crore during the preceding three financial years, calculated on the basis of restated and consolidated financial statements. The Main Board framework already recognises that failure to satisfy the prescribed financial eligibility conditions need not, by itself, constitute an absolute bar to accessing the public market. Regulation 6(2) provides an alternative eligibility route to an issuer which does not satisfy the conditions specified in Regulation 6(1), subject to the issue being undertaken through the book-building process, at least 75% of the net offer being allotted to Qualified Institutional Buyers, and refund of the entire subscription money where the prescribed QIB allocation is not achieved. A similar policy consideration arises in the context of SME issuers. While the proposed increase and recalibration of the profitability criterion under Regulation 229(6) would ensure that issuers accessing the SME Platform demonstrate a meaningful level of operating performance, it may also exclude certain growth-oriented enterprises which have an established business and adequate operating history but have not yet achieved the prescribed level of profitability. This issue is particularly relevant for enterprises in a growth or scale-up phase, where resources may be deployed towards technology, capacity expansion, product development, market development or geographical expansion, resulting in lower or deferred profitability despite the existence of an established and viable business. Accordingly, the question arises whether the SME framework should similarly provide a calibrated alternative route for issuers which do not satisfy the profitability criterion, while retaining the fundamental safeguards relating to business track record, business continuity and promoter stability. The subsequent deliberation therefore considered whether the approach under Regulation 6(2) could be adapted to the SME framework, while appropriately recognising the distinct characteristics and risk profile of SME issuers. 5.1.4.2. Proposal It is proposed to introduce a specific alternative route under Regulation 229(7) for an issuer which does not satisfy the profitability requirement prescribed under Regulation 229(6). Under the proposed route: 5.1.4.2.1. the issuer would continue to be required to satisfy the eligibility requirements under Regulations 229(3), 229(4) and 229(5); www.taxguru.in 5.1.4.2.2. the relaxation would operate only in respect of the profitability criterion under Regulation 229(6); 5.1.4.2.3. the issue would necessarily be undertaken through the book-building process; 5.1.4.2.4. at least 75% of the net offer would be required to be allotted to Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIBs); and 5.1.4.2.5. if the issuer is unable to achieve the prescribed 75% QIB allocation, the entire subscription money would be required to be refunded. Thus, Regulation 229(7) would create an alternative route within Chapter IX for issuers which have demonstrated business maturity but do not satisfy the prescribed profitability threshold. 5.1.4.3. Rationale The proposed provision seeks to provide a calibrated alternative route for issuers which have an established business and satisfy the requirements relating to track record, business continuity and promoter continuity, but do not meet the profitability criterion under Regulation 229(6). The rationale is to distinguish business maturity from financial performance. While profitability is an important indicator of financial performance, the absence of the prescribed profitability should not, by itself, prevent an otherwise established growth-oriented enterprise from accessing the public market. The relaxation is therefore proposed only in respect of Regulation 229(6). The requirements under Regulations 229(3), 229(4) and 229(5) would continue to apply, as these requirements are intended to ensure that an issuer has an adequate operating history and business and promoter continuity. Such requirements cannot be substituted merely through participation by QIBs. The proposed approach is also broadly aligned with the principle underlying Regulation 6(2) of the Main Board framework, which permits an issuer not satisfying the prescribed financial eligibility conditions to access the public market subject to at least 75% of the net offer being allotted to QIBs. The higher QIB participation under Regulation 229(7) would provide an additional layer of institutional assessment and market-based validation of the issuer. The proposal would consequently enable growth-stage enterprises which have not yet achieved significant profitability or enterprise value, but have demonstrated an established business, to access the capital market. At the same time, the continued applicability of the business maturity requirements, coupled with the 75% QIB allocation and full-refund safeguard, would ensure that such access is accompanied by appropriate investor protection. 5.1.4.4. Existing Provisions Regulation 230(2) permits an issuer to utilize a portion of the proceeds raised through the public issue towards General Corporate Purposes (“GCP”). The amount proposed to be utilised for GCP, as disclosed in the Draft Offer Document and the Offer Document, is presently subject to a dual limitation of 15% of the amount being raised by the issuer or ₹10 crore, whichever is less. 5.2.2. Need for Review While the 15% ceiling ensures that only a defined portion of the issue proceeds can be utilised for general corporate purposes, the additional ₹10 crore absolute cap results in the permissible amount remaining unchanged even where the size of the public issue is substantially higher. This may disproportionately constrain issuers undertaking relatively larger SME IPOs, notwithstanding that the underlying corporate requirements for which funds may legitimately be deployed could increase with the scale of the enterprise and the size of the issue. 5.2.3. Recommendation of the Working Group 5.2.3.1. Removal of the ₹10 crore absolute ceiling prescribed for utilization of issue proceeds towards General Corporate Purposes, while retaining the existing ceiling of 15% of the amount being raised by the issuer. 5.2.4. Rationale The proposed removal of the ₹10 crore absolute ceiling would provide greater flexibility to SME issuers while retaining the 15% ceiling as a proportionate safeguard on the use of issue proceeds towards General Corporate Purposes. At the same time, retaining the 15% limit would ensure that the SME framework continues to prescribe a stricter framework as compared to the Main Board, balancing issuer flexibility with investor protection. 5.2.5. Existing Provisions Regulation 230(1)(f) and Regulation 230(1)(g) prescribe that an OFS by a selling shareholder shall not exceed 20% of the total issue size, and the shares offered through OFS by the selling shareholders shall not exceed 50% of their respective pre-issue shareholding, respectively. 5.3.2. Need for Review The existing SME framework does not provide a dedicated regulatory framework for OFS, unlike the Main Board framework, and does not prescribe a minimum holding period or corresponding exemptions for securities proposed to be offered through an OFS. The absence of a one-year holding requirement may permit securities acquired shortly before the IPO to be offered to public investors. Further, the existing limits of 20% of the issue size and 50% of the selling shareholder’s pre- issue shareholding may restrict genuine shareholders, particularly QIBs, who have invested in the issuer for a longer period and may seek to realise a portion of their investment through the IPO. The existing restriction may therefore limit the ability of genuine institutional investors to provide capital to growth-oriented enterprises at the pre-IPO stage, as the potential for an appropriate exit through the IPO is an important consideration in such investment decisions. 5.3.3. Recommendations of the Working Group 5.3.3.1. Delete the existing OFS-related provisions from Regulation 230 and introduce a separate, dedicated regulation setting out the conditions and safeguards applicable to an Offer for Sale in an SME IPO. 5.3.3.2. Align the SME OFS framework, including the one-year holding period and relevant exemptions, with the Main Board framework under Regulation 8, suitably calibrated for SME IPOs. 5.3.3.3. Provide flexibility to QIB selling shareholders to offer their securities through the OFS beyond the existing 20% ceiling, subject to the proposed one-year holding- period requirement, the individual ceiling of 50% of the selling shareholder’s pre- issue shareholding on a fully diluted basis, and other applicable safeguards. 5.3.4. Rationale A dedicated OFS framework would provide greater clarity and certainty regarding the conditions applicable to selling shareholders in an SME IPO, while aligning the framework, where appropriate, with the Main Board framework. Introducing a one-year holding period with specified exemptions would ensure that the OFS primarily facilitates exits by shareholders who have remained invested in the issuer for a meaningful period. www.taxguru.in Providing greater flexibility to genuine QIB selling shareholders would facilitate appropriate partial exits and support continued institutional participation in growth-stage enterprises. At the same time, retaining appropriate safeguards, including the holding-period requirement, would ensure that the OFS does not become predominantly a promoter exit mechanism and that the IPO continues to serve its primary capital-raising objective. 5.3.5. PMAC Recommendation The recommendations of the Working Group were deliberated upon by PMAC. PMAC accepted the recommendations of the Working Group, except for the recommendation to retain the 50% ceiling on the shares offered through OFS by a QIB selling shareholder. PMAC recommended that, in addition to removal of the existing 20% ceiling on the OFS component, the 50% ceiling on the shares offered by a QIB selling shareholder, relative to its pre-issue shareholding, may also be removed. Rationale :- The proposed removal of the 50% ceiling for QIB selling shareholders would provide greater flexibility to genuine institutional investors to realize their investments, particularly where such investors have provided capital to the issuer at the pre-IPO stage and have held their investment for a meaningful period. As QIBs are subject to a one-year holding-period requirement, removal of the 50% ceiling would facilitate legitimate institutional exits while reducing restrictions that may otherwise affect their willingness to invest in growth-stage enterprises. At the same time, the holding-period requirement would continue to ensure that the flexibility is not available for short-term acquisitions made with the primary objective of exiting through the IPO. The distinction between QIB selling shareholders and promoters would also ensure that the proposed flexibility does not dilute the safeguards against promoter-led exit. 5.3.6. Subsequent Internal Deliberations The proposed Regulation 229(7) is intended to facilitate access to the SME Platform for issuers that have an established business but do not satisfy the prescribed profitability criterion, with 75% QIB participation serving as an additional safeguard. Therefore, It may be proposed that the exemption available to a QIB selling shareholder from the quantitative OFS limits under Regulation 230A shall not apply to an issue undertaken pursuant to Regulation 229(7). Accordingly, QIB selling shareholders in an issue under Regulation 229(7) would remain subject to the applicable OFS limits prescribed under Regulation 230A. Rationale :- The proposed restriction is also in line with the approach under Regulation 8A of Chapter II, which prescribes specific limits on OFS by selling shareholders in issues undertaken www.taxguru.in pursuant to Regulation 6(2), notwithstanding the mandatory QIB participation. Existing Provisions Regulation 238 and Regulation 239 of ICDR Regulations prescribes lock-in requirements for specified securities held by promoters and persons other than promoters. In respect of minimum promoters’ contribution, the securities are required to be locked in for three years from the date of allotment in the initial public offer or commencement of commercial production, whichever is later. The promoters’ holding in excess of the minimum promoters’ contribution is subject to a staggered lock-in, with 50% locked in for two years and the remaining 50% for one year. For pre-issue capital held by persons other than promoters, the existing framework prescribes a one-year lock-in from the date of allotment. The framework also contains requirements relating to specified securities held by the promoter group and the manner in which lock-in is implemented through the depository mechanism. 5.4.2. Need for Review The existing lock-in framework contains certain requirements that differ from the Main Board framework and may result in a longer or more complex lock-in period for similarly placed shareholders. In particular, the linkage of the promoters’ minimum contribution lock-in to commencement of commercial production and the staggered lock-in of excess promoter holding may result in extended and variable lock-in periods. Further, the one-year lock-in applicable to non-promoter pre-issue capital is longer than the corresponding Main Board requirement, thereby impacting liquidity. There is also a need to provide greater clarity and operational facilitation for promoter group holdings and for creation of lock-in through the depository mechanism where the securities cannot otherwise be marked as locked-in. 5.4.3. Recommendations of the Working Group 5.4.3.1. Remove the linkage of three-year lock-in applicable to the minimum promoters’ contribution to the date of commencement of commercial production. 5.4.3.2. The promoters’ holding in excess of the minimum promoters’ contribution be locked in for one year from the date of allotment, replacing the existing staggered requirement under which 50% of such holding is locked in for two years and the remaining 50% for one year. 5.4.3.3. Reduce the lock-in applicable to pre-issue capital held by persons other than promoters from one year to six months from the date of allotment, aligning it with the Main Board framework. 5.4.3.4. Provide for a one-year lock-in from the date of allotment for specified securities held by the promoter group, to ensure continued promoter alignment and prevent circumvention of promoter lock-in requirements. www.taxguru.in 5.4.3.5. Extend to SME issuers the Main Board mechanism whereby, in cases where lock-in cannot be created through the depository mechanism, the depositories may, upon instructions from the issuer, record such securities as “non-transferable” for the applicable lock-in period. 5.4.4. Rationale The proposed changes seek to simplify and rationalise the lock-in framework while aligning the SME framework, where appropriate, with the Main Board framework. Reckoning the three-year lock-in for minimum promoters’ contribution from the date of IPO allotment would provide certainty and remove the variability arising from linkage to commencement of commercial production. A uniform one-year lock-in for promoter holdings in excess of minimum promoters’ contribution would further simplify the existing staggered mechanism. Reducing the lock-in for non-promoter pre-issue capital to six months would align the requirement with the Main Board framework and facilitate legitimate exits by pre-IPO investors, while the proposed one-year lock-in for promoter group holdings would ensure continued promoter alignment and prevent circumvention of promoter lock-in requirements. Extending the depository mechanism for recording locked-in securities as “non-transferable” would also provide operational clarity and facilitate effective implementation of the lock-in requirements where securities cannot otherwise be marked as locked-in. 5.4.5. Existing Provision Regulation 260 requires an SME IPO to be 100% underwritten, without restricting the underwriting obligation to the minimum subscription level. Further, requiring the entire issue to be underwritten irrespective of actual investor demand may result in underwriting costs even where adequate subscription is received. Existing Provisions The existing framework provides for migration between the SME Exchange and the Main Board primarily based on the issuer’s post-issue paid-up capital, subject to fulfilment of the eligibility criteria of the respective platform. Under Regulation 276, a listed issuer with post-issue paid-up capital of less than ₹25 crore may migrate to the SME Exchange, subject to shareholder approval through a special resolution and fulfilment of the SME Exchange’s listing eligibility criteria. Under Regulation 277, an SME-listed issuer with post-issue paid-up capital of more than ₹10 crore and up to ₹25 crore may migrate to the Main Board, subject to shareholder approval and fulfilment of the Main Board listing eligibility criteria. Further, Regulation 280(2) requires an SME-listed issuer to migrate to the Main Board where its post-issue paid-up capital is likely to exceed ₹25 crore pursuant to a further issue of capital, subject to specified conditions. However, the issuer may undertake such further issue without migration if it undertakes to comply with the applicable LODR requirements for Main Board-listed companies. 5.7.2. Need for Review The existing migration framework is primarily based on post-issue paid-up capital thresholds, which may not adequately reflect the issuer’s actual size, scale and maturity. In light of the proposed revision of the SME eligibility framework, including the introduction of post-issue market capitalization-based thresholds, there is a need to review the migration framework to ensure that the criteria for migration are aligned with the revised eligibility framework and appropriately reflect the issuer’s size and readiness to operate on the Main Board. 5.7.3. Recommendations of the Working Group 5.7.3.1. Remove the provision for migration from the Main Board to the SME Platform, thereby retaining the SME Platform as a pathway for growth-oriented enterprises to graduate to the Main Board, rather than as a reverse migration route. 5.7.3.2. Retain the principle of optional migration from the SME Platform to the Main Board, without linking such migration to a specific paid-up capital threshold. An issuer may migrate to the Main Board where its market Capitalization is ₹1,000 crore or more but less than ₹4,000 crore, subject to the issuer fulfilling the criteria specified by the Main Board Exchange. 5.7.3.3. Considering that the issuer would, upon migration, become subject to the more stringent Main Board regulatory framework, approval of the Board of Directors may be considered sufficient for migration to the Main Board. www.taxguru.in 5.7.3.4. Omit the existing provision requiring mandatory migration to the Main Board where the post-issue paid-up capital is likely to exceed ₹25 crore pursuant to a further issue of capital, including through rights issue, preferential issue or bonus issue, along with the related conditions for undertaking such further issue without migration. 5.7.3.5. The migration framework should instead be aligned with the proposed market-Capitalization-based threshold, with migration to the Main Board being triggered where the issuer's post-issue market Capitalization, calculated at the prevalent market price, exceeds ₹4,000 crore, subject to the issuer fulfilling the criteria specified by the Main Board Exchange. 5.7.4. Rationale The proposed framework would establish the SME Platform as a growth and graduation platform, with migration principally being from the SME Platform to the Main Board, rather than permitting reverse migration from the Main Board. Moving away from a fixed paid-up capital threshold and aligning migration with market capitalisation would provide a more appropriate measure of the issuer’s size and scale. Optional migration at market capitalisation of ₹1,000 crore or more but below ₹4,000 crore would provide flexibility to eligible issuers, while mandatory migration beyond ₹4,000 crore would ensure that issuers of a larger scale operate under the Main Board framework. Since migration would subject the issuer to the more stringent Main Board regulatory framework, requiring shareholder approval may be unnecessary, and Board approval may provide an efficient mechanism for optional migration. Omission of the existing mandatory migration trigger linked to further issue of capital would also align the framework with the proposed market-capitalisation-based migration thresholds and provide greater flexibility for SME-listed issuers to raise further capital. 5.7.5. Proposed Methodology for determination of Market Capitalization for Migration www.taxguru.in In order to ensure consistency and certainty in the application of thresholds, proposed above, it is necessary to prescribe a standardised methodology for determining the market capitalisation of an issuer. In the absence of a specified methodology, fluctuations in the market price of the securities around the relevant threshold may result in uncertainty regarding the applicability of the migration requirements. Accordingly, it is proposed to specify the manner in which the applicable market price and the number of equity shares shall be considered for determining the market capitalisation for the purpose of migration. 5.7.6.1.1. Proposal It is proposed that the market capitalisation of an issuer, for the purpose of determining the applicability of the ₹1,000 crore and ₹4,000 crore migration thresholds, shall be calculated as on the date of application for migration. The market capitalisation shall be determined as follows: Market Capitalisation = Applicable Market Price × Total number of equity shares outstanding as on the date of application for migration For this purpose, “Applicable Market Price” means the average of (i) the average of the weekly high closing prices, and (ii) the average of the weekly low closing prices of the equity shares quoted on the SME Platform during the twelve weeks preceding the date of application for migration, adjusted for bonus issues, stock splits and consolidation of shares, as applicable. Accordingly, the market capitalisation test shall be undertaken only as on the date of application for migration and shall not be recalculated on a continuing basis thereafter. In case the market capitalisation determined as on the date of application exceeds ₹4,000 crore, the issuer shall be required to migrate to the Main Board, subject to fulfilment of the criteria specified by the Main Board Exchange. Any subsequent decline in the market price or market capitalisation of the issuer after the date of application shall not affect the requirement to migrate to the Main Board. Similarly, where the market capitalisation as on the date of application is ₹1,000 crore or more but less than ₹4,000 crore, the issuer may undertake voluntary migration to the Main Board, subject to fulfilment of the applicable Main Board eligibility requirements. 5.7.6.1.2. Rationale The proposed methodology seeks to provide a clear, objective and consistent basis for determining the migration thresholds. Using the average of the weekly high and low of closing prices over the twelve weeks preceding the date of application would reduce the impact of short-term price volatility on the determination of the applicable market capitalisation. Limiting the assessment to the date of application would also provide certainty to issuers, stock exchanges and investors regarding the applicable migration requirement. In particular, where the market capitalisation exceeds ₹4,000 crore on the date of application, treating the migration requirement as continuing notwithstanding subsequent fluctuations in market price would prevent repeated re-testing of the threshold and avoid uncertainty regarding whether an issuer is required to migrate. The proposed approach would also ensure that an issuer does not move in and out of the Main Board migration requirement merely on account of short-term movements in its market price. 5.7.7. Background The proposed revisions to the SME framework involve changes across various aspects of the IPO and post-listing framework. Accordingly, upon implementation of the revised provisions, there may be issuers already listed on the SME Platform, issuers who are in the process of making a public issue, and transactions or arrangements initiated under the existing framework. The applicability of the revised provisions to such existing or ongoing matters therefore requires appropriate transitional arrangements to provide clarity on the manner and stage from which the revised framework would apply. In the absence of such arrangements, the implementation of the revised framework may result in uncertainty or unintended retrospective application of the revised requirements. The Working Group also recommended that SEBI may provide appropriate transition provisions for implementation of the revised SME framework. 5.9.2. Proposal 5.9.2.1. Draft Offer Documents filed after the transition period The revised framework shall mandatorily apply to draft offer documents filed on or after the expiry of three months from the date of notification of the revised framework in the Official Gazette. Illustration: Where the revised framework is notified on December 31, 2026, the revised framework shall mandatorily apply to draft offer documents filed on or after April 01, 2027. 5.9.2.2. Draft Offer Documents filed during the three-month transition period Where a draft offer document is filed during the three-month period commencing from the date of notification of the revised framework in the Official Gazette, the issuer may, at its option, elect to be governed by either the existing framework or the revised framework. The issuer shall clearly specify in the draft offer document the framework under which the proposed issue is to be undertaken and shall thereafter comply with the eligibility, listing and other applicable requirements of the framework so opted for. This would allow issuers to make an informed choice between the existing and revised frameworks during the transition period, particularly where the eligibility and permissible listing route may differ under the two frameworks. Illustration: Where the revised framework is notified on December 31, 2026, an issuer filing its draft offer document between January 01, 2027 and March 31, 2027 may elect either the existing framework or the revised framework. www.taxguru.in The following illustrates the possible position: Paid-up Capital Market Capitalisation Existing Framework Revised Framework Below ₹10 crore Below ₹1,000 crore SME SME Below ₹10 crore ₹1,000 crore to < ₹4,000 crore SME SME / Main Board Below ₹10 crore ₹4,000 crore or more SME Main Board ₹10 crore to < ₹25 crore Below ₹1,000 crore SME / Main Board SME ₹10 crore to < ₹25 crore ₹1,000 crore to < ₹4,000 crore SME / Main Board SME / Main Board ₹10 crore to < ₹25 crore ₹4,000 crore or more SME / Main Board Main Board ₹25 crore or more Below ₹1,000 crore Main Board SME ₹25 crore or more ₹1,000 crore to < ₹4,000 crore Main Board SME / Main Board ₹25 crore or more ₹4,000 crore or more Main Board Main Board Thus, the choice during the transition period is a choice of framework, and not merely a choice of stock exchange.
