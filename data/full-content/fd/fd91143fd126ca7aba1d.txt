ET Compagnie Vs ACIT (Delhi High Court) The Delhi High Court allowed petitions by ESS Advertising (Mauritius) S.N.C. Et Compagnie and ESS Distribution (Mauritius) S.N.C. Et Compagnie for AY 2013–14. The Assessing Officer had issued draft assessment orders under Section 144C read with Section 143(3), proposing additions concerning advertising receipts and subscription income. The Dispute Resolution Panel declined jurisdiction because the Mauritius partnership firms were not eligible assessees within Section 144C(15)(b). Following that decision, the Assessing Officer issued reassessment notices under Section 148 on 29 March 2018, relying on the same material previously considered. The petitioners challenged the absence of fresh material, the attempt to overcome the failed draft-assessment procedure, and the mechanical sanction under Section 151. The High Court held that the Assessing Officer had used Section 144C despite earlier findings that the petitioners were ineligible for that procedure. The failure to complete the draft assessments could not justify reassessment on the same facts. The Court also held that the Additional Commissioner’s endorsement approving reopening lacked discernible application of mind. It quashed the notices, the recorded reasons and the orders disposing of objections, without costs. It could also happen that a person may file a return of income incorrectly offering for purposes of taxation a sum lower than the correctly calculated income. Both these situations have been obviously kept in view in 2nd Explanation to section 147 and in its clauses (a) and (b). In either event the Assessing Officer would invoke the powers conferred upon him by section 147 of the Income- tax Act culminating with the completion of the asses sment. It is also conceivable that the incorrectness of the return may not be detected or noticed within the time period set- down in section 153.
