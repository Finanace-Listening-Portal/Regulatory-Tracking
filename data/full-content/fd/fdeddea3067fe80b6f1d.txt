Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Vs Artificial Limb Manufacturing Corporation of India (ITAT Lucknow) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Lucknow Bench “A”, dismissed the Revenue’s appeal against relief granted to Artificial Limb Manufacturing Corporation of India (ALIMCO) for Assessment Year 2014-15. Following a TDS verification survey on 18 January 2017 and proceedings initiated under sections 201(1) and 201(1A) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, the Assessing Officer had treated ALIMCO as an assessee in default and raised an aggregate demand of ₹2,80,84,059, comprising ₹2,80,81,413 and ₹2,646. The Addl./Joint CIT(A)-4, Bengaluru, by order dated 6 February 2025, deleted the disputed demand while partly allowing the assessee’s appeal. The Revenue argued that ALIMCO had failed to supply the requested evidence despite adequate opportunities during the original proceedings. It also objected that the appellate authority had admitted additional evidence without granting the Assessing Officer an effective opportunity to comment, contrary to rule-46a -section-2504.html” title=”Rule 46A admission of additional evidence in appeal” Rule 46A of the Income-tax Rules, 1962. The Department sought reversal of the appellate order and restoration of the matter to the Assessing Officer for fresh adjudication. ALIMCO responded that it had furnished details on several occasions and that the final request, dated 17 March 2021, required extensive employee-wise information by 22 March 2021 during the COVID-19 period. Although it sought additional time, the Assessing Officer passed the order on 26 March 2021. On merits, the corporation submitted that section 192A concerns payment of accumulated provident fund balances to employees rather than an employer’s regular contributions. Its recognised provident fund contributions were stated to remain within 12% of basic salary plus dearness allowance, while the EPFO pension component represented 8.33% of the relevant pay. For gratuity and leave encashment, ALIMCO explained that premiums were paid directly to Life Insurance Corporation of India on an actuarial valuation basis to fund future employee benefits, without direct payments to employees or directors. It similarly described its LIC-administered superannuation scheme, to which it contributed 10% of basic salary plus dearness allowance. The first appellate authority relied on the recognised provident fund trust documentation, LIC confirmation and employee TDS certificates, and concluded that the disputed employer contributions did not give rise to the alleged withholding default on the facts presented. The Tribunal found that the CIT(A) had extensively examined the dispute and recorded detailed findings after providing adequate opportunity to the parties. Crucially, the Revenue produced no adverse material rebutting the findings that the contributions went to the recognised provident fund, EPFO or LIC rather than directly to employees, and identified no material showing that contributions exceeded the applicable statutory limits. It therefore rejected the Revenue’s grounds, including its challenge concerning the appellate proceedings, and dismissed ITA No. 59/LKW/2026. The order does not establish an unrestricted exemption for all employer contributions irrespective of their nature or limits. Cases Discussed MMTC Limited Vs Employees Provident Fund Organisation, (2013) LLR 347; W.P.(C) Nos. 7949/2005, 2045/2006 and 11906/2006; 14/02/2013 (Delhi High Court) — The decision is cited in the CIT(A)’s reproduced discussion regarding employer provident fund obligations. The ITAT did not separately analyse this precedent. The facts in brief are that a Tax Deduction at Source (“TDS”) verification survey under section 133A(2A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (“Act”, for short) was conducted by the Revenue on 18.01.2017 at the premises of the assessee. Thereafter, a notice under sections 201(1)/201(1A) of the Act dated 01.05.2019 was issued and duly served upon the deductor-assessee. In response thereto, the assessee furnished the requisite details and tendered its explanation. The Assessing Officer (“AO”, for short), after examining the material placed on record and considering the submissions made by the assessee, treated the assessee as an assessee in default under sections 201(1)/201(1A) read with sections 206C(6A)/206C(7) of the Act and raised a demand of Rs.2,80,81,413/- and Rs.2,646/- respectively. Accordingly, the aggregate demand raised by the AO amounted to Rs.2,80,84,059/-. Aggrieved against the impugned tax demand, the assessee preferred an appeal before the Ld. CIT(A) who after considering the submissions partly allowed the appeal of the assessee. Thereby, the impugned demand raised by the Assessing Authority was deleted. Aggrieved, the Revenue is in appeal before this Tribunal. 3. Apropos to the grounds of appeal, the Ld. Departmental Representative for Revenue vehemently argued that the Ld. Addl. CIT(A) was not justified in passing the impugned order. He submitted that sufficient opportunities were afforded to the assessee during the proceedings u/s 201(1) and 201(1A) of the Act to furnish the requisite details and supporting documents. However, the assessee failed to comply with the statutory notices and did not furnish the requisite evidence before the Assessing Officer. It was further submitted that the ld. CIT(A) admitted additional evidence furnished by the assessee during the appellate proceedings without providing an effective opportunity to the Assessing Officer to examine the same and offer his comments thereon as required under Rule 46A of the Income Tax Rules, 1962 (“Rules”, for short). We have heard the rival submissions and perused the material available on record. We find that the Ld. CIT(A) has extensively examined the issue in dispute and recorded detailed findings of fact and provided adequate opportunity to the parties. He decided the dispute by observing as under:– “5. Section 192A reads as under: 192A. Payment of accumulated balance due to an employee Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, the trustees of the Employees’ Provident Fund Scheme, 1952, framed under section 5 of the Employees Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952 (19 of 1952) or any person authorized under the scheme to make payment of accumulated entrance due to employees, shall, in a case where the accumulated balance due to an employee participating in a recognized provident fund is includible in tis total income owing to the provisions of rule 8 of Part A of the Fourth Schedule not being applicable, at the time of payment of the accumulated balance due to the employee, deduct income-tax thereon at the rate of ten per cent Provided that no deduction under u this section shall be made where the amount such payment or, as the case may be, the aggregate amount of such payment to the payee is less than [fifty] thousand rupees.” 5.1.1 Employer’s Contribution to Provident Fund: Contribution is made to the Provident Fund for the employee’s welfare by the employee and the employer. The deduction on this contribution made is available under section 80C of the Act. Provident fund is a kind of security fund in which the employees contribute a part of their part and the employer also contributes on behalf of their employees. Section 10/11 and 10(12) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 defines the exemption on the amount add the provident fund. Additionally, the amount allowed as a deduction on controlling to the provident fund is dealt in section 80C of the Income Tax Act. The types provident funds are: i. Recognised Provident Fund (RPF) as recognised by Commissioner of Income Tet under EPF and Miscellaneous Provision Act, 1952. It applies to enterprises employing at least 20 employees. 2. Unrecognised Provident Fund (UPF) is not recognised by the Commissioner of Income Tax. The employers and employees start these schemes. 3. Public Provident Fund (PPF) under Public Provident Fund Act, 1968 is another system of contributing to the provident fund. Self-employed people can also take part in this scheme. A minimum contributing limit of Rs. 500 per annum and a maximum of Rs. 150000 per annum are set 4. Statutory Provident Fund (SPF) is meant for employees of Government or Universities or Educational Institutes affiliated to university. 5.1.1.1 The Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF) is a savings scheme designed to help Individuals build a retirement corpus. While it encourages savings, it is subject to taxation under Section 192A of the Income Tax Act. This section mandates Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on premature withdrawals from the EPF, However, there are several deductions and exemptions available that can help individuals optimize their EPF benefits and minimize tax abilities. 5.1.1.2 Section 192A of the Income Tax Act deals with the Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on premature withdrawals from the Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF). This 192A section mandates that the Employees’ Provident Fund Scheme, 1952, must deduct TDS if an employee withdraws their EPF balance without meeting the conditions specified under Rufe 8, Part A of the Fourth Schedule. Introduced through the Finance Act 2015, section 192A of the Income Tax Act 1961 requires that tax be deducted at the time of EPF payment. The deducted TDS must be deposited with the government by the entrusted deductors within a week of the following month in which the tax is deducted. However, for TDS deducted in March, the deposit deadline is on or before April 30th. 5.1.1.3 Employer’s contribution to the EPF account of an employee is exempt upto 12% of the ‘PF Salary’. Any excess contribution by employer over and above 12% of ‘PF Salary’ is taxable in hands of employee. However, an overall cap on exemption limit was introduced in the Union Budget 2020. Effective from 1st April 2020, any employer’s contribution to Provident Fund (PF), NPS and superannuation exceeding INR 0.75 mn a year is taxable as perquisites in the hands of the employee under section 17(2)(vii) of IT Act, 1961 under the head “Income from Salary. Necessary withholding to be done by the employer on the same and required to be reported in Employee’s Form 16 and Form 12BA. 5.1.1.4 As per Finance Notification (31 Aug 2021) released by CBDT, the interest relating to the contribution in Provident Fund exceeding a specified limit of Rs.2.50,000/- shall be a part of taxable Income of the subscriber. This is application with effect from 01 April 2022 for the financial year 2021-2022. 5.1.1.5 Thus, Employer’s Contribution to Provident Fund is chargeable to tax as per rule to Part A of the Fourth Schedule of the Income Tax Act, 1961 which prescribes as under “Employer’s annual contributions, when deemed to be income received by employee That portion of the annual accretion in any previous year to the balance at credit of an employee participating in a recognised provident fund as consists of (a) contributions made by the employer in excess of twelve per cent, of the part of the employee, and interest credited on the balance to the credit of the employee in so far as it coeds one third of the salary of the employee or is allowed at a rate exceeding such rate as may be fixed by the Central Government in this behalf notification in the official Gazette, shall be deemed to have been received the employee in that previous year and shall be included in his total income that previous year, and shall be liable to income-tax [*].” 5.1.1.6 The Appellant has paid 12% of Basic Salary plus DA (minus Employees Pension Scheme share) towards Employer’s Contribution to Provident Fund in accordance with the EPF Act and within prescribed limit of Part A of the Fourth Schedule of the Income Tax Act, 1961. ALIMCO ECPF Trust is a recognized Provident Fund Trust.
