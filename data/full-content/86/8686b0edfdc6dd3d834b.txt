Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Sevantilal Fakirchand And Company Private Limited (NCLT Mumbai) Summary: The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Mumbai Bench-I, admitted a voluntary corporate insolvency application filed by Sevantilal Fakirchand and Company Private Limited under, holding that a corporate guarantor may seek initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against itself when its liability under an invoked corporate guarantee becomes enforceable and it is unable to discharge that liability. The company had furnished corporate guarantees and securities in favour of a consortium of lenders led by Bank of India for credit facilities extended to Antrix Diamond Exports Private Limited. Following default by the principal borrower, which was subsequently subjected to insolvency proceedings and ordered to be liquidated, the consortium initiated recovery action under the. The Tribunal held that the liquidation of the principal borrower did not, by itself, extinguish the corporate guarantor’s continuing contractual obligations. The corporate applicant disclosed aggregate defaulted liabilities of ₹13,76,00,96,154.65, comprising ₹13,73,42,55,039.36 arising from corporate guarantees to consortium lenders, ₹1,62,85,476.29 owed to a related-party unsecured financial creditor, and ₹95,55,639 owed to operational creditors. The underlying debt was stated to have been incurred on 31 March 2006, while default occurred on 7 January 2016, when the principal borrower’s account was classified as a non-performing asset. Bank of India had issued a demand notice on 8 January 2018 under, followed by symbolic possession measures on 16 April 2019. A further notice dated 30 July 2026 demanded ₹13,73,42,55,039.36, including interest, from the guarantors and mortgagors. The corporate applicant also produced board and shareholder approvals, creditor records, audited financial statements and documents relating to the principal borrower’s insolvency proceedings. Examining the enforceability of the guarantees, the Tribunal relied on the principle contained in, under which the liability of a surety is co-extensive with that of the principal debtor unless the contract provides otherwise. The Bench observed that upon default by the principal borrower and invocation of the corporate guarantees, the guarantor’s liability became enforceable in accordance with the guarantee terms. Consequently, where the guarantor cannot discharge those enforceable financial obligations, it may invoke Section 10 of the IBC, provided the applicable statutory requirements are satisfied. The Tribunal also examined the company’s financial position, noting that its audited statements for FY 2023-24, FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26 disclosed no operating revenue or other income and reflected losses of ₹10,070, ₹5,236 and ₹5,000, respectively. The Tribunal further considered the procedural compliance accompanying the application. The company’s board had approved commencement of CIRP on 1 August 2026, followed by a special resolution on 27 August 2026. The application was filed in Form 6 under Rule 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016. Pursuant to the Tribunal’s direction dated 5 October 2026, the directors submitted an affidavit on 7 October 2026 undertaking to bear CIRP expenses and cooperate with the Interim Resolution Professional. Finding the application complete, the default established and the default amount exceeding the statutory threshold under, the NCLT admitted CP (IB) No. 998 of 2026 and ordered commencement of CIRP. By its order dated 27 October 2026, the Tribunal appointed Sachin Rajendra Singhvi as Interim Resolution Professional and declared a, restricting legal proceedings, disposal of assets, enforcement of security interests and recovery of property from the corporate applicant. It directed an immediate public announcement, transfer of management to the IRP, cooperation by the company’s officers and directors, and provision of funds for insolvency expenses. The directors’ contributions from personal sources towards CIRP expenses were directed to be treated as interim finance, subject to the order’s terms. The applicant was also directed to pay ₹1,50,000 towards initial IRP fees and expenses up to the first meeting of the Committee of Creditors. The decision establishes, on the facts of the admitted application, that an enforceable corporate guarantee liability may provide the foundation for a corporate guarantor’s own insolvency proceedings under Section 10 of the IBC. The present Company Petition has been filed by SEVANTILAL FAKIRCHAND AND COMPANY PRIVATE LIMITED (hereinafter referred to as the “Corporate Applicant” or “Corporate Debtor”) under Section 10 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (“Code”), read with Rule 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016, seeking initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (“CIRP”) against itself. Brief Facts 2. The Corporate Applicant was incorporated as a private limited company on 15.05.1989 under the provisions of the Companies Act, 1956. The authorised share capital of the Corporate Debtor is ₹5,00,00,000/-, while its paid-up equity share capital is ₹1,00,00,000/-. 3. It is submitted that the Corporate Debtor has outstanding liabilities towards various creditors, comprising Operational Creditors, an Unsecured Financial Creditor who is a Related Party, and a consortium of Financial Creditors in respect of the Corporate Guarantee(s) furnished by the Corporate Debtor for the credit facilities availed by M/s. Antrix Diamond Exports Private Limited (hereinafter referred to as the “Principal Borrower”). 4. It is further submitted that the principal financial liability of the Corporate Debtor arises from the Corporate Guarantee(s) and security furnished in favour of a consortium of lenders led by Bank of India, in respect of the credit facilities availed by the Principal Borrower. Upon the Principal Borrower committing default in repayment of the said credit facilities, the liability of the Corporate Debtor under the Corporate Guarantee(s) became enforceable. Consequently, the consortium lenders initiated recovery and enforcement proceedings under the Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 (“SARFAESI Act”). 5. It is stated that Bank of India, being the Lead Bank of the consortium, had issued a demand notice dated 08.01.2018 under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act. The enforcement proceedings thereafter progressed to measures under Section 13(4) of the SARFAESI Act, including the taking of symbolic possession of the relevant secured assets on 16.04.2019. The Principal Borrower subsequently underwent insolvency proceedings and was ordered to be liquidated. However, the liability of the Corporate Debtor under the Corporate Guarantee(s) continued to subsist. Thereafter, Bank of India, acting as the Lead Bank of the consortium, issued a fresh demand notice dated 30.07.2026 under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act against, inter alia, the guarantors and mortgagors, including the Corporate Debtor, setting out the updated outstanding liability claimed by the consortium lenders. 6. It is further stated that the requisite approvals of the Board of Directors and the members of the Corporate Debtor have been obtained, including the approval dated 01.08.2026 and the Special Resolution dated 27.08.2026 passed under Section 10 of the Code. Accordingly, the present Application has been filed seeking initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (“CIRP”) against the Corporate Debtor. 7. It is a settled position of law that, in terms of Section 128 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, the liability of a surety is co-extensive with that of the principal debtor, unless otherwise provided by the contract. Accordingly, the liability of the Corporate Applicant under the Corporate Guarantee(s) is enforceable in accordance with the terms thereof upon the occurrence of the stipulated conditions. 20. Upon the Principal Borrower committing default and the creditor invoking the Corporate Guarantee(s), the liability of the Corporate Applicant as guarantor becomes enforceable in accordance with the terms of the guarantee. Where the Corporate Applicant is itself unable to discharge its enforceable financial obligations arising therefrom, it may seek initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process against itself under Section 10 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, subject to fulfilment of the statutory requirements prescribed thereunder. 21. This Tribunal had required, vide order dated 05.10.2026, the Petitioner’s directors to place on record an affidavit stating that they shall make available requisites funds to fund the CIRP process in case the Petition is admitted. Pursuant to that, the petitioner’s directors have filed the affidavit dated 07.10.2026 undertaking as follows: “3. I hereby undertake that the Suspended Board of Directors of Sevantilal Fakirchand and Company Private Limited shall bear the costs and expenses of the CIRP, including the CIRP costs incurred during the course of the CIRP, in accordance with the provisions of the Code and applicable law. 4. I further undertake that the Suspended Board of Directors shall extend full cooperation and assistance to the Interim Resolution Professional / Resolution Professional (“IRP/RP”) appointed by this Hon’ble Tribunal in accordance with law. 5. I further undertake that the Suspended Board of Directors shall provide to the IRP/RP all documents, books, records, information, particulars and other materials as may be sought or required by the TRP/RP for the purpose of conducting and completing the CIRP in accordance with the provisions of the Code and the applicable regulations. 6. I further undertake that the Suspended Board of Directors shall make available and provide access to all relevant documents and information as may be required by the IRP/RP in discharge of their duties and functions under the Code. 7. I further undertake that the Suspended Board of Directors shall provide all necessary assistance, cooperation and information to the TRP/RP and shall comply with all lawful requirements and directions issued by the IRP/RP in connection with the CIRP.” 22. After hearing the submissions and upon perusing the supporting documents annexed with the Petition, this Bench is of the view that the application made by the Corporate Applicant is complete in all respects as required by law. It clearly shows that the Corporate Applicant is in default of a debt due and payable, and the default is in excess of minimum amount stipulated under section 4(1) of the IBC, at the relevant time. 23. Therefore, the default stands established and there is no reason to deny the admission of the Petition. In view of this, this Adjudicating Authority admits this Petition and orders initiation of CIRP against the Corporate Applicant. ORDER 24.
