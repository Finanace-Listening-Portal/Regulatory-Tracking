Summary: An appeal under the Goods and Services Tax (GST) law is an important statutory remedy available to taxpayers against orders passed by adjudicating authorities involving disputed tax demands, denial of Input Tax Credit, incorrect calculations, procedural irregularities or differences in interpretation of legal provisions. Section 107 of the CGST Act, 2017 permits an aggrieved taxpayer to approach the Appellate Authority within three months from the date of communication of the order, with a further one-month extension where sufficient cause for delay is established. Appeals are generally filed electronically through Form GST APL-01 with supporting documents and the prescribed pre-deposit. The appellant must ordinarily pay the admitted tax, interest, fine, fee and penalty, together with 10% of the remaining disputed tax, subject to applicable statutory provisions and limits. The article highlights the financial difficulties faced by small taxpayers in arranging pre-deposits, particularly where demands arise from technical discrepancies or genuine interpretational disputes. It also discusses the importance of clearly drafted grounds of appeal, proper documentary evidence, consideration of taxpayer submissions, reasoned adjudication orders and compliance with the principles of natural justice. Practical challenges such as GST portal errors, mismatches in records, delayed communication of orders and procedural complexities are also examined. The article emphasises that appellate authorities should independently examine the facts and legal provisions while providing taxpayers a fair opportunity of being heard, and that taxpayers should act within the prescribed limitation period, preserve relevant records and present their appeals with complete supporting evidence. A fair, transparent and effective appellate mechanism is essential to protect taxpayer rights, prevent unjustified demands and reduce unnecessary GST litigation. Sometimes, the proper officer passes an order against the taxpayer due to a difference of opinion, a mistake in calculation, non-availability of documents at the time of hearing or a different interpretation of the provisions of law. In such cases, the taxpayer has the right to challenge the order before the appellate authority. Legal Provisions Section 107 of the CGST Act, 2017 provides for an appeal before the Appellate Authority against an order passed by an adjudicating authority. Generally, the appeal is required to be filed within three months from the date of communication of the order. The appellate authority may condone a further delay of one month if sufficient cause is shown. The appeal is normally filed electronically in Form GST APL-01, along with the required documents and prescribed pre-deposit. Pre-Deposit for Appeal One of the major difficulties faced by taxpayers is the requirement to deposit the prescribed amount before filing an appeal. Under Section 107, the appellant is generally required to pay the admitted amount of tax, interest, fine, fee and penalty, along with 10% of the remaining disputed tax amount, subject to the statutory limit and applicable provisions. For small taxpayers, arranging this amount can become difficult, particularly where the demand has been raised on account of a technical issue or a genuine difference of interpretation. Grounds of Appeal While filing an appeal, the taxpayer should clearly explain the facts of the case and the grounds on which the order is being challenged. The grounds may relate to an incorrect demand of tax, denial of eligible Input Tax Credit, violation of the principles of natural justice, non-consideration of documents, incorrect calculation of tax or interest, or an order passed without properly examining the reply submitted by the taxpayer. A speaking order based on the facts, documents and applicable legal provisions is essential for fair tax administration. Practical Difficulties In practice, taxpayers sometimes face difficulties due to GST portal errors, mismatch of records, delayed communication of orders and lack of proper consideration of submissions made during adjudication. Small businesses may also find it difficult to understand the technical requirements and complete the appeal process within the prescribed time. The appellate authority should examine each case independently and provide a reasonable opportunity of being heard wherever required by law. Conclusion The right to appeal is an important safeguard against an incorrect or unjustified tax demand. The purpose of GST should be to collect the tax legally payable and not to create unnecessary hardship for genuine taxpayers. In my view, the department should pass reasoned orders, consider the taxpayer’s reply and supporting documents, and follow the principles of natural justice. Taxpayers, on their part, should file appeals within the prescribed time, prepare proper grounds and maintain all relevant records. A fair and transparent appellate system will strengthen taxpayers’ confidence in GST and reduce unnecessary litigation.
