While the immediate impact of the rate hike is expected to be manageable, the change in stance could have a larger bearing on lenders if funding costs remain elevated for an extended period. Advt For NBFCs, the key issue is the mismatch between the repricing of liabilities and assets. NBFCs rely on bank loans, market borrowings, commercial paper and other wholesale funding, while several of their lending products, including vehicle finance, microfinance, gold loans and small-ticket consumer credit, are either fixed-rate or reprice relatively slowly.“Funding costs can rise quickly, while loan repricing depends on the product and competition,” CareEdge said in its analysis of the RBI’s rate hike.This could put pressure on spreads, particularly for NBFCs that have a high dependence on wholesale funding, shorter liability maturities or limited pricing power. Even a modest increase in funding costs could quickly erode spreads for such lenders, CareEdge said.NBFCs therefore face a trade-off between passing on the higher cost of funds to borrowers and risking slower loan growth, or absorbing part of the increase and taking a hit to profitability.The competitive environment could make the pass-through more difficult. RBI data for August 2026 showed non-food bank credit growing 18.8 per cent year-on-year, compared with 15.8 per cent growth in lending by NBFCs. This could limit the ability of some NBFCs to raise lending rates without affecting demand, CareEdge said.Impact will differ across NBFCsThe impact of the rate hike is not uniform across the non-bank sector and will depend on the extent to which assets and liabilities are linked to floating rates. Advt Jefferies said smaller private banks and NBFCs generally have a higher share of fixed-rate or internally benchmarked loans, which means their loan books do not reprice as quickly following changes in policy rates. It therefore sees smaller banks and NBFCs as facing neutral to slight risk from rate hikes.However, some NBFCs could benefit where the proportion of floating-rate assets is higher than floating-rate liabilities. Jefferies identified prime housing finance companies such as LIC Housing Finance and Bajaj Housing Finance, as well as diversified NBFC Aditya Birla Capital, as lenders with relatively favourable floating-asset versus floating-liability positions. Advt On the other hand, Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services and SBI Cards are more exposed to rate hikes, according to Jefferies.
