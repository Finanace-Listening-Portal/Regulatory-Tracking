Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Salman Basith Vs State of Kerala (Kerala High Court) Summary: Salman Basith, the highest bidder in an e-auction of a secured asset, paid the full sale consideration and received a sale certificate on 18 June 2026. A communication dated 20 July 2026 asked the Sub Registrar to enter the certificate in Book No. 1. Although Basith paid the required stamp duty and registration fees, the Sub Registrar refused registration by memo dated 8 September 2026, citing a GST attachment order dated 14 August 2026 that restrained transfer of properties standing in the borrower’s name. Mutation was refused on the same ground. Basith argued that the attachment came after the completed sale, when the property no longer belonged to the borrower. The respondent bank supported his case. The Government Pleader submitted that the attachment authority was the proper respondent to contest the petition, but that authority did not appear despite service. Applying the Full Bench decision in Fathima v. Canara Bank, the Kerala High Court held that an attachment directed after completion of the sale could not justify refusal to register the sale certificate. It allowed the writ petition, directed registration of Ext.P4 within two weeks and mutation within three weeks of production of a copy of the judgment before the respective respondents, and ordered deletion of the attachment entry from the relevant records. Cases Discussed Fathima v. Canara Bank, Palakkad — 2025 KHC 521, 2025 (3) KLT 367 (Kerala High Court, Full Bench). The Court relied on this decision for the principle that an attachment ordered after completion of the sale cannot justify refusing registration. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF KERALA HIGH COURT 1. Petitioner has filed this writ petition seeking a direction to the 2nd respondent to register Ext.P4 sale certificate and a further direction to respondents 2 and 3 to delete/efface from their records the attachment recorded over the property covered by Ext.P4 sale certificate on the basis of Ext.P6 order of the 5th respondent. It is also prayed that the 3rd respondent be directed to effect mutation. 2. Petitioner participated in an e-auction conducted by the 3rd respondent for sale of a secured asset and emerged as the highest bidder. He paid the entire amount due and Ext.P2 sale certificate was issued on 18.06.2026. The 4th respondent requested the 2nd respondent, Sub Registrar, to enter the sale certificate in Book No.1 by Ext.P3 communication dated 20.07.2026. Petitioner remitted the required stamp duty and registration fees and approached the 2nd respondent for registering the sale certificate. However, the same was refused, pointing out that the 5th respondent has issued an attachment order dated 14.08.2026 under Section 79 read with Section 150 of the CGST Act, 2017, restraining transfer of properties standing in the name of the 6th respondent, the borrower. A memo was issued on 08.09.2026 by the 2nd respondent. A copy of the same has been produced as Ext.P5. Mutation was also refused for the same reason. 3. Heard the learned counsel for the petitioner, the learned Government Pleader and the learned counsel for the Respondent Bank. 4. It is pointed out by the learned counsel for the petitioner that the communication was issued by the 5th respondent after the sale in favour of the petitioner. He, therefore, submitted that the said attachment has no force, as the property no longer belonged to the 6th respondent, the borrower. 5. The learned counsel for the respondent Bank also supported the case of the petitioner. The learned Government Pleader submitted that the 5th respondent is the proper authority to contest this writ petition. Though notice was issued and duly served on the 5th respondent, there is no appearance for the 5th respondent. I find considerable force in the submission made by the learned counsel for the petitioner, relying on the law laid down by the Full Bench of this Court in Fathima v. Canara Bank (2025 KHC 521), that registration cannot be refused for the reason that an attachment has been directed by an authority after the sale was completed. Therefore, I allow this writ petition. The 2nd respondent is directed to register Ext.P4 sale certificate within a period of two weeks from the date of production of a copy of this judgment by the petitioner before the 2nd respondent. The 3rd respondent is directed to effect mutation within a period of three weeks from the date of production of a copy of this judgment by the petitioner before the 3rd respondent. It is also directed that the entry pertaining to the attachment by the 5th respondent shall be deleted from the relevant records.
