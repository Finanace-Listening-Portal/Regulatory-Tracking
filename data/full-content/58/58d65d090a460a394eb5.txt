How to Calculate a Sensible Sum Assured Life cover should be estimated from the household’s actual financial commitments: outstanding debt, dependants’ living expenses, education costs, income replacement needs and existing liquid assets. An arbitrary multiple of salary can be a starting heuristic, but it should not replace a cash-flow analysis. Inflation, the age of dependants and the surviving household’s ability to earn income materially affect the required amount. Consider a household with a housing loan and two dependent children. The death benefit must first be evaluated against liabilities and the present value of future spending, not simply compared with the insured’s current annual salary. Deduct assets that are genuinely available to the family, while allowing for emergency liquidity. Reassess cover after material events such as a new loan, birth of a child or change in income. Premium Quotes and Underwriting Disclosure An online premium illustration is provisional until the insurer completes applicable underwriting. Age, tobacco use, medical history, occupation and other disclosed risk factors can affect eligibility and the final premium. The applicant should answer health and lifestyle questions completely and consistently, including questions about prior investigations and existing insurance. Compare the total premium payable over the intended period, not only a prominently displayed first-year amount. Policy Term, Nomination and Family Readiness The policy term should align with the years during which dependants face meaningful financial exposure. Extending coverage far beyond that horizon may increase cost without solving a real need, while selecting a term that ends before major liabilities mature can leave a protection gap. The actual maximum maturity age and eligible term must be checked against the current product version. Nomination should be completed accurately and reviewed after marriage, divorce, childbirth or death of a nominee. Family members should know the insurer, policy number, premium status and claim-contact route. A policy that cannot be located promptly creates avoidable hardship. Nomination, beneficial entitlement and succession can involve legal distinctions; complex family situations may warrant legal advice rather than reliance on a single form. Claims, Exclusions and Customer Information Sheet The issued policy wording controls the circumstances and process for a claim. Review exclusions, waiting periods if any, suicide-related provisions, grace-period and lapse rules, revival conditions and the consequences of non-payment. Do not assume a term plan pays a maturity amount unless a specific contractual benefit provides one. Product advertisements are not a substitute for the policy contract. The Customer Information Sheet is designed to present key features in an accessible form, but it must be read together with the full wording. A claimant may need proof of death, identity, policy details and additional documents depending on the cause and circumstances. Families should keep copies securely. Where a claim is disputed, use the insurer’s grievance process and applicable regulatory escalation channels.
