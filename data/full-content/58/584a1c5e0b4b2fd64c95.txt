The proposal seeks to clarify the scope of research recommendations under the IFSCA (Capital Market Intermediaries) Regulations, 2025. Under the proposed framework, research entities would be permitted to recommend baskets of securities with specified weightages, based on defined investment strategies or themes. Any entity, including a broker dealer, intending to offer model portfolios would be required to obtain registration as a Research Entity. The consultation paper proposes safeguards relating to portfolio construction, investment methodology, geographical coverage, asset classification, disclosures, benchmarking and portfolio rebalancing. A model portfolio would be distinguished from an ordinary collection of security recommendations through the assignment of specific constituent weightages. Research entities would be required to issue formal research reports containing factsheets, selection methodology, investment objectives, recommended investment horizons and relevant risk disclosures. Portfolio recommendations must also remain consistent with the research entity’s individual opinions on constituent securities. The proposed guidelines further require research entities to define portfolio update intervals and communicate changes to clients, including reasons for rebalancing outside predetermined intervals. Where benchmarks are adopted, they must be clearly identified and consistently applied. Compliance with the proposed guidelines would fall within the compliance audit framework under the CMI Regulations. The draft circular cites Sections 12 and 13 of the IFSCA Act, 2019, read with Regulation 45 of the CMI Regulations, as its enabling provisions. Public comments are invited until 30 November 2026. The document is a consultation proposal, and its draft circular has not yet been issued as a final operative circular. International Financial Services Centres Authority Consultation Paper on Offering ‘Model Portfolio’ by Research Entities in the International Financial Services Centre 1. Objective The objective of this consultation paper is to seek comments from the public and stakeholders on the draft circular for providing clarification that Research Entities may offer model portfolios and the guidelines to be followed by them while offering Model Portfolio. 2. Background and Proposal 2.1 The International Financial Services Centres Authority (“IFSCA”) (Capital Market Intermediaries) Regulations, 2025 (“CMI Regulations”) provide for the regulatory framework for Research Entities operating within the International Financial Services Centre (“IFSC”). Currently, Research entities primarily provide research reports on specific securities, including “buy/sell/hold” recommendations, price targets etc. 2.2 With the growth of the IFSC as a global financial hub, there is an increasing demand for sophisticated research products that go beyond individual security analysis. Market participants have expressed a need to offer ‘Model Portfolios’ as curated baskets of securities with specific weightages based on defined investment themes or strategies. 2.3 Given that IFSC is a jurisdiction for international financial services, these model portfolios are largely expected to be global in nature, often focusing on global markets and utilizing multi-asset strategies including equities, fixed income ETFs, commodity ETFs, etc. 2.4 Currently, the CMI Regulations do not explicitly provide for research entities to offer Model Portfolios. Accordingly, with a view to provide a structured mechanism for research entities to offer diversified investment strategies, it is proposed to clarify that a research recommendation under a research report includes offering a Model Portfolio. 2.5 Accordingly, any entity, including a Broker Dealer, shall be required to seek registration as Research Entity in order to offer Model Portfolio. 3. Key Proposals The major safeguards for research entities offering model portfolio are as under: (a) Weightages: Distinguishing a ‘Model Portfolio’ from a summary of recommendations by requiring the assignment of specific weightages to constituents. (b) Global & Multi-Asset Scope: Explicitly requiring Research Entities to define the geographical universe (e.g., US Markets, Emerging Markets, or other markets) and asset classes (e.g., multi-asset) to ensure investors understand the jurisdictional and market risks. (c) Performance & Benchmarking: Ensuring that performance claims are consistent and comparable against relevant, predefined indices. (d) Transparency in Rebalancing: The rebalancing of portfolios shall be transparently disclosed. The rebalancing of portfolios shall generally be at pre-defined intervals. However, in case of any rebalancing of portfolios other than at pre-defined intervals, the rationale for such rebalancing shall be disclosed. (e) Disclosures: Mandating disclosures for every portfolio including details of the selection methodology, rationale and investment horizon. 4. Reference may be drawn to the International Financial Services Centres Authority (Capital Market Intermediaries) Regulations, 2025 (“CMI Regulations”) which inter-alia specify the regulatory framework for capital market intermediaries, including research entities in the International Financial Services Centre (“IFSC”). 2. CMI Regulations allow a research entity to publish or provide research report with respect to securities. Research report is defined therein as any written or electronic communication that includes research analysis or research recommendation, or an opinion concerning securities or public offer, providing a basis for investment decision. 3. International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA”) is in receipt of representations from stakeholders to allow to offer recommendations in the form of a basket of multiple securities based on specific investment strategies or themes, commonly referred to as ‘model portfolio’. 4. In order to provide a structured mechanism for research entities to offer diversified investment strategies, it is hereby clarified that providing research recommendation by the research entities includes offering model portfolio. 5. Operational Guidelines for offering ‘model portfolio’ 5.1. Definitions For the purpose of this circular, the following terms shall have the meaning as mentioned below: (a) “Model Portfolio” shall mean a basket of securities for which a research report is issued by a research entity recommending the relevant weightages for one or more securities mentioned therein. Explanation- If the research report does not ascribe weightages to the components of basket of securities, then merely a summary or consolidated presentation of securities recommended shall not be regarded as a ‘model portfolio’. (b) “Disclosures” shall mean the set of disclosures as specified in this Circular to be mandatorily included in a model portfolio, to ensure that all the relevant facts and information which could impact the investment decision of a potential investor are adequately made known to the investors. (c) “Launch Date” shall mean the date on which model portfolio was issued by the research entity. (d) “Update Date” shall mean the date of issuance of each updated model portfolio following its review and update by the research entity at predefined intervals. 5.2. Obligations and Responsibilities A research entity offering model portfolios shall ensure compliance with the following obligations and responsibilities: 5.2.1. Model Portfolio Report: A model portfolio shall be issued only through a formal research report. The opinion expressed on any constituent security within the portfolio shall not be contrary to the research entity’s individual research opinion on that security. Each report shall contain a comprehensive ‘factsheet’ setting out basic information, rationale, and underlying methodology. 5.2.2. Methodology: The report shall define the framework for selection of securities and shall indicate the following factors: a) Geographical Universe: The report shall specify the geographical or jurisdictional universe. b) Asset Class/Classification: It shall be labelled to indicate the type of securities included. c) Underlying theme: If applicable, the report shall indicate an underlying theme (such as Tech Momentum, Space Tech, Artificial Intelligence, Fintech Innovation, or Sustainable Finance, etc.) 5.2.3. Parameters: The selection parameters, such as fundamental analysis, technical analysis, or quantitative factor-based modelling, shall be discussed in detail in the report. 5.2.4. Labelling: Model portfolios shall be ‘true to label’, the nomenclature shall clearly state the portfolio type and provide a description of the investment objective for ease of understanding. 5.2.5. Investment Horizon: Every model portfolio report shall specify the recommended investment horizon to allow investors to align the strategy with their own investment period. 5.2.6. Frequency of update of Model Portfolio: Whether the model portfolio would be updated and at what intervals shall be predefined in the report. The rebalancing, if any, of the constituent securities in the model portfolio shall generally be done within the overall framework of the model portfolio and shall be communicated to the clients. However, in case of any rebalancing of portfolios other than at pre-defined intervals, the rationale for such rebalancing shall be communicated to the clients. 5.2.7. Risk Disclosure: The risks associated with the Model portfolio shall be clearly mentioned in model portfolio report. 5.2.8. Benchmarking: A portfolio may be benchmarked against an index. The benchmark index shall be clearly defined and used consistently across all reports. 5.2.9. Audit Requirement: Compliance with these guidelines shall be covered under the compliance audit set out under the CMI Regulations. 6. This Circular is issued in exercise of powers conferred under sections 12 and 13 of the International Financial Services Centres Authority Act, 2019, read with regulation 45 of the CMI Regulations, and shall come into force with immediate effect.
