Personalized solutions can enhance customer trust in insurance. Technology should complement, not replace, human engagement. Alok RungtaInsurance may not have the natural appeal of categories such as travel, apparel or food, but that makes customer experience and marketing technology particularly important for the sector, said Alok Rungta, MD and CEO, Generali Central Life Insurance.Speaking at the seventh edition of ETBrandEquity.com’s MarTech+ Summit 2026, Rungta said insurance customers today have more information and choices than ever before, but this has not necessarily translated into confidence when making purchase decisions.He cited an audience poll conducted during the session, in which only 20 percent of respondents said they were very confident about buying the right insurance policy entirely on their own, while another 30 percent were not sure. Advt According to Rungta, the finding points to a gap between the availability of digital information and a customer's ability to use that information to make a long-term financial decision."Do I have the complete information? Do I still have the right information? Is my understanding right? And the last and most important aspect of this entire model is: do I still trust the information which is shared?" he said.Rungta said insurance has evolved considerably since the sector opened up in 2000. The traditional agency-led model, where customers largely relied on an intermediary's recommendation, has gradually given way to a journey involving search, comparison, research, validation and purchase.While the first stages of this journey have become increasingly digital, he said the final decision still requires confidence, particularly because insurance is a long-term savings and protection category."Even if the first three steps are done very smartly through homework, video and research there is still a gap when it comes to decision-making," Rungta said.He also pointed to the continued role of agency distribution, saying that face-to-face interaction remains relevant not only in India but across mature insurance markets.The next challenge, according to Rungta, is moving from customer segmentation to genuine individual-level personalisation. Advt "Each customer for us is different — his background, his thinking, his value system, his income level and his family structure," he said.He compared conventional segmentation to a set menu, where customers are asked to choose from a predetermined set of options, while true personalisation would be closer to a buffet where the offering is built around an individual's requirements.Technology, he said, now makes it possible to deliver this degree of personalisation at scale. The objective is to use customer data and AI to understand an individual's circumstances, risk appetite and financial outlook before recommending a solution. Advt This also has implications for trust. Rungta argued that an independent recommendation engine could reduce some of the potential biases associated with intermediary-led selling. "We are challenging ourselves to say: let me not push a product for you. You tell me who you are and what you want, and let us recommend something to you," he said.He described the approach as moving from product selling to solution creation, with the aim of giving customers "one customer, one view and one solution".However, Rungta cautioned against treating technology as a replacement for human engagement.He said the industry has historically focused heavily on acquisition, while customer engagement after a policy is issued has received less attention. In the past, insurance agents would maintain relationships with customers through regular face-to-face interactions. As more of these interactions move to machines, the industry needs to ensure that the customer relationship does not weaken. Advt "A large part of India still wants to know: where is your office? Are you a one-man operation? Are you real?" he said.Rungta expects digital and physical models to coexist, particularly because insurance decisions often involve emotional and social considerations around family protection, children's education and retirement. "Digital will work alongside physical. Both models will coexist," he said.He also sees AI playing a role across multiple stages of the customer journey, from discovery and research to validation, lead management and solution-based recommendations.For Rungta, the objective is ultimately not simply to deploy more technology, but to make technology more relevant to individual customers. "We can deploy a lot of technology, but unless it is hyper-personalised, it doesn't really work," he said.
