(ITAT Delhi) Section 68 Addition in Wrong Assessment Year Unsustainable: Delhi ITAT Upholds ₹38.70 Lakh Relief The Delhi Income Tax Appellate Tribunal has upheld the deletion of an addition of ₹38,70,000 under Section 68 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, after finding that the underlying loan transaction belonged to Financial Year 2017–18, relevant to Assessment Year 2018–19, and not the assessment year under appeal. The decisive evidence was a certified bank statement showing receipt of ₹43,00,000 on 3 January 2018. Since the transaction did not pertain to Assessment Year 2019–20, the Tribunal found no reason to interfere with the relief granted by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) and dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. This article is based on the supplied order: C:UsersUserDesktoptaxguru officeviewOrder (2).pdf Facts of the Case The assessee, Maan Infosystems Pvt. Ltd., was engaged in information technology, software development and support services, as well as rental of immovable property. It filed its original return for Assessment Year 2019–20 declaring a total income of ₹12,27,570. The Assessing Officer received information through the Insight portal following a search in the case of KK Spun India Ltd. According to that information, an alleged entry operator was providing accommodation entries through various bogus or paper entities. The information alleged that the assessee had received accommodation entries of ₹38,70,000 in the form of an unsecured loan from Odisha Finlease Pvt. Ltd. Reassessment proceedings followed, and the Assessing Officer made an addition of ₹38,70,000 under Section 68 for Assessment Year 2019–20. Relief Granted by CIT(A) Before the CIT(A), the assessee contended that the transaction related to Financial Year 2017–18, rather than Financial Year 2018–19 relevant to the assessment year under consideration. The CIT(A) accepted this contention and deleted the addition. The Revenue challenged that decision before the Tribunal. Revenue’s Contentions The Revenue argued that the CIT(A) had accepted the assessee’s explanation regarding the transaction year on the basis of ledger entries without adequate examination of the relevant bank statement. It also questioned whether the assessee had established the identity and creditworthiness of the creditor and the genuineness of the transaction. Other grounds referred to material gathered during the search and challenged the assessee’s contention that the loan had subsequently been written back and offered as business income in Assessment Year 2023–24. Evidence Produced Before the Tribunal During the appellate proceedings, the assessee’s counsel sought to place a document on record while explaining that it was already available before the Assessing Officer. An e-proceeding response acknowledgement dated 16 October 2023 was also furnished to support the claim of earlier submission. Crucially, the assessee produced a certified copy of its Yes Bank statement for January 2018. The Tribunal recorded that this statement clearly established that ₹43,00,000 was credited to the assessee’s bank account on 3 January 2018. That date fell in: Financial Year 2017–18; and Assessment Year 2018–19. It therefore did not pertain to Assessment Year 2019–20, for which the disputed addition had been made. Tribunal’s Findings The Tribunal identified the central issue as the assessment year to which the loan transaction belonged. After examining the certified bank statement, it accepted that the transaction pertained to Assessment Year 2018–19 only. It found that the CIT(A)’s conclusion was supported by the documentary evidence and that no interference was warranted. Accordingly, the Tribunal dismissed the Revenue’s appeal, leaving the deletion of the addition undisturbed. The order refers to ₹38,70,000 as the disputed addition and ₹43,00,000 as the loan credit established by the bank statement. It does not explain the reconciliation between these amounts. Section 68: Correct Year of Credit Is Essential Section 68 links the taxation of an unexplained credit to the previous year in whose books the sum is found credited. Where the statutory conditions are satisfied, the amount may be charged as income of that previous year. The year of credit is therefore a material requirement. Income Tax Department—Section 68 In this case, the bank evidence established a receipt in January 2018. That factual finding defeated the addition made for Assessment Year 2019–20. Scope of the Ruling The relief was sustained on the year-of-transaction issue. The Tribunal did not record a separate merits finding conclusively establishing the genuineness of the loan or the creditor’s creditworthiness. Similarly, although the Revenue’s grounds mentioned reopening and the alleged subsequent write-back, the Tribunal’s operative reasoning rested on the certified bank statement establishing the earlier assessment year. The decision should therefore be understood as a ruling against the addition in the assessment year under appeal, rather than a general approval of the underlying loan transaction. Practical Takeaway The order highlights the importance of verifying the actual date and year of a credit before making an addition under Section 68. Information alleging an accommodation entry must still be examined against the transaction records for the relevant assessment year. For taxpayers, certified bank statements, supporting ledgers and acknowledgements of documents previously submitted can provide decisive evidence where the dispute concerns the year of receipt. Here, the bank statement established that the transaction belonged to an earlier year, resulting in the dismissal of the Revenue’s appeal. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI This appeal is filed by the Department against the order of Ld. CIT(A) dated 12-Dec-2025 for the Assessment Year 2019-20. 2. The Department has raised the following grounds of appeal: 1) Whether on the facts of the circumstances of the case, the Ld. CIT(A) in deleting the addition of Rs. 38,70,000 under section 68 of the I.T. Act, 1961 on account of unexplained credit. 2) Whether on the facts of the circumstances of the case, the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in considering transaction year F.Y. 2017- 18 instead of F.Y. 2018-19 on the basis of the book entries reflected in ledger extracts of the loan account and invalidating the reopening under section 147 of the Act, without examining it with relevant bank statement to establish the relevant assessment year in which entry was received. 3) Whether on the facts of the circumstances of the case, the Ld. CIT(A) has erred holding that Section 68 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 is not applicable merely by relying on book entries which do not prove the identity, creditworthiness, and genuineness of the creditor, Odisha Finlease Pvt. Ltd. 4) Whether on the facts of the circumstances of the case, the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in considering contention of assessee that alleged loan was rightly written back and added to the business income in the AY 2023-24 without examination of facts, thereby, deleting the addition made by the Assessing Officer. 5) Whether on the facts of the circumstances of the case the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in not considering the incriminating material Exibit No. 7 gathered during search proceedings in the case of KK Spun India Limited wherein it is mentioned that Odisha Finlease Pvt. Ltd. is one of the bogus entities and had transaction of Rs. 38,70,000 with Maan Infosystem Pvt. Ltd. 6) The appellant craves leave to add, amend, modify OR delete any of the above grounds of appeal at OR before the time of hearing. 3. Brief facts of the case as given in the order of the Ld. CIT(A) are as under :- 1. The Appellant in question is M/s Maan Infosystem Private Limited, a company registered under the Companies Act, 1956. The company is primarily involved in providing Information Technology and Software development and support services, as well as rental of immovable property services. The Appellant initially filed their original Income Tax Return under section 139(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 on 25.10.2019, with acknowledgment number 221120611251019, declaring a total income of Rs. 12,27,570/- for the Assessment Year 2019-20. Subsequently, the Appellant filed a rectification of the Income Tax Return on 11.12.2019, with acknowledgment number 270646510111219, maintaining the total income at Rs. 12,27,570/-. Furthermore, in response to a notice issued under section 148 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 on 28.02.2024, the Appellant filed another return with acknowledgment number 127834211280224, declaring the same total income of Rs. 12,27,570/-. It is important to note that there have been no changes in the income reported in all three returns filed by the Appellant. 2. In this case, the information was made available to Learned Assessing Officer through insight portal (High Risk CRIU/VRU Information). As per the information, a search was conducted in the case M/s KK Spun India Ltd wherein various incriminating evidence were gathered and examined which revealed that Sh. Aditya Jain was indulge in providing accommodation entries to beneficiaries in the form of bogus unsecured loan, bogus bills etc. by using various bogus/paper entities. It was also revealed that Appellant company M/s Maan infosystem Pvt. Ltd. has also taken accommodation entries of Unsecured loan of Rs. 38,70,000 through one of the bogus concerns Odisha Finlease Pvt. Ltd. used by Sh. Aditya Jain to provide accommodation entries. During proceedings u/s 148A of the Income Tax Act, 1961 notice u/s 148A(b) of the Act was issued to the Appellant on 01.03.2023 requesting him to explain the above transactions. The said notice was served electronically through email and speed post. However, there was no compliance from the Appellant. As such, order u/s 148A(d) of the Act was passed on 20.03.2023 and notice u/s 148 of the Income Tax Act,1961 has been issued on 20.03.2023. Here Appellant add the fact that such notice is never received on the official email as well as registered office address mentioned on return. The fact that notice issued was never known to the Appellant. 4. During proceedings before us the Ld. Counsel of the assessee has filed an application for taking an additional document on record. At the same time, he has submitted that this documentary evidence is not new document the same was available on record of the AO during assessment proceedings. The Ld. Counsel has also filed e-proceeding response acknowledgement dated 16.10.2023 showing that this document has already been filed before the AO. 5. The Ld. DR argued that the Ld. CIT(A) erred in deleting the addition of Rs.3870000/- made u/s. 68 of the IT Act by the AO as transaction for Financial Year 2017-18 instead of 2018-19. On the basis of book entries reflected in registration extracts and the loan account without examining the relevant ledger and statement which established the relevant assessment year in which the entry was received. 6. Infact the main issue and the only issue involved in this case is resulting a loan amount of Rs.43,00,000/- taken by the assessee. The AO rejecting the claim of the assessee made the addition in A.Y. 2019-20 while the Ld. CIT(A) accepted the claim of the assessee that said transaction pertained to F.Y. 2017-18 and accordingly, the Ld.CIT(A) deleted the addition made by the AO u/s.68 of the Act. 7. During proceedings before us the ld. Counsel of the assessee filed certified copy of the Yes Bank Statement for January, 2018 which clearly establishes that a sum of Rs.43,00,000/- was credited to assessee’s bank account on 03.01.2018 i.e. during financial year 2017-18 relevant to A.Y.2018-19 and it clearly pertained only to A.Y. 2018-19 and not A.Y. 2019-20. Therefore, on this basis the Ld. CIT(A) has given relief to the assessee. We are of this considered view that the finding given by the Ld. CIT(A) is on the basis of facts made available through bank statement and certificate by the assessee, therefore, we do not find any reason to make any interference given by the Ld. CIT(A). Accordingly, the Revenue’s appeal on this issue is dismissed. 8. In the result, the appeal filed by the Revenue is dismissed. Order pronounced in the court on 16.11.2026.
