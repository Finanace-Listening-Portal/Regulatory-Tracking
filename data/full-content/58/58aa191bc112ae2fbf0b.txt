Articles 3 min read Insurers’ Q2 outlook: LIC leads VNB growth, HDFC Life recovery may moderate The impact of proposed changes to insurance distribution payouts and expense of management (EoM) norms is expected to dominate insurers’ Q2FY27 earnings calls and stock performance, with less emphasis on quarterly financial results. Protection business growth is supporting life insurers’ margins and value of new business (VNB), while motor third party (TP) claims provisioning will be a key driver for general insurers. Strong protection business growth and the resolution of concerns over margin losses following the GST exemption are expected to support performance. Health claims typically rise in the second quarter, although structural improvements and higher operating leverage continue to support core earnings. PB is expected to maintain steady growth, according to Kotak Institutional Equities. Growth is expected to remain moderate.ICICI Prudential Life: Strong protection business growth continues to support performance. The report does not provide a specific company-level APE or VNB growth forecast in this section.SBI Life: APE is expected to grow 14 per cent, while the loss of input tax credit following the GST exemption is expected to weigh on margins.Axis Max Life: APE and VNB are each expected to grow 14 per cent.LIC: A higher share of non-participating products is expected to drive year-on-year VNB margin expansion of around 320 basis points to 22.5 per cent, supporting 40 per cent VNB growth.Distribution norms to dominate outlookProposed changes to distribution payouts and EoM limits are expected to dominate management discussions. Key issues include potential VNB losses from lower credit protection business, channel strategy, open or closed architecture at banks, dependence on PB and insurers’ ability to comply with the proposed EoM norms.Near-term growth could remain strong during the transition or sunset period, but FY2028 could be weaker as insurers initially adjust to the new norms and subsequently face a high base. These trends may not reflect the sector’s longer-term growth trajectory. The IRDA is expected to draft additional norms covering products, customer redressal and claims, while IFRS 17 and risk-based capital (RBC) requirements are also expected to be implemented. Regulatory uncertainty could keep insurance stocks under pressure despite undemanding valuations. Advt General insurers: Motor TP claims in focusHigher motor TP claims following a court verdict on compensation to homemakers have resulted in large claims for ICICI Lombard, while peers have indicated a lower impact. Some moderation in the impact on ICICI Lombard is possible, as the company appears to have slowed its motor business in August.The issue could become less relevant if the proposed TP commission rates are reduced to 0-2.5 per cent from the current average of 26 per cent. Insurers will need to adapt to the proposed distribution structure, including zero commissions for new vehicles, while maintaining their market positions. Advt ICICI Lombard appears best placed to respond, given its digital strategy, brand recall and stated intention to increase its direct-to-customer share to 20 per cent from 6 per cent. The ability of competing insurers to strengthen their own capabilities will shape competitive dynamics.
