Summary: The increase in the provident fund wage ceiling from Rs 15,000 to Rs 25,000 per month with effect from 17 September 2026 has created a practical question for employers paying retrospective salary increments and dearness allowance arrears. The Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation’s Revamped Electronic Challan-cum-Return FAQ distinguishes arrears relating to earlier wage periods from delayed payment of regular salary and identifies the month of disbursement as the due month for contributions. However, the available EPFO guidance does not expressly specify whether the old or revised wage ceiling applies when arrears relate to months before the ceiling revision. The article examines the relevant provisions of the Employees’ Provident Funds Scheme, 2026 and explains the author’s interpretation that contributions should be calculated by reference to the ceiling applicable to each original wage month. A worked example involving arrears of Rs 28,000 for April to October 2026 illustrates how the old ceiling, the September transition and the revised ceiling may affect contributory wages, employee PF and the employer’s EPF and EPS allocations. It also discusses the distinction between arrear, revised and regular ECR filings, the contribution payment deadline, higher-wage options and the payroll records employers should reconcile. The suggested treatment of historical arrears and September arrears is expressly presented as an interpretation pending specific EPFO clarification. That date matters for PF. On 17 September 2026 the Ministry of Labour and Employment raised the PF wage ceiling from Rs 15,000 to Rs 25,000 a month, through notification S.O. 5109(E). So the payroll team has a simple question to answer: do we work out PF on these arrears at Rs 15,000 or at Rs 25,000? What EPFO Has Said So Far There are two different dates here. One is the wage month the arrears belong to. The other is the month in which you actually pay them. EPFO’s FAQ on the Revamped ECR (question 15) covers arrears. It says an arrear payment is PF on wages for an earlier wage period that are paid later, for example after a revision of dearness relief. The date on which you disburse the arrears decides the “due month” for paying PF. EPFO’s example: arrears for January to March 2025 paid with April 2025 salary have April 2025 as the due month. The same FAQ says this is not the same as salary paid late because the company delayed or defaulted. The PF amount itself is still worked out month by month. Under the Employees’ Provident Funds Scheme, 2026, para 18(4) says contributions are calculated on wages actually drawn or payable during the month. Para 18(3) says contributions are subject to the wage ceiling notified from time to time. If the monthly wage is above the ceiling, both the employee and employer share stop at the ceiling, unless the two have jointly opted under para 9(4) to contribute on higher wages. What EPFO Has Not Said EPFO’s FAQs on the new ceiling explain how to split September 2026 in the regular ECR, but they say nothing about arrears. None of the EPFO papers listed below says which ceiling to use for arrears of months before 17 September 2026. Our reading of the rules above is this: use the ceiling that applied in the wage month the arrears belong to, not the ceiling of the month in which you pay. This is our reading, not EPFO’s word. If EPFO issues a clarification, follow that. A Worked Example: PF on Rs 28,000 Salary Arrears Ravi’s PF wage (basic plus DA) was Rs 14,000 a month. He is an EPS member and has no higher-wage option. In October the company approves an increment of Rs 4,000 a month with effect from 1 April 2026, so his PF wage becomes Rs 18,000. November salary is paid on the new wage, and arrears for April to October 2026, Rs 28,000 in all, are paid along with it. April to August 2026: Old Rs 15,000 Ceiling April to August: the ceiling was Rs 15,000. Only Rs 1,000 of the Rs 4,000 increase fits under it each month. So the arrear PF wage is Rs 1,000 a month, or Rs 5,000 for the five months. September 2026: Split-Month Wage Ceiling September: EPFO’s ceiling FAQs split the regular September ECR into 1 to 16 September at the Rs 15,000 ceiling and 17 to 30 September at Rs 25,000, on a 30-day month. Using the same method, Ravi’s revised September PF wage is Rs 15,000 x 16/30 plus Rs 18,000 x 14/30, which is Rs 8,000 plus Rs 8,400, or Rs 16,400. Rs 14,000 was already reported, so the arrear PF wage is Rs 2,400. EPFO gave this split for the regular ECR. Using it for September arrears is our extension of the same method. October 2026: Revised Rs 25,000 Ceiling October: Rs 18,000 is below the new Rs 25,000 ceiling, so the full Rs 4,000 counts. Employee share is 12%. Employer share is also 12%, with 8.33% going to EPS and 3.67% to EPF. Each figure is rounded to the nearest rupee, as para 18(5) says. Arrear month Ceiling Arrear PF wage Employee PF (12%) Employer EPS (8.33%) Employer EPF (3.67%) April to August 2026 (5 months) Rs 15,000 Rs 5,000 Rs 600 Rs 415 Rs 185 September 2026 (16 + 14 days) Rs 15,000, then Rs 25,000 Rs 2,400 Rs 288 Rs 200 Rs 88 October 2026 Rs 25,000 Rs 4,000 Rs 480 Rs 333 Rs 147 Total Rs 11,400 Rs 1,368 Rs 948 Rs 420 So PF goes on Rs 11,400 of the Rs 28,000 arrears. If you used November’s Rs 25,000 ceiling for every month, PF would go on the full Rs 28,000, and Ravi’s own share alone would be Rs 3,360 instead of Rs 1,368. If an employee was already contributing on the Rs 15,000 cap, say on a salary of Rs 22,000, arrears for April to 16 September add no statutory PF. Those months were already at the cap. Room opens only from 17 September, up to Rs 25,000. Which EPFO Return to File, and by When The employer portal’s return home page has a separate link to “file arrear return”, along with the regular, supplementary and revised returns. EPFO’s Re-Engineered ECR user manual (version 3.0) shows this. It also describes a revised return as the way to correct wrong wages or contribution already filed. An increment approved later is not a mistake in the original return. So in our view the arrear return is the right route. Do not add these arrears to November’s regular ECR as if they were November wages. EPFO’s papers do not say this in one line, so if you are unsure, check with your EPFO office. The arrears are paid in November 2026, so November is the due month, going by FAQ question 15. Para 20(1) of the Scheme says the employer must pay both shares, with admin charges, within 15 days of the close of every month. Read together, PF on these arrears should be paid by 15 December 2026. If you pay late, the same FAQ says interest and damages are worked out from the date the arrears were disbursed. Payroll Checks Before Uploading the Arrear ECR Before you upload, check these four things for each employee: 1. The PF wage already reported for each arrear month, and the revised PF wage. 2. The ceiling for that month, with September in two parts. 3. Whether there is a joint option under para 9(4) to contribute above the ceiling. If yes, the cap does not cut the arrears the way it does above. 4. Whether this is really an arrear (a wage or DA revision paid later) or only the same month’s salary paid late.
