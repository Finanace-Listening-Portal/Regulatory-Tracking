Summary: Zerodha Broking Limited is a SEBI-registered stockbroker with registration INZ000031633. The same material says cross-currency contracts such as EURUSD, GBPUSD and USDJPY are not available, while BSE currency derivatives through Zerodha were discontinued from 30 January 2025. This is therefore an exchange-traded derivatives profile, not a foreign-currency remittance or overseas leveraged-forex profile. RBI rules require an underlying contracted foreign-currency exposure; users remain responsible for eligibility and compliance. Brokerage, exchange charges, GST, SEBI charges and stamp duty apply as documented, while market spread and margin vary. This article covers identity, scope, KYC, activation, order process, settlement and withdrawal, grievance routes, tax treatment and limitations without ranking or endorsing the broker. Zerodha’s current page states that RBI requires an underlying contracted exposure to foreign currency before a client trades currency derivatives. It defines contracted exposure as currency risk arising from a permissible current-account or capital-account transaction under FEMA and related rules that has already been entered into. This means platform access is not a licence for unrestricted speculation. The broker’s declaration and income-proof process operationalise the eligibility requirement, but the client remains responsible for the accuracy of declarations and the existence of the exposure. A person hedging import, export, education, travel, loan or another permissible transaction should keep the agreement, invoice, loan document or other evidence that created the currency risk. Position size and tenor should be reviewed against that exposure and applicable RBI directions. The listed contracts are derivatives settled under exchange rules; they are not a means to obtain banknotes or remit foreign currency. Zerodha’s page says contracts are settled at the RBI reference rate. A customer wanting travel cash, a forex card or an outward remittance needs an appropriately authorised bank or money changer and must complete the purpose-specific FEMA process. Zerodha Currency Segment Activation and KYC Documents A resident individual normally completes securities-market KYC and opens the broker account with PAN, identity, address, bank and contact details. For the currency segment, the current online process uses the account portal, selection of the currency segment, an income range, submission of bank-statement information through an RBI-approved Account Aggregator route, acceptance of the declaration and Aadhaar e-sign. The official page states that the equity segment must already be active. Where online signing is unavailable or the account is joint, Zerodha describes an offline declaration route. Its listed income-proof alternatives include a six-month bank statement meeting the stated average balance, salary slip, ITR acknowledgement, Form 16, net-worth certificate, demat-holding statement or qualifying fixed-deposit receipt. Thresholds and accepted documents can change; the live activation page should be followed rather than relying on a saved checklist. Activation requested through Console by the stated evening cut-off is intended for availability on the next trading day; couriered declarations can take longer. Segment activation does not ensure that every contract is liquid or that a position will be accepted. Exchange, broker and risk-management limits can affect order quantity, margin and product availability. NSE Currency Contracts, Order Placement, Margins and Settlement On Kite, the user searches an available currency pair and selects the desired futures or options contract. The current support page lists standard lot sizes: USDINR, EURINR and GBPINR use 1,000 units of the foreign currency per lot, while JPYINR uses 100,000 yen per lot. Contract specifications, expiry cycles, price steps and trading hours should be confirmed on NSE because exchanges can revise them. Currency derivatives are leveraged. Margin is a performance safeguard, not the maximum possible loss. Adverse movement can trigger additional margin, broker risk reduction or exchange penalties. Zerodha states that the client is responsible for squaring off open positions; the broker may act but is not obliged to do so. A user should understand expiry settlement and avoid assuming that an open position will be closed automatically at a preferred time or price. Profits, losses, margins and applicable charges are posted to the trading ledger under exchange and broker settlement rules. Withdrawal from the trading account is separate from contract settlement. Available cash can be requested through the funds interface, subject to completed settlement, retained margin, bank verification and processing cut-offs. A failed or delayed payout should first be reconciled against unsettled obligations and then raised through a support ticket. Zerodha Currency Derivative Brokerage and Other Charges Zerodha’s current charges page quotes currency futures brokerage at 0.03% or ₹20 per executed order, whichever is lower, and currency options brokerage at ₹20 per executed order. It separately lists exchange transaction charges, SEBI charges, GST and buy-side stamp duty. A call-and-trade or dealer-assisted order can attract an additional fee. These are documented tariff items, not the full economic cost of a trade. The bid-offer spread is determined by the order book and is not a fixed broker fee. Margin depends on the contract, volatility and exchange risk parameters. Slippage, rejected orders and forced reduction can change realised cost. The user should review the contract note because the relevant rates and taxable base can change. Securities Transaction Tax and Commodities Transaction Tax are not shown for the currency segment on Zerodha’s current tariff, but other statutory charges remain. For an active trader or business hedge, profit or loss is generally examined under business-income principles rather than the capital-gains rules applicable to delivery investments. Classification depends on facts, books, purpose and consistency, so every user should not be assigned the same head of income. Turnover for tax-audit analysis is not the notional foreign-currency contract value. Derivative turnover is generally derived from recognised profit-and-loss computation principles, including absolute differences and option premium treatment where relevant. Audit thresholds, cash-receipt/payment conditions and presumptive provisions require current professional review. Contract notes, daily margin statements, ledger, bank entries and year-end open-position valuation should reconcile. GST charged by the broker on brokerage and specified charges is a service-tax cost. An eligible registered business may evaluate input-tax credit under normal conditions and documentation, but an individual investor ordinarily bears it as cost. Income tax on trading results and GST on brokerage are separate. Currency-derivative trading does not create LRS remittance or TCS merely because the underlying references foreign exchange. SEBI registration and exchange membership do not guarantee profit, exchange liquidity or recovery of every loss.
