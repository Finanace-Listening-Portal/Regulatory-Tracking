Summary: Limited liability partnerships closing their financial year on 31 March 2026 should prepare Form 8, the Statement of Account and Solvency, for filing with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs by the ordinary deadline of 30 October 2026. Section 34 of the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 requires proper books and preparation of the statement within six months of year-end; Rule 24 governs the prescribed filing and certification. The MCA V3 webform contains financial information and a solvency declaration, rather than operating merely as a cover sheet for attached accounts. Its current instruction kit provides detailed entry, digital-signature and professional-certification steps. A significant practical issue is the difference between the literal “or” wording of the audit exemption in Rule 24(8) and the auditor-certification instruction applied by the MCA webform when turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh. Where only one threshold is crossed, the legal and portal positions should be documented before filing rather than assumed identical. Form 8 is separate from Form 11, income-tax returns and tax-audit reporting. Late filing can attract additional fees under the amended LLP fee schedule and statutory consequences. This article gives a 2026 deadline-focused checklist covering final accounts, partner signatures, MCA data validation, solvency, audit questions and filing acknowledgement. Key Takeaways Official Sources Reviewed Show all 21 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Why LLPs Need to Act Before 30 October 2026 For an LLP with a financial year ending 31 March 2026, the six-month period for preparing its Statement of Account and Solvency runs to 30 September 2026. The prescribed filing period under the LLP Rules ordinarily makes 30 October 2026 the last date for filing Form 8 for FY 2025–26. The deadline is not calculated from the date of a company annual general meeting because LLPs operate under a different statute. An LLP that was incorporated during the year, became inactive, or changed its business should still check the applicable statutory obligation rather than assuming that no sales means no filing. Section 34 of the LLP Act requires proper books of account and annual preparation and filing of the Statement of Account and Solvency. The relevant primary text is available in the India Code copy of the LLP Act. MCA’s current Form 8 instruction kit explains the webform process. TaxGuru’s annual LLP compliance guide gives wider context, but the live statutory text and webform control the current filing. Form 8 and Form 11 serve different purposes Form 8 is principally a financial and solvency disclosure. Form 11 is the LLP annual return addressing information such as partners and contribution and follows a separate annual timeline. The fact that Form 11 was filed earlier does not replace Form 8. Equally, the LLP’s income-tax return and any audit report under tax law are not substitutes for the MCA statement. Maintain one reconciliation schedule that ties the figures appearing in Form 8 to the LLP books and to the corresponding contribution information in other filings. The designated partners must verify the accuracy of the figures and declarations. Part B contains financial heads for assets, liabilities, income and expenditure, and the totals must reconcile. If a transaction does not fit an obvious heading, use the permitted “Others” field with an adequate description rather than forcing it into an unrelated category. Every number should be traceable to final ledgers and signed management accounts. The MCA instruction kit outlines login, selecting LLP e-filing, identifying the LLPIN or FLLPIN, entering the webform, submission, generation of an SRN, digital signing, upload of the signed PDF and payment of fees. It specifies operational windows for completing digital-signature and payment steps after generating an SRN. These windows are portal-processing rules, not permission to extend the underlying statutory due date. Where a filing is started on 30 October but not fully completed, an SRN alone should not be assumed to establish timely filing. Are signed financial statements compulsory attachments? The current MCA V3 instruction kit does not list separately prepared signed accounts or an audit report as mandatory uploads merely for the annual Statement of Account and Solvency purpose. The prescribed financial particulars are entered within the form, with an optional-attachment facility. Charge-related attachments relate to the separate charge-filing functionality and should not be copied mechanically into every annual filing. A separately prepared balance sheet and income-and-expenditure statement remain essential underlying accounting records even where the portal does not demand them as mandatory attachments. TaxGuru’s analysis of Form 8 financial-statement attachments discusses this important distinction. Audit Exemption and MCA Auditor Certification Are Not Identical Questions Rule 24(8) and its provisos govern the audit of LLP accounts. The exemption wording has an important drafting feature: it refers to an LLP whose turnover does not exceed ₹40 lakh OR whose contribution does not exceed ₹25 lakh. On a literal reading, the use of “or” can produce a different result from a shorthand assertion that audit is mandatory whenever either number exceeds the threshold. The MCA Form 8 instruction kit, however, directs auditor certification of the webform where turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh. An administrative instruction does not amend a rule, but the certification requirement creates a real filing issue. For example, an LLP with turnover of ₹1.10 crore and contribution of ₹20 lakh crosses only the turnover threshold. Its audit position should be analysed with reference to the exact Rule 24(8) wording, any relevant amendments, the LLP agreement, applicable professional obligations and the live MCA certification requirement. It would be unsafe to publish that such an LLP is unquestionably exempt from all auditor involvement, or that the rule itself has been silently rewritten. A prudent compliance team should obtain a reasoned decision and arrange audit or certification as required for a valid filing. It should not be conflated with the wording of the audit exemption in Rule 24(8). In particular, an entity’s ability to claim a reduced additional filing fee or statutory penalty depends on the relevant small-LLP conditions and the correct fee schedule. Do not use the word “small” in ordinary conversation as proof of legal eligibility. Solvency Declaration: More Than a Checkbox Solvency concerns the LLP’s capacity to pay debts as they become due and the accuracy of the designated partners’ declaration. A positive balance-sheet net worth does not, by itself, establish that all near-term creditors can be paid. Before signing, review ageing of trade payables, overdue statutory dues, loan instalments, contingent liabilities, related-party balances, disputed claims and material post-balance-sheet developments. The form should be consistent with information available when the declaration is made, not only with an unreconciled 31 March trial balance. Where the LLP has accumulated losses or negative working capital, the designated partners should not simply choose a favourable solvency response to clear validation. Prepare a cash-flow forecast, identify committed financial support and evaluate whether debts can be discharged in the ordinary course. Any material uncertainty should be discussed with the auditor or legal adviser, and the final declaration must reflect the actual facts. Signing a knowingly incorrect declaration can create a risk beyond an ordinary additional filing fee. Accounting and Audit Working Papers to Prepare Start with the final trial balance and reconcile bank balances to statements, debtors and creditors to confirmations, loans to agreements, fixed assets to registers and depreciation schedules, and tax balances to GST and income-tax records. Check partner capital and current accounts separately from third-party borrowings. Reconcile contributions to LLP agreements, statutory changes and Form 11, and resolve any mismatch before entering the contribution field. Record material contingencies and related-party transactions according to the applicable accounting framework. Where audit applies, ensure that the auditor has completed necessary verification and that Form 8 figures agree with the final audited accounts. Where audit does not apply, designated partners remain responsible for maintaining proper books and preparing reliable accounts. Audit exemption is not an exemption from accounting. The absence of business activity is likewise not an exemption from maintaining bank records, documenting expenses, closing balances or assessing solvency. Any financial information prepared for lenders, investors or partners should reconcile to the MCA filing or contain a documented explanation for differences. How to avoid rejected or invalid filings Validate the LLPIN, authorised business-user profile, digital-signature registration and relevant professional membership or certification details before the last day. Confirm that the MCA V3 system displays the correct financial year and LLP details. Review each mandatory field and the attachments selected for the relevant purpose. After submission, download the DSC-affixed document, complete upload and payment within the applicable portal window and obtain final acknowledgement. If the system rejects the form, identify whether the defect is a field-validation issue, a signature issue, a fee issue or an actual legal non-compliance. Late Filing: Additional Fees and Statutory Penalties The pre-2022 shorthand of ₹100 per day without limit is not a reliable statement of the current LLP Form 8 additional-fee schedule. Following the LLP amendments and revised rules, additional fees generally depend on the number of days delayed, the normal filing fee and whether the entity qualifies as a small LLP; long delays may involve a multiplier plus a further per-day amount. The live MCA fee calculator and applicable schedule should be used rather than a guessed amount. Separately, section 34(5) provides statutory consequences for failure to file the statement. Paying a portal additional fee does not necessarily settle a separate adjudicatory penalty. Where filing is already overdue, the LLP should complete accounts and regularise the position rather than waiting for a reminder. A multi-year default requires year-by-year financial statements, separate form filings and careful treatment of partner changes, charges and outstanding statutory obligations. Inactive LLPs should examine lawful closure or strike-off procedures rather than simply stop filing. Each year’s liability and available relief should be evaluated against the law applicable to that year. Final 16–30 October 2026 Review Checklist Assign a named preparer and reviewer. Complete and lock the books, resolve audit and certification issues, review solvency evidence, reconcile partner contribution, validate the MCA profile, and confirm digital signatures. Recheck whether any MCA notification changes the ordinary 30 October deadline for the relevant class of LLPs. File early enough to address portal validation failures, then preserve the final Form 8 PDF, SRN, challan, acknowledgement and supporting schedules. The defensible filing is one in which every disclosed figure and declaration can be traced to contemporaneous evidence. Frequently Asked Questions 1. What is the ordinary Form 8 due date for FY 2025–26? 30 October 2026 for an LLP whose financial year ended 31 March 2026, subject to any applicable official extension. 2. Does an LLP with no turnover still need Form 8? Generally yes. Lack of turnover does not automatically cancel annual accounting and filing obligations. 3. Is Form 8 the same as Form 11? No. Form 8 reports accounts and solvency; Form 11 is the separate LLP annual return. 4. Must audited financial statements always be attached? The current MCA V3 kit provides optional attachments for the annual statement purpose; financial figures must still be accurately entered and certified where required. 5. Is audit automatically mandatory when only one threshold is crossed? The wording of Rule 24(8) and the MCA webform’s auditor-certification requirement need separate examination; do not assume the question is settled by a simplified threshold slogan. 6. Can an SRN without final payment prove timely filing? No. Complete all prescribed digital-signature, upload and fee steps and retain the final acknowledgement. 7. Is the current late fee always ₹100 per day? No. The revised LLP additional-fee schedule uses delay bands and multipliers; statutory penalties are separately considered. Check Rule 24(8) wording separately from the MCA instruction kit’s auditor-certification requirements. Signed accounts remain necessary working papers even where separate attachments are optional.
