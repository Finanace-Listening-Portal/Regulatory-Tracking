Summary: Filing GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C for FY 2025-26 requires careful review of mandatory disclosures, optional reporting relaxations, input tax credit reconciliation and GST rate changes. Although several reporting concessions continue, certain relaxations withdrawn from FY 2024-25 remain unavailable. Taxpayers must pay particular attention to GSTR-9 Tables 6E, 7, 12 and 13, distinguish prior-year ITC claims under Table 6A1 from reclaims under Table 6H, and reconcile GSTR-2B with the books of account. The GST rate rationalisation effective 22 September 2025 also requires accurate rate-wise and HSN-wise reporting, particularly in Table 17. For GSTR-9C, taxpayers must reconcile turnover, tax liability and ITC with their financial records while maintaining supporting workings even where detailed disclosures are optional. The general filing deadline is 31 December 2026, and eligible FY 2025-26 ITC claims must satisfy the applicable November 2026 statutory cut-off. Since annual returns cannot ordinarily be revised, taxpayers should complete reconciliations, verify system-generated figures, identify tax shortfalls and preserve documentary evidence before filing. A table-wise approach can reduce reporting errors and subsequent scrutiny disputes. GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C for FY 2025-26: Which Tables Are Mandatory, Which Are Optional, and Where People Usually Go Wrong Every year around November, the same question lands in my inbox from clients and from fellow professionals: “Do we really need to fill all of this?” With the annual return for FY 2025-26 due on 31 December 2026, and the 30 November cut-off for ITC and GSTR-1 amendments sitting just before it, the question is more relevant than usual. The short answer is that the relaxations many of us rely on are still there for FY 2025-26, but a few of them were quietly withdrawn from FY 2024-25 and have not come back. If you file this year’s return on last year’s muscle memory from FY 2023-24, you will leave gaps. This article goes table by table, so you can see at a glance what you must report, what you can skip or consolidate, and where I would spend my time. Check the current notification before you tell a small client he is out of scope. GSTR-9C: required where aggregate turnover exceeds ₹5 crore. It is self-certified; no CA certification is needed, but the figures still have to come from audited books. Due date: 31 December 2026, unless extended. Neither form can be revised once filed. As per Notification No. 05/2026–Central Tax, the instructions to GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C were amended so that the existing optional reporting continues for FY 2025-26. That is the good news. The less advertised part is that the relaxations withdrawn from FY 2024-25 (Tables 6E, 7, 12 and 13) stay withdrawn. Watch the 22 Sept 2025 rate change: the same HSN can appear on two lines at two rates. 18 HSN summary of inward supplies Optional What changed compared with older years If your last “full” look at GSTR-9 was for FY 2022-23 or FY 2023-24, these are the points that will trip you up: Table 7 must be filled row by row. Earlier you could put reversals of 7A to 7E in 7H. That option was withdrawn from FY 2024-25. Rule 42/43 reversals, Section 17(5) blocked credits and Rule 37/37A reversals each have their own place now. Tables 12 and 13 are no longer optional. If you reversed or claimed FY 2025-26 credit in the 3B returns of April to November 2026, it has to be shown. 6A1 and 6H are easy to mix up. 6A1 is for last year’s ITC claimed this year. 6H is for credit you reversed earlier and reclaimed (for example under Rule 37A once the supplier filed his return). Table 17 can have the same HSN twice. Because of the GST rate rationalisation effective 22 September 2025, many products were sold at one rate before that date and another after. Report them on separate lines with the correct rate. A table being optional in the form does not make the underlying reconciliation optional. The officer looking at your GSTR-9 during scrutiny will still compare Table 8 with 2B, and Table 5 with your financial statements. If you skip Table 14 in 9C, keep the expense-wise working in your file anyway. It is the first thing you will need when an ASMT-10 or a DRC-01C arrives. A working checklist before you file Complete the 2B vs books reconciliation and claim any missed FY 2025-26 ITC before 30 November 2026. After that date, GSTR-9 will not help you. Reconcile 3B with GSTR-1 and GSTR-1A month by month, so Table 4/5 differences can be explained. Pay any shortfall through DRC-03 before filing, and reflect it in Table 9 or 14 as applicable. Prepare Table 17 from the sales register, split by rate around 22 September 2025. Download the draft GSTR-9 from the portal and check the system-computed figures before you start editing them. For 9C, tie every figure back to the audited trial balance. If the audit is still in progress, plan for it now rather than in the last week of December. Closing thought The annual return has become less of a summary and more of a reconciliation statement. Treat it that way and the optional tables stop being a source of anxiety: you fill what the law requires, keep the rest of the working on file, and you are ready if a question comes later.
