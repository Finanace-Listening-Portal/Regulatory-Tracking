The company’s investor presentation shows interest cost at 6.55% of average lending book in Q2, compared with 6.52% in Q1, while net interest margin, including fee income, declined to 6% from 6.07%. Advt Rakesh Singh, MD & CEO, Aditya Birla Finance, said the benefit from a change in product mix towards higher-yielding unsecured loans was offset by the increase in cost of funds, which meant there was no margin expansion in Q2.“The benefit which we saw was mitigated by the increase in cost of funds,” Singh said, adding that the company expects margin improvement to come over the next few quarters.The NBFC increased the share of unsecured loans by about 1 percentage point sequentially. However, management said it was focused on building a risk-adjusted portfolio rather than using higher-risk customers to improve margins.“We are not in a hurry to onboard high-risk customers to improve the margin,” Singh said, adding that the company would continue to build the portfolio within its risk appetite.Floating-rate book offers some protectionThe lender expects to have some ability to pass on higher borrowing costs through asset repricing. According to the investor presentation, 70% of its advances are floating-rate, while 30% are fixed-rate. On the borrowing side, 57% is floating and 43% is fixed.Singh said this asset-liability mix gives the company an advantage if the cost of borrowing rises, as higher funding costs can be transmitted through repricing. Advt However, the transmission is not uniform across the portfolio. Personal and consumer loans are largely short-tenor and fixed-rate, while SME, mid-corporate and corporate loans are primarily floating-rate. This means the benefit of repricing is more relevant to the latter segments.The pressure on margins comes even as the NBFC continues to improve its operating efficiency. Its opex-to-average lending book declined to 1.84% in Q2 from 1.95% in Q1, while credit provision fell to 0.92% from 1.03%. RoA rose to 2.45% from 2.39% sequentially.Management had earlier guided to reaching a 2.5% RoA by Q4 FY27 and said it was already “almost there” in Q2. It said the remaining improvement would come through a combination of growth, margins, operating expenses, credit costs and product mix, rather than through aggressive pricing or underwriting. Advt ABCL creates overlay as it monitors macro stressAlongside the funding-cost pressure, the NBFC has also built a management overlay against emerging macroeconomic risks.Singh said the company had reviewed segments coming under stress when the Iran-US war started and created an overlay as part of that exercise. The overlay is included in the overall credit cost reported by the NBFC.“It’s an ongoing exercise. We look at that. We created some management overlay, but that's part of the overall credit cost which you see here,” Singh said.He added that the company continues to review the macro environment and individual customer segments and will take corrective action wherever required. Advt The overlay comes even as reported asset quality remains strong. The investor presentation shows NBFC credit provision at 0.92% of average lending book in Q2, compared with 1.03% in Q1 and 1.16% a year earlier. RoA stood at 2.45%, while the NBFC's AUM grew 26.5% year-on-year to Rs 1,76,557 crore.Management said Stage 2 assets account for only around 1% of the NBFC's portfolio, which it considers an indicator of the underlying quality of the book. It said the focus remains on selecting customers with a better risk profile and building a resilient portfolio rather than maximising near-term margins.The company is also monitoring inflation-related risks to its personal-loan customers. Management said it reviews the performance of its customer segments on a monthly basis and takes corrective action wherever it sees risks emerging.
