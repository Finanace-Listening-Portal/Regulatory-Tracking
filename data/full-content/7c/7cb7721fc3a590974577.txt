Articles 2 min read Exclusive: Central Bank of India mobilises USD 760 million via FCNR(B), nearly twice its initial target Central Bank of India has mobilised about USD 760 million through FCNR(B) deposits, nearly double its USD 400 million target, a senior bank official told ETBFSI. The RBI also closed the special window a month prior on August 31 and the target announced by the lender was supposed to be achieved by September end. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 11, 2026 at 12:37 AM IST var news_msid = 134026926; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook State owned Central Bank of India has mobilised around USD 760 million through Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank), or FCNR(B), deposits, significantly exceeding its earlier target of USD 400 million, its senior official told ETBFSI. The lender leveraged its International Banking Unit (IBU) at GIFT City to tap the NRI deposit market.Central Bank of India had initially set a target of USD 400 million by September 30. However, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) subsequently brought forward the deadline for mobilisation of fresh FCNR(B) deposits to August 31 from September 30, following a sharp increase in inflows through the special swap facility. Advt The RBI had introduced the concessional USD-INR swap facility on June 8 to encourage fresh FCNR(B) deposits, along with overseas foreign currency borrowings (OFCBs) and external commercial borrowings (ECBs). As of August 31, banks had mobilised USD 127.22 billion through FCNR(B) deposits, while total foreign currency inflows through the three components stood at about USD 136.4 billion.The scale of mobilisation accelerated sharply after the RBI announced the early closure of the FCNR(B) window. As of August 13, FCNR(B) deposits had stood at USD 52.3 billion, accounting for the bulk of the USD 56.85 billion mobilised through the three components of the special facility. The RBI said the deposit mobilisation window would close on August 31, although swaps against eligible FCNR(B) deposits could still be undertaken with the central bank until September 11.Central Bank had reported a much lower mobilisation earlier in the programme. The lender had mobilised about USD 8.4 million by July 17 and had set itself a USD 400 million target by September end. By August 12, its mobilisation had risen to USD 250 million, with the bank saying the entire FCNR(B) flow was coming through its GIFT City unit.The bank had started offering up to 9x leverage, under which a customer placing USD 100,000 as an FCNR(B) deposit could avail financing of nearly nine times the deposit amount through the IBU. Central Bank had also lined up forex resources to meet the demand for such loans. Advt Central Bank had around 75,500 NRI customers with nearly 1.97 lakh accounts and had created 159 NRI desks, besides setting up a dedicated international banking war room and helpline to drive mobilisation.
