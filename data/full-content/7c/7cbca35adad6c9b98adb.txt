Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); ACIT Vs Rajesh Gupta (ITAT Delhi) Gross Alleged Bogus Sales Cannot Satisfy the ₹50 Lakh Threshold for Reopening U/s 149(1)(b) Without Evidence of Escaped Taxable Income The Delhi ITAT held that the gross value of alleged bogus sales could not, on the facts of the case, establish escapement of income chargeable to tax of ₹50 lakh or more for reopening beyond three years under section 149(1)(b) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Assessing Officer treated sales of ₹1.65 crore as unexplained bank credits at the reopening stage but ultimately assessed an estimated profit of ₹20.69 lakh. The Tribunal found that the material available when the notice was issued failed to substantiate either the statutory threshold or the claim that the alleged escaped income was represented by an asset. How the Reassessment Began Rajesh Gupta filed his return for AY 2013–14 declaring ₹7,38,410. A survey under section 133A in the case of third parties yielded statements indicating that certain concerns supplied accommodation entries for purchases and sales. Based on this information, the Assessing Officer identified the assessee as a beneficiary of entries from Gayatri Maa Enterprises and Parth International. The original notice under section 148, dated 16 April 2021, was treated as a show-cause notice under section 148A(b) pursuant to the Supreme Court’s decision in Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal. On 28 July 2022, the Assessing Officer passed an order under section 148A(d) and issued a fresh notice under section 148. In the reassessment order dated 24 May 2023, he applied a 12.5% profit rate to the alleged bogus sales of ₹1,65,50,083 and added ₹20,68,760 as unexplained business income. The Revenue’s Argument on the ₹50 Lakh Threshold The CIT(A) quashed the reassessment as time-barred. Before the Tribunal, the Revenue argued that the validity of the notice had to be tested against the material available at the time of issue. It emphasised that section 149(1)(b) covers income that amounts to or is likely to amount to ₹50 lakh or more. According to the Revenue, the corresponding bank credits qualified as deposits and therefore as assets. It also argued that recording an accommodation entry in audited books does not establish the genuineness of the transaction. Why the Tribunal Held the Reopening Time-Barred The Tribunal referred to Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal and examined the notice under section 149 as amended by the Finance Act, 2021. The ordinary three-year period for AY 2013–14 expired on 31 March 2017. To invoke the extended period under the provision applicable to this case, the Assessing Officer needed material showing that the escaped taxable income, represented in the form of an asset, reached or was likely to reach the statutory threshold. The Tribunal found that the sales were already reflected in the audited books, VAT returns and profit and loss account. The assessee had included the profit element, computed at the declared gross profit rate of 0.7%, in the returned income. The Assessing Officer had simply characterised the gross sales as unexplained bank credits without substantiating how those credits represented escaped taxable income. Referring to Ankit Khandelwal v. ITO, the Tribunal emphasised that the determination in the section 148A(d) order must rest on tangible material. It also took into account that the reassessment ultimately brought to tax only the estimated profit of ₹20,68,760. The Evidence and the Denial of Cross-Examination The Tribunal considered the 2016 raid report, statements specifically confirming receipt of goods and genuineness of the bills in the assessee’s case, and the District Magistrate’s order releasing seized stock after verification of invoices. Despite repeated requests, the assessee was denied cross-examination of the person whose general statement the Assessing Officer relied upon. In these circumstances, the Tribunal held that using the general statement to override the specific confirmations violated natural justice, drawing on Andaman Timber Industries v. CCE and PCIT v. Best Infrastructure (India) Pvt. Ltd. The order under section 148A(d) also incorrectly stated that the assessee had failed to file a return in response to the original notice. The assessee had filed that return on 23 May 2021. Author’s Comments The distinction between transaction value and escaped taxable income is central to the ruling. Where the Department relies on the gross amount of recorded sales, the section 148A(d) order must explain, with reference to the available evidence, how much of that amount represents income that escaped assessment and how it is represented by the alleged asset. Here, the final profit estimate illustrated the gap between the turnover cited and the income assessed. The Tribunal’s reasoning also turned on the lack of supporting material at the notice stage, a point that matters when applying this decision to another case involving a final addition below ₹50 lakh. The Tribunal upheld the quashing of the section 148 notice, the section 148A(d) order and the consequential reassessment. It dismissed the Revenue’s appeal and dismissed the assessee’s cross-objection as infructuous. This appeal is filed by the Revenue against the order of ld. Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals)/National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [“Ld. CIT(A)”, for short] dated 23.12.2025 for the Assessment Year 2013-14. The assessee has also filed cross objections. 2. Brief facts of the issue raised by the Revenue in Department’s appeal are, assessee, an individual, has filed its return of income for assessment year 2013-14 on 28.09.2013 declaring total income of Rs.7,38,410/-. Based on the information available with the AO, on the basis of a survey action conducted under section 133A of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (for short ‘the Act’) on 30.11.2018 in the case of Ashok Kumar Gupta, Sandeep Gupta and Anuj Gupta. During the survey, it was found that they were engaged in providing accommodation entries of non-genuine purchases and non- genuine sales to various parties. Shri Ashok Kumar Gupta in his statement on oath recorded during survey on 30.11.2018 admitted that he has given accommodation entries through his entities, i.e. M/s. Gayatri Maa Enterprises, M/s. Ridhi Siddhi Impex and M/s. Parth International and others to various individuals during financial year 2012-13. It was found during the search that there were no supporting evidences of actual sales/purchase found at the possession of Shri Ashok Kumar Gupta. On analysis of the information gathered, it was also observed that all the abovesaid entities are shell/paper concerns, which are controlled by Ashok Kumar Gupta or his associates, which was admitted to be shell concerns. Further, at the time of search operation, Anuj Kumar Gupta, son of Ashish Kumar Gupta was recorded on oath on 03.03.2020, in which he has admitted that he was also getting commission income through providing accommodation entries to various parties in his proprietorship concern, Parth International. He further accepted that the activities of Parth International are managed by his father and he is not aware of the business activities of Parth International. After analyzing the information and statement, the AO came to the conclusion that Ashok Kumar Gupta is engaged in providing accommodation entries to various parties, which was an admitted fact as per oath. Since the assessee is one of the beneficiaries of the accommodation entries provided by the entities controlled and managed by Ashok Kumar Gupta, he noticed that assessee has taken bogus entries from M/s. Gayatri Maa Enterprises and Parth International. Based on the above information, case of the assessee was reopened by issue of notice under section 148 of the Act after passing an order under section 148A(d) of the Act dated 28.07.2022. After the issue of several notices and as per the information submitted by the assessee in response to various notices, the AO, after considering the submissions of the assessee, sustained the addition by estimating the net profit @ 12.5% of the total bogus sales and added the same to the income of the assessee to the extent of Rs.20,68,760/-. 3. Aggrieved with the above order, assessee preferred an appeal before the NAFAC, Delhi and filed a detailed submissions along with copies of Form 26AS, return of income, computation of total income, audited report, audited financial statement, copy of bank statement of HDFC Bank and State Bank of Bikaner & Jaipur and other bank statements, statement account of the other alleged entities and other details which are reproduced at pages 3 to 8 of the impugned order. After considering the relevant facts of the case, ld. CIT (A) first adjudicated technical ground raised by the assessee on the issue of validity of reopening of assessment under section 147 r.w.s. 148 and 148A of the Act. Ld. CIT(A) allowed the technical ground raised by the assessee on the basis of decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Union of India vs. Ashish Agarwal and relevant findings of the ld. CIT(A) are reproduced below “6.1 Ground No.1 pertains to the validity of the reopening proceedings under section 147 read with sections 148 and 148A, including contentions on limitation under section 149, incorrect facts in the order under section 148A(d), and denial of cross-examination of Shri Ashok Kumar Gupta. Meanwhile, ground No. 2 contests the addition of Rs.20,68,760/- made by applying a rate of 12.5% on the alleged bogus sales of Rs.1,65,50,083/-. Ground No. 3 is general in nature. All grounds are addressed below. The assessment for AY 2013-14 was reopened pursuant to the Supreme Court’s judgment in Union of India vs. Ashish Aggarwal [2022 SCC OnLine SC 543], which mandated the application of the new reassessment regime (effective 01.04.2021) to all notices issued after that date, including those originally issued under the old regime between 01.04.2021 and 30.06.2021 (extended by TOLA). The original notice under section 148 dated 16.04.2021 was treated as a deemed notice under section 148A(b) dated 23.05.2022, followed by the order under section 148A(d) dated 28.07.2022 and a fresh notice under section 148 on the same date. The appellant filed returns in response to both notices, provided detailed replies to questionnaires under sections 142(1) and 143(2), and availed personal hearing on 16.05.2023. The assessment order under section 147 read with section 144B dated 24.05.2023 added Rs. 20,68,760/- as unexplained business income from alleged bogus sales to entities of Shri Ashok Kumar Gupta (M/s Gayatri Maa Enterprises and M/s Parth International), treating these as non-genuine based on survey findings under section 133A dated 30.11.2018 and Shri Gupta’s statement recorded. The appellant’s primary challenge under Ground No. 1 is that the reopening is barred by limitation under section 149 of the Act, as amended by Finance Act, 2021. For AY 2013-14, the end of the relevant assessment year is 31.03.2014. Under the new regime, as clarified by the Supreme Court in Union of India vs. Rajeev Bansal [(2024) 301 TAXMAN 238 (SC)], reassessment notices issued after 01.04.2021 (including those converted from old-regime notices under TOLA extensions) must strictly comply with the time limits in section 149. Specifically, for AY 2013-14, notices can only be issued beyond three years from the end of the AY (i.e., after 31.03.2017) if the case falls under section 149(1)(b) — where the Assessing Officer possesses books, documents, or evidence showing escaped income chargeable to tax amounting to or likely to amount to Rs. 50 lakhs or more, and such income is “represented in the form of” an asset (as defined in the Explanation to section 149(1), including immovable property, shares, loans/advances, or bank deposits). The Supreme Court in Rajeev Bansal emphasized that TOLA‘s extensions do not revive time-barred proceedings under the old regime for such AYs; instead, the “surviving period of limitation” is computed under the new section 149 based on material available at the time of issuing the notice, not the final assessed quantum. Further, CBDT Instruction No. 1/2022 dated 11.05.2022 (implementing Ashish Aggarwal) explicitly provides in para 6.2 that for AY 2013-14, reopening is permissible only under section 149(1)(b), as the general three-year limit under section 149(1)(a) has long expired. In the order under section 148A(d) dated 28.07.2022, the Assessing Officer invoked section 149(1)(b) by alleging escaped income exceeding Rs. 50 lakhs from “bogus sales” of Rs. 1,65,50,083/-, classifying these as “unexplained bank credits” qualifying as “bank deposits” (an asset). However, this formation of belief is flawed on multiple counts. First, the alleged “bogus sales” were fully recorded in the appellant’s audited books of account, VAT returns, and profit & loss account for AY 2013-14, and the profit element thereon (at the appellant’s declared GP rate of 0.7%) was already offered and included in the returned income of Rs. 7,38,410/-. As held by the Delhi High Court in Pr. CIT-5 vs. Jatin Investment Pvt. Ltd. [ITA Nos. 43 & 44/2016], sales already shown and offered to tax cannot constitute escapement of income, as any further addition would result in double taxation of the same income. Thus, there is no underlying escapement justifying reopening under section 147. Second, even assuming, for argument’s sake, an initial belief in escapement, the quantum finally determined in the assessment order (Rs. 20,68,760/-) is well below Rs. 50 lakhs, bringing the case squarely under the three-year limitation of section 149(1)(a), which expired on 31.03.2017. The Delhi High Court in Ankit Khandelwal vs. ITO [W.P.(C) No. 297/2023, dated 01.04.2025] has clarified that the Rs. 50 lakh threshold for invoking the 10-year period under section 149(1)(b) must be based on the Assessing Officer’s determination in the section 148A(d) order itself, supported by tangible material. Here, the AO’s classification of “bogus sales” as an “asset” (unexplained credits) lacks substantiation, as the transactions involved outward sales (not inward credits or deposits) and were rebutted by contemporaneous evidence: the 2016 raid report, statements from Shri Ashok Kumar Gupta and his sons (Anuj and Sandeep Gupta) specifically confirming receipt of goods and genuineness of bills in the appellant’s case, and the District Magistrate’s order (Case No.467/DM/North/2016) releasing seized stock after verifying the invoices. The general statement of Shri Gupta recorded in 2021 (post-survey) cannot override these specific, case- specific confirmations without opportunity for cross-examination, which was denied despite repeated requests — a violation of natural justice principles as laid down in Andaman Timber Industries vs. CCE [(2015) 7 SCO 799] and PCIT vs. Best Infrastructure (India) Pvt. Ltd. [2017 (398) ITR 218 (Del)]. The order under section 148A(d) was also passed on incorrect facts, stating that no return was filed in response to the original notice dated 16.04.2021, whereas the appellant filed it on 23.05.2021 (Acknowledgment No. 357836701230521). In light of Rajeev Bansal, the “borrowed satisfaction” from the Insight Portal and Shri Gupta’s general statement does not constitute credible material for invoking section 149(1)(b), as the alleged escapement neither exceeds Rs. 50 lakhs nor manifests as an “asset.” The proceedings are thus time- barred under section 149(1)(a), and the notice under section 148 dated 28.07.2022, order under section 148A(d), and consequent assessment order dated 24.05.2023 are quashed. Ground No. 1 is allowed.” 4. Aggrieved with the above order, Revenue is in appeal before us raising following grounds of appeal:- “1. The learned CIT(A) has failed to appreciate that, as per the ratio laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Union of India vs. Rajeev Bansal (2024) 301 Taxman 238 (SC), the validity of a notice u/s 148 issued beyond three years is to be examined with reference to the material available and the AOs prima facie satisfaction at the time of issuance of notice, and not with reference to the quantum of income finally assessed. 2. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred in law in holding that since the final addition made was Rs. 20,68,760/-, the reassessment could not have been initiated u/s 149(l)(b), overlooking the statutory expression “amounts to or is likely to amount to Rs. 50 lakh or more” as interpreted by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Rajeev Bansal (supra). 3. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred in holding that alleged bogus sales recorded in the audited books of account cannot constitute escapement of income, without appreciating that accommodation entries routed through banking channels and reflected in books may still represent non genuine transactions resulting in escapement of income. 4. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred in law and on facts in holding that the alleged escaped income was not “represented in the form of an asset,” ignoring that the bogus sales resulted in corresponding bank credits/deposits, which squarely fall within the definition of “asset” under the Explanation to section 149(l)(b). 5. The Ld. The right of cross examination must depend upon the circumstances of each case and also on the statute concerned (AIR 1967 SC 122). 5. Hon’ble Supreme Court, in the judgement of Union of India Vs Rajeev Bansal in para no 48 has held that a notice shall be judged in accordance with the law existing at the time it is issued, consequently notices issued after 01-04-2021, are to be examined as per the law laid down under section 149 as amended by Finance Act, 2021, relevant para of the said judgement is reproduced hereunder:- “48. Notices have to be judged according to the law existing on the date the notice is issued. Section 149 of the old regime primarily provided two time limits: (i) four years for all situations and (ii) beyond four years and within six years if the income chargeable to tax which escaped assessment amounted to Rupees one lakh or more. After 1 April 2021, the time limits prescribed under the new regime came into force. The ordinary time limit of four years was reduced to three years. Therefore, in all situations, reassessment notices could be issued under the new regime if not more than three years have elapsed from the end of the relevant assessment year. For example, for assessment year 2018-2019, the four year period would have expired on 31 March 2023 under the old regime. However, if the notice is issued after 1 April 2021, the three year time limit prescribed under the new regime will be applicable. The three year time limit will expire on 31 March 2022.” 4.
