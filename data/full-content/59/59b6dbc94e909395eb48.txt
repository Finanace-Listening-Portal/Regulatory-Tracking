Summary: ABSLI Assured Income Plus, UIN 109N127V19, is a non-linked, non-participating individual savings life insurance plan issued by Aditya Birla Sun Life Insurance Company Limited. It combines life cover during the policy term with a guaranteed income stream after that term, subject to the policy remaining entitled to benefits. The policyholder chooses at inception between Income Only and Income with Lumpsum, along with the premium-paying term, policy term, payout period and payout frequency. Those choices, the issued benefit illustration and the policy schedule determine the rupee amounts; the word “guaranteed” does not mean that every quoted total is payable despite non-payment, surrender, a loan deduction or a reduced paid-up event. The current V19 contract also provides a 30-day free-look period, grace periods, reduced paid-up and surrender rules, revival rights and a loan facility after value acquisition. For tax, an individual life policy is within the GST exemption introduced from 22 September 2025, while income-tax deduction and receipt exemptions depend on the taxpayer’s regime, premium thresholds, sum-assured tests and the nature of the receipt. This article explains how the contractual pieces fit together without treating the plan as a deposit or calculating an investment return from promotional figures. Premium term, policy term and payout period are different clocks The plan separates three timelines. The premium-paying term is how long scheduled premiums are due. The policy term is the protection-and-accumulation period ending before the survival-income phase. The benefit payout period is the later 20, 25 or 30 years during which income is paid if the life insured survives to the end of the policy term and contractual conditions are met. Treating those three clocks as one is a common source of mistaken comparisons with fixed deposits or annuities. Income Only and Income with Lumpsum produce different cash-flow shapes Income is payable after the policy term, not during premium payment If the life insured survives to the end of the policy term and all due premiums have been paid, the guaranteed Income Benefit becomes payable during the chosen benefit payout period at the chosen frequency. The Income Only option concentrates the survival value in that stream. Under Income with Lumpsum, the policy adds a guaranteed lumpsum at the end of the payout period according to the schedule and applicable factor. The illustration must be read for the selected age, premium, term and option; the insurer’s promotional example is not a universal yield. Loyalty additions enhance income only on stated conditions The V19 contract states that, provided all due premiums have been paid during the premium-paying term, the Income Benefit is enhanced by Loyalty Additions calculated as a percentage of the Income Benefit. The schedule states the applicable income and enhancement. The label should not be confused with a discretionary participating bonus: this is a non-participating product, and the issued contractual figures control. Commutation changes timing, not the underlying contract At or after the end of the policy term, the policyholder can use the commutation option to receive a discounted value of outstanding Income Benefits and the enhanced guaranteed lumpsum, if applicable. The V19 wording sets a contractual floor linked to total premiums paid less survival benefits already paid and describes the discount-factor methodology. Commutation accelerates future cash flows and therefore should not be described as collecting every scheduled instalment early at face value. If the life insured dies during the benefit payout period, the survival benefits continue to the nominee under the contract, with an option to take the applicable future benefits as a lump sum. That is distinct from death during the policy term, when the death-benefit formula applies. Death cover during the policy term and the staggered option For an in-force policy, death of the life insured during the policy term triggers the Sum Assured on Death. V19 defines it as the highest of three amounts: ten times annualised premium, 150% of total premiums paid up to death, or the sum assured. The policy covers death generally, subject to the suicide clause and other contractual provisions. If death occurs in grace, unpaid premium due at that date can be recovered from the benefit. Missed premiums, reduced paid-up value and surrender Grace and value acquisition determine the immediate consequence The grace period is 15 days for monthly mode and 30 days for annual, semi-annual or quarterly modes. Benefits continue during grace. If less than one full year’s premiums have been paid and the overdue amount remains unpaid after grace, the policy lapses and benefits cease unless revival succeeds. Once one full year premium for the first policy year has been received, the policy can continue as reduced paid-up after a missed premium. The V19 RPU factor broadly compares instalments paid with instalments originally payable, reducing death and income benefits rather than preserving the full illustration. Surrender becomes available before the second-year GSV test V19 says the policy acquires surrender value after one full year premium for the first policy year has been received. A surrender during the first policy year is paid only at that year’s end and is based on Special Surrender Value. After all due instalment premiums for the first two policy years have been paid, surrender value is the higher of Guaranteed Surrender Value and Special Surrender Value. GSV is a stated percentage of total premiums paid; SSV follows the applicable regulatory method and may be revised subject to approval. Neither figure should be estimated by subtracting charges from premiums. A written surrender quotation is the appropriate decision document. Revival and policy loan are conditional facilities A lapsed or reduced paid-up policy may be revived within five consecutive complete years from the first unpaid premium, subject to arrears, interest or late fees, evidence of insurability and insurer approval. A loan is available after the first policy year once surrender value has been acquired. The current contract states a ₹5,000 minimum and a maximum of 80% of applicable surrender value after outstanding loan adjustment. Interest accrues, benefits are reduced by outstanding balances and an RPU policy may terminate if loan plus interest exceeds surrender value. The contract’s 2025 example interest rate is historical; the insurer declares the applicable rate annually, so it should not be quoted as a current 2026 rate without a fresh written confirmation. Free-look, proposal disclosures, claims and grievance records The V19 policy provides a 30-day free-look period from receipt of the policy. Cancellation can deduct proportionate risk premium, medical-examination expense and stamp duty as permitted by the policyholder-protection rules. The free-look is the best time to reconcile the illustration with the schedule, inspect nomination, confirm premium mode and verify that the selected option, term and payout frequency match the proposal. Review every proposal answer before signing, especially health history, tobacco use, occupation, income, existing insurance and the identity of proposer and life insured. Keep the schedule, illustration, customer information sheet, proposal copy, premium receipts, medical reports, endorsements and nomination record together. For a claim, notify ABSLI through an official channel, retain the claim reference and submit the event-specific documents requested under the contract. Raise a written grievance with the insurer first and retain the acknowledgement. If unresolved within the applicable timeline or the response is unsatisfactory, use IRDAI Bima Bharosa and, where eligible, the Insurance Ombudsman route. GST and income-tax treatment for policies issued in 2026–27 Individual life-insurance GST exemption applies from 22 September 2025 Government FAQs implementing the 56th GST Council decisions state that the rate changes took effect on 22 September 2025 and that the life-insurance exemption covers all individual life policies, including term, ULIP and endowment policies. ABSLI Assured Income Plus is an individual non-linked savings life policy, so the base policy premium is within that exemption under the current framework. The V19 contract was issued in August 2025 and contains a general clause that taxes, if any, are borne by the policyholder; that savings clause does not override the later exemption. The actual premium receipt and invoice should still be checked, especially for optional riders, group arrangements or separately supplied services whose classification may differ. Premium deduction depends on the taxpayer’s chosen regime From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 governs current tax years. Section 123 permits an eligible individual or Hindu undivided family a deduction for specified payments, including qualifying life-insurance premiums through Schedule XV, within the aggregate statutory ceiling of ₹1,50,000 and subject to the schedule’s conditions. The deduction is not automatic: the selected tax regime, the insured relationship, premium-to-capital-sum ratio, payment evidence and other claims under the same ceiling matter. A person taxed under the default concessional regime should not assume this deduction is available merely because the premium receipt carries the policyholder’s name. Death receipts and survival receipts must be tested separately The current exemption schedule distinguishes death receipts from non-death receipts. Death benefits remain protected by the death exception, subject to special exclusions such as a Keyman policy. For a non-ULIP life policy issued on or after 1 April 2023, a survival, maturity, surrender or commuted receipt can fail the exemption where annual premium or aggregate premium across relevant policies exceeds ₹5,00,000 in a tax year. Premium-to-sum-assured conditions and policy-specific exclusions must also be tested. If exemption fails, taxation and any withholding are not settled merely by the insurer’s tax deduction; the taxpayer should compute the final position from the governing provisions and premium history. Income Benefit instalments received over many years therefore require a policy-year and taxpayer-specific review rather than a one-time “tax-free” label. Frequently Asked Questions on ABSLI Assured Income Plus V19 1. Is UIN 109N127V19 the current identity to check? Yes. The current insurer page, V19 customer information sheet and V19 policy contract identify this version. An older V-number should not be used to describe a new proposal. 2. Does premium payment for five years mean income begins after five years? Not always. Income begins after the policy term. With a five-year premium-paying term, the selected policy term can run from five to ten years. 3. Can the policyholder switch from Income Only to Income with Lumpsum later? No. The benefit option is chosen at inception. The commutation facility is different: it converts eligible outstanding benefits to a discounted lump sum under the contract. 4. Are Loyalty Additions a market-linked bonus? No. This is a non-linked, non-participating plan. V19 describes Loyalty Additions as a contractual enhancement to Income Benefit, conditional on all due premiums being paid and stated in the schedule. 5. Is surrender value equal to premiums paid? No. SSV or the higher of GSV and SSV applies depending on premiums paid and timing. The policy terminates on surrender, so a written quotation should be obtained before acting. 6. Does a missed premium erase the policy immediately? During grace, cover continues. After grace, a policy with less than one full year premium paid lapses; a policy that has acquired surrender value generally continues on reduced paid-up terms unless revived. 7. Is GST still charged on this individual policy in November 2026? Under the current government framework, qualifying individual life policies are exempt from GST from 22 September 2025. Check the invoice for riders or other separately classified supplies. 8. Are all income and lump-sum receipts tax-free? No. Death and non-death receipts are tested differently. Issue date, annual and aggregate premium, capital-sum ratios, policy type and taxpayer facts determine the result.
