Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Rajeshwari Ganapathi Vs Assessment Unit (Telangana High Court) Summary: The Telangana High Court examined a writ petition challenging an order dated 18 August 2026 passed under Section 220(6) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, requiring the petitioner to deposit 20% of the tax demand for obtaining a stay on recovery of the remaining demand during the pendency of her appeal. The petitioner submitted that she had already deposited ₹5 lakh and that her financial difficulties prevented her from complying with the stipulated pre-deposit requirement. She accordingly sought a reduction in the amount payable and expressed willingness to deposit the reduced amount to enable the appellate authority to decide her appeal on merits. Considering the submissions of both parties and the material on record, the High Court noted the amount of ₹83,99,182 in connection with the 20% deposit requirement and the payment of ₹5 lakh already made. The Court directed the petitioner to deposit an additional ₹45 lakh within four weeks, thereby bringing the aggregate payment to ₹50 lakh. Upon compliance, the amount would be treated as the requisite pre-deposit for granting interim protection against recovery until the appeal was finally decided by the appellate authority. The writ petition was accordingly disposed of without any order as to costs. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF TELANGANA HIGH COURT Heard Mr. A.V.A.Siva Kartikeya, learned counsel appearing for the petitioner and Ms. Bokaro Sapna Reddy, learned Senior Standing Counsel for Income Tax Department appearing for the respondents. 2. The limited challenge in the writ petition is to the order passed by the 2nd respondent dated 18.08.2026 whereby on an application under Sectionnd220(6) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, the 2nd respondent has directed the petitioner to deposit 20% of the demand raised in order to get a stay of the remaining demand. 3. Learned counsel for the petitioner submits that the petitioner has already deposited Rs.5,00,000/- with the authorities concerned and her financial condition is not so good whereby the petitioner would be able to make 20% of the payment in order to pursue the appeal. He further submits that subject to this Court reducing the percentage of tax to be paid, the petitioner shall make all endeavours in depositing the same so that the appeal can be decided on its own merits. 4. Upon due consideration of the submissions put forth on either side and on perusal of record, the admitted amount that comes upon 20% of the demand raised is from Rs.83,99,182/-, of which Rs.5,00,000/- have already been deposited. If that be so, subject to the petitioner depositing Rs.45,00,000/- more to make the total tax paid to be at Rs.50,00,000/- out of the total outstanding amount within a period of four weeks, the same may be treated as pre-deposit required for the interim protection till the appeal is finally decided by the appellate authority. 5. With the aforesaid direction, the Writ Petition is disposed of. There shall be no order as to costs. Miscellaneous applications, if any pending, shall stand closed.
