Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); CIT Vs J. Jayalalitha (Madras High Court) Summary : The Madras High Court, in Commissioner of Income Tax, Central II, Chennai Vs Ms J. Jayalalitha, disposed of six departmental income tax appeals relating to assessment years 1987-88 to 1992-93 after the Income Tax Department expressed its unwillingness to pursue the proceedings on account of low tax effect. The appeals, numbered T.C. Nos. 194 to 199 of 2008, arose from the common order dated 20 April 2007 passed by the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Madras ‘B’ Bench, in Cross Objection Nos. 37 to 42/Mds/2002 connected with ITA Nos. 521 to 526/Mds/2002. The appeals had been instituted under Section 260A of the Income-tax Act, 1961. When the matters were taken up, Mrs. V. Pushpa, Senior Standing Counsel appearing for the Revenue, submitted that the Department did not wish to pursue the six appeals in view of CBDT Circular No. 9/2024 dated 17 September 2024, governing litigation involving low tax effect. The Division Bench comprising Dr. Justice Anita Sumanth and Justice Krishnaswamy Govindarajan recorded the Department’s submission and dismissed all six appeals as withdrawn, expressly leaving the questions of law open for determination in an appropriate future case. The Court did not adjudicate the underlying income tax disputes or pronounce upon their merits. The respondent had died during the pendency of the proceedings, and several miscellaneous petitions filed in 2022 sought condonation of delay in setting aside abatement and impleadment of proposed legal representatives. Since the Department had decided not to pursue the principal appeals, the Court held that those applications no longer required consideration and closed them as unnecessary. No costs were awarded. The order illustrates the procedural consequence of withdrawal under the CBDT’s monetary-limit litigation policy, while preserving the substantive legal questions for future adjudication.
