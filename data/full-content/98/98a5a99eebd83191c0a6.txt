Resident Eligibility, LRS Limit and Permitted Purposes LRS is available to resident individuals, including minors through the required guardian process, for permitted current and capital account transactions. RBI’s current Master Direction states an aggregate limit of USD 250,000 per financial year. The aggregate covers the resident’s LRS remittances through all authorised dealers, not a separate limit for each bank, beneficiary or purpose. Once the limit is used, a new bank account or platform does not reset it. Purpose codes and documentary evidence determine eligibility Common permitted purposes can include education, medical treatment, travel, maintenance of close relatives, gifts, donations and specified overseas investments, subject to the rules applicable to each transaction. The remitter must choose the correct purpose and support it with invoices, admission letters, medical estimates, beneficiary details, investment documents or other evidence as applicable. The bank may require a declaration of previous LRS usage and source of funds. LRS does not permit remittances for overseas margin or margin calls, foreign exchange trading on prohibited electronic venues, lottery or sweepstake transactions, or other purposes barred under FEMA and current RBI directions. It should not be described as a route for offshore leveraged forex, binary options or CFDs. An outward payment for a lawful investment is not the same as funding margin for a leveraged position. KYC, A2 Form and Supporting Documents The customer must complete the bank’s KYC and transaction documents. The A2 form records the remitter, beneficiary, currency, amount, purpose and regulatory declaration. PAN, bank-account details, source of funds and beneficiary-bank information are generally relevant. A student payment, medical remittance, gift, property transaction or NRO repatriation can require different supporting evidence. The official NRO documentation page also identifies source-trail and tax-document needs for repatriation. Pricing, Exchange Rate and T+1 Processing The bank’s current service summary shows “NIL” service charge for several listed remittance categories and indicates T+1 processing for resident outward, NRE outward and NRO repatriation services. Those fields must be read narrowly. Nil service charge does not establish a zero-cost transfer because the customer exchanges INR at the bank’s quoted rate and may bear an exchange-rate margin, correspondent charge, beneficiary-bank deduction, tax or destination fee. The customer should obtain a timestamped quote and ask whether charges are OUR, SHA or BEN where that convention applies. Processing time is not guaranteed beneficiary credit T+1 describes the bank’s stated processing target for specified services after complete documentation and acceptance. Cut-off times, weekends, currency holidays, regulatory review, intermediary routing, repair messages and beneficiary-bank procedures can extend credit. A SWIFT message or debit confirmation should be retained, but neither proves that the beneficiary received the full amount. Refunds can be reduced by conversion differences and upstream fees. Before authorising, compare the INR debit, foreign-currency amount, exchange rate, bank margin, service charge, GST, correspondent allocation and expected beneficiary credit. The bank’s invoice or advice should identify the components actually charged. Promotional language or a nil line item should not be converted into a claim that the provider is cheapest or that spreads are fixed. Settlement, Cancellation, Refund and Grievance Route Once an outward instruction is released into correspondent banking, cancellation is not assured. The bank may send a recall or amendment, but the correspondent and beneficiary bank control whether funds can be returned or corrected. Currency conversion on refund may occur at a different rate, and third-party deductions may be irreversible. Customers should immediately report an error with the transaction reference, beneficiary details, amount and supporting correspondence. Escalation should begin with the bank The official service summary routes support through the mapped relationship manager or branch and the bank’s published customer-care channels. A written complaint should state the requested remedy and attach the A2 form, quote, debit advice, SWIFT reference and beneficiary evidence without exposing passwords or one-time codes. If the bank does not resolve an eligible complaint through its internal process within the applicable period, the customer can examine the RBI Integrated Ombudsman route. Fraud or cyber incidents may also require immediate account blocking and reporting to the appropriate authorities. GST, TCS and Tax Records for Remitters GST can apply to taxable banking or foreign-exchange service components under the applicable valuation rules. It is not a tax on the entire foreign-currency principal in the same manner as a simple percentage fee. The bank’s tax invoice and debit advice should be used rather than estimating GST from the remittance amount. Correspondent deductions outside India can have different legal and tax treatment. TCS depends on purpose, aggregate and current law Tax collection at source on LRS remittances can depend on the remitter’s aggregate remittances, purpose category, applicable threshold, payment date and current statutory rate. Education financed by a qualifying loan, other education or medical purposes, and other LRS purposes can have different treatment. From 1 April 2026 the Income Tax Act, 2025 and corresponding collection provisions apply. The bank’s collection is a tax credit, not necessarily the remitter’s final income-tax liability. Records should include the purpose code, A2 declaration, prior LRS usage, invoice or admission evidence, live exchange quote, debit advice, tax invoice, TCS certificate or statement credit, SWIFT message, beneficiary confirmation and any refund trail. The remitter should reconcile TCS with the annual information statement and return and obtain professional advice for cross-border assets, gifts, securities, overseas income, NRO repatriation or business-linked payments. Is the USD 250,000 LRS limit available separately at each bank? No. It is an aggregate per resident individual per financial year across authorised dealers. Can LRS fund an overseas forex or CFD margin account? No. LRS must not be described as permitting overseas margin or margin-call remittances or prohibited leveraged forex activity. Does a nil service-charge line mean the remittance has no cost? No. The exchange rate, bank margin, correspondent or beneficiary deductions, GST and taxes must still be checked. What documents are normally central to an LRS transfer? Form A2, PAN and KYC, source of funds, beneficiary details and purpose-specific evidence are central; the bank may ask for more.
