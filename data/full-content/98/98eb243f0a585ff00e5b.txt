Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Nitin Singhania Vs CIT(A) (ITAT Delhi) Political Donation Receipts Alone Do Not Support Section 80GGC Deduction Where the Mode of Payment Is Unproved Delhi ITAT Upholds Disallowance of Political Donations The Delhi ITAT upheld the disallowance of a deduction under section 80GGC of the Income-tax Act, 1961 because the assessee failed to establish the mode of payment of his political donations. The assessee furnished receipts before the CIT(A), but they did not specify how the donations had been paid. He also failed to furnish bank statements to substantiate the payments. The Tribunal separately upheld an addition of ₹7,88,501 for undisclosed bank interest reflected in Form 26AS and credited to the assessee’s bank accounts. Reassessment Completed Under Section 147 Read With Section 144 The assessee filed his return for AY 2020–21, declaring taxable income of ₹14,19,990. He disclosed business income and claimed a deduction for political contributions under section 80GGC. The Assessing Officer reopened the case by issuing a notice under section 148. The assessee did not file a return in response, and the assessment was completed on 3 February 2025 under section 147 read with section 144. The CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, sustained both adjustments by order dated 9 March 2026. No one appeared for the assessee before the Tribunal despite issuance of notice. The Tribunal heard the Departmental Representative and decided the appeal on the basis of the material available on record. Form 26AS and Bank Credits Supported the Interest Addition The Tribunal recorded that the assessee received total interest of ₹19,60,051 during the year. The disputed addition concerned two specific amounts, namely ₹7,69,719 from HDFC Bank and ₹18,782 from Kotak Mahindra Bank. The Assessing Officer found that both amounts appeared in the assessee’s Form 26AS, had been credited to his bank accounts and had not been offered to tax in his return. Before the CIT(A), the assessee could not produce documentary evidence to explain the omission or show that the amounts had already been included in his returned income. The Tribunal found no infirmity in the CIT(A)’s decision and dismissed this ground. The addition thus rested on matching information from Form 26AS and the bank statements, together with the absence of a reconciliation showing where the interest had been offered to tax. Donation Receipts Did Not Establish the Mode of Payment The Assessing Officer disallowed the section 80GGC deduction because the assessee had furnished no documentary evidence supporting the claim during assessment. At the first appellate stage, the assessee produced receipts for donations to political parties. The CIT(A) examined them and found that they did not mention the mode of payment. The assessee also failed to produce his bank statements to establish that the payments had been made through banking channels from his disclosed account. This omission mattered because section 80GGC excludes contributions made in cash. A receipt may show that the recipient acknowledged a donation, while leaving unanswered how the donor paid it. Here, the receipts did not resolve that question, and the assessee furnished no banking evidence to fill the gap. The CIT(A) therefore sustained the disallowance. Tribunal Sustains Both Adjustments on the Evidence The Tribunal agreed with the CIT(A)’s assessment of the donation receipts and the absence of supporting bank statements. The finding was that the assessee had failed to prove the mode of payment. The outcome did not require an affirmative finding that the donations had in fact been paid in cash. The two grounds turned on different uses of bank records. For the interest addition, the bank credits supported the entries already appearing in Form 26AS. For the deduction, the missing statements meant that the donation receipts could not be matched to any identifiable non-cash payment. The Tribunal upheld both adjustments and dismissed the appeal in full. Author’s Comments For a claim under section 80GGC, the receipt and the payment record establish different facts. The receipt identifies the contribution acknowledged by the political party. The payment record establishes how the donor transferred the money. Where the receipt is silent about the mode, a corresponding bank debit, cheque clearance or electronic transfer record can establish the link between the donor, the recipient and the amount claimed. In this case, that link was missing even after the receipts were furnished in appeal. The interest issue also illustrates the value of a bank-wise reconciliation. A difference between Form 26AS and the return can be examined by tracing each entry to the bank statement and then to the income computation. Here, the two bank credits matched the Form 26AS entries, and the assessee produced no evidence showing that either credit formed part of the interest already disclosed. That combination supported the addition of the two specific amounts. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. The appeal in ITA No. 5225/Del/2026 for AY 2020-21, arises out of the order of the ld National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. CIT(A)’, in short] dated 09.03.2026 against the order of assessment passed u/s 147 r.w.s. 144 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’) dated 03.02.2025 by the Assessing Officer, Assessment Unit, Income Tax Department (hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. AO’). 2. None appeared on behalf of the assessee despite issuance of notice. Hence I proceed to dispose of this appeal on hearing the learned DR and based on materials available on record. 3. The effective issues to be decided in this appeal is as to whether the learned CITA was justified in confirming the addition made on account of interest income in the facts and circumstances of the instant case. Yet another issue to be decided in this appeal is as to whether the learned CITA was justified in confirming the disallowance of deduction under section 80 GGC of the Act in the facts and circumstances of the instant case. 4. I have heard the learned DR and perused the materials available on record. The return of income for the assessment year 2020-21 has been filed by the assessee declaring taxable income of ₹14,19,990. The assessee was in receipt of interest income of ₹19,60,051 during the year under consideration. The case of the assessee was sought to be reopened by issuance of notice intersection 148 of the Act. In response to the said notice, the assessee did not file return of income. The learned AO noted that assessee was in receipt of interest income of ₹7,69,719 from HDFC Bank and ₹18,782 from Kotak Mahindra Bank. These 2 interest incomes were duly reflected in Form No. 26AS of the assessee and also credited in the bank statement of the assessee but the same were not offered to tax by the assessee in the return of income. Accordingly, the same were added as undisclosed interest income by the learned AO in the assessment. Even before the learned CITA, the assessee was not able to produce any documentary evidence which resulted in upholding the addition made on this account. I do not find any infirmity in the action of the learned CITA in upholding this addition made on account of undisclosed interest income. Hence the ground raised by the assessee in this regard is dismissed. 5. The learned AO noted that assessee disclosed business income in the return of income and had claimed deduction under section 80 GGC of the Act on account of political donations paid by him. Since no documentary evidences were furnished by the assessee before the learned AO to support the claim of deduction under section 80 GGC of the Act, the said deduction was disallowed by the learned AO in the assessment. Before the learned CITA, the assessee furnished the receipts for having made the payment of donations to the political parties. The learned CITA noted that the receipts submitted by the assessee did not mention the mode of payment of donation by the assessee. The assessee further could not substantiate the mode of payment by furnishing his bank statements. Hence there was no proof submitted by the assessee to establish that the donations were paid through regular banking channels from the disclosed bank account by the assessee. Accordingly, the learned CITA upheld the action of the learned AO in disallowing the claim of deduction under section 80 GGC of the Act. I do not find any infirmity in the said order of the learned CITA. Accordingly, the ground raised by the assessee in this regard is dismissed. 6. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is dismissed. Order pronounced in the open court on 18-Nov-2026.
