Background of the Case and Search Proceedings: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Surat Bench, in ACIT Vs. M/s Patel Madhavlal Maganlal & Co., decided five interconnected appeals filed by the Income Tax Department (Revenue) and M/s Patel Madhavlal Maganlal & Co. (Assessee) for Assessment Years 2018-19, 2019-20 and 2020-21. The assessee was engaged in the business of Angadia services, involving transportation of cash, jewellery, diamonds and other valuables between different locations for commission. On 4 January 2018, the Central Goods and Services Tax Department intercepted cash and valuables at Mumbai Central Railway Station, resulting in the seizure of approximately ₹4.29 crore, including ₹89,83,700 attributed to the assessee firm. Subsequently, survey proceedings conducted at the assessee’s business premises in April 2019 were converted into search proceedings under Section 132 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. During the search, further cash was seized, and various loose papers, business records and digital images were examined. The Assessing Officer subsequently made substantial additions under Sections 68 and 69C and on account of alleged suppression of commission income. Although the CIT(A), Surat, granted considerable relief, both parties challenged different findings before the Tribunal. The principal questions were whether cash belonging to customers could be treated as the assessee’s undisclosed income, whether recorded cash balances sufficiently explained seized amounts, and whether additions based on estimates, assumptions and extrapolation of seized transactions could legally survive. 2. AY 2018-19 – Deletion of ₹89,83,700 Addition for Cash Seized at Mumbai: For Assessment Year 2018-19, the Assessing Officer treated ₹89,83,700 seized at Mumbai Central Railway Station as unexplained cash, principally because the assessee had failed to furnish documentary evidence relating to a proposed property purchase in Mumbai. The assessee firm, M/s Patel Madhavlal Maganlal & Co., explained that the amount represented cash transferred from its regular business branches, comprising ₹40,00,000 from Rajkot, ₹43,00,000 from Ahmedabad and ₹6,83,700 from Bhavnagar. The assessee produced branch-wise cash books, historical cash balances and a Chartered Accountant’s certificate supporting the availability and movement of funds. The CIT(A) verified that the respective branches possessed sufficient cash balances and observed that the Assessing Officer had disregarded material evidence while concentrating on the absence of documents concerning the proposed property transaction. Upholding the appellate order, the ITAT Surat held that the source of seized cash and its proposed utilisation were separate considerations. Once the assessee established the availability of cash through regularly maintained books, the amount could not be treated as unexplained merely because the intended property purchase was not substantiated. The Tribunal relied upon CIT Vs. Patel Natverlal Chinubhai & Co. (2013) 40 taxmann.com 304 (Gujarat High Court), wherein cash seized from an Angadia firm was held not taxable as unexplained money when supported by branch cash books. Accordingly, the Tribunal confirmed the deletion of the entire ₹89,83,700 addition and dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. 3. AY 2019-20 – Estimated Salary, Rent and Vehicle Expenses Cannot Be Added Without Evidence: For Assessment Year 2019-20, the Assessing Officer made additions of ₹1,72,25,981 towards alleged unexplained salary expenses, ₹8,43,788 towards rent expenses and ₹4,03,710 towards stationery and vehicle expenses under Section 69C. The salary addition was based on an estimated workforce of 144 employees earning an average monthly salary of ₹12,000, whereas the assessee had recorded salary expenditure of ₹35,10,019 in its books. The Assessing Officer relied on approximate employee numbers mentioned by Shri Babubhai N. Patel, partner of the assessee firm, without establishing that the estimated salaries were actually paid from undisclosed sources. Similarly, rental expenditure was estimated by assuming that 20 offices incurred rent of ₹12,000 per month, while stationery and vehicle expenditure was estimated at ₹12 lakh annually against recorded expenses of ₹7,96,290. The CIT(A) deleted these additions because no incriminating documents, actual payment records or corroborative evidence established expenditure exceeding the amounts disclosed in the books. The ITAT Surat agreed that the Assessing Officer had made additions on mere assumptions and presumptions, without proving that the assessee incurred unexplained expenditure. The Tribunal particularly observed that the partner’s approximate statement regarding employee strength could not establish salary expenditure for the relevant assessment year. Since the Revenue failed to produce contrary evidence, the Tribunal upheld the deletion of all three additions aggregating ₹1,84,73,479 and dismissed the relevant Revenue ground. 4. AY 2019-20 – ₹29.74 Crore Commission Addition Based on Extrapolation Rejected: A major dispute concerned the addition of approximately ₹29.74 crore towards alleged suppression of commission income. The Assessing Officer relied upon loose papers seized during search proceedings, particularly Annexure A-1, and statements of an employee, Shri Ashokbhai V. Patel, to estimate commission income from cash-transfer transactions. On the basis of selected entries, the Assessing Officer estimated daily commission income at approximately ₹10.29 lakh and extrapolated the amount over 313 working days, arriving at annual commission income of approximately ₹32.22 crore. After reducing the commission already disclosed by the assessee, an addition of approximately ₹29.74 crore was made. The CIT(A) rejected the Assessing Officer’s method of extrapolation but sustained an addition of ₹21,55,608 by applying an estimated commission rate of ₹200 per ₹1 lakh transferred. The assessee contended that its actual commission rate was ₹100 per ₹1 lakh, consistently followed in its regular business, and that the higher rate adopted by the CIT(A) was unsupported. The ITAT Surat found that the Assessing Officer’s estimation of annual commission from isolated seized entries was hypothetical and lacked a reliable evidentiary foundation. It further observed that the assessee’s disclosed commission rate was reasonable in the circumstances. Referring to ITO Vs. Deepak Vithaldas Suchak, the Tribunal accepted the assessee’s position and directed deletion of the ₹21,55,608 addition sustained by the CIT(A). Consequently, the Revenue’s attempt to restore the substantially larger commission addition failed. 5. AY 2019-20 – ₹2.32 Crore Mobile Phone Transactions Were Not Automatically Taxable Income: Another disputed addition of ₹2,32,18,000 arose from images and transaction details extracted from the mobile phone of the assessee’s employee, Shri Ashokbhai V. Patel. The Assessing Officer treated the amounts reflected in these images as undisclosed transactions attributable to M/s Patel Madhavlal Maganlal & Co.. The assessee disputed the execution of the transactions and maintained that the figures could not automatically represent its taxable income. The CIT(A) considered the nature of the Angadia business and held that cash transferred on behalf of customers could not be equated with the firm’s commission income. Accordingly, the addition was restricted to ₹46,436, calculated by applying commission at ₹200 per ₹1 lakh to the disputed amount. Before the Tribunal, the Revenue sought restoration of the entire addition. The ITAT Surat observed that the Assessing Officer had failed to establish through corroborative evidence that the transactions reflected in the mobile images had actually been executed by the assessee. It further emphasised that even where cash-transfer transactions were attributable to an Angadia firm, the entire cash amount could not automatically be taxed as the firm’s income. Since the assessee did not press its challenge to the small addition retained by the CIT(A), the Tribunal left the ₹46,436 addition undisturbed while dismissing the Revenue’s appeal against the substantial relief granted by the CIT(A). 6. AY 2020-21 – ₹77.07 Lakh Seized Cash and ₹17.29 Crore Customer Transactions: For Assessment Year 2020-21, the Assessing Officer made an addition of ₹77,07,280 under Section 68 concerning cash seized from different premises of the assessee. The assessee explained that the cash included ₹21,44,960 recorded in the Surat head-office cash book, ₹9,83,480 supported by the Lehripura branch cash book and ₹26,64,100 belonging to customers, apart from amounts already disclosed as income. The Tribunal found that ₹19,14,740 had already been offered to tax and upheld the CIT(A)’s deletion of the corresponding addition. Regarding cash found at the Surat and Lehripura (Baroda) premises, the Tribunal directed the Assessing Officer to verify the books and relevant evidence in accordance with CIT Vs. Patel Natverlal Chinubhai & Co. and pass an appropriate order within six months of receiving the Tribunal’s order. A separate addition of ₹17,29,16,910 arose from entries in Annexure A-2, which the Assessing Officer treated as undisclosed income. The assessee explained that these entries represented customer cash bookings and inter-branch cash transfers undertaken in the ordinary course of Angadia business. The CIT(A) accepted that the gross cash movements did not constitute the firm’s income and restricted the addition to estimated commission of approximately ₹3.46 lakh at ₹200 per ₹1 lakh. The ITAT Surat upheld the principle that customer funds merely handled or transferred by an Angadia firm cannot be taxed as its own income. Applying consistency with its findings for AY 2019-20, the Tribunal accepted the assessee’s commission rate of ₹100 per ₹1 lakh, allowed the assessee’s ground concerning the commission estimate and dismissed the Revenue’s corresponding challenge. 7. Final Decision – All Revenue Appeals Dismissed; Assessee Appeals Partly Allowed: The ITAT Surat, while deciding the consolidated appeals in ACIT Vs. M/s Patel Madhavlal Maganlal & Co., concluded that the Revenue had failed to establish a sufficient evidentiary foundation for several substantial additions. The Tribunal affirmed the deletion of ₹89,83,700 relating to explained cash seized in Mumbai, upheld the deletion of ₹1,84,73,479 towards estimated salary, rent, stationery and vehicle expenditure, rejected the Assessing Officer’s ₹29.74 crore estimated commission addition and granted further relief against the commission addition sustained by the CIT(A). It also refused to treat ₹2.32 crore reflected in mobile-phone images as taxable income merely on the strength of those images, while leaving the unpressed addition of ₹46,436 undisturbed. For AY 2020-21, the Tribunal directed verification of the disputed seized cash and rejected the treatment of approximately ₹17.29 crore in customer cash movements as the assessee’s income. The central finding was that an Angadia firm acts as a carrier or intermediary for customers’ funds, and the gross amount handled in such transactions does not, merely by reason of handling, become its taxable income. The Tribunal further held that additions based on unsupported estimates, assumptions, extrapolation or uncorroborated material cannot be sustained without examining the relevant books and evidence. In the final result, all appeals filed by the Revenue were dismissed, while the assessee’s appeals were partly allowed, including relief for statistical purposes. Cases Discussed 1. CIT Vs. Mauli Kumar Shah (Gujarat High Court); (2008) 307 ITR 137: The assessee cited this judgment while opposing additions based on assumptions and presumptions. The decision was included among the authorities relied upon to support the argument that additions must have a proper evidentiary foundation. The Tribunal’s order records the citation but does not separately analyse its ratio. 2. CIT Vs. Patel Natverlal Chinubhai & Co. (Gujarat High Court); (2013) 40 taxmann.com 304: The Tribunal expressly relied upon this decision involving an Angadia business. The Gujarat High Court had held that where cash seized during search was supported by the assessee’s cash book and shown as belonging to a particular branch, the amount could not be treated as unexplained money and added to taxable income. Applying this principle, the Tribunal upheld the deletion of ₹89,83,700 for AY 2018-19. It also directed the Assessing Officer to follow this precedent while verifying the seized cash for AY 2020-21. 3. Savaliya Buildcon Vs. DCIT (ITAT Ahmedabad); ITA No. 401/Ahd/2014: The assessee relied upon this decision while challenging estimated additions towards salary, rent, stationery and vehicle expenses. The citation appears in the assessee’s submissions. The Tribunal did not separately discuss the facts or ratio of this decision. 4. DCIT Vs. Hitesh M. Bagdai (ITAT Rajkot); IT(SS) No. 3/Rjt/2013: This decision was cited by the assessee in support of its challenge to additions made without adequate corroborative material. The Tribunal recorded the authority among the assessee’s cited cases but did not independently examine its reasoning. 5. ITO Vs. Deepak Vithaldas Suchak (ITAT Surat); ITA No. 361/Srt/2018; 30/07/2021: The Tribunal considered this decision concerning the taxation of commission income from a cheque-discounting business. In that case, the Assessing Officer had added entire bank deposits, whereas the appellate proceedings focused on determining a reasonable commission rate. The Tribunal noted that commission had ultimately been estimated at ₹75 per ₹1 lakh. Referring to this approach, the Tribunal in the present case accepted the Angadia firm’s disclosed commission rate of ₹100 per ₹1 lakh as reasonable and rejected unsupported higher estimates. 6. Pradeep Kumar Sharma Vs. DCIT; (2021) 132 taxmann.com 41: The Tribunal expressly discussed this decision concerning additions based on material seized during search proceedings. It noted that an addition could not be sustained where the material relied upon did not establish the assessee’s connection with the alleged transactions and adequate incriminating evidence was absent. The decision supported the Tribunal’s rejection of additions founded on insufficiently corroborated seized material. 7. Pr. CIT Vs. Anand Kumar Jain HUF (Delhi High Court); Decision dated 12/02/2021:The assessee cited this decision while contesting estimated additions and emphasising the requirement for supporting evidence. The Tribunal recorded the citation in the assessee’s submissions without separately examining its ratio. The appeal-number particulars reproduced in the supplied order require independent verification.
