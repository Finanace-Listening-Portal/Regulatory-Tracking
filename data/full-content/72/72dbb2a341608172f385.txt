ETBFSI Research & Debasmita Ghosh ETBFSI Published On Oct 31, 2026 at 02:55 AM IST var news_msid = 135378140; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Equitas Small Finance Bank’s next managing director and chief executive will have to navigate the competing demands of sustaining rapid loan growth, recovering net interest margins and consolidating asset quality improvements as the lender pursues a return on assets (RoA) target of 1.5%. The leadership transition comes at a time when the bank is showing signs of an operational recovery, with gross advances growing 30% year on year, deposits rising 19% and gross non-performing assets declining to 2.29% in the September quarter.The immediate challenge will be to maintain business momentum while addressing pressure on profitability and emerging risks in select lending segments. The bank has initiated the succession process and expects to finalise a candidate within a month and complete the selection process in three to four months, with the recommended candidate subsequently requiring Reserve Bank of India approval. The board will evaluate both internal and external candidates. The leadership change follows the outgoing MD and CEO's resignation over a perceived conflict of interest arising from a key family member's involvement in running a non-banking financial company, an issue that investors had repeatedly flagged over the past three years. These challenges and the bank's operating performance were highlighted by Elara Capital in its assessment of the lender's second-quarter results. Advt Growth and funding pressuresSustaining loan growth while strengthening the deposit franchise will be a key priority for the incoming chief executive. Gross advances grew 30% year on year, driven by housing finance, vehicle finance, gold loans and retail businesses. The analyst report put advances growth at 28.6%. Deposits increased 19% year on year and 7% sequentially, with retail deposits accounting for 68% of the total. The current account and savings account (CASA) ratio improved to 26%.The bank's ability to fund this expansion without further pressure on margins will be a key challenge. The cost of funds increased seven basis points sequentially, reflecting the full-quarter impact of deposit rate revisions in the previous quarter. Management expects a further 8-10 basis points increase over the next two quarters, with potential repo rate hikes adding to the pressure.Net interest margin (NIM), the difference between the yield earned on loans and the cost of deposits, declined 55 basis points sequentially to 6.69%. This was due to excess liquidity at the bank level, arising partly from FCNR(B) deposits that it has been unable to deploy, and higher funding costs. The bank mobilised $110 million through these deposits, of which 50% have a five-year tenor, 30% a four-year tenor and 20% a three-year tenor. Management expects margins to recover by 18-20 basis points by the fourth quarter of FY27 as surplus liquidity is deployed, with medium-term NIM expected at around 7%. Advt Asset quality remains a key monitorableThe incoming CEO will also have to sustain the improvement in asset quality while managing emerging stress in vehicle finance and risks arising from drought conditions in Maharashtra and Karnataka. Gross non-performing assets declined 13 basis points sequentially to 2.29%, while net NPAs fell one basis point to 0.7%. Credit costs declined to 0.94% in the September quarter from 1.37% in the preceding quarter. Excluding the income from the asset reconstruction company transaction, credit costs stood at 1.12%.Microfinance collections have improved, with collection efficiency at 99.6%. Gross slippages in the microfinance portfolio declined to 2.57% from 3.09% in the preceding quarter. However, the bank continues to monitor multiple borrowing among microfinance customers. Exposure to borrowers with loans from more than three lenders stood at Rs 82 crore, against which the bank has made 50% provisions. About 80% of the bank's customers are new-to-credit, making underwriting standards particularly important as it expands lending. Advt The bank sees substantial growth opportunities in small business lending (SBL) and affordable housing, with management estimating unmet credit demand of Rs 25 trillion and Rs 45 trillion, respectively. Expanding in these segments while maintaining asset quality will be another challenge for the incoming chief executive.Vehicle finance witnessed a sequential increase in gross NPAs, which management attributed partly to cyclical trends. The bank is also reducing its exposure to the heavy commercial vehicle segment amid the West Asia crisis. Drought-related risks in Maharashtra and Karnataka remain another monitorable, although exposure to affected areas accounts for only 2.9% of total advances. Management said collections in Karnataka improved in October compared with September, while collections in the affected districts of Maharashtra continue to be monitored. Advt Profitability recovery in focusThe leadership transition will take place as Equitas seeks to improve profitability through margin recovery and lower credit costs. The bank has raised its FY27 RoA guidance to 1.35% from 1.2% earlier, while retaining a long-term target of 1.5%.The bank's ability to achieve these targets will depend on sustaining business growth without weakening underwriting standards, improving its liability franchise and managing funding costs. The succession process is therefore a near-term monitorable, particularly given the need for a smooth transition and the possibility of dependence on an external candidate.
