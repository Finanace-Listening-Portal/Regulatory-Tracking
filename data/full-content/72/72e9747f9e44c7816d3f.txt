The textbook is designed to assist readers in understanding fundamental legal principles, identifying important statutory provisions and applying them to examination questions. Its chapter-wise arrangement makes it useful both for systematic study and for revising individual legal subjects. Students should, however, verify the latest statutory amendments, examination syllabus and applicable legal developments against official sources before relying on the material for examinations or professional purposes. Chapter-Wise Coverage of the Textbook Lesson 1: Sources of Law (Pages 22–27) The opening lesson introduces the foundations of the Indian legal system and explains the principal sources from which legal rules derive their authority. It discusses customs, judicial precedents, legislation and personal laws, together with secondary sources such as justice, equity and good conscience. The lesson also examines important jurisprudential theories associated with Bentham, Austin, Roscoe Pound and Kelsen. Particular attention is given to judicial precedents, the distinction between ratio decidendi and obiter dicta, and the binding effect of Supreme Court decisions under Article 141 of the Constitution. Lesson 2: Constitution of India (Pages 28–40) This lesson covers the structure and principal features of the Indian Constitution, including the Preamble, Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles of State Policy and Fundamental Duties. It discusses Articles 14, 19, 21, 32 and 226, constitutional remedies, the five writs, legislative powers, judicial review and delegated legislation. Important constitutional decisions are used to explain equality before law, personal liberty, constitutional interpretation and the relationship between the State and citizens. Lesson 3: Interpretation of Statutes (Pages 41–58) The chapter explains how courts interpret statutory language and resolve ambiguities in legislation. It covers the literal rule, golden rule, mischief rule and harmonious construction, along with principles such as ejusdem generis, noscitur a sociis and expressio unius est exclusio alterius. Separate discussions address internal and external aids to interpretation, statutory presumptions, retrospective operation, mandatory and directory provisions, and the interpretation of fiscal and penal statutes. The chapter also examines important provisions of the General Clauses Act, 1897, including Sections 6, 8, 9, 13, 14 and 21. Lesson 4: Administrative Law (Pages 59–71) This lesson examines the legal principles governing administrative authorities and the exercise of public powers. It discusses the rule of law, administrative discretion, judicial review, abuse of discretion and the principles of natural justice. The material explains the rule against bias, the right to a fair hearing, speaking orders, exceptions to natural justice and the liability of government authorities and public corporations. Lesson 5: Law of Torts (Pages 72–88) The chapter introduces civil liability arising from wrongful acts and omissions. It covers legal injury, damages, strict liability, absolute liability and vicarious liability, together with important distinctions such as damnum sine injuria and injuria sine damno. Other topics include negligence, defamation, assault, battery, false imprisonment, malicious prosecution and remedies available under tort law. Lesson 6: Law Relating to Civil Procedure (Pages 89–102) This lesson explains important provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, and the procedural framework governing civil litigation. It covers jurisdiction, institution of suits, pleadings, written statements, summons, discovery, judgments, decrees and execution. The chapter also examines res judicata, stay of suits, appeals, review, revision, temporary injunctions, summary suits and selected aspects of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015. Lesson 7: Laws Relating to Crime and Its Procedure (Pages 103–124) This chapter examines criminal law and procedure with reference to the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, and the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023. It discusses the elements and stages of crime, mens rea, actus reus, punishments, arrest, warrants, bail and criminal court procedures. Substantive offences covered include theft, robbery, extortion, cheating, criminal breach of trust, forgery and defamation. The chapter also addresses general exceptions and the right of private defence. Lesson 8: Law Relating to Evidence (Pages 125–142) This lesson explains the principles of evidence under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023. Important topics include relevancy and admissibility of evidence, admissions, confessions, dying declarations, expert opinions and privileged communications. It also discusses oral and documentary evidence, primary and secondary evidence, electronic records, statutory presumptions and estoppel. Special attention is given to the admissibility of electronic evidence under Section 63. Lesson 9: Law Relating to Limitation (Pages 143–153) The chapter covers the Limitation Act, 1963, and the legal consequences of failing to initiate proceedings within prescribed time limits. It discusses the bar of limitation, condonation of delay, legal disability, continuous running of time and computation or exclusion of limitation periods. The material also contains tables of important limitation periods, examination questions and problem-based illustrations. Lesson 10: Alternative Dispute Resolution (Pages 154–166) This lesson introduces arbitration, conciliation and mediation as alternatives to conventional litigation. It examines the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, including arbitration agreements, appointment of arbitrators, interim measures, arbitral jurisdiction, awards, challenges and enforcement. The chapter also covers the Mediation Act, 2023, and compares different dispute resolution mechanisms. Lesson 11: Right to Information (Pages 167–176) The chapter examines the Right to Information Act, 2005, and its role in promoting transparency and accountability in public administration. It explains the obligations of public authorities, duties of Public Information Officers, application procedures, disclosure exemptions and third-party information. It also covers Information Commissions, appeals, penalties and statutory time limits. Lesson 12: Information Technology and Cyber Law (Pages 177–189) This lesson discusses the Information Technology Act, 2000, and the legal recognition of electronic records, digital signatures and electronic contracts. It covers electronic governance, Certifying Authorities, cyber offences, intermediary liability and statutory penalties. The chapter also introduces the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and discusses issues concerning electronic information and digital transactions. Lesson 13: Law of Contracts (Pages 190–205) The chapter provides a detailed introduction to the Indian Contract Act, 1872. It discusses offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity to contract, free consent, lawful objects, void agreements and performance of contracts. Further topics include breach and remedies, indemnity, guarantee, bailment, pledge, agency and electronic contracts. Model questions and practical problems are included to help readers understand the application of contractual principles. Lesson 14: Law Relating to Negotiable Instruments (Pages 206–219) The concluding chapter examines the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881. It covers promissory notes, bills of exchange, cheques, endorsement, negotiation, holder in due course, crossing of cheques and banker responsibilities. Particular attention is given to cheque dishonour under Section 138, company liability under Section 141, interim compensation under Section 143A and appellate deposits under Section 148. The lesson also introduces NEFT and RTGS and includes revision questions and statutory reference tables. Its combination of statutory explanations, judicial principles, illustrations, comparison tables and practice questions makes it a useful supplementary study resource.
