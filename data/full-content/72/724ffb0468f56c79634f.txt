Vs Commissioner of Central Excise and Service Tax (CESTAT Chandigarh) Summary: The Chandigarh Bench of the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT) held that Business Exhibition Services performed entirely outside India were not liable to service tax under the reverse charge mechanism in terms of Rule 3(ii) of the Taxation of Services (Provided from Outside India and Received in India) Rules, 2006. The Tribunal allowed the appeal filed by Sarup Industries Ltd., a manufacturer and exporter of footwear, challenging the order dated 29 January 2014 passed by the Commissioner (Appeals), which had upheld a service tax demand of Rs.49,307, interest of Rs.55,512, a penalty of Rs.1,000 under Section 77 and an equal penalty of Rs.49,307 under Section 78 of the Finance Act, 1994. During audit, the Department noticed that the appellant had paid Rs.5,72,027 in foreign currency to various overseas persons during FY 2005-06 and FY 2006-07 towards advertisement, publicity, exhibition and display services. A show cause notice dated 22 March 2011 proposed recovery of service tax amounting to Rs.66,566 under the reverse charge mechanism for the period from November 2005 to January 2007, together with interest and penalties. The original authority dropped the demand of Rs.17,258 relating to the period prior to 18 April 2006, when Section 66A of the Finance Act came into effect, but confirmed the demand of Rs.49,307 pertaining to Business Exhibition Services. The Commissioner (Appeals) subsequently upheld the remaining demand and penalties. Before the Tribunal, the appellant contended that the Commissioner (Appeals) had erroneously proceeded on the assumption that the dispute concerned both Business Exhibition Services and Commission Agent Services, even though the demand concerning commission agents had already been dropped by the original authority. The appellant submitted that Business Exhibition Services were covered under Rule 3(ii) of the applicable rules, which governed services involving physical performance. Such services received from overseas providers could attract service tax in India only when performed wholly or partly in India. Since the exhibitions were conducted entirely outside India, the appellant argued that the statutory condition for levy of service tax was not satisfied. Reliance was placed upon the decisions in K.G. Denim Ltd. Vs CST, Salem and Intas Pharmaceuticals Limited Vs CST, Ahmedabad. The Tribunal accepted the appellant’s contention and observed that the Commissioner (Appeals) had incorrectly presumed that Commission Agent Services continued to form part of the dispute. It held that Business Exhibition Services fell within Rule 3(ii), which required physical performance of the relevant services wholly or partly in India for attracting service tax under the applicable import of services provisions. Since the overseas organisers had performed the entire exhibition and trade fair services outside India, the payments made by the appellant were not chargeable to service tax. Following the cited coordinate Bench decisions, CESTAT set aside the impugned order and allowed the appeal. The ruling concerns the territorial performance requirements applicable to Business Exhibition Services under the erstwhile service tax regime. Cases Discussed: K.G. Denim Ltd. Vs CST, Salem (CESTAT Chennai); 2015 (37) STR 616 (Tri.-Chennai). – Followed for the principle that payments made to overseas organisers for business exhibitions conducted entirely outside India are not liable to service tax under the reverse charge mechanism. Intas Pharmaceuticals Limited Vs CST, Ahmedabad (CESTAT Ahmedabad); 2009 (16) STR 748 (Tri.-Ahmd.). – Followed regarding non-taxability of services involving physical performance where the entire service is performed outside India under Rule 3(ii) of the Taxation of Services (Provided from Outside India and Received in India) Rules, 2006. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CESTAT CHANDIGARH The present appeal is directed against impugned order dated 29.01.2014 passed by the Commissioner (Appeals), Central Excise & Customs, Chandigarh, whereby the learned Commissioner (Appeals) has modified the order passed by the original authority and has confirmed the demand of service tax to the extent of Rs.49,307/- along with interest amounting to Rs.55,512/- and also imposed a penalty of Rs.1000/- under Section 77 and an equal penalty of Rs.49,307/- under Section 78 of the Finance Act, 1994. 2. Briefly stated facts of the present case are that the appellant is engaged in the manufacture and export of footwear. During the audit of the records of the appellant, it was noticed that during financial year 2005-06 & 2006-07, the appellant had paid an amount of Rs.5,72,027/- in foreign currency to various foreign based persons for service of advertisement/publicity and exhibition/display in relation to their activities in India. As per the department, the services provided by the foreign based persons were covered under the category of Business Auxiliary Service as well as Business Exhibition Service, as defined under Sections 65(19) & 65(19)(a) of the Finance Act, 1994 respectively. A show cause notice dated 22.03.2011 was issued to the appellant demanding service tax amounting to Rs.66,566/- under reverse charge mechanism for the period November 2005 to January 2007, along with interest and penalties. The appellant filed reply to the show cause notice and after following the due process, the original authority dropped the demand of service tax of Rs.17,258/- for the period prior to 18.04.2006 but confirmed the demand of service tax of Rs.49,307/- pertaining to ‘Business Exhibition Service’, along with interest and penalties. Aggrieved by the said order, the appellant filed appeal before the Commissioner (Appeals), who vide the impugned order, has upheld the order of the original authority in so far as it relates to demand of service tax and imposition of penalties under Sections 77 & 78 of the Act. Hence, the present appeal. 3. Heard both the parties and perused the material on record. 4. The learned Counsel for the appellant submits that the impugned order is not sustainable in law and is liable to be set aside as the same has been passed without properly appreciating the facts and the law. He further submits that the Commissioner (Appeals) has failed to appreciate that principal issue involved in the present case was not taxability of services received from commission agents located outside India, but was taxability of ‘Business Exhibition Service’ received outside India; therefore, the impugned order is based on erroneous assumption of facts and is liable to be set aside. He further submits that the Commissioner (Appeals) has failed to appreciate that demand pertaining to commission agents had already been dropped by the original authority as being prior to introduction of Section 66A of the Finance Act w.e.f. 18.04.2006 and the only demand remaining for the period April 2006 to January 2007 pertained to ‘Business Exhibition Service’ and not to foreign based commission agents. He further submits that as per the Circular dated 19.04.2006 and the provisions relating to Taxation of Services (Provided from outside India and Received in India) Rules, 2006, Business Exhibition Service falls under Rule 3(ii) of the aforesaid Rules, which involves physical performance and it can only be taxed under service tax if such services are partly or wholly performed in India. He also submits that impugned services relating to ‘Business Exhibition Services’ are not performed in India and therefore, are not liable to service tax as per above rule also. In support of his submissions, he relies on the following decisions of the Tribunal: a) K. G. Denim Ltd vs. CST, Salem – 2015 (37) STR 616 (Tri. Chennai) b) Intas Pharmaceuticals Limited vs. CST, Ahmedabad – 2009 (16) STR 748 (Tri. Ahmd.) He submits that the issue is squarely covered by the above decisions (supra) of the Tribunal. 5. On the other hand, the learned Authorized Representative for the Revenue reiterates the findings of the impugned order. 6. After considering the submissions made by both the parties and perusal of the material on record, we find that in the impugned order, the learned Commissioner (Appeals) has wrongly presumed that the service involved in the present case relates to ‘Business Exhibition Service’ as well as ‘Commission Agent Service’. In fact, the demand on ‘Commission Agent Service’ has already been dropped by the original authority and the only issue involved in the present case is related to ‘Business Exhibition Service’. We also find that ‘Business Exhibition Service’ falls under Rule 3(ii) of Taxation of Services (Provided from outside India and Received in India) Rules, 2006 and the said rule specifies the taxable services, which involve physical performance, and the same are treated as services provided from outside India and received in India only if such services are partly or wholly performed in India; whereas, in the present case, it is a fact that ‘Business Exhibition Services’ were performed outside India and therefore, the same are not liable to service tax in India. Further, we find that the decisions relied upon by the appellant cited supra, are clearly covering the present case because no service was performed by the overseas organizers of trade fair/exhibition in India and the entire service was performed outside India, the payment made by the appellant to them was not chargeable to service tax. 7. By following the ratios of the decisions cited above, we are of the considered view that the impugned order is not sustainable in law, therefore, we set aside the same and allow the appeal of the appellant.
