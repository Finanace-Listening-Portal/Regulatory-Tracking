(Calcutta High Court) Summary: The Calcutta High Court, in Shankar Prasad Gupta Vs State of West Bengal & Ors., quashed an ex parte GST adjudication order dated 6 March 2024 and the consequential recovery notice on the ground that the petitioner had been denied the mandatory opportunity of personal hearing under Section 75(4) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. Justice Smita Das De held that the statutory provision requires an opportunity of hearing where a written request is received from the person chargeable with tax or penalty, or where an adverse decision is contemplated against such person. The Court also considered the failure to provide separate intimation of the show cause notice and adjudication order uploaded under the GST portal’s “Additional Notice and Orders” tab. The petitioner challenged the adjudication order passed under Section 73 of the West Bengal Goods and Services Tax Act and the CGST Act, 2017. According to the petitioner, a show cause notice in Form GST DRC-01 dated 1 November 2023 demanding tax, interest and penalty had been uploaded under the “Additional Notice and Orders” tab of the GST portal. The petitioner submitted that, because of the manner in which the notice was uploaded, he could not file a reply. The adjudicating authority subsequently passed an ex parte order dated 6 March 2024, which was also uploaded under the same tab. The petitioner claimed that he became aware of the adjudication order only after receiving a recovery notice from the bank on 2 April 2026. The petitioner contended that the proceedings violated the principles of natural justice and the procedure prescribed under the GST legislation. The State respondents opposed the petition, submitting that the petitioner had not filed a reply or requested an opportunity of personal hearing. They further contended that no specific statutory rule required such an opportunity before adjudication. The petitioner relied upon the Division Bench decision in Goutam Bhowmik Vs State of West Bengal, (2024) 158 taxmann.com 399 (Calcutta), which recognised the mandatory nature of personal hearing under Section 75(4) where an adverse GST decision is contemplated. Examining Section 75(4) of the CGST Act, 2017, the High Court observed that an opportunity of hearing must be granted when either of the statutory conditions is satisfied. The Court found that the petitioner had been denied a personal hearing, as was evident from the summary of the show cause notice dated 1 November 2023. It further observed that the show cause notice and adjudication order had been uploaded only under the “Additional Notice and Orders” tab without separate intimation, thereby preventing the petitioner from submitting a reply. On these facts, the Court concluded that the petitioner had established a prima facie case warranting interference. Accordingly, the High Court quashed and set aside the adjudication order dated 6 March 2024 and the consequential recovery notice. It directed the petitioner to submit a comprehensive reply to the show cause notice dated 1 November 2023 within three weeks from communication of the Court’s order. Respondent No. 3 was directed to reconsider the matter after examining the reply, afford an opportunity of personal hearing and pass a fresh reasoned adjudication order in accordance with law within 12 weeks. The fresh decision must be communicated to the petitioner within one week thereafter. The writ petition was disposed of without adjudicating the underlying tax demand on its merits. Cases Discussed Goutam Bhowmik Vs State of West Bengal & Ors. (Calcutta High Court); MAT No. 205 of 2023 arising from WPA No. 1866 of 2023; 09/01/2024; (2024) 158 taxmann.com 399 (Calcutta) — The Division Bench held that Section 75(4) requires an opportunity of personal hearing where an adverse decision is contemplated, even without a separate request from the taxpayer. The present Court reproduced paragraphs 7 to 9 and relied upon the statutory interpretation. Appellant Was Represented by: Mr. Himangshu Kumar Ray. Mr. Saptak Sanyal. Mr. Subhasis Podder. Mr. Sushant Bagaria. Ms. Shiwani Shaw. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CALCUTTA HIGH COURT 1. Affidavit of service filed in Court today be kept with the record. 2. The present writ petition has been filed challenging inter alia, the legality and validity of the ex-parte adjudication order dated 06.03.2024 passed by the respondent No. 3 under Section 73 of the West Bengal Goods and Services Tax Act (hereinafter referred to as the said ‘WBGST Act’) and the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017(hereinafter referred to as the said ‘CGST Act’). 3. Learned counsel for the petitioner submits as follows; i. That a show cause notice in Form DRC 01 dated 01.11.2023 has been issued to the petitioner demanding tax, interest and penalty. ii. The said notice has been uploaded on the GST portal under the tab ‘Additional Notice and Orders’. Due to this, the petitioner could not submit reply. iii. The adjudicating authority passed an ex parte order of adjudication dated 06.03.2024 which was accordingly uploaded in the ‘Additional Notices and Orders’ tab. iv. The petitioner came to know about the passing of the order of adjudication dated 06.03.2024 only after receiving a recovery notice from Bank on 02.04.2026. v. That the entire proceedings and the impugned order are contrary to law and have been passed in violations of principles of natural justice and without due compliance with the procedure prescribed under the said Act. 4. Learned counsel appearing for the State respondents vehemently opposes the submissions made by the petitioner and submits that the petitioner did not file any reply. The petitioner did not make any request for an opportunity of personal hearing, thus the GST Department proceeded on the basis of the reply filed by the petitioner. 5. It is further submitted that there is no specific rule prescribed under the statute to provide any opportunity of hearing to the petitioner prior to the passing of an order of adjudication. 6. In this context the petitioner places reliance upon paragraph nos. 7 to 9 of a Division Bench Judgment in Goutam Bhowmik -v.- State of West Bengal reported in (2024) 158 taxmann. Com 399 (Calcutta), which are reproduced below: “7. From bare perusal of the show cause notice under Section 73 of the WBGST/CGST Act, 2017, it is evident that no opportunity of hearing was afforded by the proper officer before passing the impugned assessment order for the Financial Year 2018-19 i.e., from April 2018 to March 2019. Although in the show cause notice dated 15.01.2021 under Section 73 of the Act it was specifically mentioned by the proper officer addressing the petitioner that “You may appear before the undersigned for personal hearing either in person or through authorised representative for representing your case on the date, time and venue, if mentioned in table below” but in the table neither dated and time nor venue for personal hearing was mentioned. 8. Section 75(4) of the WBGST/CGST Act, 2017 specifically provides as under:- “An opportunity of hearing shall be granted where a request is received in writing from the person chargeable with tax or penalty, or where any adverse decision is contemplated against such person.” 9. Thus, as per provisions of sub-section 4 of Section 75 of the WBGST/CGST Act, 2017, when the proper officer contemplated a decision against the petitioner/assessee, then it was mandatory for him to afford an opportunity of hearing. From the perusal of the show cause notice dated 15.01.2021, it is evident that the proper officer has declined to afford an opportunity of hearing to the petitioner inasmuch as it has not communicated any date, time and venue of hearing.” 7. From the plain reading of Section 75(4) of the CGST Act, 2017 it clearly envisages that an opportunity of hearing shall be granted where a request is received in writing from the person chargeable with tax or penalty, or where any adverse decision is contemplated against such person. 8. In the present case, the petitioner was denied of personal hearing which is evident from the summary of show cause notice dated 01.11.2023. 9. Having heard the parties and upon perusing the records made available this Court observes as follows; i. The petitioner has been able to make out a prima facie case. ii. The fact that show cause notice and adjudication order have been uploaded only under the tab ‘Additional Notice and Orders’ and no separate intimation has been given therefore, the petitioner could not submit the reply. 10. In view of the above this Court directs as follows: a. The adjudication order dated 06.03.2024, consequential recovery notice are hereby quashed and set aside. b. The petitioner is directed to file a comprehensive reply before the authority concerned to the show cause notice dated 1.11.2023 within a period of three weeks from the date of communication of this order. c. Respondent no. 3 is directed to revisit the issue by considering the reply filed by the petitioner. d. Respondent no. 3 shall afford an opportunity of personal hearing to the petitioner and pass a fresh reasoned adjudication order in accordance with law within 12 weeks from date. Such decision shall be communicated to the petitioner within a week thereafter. 11. With the above observations and directions this writ petition stands disposed of. 12. Since no affidavit in opposition has been called for, the allegations contained in the writ petition are deemed to have been denied and not admitted. 13. Urgent Photostat certified copy of this order be supplied to the parties, if applied for, as early as possible.
