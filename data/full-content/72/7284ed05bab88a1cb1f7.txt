Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Maruti Suzuki India Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Delhi Bench ‘I’, comprising Shri S. Rifaur Rahman, Accountant Member, and Shri Vimal Kumar, Judicial Member, decided a batch of four connected appeals involving Maruti Suzuki India Limited and the Income Tax Department for Assessment Years 2011-12, 2012-13 and 2013-14. The consolidated order dated 8 October 2025 examined several significant issues relating to disallowance of expenditure under Section 14A read with Rule 8D, weighted deduction for research and development expenditure under Section 35(2AB), provisional liabilities arising from foreseen price increases, expenses incurred for sharing resources with group companies, corporate social responsibility expenditure, taxation of mutual fund transactions, withholding tax obligations on purchases from Suzuki Motor Corporation, Japan, and transfer pricing adjustments relating to royalty payments. The Tribunal also considered disputes concerning customs and excise duties, research and development cess, sales tax exemptions, tax deducted at source, foreign tax credit and other consequential matters. Several issues had arisen repeatedly in earlier assessment years and were examined in light of previous Tribunal orders, decisions of the Delhi High Court and applicable Supreme Court judgments. One of the principal disputes concerned disallowance under Section 14A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, read with Rule 8D of the Income-tax Rules, 1962. For Assessment Year 2011-12, Maruti Suzuki had earned substantial exempt dividend income and voluntarily disallowed ₹2,09,65,159 towards related salary and administrative expenses. However, the Assessing Officer computed an additional disallowance of ₹19,20,34,841 by applying Rule 8D. The assessee argued that its investments were financed through sufficient interest-free surplus funds and that the Assessing Officer had not demonstrated any direct connection between borrowed funds and investments yielding exempt income. The Tribunal observed that the Assessing Officer had recorded satisfaction regarding the inadequacy of the assessee’s computation, but held that interest expenditure could not be disallowed where borrowed funds were used for business purposes and investments were made from surplus funds. Regarding administrative expenditure, it directed the Assessing Officer to consider only investments that had actually yielded dividend income during the relevant year. The Tribunal followed the principles laid down in PCIT v. Caraf Builders & Constructions (P.) Ltd. and ACIT v. Vireet Investment (P.) Ltd., granting relief to the assessee on the disputed Section 14A adjustment. Another important issue related to weighted deduction under Section 35(2AB) for expenditure incurred at Maruti Suzuki’s research and development facility at Rohtak. The Assessing Officer had initially disallowed the deduction on the ground that the facility had received recognition from the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research (DSIR) with effect from 1 April 2013, whereas the expenditure related to earlier assessment years. The assessee relied upon the Delhi High Court’s judgment in Maruti Suzuki India Ltd. v. Union of India (2017) 397 ITR 728, which held that the date of recognition or the cut-off date mentioned in the DSIR certificate was not decisive for determining entitlement to the deduction. Following that judgment, the Assessing Officer had already granted substantial relief, leaving a disputed amount of ₹5 lakh arising from short approval in Form 3CL. The Tribunal held that this remaining amount formed part of the same research expenditure claim already considered by the High Court and allowed the deduction. The Tribunal also examined the disallowance of ₹36.27 crore relating to provisions for foreseen price increases payable to suppliers of original equipment components. Following earlier decisions in the assessee’s own cases, it allowed the relevant grounds. Similarly, the Tribunal granted relief concerning expenses attributed to sharing common resources with group and subsidiary companies, taking into account the earlier appellate and miscellaneous application orders. The appeals also raised disputes regarding corporate social responsibility expenditure and the tax treatment of gains arising from investments in mutual funds and shares. The Assessing Officer had disallowed ₹15.20 crore of CSR expenditure for Assessment Year 2011-12, questioning its business purpose and alternatively treating the expenditure as capital in nature. The assessee maintained that the expenditure was incurred for business purposes and relied upon favourable decisions in earlier assessment years. The Tribunal considered the recurring nature of the issue and the earlier appellate findings while disposing of the relevant grounds. Another controversy concerned whether gains from mutual fund transactions should be assessed as business income or capital gains. The Assessing Officer had treated gains from mutual fund transactions as business income despite the assessee’s classification of the investments as capital assets. The dispute involved long-term capital loss after indexation, short-term capital gains, the treatment of investments in the books of account and the distinction between investment activity and systematic trading. The Tribunal examined these issues with reference to earlier decisions and the factual treatment of the transactions, granting relief in accordance with the findings recorded in the consolidated order. A substantial international taxation dispute concerned purchases made by Maruti Suzuki from Suzuki Motor Corporation, Japan, and the proposed disallowance under Section 40(a)(i) for alleged failure to deduct tax at source. The Revenue contended that Suzuki Motor Corporation had a permanent establishment in India under Article 5 of the India-Japan Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement because Japanese nominee directors allegedly exercised significant influence over the Indian company’s business affairs. The Assessing Officer also advanced arguments relating to fixed-place, dependent-agent and service permanent establishments and attributed profits to the alleged Indian operations of the Japanese company. Maruti Suzuki disputed these conclusions, contending that the directors’ participation in its management did not establish that Suzuki Motor Corporation carried on business through a permanent establishment in India. The assessee also relied upon the treaty provisions, including the non-discrimination clause under Article 24. The Tribunal examined the relationship between the two companies, the role of nominee directors, the permanent establishment allegations and the legal requirements for withholding tax. The order also dealt with a substantial transfer pricing adjustment relating to royalty paid to Suzuki Motor Corporation for technology and trademark rights. The Revenue had sought to separate royalty attributable to the Suzuki trademark from royalty attributable to technology, whereas the assessee maintained that the rights arose under an integrated licence arrangement and that the international transactions were appropriately benchmarked under the Transactional Net Margin Method. Following the applicable judicial precedents and earlier decisions in the assessee’s own cases, the Tribunal granted relief on these international taxation and transfer pricing disputes. In addition to the major issues, the Tribunal considered several other disputed deductions and adjustments, including customs duty and excise duty balances, duties paid under protest, research and development cess, sales tax exemption benefits, short credit for tax deducted at source and consequential interest calculations. The Tribunal examined the treatment of these items under the relevant provisions of the Income-tax Act and considered earlier appellate decisions concerning the same assessee. For Assessment Years 2012-13 and 2013-14, many of the disputed matters were substantially similar to those arising in Assessment Year 2011-12, and the Tribunal applied its findings consistently wherever the facts and legal issues were comparable. It also directed the Assessing Officer to verify and allow the eligible foreign tax credit claimed for Assessment Year 2013-14. The consolidated decision demonstrates the significance of consistency in adjudicating recurring tax disputes, the requirement to establish a factual and legal basis before making disallowances, and the relevance of earlier binding judicial findings in transfer pricing and international taxation matters. Ultimately, the Tribunal allowed all three appeals filed by Maruti Suzuki India Limited in the terms recorded in its order, while the Revenue’s cross-appeal was partly allowed. List of Cases Discussed: 1. Maruti Suzuki India Ltd. v. Union of India (2017) 397 ITR 728 (Delhi High Court): Relied upon in relation to deduction under Section 35(2AB) for the Rohtak R&D facility. The High Court had held that the date of recognition or approval was not decisive where the required recognition existed. 2. Maruti Suzuki India Ltd. v. DCIT, ITA No. 961/Del/2015, order dated 09.02.2023 (ITAT Delhi): Followed on several recurring issues, including Section 14A disallowance, foreseen price increase liabilities, sharing of resources, and other matters previously adjudicated for AY 2010-11. 3. Hero Cycles Ltd. v. CIT (2015) 379 ITR 347 (Supreme Court): Cited regarding the deductibility of business interest expenditure and the limits of Revenue’s interference in commercial decisions. 4. Godrej & Boyce Manufacturing Co. Ltd. v. DCIT (2017) 394 ITR 449 (Supreme Court): Cited in connection with Section 14A, Rule 8D and the requirement to examine expenditure attributable to exempt income. 5. CIT v. Reliance Industries Ltd. (2019) 410 ITR 466 (Supreme Court): Cited regarding the treatment of investments where the assessee possesses sufficient interest-free funds. 6. CIT v. Reliance Utilities and Power Ltd. (2009) 313 ITR 340 (Bombay High Court): Relied upon in submissions concerning the presumption that investments are made from available interest-free funds. 7. PCIT v. Caraf Builders & Constructions (P.) Ltd. (2019) 414 ITR 122 (Delhi High Court): Followed in determining that only investments actually yielding exempt income should be considered for computing the relevant Section 14A disallowance. 8. ACB India Ltd. v. ACIT (2015) 374 ITR 108 (Delhi High Court): Cited on the exclusion of investments not yielding exempt income from the Rule 8D computation. 9. ACIT v. Vireet Investment (P.) Ltd. (2017) 165 ITD 27 (Delhi Special Bench): Followed concerning the computation of disallowance under Rule 8D with reference to investments yielding exempt income. 10. CIT v. Taikisha Engineering India Ltd. (2015) 370 ITR 338 (Delhi High Court): Cited concerning the satisfaction requirement and the determination of expenditure relating to exempt income. 11. CIT v. Woodward Governor India (P.) Ltd. (2009) 312 ITR 254 (Supreme Court): Cited in support of the deductibility of accrued business liabilities and recognised accounting principles. 12. Rotork Controls India (P.) Ltd. v. CIT (2009) 314 ITR 62 (Supreme Court): Cited regarding recognition of provisions and accrued liabilities based on reliable estimation. 13. Bharat Earth Movers v. CIT (2000) 245 ITR 428 (Supreme Court): Cited for principles governing deductions for accrued liabilities even where the precise amount remains to be quantified. 14. United Commercial Bank v. CIT (1999) 240 ITR 355 (Supreme Court): Cited in the submissions on consistent accounting treatment and recognition of income or expenditure. 15. CIT v. Claris Lifesciences Ltd. (2010) 326 ITR 251 (Gujarat High Court): Cited concerning the approval and recognition requirements applicable to weighted deduction for in-house research expenditure. 16. CIT v. Sandan Vikas India Ltd. (2011) 335 ITR 117 (Delhi High Court): Cited in support of the assessee’s claim under Section 35(2AB) and the relevance of approval for its research facility. 17. Nagravision India (P.) Ltd. v. Secretary, DSIR (2024) 159 taxmann.com 558 (Delhi High Court): Cited regarding the DSIR approval framework for research and development expenditure. 18. CIT v. Dharampal Satyapal Sons (P.) Ltd., 50 DTR 287 (Delhi High Court): Followed in relation to deduction of customs duty, sales tax and excise duty amounts paid under protest. 19. East India Pharmaceutical Works Ltd. v. CIT (1997) 224 ITR 627 (Supreme Court): Cited in the discussion concerning business interest expenditure and the availability of funds. 20. CIT v. UTI Bank Ltd., 215 Taxman 8 (Gujarat High Court): Cited regarding the treatment of interest expenditure and the utilisation of interest-free funds.
