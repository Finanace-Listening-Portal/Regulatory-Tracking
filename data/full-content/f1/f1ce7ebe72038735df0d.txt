(Delhi High Court) PMLA-Attached Land May Be Substituted With Assets Covering the Revised Proceeds of Crime, Subject to Safeguards – Delhi High Court Summary: The Delhi High Court permitted the substitution of land attached under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA) with alternative assets whose verified value was sufficient to cover the revised quantum of alleged proceeds of crime. Justice Amit Mahajan took into account the Enforcement Directorate’s position that it had no serious objection to substitution if adequate safeguards were imposed. The Court required proof of clear title, disclosure of the acquisition funding, restrictions on alienation and an undertaking to hand over possession. It also preserved the ED’s rights and the merits of the ongoing trial. How the Attachment AroseThe CBI registered a case concerning alleged irregularities in the acquisition, release and licensing of land in Gurugram. The case invoked provisions of the Indian Penal Code and the Prevention of Corruption Act. On the strength of these predicate offences, the ED registered an Enforcement Case Information Report and initially quantified the alleged proceeds of crime at Rs. 1,813.74 crore. By Provisional Attachment Order No. 12 of 2024, dated 14 October 2024, the ED attached properties forming part of land covered by Licence No. 107/2010. On 1 April 2025, the Adjudicating Authority partly confirmed the attachment, covering 52.7609 acres valued at approximately Rs. 1,759.84 crore out of the 60.3009 acres originally attached. Commander Realtors appealed to the Appellate Tribunal and filed interlocutory applications seeking recomputation of the proceeds of crime and substitution of the attached properties. Reconciliation Reduced the Alleged Proceeds by Rs. 796.55 Crore Following the Appellate Tribunal’s directions of 27 January 2026, the ED reconciled the computation and revised the alleged proceeds of crime from Rs. 1,813.74 crore to Rs. 1,017.19 crore, a reduction of approximately Rs. 796.55 crore. The Tribunal then sought information on the properties proposed for release following the reduction and directed verification of the properties offered in substitution. The proposed replacement comprised land in Phagwara, Punjab, valued at Rs. 408 crore, and the developable portion of land in Sohna, Haryana, valued at Rs. 654.89 crore. The ED found the aggregate value of Rs. 1,062.89 crore, after excluding the undevelopable component, sufficient to cover the revised figure. The writ petition sought release of 22.231 acres following the reconciliation and substitution of the balance attached licensed land. The High Court’s operative directions addressed substitution of the balance land. Why the High Court Permitted Substitution Counsel for Commander Realtors submitted that the ED had no objection in principle and that the company was willing to comply with conditions imposed by the Court. The ED confirmed that it had no serious objection, provided the replacement properties were adequately secured. Taking account of that position and the limited relief sought, the Court permitted the balance attached licensed land to be substituted with the alternative properties. The permission was expressly made subject to conditions governing the title, possession and source of funds of the replacement assets. Safeguards Imposed by the Court The company was required to file an affidavit establishing clear and marketable title and undisputed ownership, supported by verifiable documents, together with a certificate confirming that the assets were free from encumbrances and third-party claims. It also had to disclose its relationship with the entities owning the proposed properties and give an undertaking concerning its right to offer those parcels. The replacement properties could neither be sold nor otherwise alienated during the proceedings. The original title deeds had to be deposited with the ED against acknowledgment, and a demarcated map or site plan had to be supplied. The company had to undertake to hand over possession to the ED, subject to its right to challenge the provisional attachment, and furnish an indemnity bond covering any loss or legal deficiency arising from the substitution. The Court also required protection of bona fide third-party rights, full disclosure of the source of funds used to acquire the replacement assets, and supporting financial records to establish that those assets were untainted. The company was required to continue cooperating with the investigation, produce documents and appear for inquiry when called upon. Author’s Comments Two facts explain why substitution was feasible here. The ED’s own reconciliation established the revised amount to be secured, and its valuation of the identified replacement parcels established that they could cover that amount. The title and possession conditions gave the ED practical control over the substitute properties, while the funding disclosure addressed whether those assets could themselves be traced to proceeds of crime. The decision is best read as a fact-specific exercise of writ jurisdiction, shaped by verified asset coverage, the ED’s qualified concurrence and safeguards tailored to the properties on offer. The Court allowed the petition on those terms and expressly preserved the ED’s rights. Questions concerning the legality of the source and legitimacy of the attached properties remained open, and the merits of the ongoing trial were unaffected. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF DELHI HIGH COURT 1. By the present petition, the petitioner seeks the following reliefs: “a) issue a writ of mandamus, or any other appropriate writ, order or direction, directing Respondent No. 2 to give effect to the reconciliation carried out by it and to forthwith release the land admeasuring 22.231 acres, which has been verified by the Directorate as releasable, consequent upon the revision of the proceeds of crime to Rs. 1,017,19,03,577/-, from the attachment effected vide Provisional Attachment Order No. 12 of 2024 dated 14.10.2024 and confirmed by the order dated 01.04.2025; b) issue a writ of mandamus, or any other appropriate writ, order or direction, directing that the balance attached licensed land under Provisional Attachment Order No. 12 of 2024 dated 14.10.2024 be substituted with the alternate assets offered by the Petitioner, namely the land situated at Villages Chaheru, Khajurla and Maheru, Tehsil Phagwara, District Kapurthala, Punjab, and the land situated at Villages Berka, Khaika and Sirsika, Tehsil Sohna, District Gurugram, Haryana, of an aggregate value of approximately Rs. 1,062.89 crore, subject to the Petitioner furnishing the undertakings set out in paragraph 12 of the reply of the Directorate dated 02.07.2026, and further directing that the said licensed land be de-attached and released in lieu of the substituted land; c) pass an order of interim relief directing that, during the pendency and until the final disposal of the present petition, the Respondents shall not take any step or action to the prejudice of the Petitioner in respect of the land admeasuring 22.231 acres and the assets offered for substitution;” 2. Briefly put, Regular Case No. RC AC-1/2019/A0002 was registered by the CBI under Section 120B/420 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860 and Section 13(2)/13(1)(d) of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 pertaining to certain alleged irregularities in the acquisition, release and licensing of land in Sectors 58-63 and 65-67 Gurgaon. On the strength of the said predicate offences, the Directorate of Enforcement registered ECIR/HIU/03/2019. Thereafter, in furtherance of the registration of the ECIR, the respondent by Provisional Attachment Order (‘PAO’) No. 12/2024 dated 14.10.2024 provisionally attached various immovable properties forming part of land under License No. 107/2010 after quantifying the alleged proceeds of crime at ₹1,813,74,06,526/-. 3. Pursuant to the PAO, Original Complaint No. 2470/2024 was filed and by order dated 01.04.2025, the learned Adjudicating Authority partially confirmed the attachment of land admeasuring 52.7609 acres having an aggregate value of ₹1,759,84,11,795/- out of the total admeasuring 60.3009 acres which had originally been attached. Aggrieved thereby, the petitioner preferred FPA-PMLA No. 840/DLI/2025 before the Hon’ble Appellate Tribunal, challenging the attachment and confirmation proceedings. Further, the petitioner also preferred interlocutory applications seeking reconciliation/re-computation of the proceeds of crime and substitution of the attached properties. 4. Thereafter, pursuant to the directions contained in order dated 27.01.2026, the Directorate carried out the reconciliation of the quantified proceeds of crime, and upon such reconciliation, the quantified proceeds of crime were revised from ₹1,813,74,06,526/- to ₹1,017,19,03,577/-, resulting in a reduction of ₹796,55,02,949/-. 5. Subsequently, the Appellate Tribunal directed the Directorate to seek instructions pertaining to firstly, the list of properties proposed to be released on account of downward revision in the quantum of proceeds of crime; and secondly, the necessary verification in respect of the properties offered by the petitioner in lieu of the attached properties for substitution. 6. In the aftermath of the same, the Directorate took on record the land schedules and valuation reports relating to the properties proposed for substitution and recorded that the aggregate value available for substitution comprising ₹408 crore in respect of land situated at Chaheru, Khajurla and Maheru and ₹654.89 crore towards the developable component of the additional land situated at Berka, Khaika and Sirsika worked out to ₹1062.89 crore excluding the undevelopable component. The said aggregate value was found to be sufficient to cover the revised proceeds of crime quantified at ₹1,017,19,03,577/-. Consequently, the present petition has been preferred seeking substitution of the balance attached land with the alternate assets offered by the petitioner. 7. The learned Senior Counsel for the petitioner submits that the respondent does not principally object to the substitution and the petitioner may be put to any condition by this Court for the substitution of the property. 8. On being asked, the learned counsel for the ED submits that the respondents have no serious objection to the substitution of the property provided adequate safeguards in respect of the substituted property are put in place by the Court. 9. Considering the limited relief sought and in view of the stance of the ED, this Court does not find any impediment in allowing the balance attached licensed land under Provisional Attachment Order No. 12 of 2024 dated 14.10.2024 to be substituted with the alternate assets offered by the petitioner. The said substitution is however subject to the following stipulations: a. The petitioner shall furnish an undertaking in the form of an affidavit before this Court establishing clear and marketable title along with undisputed ownership of the assets proposed for substitution, supported by verifiable documentary evidence. The substituted assets must be free from all encumbrances, including mortgages, liens, pledges or any third-party claims or security interests and a certificate to this effect must be submitted by the petitioner. b. The petitioner, in its undertaking in the form of an affidavit to this Court, shall also declare its relation with respect to entities owning the subject properties offered for substitution along with an undertaking regarding its rights to offer such land parcels for substitution of the attached properties. c. The substituted property shall not be sold, transferred, or otherwise alienated during the pendency of proceedings. d. Original title documents of the substituted property shall be deposited with the ED, with a formal acknowledgment in that regard. e. The petitioner shall furnish an indemnity bond to indemnify the ED/government in case of any loss or legal deficiency arising from the substitution. f. The petitioner shall ensure that the rights and interests of bona fide third parties remain unaffected by the substitution of the attached properties. g. The petitioner shall undertake to handover the possession of the alternate assets to the ED, subject to its rights to challenge the provisional attachment. h. The petitioner shall also submit a duly demarcated map/site plan to ED delineating the land parcels proposed for substitution. i. The petitioner shall provide a complete and transparent disclosure of the source of funds used to acquire the substituted assets, with supporting financial records, to ensure the substituted assets are untainted and not derived from proceeds of crime. j. The petitioner shall continue to fully cooperate with the investigation by the ED or any other authority under the PMLA and shall produce any documents or appear for inquiry when required. 10. It is made clear that the substitution of properties shall be without prejudice to the rights of the Directorate of Enforcement and shall not be construed as an acknowledgment of the legality of the source or legitimacy of the attached properties. The same shall also not affect the merits of the ongoing trial. 11. The present petition is allowed in the aforesaid terms. Pending application also stands disposed of.
