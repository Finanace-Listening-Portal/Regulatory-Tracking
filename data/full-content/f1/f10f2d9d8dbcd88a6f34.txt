Articles 3 min read GFF 2026: PM Modi sets next fintech agenda: Curated finance for small businesses, cyber resilience and consumer protection Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Tuesday laid out a new agenda for India’s fintech sector, calling for technology to build curated financial products for small businesses while strengthening cyber resilience, data protection and consumer safeguards. Addressing the Global FinTech Fest 2026, Modi said India’s next fintech chapter must move beyond the scale achieved in payments and use digital capabilities to better understand, anticipate and address the financial needs of underserved businesses and workers. Advt “We have shown speed and scale in payments. Now we will have to help increase the share of non-payment transactions,” Modi said.The Prime Minister illustrated the opportunity through the example of a woman running a small shop. With customers increasingly making digital payments, her income and expenditure patterns can become visible through digital transaction trails.As her business grows, she may need additional capital to buy equipment and expand. However, the credit requirement may be too small, or the business too informal, for traditional lending models to efficiently serve.“Technology can help us study the economic activities of such businesses and design curated models for them. I believe that the next chapter of fintech will be written here,” Modi said.From payment data to curated financial productsThe Prime Minister's remarks mark a push for India's fintech ecosystem to use the digital footprint created by payments to solve deeper challenges around credit access and financial inclusion.Millions of small merchants and businesses now generate regular digital transaction records, creating an opportunity for fintech companies to analyse their economic activity, understand cash flows, anticipate future requirements and design more customised financial products.The opportunity extends beyond small businesses to delivery partners, gig workers and other sections of the workforce who earn regularly but often lack easy access to financial products such as savings and pensions. Advt “For example, if there is a delivery partner or a small-time worker, he earns every month. But he needs access to services like savings and pension products. Fintech can address the needs of such people,” Modi said.Fintech can similarly make insurance and other financial services easier to access, he added, arguing that the experience of accessing these products should eventually become as simple and convenient as making a payment through a QR code.The Prime Minister said the real opportunity now lies in using fintech to expand India's broader economic and financial structure rather than allowing the sector to remain overwhelmingly focused on payments. Advt Cyber resilience and consumer trust must keep paceBut Modi also highlighted that the next phase of fintech innovation must be accompanied by stronger systems to protect consumers and safeguard the financial ecosystem.With technologies such as agentic AI, tokenisation and quantum computing opening up new possibilities in finance, the challenge would be to convert innovation into real economic impact without compromising security, privacy and public trust.Modi said the first priority for the industry should be building a top-notch cybersecurity framework and stronger cyber resilience.“First, we have to make cyber security top-notch in India. Second, our industry should have its own ethical data protection standards,” he said. Advt The Prime Minister also called for a stronger fintech-regulator-industry-innovation ecosystem to ensure that innovation and regulatory oversight evolve together as the sector adopts next-generation technologies.Importantly, Modi proposed the creation of a FinTech Consumer Protection Index, under which companies could receive transparent ratings based on consumer protection standards.“We should have a FinTech Consumer Protection Index, on the basis of which every company can have a transparent rating,” Modi said.The proposal places consumer trust alongside innovation as a key pillar of the next phase of India's fintech growth.India's fintech sector, Modi said, has already built scale, expanded access, democratised technology and earned public trust through the success of Digital India and digital financial infrastructure.The next challenge, however, is more complex, using that scale to build customised finance for India's vast underserved economy while ensuring that cyber security, data privacy and consumer protection remain strong enough to sustain the next generation of innovation.
