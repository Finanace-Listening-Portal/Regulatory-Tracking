Bank loans against gold jewellery rose 83% year on year to Rs 5.6 lakh crore in August 2026, while NBFC gold loans grew 69% to about Rs 3.6 lakh crore, according to Reserve Bank of India data. Shriram Finance reported a rise in bad loans against gold this week, following concerns flagged by Bajaj Finance and Piramal Finance. Advt A key concern is that some borrowers may be using gold backed loans to repay overdue debt from other lenders, shifting liabilities without improving their ability to service them. An estimated Rs 4.7 lakh crore of gold loan disbursements in 2025-26 went to borrowers who were already more than 90 days overdue on at least one other loan, according to an industry estimate. The figure does not mean these gold loans have turned bad, but highlights the overlap between gold backed borrowing and existing repayment stress.How loans stackShriram Finance's gross Stage 3 gold loans, overdue by more than 90 days, rose to 3.12% of its portfolio at the end of September from 2.57% in June and 2.28% a year earlier. Bajaj Finance, which has a gold loan portfolio of about Rs 24,000 crore, also reported a sequential increase in Stage 2 and Stage 3 assets. The lender said much of the increase was linked to short tenure gold loan schemes that it has since discontinued.A fall in gold prices can further weaken lenders' protection by reducing the value of pledged jewellery. Gold prices had already retreated substantially from their January peak by mid October. A further decline, particularly if accompanied by more defaults, could leave lenders with a thinner collateral buffer when they auction jewellery to recover dues. However, lower gold prices alone do not automatically translate into losses, as the outcome also depends on initial loan to value ratios, repayments and auction recoveries.RBI rules effective April 1, 2026, cap loan to value ratios for consumption loans against eligible gold collateral at 85% for loans up to Rs 2.5 lakh, 80% for loans above Rs 2.5 lakh and up to Rs 5 lakh, and 75% for larger loans. These are maximum permissible ratios, not lending targets. Many gold focused NBFCs have historically maintained lower initial ratios to preserve a buffer against price volatility. Advt The immediate test for lenders is whether the recent rise in delinquencies remains concentrated in specific products or spreads across their portfolios. Gold loans offer collateral protection, but pledged jewellery cannot guarantee repayment capacity. Conservative lending margins, regular collateral valuations and timely recovery action will be important if borrower stress deepens or gold prices fall further.
