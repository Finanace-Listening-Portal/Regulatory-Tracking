AI-enabled workflows can track borrower filings, rating changes, market developments and sector-specific risks, triggering targeted reassessments of debt service capacity, covenant headroom and facility limits.Banks are beginning to connect credit origination with ongoing borrower monitoring, allowing credit teams to identify borrower deterioration earlier and intervene more promptly. Where integrated enterprise data and analytical capabilities are available, they can also assess the implications of macroeconomic and sector-specific scenarios across individual borrowers and portfolios, according to McKinsey. Advt Banks are testing agentic AI workflows across wholesale lending, including retrieval of borrower data, financial analysis, credit policy checks, documentation and exception management. Early implementations show productivity improvements of 30 to 50 per cent across the end-to-end credit life cycle, shorter turnaround times and output quality rates of approximately 95 per cent.How it worksThe degree of automation is calibrated to transaction complexity and exposure. Standardised, lower-risk transactions are handled through highly automated workflows, while moderately risky transactions retain human-assisted processes. Complex and high-exposure transactions are dominated by human-led assessments supported by AI-generated analysis.Some leading Europe banks are extending automated credit assessment to higher exposures, with credit officers retaining accountability for final approvals.AI-enabled workflows can also support early-warning indicators, anomaly detection and automated scenario modelling, synthesising a broader set of internal and external signals at greater depth and speed. The technology enables continuous, event-triggered monitoring of borrower positions, financial covenants, risk limits, policy adherence, model drift and risk appetite.Automated quality checks, evidence logging and exception-based routing can support more responsive and targeted risk assessments. Human experts remain accountable for complex judgments, exceptions and consequential decisions. Advt McKinsey said the competitive opportunity from AI in risk management extends beyond productivity improvements to better foresight and business enablement. This could allow banks to adopt more dynamic portfolio strategies and lending policies, improve their understanding of risk concentrations and transmission channels, and respond faster to sector trends and disruptions.
