Unlike unit-linked insurance plans, the policy does not directly expose policyholders to equity or debt market fluctuations. Being non-participating, it does not provide participation in the insurer’s distributable surplus through bonuses. The product version discussed in the draft is UIN 101N189V04; confirm the applicable version and contractual terms with the insurer. Life Cover and Accidental Death Benefits Life Option The Life option provides financial protection through payment of the applicable death benefit upon the death of the insured person during the coverage period, subject to policy conditions. The proceeds can help surviving family members meet household expenses, repay outstanding loans, finance children’s education and manage other financial commitments. Life Plus Option The Life Plus option incorporates additional accidental death protection. An accidental death claim must satisfy the contractual definition of an accident and the relevant eligibility and exclusion requirements. Accidental death protection should not be confused with comprehensive personal accident insurance, which may separately cover disability and other consequences of accidents. Premium Payment Options and Policy Lapse Depending on eligibility, the policy may allow premiums to be paid throughout the policy term, over a shorter period or through a single payment. Limited premium payment can reduce the number of years during which the policyholder must make payments. Nevertheless, annual premiums may be higher than under a regular payment arrangement. Failure to pay premiums within the applicable period may result in lapse or reduced benefits where paid-up provisions apply. Policyholders should not assume that discontinuing premiums automatically creates a refundable balance. Income-Tax Benefits of Life Insurance Deduction for Eligible Premiums Under the Income-tax Act, 1961, eligible life insurance premiums historically qualified for deduction under Section 80C, subject to the overall ₹1.50 lakh deduction ceiling and applicable conditions. Such deductions are ordinarily unavailable under the new tax regime. For the tax year beginning 1 April 2026, taxpayers should apply the corresponding provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025, and applicable rules. Taxability of Death and Maturity Benefits Death benefits under an ordinary qualifying individual life insurance policy are generally exempt, subject to statutory exceptions. Maturity proceeds, including return-of-premium benefits, require a separate assessment of exemption conditions. Relevant considerations include the issue date, premium relative to actual capital sum assured, aggregate premiums, nature of proceeds and applicable legislation. For relevant non-ULIP policies issued from 1 April 2023, the ₹5 lakh aggregate annual premium threshold also requires consideration for exemption of eligible non-death proceeds. GST on Individual Life Insurance Premiums Individual life insurance services became eligible for GST exemption with effect from 22 September 2025. Eligible individual term insurance premiums may benefit from the exemption. Policyholders should separately examine optional riders, the nature of the insurance arrangement and the actual invoice. Insurance Claims and Disclosure Requirements A life insurance proposal involves disclosure of material information concerning health, occupation, income, lifestyle and existing insurance coverage. Incorrect or incomplete disclosure may create complications during claim assessment, subject to statutory protections under the Insurance Act, 1938. Nominees should retain access to policy details, proof of identity and relevant claim documentation. Additional documentation may be required where death results from an accident.
