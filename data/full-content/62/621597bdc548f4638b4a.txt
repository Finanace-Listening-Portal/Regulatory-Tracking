Bloom divided it into six progressive levels, each representing a higher stage of mental development. LevelMeaningExample in a CA Context 1. RememberRecall facts and definitions. Recalling Sections of the Income Tax Act or Ind AS numbers. 2. Understand Explain ideas and interpret meaning. Explaining the logic behind AS 16 capitalization or GST composition rules. 3. Apply Use knowledge in new situations. Applying audit sampling concepts to a new client’s environment. 4. Analyze Break down information and identify relationships. Comparing cash flow and profit patterns to detect irregularities. 5. Evaluate Make judgments based on criteria. Assessing whether revenue recognition aligns with substance over form. 6. Create Combine knowledge to produce something new. Designing a new internal control process or tax-efficient business structure. “Thinking is difficult, that’s why most people judge.” - Carl Jung www.taxguru.in 2. The Affective Domain — Emotion-Based Thinking If the cognitive domain is the head, the affective domain is the heart of learning. It captures how attitudes, ethics, and empathy shape professional behavior — the emotional intelligence behind intellectual skill. LevelMeaningExample in a CA Context 1. ReceivingBeing open and attentive. Listening carefully to client concerns or peer feedback. 2. RespondingParticipating actively. Asking questions during audits instead of staying silent. 3. Valuing Seeing importance in ideas or ethics. Valuing transparency over convenience in reporting. 4. Organizing Integrating new values into one’s framework. Balancing profitability with compliance in decision-making. 5. CharacterizingLiving by internalized principles. Acting ethically even when no one is watching. 3. The Psychomotor Domain — Action-Based Thinking This domain connects mind to movement — turning knowledge into action. Though Bloom’s original taxonomy didn’t define it, later researchers like Elizabeth Simpson (1972) expanded it into seven practical levels. LevelMeaningExample in a CA Context 1. Perception Using sensory cues for awareness. Noticing non-verbal signals in client meetings. 2. SetReadiness to act. Preparing mentally and technically for a stock audit. 3. Guided ResponseImitation and trial. Following a senior’s walk-through to understand audit trails. 4. MechanismDeveloping routine skill. Performing reconciliations with consistent accuracy. 5. Complex Overt Response Mastery and precision. Handling concurrent audits confidently under deadlines. 6. Adaptation Modifying techniques for new conditions. Adjusting audit procedures for a technology- driven client. 7. OriginationCreating new methods.
