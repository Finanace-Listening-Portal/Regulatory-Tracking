Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Vs Nilachal Iron and Power Limited (ITAT Kolkata) Sections 145(3) and 68: Books Cannot Be Rejected on Vague Comparisons, Nor Reconciled Sales Taxed Again as Cash Credits Background of the Dispute The assessee, a company belonging to the Jai Balaji Group, was engaged in manufacturing and selling sponge iron. It filed its return declaring a loss of ₹24,91,49,420. During scrutiny, the Assessing Officer rejected the books of account and made several additions concerning raw-material consumption, alleged unrecorded sales, stock valuation, interest and other expenses. The Commissioner (Appeals) held the rejection of books invalid and deleted the disputed additions. The Revenue challenged that relief before the Tribunal. After condoning the Department’s delay of 72 days, the Tribunal examined the grounds and dismissed the Revenue’s appeal in its entirety. Books Cannot Be Rejected Without Specific Defects The Assessing Officer questioned the financial results because production costs exceeded sales realisations, the assessee’s selling price differed from that of a group company, and certain stock and consumption figures appeared inconsistent to him. However, the Tribunal found that no specific defects or deficiencies in the books had been identified. The conclusions supporting estimated turnover, stock adjustments and inflated expenditure were vague and ambiguous. The Commissioner had also noted the absence of a meaningful examination of the primary records, including invoices and bills. The Tribunal upheld the finding that rejection under section 145(3) was invalid. Unfavourable results and broad comparisons could not replace identification of material accounting defects. ₹6.95 Crore Sales Already Accounted for Could Not Be Added Again The Assessing Officer treated iron ore sales of ₹6,95,35,221 as unexplained cash credits under section 68, questioning their absence as a separate item in the tax audit reporting. The assessee explained that iron ore and iron ore pellets had been grouped together in the tax audit report. The sales were already included in revenue from operations in the audited accounts. It also furnished purchase and sales bills and a quantitative reconciliation. Of 14,786 MT purchased, 9,857 MT had been sold directly at the port, 2,992 MT remained in transit and 1,937 MT reached the plant. The Tribunal accepted the Commissioner’s factual finding that the quantities were reconciled and the sales were already accounted for. It upheld deletion of the section 68 addition, which would otherwise duplicate the revenue already recognised. Grouped Reporting Did Not Establish Unrecorded Pellet Sales The Assessing Officer also disallowed approximately ₹15.45 crore of iron ore pellet consumption and made a further addition of approximately ₹16 crore towards alleged unrecorded sales. The assessee explained that pellet consumption was separately reflected in the audited accounts but included with iron ore in the tax audit report. It furnished the movement of opening stock, purchases, closing stock and consumption. The Commissioner considered the explanations, the remand report and the assessee’s rejoinder before deleting the additions. The Tribunal upheld the relief. A difference in the grouping of raw materials did not establish that the materials had remained unconsumed or had been sold outside the books. Imported Coal Could Not Be Compared Mechanically with Local Coal The Assessing Officer disallowed ₹7,15,17,281 of coal consumption expenditure by comparing the assessee with Jai Balaji Jyoti Steels Limited. He also estimated alleged outside-book coal sales of ₹7,86,69,009. The Tribunal noted that the assessee used imported coal, while the comparable group company mainly used lower-grade indigenous coal. Their costs and consumption characteristics differed. The comparison ignored these material differences. Further, the quantity consumed was not disputed, undermining the inference that coal had instead been sold outside the books. Both deletions were upheld. The Tribunal similarly sustained deletion of ₹17,54,596 concerning coal-dust sales, where the estimate lacked corroborative evidence and no defect had been established in the supporting bills. Different Product Quality Defeated Estimated Sales Addition An addition of ₹6,01,43,629 arose from applying the group company’s average selling price to the assessee’s sales and using that rate to derive an alleged closing-stock shortage. However, the products were materially different. The group company manufactured sponge iron with metallic iron content of approximately 85% ±2%, while the assessee’s product had content of approximately 60% ±2%. The Tribunal upheld deletion because the comparison ignored product quality and there was no evidence of higher sale realisations or disposal of the alleged stock difference. It also upheld deletion of ₹1,33,43,354 relating to interest, accepting that the advances concerned supplies of raw materials, expenses and sundry items. The ad hoc disallowance of ₹1,18,32,970 from manufacturing and other expenses was likewise rejected. Author’s Comments The decision demonstrates the importance of commercial comparability and quantitative reconciliation in manufacturing assessments. A group relationship does not make two manufacturers automatically comparable. Raw-material quality, consumption efficiency and finished-product specifications can materially affect costs and selling prices. Equally, a presentation difference between audited accounts and tax audit disclosures requires reconciliation before an adverse inference is drawn. The ruling does not prevent rejection of defective books or evidence-based estimation. It establishes that specific accounting defects and supporting material must precede additions; assumptions cannot supply the missing evidence. Here, the detailed factual explanations defeated the Revenue’s estimates, and the relief was upheld on merits. Cases Discussed Jai Balaji Jyoti Steels Limited (CIT(A); AY 2015-16; appeal number and order date not specified): The Tribunal referred to the appellate proceedings involving this group company while examining the comparison of coal consumption and production costs. The distinction between imported coal and indigenous coal was relevant to rejecting the Assessing Officer’s estimates. This was a factual comparison involving a related assessee, not a judicial precedent relied upon for a legal proposition. AO noted that the assessee has under stated the coal price and came to the conclusion that the sale of the said coal should be ₹36,11,464/- (1044×3459.26) and thus estimated the sales at ₹39,72,613/- by taking a GP rate of 10% and thus, made the addition of ₹17,54,596/-. 8.2. After hearing the rival contentions and perusing the materials available on record, we find that the ld. CIT (A) noted that the ld. AO has not disputed the quantity of coal (dust coal) sold by the assessee. The ld. CIT (A) also noted that in the assessment order stated that bill of such dust coal was produced before him for examination thus, the ld. CIT (A) noted that the ld. AO without pointed out any defect with the documents produced before the ld. CIT (A) just estimated the sale without any corroboration evidence and thus, deleted the addition. We observed from the facts before us that the addition made by the ld. AO was totally based on the presumption and surmises without any basis and so rightly deleted by the ld. CIT (A). Consequently, upheld the order of the ld. CIT (A) on this issue by dismissing the ground no.6 of Revenue’s appeal. 9. The issue raised in ground no.7 is against the deletion of addition of ₹6,01,43,629/- on account of under valuation of closing stock and unaccounted sales. 10. Facts in brief are that during the course of assessment proceedings the ld. AO noted that the value of the closing stock of the assessee as per the audited accounts was ₹86,12,320/-. The ld. AO derived the said amount of 22,604 MT and arrived at that closing stock was 382.77 MT instead of actual closing stock declared by the assessee of 539 MT. the ld. AO further noted that the so far as the group entity M/s Jai Balaji Jyoti Steels Limited is concerned was ₹22,604/- and thus, there is a difference in the closing stock as well as the sales disclosed by the assessee. Accordingly, the ld. AO estimated the undisclosed sales at ₹6,01,43,629/- and added the same to the income of the assessee. We further note that the ld. AO has blatantly taken the facts and figured all the sister concerns without taking into account the basic facts. The assessee submitted that M/s Jai Balaji Jyoti Steels Limited manufacturing sponge iron with Fe(m) 85±2% and assessee manufacture Fe(m) 60±2%, such comparison rate of both product is unacceptable. 11. The ld. CIT (A), after considering of the facts of the case and the submissions and arguments of the counsel for the assessee as also the documents furnished by the assessee, held that the addition made by the ld. AO was only on the assumption and surmises by observing and holding as under:- “7.8 Ground Nos. 8 & 12: Revenue from operation has been estimated at Rs.37,35,53,704/- in place of actual Rs. 31,34,10,075/- leading to addition of Rs. 6,01,43,629/-. AO in assessment order has taken rate of average sales of finished goods of Jai Jyoti Balaji Steels Limited @ 22604/MT as rate of closing stock of assessee instead of Rate of Closing Stock @ Rs.16,500/- taken by assessee. The value of closing stock of assessee as per audited accounts is Rs.86,12,320/-. AO divided Rs.86,12,320/- by 22500 and arrived that closing stock is 382.77 MT instead of actual closing stock of 539 MT. AO, further applied average rate of sale of Jai Balaji Jyoti Steel Limited @22,604/- to total sales of assessee and also included 156 MT (539-382.77) being difference of closing stock arrived by him and of assessee as undisclosed sale. Working of estimation of revenue from operation from Rs.37,35,53,704/- in place of actual Rs. 31,34,10,075/- is given below: Total quantity sold by assessee 16370 MT Quantity being difference in closing stock alleged to be sold by assessee 156 MT Total quantity sold as per AO 16526 MT Sales rate as per AO 22604/MT Total sales value as per AO Rs. 37,35,53,704/- Sales as per P & L Account Rs. 31,34,10,075/- Addition as undisclosed sales Rs. 6,01,43,629/- Assessee in its submission has submitted that the AO has taken average rate of sale of Jai Jyoti Balaji Steels Limited without considering the fact that Jai Jyoti Balaji Steels Limited manufactures sponge iron with Fe(M) 85+-2 % and assessee manufactures Fe(M) 60+-2%. As such comparison of rate of both product is totally unacceptable. It further submitted that if rate of Fe(M) 85+-2% is converted to Fe(M) 60+-2% then rate of sales would come to Rs. 15,955/-(22604*60/85) as such valuation of closing stock @ 16000/MT is correct. I find that the addition made by the AO is not based on any facts. It is also found that the AO in the assessment order has arrived at a conclusion that assessee has sold goods at a rate higher than recorded in books of accounts without any iota of evidence or material. Further, no evidence has been produced regarding sale of closing stock difference of 156MT arrived by AO. The assessment order made by the AO is totally vague in nature and merely made on assumption and presumptions. Therefore, the addition of Rs.6,01,43,629/- made by AO merely on assumption and presumptions is deleted.” 11.1. After hearing both the parties and taking into accounts the facts on records, we find that the addition was made by the AO without any basis and on presumptions and surmises. The Ld. CIT(A) after detailed analysis of the facts has rightly deleted the addition by holding that the addition was made by the on surmise only. Therefore we do not find any infirmity in the appellate order and are inclined to uphold the same on this issue by dismissing the ground no.7 of the Revenue’s appeal. 12. The issue raised in ground no.8 is against the deletion of disallowances of ₹1,33,43,354/- on account of interest. 12.1. The ld. AO during the course of assessment proceedings, noted that the assessee has given an interest on loans and advances to the tune of ₹7,31,14,269/- and has not furnished the details of the same as he applied a rate of 18.2% of the said amount and added the same to the income of the assessee by holding that these loans and advances were not for the purpose of business. The ld. CIT (A) deleted the disallowances by observing and holding as under:- “7.10 Ground No. 10 & 11: Estimating the production expenses including cost of consumption of coal to Rs. 6,14,90,448/- instead of actual production expenses on ad hoc basis and Estimating the Other Expenses to Rs.20,00,000/- instead of actual other expenses on ad-hoc basis; The AO out of total manufacturing and other expenses other than finance cost and depreciation amounting to Rs.8,52,46,199/- has only allowed total expenditure of Rs. 7,34,13,229/- only on estimate basis by rejecting books of accounts of assessee. Since the rejection of books of accounts has been held invalid as such disallowance of Rs. 1,18,32,970/- (Rs. 8,52,46,199/-7,34,13,229/-) being consequential in nature is hereby deleted.” 12.2. After hearing the rival contentions and perusing the materials available on record, we find that the ld. AO has completely misunderstood the fats that the ld. CIT (A) has given a table which is extracted above, which proved that the advances were given for supply of raw materials against expenses and sundry items, therefore, the very premises of the ld. AO in estimating the interest on the said advance was wrong and therefore, the ld. CIT (A) rightly deleted the addition. We accordingly upheld the order of ld. CIT (A) by dismissing the ground no.8. 13. The issue raised in ground no.9 is against the deletion of addition of ₹1,18,32,970/-, out of the total manufacture and other expenses. 13.1. The ld. AO during the course of assessment proceedings only allowed the total expenditure on account of cost od consumption of coal and other expenses at ₹7,34,13,229/- as against the claim of the assessee of ₹8,52,46,199/-, which was without any basis. The ld. AO has resorted to this allowance only on the ground that the books of accounts were rejected and books were not reliable. In our opinion, the disallowance made by the ld. AO was only whimsical basis on the presumption and surmises. Accordingly, we upheld the order of ld. CIT (A) by dismissing the appeal of the Revenue. The ground no. 9 of Revenue’s appeal is dismissed. 14. In the result, the appeal of the Revenue is dismissed. Order pronounced on 13.10.2026.
