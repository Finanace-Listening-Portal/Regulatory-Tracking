Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Busy Infotech Pvt Ltd Vs PCIT (Delhi High Court) Summary: The Delhi High Court, in Busy Infotech Pvt Ltd Vs Pr Commissioner of Income Tax Delhi 1 & Ors., set aside an assessment order involving an addition of ₹63.90 crore under Section 56(2)(viib) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, along with two subsequent orders challenged by the assessee. The dispute concerned the issuance of optionally convertible redeemable preference shares (OCRPS) by the petitioner to its holding company, IndiaMart, during Assessment Year 2017-18. The Division Bench comprising Justice Dinesh Mehta and Dr. Justice Aditi Choudhary, by judgment dated 19 November 2026, remanded the matter to the Assessing Officer for fresh consideration in accordance with law, including consideration of the Delhi High Court’s earlier decision in FIS Payment Solutions and Services India (P) Ltd. v. UOI, (2024) 166 taxmann.com 354. The Court did not finally adjudicate the taxability of the share premium. The petitioner, formerly known as Tolexo Online Pvt. Ltd., operated an e-marketplace connecting domestic buyers and suppliers and was also engaged in selling accounting software and related products. During AY 2017-18, it issued 0.01% OCRPS having a face value of ₹10 each at a premium of ₹90 to its holding company, IndiaMart. According to Note No. 3 accompanying the audited balance sheet, the preference shares could be converted into equity shares at the issue price, including premium, or at fair market value on the conversion date, at the holder’s option. The instruments were also redeemable, wholly or partly, at the option of the company or holder after five years but before twenty years from allotment, at premium or fair market value at redemption. The Assessing Officer completed the assessment under Section 143(3) on 30 December 2019 and made an addition of ₹63,90,00,000 under Section 56(2)(viib), raising consequential demand for tax and interest. The assessee challenged the assessment in appeal and sought a stay of the outstanding demand. Its application before the Assessing Officer was rejected on 3 March 2020. The assessee thereafter approached the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax, but the stay application remained pending until its rejection on 24 September 2026. Consequently, the petitioner approached the High Court challenging the two rejection orders. Senior Advocate Ajay Vohra, appearing for the petitioner, argued that the Assessing Officer had disregarded relevant facts and applicable law while making the substantial addition. He relied upon the Delhi High Court’s judgment in FIS Payment Solutions and Services India (P) Ltd. v. UOI, contending that the decision covered the issue in the petitioner’s favour. Although that judgment had been delivered after the original assessment order, the petitioner submitted that it was relevant to the dispute. Counsel further explained that neither the petitioner nor its representative could appear when the Principal Commissioner rejected the stay application, and the precedent therefore could not be brought to the Commissioner’s attention. On that basis, the petitioner sought a stay of the entire outstanding demand. During the hearing, Senior Standing Counsel Monica Benjamin, appearing for the Revenue, submitted on written instructions that the assessment order could be set aside and the matter remanded to the Assessing Officer for fresh consideration, including examination of the judgment in FIS Payment Solutions. Taking note of this stand, the High Court set aside the assessment order dated 30 December 2019, the order dated 3 March 2020 passed under Section 154, and the Principal Commissioner’s order dated 24 September 2026. The matter was restored to the Assessing Officer for passing a fresh assessment order in accordance with law. The High Court permitted the petitioner to submit a representation or written submissions, together with copies of the judgments on which it intended to rely, within three weeks from 19 November 2026. It directed the Assessing Officer to provide a personal hearing after giving at least seven days’ prior intimation and thereafter pass a reasoned speaking order. The Court expressly preserved the petitioner’s right to pursue appropriate legal remedies if the fresh order proved prejudicial. The writ petition and pending applications were accordingly disposed of. The operative relief was a fresh assessment, not an outright deletion of the ₹63.90 crore addition on merits. Cases Discussed 1. FIS Payment Solutions and Services India (P) Ltd. Vs Union of India & Ors. (Delhi High Court); W.P.(C) 10289/2024; (2024) 166 taxmann.com 354. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF DELHI HIGH COURT 1. By way of present writ petition, the petitioner has challenged the order dated 24.09.2026 so also the order dated 03.03.2020, whereby the stay applications filed by the petitioner were rejected by the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax (hereinafter referred to as ‘PCIT’) and the Assessing Officer (AO), respectively. 2. The facts relevant for the disposal of the writ petition, as pleaded in the petition are that the petitioner which was earlier known as ‘Tolexo Online Pvt. Ltd.’ is engaged in providing an e-marketplace – an interactive hub for domestic buyers and suppliers, catering to the business needs of customers. It is also engaged in selling accounting software and other related products. 3. During the Assessment Year in question (2017-18), the petitioner issued 0.01% Optionally Convertible Redeemable Preference Shares (hereinafter referred to as ‘OCRPS’) of face value of Rs. 10/- at a premium of Rs. 90/- to its holding company, known as IndiaMart. 4. Note No.3 appended with the Audited balance sheet contained a stipulation that OCRPS will be optionally convertible into equity share at issued price including premium or at fair market value on the date of conversion, at the option of the holder. OCRPS will be redeemable (wholly or partly) either at the option of the company or the holder after the expiry of 5 years but before 20 years from the date of allotment of OCPRS at premium or at fair market value at the time of redemption. 5. While framing an assessment order under Section 143(3) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act of 1961’), the AO vide order dated 30.12.2019, made an addition of Rs. 63,90,00,000/- to the total income of the assessee under Section 56(2)(viib) of the Act of 1961 and raised a demand of tax and interest accordingly. 6. The petitioner preferred an appeal against the above referred Assessment Order and also preferred a stay application before the AO which was rejected vide order dated 03.03.2020. 7. After rejection of the said stay application, the petitioner moved a stay application before the PCIT, which remained pending and came to be rejected by the PCIT vide its order dated 24.09.2026. 8. Mr. Ajay Vohra, learned senior counsel for the petitioner argued that the AO has ignored the relevant facts and law while framing the assessment order and made huge addition without any legal basis. 9. He further submitted that the judgment of this Court in the case of FIS Payment Solutions and Services India (P) Ltd v. UOI reported in (2024) 166 taxmann.com 354, though delivered subsequent to the passing of the Assessment Order nevertheless clinches the issue in petitioner’s favour. 10. He further submitted that when the PCIT rejected the petitioner’s stay application, neither the petitioner nor its representative could appear and consequently, the aforesaid judgment could not be brought to the notice of the PCIT. 11. He submitted that in any event, since the issue stands covered by the judgment of this Court in the case of FIS Payment Solutions (supra), the petitioner is entitled for stay of the entire outstanding demand. 12. Ms. Monica Benjamin, learned Senior Standing Counsel for the respondents on the basis of written instructions, submitted that the Assessment Order be set aside and the matter be remanded to the AO for fresh consideration, including consideration of the judgment of this Court in the case of FIS Payment Solutions (supra). 13. In view of the fair stand taken by the respondents, the impugned Assessment Order dated 30.12.2019 (Annexure P-5) so also the order dated 03.03.2020 (Annexure P-10) passed under Section 154 of the Act of 1961 and the order dated 24.09.2026 (Annexure P-1) are hereby set aside. The matter is restored back to the dockets of the AO, who shall pass a fresh Assessment Order in accordance with law. 14. The petitioner shall be free to file a representation/written submissions before the AO, along with copies of the judgments on which it proposes to rely, within a period of 3 weeks from today. 15. The AO shall provide a personal hearing to the petitioner by giving at least 7 days prior intimation to the petitioner/assessee and thereafter pass a speaking order in accordance with law. 16. The petitioner’s right to take legal recourse in case order so passed is in any manner prejudicial to it, is reserved. 17. The writ petition is accordingly disposed of. 18. Pending application(s), if any, stand disposed of.
