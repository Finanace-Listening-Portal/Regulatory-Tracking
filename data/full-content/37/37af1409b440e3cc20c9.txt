Articles 3 min read NBFCs must shift focus from loan book expansion to risk-adjusted profitability: Industry leaders Rising credit costs, delayed defaults, AI-related risks and cybersecurity threats are pushing NBFCs to prioritise portfolio quality, stronger governance and sustainable returns over disbursement growth, risk and compliance leaders said at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Published On Oct 12, 2026 at 05:17 AM IST var news_msid = 134879841; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook NBFCs must move beyond loan book expansion and assess growth through risk-adjusted returns, portfolio quality and operational resilience as credit and technology risks evolve, industry leaders said.At the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026, Abhishikta Munjal, CRO, Clix Capital; Mallika Mittal, CRO & President, InCred Finance; Ashutosh Tiwary, Head Credit Policy & Risk, Lendingkart Finance; Pinki Jha, Chief Compliance Officer, Oxyzo; and Jimit Shah, CISO, LIC Housing Finance, discussed the changing risk priorities for lenders.Profitability matters more than disbursement growthMunjal said lenders must evaluate growth against expected credit losses, operating expenses and returns rather than AUM alone. Clix Capital has revamped its expected credit loss model and introduced regular portfolio reviews involving business and risk teams to identify segments generating sustainable returns. Advt Mittal highlighted risk-adjusted return on capital (RAROC) as a more meaningful measure of performance because it accounts for credit costs, operating expenses and capital deployed. She said lenders should assess whether returns can be sustained over a two-to-three-year horizon.Tiwary cautioned that the true cost of lending often emerges with a delay, with losses on some 36-month business loans peaking around 18–20 months into the tenure.“The true cost of growth in the lending business is only known after a time lag,” he said.He advised lenders entering new products or geographies to start with limited exposure and scale up only after validating portfolio performance. Model drift and rising exceptions to credit policies should also trigger closer scrutiny.AI needs stronger model governanceMunjal said digital verification and scorecard-based underwriting can improve consistency and reduce reliance on manual checks, but human intervention remains necessary for exceptions and complex cases.Tiwary warned that larger datasets do not automatically produce better credit decisions. Poor-quality information and correlations mistaken for causation can embed flawed assumptions into lending models.He also called for professionals who combine credit expertise with data science capabilities, arguing that NBFCs need specialists who can evaluate model outputs and translate them into sound lending decisions. Advt Mittal highlighted the growing importance of model governance as lenders deploy automated systems across underwriting, collections and marketing. Organised fraud, mule accounts and multiple borrowing are adding to the challenges.Compliance and cybersecurity must start earlyJha argued that compliance should be incorporated during product design rather than introduced after business decisions have been made.“Compliance at the end is a brake. Compliance at the beginning is a steering wheel,” she said.Early involvement of compliance teams, she added, can help lenders address customer protection and regulatory requirements without costly redesigns later. Advt Shah highlighted the need to integrate fraud prevention into customer onboarding and improve coordination between verification, credit and disbursement teams. He also flagged gaps in third-party inventories and data governance as potential sources of operational and cybersecurity risk.As NBFCs prepare for the Digital Personal Data Protection framework, institutions will need clearer visibility over how customer information is collected, used, retained and shared.Funding costs and customer trust pose fresh risksMunjal warned that rising funding costs could force lenders to increase borrowing rates, potentially weakening repayment capacity or pushing stronger customers towards cheaper alternatives. Advt Mittal flagged geopolitical uncertainty and poorly governed AI as additional risks, while Tiwary cautioned that automated errors could affect large numbers of borrowers if left unchecked.“Earlier, this consent was assumed. Tomorrow, this consent has to be earned,” Tiwary said, highlighting the growing importance of customer consent in data-driven lending.NBFC leaders said that sustainable NBFC growth will depend on disciplined capital allocation, reliable credit models and governance that keeps pace with business expansion. As lenders adopt more AI and alternative data, risk management must increasingly shape business strategy rather than merely respond to losses after they occur.
