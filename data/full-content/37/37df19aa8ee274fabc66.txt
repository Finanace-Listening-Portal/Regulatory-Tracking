Summary: Invesco India Contra Fund is a current open-ended equity scheme following a contrarian investment strategy. Its objective is capital appreciation through equity and equity-related instruments, without any assurance that the objective will be achieved. The scheme normally allocates 65% to 100% to equity and equity-related instruments and up to 35% to debt and money-market instruments. Its Tier I benchmark is the BSE 500 Total Return Index, and both the scheme and benchmark were displayed in the Very High Risk category in the current official product material. The portfolio disclosed for 30 September 2026 held 96.56% in equity and 3.44% in cash and cash equivalents, led by ICICI Bank and Larsen & Toubro. This article explains what the contra label means, how portfolio concentration and style cycles can affect outcomes, how the 10% exit-load carve-out works, why Direct and Regular plans have different costs, and how equity-oriented mutual fund taxation generally applies. It does not rank the scheme, forecast returns or treat recent holdings as a recommendation. It is not a closed-ended offer or a newly launched scheme. The Regular Plan dates to 11 April 2007 and the Direct Plan to 1 January 2013. The scheme objective is to generate capital appreciation by investing predominantly in equity and equity-related instruments through contrarian investing. A contra approach generally looks for businesses, sectors or securities that are out of favour or under-owned relative to the manager’s assessment of fundamentals and value. That description is an investment process, not a guarantee that the market will reverse or that a selected security will recover. Asset allocation creates room but not certainty The scheme document permits 65% to 100% in equity and equity-related instruments and 0% to 35% in debt and money-market instruments. This range allows the manager to hold liquidity or defensive assets, but the fund remains an equity scheme and can experience significant market losses. Derivatives may be used within the disclosed limits for hedging, portfolio balancing or other permitted purposes. Investors should read the current SID and monthly portfolio together because the permitted range describes what the fund may do, while the portfolio shows what it held on a particular date. The fund’s research process and diversification reduce neither market risk nor the possibility of permanent loss in individual holdings. Exit load uses a 10 percent redemption carve out How the one year test works For each purchase of units through a lump sum, switch-in, SIP or STP, the current official load structure provides no exit load if up to 10% of the units allotted are redeemed or switched out within one year. Redemption or switch-out in excess of that 10% within one year attracts a 1% exit load. Units redeemed or switched out after one year carry no exit load under the stated structure. Each purchase lot has its own allotment date, so a SIP creates multiple holding periods rather than one common anniversary. Exit load is separate from tax and market movement The load is calculated under scheme rules and is not the same as capital-gains tax, STT, stamp duty or the fall in NAV between purchase and redemption. A partial redemption should be checked against the applicable unit-lot accounting and the remaining free-unit entitlement. A switch is generally treated as a redemption from one scheme or plan and a purchase into another, which can trigger load and tax even when no cash reaches the investor’s bank account. The current statement, transaction confirmation and scheme load notice should be checked before placing the instruction. Direct and Regular plans differ mainly through distribution cost The scheme offers Direct and Regular plans with Growth and IDCW-related options. The underlying portfolio is ordinarily common to plan options, while the expense structure and resulting NAV differ. A Direct Plan excludes distributor commission and must have a lower base expense ratio than the corresponding Regular Plan under the applicable framework. It is intended for an investor dealing directly with the fund house and does not by itself provide advice. A Regular Plan includes distribution expenses and may be used through an intermediary. Total Expense Ratio is charged to scheme assets and therefore reduces NAV over time; it is not normally collected as a separate invoice from the investor. The current figure can change and should be read from the AMC or AMFI daily disclosure for the exact plan and option. The official page also discloses statutory levies and trading costs under the 2026 mutual fund regulations. Comparing only the maximum permissible base expense ratio with a current plan TER can mislead because they answer different questions. Costs should be compared on the same date, plan and regulatory basis. Purchase redemption and operational records The product page states a ₹1,000 fresh and additional lump-sum minimum and a redemption minimum of ₹1,000, 0.001 unit or the account balance, whichever is lower. SIP minima and instalment conditions should be verified on the live transaction screen because facility terms can change. Applicable NAV depends on receipt of a valid request, funds realisation, cut-off rules and the transaction channel. A failed bank mandate, incomplete KYC, nomination issue, account mismatch or restricted folio can delay processing. Investors should retain the application confirmation, account statement, folio number, plan and option, allotment date, NAV, units, bank debit, exit-load computation and capital-gains statement. For joint holdings, minors, non-residents, transmission or pledge, additional rules apply. IDCW is not guaranteed income: a distribution reduces NAV to the extent of the payout and applicable taxes, and the trustee declares it only from distributable surplus. Growth and IDCW choices should therefore be evaluated by cash-flow need and tax facts, not by treating IDCW as extra return. Equity mutual fund tax treatment Holding period and capital gains rates As an equity-oriented mutual fund, units held for not more than 12 months are generally short-term capital assets. Where the statutory STT conditions are met, short-term capital gains on transfer are taxed at 20% for transfers on or after 23 July 2024. Long-term capital gains covered by the specified equity provision are taxed at 12.5% on the aggregate covered gain exceeding ₹1.25 lakh, subject to the current conditions. Surcharge and health and education cess can apply. Tax is based on the actual redemption or switch and taxpayer, not on an illustration in a factsheet. Other tax and transaction points STT generally applies on redemption or sale of equity-oriented fund units under the relevant rules. Stamp duty applies to specified purchases. IDCW is generally taxable in the hands of the unitholder at the applicable rate, with withholding where the statutory conditions are met. Set-off and carry-forward of capital losses depend on the nature of the loss, return-filing timelines and other income. Non-residents can face withholding and treaty questions. Investors should not treat an exit-load-free redemption as tax-free or assume that a switch between plans or schemes is ignored for tax.
