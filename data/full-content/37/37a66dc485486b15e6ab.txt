Cumulative forex inflows under the facility reached USD136.4 billion by August 31, with FCNR(B) deposits accounting for USD127.2 billion, or 93% of the total.For banks, the front-loaded deposit gathering has created a substantial low-cost funding base, although the flows have been unevenly distributed. ICICI Bank accounted for 14.1% of industry FCNR(B) flows against a 7.1% deposit share, followed by SBI, HDFC Bank, Axis Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank, according to Nomura. Advt Among mid-tier banks, IDFC First Bank accounted for 2.8% of FCNR(B) flow share against a 1.2% deposit share, while RBL Bank accounted for 2.7% of flows against a 0.5% deposit share.How banks gainThe fresh FCNR(B)-driven liquidity could allow private banks to re-enter lower-yielding but strategically important segments such as mortgages and higher-rated corporate loans. Private banks gained around 20 basis points of loan market share quarter-on-quarter in the first quarter of FY27 for the first time in several quarters, as PSU banks' liquidity buffers dried up and their loan growth decelerated more sharply.Mid-tier banks are expected to see a sharper earnings benefit because both FCNR(B) as a share of deposits and the potential spread on redeployment are higher, with their loan yields sitting well above the cost of FCNR funds. IDFC First Bank has the largest FY28F PBT upgrade at 12%, followed by YES Bank at 7%, IndusInd Bank at 5% and Federal Bank at 4%.The benefit is more muted for PSU banks. SBI's FCNR(B) flow was equivalent to just 2% of its first-quarter FY27 deposits and Bank of Baroda's to 4%, compared with 9-15% for large private peers. Both also carry lower loan yields, limiting their PBT upgrades to 1% each. Advt The large inflow of deposits is likely to result in margin compression for two to three quarters as banks park undeployed FCNR(B) funds in reverse repo at negative carry. However, leverage expansion could offset the drag on return on equity. For a symbolic large private bank, leverage rises from 7.2x to 7.7x, holding RoE at 16.3% even as RoA falls 15 basis points.The uplift from FCNR(B) is tenure-bound rather than permanent. The report therefore values the benefit as an annuity rather than at core banking multiples, with the implied multiple at 2.4x for a three-year tenure and 3.5x for five years at a 13% cost of equity. Advt While FCNR(B) deposits entail hedging costs at maturity, which could escalate in case of further rupee depreciation, the scheme delivers near-term benefits by supporting the balance of payments and helping arrest the currency decline.The FCNR(B) scheme delivered a sharp acceleration in the final 10 days of the window, with deposits surging from USD65.4 billion on August 21 to USD127.2 billion by month-end after the deadline was brought forward on August 14. The mobilisation far exceeded the initial expectation of USD50-60 billion.The RBI's USD-INR forex swap facility, announced on June 8, catalysed USD136.4 billion in cumulative foreign exchange inflows by August 31. OFCBs contributed USD5.3 billion and ECBs USD3.9 billion, with FCNR(B) deposits dominating the inflows. Advt The sharp acceleration in FCNR(B) mobilisation came after the deadline advancement was announced, triggering a late surge in deposits. The RBI has largely achieved its objective of boosting dollar inflows amid capital outflows and high global oil prices.
