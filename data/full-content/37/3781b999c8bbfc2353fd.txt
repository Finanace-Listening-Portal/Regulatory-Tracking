(PTI Photo/Shiva Sharma)(PTI11_27_2026_000211B)Bank of Maharashtra and Canara Bank face higher agricultural loan stress exposure due to concentration in drought-hit states.Agricultural GNPAs have fallen to 5 per cent from 9-10 per cent, with slippages at 1-3 per cent.Gold-backed loans account for 25-75 per cent of agricultural loans at most PSBs and 80-95 per cent at smaller private banks.Restructuring could reach Rs 10,000 crore, with an estimated 1-4 per cent impact on FY27 EPS.India’s deficient 2026 monsoon could expose public sector banks (PSBs) to higher agricultural loan stress than private banks, with Bank of Maharashtra and Canara Bank disproportionately exposed because of their concentration in the agricultural segment. Maharashtra and Karnataka, the worst-hit states, have declared drought across large parts of their territories.India recorded its driest monsoon in over a decade, with rainfall deficit of around 13 per cent. Among private banks, Federal Bank and DCB Bank have exposure to crop loans in Maharashtra and Karnataka. Unlike the stress seen in microfinance, agricultural portfolio performance has remained reasonably strong over the past few years, with slippages of 1-3 per cent and improving gross non-performing assets (GNPAs), according to ICICI Securities. Advt The agricultural GNPA ratio has declined to around 5 per cent from 9-10 per cent a few years ago, although the improvement has lagged the corporate segment. Slippages in the small and marginal farmer (SMF) segment have remained below 2 per cent at select banks, though GNPAs in this segment are slightly elevated.A large proportion of incremental agricultural lending is now backed by gold loans. Banks have consciously shifted their portfolios away from crop loans towards gold loans, self-help groups (SHGs) and other collateral-backed lending following the microfinance crisis in FY25 and FY26.Individual bank performanceNearly 100 per cent of incremental agricultural lending over the past year at Bank of Maharashtra, Karur Vysya Bank and Indian Bank, among others, was gold-backed. Gold loans account for 25-75 per cent of agricultural loans at most PSBs, except Punjab National Bank. At smaller private banks, gold-backed loans account for around 80-95 per cent of agricultural loan portfolios.Select banks, including Indian Bank, Union Bank of India, Bank of Maharashtra and State Bank of India, also have an additional 10-20 per cent exposure to SHGs. Asset quality in these portfolios has held up well.The State Level Bankers' Committee (SLBC) has proposed a restructuring framework for the drought-hit regions of Maharashtra and Karnataka. However, discussions with banks indicate that requests for restructuring have so far been few and far between. State Bank of India estimates that the upper limit for any such restructuring could be around Rs 10,000 crore. Advt ICICI Securities estimates a 1-4 per cent impact on FY27 earnings per share, assuming around 35 per cent of crop loans are restructured and banks make provisions of 15 per cent. Historical experience suggests that state governments have stepped in to alleviate agricultural stress, with Maharashtra being the most recent example.Banks are monitoring the situation, and reasonable risk mitigants exist. However, the key risk is that stress could widen beyond crop loans to the broader agricultural and rural ecosystem.
