Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Oct 15, 2026 at 06:18 PM IST var news_msid = 134968436; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Insurance companies should not be allowed to shift the burden of mis-selling penalties entirely to intermediaries, former IRDAI member (Non-Life) Thomas Devasia has said, challenging a key element of the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India’s (IRDAI) proposed distribution reforms.“The core presumption of commission clawback is that the fault ultimately lies with the intermediary. That is an unfair premise since every case of mis-selling has been to the benefit of the insurer and the intermediary,” Devasia said in an interview with ETBFSI.His comments come as IRDAI proposes stronger safeguards against mis-selling, including tagging policies to individual salespersons, documenting suitability assessments, disclosing mis-selling incidents and recovering commissions in specified cases. Advt Devasia argued that unless insurers and other regulated entities are held accountable for acquiring business through unsuitable sales, the underlying incentives that encourage mis-selling may remain intact.“Unless regulated entities that acquire business that is an outcome of mis-selling are questioned and penalised, the problem of mis-selling will continue to be a model of business acquisition,” he said.Complaint should not automatically become a mis-selling markOn a question by ETBFSI, on what qualifies as a mis-selling report before it goes into a person’s permanent record by IRDAI through a public domain, or what adjudication process is in place for commission clawback cases, Devasia also questioned how mis-selling incidents should be recorded and adjudicated before they enter a salesperson’s permanent record. He said customer grievances already pass through internal insurer processes and regulatory mechanisms, while unresolved disputes may proceed to insurance ombudsmen, consumer forums and courts.He cautioned against treating a complaint itself as conclusive proof of wrongdoing, arguing that the regulator should consider established findings and repeated decisions by adjudicating forums.“What the regulator could do as minimum reform is to categorise declinatures of claims, including partial ones, across all lines of business, based on prior repeat decisions of adjudicating forums, much akin to the principle of judicial precedent,” Devasia said.The proposed public disclosure of mis-selling incidents raises questions about the evidentiary threshold, the process for correcting disputed records and the distinction between an allegation and a confirmed violation. Advt Devasia’s position is that the framework should focus on demonstrable conduct and accountability across the distribution chain, rather than placing the entire burden on the individual who sold the policy.Accountability must extend beyond the point of saleThe consultation paper seeks to make suitability an enforceable obligation, including documented needs assessments for certain life insurance sales. It also proposes stronger safeguards against compulsory bundling and other forms of mis-selling.Devasia’s argument is that these safeguards need to address the commercial incentives of the insurer as well as the conduct of the intermediary. Advt His criticism also raises a broader question for the regulator: if commissions are clawed back from the seller but the insurer retains the benefit of the premium and the acquired business, does the penalty adequately address the underlying incentive?IRDAI’s proposals remain open for stakeholder feedback until October 25.
