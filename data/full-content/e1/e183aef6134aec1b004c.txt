(CESTAT Delhi) Summary: CESTAT Delhi allowed the Revenue’s appeal against the appellate relief granted to M/s. AGC Reality Private Ltd., a provider of construction of residential complex services undertaking the “Homes-121” project at Noida. The investigation concerned alleged unaccounted cash collections from flat buyers. During a search on 21 January 2014, DGCEI officers recovered six pen drives from Assistant Manager (Accounts) Shri Inder Kumar Gupta. The Department alleged that the retrieved records disclosed cash receipts of ₹17,47,15,000 during June 2011 to January 2014. Gupta explained the entries, signed the printouts and subsequently reaffirmed his statement; Director Shri Deepak Kapoor also confirmed its contents. The assessee deposited ₹25 lakh during investigation. Paragraph 1.5 records a service tax demand of ₹50,77,000 on the alleged cash receipts and a further demand of ₹50,49,750 based on differential flat prices. The order also records a follow-up demand of ₹11,36,830 for 2014-15, with interest and penalties. The Commissioner (Appeals) had set aside the adjudication order for want of corroborative evidence, including buyers’ statements, bank details and examination of financial records. Revenue argued that the electronic records, admissions by the employee and Director, and voluntary deposit sufficiently established the liability. It relied on authorities concerning the evidentiary value of admissions and documentary records, including Tulip Lamkraft Pvt. Ltd. The assessee disputed both the alleged cash receipts and the Department’s extrapolation of flat prices. It submitted that no cash was recovered, no buyer confirmed any cash payment and no money trail was established. It relied on buyers’ affidavits, agreements showing discounts and the inherent variability of real estate prices. It also contended that additions based on the same DGCEI material had failed in income-tax proceedings for Assessment Years 2012-13 to 2014-15, and challenged the invocation of the extended limitation period. The Tribunal held that the Commissioner (Appeals) had wrongly treated the statements as promptly retracted. Gupta’s initial statement dated 21 January 2014 had been reaffirmed on 19 December 2014, approximately eleven months later, and the Director had confirmed it on that date. The purported retraction appeared only in the reply to the show cause notice issued on 10 June 2015. The Tribunal regarded that delayed retraction as an afterthought. Applying Section 106 of the Indian Evidence Act, it reasoned that the employee maintaining the accounts had exclusive knowledge of the pen-drive files and had explained their contents. The signed printouts constituted documentary material additional to the statements; the absence of corresponding entries in regular books or bank records did not negate the separately maintained cash records. The Tribunal discussed statements recorded under Section 14 of the Central Excise Act, made applicable to service tax through Section 83 of the Finance Act, 1994, and emphasised that such statements must be voluntary and true. Referring to Vinod Solanki Vs. Union of India and K. I. Pavunny, it considered the reaffirmed admissions together with the recovered and signed electronic records sufficient to establish the allegations. It set aside the Commissioner (Appeals)’ order, restored the Additional Commissioner’s order and allowed Revenue’s appeal. Although the assessee’s objections concerning extrapolation, the income-tax proceedings and extended limitation are recorded, the operative reasoning principally addresses the evidentiary material and delayed retraction without separately resolving each objection. FULL TEXT OF THE CESTAT DELHI ORDER Revenue has filed this appeal to assail the Order dated18.6.2018 passed by the Commissioner (Appeals) setting aside the order dated 12.6.2018 passed by the Additional Commissioner in which he decided the proposals made in the Show Cause Notice (SCN) dated 10.6.2015 covering the period 2010-11 to 2013-14 and the follow up Statement of Demand dated 19.5.2016 (covering the period 2014-15) issued to M/s. AGC Realty, the respondent herein. With respect to the first SCN, the Additional Commissioner had confirmed demand of service tax of Rs. 50,77,00/- for the period June 2011 to Jan 2014 and service tax of Rs. 50,49,750/- for the period 1.7.2010 to 31.3.2014 under the proviso to section 73(1) of the Finance Act, 1994 (the Act) on the amounts said to have been received by the appellant in cash. She appropriated an amount of Rs. 25,00,000/- paid by the respondent during investigation, confirmed demand of appropriate interest and imposed penalties. With respect to the follow up Statement of Demand, the Additional Commissioner confirmed demand of service tax of Rs. 11,36,830/- along with interest and penalties. 1.1 On appeal by the respondent herein, the Commissioner (Appeals) set aside the order of the Additional Commissioner holding that the demand was unsustainable in the absence of any record/evidence and statements of buyers, examination of bank details, balance sheets, cash transaction to say the least. He further said that the statements of the Assistant Accounts Manager cannot be the only basis to work out details on some hypothesis which is not even put to any test for verification. 1.2 M/s. AGC Reality Pvt. Ltd., the respondent-assessee were engaged in providing taxable services of construction of residential complex and thus were registered with the service tax Commissionerate Delhi-I. Intelligence received in the Directorate General of Central Excise Intelligence Hqrs. New Delhi (hereinafter referred to as ‘DGCEI’) indicated that respondent-assessee were collecting a part of the amount in cash from their customers against booking of residential flats in their project ‘Homes-121’, Noida (UP), but were not accounting for the same in their statutory records and were not paying service tax on such un-accounted cash receipts. The intelligence further indicated that the said details of un-accounted cash receipts were maintained by Shri Inder Kumar Gupta, an employee of respondent-assessee, in their office. 1.3 On the basis of above intelligence, the office of the respondent-assessee was searched by the officers of DGCEI on 21.01.2014. Six pen drives were recovered from the possession of Shri Inder Kumar Gupta and were examined at the office of respondent-assessee in the presence of said Shri Inder Kumar Gupta under Panchnama dated 21.01.2014. Print outs of the relevant pages were taken out in his presence only on which Shri Inder Kumar Gupta put his dated signature. Regarding the data available in the pen drives, Shri Inder Kumar Gupta stated that he was working as Assistant manager (Accounts) with respondent-assessee and was looking after accounts and administration work of the company. 1.4 Scrutiny of the data retrieved on 21.01.2014 and 15.10.2014 from the pen drives recovered from the possession of Shri Inder Kumar Gupta, revealed that respondent-assessee had collected an amount of Rs.17,47,15,000/- in cash from various customers of their residential project “Homes-121” at Noida during the period from June, 2011 to January, 2014 (up to 20.01.2024) towards the basic cost of residential flats and the said amount of receipt in cash was not accounted for nor reflected in their books of account so as to avoid the payment of service tax. Shri Inder Kumar Gupta further confirmed that the amount mentioned against the flat no. and the name of the buyers, was rupees in Lacs. For example, where the amount 2.00 was mentioned, it was actually Rs.2,00,000/- and likewise. He admitted the liability of service tax on this amount and an amount of Rs.25.00 Lac was voluntarily deposited by respondent-assessee towards their anticipated service tax liability vide e-receipt dated 24.01.2014 and the same was intimated to DGCEI, New Delhi vide their letter dated 27.01.2014. Shri Inder Kumar Gupta further confirmed that respondent-assessee had collected additional charges towards PLC-Preferential Location charges (corner, location, park, floor and Road), car parking, EEC (external electrification charges), FFG (firefighting charges), club membership and power backup from some of the customers and were paying service tax on all the said additional charges at the full rate i.e. 12.36% or 10.30%, as applicable. 1.5 In order to arrive at the service tax liability on the alleged unaccounted cash amounting to Rs.17,47,15,000/-, the details submitted by respondent-assessee vide their letter dated 26.12.2014 related to customer wise booking of 1601 No. of flats, under different payment plans, were scrutinized. For the purpose of arriving at the basic price, 841 flats sold during the period 01.07.2010 to 31.03.2014 were segregated and grouped under three separate categories on the basis of payment plans viz. (i) Down Payment Plan (466 flats), (ii) Flexi Payment plan (257 Flats) and (iii) Construction Link Payment plan (78 Flats). Having heard the rival contentions and perusing the entire records, it is observed and held as follows: 5.1 This case was initiated by DGCEI Hqrs., New Delhi, on receiving information that M/s. AGC Reality Pvt. Ltd. was collecting a part of the payment in cash from customers of residential flats in their ‘Homes-121’ project which was not being accounted for in the statutory records, and service tax was not paid on these un-accounted receipts, indicating an intention to evade tax. A search operation recovered documents, electronic gadgets, and six pen drives from Shri Inder Kumar Gupta, the Assistant Manager (Accounts) of the assessee. There is no dispute that the pen drives were recovered under a Panchnama from the respondent assessee. 5.2 The pen drives had several Excel sheets and files which could, evidently have been explained by Shri Inder Kumar Gupta and he did so in his statement. Printouts of the files were also taken and were signed by Shri Gupta. 5.3 Scrutiny of the retrieved data revealed that the assessee had collected Rs.17,47,15,000/- in cash from various customers towards the basic cost of residential flats, which was not reflected in their books of account. It was also found that respondent had sold a number of flats at abnormally low prices which varied from customer to customer, even under the same payment plan and period. 5.4 In his statement, Shri Inder Kumar Gupta said that the Director of the company was Shri Deepak Kapoor and that he (Inder Kumar Gupta) reports to Shri Deepak Kapoor and to the chartered accountant Shri Navratan Tater. Statement of Shri Deepak Kapoor was also recorded in which he affirmed the statement of Shri Inder Kumar Gupta. The statements recorded during the investigation and relied upon the SCN were as follows: a) Statement of Shri Inder Kumar Gupta on 21.1.2014 (RUD-2) in which he explained the contents of the pen drives and signed printouts of the files of the pen drive (RUD-3). b) Statement of Shri Inder Kumar Gupta on 19.12.2014 (RUD-6) in which he affirmed his earlier statement dated 21.1.2014. c) Statement of Shri Deepak Kapoor on 19.12.2014 (RUD-7) in which he agreed with the contents of statement of Shri Inder Kumar Gupta. 5.5 The SCN was issued on 10.6.2015. The Commissioner (Appeals) recorded that the assessee had retracted the statements in the reply to the SCN. Learned counsel for the respondent has, in his synopsis submitted that ‘the statements were recorded under coercion and were retracted at the earliest opportunity’. We find this finding of the Commissioner (Appeals) and the submission of the learned counsel are not correct. The details of the statements listed above would show that the first statement of Shri Inder Kumar Gupta was recorded on 21.1.2014. If it was recorded under coercion, it was incumbent on him to have retracted it immediately thereafter. He should have also explained as to what the files in the pen drives pertained to because they were recovered from him and he was maintaining the accounts and therefore, had the exclusive knowledge of the contents of the files in the pen drives. Not only did he not retract the statement immediately, he reaffirmed the statement in his subsequent statement recorded after eleven months on 19.12.2014. In his statement dated 21.1.2014, Shri Inder Kumar Gupta also said that Shri Deepak Kapoor was the Director of the respondent. Shri Deepak Kapoor has also in his statement dated 19.12.2014, confirmed the statement of Shri Inder Kumar Gupta. 5.6 The Commissioner (Appeals) recorded that the statements were retracted in the reply to the SCN. Needless to say that the reply could have only been filed after the SCN was issued on 10.6.2015, i.e., after six months of the second statement. Any retraction in such reply cannot be called anything other than an afterthought. The Commissioner (Appeals) committed an error in taking cognizance of such afterthought as a retraction. 5.7 It must also be noted that as per section 106 of the Indian Evidence Act, the burden of proving a fact within the exclusive knowledge of a person will rest on such person. It reads as follows: 106. Burden of proving fact especially within knowledge. When any fact is especially within the knowledge of any person, the burden of proving that fact is upon him. Illustrations (a) When a person does an act with some intention other than that which the character and circumstances of the act suggest, the burden of proving that intention is upon him. (b) A is charged with travelling in a railway without a ticket. The burden of proving that he had a ticket is on him. 5.8 It is undisputed that the pen drives were recovered from the respondent and Shri Inder Kumar Gupta who was maintaining the accounts. So, he had exclusive knowledge of the contents of the files in the pen-drives and he explained them in his statement dated 21.1.2014. He re-affirmed the statement in his subsequent statement eleven months later. The Director Shri Deepak Kapoor also affirmed his statement. These statements made by persons who had exclusive knowledge of the content of the pen drives was wrongly ignored by the Commissioner (Appeals). 5.9 The other reason for the Commissioner (Appeals) to set aside the order of the Additional Commissioner is that the cash receipts were not recorded in the books of accounts, etc. and that there was no other evidence. We note that if the cash is recorded in the books of accounts and bank statements, it will not be a cash transaction at all. Cash was collected and separate records were maintained flat wise. These records were in the pen drives and the person who maintained them explained the files and re-affirmed them after eleven months and the Director of the firm also affirmed the statement. 5.10 The Commissioner (Appeals) statement that there was no other evidence is also not correct because the printouts of the pen drive were taken and they were also signed by Shri Inder Kumar Gupta. 5.11 As per section 14 of the Central Excise Act, 1944 as made applicable to service tax matters by section 83 of the Finance Act, 1994, a central excise officer is empowered to summon any person either give to evidence or to produce documents specified under the summon which are under the possession or control of the summoned person. A duty is cast upon summoned person to the state the truth or produces the document or other things as required by the Central Excise Officers. The duty is to tell the truth is not fettered by any limitation or prohibition described in law of evidence. So once the maker of the statement made the statement, it has to be red in the proceeding before the department or the appellate stage. The only rider is that the statement given is voluntary and true. In fact the officers when record statement under section 14 usually explains the maker of the statement regarding voluntariness and further explain that it may be read against the maker of the statement during the departmental adjudication and prosecution. The cardinal principle is the statement has to be voluntary and it has to be true and if either of the elements is missing then the statement has to be discarded or proved otherwise. 5.14 Though the statement recorded under Section 14 of Central Excise Act is the part of evidence only if it is voluntary and free from any sought of pressure and though the appellant herein had retracted the said statement hitting the veracity thereof. Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Vinod Solanki Vs. Union of India reported 2009 (233) ELT 157 (SC) has held that the evidences brought on record by way of confession which stood retracted, if substantially corroborated by other independent and cogent evidences, the relevance thereupon can be placed. In the present case, the respondent appeared before the authorities an year after the initial seizure and voluntarily reaffirmed his statement. The silence of respondent for over two years, followed by retraction, marks it as a “calculated afterthought” and a strategic ruse. 5.15 Hon’ble court also observed as below: “We must also noticed that in some of the cases retracted confession has been used as a piece of corroborative evidence and not as the evidence on the evidence basis whereof alone a judgment of conviction and sentence has been recorded. See Ponadithan Vs. Narcotics Control Bureau Madras (1999) 6 SCC 1. 5.16 Hon’ble Apex Court in K I PAVUNNY VS. Assistant Collector (HQ) Central Excise Collectorate, Cochin reported 1997 (3) SCC 721 Hon’ble Supreme Court while holding that for a retraction to be legally potent, it must be contemporaneous, has recorded as below: “It is the duty of the court to look into the facts and circumstances of each case to find whether the confession was voluntary. If the court is satisfied that the confession was voluntary and true, it can form the basis of conviction.” 5.17. As already observed above that the retraction of the appellant is a delayed act and is an afterthought. We believe that the earlier statements about receiving cash from the buyers of the flat in the statement of Shri Inder Kumar Garg recorded under Section 14 of the Central Excise Act is the cogent evidence proving the allegations leveled by the appellants. 5.18 Further, it is also the settled principle of Law of Evidence that documentary evidence prevails over the oral one. In this case, in addition to the statement of Shjri Inder Kumar Gupta there are the print out extracted from the pen drives recovered from said Shri Inder Kumar Gupta which got retrieved in presence of Shri Inder Kumar Gupta himself in the office of the respondent-assessee. Those documents sufficiently prove the allegations of show cause notice as was held by the Additional Commissioner. 6. In light of the above discussion, we hold that Commissioner (Appeals) has committed an error while setting aside the order confirming the demand of service tax. The impugned order is therefore set aside and the order of the Additional Commissioner is therefore, restored. Consequent thereto, the appeal of the department is hereby allowed.
