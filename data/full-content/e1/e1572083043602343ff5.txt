SpaceX gets approval for a million-satellite networkSpaceX says its proposed orbital data centres would use near-constant solar power to process data for use on Earth, as demand for AI computing risesSpaceX has received US regulatory approval to deploy up to 1 million orbital compute modules for data centres in space under its Starmind constellation. (Photo: Reuters)Barkha Mathur New Delhi 3 min read Last Updated : Nov 26 2026 | 8:17 AM IST @media (min-width:1100px) and (max-width:1280px) { .storymeta .meta-info { flex: 0 0 45%; } } Listen to This ArticleThe US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) authorised Elon Musk's SpaceX on Wednesday to deploy up to 1 million orbital computing modules to establish data centres in space. The FCC said in a statement that it granted SpaceX permission to establish its proposed 'Starmind' constellation, which will process large volumes of data for use on Earth. The approval comes amid growing demand for computing infrastructure to support artificial intelligence (AI) and what the agency described as the emerging era of superintelligence (SI). “Compute campuses or data centers are essential to sustaining America’s leadership in SI. By moving compute capacity beyond Earth, SpaceX and the many competitors working on their own orbital data centers are meeting the moment. This is the kind of American ingenuity that can unite our strengths in space and advanced technology, reinforce our economic leadership, and expand prosperity here at home,” FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said in the statement. According to a Reuters report, under the licence, SpaceX must begin deployment by November 2033, launch at least 100,000 satellites, or 10 per cent of the authorised total, by November 2035, and deploy 50 per cent of the constellation by November 2038.
