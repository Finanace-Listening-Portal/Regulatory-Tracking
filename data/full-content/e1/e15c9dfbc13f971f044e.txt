Articles 4 min read The Art of Lending Is Knowing When to Let Go For India’s salaried borrowers, a loan can help meet an expense that cannot wait until payday. But when circumstances change, the terms of loan closure matter just as much. Capital Now’s incentive on foreclosure policy addresses this part of the borrowing journey. While access to credit has become faster, customers also want the freedom to repay when their circumstances improve. This brings an important question into focus: how can lending become more inclusive?Convenience at the beginning is only one part of the experience. A borrower also needs to understand how the commitment will fit into their life over time. From choosing a manageable EMI to knowing what happens when they can repay sooner, each stage influences how comfortable they feel about borrowing.A more personalised approach can help meet this need. Understanding a borrower involves looking beyond their monthly salary to consider their wider financial circumstances. For borrowers with irregular income, multiple sources of earnings or financial circumstances that do not fit a standard template, this can create opportunities that conventional assessments may overlook.The same understanding matters after approval. A customer’s finances can change during the repayment period, creating new priorities or making earlier repayment possible. Capital Now’s incentive on foreclosure policy supports an easy exit strategy, allowing borrowers to close their loans sooner without an additional foreclosure charge. This flexibility helps make the borrowing experience more supportive, from accessing funds to repaying the loan.When Life Changes Before the Loan EndsConsider Rohit, an illustrative borrower whose situation reflects experiences many customers describe. At 29, he had a steady job and used his salary to manage rent, household expenses and financial support for his parents. When an urgent family requirement exceeded his savings, he took a personal loan.Rohit chose an EMI that fit his monthly budget and planned to repay the loan over the agreed tenure. This helped him meet an immediate need without having to fund the entire expense from his savings.For him, the decision was about meeting a family responsibility while keeping everyday expenses manageable. He expected the EMI to remain part of his budget until the loan ended.Several months later, a family member passed away unexpectedly.Once the family’s affairs were settled, Rohit received an inheritance. The money came at a difficult time and was not an inflow he had expected. As he reviewed his responsibilities, he decided to use part of it to repay his outstanding loan. Closing the account would remove the monthly EMI and give him one less financial commitment to manage.When Exit Conditions MatterHowever, when Rohit began the loan closure process, he learned that he would have to pay a foreclosure fee charged for closing the loan ahead of schedule.He paid every instalment on time and now had enough to clear the remaining balance. What he hadn’t anticipated was an extra charge for repaying before the agreed end date.The charge complicated what Rohit had hoped would be a simple decision. Beyond the outstanding balance, he now had to weigh the cost of exiting the loan early. Borrowing had been his choice, but was walking away still one?His experience shows why borrowers need to understand a loan’s closure terms. A repayment schedule may stay the same even when personal circumstances change. For customers ready to repay, this raises a straightforward question: why should closing a loan early come with a penalty?A Policy Built Around the CustomerCapital Now addresses this need through an inclusive foreclosure policy built around a simple belief: credit should be easy to enter and even easier to exit. While borrowing should be simple when people need money, repaying it early should be just as straightforward if their circumstances improve.With this approach, their customers are not just free to close their loans early without penalties; they are actively incentivised to do so. By offering discounts on early repayment, they can reduce their overall borrowing costs and pay less than they would have otherwise.Whether they receive a bonus, an inheritance or simply find themselves in a better financial position, repaying early becomes a financially rewarding choice.For someone like Rohit, this flexibility can make a meaningful difference. Financial circumstances change, and credit should adapt to those changes rather than hold people back. Capital Now aims to make borrowing a simpler experience, giving customers the freedom to enter the credit cycle when they need to and exit it when they are ready.What It Signals for the IndustryBorrowers today increasingly look for convenience throughout the loan journey. Quick access to funds is important, but so are manageable repayment terms and the flexibility to close the account early. Clear, customer-focused policies can help build trust, while rigid conditions and exit penalties can weaken it.Capital Now’s approach is guided by three principles.1. Financial needs differ from person to person, and lending solutions should reflect those differences.2. Creditworthiness involves more than salary alone, making a customer’s wider financial profile important.3. Flexibility should also continue throughout the loan lifecycle, from the initial application to foreclosure.As borrowers compare their options, lenders that understand their changing needs may become a preferred choice. This approach brings together speed, flexibility and fairness, supporting customers when they need credit and when they are ready to repay it and close their loan comfortably.(Disclaimer: The above content is non-editorial, and TIL hereby disclaims any and all warranties, expressed or implied, relating to it, and does not guarantee, vouch for or necessarily endorse any of the content.)
