Articles 2 min read SBI identifies Rs 10,000 Cr agri loan pool for restructuring across three drought hit states; uptake likely to be limited State Bank of India has identified a potential agricultural loan portfolio of around Rs 10,000 crore for restructuring across three states that have declared drought, with the actual uptake depending on farmers opting for the facility and giving their consent. Chairman C S Setty said the bank's past experience suggests that not many borrowers avail of such restructuring. However, the identified amount is a potential pool and not the quantum of loans already restructured.SBI Chairman C S Setty said the actual uptake would depend on farmers coming forward and consenting to the restructuring. Advt “Our past experience shows that not many come for the restructuring,” Setty said, adding that SLBCs are required to provide a restructuring dispensation after a natural calamity such as drought or floods is declared.The committees lay out the package available to borrowers affected by the calamity, but the number of farmers who eventually avail of the facility depends on their response. “Many people may come, may not come. Based on our past experience, not many people avail the restructuring,” he said.SBI's agricultural advances stood at Rs 4,51,438 crore as of September 2026, compared with Rs 3,68,112 crore a year earlier, registering a growth of 22.64 per cent. The segment accounted for 10.31 per cent of the bank's domestic advances, according to its Q2 FY27 investor presentation.The potential restructuring pool of Rs 10,000 crore is therefore a limited portion of the bank's overall agricultural loan book and should not be read as the quantum of stressed or non-performing agricultural loans.Agricultural gold loan portfolio remains soundA senior SBI official said the bank's agricultural gold loan portfolio continued to remain of good quality, supported by conservative loan-to-value (LTV) ratios of around 60-61 per cent.The official said the proportion of agricultural gold loans exceeding the regulatory LTV threshold of 85 per cent was very low, and that the portfolio's asset quality remained sound. Advt Nuvama's report also noted that agricultural gold loans continued to remain of good quality, with conservative LTV ratios and very low exposure above the 85 per cent threshold.On the broader asset-quality front, SBI's gross non-performing asset (GNPA) ratio stood at 1.39 per cent as of September 2026, while its net NPA ratio was 0.36 per cent. The bank reported a credit cost of 0.22 per cent and a provision coverage ratio of 74.43 per cent in Q2 FY27.Nuvama said that the bank's SMA 1 and SMA 2 balances had increased around 15 per cent sequentially, largely driven by SMA 1. However, more than half of the increase in SMA 1 had subsequently been recovered and moved back to the standard category.
