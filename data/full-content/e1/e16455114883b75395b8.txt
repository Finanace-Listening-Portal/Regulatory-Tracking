Summary: Canara Robeco Bluechip Equity Fund was renamed Canara Robeco Large Cap Fund with effect from 20 June 2025. The AMC’s addendum states that the rename did not change the scheme’s fundamental attributes, investment objective, asset allocation or terms. It is therefore the same underlying scheme, not a second product or a fresh track record. The current open-ended equity scheme invests predominantly in large-cap stocks and uses the BSE 100 Total Return Index as its benchmark. The September 2026 factsheet reports a permitted 80%–100% allocation to large-cap equity and equity-related instruments, a 1% exit load for redemption or switch-out within one year and no exit load after one year. It also reports base expense ratios of 1.44% for the Regular Plan and 0.48% for the Direct Plan as at the factsheet date; these are variable disclosures, not permanent prices. This article explains the current name, mandate, plans, portfolio evidence, costs, redemption mechanics, risk and Indian equity-oriented-fund tax without recommending the scheme or projecting returns. The addendum expressly says that all other features, including the nature, investment objective, asset allocation pattern, fundamental attributes and terms and conditions, remained unchanged. Existing folios and the pre-rename performance history belong to the same scheme. Creating a second return history from the rename date would therefore be misleading. The scheme was allotted on 20 August 2010. The current September 2026 factsheet identifies Canara Robeco Asset Management Company Limited as manager and Canara Robeco Mutual Fund as the registered mutual fund. Investors should use the current name on applications and search results, while retaining the former name when reconciling older statements, tax lots or mandates. A bank distributor or app does not become the scheme’s investment manager merely because it accepts an order. Other equity and equity-related instruments may occupy 0%–20%. Money-market instruments, other liquid instruments, gold ETFs, silver ETFs and other permissible securities may together occupy 0%–20%, while units issued by infrastructure investment trusts may occupy up to 10%, subject to the operative scheme document. These are ranges, not a promise that the portfolio will always be fully invested in the largest one hundred companies. The large-cap classification follows the SEBI/AMFI market-cap framework and current master circular. Market rankings can change, and the manager may rebalance within regulatory timelines. A large-cap category may have lower company-size risk than a small-cap category, but it still carries equity-market, valuation, sector, issuer, liquidity, policy and concentration risk. The investment objective seeks capital appreciation; the scheme documents expressly state that achievement is not assured. The BSE 100 TRI includes reinvested distributions The Total Return Index form of the benchmark incorporates dividends on the benchmark constituents, unlike a price-only index. It remains an unmanaged reference with no scheme expenses, cash flows or portfolio constraints. A scheme can underperform or outperform for extended periods because of stock selection, sector weights, cash, fees and investor transactions. Benchmark selection is not a forecast and should not be converted into a return target. September 2026 Portfolio Data Is a Dated Snapshot At 30 September 2026, the AMC’s digital factsheet reported 97.70% in listed or awaiting-listing equities. Banks represented 26.31% of NAV, with ICICI Bank and HDFC Bank the two largest disclosed individual holdings at 8.36% and 8.26%. The same snapshot reported month-end AUM of ₹15,962.68 crore. These figures help readers understand concentration on that date but can change through trades, price movement, subscriptions and redemptions. AUM is the value managed by the scheme, not a measure of guaranteed liquidity or investor safety. A large scheme may benefit from scale but may also face deployment and market-impact constraints. Sector and issuer percentages should be read together with the complete monthly portfolio, not only the top holdings. The scheme’s own Riskometer and the benchmark Riskometer are recalculated periodically and may change with portfolio and market conditions. The Regular Plan includes distribution-related expenses and generally has a higher expense ratio; the Direct Plan excludes distributor commission. Because expenses are deducted in the NAV, the plans can develop different NAVs and returns despite identical holdings. A higher NAV does not mean one plan is more expensive to enter; units purchased are simply calculated using the applicable NAV. The September 2026 factsheet reported base expense ratios of 1.44% for the Regular Plan and 0.48% for the Direct Plan. Expense ratios can change within regulatory limits, and the applicable total expense ratio should be checked on the transaction date from the AMC’s disclosure. The factsheet notes that its expense ratio presentation includes GST on investment-management fees. Transaction charges, stamp duty and investor-specific advice costs may be separate. Growth and IDCW options distribute value differently Under a Growth option, distributable surplus remains reflected in NAV until redemption. An Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal option may make a payout or reinvest it when the trustees declare a distribution, but no distribution is assured. The NAV normally falls by the distribution and applicable statutory adjustment. IDCW is not interest and can include a return of the investor’s own capital through the NAV mechanism. Tax and cash-flow needs should be assessed separately. Minimum Transactions and Exit Load Affect Execution The current factsheet states a ₹100 minimum for lump-sum purchase and multiples of ₹1 thereafter. It also states ₹100 minima for the listed SIP, STP and SWP frequencies, subject to current transaction rules. A small minimum does not make the risk small; it only determines order eligibility. The applicable NAV depends on valid application, funds realisation, cut-off rules and the transaction channel. The disclosed exit load is 1% when units are redeemed or switched out within one year from allotment and nil after one year. Each purchase or SIP instalment has its own allotment date. A switch from one plan or scheme to another can be treated as a redemption and fresh purchase, creating exit-load and tax consequences. The load applies to the redemption value under scheme rules; it is not an annual management charge. It is not a probability of loss, a credit rating or a guarantee that volatility will remain within a fixed band. The AMC publishes both scheme and benchmark Riskometers. Investors should read the current month rather than rely on a historical image, because the category can change with concentration, volatility, liquidity and other factors. A large-cap active fund also carries manager and style risk. Portfolio choices may differ substantially from the BSE 100 TRI, and the scheme can hold cash or permitted non-equity instruments. Returns can lag the benchmark after expenses. Behavioural risk arises when an investor buys after strong recent performance or exits during a decline without regard to goals and holding period. None of the portfolio statistics or prior returns establish future performance. Indian Tax Follows Equity-Oriented Fund Rules and Transaction Dates The scheme’s equity allocation ordinarily supports treatment as an equity-oriented fund, but the investor should verify the applicable statutory conditions and transaction. For units transferred on or after 23 July 2024 with Securities Transaction Tax conditions met, short-term capital gain under section 111A is taxed at 20% when the holding period is not more than twelve months. Long-term capital gain under section 112A is taxed at 12.5% on the aggregate covered gain exceeding ₹1.25 lakh in the financial year, when the holding period exceeds twelve months. Surcharge and cess may apply. SIP and switch transactions create lot-by-lot calculations Each SIP instalment receives its own acquisition date and cost. Redemptions are generally matched under the prescribed method, commonly first-in-first-out for mutual-fund units in a folio. A switch between Direct and Regular plans, Growth and IDCW options, or two schemes can realise a gain even when no cash enters the bank account. Exit load reduces redemption proceeds but should be reflected consistently in the computation and statement reconciliation. IDCW is taxable at the investor’s applicable rate IDCW received from a mutual fund is generally included in the investor’s income and taxed at the applicable rate, subject to current withholding provisions and individual facts. It is not exempt merely because the scheme invests in equities. Capital-loss set-off, surcharge, residency, grandfathering of older units and business classification can alter results. The consolidated account statement, capital-gain statement, bank records and tax information statement should be reconciled rather than used in isolation. Frequently Asked Questions 1. No. The allotment date remains 20 August 2010 and the pre-rename record belongs to the same scheme, subject to ordinary disclosure caveats. 3. Must the fund hold only the largest 100 companies? No. The mandate requires 80%–100% in large-cap equity and permits limited other equity and specified instruments within stated ranges. 4. Are the 1.44% and 0.48% expense ratios permanent? No. They are the Regular and Direct base expense ratios reported in the September 2026 factsheet. Current AMC disclosures govern and can change. 5. Does every redemption after one year avoid exit load? The current schedule says nil after one year, but each purchase or SIP instalment has its own allotment date and the operative scheme terms must be checked. 6. Is switching from Regular to Direct tax-free? No. A switch is generally treated as redemption and fresh purchase, so exit load and capital-gains consequences can arise. 7. How are post-23 July 2024 equity-fund gains taxed? Subject to STT and other conditions, short-term gains are taxed at 20%, while long-term gains are taxed at 12.5% on aggregate covered gains above ₹1.25 lakh, plus applicable surcharge and cess. 8. Does large-cap classification mean low risk? No. The scheme remains an equity product exposed to market, valuation, issuer, sector, manager, liquidity and behavioural risks.
