The benchmark 10-year government bond yield rose to 7.035 per cent on September 11, its highest level in more than three months, after expectations of outright bond sales increased. ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Sep 14, 2026 at 08:34 AM IST var news_msid = 134231471; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India will sell government securities worth Rs 1 lakh crore through open market operations over the next two weeks, stepping up liquidity absorption as surplus funds in the banking system rise above Rs 10 lakh crore and short term rates fall below the policy repo rate.The RBI will conduct the first sale of Rs 50,000 crore on September 17, followed by two sales of Rs 25,000 crore each on September 21 and September 28. The securities offered in the first tranche will mature between 2029 and 2032.The move comes after the banking system accumulated a large liquidity surplus following mobilisation of about $127 billion through the special foreign currency deposit scheme. The surplus averaged around Rs 10.25 lakh crore in September and was about Rs 10.4 lakh crore on September 11, compared with an average of Rs 3.67 lakh crore in August and Rs 1.07 lakh crore in July. Advt The excess liquidity has pushed money market rates below the repo rate. The weighted average call rate was around 5.02 per cent against the RBI's 5.25 per cent repo rate, increasing the need for the central bank to absorb some of the surplus funds and keep short term rates aligned with its operating framework.For banks, the OMO sales will have a direct bearing on treasury portfolios as the central bank releases government securities into the market while withdrawing an equivalent amount of banking-system liquidity. The benchmark 10-year government bond yield rose to 7.035 per cent on September 11, its highest level in more than three months, after expectations of outright bond sales increased.Higher bond yields reduce the market value of existing fixed rate securities. The impact on individual banks will depend on the duration and accounting classification of their government securities portfolios, with longer duration holdings generally more sensitive to changes in yields.The OMO sales also come after other liquidity absorption operations drew limited demand. The RBI used variable rate reverse repo operations and dollar-rupee sell-buy swaps during the week, but banks did not absorb the full amounts offered through some of the longer duration operations. The central bank has now turned to outright sales of government securities to withdraw a larger amount of durable liquidity. Advt The additional supply of government securities could put further pressure on bond yields, particularly as the domestic bond market is already facing global yield and oil price pressures. Market expectations put the immediate impact of the Rs 1 lakh crore OMO at around 3-4 basis points on bond yields, although the actual movement will depend on demand from banks and other institutional investors.The OMO does not represent additional government borrowing. The securities being sold are from the RBI's holdings, but their sale increases the supply of government bonds available to the market and transfers liquidity from banks to the central bank. Advt The liquidity build up has been unusually large because of the special foreign currency mobilisation. The $127 billion raised by banks under the scheme increased foreign exchange reserves and resulted in a corresponding build up of rupee liquidity in the banking system.The RBI's liquidity operations are also taking place against a backdrop of higher global bond yields and elevated oil prices. The US 10 year Treasury yield recently approached 5 per cent, while the rise in crude prices has increased concerns over inflation and interest rates. These factors have contributed to pressure on Indian government securities. Advt The benchmark 10 year Indian government bond yield has risen sharply in recent weeks. The 7.035 per cent level recorded on September 11 came after a rise of 6 basis points during the day, while the five year yield rose as much as 10 basis points to 6.6222 per cent.The impactFor banks, the effect of tighter surplus liquidity will extend beyond their investment books. As excess funds are withdrawn, short term money market conditions can firm up from the unusually low levels created by the FCNR(B) inflows. This could affect the cost of funds for banks and the pricing of short duration assets.At the same time, the withdrawal of liquidity is intended to prevent overnight rates from remaining materially below the policy rate. The RBI has indicated that foreign exchange intervention, reserve requirements and other liquidity management tools remain available as the banking system adjusts to the large inflows.The scale of the OMO also marks a shift towards outright bond sales after the recent reliance on shorter term liquidity operations and foreign exchange swaps. The RBI last sold government bonds in the secondary market in September 2024, while scheduled auction based sales were last conducted in 2014.The Rs 1 lakh crore operation will therefore simultaneously withdraw a substantial amount of surplus cash from banks and place additional government securities with the market. Its immediate impact will be reflected in banking-system liquidity and bond yields, while the effect on individual bank treasury portfolios will depend on their holdings, duration and accounting classification.
