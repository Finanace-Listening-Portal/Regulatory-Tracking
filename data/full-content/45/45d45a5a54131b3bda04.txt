Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Ajay Singh Vs DCIT (ITAT Jaipur) Section 153C: Completing Assessment Within Time Cannot Cure Reopening of an Assessment Year Outside the Permissible Period The controversy The Jaipur Tribunal quashed an assessment under Section 153C for AY 2016-17, holding that the assessment year fell outside the permissible period for proceedings against the assessee. The Revenue defended the assessment by demonstrating that it had been completed within the deadline under Section 153B. The Tribunal held that this did not answer the assessee’s objection. The period within which an assessment must be completed and the assessment years which can lawfully be reopened are separate questions. Compliance with the former cannot supply jurisdiction where the latter requirement is not satisfied. Search on developer led to alleged on-money addition A search under Section 132 was conducted on the Friends SPS Group, Kota, on 1 August 2019. According to the Revenue, the material found and the subsequent investigation disclosed receipt of unaccounted cash consideration from purchasers of flats in the Royal Palm project. The assessee had purchased Flat No. B-602 under an agreement dated 21 July 2015. The agreement consideration was ₹37 lakh, whereas the Revenue alleged that the actual consideration was ₹57 lakh. The difference of ₹20 lakh was treated as cash paid from undisclosed sources. Notice under Section 153C was issued on 13 October 2023. The assessment was completed on 23 March 2024, making an addition of ₹20 lakh under Section 69. The Commissioner (Appeals) sustained the assessment and addition. Assessee challenged the permissible assessment-year range Before the Tribunal, the assessee raised the legal objection that AY 2016-17 was beyond the years assessable under Section 153C. He relied upon Section 153C, its first proviso and the provisions of Section 153A. His submission was that, for a person other than the searched person, the relevant starting point was the date on which the seized material was received by the Assessing Officer having jurisdiction over that other person. The assessee also submitted that the alleged escaped income was ₹20 lakh and that the conditions for extending proceedings beyond the ordinary six-year period had not been satisfied. The Tribunal sought the Revenue’s response and granted time for an Assessing Officer’s report. Supreme Court’s Jasjit Singh ruling applied The Tribunal referred to CIT v. Jasjit Singh, (2025) 476 ITR 157 (SC). As explained in the order, the Supreme Court held that the proviso to Section 153C was relevant not merely to abatement of pending assessments, but also to reckoning the six-year assessment period for the other person. The statutory reference to receipt of seized material by that person’s Assessing Officer therefore assumed importance in determining the permissible assessment years. The assessee’s case was that the notice date, 13 October 2023, represented the relevant handover date. On that footing, the corresponding assessment year was AY 2024-25, and the six preceding years were AYs 2018-19 to 2023-24. AY 2016-17 consequently fell outside that range. Revenue answered the completion deadline instead The Assessing Officer’s report stated that information had been passed to the jurisdictional Assessing Officer on 17 January 2023. However, its substantive defence focused on Section 153B. It argued that the assessment, completed on 23 March 2024, was within the asserted completion deadline of 31 March 2024. The Tribunal found that the report did not address the assessee’s actual argument concerning the assessment years open to proceedings under Section 153C. It expressly distinguished Section 153B’s completion deadline from the provisions governing which assessment years could be assessed. The defence therefore failed to meet the jurisdictional objection. Notice lacked authority and assessment was void The Tribunal recorded that the assessee’s factual and legal position concerning the permissible assessment period remained uncontroverted before it. It held that the Section 153C notice for AY 2016-17 had been issued without authority of law. Consequently, the Assessing Officer’s assumption of jurisdiction was invalid. The assessment was quashed as void ab initio, and the assessee’s jurisdictional ground was allowed. Since the assessment itself had been annulled, the Tribunal did not examine the remaining objections concerning the alleged on-money payment, the supporting spreadsheet, cross-examination or the year of the alleged investment. The appeal was allowed on the jurisdictional ground. Author’s comments The decision highlights a distinction that can be decisive in search-related assessments: an assessment completed before its deadline may still concern a year which the Assessing Officer had no power to reopen. The record also contains different dates concerning transmission of information and the assessee’s asserted handover date. Although the Tribunal accepted the jurisdictional objection, the precise receipt date should be established from the relevant records when applying this decision elsewhere. The useful principle is therefore to examine both the permissible assessment-year range and the completion deadline independently. A defence addressing only the latter leaves the former unanswered. Cases Discussed CIT Vs Jasjit Singh (Supreme Court), (2025) 476 ITR 157 — Proviso to Section 153C governs the starting point for the six-year period in the case of a person other than the searched person. It was also pointed out that the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of CIT vs Jasjit Singh (2025) 476 ITR 157 had interpreted the provisions of section 153C r.w first proviso and held that for the purposes of section 153C of the Act the date on which the six year assessment period is to be reckoned in the case of the other person is governed by the statutory proviso referring to the date on which the seized material is received by the Assessing Officer having jurisdiction over that person. Our attention was drawn to the relevant portion of the order of the Hon’ble apex court holding as above : ……. 9. It is evident on a plain interpretation of Section 153C(1) that the Parliamentary intent to enact the proviso was to cater not merely to the question of abatement but also with regard to the date from which the six year period was to be reckoned, in respect of which the returns were to be filed by the third party (whose premises are not searched and in respect of whom the specific provision under Section 153-C was enacted. The revenue argued that the proviso [to Section 153(c)(1)] is confined in its application to the question of abatement. ……. 10. The facts of the case, it was pointed out to us wasthat notice u/s 153C of the Act was issued to the assessee on 13-10-2023 which is to be considered the date on which the documents pertaining to the assessee were handed over by the AO of the searched person to the AO of the assessee. Therefore as per Section 153C of the Act read with first proviso thereto, 13.10.2023 was to be treated as the date of initiation of search on the assessee, pertaining to A.Y 2024-25. That as per the provisions of Section 153C only six assessment years prior to the year in which the search action was initiated on the assessee could be assessed. That accordingly only assessment years A.Y. 2018-19 to A.Y. 2023-24 could have been reopened as per the extant provisions of the law, while the impugned assessment year before us was AY 2016-17. That therefore, the assessment in the present case was beyond the scope of power of the AO to reopen/ take up for assessment u/s 153C of the Act. 11. The above facts and position of law have remained uncontroverted before us. The AO’s report furnished by the Ld. DR before us, we find,contains no comment on the arguments made by the ld. Counsel for the assessee. On the contrary it only refers to the provisions of Section 153B of the Act, which are in relation to the time limit for completion of assessments u/s 153A of the Act and have nothing to do with the assessments prescribed as per law to be considered for assessment y/s 153C of the Act. 12. In view of the above, since the facts and the position of law before us have remained uncontroverted, the argument of the ld. Counsel for the assessee is found to be correct. Accordingly the notice issued u/s 153C of the Act for the impugned year before us is held to be issued without authority of law. The jurisdiction assumed by the AO therefore to frame assessment u/s 153C of the Act for A.Y 2016-17, is held to be invalid and the assessment order passed as a consequenceis quashed as void ab initio. 13. Ground No.1 raised by the assessee is allowed. 14. Since we have quashed the assessment order passed, the other grounds of appeal raised by the assessee are not being dealt with by us, as they are mere academic in nature . 15. In effect, the appeal of the assessee is allowed in above terms.
