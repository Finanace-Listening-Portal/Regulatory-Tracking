Summary: A representation dated 25 November 2026 submitted by S. Prasad and Co. to the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) seeks safeguards for genuine recipients under the proposed amendments to Sections 16(2), 41 and 155 and insertion of Section 16A of the CGST Act, 2017. Referring to the draft agenda note forwarded through Office Memorandum bearing File No. CBIC-20006/77/2026-GST, the representation welcomes measures against fraudulent invoicing but emphasises the need to distinguish bona fide purchasers from persons knowingly participating in fraudulent arrangements. It proposes express statutory protection against denial of input tax credit solely because of supplier defaults, subject to fulfilment of applicable eligibility conditions and absence of knowing participation in fraud. The representation recommends initiating recovery proceedings against defaulting suppliers in the first instance and requiring the department to establish the recipient’s knowing involvement through reliable evidence. It further seeks a clear distinction between the recipient’s burden of establishing ITC eligibility and the department’s burden of proving fraud. Genuine bill-to/ship-to transactions, direct dispatch arrangements and subcontracted services should remain protected. Additional recommendations include disclosure of relied-upon documents, personal hearings, cross-examination where appropriate, reasoned adjudication orders and restricting ITC denial to affected transactions. The representation also seeks clarity regarding pending disputes, prospective application of new disqualifications, interest, penalties and mechanisms to prevent duplicate tax recovery. CBIC has been requested to place these suggestions before the Standing Committee of Officers and the GST Council. Respected Sir, We submit this representation for consideration while examining the proposed amendments concerning input tax credit under the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. We welcome the recognition in the draft agenda note that a genuine buyer cannot verify the tax payments and eligibility of input tax credit at every stage of the supply chain. We also support the proposed action against persons who knowingly participate in issuing invoices without an actual supply. The proposed changes should clearly separate a genuine purchaser from a person involved in a fraudulent arrangement. With this objective, we request consideration of the following suggestions. 1. Provide express protection to genuine recipients The draft proposes to delete certain words from Section 16(2)(c), while retaining the requirement concerning payment of tax to the Government. The agenda note also states that payment by the immediate supplier would remain necessary. Consequently, the proposed deletion alone may not fully resolve the difficulties faced by a genuine buyer when the immediate supplier fails to pay tax. A buyer may have received the goods or services, paid the invoice amount together with GST, and maintained the relevant records, but may still have no practical means of ensuring that the supplier deposits the tax. We therefore request an express provision protecting a recipient who establishes the genuineness of the transaction and has not knowingly participated in the supplier’s default or fraud. Suggested wording for consideration: “Input tax credit shall not be denied to a recipient solely on account of non-payment of tax by the supplier, where the recipient establishes receipt of the goods or services and payment of the consideration, including tax, subject to the applicable statutory requirements, unless the recipient’s knowing participation in fraud or collusion is established.” This protection may remain subject to the other applicable conditions for claiming ITC. 2. Require recovery proceedings against the defaulting supplier in the first instance The draft clarifies that the proposed amendments would not affect the department’s powers to recover tax from the defaulting supplier. We request that the law or the accompanying procedure require the department to proceed against the supplier responsible for the default in the first instance. Where action against the recipient is considered necessary, the notice should explain: The specific default and the invoices concerned. The steps taken against the supplier. The evidence connecting the recipient with the alleged wrongdoing. The legal and factual grounds for proceeding against the recipient. The supplier’s failure to respond to a notice, closure of business or cancellation of registration should not, by itself, be treated as proof of the recipient’s involvement. 3. Define the scope of “knowingly” under proposed Section 16A Proposed Section 16A would deny ITC where an investigation establishes knowing assistance, involvement or participation in invoicing without supply, including at an antecedent stage of the connected supply chain. We support action against such conduct. However, the expressions “knowingly assisted,” “involved or concerned in” and “knowingly been a party to” should be supported by clear statutory guidance. We request clarification that: Knowing involvement must be established through specific and reliable evidence. A recipient’s knowledge cannot be presumed merely because a supplier or an upstream supplier committed a default. A lapse in vendor verification should not, by itself, be equated with knowing participation in fake invoicing. The investigation must establish a connection between the recipient’s conduct and the particular fraudulent transaction. This would help ensure that the provision targets deliberate wrongdoing rather than ordinary commercial transactions. 4. Keep the burden of proving fraud separate from the burden of proving ITC eligibility The draft retains the recipient’s burden of proving eligibility for ITC under Section 155 and proposes to refer expressly to Chapter V. A recipient can reasonably be asked to produce records of its own purchases. However, it should not be required to prove that no fraud occurred in transactions between other persons over whom it has no control. We therefore request an express clarification that: “The recipient shall bear the burden of establishing the factual conditions of its claim to input tax credit. The burden of establishing knowing involvement in the conduct specified under Section 16A shall lie on the department.” 5. Preserve genuine third-party delivery and service arrangements The proposed addition of “from the said supplier” in Section 16(2)(b) seeks to address cases where goods are received from one person but the invoice is obtained from another. While supporting this objective, we request that the amendment expressly preserve genuine commercial arrangements involving delivery or performance through another person. These may include: Bill-to/ship-to transactions. Direct dispatch by a manufacturer on a dealer’s instructions. Delivery through an authorised agent or warehouse. Services performed through subcontractors. Other documented arrangements under which the invoicing supplier remains responsible for the supply. Suggested clarification: “Receipt from the said supplier shall include receipt from another person acting on the instructions of, or on behalf of, that supplier, subject to the applicable provisions of this Act.” 6. Provide clear procedural safeguards An investigation finding should not, by itself, become the final determination of the recipient’s ITC entitlement. Before credit is denied under proposed Section 16A, we request that the procedure expressly provide for: A notice identifying the transactions and the recipient’s alleged role. Disclosure of the documents, statements and other material relied upon. Reasonable time to submit a reply. An opportunity for a personal hearing. An opportunity to contest third-party statements relied upon against the recipient, including cross-examination where appropriate. A reasoned adjudication order dealing with the recipient’s explanation and evidence. Any restriction or recovery during the investigation should require separate lawful authority and recorded reasons. Mere initiation of an investigation should not automatically result in denial of credit. 7. Restrict any denial to the affected transactions Where wrongdoing is established in relation to particular invoices, the consequences should ordinarily be confined to the credit connected with those invoices. We request clarification that a finding concerning one transaction or supplier should not automatically affect unrelated purchases or the recipient’s entire ITC claim. 8. Address pending disputes, interest and duplicate recovery The proposal should clearly state its effective date and treatment of past periods. We request consideration of: Extending any beneficial protection for genuine recipients to pending proceedings, through an express provision. Applying any new substantive disqualification prospectively. Issuing a review mechanism for pending demands based solely on upstream defaults. Clarifying the treatment of interest and penalty where the recipient’s transaction is genuine and no knowing involvement is established. Providing an adjustment or restoration mechanism where tax is subsequently recovered from the supplier, so that the same underlying default does not result in duplicate collection. Prayer In view of the above, we respectfully request CBIC to: Incorporate express protection for genuine recipients against supplier defaults beyond their control. Clearly define the evidentiary standard for knowing involvement under proposed Section 16A. Place the burden of proving such involvement on the department. Preserve genuine third-party delivery and service arrangements. Provide uniform procedural safeguards before denial of credit. Clarify the treatment of pending disputes and subsequent recovery from suppliers. Place these suggestions before the Standing Committee of Officers and the GST Council for consideration. The objective should be clear: recover tax from the person responsible for the default, deny credit to persons knowingly involved in fraud, and protect businesses that can establish genuine purchases. We request that these submissions be considered while finalising the proposed amendments. Yours faithfully, For S. PRASAD AND CO Sd/- S.
