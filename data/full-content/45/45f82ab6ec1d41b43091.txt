Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); National Financial Reporting Authority Vs Snehal N Muzoomdar & Anr (Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court disposed of NFRA’s application seeking clarification or modification of its interim order dated 17 February 2025 in the special leave petition arising from the Delhi High Court’s common judgment. The interim order permitted proceedings to continue, without passing final orders, in cases where no Audit Quality Review Report (AQRR) had been prepared and no final order had been passed. It also directed that final orders already passed would not be given effect. NFRA asked the Court to confirm that the directions applied only to the parties to the 11 writ petitions decided by the High Court. The Supreme Court said that no clarification was required because its interim order in the pending petition was already confined to those parties. The order disposed of the interlocutory application and left the merits of the special leave petition pending. Read HC Order in this case: Delhi High Court Upholds NFRA Powers, Quashes Tainted Auditor Proceedings Delhi High Court Judgment Under Challenge Chartered accountants and audit firms challenged Section 132(4) of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rules 3, 8, 10 and 11 of the NFRA Rules, 2018. They sought to invalidate the provisions or have them read down to exclude audits completed before 1 October 2018, when NFRA was constituted. They also challenged the individual show-cause notices and, where issued, final disciplinary orders. Their objections covered firm liability for partners’ conduct, retroactive operation, Article 20(1), differences from the disciplinary safeguards under the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949, and the overlap between audit review and disciplinary decision-making. The High Court upheld Section 132 and the NFRA Rules. It found that the statutory scheme already contemplated liability for both an audit firm and its engagement partners. Section 132(4) adopted the existing meaning of professional or other misconduct under Section 22 of the Chartered Accountants Act. An auditor had no vested right against scrutiny of conduct that already amounted to misconduct before October 2018. The Court treated the proceedings as disciplinary and held that the prohibition in Article 20(1) applies to criminal offences and punishments. The High Court also upheld Rule 11’s summary procedure, which requires compliance with natural justice. It noted the requirements to disclose the allegations and supporting material and to provide an opportunity for a personal hearing where warranted. As the proceedings turned on the audit record rather than a complainant’s oral testimony, the absence of a general right to cross-examine did not render the scheme arbitrary. The applicable standard was the preponderance of probabilities. The Court rejected the challenge to the Rule 3 classification and recorded NFRA’s decision to permit legal assistance to a charged auditor or firm. The challenged proceedings nevertheless failed because the same Executive Body drew up audit review reports recording findings in terms that conveyed finality and then relied on its own conclusions to decide that disciplinary action should begin. Reading Section 132 and the Rules together, the High Court held that a unit separate from the one that prepared the audit review reports must assess independently whether disciplinary proceedings are warranted. The overlap created a reasonable likelihood of bias. The Court rejected the doctrine of necessity because NFRA could constitute separate units. The High Court allowed the 11 writ petitions and quashed the listed show-cause notices and the four final orders identified in its disposition table. NFRA could proceed afresh from the stage of issuing fresh notices. Any decision to do so had to be taken by members unconnected with the review or preparation of the AQRRs, after reassessing the findings as prima facie opinions. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER he present SLP arises out of a common judgment and order passed by the Delhi High Court in a bunch of 11 writ petitions. This is an IA seeking clarification/modification of the interim order dated 17th February 2025 which is reproduced here under : “Issue notice, returnable in the week commencing 28.04.2025. Notice is accepted by Mr. Anshuman Srivastava, learned counsel, who is present on advance notice/caveat for respondent no. 1. Hence, notice need not be served on the said respondent. Respondent no. 2 is a proforma respondent and need not be served. Counter affidavit/reply shall be filed within four weeks from today. Rejoinder affidavit, if any, may be filed within three weeks after service of the counter affidavit/reply. We are informed that the petitioner will be filing special leave petition(s) against the impugned judgment in the context of the other writ petitions which have also been allowed. Without prejudice to the rights and contentions of the petitioner, in cases where there were no Audit Quality Review Reports (AQRR) prepared and no final orders have been passed, the proceedings may continue but no final orders will be passed. We clarify that where final orders have been passed, such orders will not be given effect to.” Mr. N Venkataraman, learned Additional Solicitor General appearing for the petitioner-applicant submits that it may be clarified that the interim order dated 17th February 2025 is confined only to the parties to the 11 writ petitions decided by the common judgment and order of the Delhi High Court impugned in the present SLP. In our considered opinion, there is no need for any clarification. It goes without saying that any interim order passed in the petition pending before this Court relates only to and is confined to the parties to the 11 petitions decided by the High Court vide impugned judgment and order. The application stands disposed of.
