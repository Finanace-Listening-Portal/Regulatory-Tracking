Regular Callable FD Is Different From Platina and Tax-Saver Variants The Regular Fixed Deposit is a resident callable product. The bank’s page says premature and partial withdrawal are permitted. By contrast, the Platina product is described as non-callable, with an amount above ₹1 crore and below ₹3 crore, a 12-to-60-month tenure range and no part-closure, pre-closure or auto-renewal facility. A tax-saving fixed deposit has its own statutory lock-in and tax conditions. Product name and callable status must appear in the booking evidence; a rate from one variant should not be applied to another. The resident Regular FD is also not an NRE or NRO deposit. Non-resident deposits have separate eligibility, tenure and rate tables. A change in residential status may require the customer to notify the bank and redesignate accounts under foreign-exchange rules. This article addresses a resident rupee callable term deposit and does not state the terms for non-residents. Premature Closure Uses the Actual Period, Then the Penalty Rule The bank’s official explanation states that when a callable FD is closed within six months, a 1% penalty is applied to the prevailing rate for the tenure actually completed. Closure after six months does not incur that penalty under the published explanation. That does not mean the depositor keeps the original contracted rate: interest is recalculated using the rate applicable to the actual period held under the bank’s policy. The result can therefore be lower even when the explicit one-percentage-point penalty no longer applies. Illustration of the method, not a bank quotation Assume a one-year deposit is closed after 90 days. The bank determines the applicable 90-day rate under the relevant booking-date schedule and, because closure is within six months, reduces that prevailing rate by one percentage point. It does not merely subtract 1% from the original one-year rate. If closure occurs after nine months, the bank uses the applicable nine-month rate; the published explanation says no separate premature penalty applies after six months. Exact day count, rate card, payout already made and deposit terms can change the settlement, so the authenticated closure quotation should be reviewed before consent. A cumulative deposit retains interest for payment at maturity under the applicable compounding method, while monthly or quarterly payout provides earlier cash flow. A monthly payout is commonly discounted relative to the nominal annual rate because interest is paid sooner. Depositors should compare the exact payout table and maturity amount shown at booking instead of applying the headline cumulative rate to every option. The deposit advice and authenticated account terms are more reliable than a calculator screenshot. Income-Tax Treatment for Tax Year 2026-27 Interest on a resident fixed deposit forms part of taxable income under the applicable provisions and chosen tax regime. TDS is only collection at source; it is not the final tax and it does not make interest exempt when no deduction occurs. For a credit or payment on or after 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies. Section 393(1), Table item 5(ii), provides a ₹1,00,000 threshold for a senior citizen and ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen where the payer is a banking company. The threshold concerns aggregate covered interest, not each deposit receipt. The official transition guidance says rates and thresholds were retained while TDS provisions moved to section 393. The applicable withholding rate for resident bank interest is generally 10% under the rates in force when required, subject to PAN, declarations, certificates and other statutory conditions. Once the threshold is exceeded, deduction applies according to the provision rather than only to the excess over the threshold. Customers should reconcile the bank interest certificate, Form 26AS, annual information statement and return records. Senior status, declarations and deductions are separate questions The senior-citizen product rate, the TDS threshold and any income deduction are distinct rules. A person may qualify for a bank’s senior rate but still need to examine total income, tax regime and declaration eligibility. Forms for non-deduction should be furnished only when the statutory conditions are met, and an invalid or missing PAN can produce a different withholding result. The customer should verify the Income-tax Rules, 2026 and the bank’s current process before interest is credited. DICGC Insurance Aggregates All Same-Capacity Balances DICGC insurance covers principal and accrued interest together up to ₹5 lakh for each depositor in the same right and same capacity at an insured bank. Savings, current, recurring and fixed deposits across all branches of the same bank are aggregated. Opening several FD receipts, using different branches or selecting different maturity dates does not create a separate ₹5 lakh limit for each receipt. Balances held in genuinely different rights and capacities may be treated separately under DICGC rules, and joint-account name combinations and order matter. A sole proprietor’s business balance is aggregated with the proprietor’s individual balance under the published guide. Insurance does not protect interest-rate expectations, guarantee immediate liquidity, cover amounts beyond the ceiling or replace assessment of concentration and access needs. The projected accrued interest should be included when a depositor reviews the insured amount. Booking and Monitoring File Save the official rate table and product terms visible on the booking date.
