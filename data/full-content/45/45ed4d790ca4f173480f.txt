JM Financial Flags 2 Key Factors That Could Decide Its Next MoveEdited by: Priya RaghuvanshiUpdated: Oct 13, 2026, 16:36 ISTJM Financial expects RBI rate hikes of 50-75 bps as food inflation, weak rainfall and elevated crude oil prices threaten India's inflation outlook.Reserve Bank of India (Image Source: iStock)follow usfollow usPersistent increases in food prices and elevated crude oil rates could shape the Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI) monetary policy decisions in the coming months, with further interest rate hikes likely to remain limited, according to domestic brokerage firm JM Financial. In its latest research report, the brokerage projected a relatively moderate rate-hiking cycle of 50-75 basis points, while identifying crude oil prices as a key factor that could influence the extent of monetary tightening. It also stressed the need for timely government intervention to contain food inflation and prevent price pressures from spreading across other sectors of the economy.“At this juncture, we continue to expect a shallow rate hike cycle (50–75bps),” JM Financial said, adding that the trajectory of crude oil prices would determine the extent of further rate increases.India’s retail inflation accelerated to 5.54 per cent in September, compared with 4.82 per cent in August, reaching its highest level since November 2024. Food prices remained the principal contributor to inflation for the fifth consecutive month.Food inflation increased to 7.7 per cent in September, with prices rising 1.3 per cent from the previous month. The brokerage said the trend warrants close monitoring, particularly as inflationary pressures could intensify in the coming quarter.JM Financial noted that average inflation stood at 4.9 per cent in the July-September quarter of FY27, broadly matching the RBI’s projections. However, it expects the rate to climb to 6 per cent in October-December before moderating to 5.7 per cent in the January-March quarter.Weak Rainfall And Kharif Sowing Add To Food Price ConcernsWeather conditions and agricultural trends could further complicate the inflation outlook. JM Financial flagged below-normal rainfall, lower reservoir levels and a decline in kharif sowing as potential risks to food supplies, particularly in rural areas.Pan-India rainfall was 13 per cent below the long-period average as of September 30, while kharif sowing had fallen 1.1 per cent as of October 2.The brokerage cautioned that supply-side measures would be important to prevent higher food prices from feeding into broader inflation. “Despite frontloading of rate hike by the RBI in Oct’26, timely supply intervention should be used to curb food inflation,” the report said.The possibility of El Nino conditions continuing until February 2027, combined with depleted reservoir levels, has also raised concerns about agricultural production and potential effects on the next monsoon season.Price pressures are not restricted to food commodities. Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and fuel components, rose to 4.39 per cent in September.Restaurant and processed food prices have also remained areas of concern. According to JM Financial, restaurant inflation stayed elevated for the sixth consecutive month, partly reflecting supply disruptions associated with the West Asia crisis.Crude Oil Prices Could Influence RBI’s Next MoveJM Financial identified crude oil as a crucial variable for the RBI’s future policy decisions.
