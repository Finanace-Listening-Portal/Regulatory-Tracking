Rajesh Mahajan and CIT, Karnal v. Sangeeta Mahajan, holding that the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal’s factual findings did not warrant interference under Section 260A of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The dispute concerned the Commissioner’s exercise of revisional jurisdiction under Section 263 on the allegation that the Assessing Officer had mechanically accepted the assessees’ returns without adequate scrutiny. The appeals had earlier been decided on 1 February 2011 without an opportunity of hearing to the assessees. On 31 October 2011, the Supreme Court set aside that judgment in Civil Appeal Nos. 8955–8956 of 2011 and remitted the matter for fresh consideration. It emphasised the need for reasoned findings when the High Court overturns an ITAT decision, while expressing no view on the merits. On remand, the Revenue argued that the Assessing Officer had not independently verified the cash available at the time of search. The assessees submitted that questionnaires and supporting evidence had been considered in respect of cash found at the Panipat and Delhi residences, unsecured loans and fresh investments. The High Court noted the ITAT’s findings that the relevant documents and enquiries appeared in the paper book, including pages 130–150 and 151–154. The High Court held that an assessment order need not detail every enquiry conducted by the Assessing Officer. Its brevity, even if confined to a single page, does not by itself establish error where the record shows that the officer sought explanations and examined evidence. The two requirements for revision under Section 263—an erroneous order and prejudice to the Revenue—were not established. As no substantial question of law arose, both appeals were dismissed and pending applications disposed of.
