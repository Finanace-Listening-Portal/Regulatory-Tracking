Summary: Ethereum (ETH) uses proof-of-stake consensus and permits direct validators, pooled staking and other arrangements with materially different custody and reward mechanics. Official Ethereum documentation confirms that a direct validator requires 32 ETH, while the May 2025 Pectra upgrade enabled compounding validators with an effective balance of up to 2,048 ETH and execution-layer-triggered exits and partial withdrawals. For Indian tax year 2026–27, income from the transfer of ETH falls under section 194(1), Table item 4, of the Income-tax Act, 2025 at 30% plus applicable surcharge and cess, with only acquisition cost deductible and no set-off or carry-forward of VDA transfer losses. Section 393(1), resident-payments Table item 8(vi), prescribes 1% TDS on qualifying VDA-transfer consideration, subject to the exemptions in section 400(2). Staking rewards should not be casually described as automatically taxed at 30% on receipt: their character, timing and later transfer require separate analysis. FIU-IND’s updated January 2026 AML/CFT guidelines concern covered VDA service providers, not every private wallet holder. GST treatment of exchange, custody and validator services, cross-border FEMA restrictions, foreign-asset reporting and accounting must be assessed by transaction. This guide separates protocol mechanics from the legal obligations of Indian users, operators and service providers, without suggesting an investment or trading strategy. Key Takeaways Show all 20 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); What Is Ethereum Staking and What Changed With Pectra? Ethereum is a public blockchain secured by validators who stake ETH and perform consensus duties. Staking is not a bank deposit, and ETH is not a claim against the Reserve Bank of India. A validator may be penalised for downtime and slashed for provable misconduct. The Ethereum Foundation’s staking documentation identifies 32 ETH as the minimum to activate a direct validator; pooled arrangements may accept smaller amounts but introduce operator, smart-contract and custody risks. The distinction matters for tax records because direct rewards, pooled distributions and transferable staking tokens are not identical events. The Shanghai/Capella upgrade enabled staking withdrawals in April 2023. Pectra, activated in May 2025, added Type 2 compounding withdrawal credentials. Legacy Type 1 validators ordinarily sweep excess balance over 32 ETH to the withdrawal address, while Type 2 validators can compound effective balances up to 2,048 ETH. Conversion of withdrawal credentials to Type 2 is irreversible. Ethereum documentation also describes execution-layer-triggered partial withdrawals and exits, which change the operational process but do not themselves decide Indian tax character. Network APR values fluctuate and are deliberately excluded from this article. Direct validator versus staking pool A direct validator controls signing and withdrawal infrastructure, has consensus duties and may receive consensus rewards plus execution-layer fees or maximal extractable value under the protocol rules. A pooled participant may instead receive periodic distributions or a transferable receipt token. The underlying ETH may remain locked, while a receipt token is transferred independently. Treating all these events as a single “staking income” entry obscures taxable receipts, possible VDA transfers and counterparty exposure. It provides a 30% rate on such income, with applicable surcharge and health and education cess in addition. Cost of acquisition, if any, may be deducted; expenses such as platform commissions, gas costs and general trading overheads should not be assumed deductible in computing the specially taxed transfer income. Losses from VDA transfers cannot be set off against other income and cannot be carried forward to succeeding tax years. Example: a taxpayer sells ETH for ₹4,80,000 that was acquired for ₹3,00,000. Ignoring any special valuation issues, the positive transfer income is ₹1,80,000, and 30% basic tax is ₹54,000 before applicable cess and surcharge. A separate loss on another VDA sale does not automatically reduce that positive income. This is a simplified illustration, not a tax calculation for any particular person. The tax result must be established from individual acquisition lots and the actual transfer dates. The old-law reference section 115BBH of the Income-tax Act, 1961 remains relevant for earlier years but should not replace the current section 194 reference in an October 2026 article. TaxGuru’s general discussion of virtual digital asset taxation gives historical context; the 2025 Act and current official rules control present-period compliance. Staking rewards: receipt and later sale are different questions Section 194 expressly targets income from transfer of a VDA. Whether newly received staking rewards are taxable upon receipt, and under which head, depends on facts such as beneficial ownership, services performed, business organisation, receipt certainty and valuation. The law should not be represented as conclusively imposing section 194 merely because ETH enters a wallet. When rewarded ETH is subsequently transferred, the special VDA-transfer computation may apply, with the cost of acquisition requiring separate legal analysis. Maintain the wallet event, timestamp, INR fair-value working and subsequent sale-lot trail rather than assuming the first receipt and later sale are one transaction. One Per Cent TDS: Section 393 and the 2026 Forms Section 393(1), resident-payments Table serial 8(vi), requires 1% tax deduction on consideration for a qualifying transfer of a VDA to a resident. Section 400(2), Table item 12, contains the monetary exceptions: ₹50,000 for specified individuals/HUFs meeting the statutory conditions, and ₹10,000 for other payers. These are tax-year aggregation tests; the payer, payee residence, form of consideration and applicable exchange arrangements must be checked. Crypto-to-crypto swaps and in-kind consideration can require special handling because tax may need to be ensured before release of consideration. The current compliance architecture distinguishes Form 141, Schedule D for applicable buyer-side challan-cum-statement reporting from Form 142 for exchanges that undertake specified reporting and tax-payment obligations under the relevant guidelines. They are not interchangeable, and an ordinary individual does not file Form 142 merely for holding ETH. TaxGuru has verified explanatory pages on Form 141 Schedule D and Form 142, but the Department’s own forms and Rule 219 should prevail if there is a discrepancy. FIU-IND’s download portal lists AML/CFT Guidelines for Reporting Entities Providing Services Related to Virtual Digital Assets updated on 8 January 2026, and its September 2025 revised registration circular. Covered businesses may need FIU registration, customer due diligence, beneficial-owner identification, transaction monitoring, record preservation and suspicious-transaction reporting. The applicability analysis turns on the activities conducted for or on behalf of another person, not simply whether the firm uses the word “crypto”. A person holding ETH in a private wallet is not automatically an FIU-IND reporting entity. By contrast, a business that exchanges, transfers, safeguards or administers VDAs for others may fall within the notified categories. A staking pool operator should map actual services, contractual relationships, custody, payment flows and geographic nexus before deciding whether registration or reporting is required. FIU registration should not be marketed as an RBI licence, investment approval or guarantee of client funds. GST, FEMA and Cross-Border ETH Transactions The GST treatment of an ETH token transfer is not resolved merely by the income-tax label “virtual digital asset”. Separately supplied exchange commissions, custody services, technology services, validation services and other consideration may attract GST according to the nature of supply, place of supply, recipient status and statutory exemptions. Where consideration is received in ETH, the tax invoice and valuation rules for a taxable service must still be examined. Do not apply an unverified blanket GST rate to the ETH asset itself or assume that all staking rewards are consideration for a taxable service. FEMA legality is a separate inquiry from income-tax payment. The fact that a person declares ETH gains or an exchange deducts TDS does not by itself authorise overseas remittances, margin arrangements or foreign-exchange dealings. Cross-border purchase, transfer, foreign-currency funding and non-resident dealings require review under FEMA, RBI directions, bank policies and the precise transaction structure. The Liberalised Remittance Scheme is not a general permission for prohibited margin trading or every offshore crypto product. Businesses should retain the bank remittance purpose, counterparty jurisdiction, source of funds and beneficial-ownership evidence. Foreign assets, return reporting and NRI position Residents to whom foreign-asset disclosure rules apply should consider whether an offshore custodial account, foreign beneficial interest or other foreign asset is reportable in the applicable return schedule. A private self-custody wallet is not automatically identical to a foreign bank account. Schedule FA is based on return eligibility, residential status and asset character; NRIs and residents but not ordinarily resident require separate analysis. Schedule VDA and foreign-asset schedules serve different functions and should not be conflated. Accounting, Audit Trail and Documentation Accounting depends on whether ETH is held for trading, used in operations or held as an investment and on the applicable reporting framework. Under Ind AS, the IFRS Interpretations Committee’s crypto-holdings analysis often informs whether IAS 38-style intangible-asset principles or IAS 2-style inventory principles are relevant, but Indian entities must apply notified Ind AS and their specific facts. Under Indian GAAP, classification and measurement require careful policy analysis; cryptocurrency is not cash simply because it is called a coin. Validator equipment, pool fees, customer assets and revenue from validation should not be mixed with proprietary ETH holdings. An audit-ready ledger should identify transaction hash, chain, wallet ownership, date/time, units, rupee valuation source, counterparty, fee, TDS, GST invoice where relevant and reconciled closing holdings. Reconcile exchange statements to on-chain balances and bank receipts. Record changes in custody and validator credentials, but do not mistake a protocol withdrawal into the same beneficial owner’s wallet for a taxable sale without examining the facts. Document the policy for hard forks, airdrops, slashing and lost private keys if they occur. Operational controls for businesses Maintain separate ledgers for proprietary ETH, customer ETH, staking rewards and service fees. Retain historical INR values and valuation methodology for each material receipt and transfer. Reconcile 1% TDS credit to the correct taxpayer and transaction, including in-kind swaps. Review FIU-IND reporting-entity status based on actual services rather than branding. Check FEMA and GST independently before structuring any offshore or cross-border activity. Frequently Asked Questions Does Ethereum still require 32 ETH for a direct validator? Yes. Ethereum’s official staking documentation states that activation of a direct validator requires at least 32 ETH. Pooled services may allow smaller participation, but their risks and rights differ. What did Pectra change for validator withdrawals? The May 2025 upgrade enabled Type 2 compounding validator credentials with an effective-balance cap of 2,048 ETH and execution-layer-triggered exits or partial withdrawals, subject to protocol conditions. Are ETH sale gains taxed at 30% in tax year 2026–27? Income from transfer of a qualifying VDA is taxed at 30% under section 194(1), Table item 4, of the Income-tax Act, 2025, plus applicable surcharge and cess. Is 1% TDS charged on every ETH transfer? No. Section 393 targets qualifying consideration paid to a resident on transfer, subject to statutory exemptions. A mere movement between wallets of the same beneficial owner is not necessarily a transfer for consideration. Are staking rewards definitely taxed at 30% on receipt? No blanket conclusion follows from section 194. The character and timing of reward receipt require separate analysis; later disposal may separately attract the VDA-transfer rule. Does every ETH holder have to register with FIU-IND? No. Registration and AML/CFT reporting apply to covered service-provider activities under PMLA, not automatically to an individual who simply holds ETH. Can a taxpayer set off ETH trading losses against other income? No. Section 194 restricts set-off and carry-forward of losses from VDA transfers. Each transaction must be computed on the correct facts. Does paying Indian tax make offshore ETH transactions FEMA-compliant? No. Income-tax reporting and foreign-exchange permission are distinct. The payment route, transaction purpose and RBI/FEMA restrictions require independent verification. Key Takeaways ETH staking mechanics changed under Pectra, but the technical upgrade did not itself settle Indian tax questions. Section 194 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 taxes qualifying VDA-transfer income at 30% plus applicable levies. Section 393 imposes 1% TDS on qualifying resident VDA-transfer consideration, subject to section 400(2). Reward receipt, swap, validator exit and later sale are legally different events and need separate records. FIU-IND obligations apply to specified service-provider activities, not automatically to passive wallet holders.
