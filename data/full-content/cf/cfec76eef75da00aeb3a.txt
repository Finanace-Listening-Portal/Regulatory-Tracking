Summary : IDFC FIRST Bank Limited’s official rate page, checked on 8 November 2026, displays domestic fixed-deposit rates for deposits below ₹3 crore effective 4 November 2026. The resident callable schedule ranges from 3.25% to 7.25% per annum for general customers and from 3.50% to 7.50% for eligible senior citizens, depending on exact tenure. The 500-day to two-year band carries the highest displayed rate: 7.25% for general customers and 7.50% for senior citizens. The product supports reinvestment, monthly and quarterly payout choices. Premature and partial withdrawal are permitted, but the bank ordinarily reprices the deposit to the lower of the booked rate or the rate prevailing on the booking date for the period actually completed, then applies a 1% premature-closure penalty; the bank states that eligible senior-citizen deposits below ₹3 crore have no such penalty. Interest remains taxable, resident bank-interest TDS for Tax Year 2026-27 is governed by section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, and DICGC cover is limited to ₹5 lakh for principal and interest together per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity. This is a verification guide, not a recommendation or ranking. Payout Choice Changes the Cash-Flow Pattern Reinvestment and Quarterly Compounding The bank offers a cumulative or reinvestment option with quarterly compounding. Interest is added to the deposit at the prescribed interval, so maturity value reflects compounding and the exact tenure. A headline annual rate should not be multiplied by years without accounting for compounding, day count, tax deduction and any broken period. The issued receipt and maturity advice should be retained as the transaction record. Monthly or Quarterly Interest Monthly and quarterly payout options provide periodic cash flow rather than reinvestment. Monthly payout is generally discounted relative to the standard quarterly basis. The customer should obtain the bank’s actual payout schedule, particularly where the deposit begins or ends mid-month. Periodic payout does not make the interest tax-free and does not change the DICGC aggregation rule. Premature Closure Reprices the Completed Tenure For a premature or partial withdrawal, the bank states that interest is paid at the rate prevailing on the deposit date for the period for which the money actually remained with the bank or at the contracted rate, whichever is lower, after applying the premature-closure penalty. For fixed deposits opened or renewed on or after 2 May 2019, the published penalty is 1%. No interest is payable when a domestic deposit has not completed its minimum seven-day tenure. The bank separately states that senior citizens face no premature-closure penalty on fixed deposits below ₹3 crore. That waiver removes the penalty; it does not necessarily preserve the original longer-tenure rate after early closure. A senior depositor should request a closure quotation showing completed days, applicable historical rate and the waiver. The applicable terms are those accepted for the specific booking, so older deposits should not be recalculated from the new rate page alone. Section 393 Thresholds from 1 April 2026 For credit or payment events from 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies. Section 393 governs relevant non-salary deductions. For interest other than securities paid by a banking company, the threshold is ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen during the tax year. Once the threshold condition is met, deduction mechanics apply to the interest under the statute; the threshold is not a deduction from taxable income. The bank’s current page states that TDS is deducted on the entire interest amount when the threshold is exceeded and refers to Form 121 for an eligible declaration under the 2025 Act. PAN status, lower or nil-deduction eligibility and the applicable rate should be checked at the time of credit or payment. A declaration should never be filed merely to prevent TDS when its income conditions are not satisfied. DICGC Protection Is Bank-Wide, Not Receipt-Wise IDFC FIRST Bank Limited appears on the RBI list of scheduled private-sector banks. DICGC states that deposits with insured commercial banks, including fixed deposits, are covered up to ₹5 lakh for principal and interest together per depositor per bank in the same right and same capacity. Savings, current, recurring and fixed deposits across all branches of the same bank are aggregated. Opening multiple receipts or using both branch and digital channels does not multiply the limit. Different legal capacities can be treated separately under DICGC rules, but ownership must genuinely differ. A sole-proprietorship balance is aggregated with the proprietor’s individual balance. Joint accounts with the same names in the same order are aggregated together. DICGC is a statutory loss-protection mechanism upon a covered bank event; it is not a guarantee of immediate access, a return guarantee or cover above the ceiling.
