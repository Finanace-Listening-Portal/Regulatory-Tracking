Chemie Pharmie Limited (CAAR Mumbai) Summary: The Customs Authority for Advance Rulings (CAAR), Mumbai, in the matter of G.C. Chemie Pharmie Limited, ruled that the proposed imports of Fructo-Oligosaccharides (FOS), described as FOS-95 Powder (Dry) and FOS-95 Syrup, are classifiable under Customs Tariff Item 2940 00 00 of the First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, rather than Heading 1702. The ruling was issued on 29 October 2026 by Shri Rajesh Fattesing Dhabre, Customs Authority for Advance Rulings, Mumbai, in CAAR/Mum/ARC/124/2026-27. The Authority examined whether the products, containing fructo-oligosaccharides of different degrees of polymerisation, qualified for classification under Heading 2940 dealing with chemically pure sugars, notwithstanding their molecular diversity and residual sugar content. The ruling followed an examination of the applicant’s submissions, technical specifications, manufacturer’s documentation, laboratory reports, the Harmonized System Explanatory Notes and the relevant provisions governing customs tariff classification. The applicant, G.C. Chemie Pharmie Limited, was engaged in the import, manufacture and trading of pharmaceutical and nutraceutical products. It proposed to import FOS-95 Powder and FOS-95 Syrup from Shandong Starlight So True Biological Technology Co., Ltd., China, through Nhava Sheva, Mumbai. The imported products were described as prebiotic carbohydrates manufactured from sucrose through enzymatic transformation using fructosyltransferases, followed by purification. The applicant explained that FOS generally comprises chains of fructose molecules with a terminal glucose unit and is recognised for its ability to support beneficial intestinal microbiota. The principal commercial products under consideration were a dry powder with declared total FOS content exceeding 95% and a syrup with total FOS content exceeding 95% on a dry-solids basis. The applicant submitted that the product consisted predominantly of fructo-oligosaccharides, including GF2 (kestose), GF3 (nystose), GF4 and higher oligomers, with reducing sugars representing a relatively small proportion. It argued that the products satisfied the requirements of Chapter 29, particularly Heading 2940, because the HSN Explanatory Notes expressly recognised oligosaccharides within the expression “sugars” covered by that heading. According to the applicant, the presence of residual glucose, fructose and sucrose arising during the manufacturing process should be regarded as permissible impurities rather than ingredients intentionally added to alter the character of the products. Alternatively, if classification under Heading 2940 was rejected, the products could fall under Heading 1702 covering other sugars and sugar preparations. The applicant relied upon the classification principles under the General Rules for Interpretation of the Customs Tariff, especially Rule 1 and Rule 3(a), together with Chapter Note 1(a) to Chapter 29 and Note 1(b) to Chapter 17. It also cited the earlier CAAR ruling in In re Nutricia International Private Limited, involving classification of a mixture of human milk oligosaccharides under Heading 2940. The applicant contended that the precedent supported classification of its FOS products under the same heading despite the presence of multiple oligosaccharide constituents and process-related impurities. During the proceedings, the Authority sought extensive clarification concerning the chemical identity of the individual FOS constituents, their molecular formulae, molecular weights, degrees of polymerisation, the actual composition of the imported goods and the chromatographic separation undertaken during manufacturing. Particular attention was given to whether the declared FOS purity above 95% represented a single chemically defined compound or the aggregate concentration of several distinct oligosaccharide species. The Authority also examined whether glucose, fructose and sucrose were unavoidable manufacturing residues, independently identifiable constituents or components whose presence affected classification under Chapter 29. The applicant furnished additional submissions through its authorised representative, including supporting technical documents and explanations concerning the analytical methods adopted. The Authority observed that the expression “chemically pure” under Heading 2940 must be understood in the context of the Chapter Notes and HSN Explanatory Notes. It examined whether the imported product satisfied the requirements of separate chemically defined organic compounds and whether the presence of different oligosaccharide constituents constituted a mixture rather than permissible impurities. It also considered the distinction between the manufacturing process employed for the FOS products and the process considered in Nutricia International Private Limited. The Authority noted that the reported purity exceeded 95% on a dry-solids basis, but that this figure represented an aggregate of different FOS components rather than the purity of a single molecular species. These considerations formed an important part of the classification analysis. In its final determination, the Authority concluded that the proposed FOS products were classifiable under CTH 2940 00 00 and not under CTH 1702. In the case of FOS-95 Powder, the conclusion was based on the product specifications and supporting analytical reports indicating predominant fructo-oligosaccharide content. In the case of FOS-95 Syrup, the conclusion relied upon the applicant’s declaration that it contained the same FOS ingredient in aqueous form, with solids content of approximately 68%–72%, subject to the supporting technical evidence. The Authority nevertheless emphasised that the ruling was based on the information, product descriptions and documents furnished by the applicant. Actual imports would remain subject to verification by the jurisdictional Customs authorities, particularly if the composition or characteristics of the imported goods differed materially from those examined. Applicant’s interpretation of Law: – 6.1 At the outset, the applicant submitted that the Tariff is aligned, up to the 6-digit level, with the Harmonized System of Nomenclature (‘HSN’) issued by the World Customs Organization (‘WCO’). It has been held by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Collector of Customs, Bombay Vs. Business Forms – 2002 (142) ELT 18 that the HSN Explanatory Notes aid in the interpretation of the Headings of the Tariff and may be used as a safe guide for the same. 6.2 The classification of the goods imported into India is to be determined based on the General Rules of Interpretation (hereinafter referred to as the “GRI”) set out in the Tariff. As per Rule 1 of the GRI, classification of the imported products shall be determined according to the terms of the headings and any relative Section or Chapter Notes. 6.3 The product in question i.e., FOS is a mixture of oligosaccharides with β-(2→1) fructosyl linkages, typically GF2 Kestose (Glucose linked to two fructose units), GF3 Nystose (Glucose linked to three fructose units), GF 4+. Oligosaccharides are specifically covered under Heading 2940. Thus, at the outset classification under Heading 2940 should be examined. 6.4. Classification under Tariff Heading 2940: 6.4.1 That Tariff heading 2940 of the Customs Tariff Act covers Sugars, chemically pure, other than sucrose, lactose, maltose, glucose and fructose; sugar ethers, sugar acetals and sugar esters, and their salts, other than products of headings 2937, 2938 or 2939. Relevant extracts of HSN Explanatory Notes to Heading 2940 are set out below: HSN Explanatory Notes to Chapter Heading 29.40: – Other Organic Compounds 29.40 – Sugars, chemically pure, other than sucrose, lactose, maltose, glucose and fructose; sugar ethers, sugar acetals and sugar esters, and their salts, other than products of headings 2937, 2938 or 2939. A) SUGARS, CHEMICALLY PURE This heading covers only chemically pure sugars. The term “sugars” covers monosaccharides, disaccharides and oligosaccharides. Each saccharide unit must consist of at least four, but not more than eight, carbon atoms and, as a minimum, must contain a potential reducing carbonyl group (aldehydic or ketonic) and at least one asymmetric carbon atom bearing a hydroxyl group and a hydrogen atom. The heading excludes: a) Sucrose, this, even when chemically pure, falls in heading 17.01. b) Glucose and lactose; these, even when chemically pure, fall in heading 17.02. c) Maltose which, even when chemically pure, falls in heading 17.02. Isomeric with sucrose. Crystalline mass. Used in medicine. d) Fructose (laevulose) which, even when chemically pure, falls in heading 17.02. Isomeric with glucose. Yellowish crystals in the pure state. Used in medicine (for diabetic diets). e) Aldol (heading 29.12) and acetoin (3-hydroxy-2-butanone) (heading 29.14), which, though they meet the criteria for being saccharide units, are not sugars. The following are included among the chemically pure sugars falling under this heading: (1) Galactose. Isomeric with glucose. Obtained by hydrolysing lactose. Found in pectin substances and mucilages. Crystalline when pure. (2) Sorbose (sorbenose). Isomeric with glucose. White crystalline powder, very soluble in water. Used in the synthesis of of ascorbic acid (vitamin C), and in the preparation of culture media. (3) Xylose (wood sugar) (C5H10O5). White crystals. Used in pharmacy. (4) Trehalose, isomeric with sucrose. Ribose and arabinose, isomeric with xylose. Raffinose (C18H32O16). Fucose, rhamnose (C6H12O5), digitoxose (C6H12O4) and other deoxy sugars. These sugars are all essentially laboratory products. The sugars of this heading may be in the form of aqueous solutions. 6.4.2 In view of the above, it is clear that Tariff Heading 2940 only includes Chemically Pure Sugars. The term “sugars” referred to in Tariff Heading 2940 specifically covers monosaccharides, disaccharides, and oligosaccharides. 6.4.3 Based on the literature provided, it is evident that FOS is composed of Fructo-Oligosaccharides (>95%), & Reducing Sugars (Glucose + Fructose + Sucrose (< 3%) and are widely recognized for their prebiotic properties, supporting the growth of beneficial gut microbiota. The Fructo-Oligosaccharides content clearly constitutes the predominant component of the product. The limited presence of reducing sugars (<3%) is merely incidental and does not alter the fundamental character of the substance as a chemically pure sugar derivative. The presence of reducing sugars in the product is attributable to impurities arising during the manufacturing process and permissible in terms of Note 1(a) of Chapter 29 read with HSN Explanatory Notes to Chapter 29. Therefore, in view of HSN Explanatory Notes to Tariff Heading 2940 which clearly states that the term “Sugars” in the heading 2940 covers Oligosaccharides, FOS (having more than 95% Fructo-Oligosaccharides) is correctly classifiable under Customs Heading 2940 of the Customs Tariff Act. 6.4.4. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in a catena of judgment has held that the specific entry should prevail over general entry. [2012 (277) E.L.T. 299 (S.C.)], wherein the Apex Court held that classification of goods cannot be under residuary entry in presence of specific entry, even if it requires product to be understood in technical sense. It was further held that the residuary entry can be taken refuge of only in absence of specific entry. 6.4.6. Further, in terms of Chapter Note 1(a) of Chapter 29, the headings of this chapter are restricted to separate chemically defined compounds, whether or not containing impurities. The relevant extract of the Note 1(a) of Chapter is reproduced herein below for ready reference: Note 1 Except where the context otherwise requires, the headings of this Chapter apply only to: (a) separate chemically defined organic compounds, whether or not containing impurities; 6.4.7 The phrase ‘separate chemically defined compounds’ has been defined in HSN Explanatory Notes to Chapter 29 as under: HSN Explanatory Notes to Chapter 29 “A separate chemically defined compound is a substance which consists of one molecular species (e.g., covalent or ionic) whose composition is defined by constant ratio of elements and can be represented by a definitive structural diagram. In a crystal lattice, the molecular species corresponds to the repeating unit cell. Separate chemically defined compounds containing other substances deliberately added during or after their manufacture (including purification) are excluded from this Chapter. Accordingly, a product consisting of saccharin mixed with lactose, for example, to render the product suitable for use as a sweetening agent is excluded.” The separate chemically defined compounds of this Chapter may contain impurities (Note 1 (a)). An exception to this rule is created by the wording of heading 29.40 which, with regard to sugars, restricts the scope of the heading to chemically pure sugars. – The term “impurities” applies exclusively to substances whose presence in the single chemical compound results solely and directly from the manufacturing process (including purification). These substances may result from any of the factors involved in the process and are principally the following: (a) Unconverted starting materials. (b) Impurities present in the starting materials. (c) Reagents used in the manufacturing process (including purification). (d) By-products It should be noted, however, that such substances are not in all cases regarded as “impurities” permitted under Note 1 (a). When such substances are deliberately left in the product with a view to rendering it particularly suitable for specific use rather than for general use, they are not regarded as permissible impurities. For example, a product consisting of methyl acetate with methanol deliberately left in with a view to improving its suitability as a solvent is excluded (heading 38.14) For certain compounds (e.g., ethane, benzene, phenol, pyridine), there are specific purity criteria, indicated in Explanatory Notes to heading 29.01, 79.02, 29.07 and 29.33. 6.4.8. The limited presence of reducing sugars (<3%) is merely incidental and does not alter the fundamental character of the substance as a chemically pure sugar derivative. 6.4.13 In terms of the HSN Explanatory Notes to Chapter 29, a separately chemically defined compound may contain certain impurities, provided such impurities arise solely and directly from the manufacturing process (including purification) and are not deliberately added. These permissible impurities expressly include unconverted starting materials and by-products. 6.4.14. Applying the above legal provisions to the present facts, the minimal quantity of reducing sugars present in the product qualify as permissible impurities under the HSN Notes. Their presence does not detract from the essential character of the product as Fructo-Oligosaccharides, nor does it alter its classification as a separately chemically defined compound. 6.4.15. Accordingly, the product satisfies the requirements of Clause (a) of Chapter Note 1 of Chapter 29 of the Tariff Act and merits classification as a pure chemical compound under the said Chapter. 6.4.16. Furthermore, in light of the foregoing analysis, it is evident that FOS satisfy the statutory and explanatory requirements of Chapter 29, particularly Chapter Note 1(a), and are squarely covered under Customs Tariff Heading 2940, which expressly covers oligosaccharides as per the HSN Explanatory Notes. The presence of residual reducing sugars is merely incidental, arising from the manufacturing process, and does not alter the essential character of FOS as a separately chemically defined compound. Even by applying Rule 3(a) of the GRI, the more specific classification under Heading 2940 must prevail over the general Heading 1702. In view of the foregoing submissions, Chapter Note 1(c) to Chapter 29, the HSN Explanatory Notes to CTH 2940 and the advance ruling in M/s Nutricia International Private Limited (CAAR/Mum/ARC/197,198/2024-25 dated 09.01.2025), it is respectfully submitted that the subject product, comprising more than 95% FOS on a dry basis, is classifiable under CTH 2940 notwithstanding that it comprises multiple oligosaccharide species. 16. Discussion and findings: – 12.1 I have carefully considered the application filed by the Applicant, the facts and documents placed on record, the product specifications, technical literature, manufacturing process, the written submissions made by the Applicant, the submissions made during the personal hearing held on 13.10.2026, and the additional submissions dated 19.10.2026 furnished in response to the queries raised by this Authority vide email dated 21.09.2026. I have also examined the relevant provisions of the Customs Act, 1962, the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, the General Rules for the Interpretation of the Import Tariff and the relevant Harmonized System of Nomenclature (HSN) Explanatory Notes. 12. Discussion and findings: – 12.1 I have carefully considered the application filed by the Applicant, the facts and documents placed on record, the product specifications, technical literature, manufacturing process, the written submissions made by the Applicant, the submissions made during the personal hearing held on 13.10.2026, and the additional submissions dated 19.10.2026 furnished in response to the queries raised by this Authority vide email dated 21.09.2026. I have also examined the relevant provisions of the Customs Act, 1962, the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, the General Rules for the Interpretation of the Import Tariff and the relevant Harmonized System of Nomenclature (HSN) Explanatory Notes. 12.2 The issue for determination in the present application is the classification of the subject goods, namely, “FOS-95 Powder (Dry)” and “FOS-95 Syrup”, proposed to be imported by the Applicant. The Applicant has claimed classification under Customs Tariff Heading (CTH) 2940 00 00, whereas the applicability of CTH 1702 is also required to be examined in view of the composition, characteristics and form of presentation of the subject goods. 12.3 I observe that the question raised by the Applicant falls within the scope of Section 28H(2)(a) of the Customs Act, 1962, as it relates to the classification of goods under the Customs Tariff Act, 1975. Nature and composition of the subject goods 12.4 I observe that the subject goods are Fructo-Oligosaccharides (FOS), manufactured from sucrose through enzymatic conversion using fructosyltransferases, followed by purification and concentration operations. The product specifications furnished by the Applicant describe FOS as a mixture of oligosaccharides having β-(2→1) fructosyl linkages, comprising principally GF2 (Kestose), GF3 (Nystose) and higher-degree-of-polymerisation fructo-oligosaccharides, collectively referred to as GF4+. 12.5 I have examined Annexure-A submitted vide letter dated 19.10.2026 in response to this office’s email dated 21.09.2026 seeking clarification/additional information. Annexure-A comprises three test reports issued by Centre Testing International (Qingdao) Co., Ltd. in respect of samples described as “Fructo oligosaccharides, Specification FOS95”, manufactured by Baolingbao Biology Co., Ltd. The reports relate to samples from lot No. 24122113, received on 03.01.2025. Report No. A2250000953101001E records total fructooligosaccharides at 95.8% on a dry basis and moisture at 2.18%. The report also records pH at 6.7 and ash as not detected, against the stated limits. These results provide analytical support for the declared high aggregate FOS content of the tested FOS95 powder sample. 12.6 Report No. A2250000953101002E, relating to a sample of the same specification and lot, reports total sugars (sum of glucose, fructose, sucrose, maltose and lactose) at 3.4 g/100 g; fructose at 0.68 g/100 g; sucrose at 2.7 g/100 g; and glucose, maltose and lactose as not detected at the stated limits of quantification. Conductivity ash is reported at 0.013%, and solubility at 99.4%. The reported total-sugar result is therefore 3.4 g/100 g and does not support an unqualified finding that all residual simple sugars are below 3%. The report is to be read according to the analytes and units actually reported; it does not separately quantify every individual FOS species. 12.7 I further observe that the Applicant, vide its additional submissions dated 19.10.2026, has expressly clarified that the declared total FOS content exceeding 95% represents the aggregate concentration of multiple chemically distinct FOS molecular species and does not represent the purity of a single chemically defined FOS compound. The Applicant has acknowledged that GF2, GF3, GF4 and higher FOS constituents possess different molecular formulae and molecular weights. 12.8 The reports in Annexure-A do not provide a quantitative distribution of GF2, GF3, GF4, GF5 and higher FOS constituents. Accordingly, the declared aggregate FOS value of 95.8% on a dry basis cannot be treated as proof of the precise percentage of each individual oligosaccharide. The report establishes the aggregate FOS content of the tested FOS95 sample, subject to the report’s own statement that the results relate only to the sample tested. 12.9 Report No. A2250000953101003E concerns 191 pesticide/residue parameters and records the results of those tests for the sample identified as FOS95. That report is relevant to the quality characteristics tested, but does not independently determine tariff classification or establish the molecular distribution of the FOS constituents. I have therefore relied on the first report for the aggregate FOS result and the second report for the reported sugar profile, without attributing to either report findings that it does not contain. 12.10 In this regard, I find it necessary to distinguish between the declared aggregate FOS content and the individual molecular composition of the product. The declaration of total FOS exceeding 95% may establish the predominance of the FOS fraction, subject to the reliability of the supporting analytical evidence. However, such declaration does not, by itself, establish the precise percentage of each individual oligosaccharide or establish that the product consists of a single molecular species. 12.11 The Applicant has also clarified that the chromatographic separation undertaken during manufacture is primarily intended for purification and removal of non-FOS components, and not for isolating each individual FOS constituent into separate fractions. According to the Applicant, some FOS fractions may also be removed during the process, while the remaining FOS constituents continue to be present together in the final product. 12.12 I find that the manufacturing process and the chromatographic separation described by the Applicant are relevant to understanding the nature of the product. However, the fact that the process is directed towards purification does not, by itself, establish that the final product is a single chemically defined compound. The classification must be determined with reference to the goods as presented for importation, their actual composition and the applicable tariff provisions. Examination of CTH 2940 12.13 Heading 29.40 of the First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, covers: “Sugars, chemically pure, other than sucrose, lactose, maltose, glucose and fructose; sugar ethers, sugar acetals and sugar esters, and their salts, other than products of headings 2937, 2938 or 2939.” 12.14 The HSN Explanatory Notes to Heading 29.40 state that the heading covers only chemically pure sugars and that the expression “sugars” includes monosaccharides, disaccharides and oligosaccharides. Accordingly, oligosaccharides are expressly contemplated within the scope of Heading 29.40, subject to the applicable conditions and limitations governing the heading. 12.15 Note 1(a) to Chapter 29 provides that, except where the context otherwise requires, the headings of the Chapter apply only to separate chemically defined organic compounds, whether or not containing impurities. The HSN Explanatory Notes explain that a separate chemically defined compound ordinarily consists of one molecular species whose composition is defined by a constant ratio of its constituent elements and which can be represented by a definitive structural diagram. 12.16 The HSN Explanatory Notes further explain that impurities permitted under Note 1(a) may include unconverted starting materials, impurities present in the starting materials, reagents used during manufacture or purification, and by-products, provided their presence results solely and directly from the manufacturing process, including purification. Substances deliberately retained in a product for a particular functional purpose are not necessarily regarded as permissible impurities. 12.17 In the present case, the Applicant’s own clarification establishes that FOS-95 is a mixture of different molecular species, namely GF2, GF3 and higher FOS constituents. These constituents collectively constitute the intended FOS fraction of the product and cannot be treated as impurities associated with a single principal FOS compound. I therefore find that the product cannot be regarded as a single separate chemically defined molecular species merely on the ground that its aggregate FOS content exceeds 95%. 12.18 However, the above finding does not, by itself, conclude the classification issue. It is necessary to examine the scope of Heading 29.40 in conjunction with the applicable Chapter Notes and the General Rules for the Interpretation of the Import Tariff, particularly where the goods consist predominantly of a mixture of oligosaccharides. 12.19 In this regard, Rule 1 of the General Rules for the Interpretation of the Import Tariff provides that classification shall be determined according to the terms of the headings and any relative Section or Chapter Notes. Rule 2(b) provides that a reference in a heading to a material or substance shall be taken to include mixtures or combinations of that material or substance with other materials or substances, and that the classification of goods consisting of more than one material or substance shall be determined according to the principles of Rule 3. 12.20 Accordingly, the fact that FOS-95 contains several molecular species does not, by itself, preclude consideration of Heading 29.40. The applicability of the heading must be assessed having regard to the express inclusion of oligosaccharides in the HSN Explanatory Notes, the nature of the mixture and the relevant interpretative rules. At the same time, the mere presence of oligosaccharides in a product cannot automatically establish classification under Heading 29.40 without examining the conditions governing the heading. 12.21 I have also examined the Applicant’s reliance on Note 1(c) to Chapter 29. The said Note refers to specified products of headings 29.36 to 29.39, sugar ethers, sugar acetals and sugar esters and their salts of Heading 29.40, and products of Heading 29.41, whether or not chemically defined. 12.22 I find that Note 1(c) to Chapter 29 cannot be interpreted in isolation or as dispensing with the requirement of chemical purity applicable to the category of sugars covered by Heading 29.40. The said Note specifically identifies the categories of products to which the expression “whether or not chemically defined” applies. It does not, merely by virtue of its reference to Heading 29.40, establish that every mixture of sugars or oligosaccharides is classifiable under that heading irrespective of its composition and characteristics. 12.23 I therefore find that the Applicant’s reliance on Note 1(c) is relevant only to the extent of its actual scope. The classification of the subject goods must independently satisfy the terms of Heading 29.40, read with the applicable Chapter Notes, HSN Explanatory Notes and General Rules for the Interpretation of the Import Tariff. Examination of the decision in Nutricia International Private Limited 12.24 I have also considered the decision of this Authority in Nutricia International Private Limited, CAAR/Mum/ARC/197,198/2024-25 dated 09.01.2025, particularly paragraphs 7.11 to 7.14 thereof, relied upon by the Applicant in its original application as well as its additional submissions. 12.25 In the said ruling, the Authority examined a product described as “5-HMO Mix”, comprising five identified human milk oligosaccharides. The ruling considered whether the mixture, predominantly comprising the identified oligosaccharides with a small quantity of impurities, could be classified under Heading 29.40. The Authority observed that the constituent oligosaccharides were individually classifiable under the said heading and that the wet-blending process did not chemically alter those constituents. The mixture was ultimately held classifiable under Heading 29.40 in the facts and circumstances of that case. 12.26 I find that the aforesaid ruling is relevant to the extent that it recognises the possibility of classifying a product comprising multiple oligosaccharide constituents under Heading 29.40, subject to examination of the nature of the constituents, the composition of the product and the applicable tariff provisions. However, the said ruling cannot be treated as laying down an unconditional proposition that every product containing a high proportion of oligosaccharides necessarily falls under Heading 29.40. 12.27 An important factual distinction in the present case is that FOS-95 is obtained through enzymatic conversion of sucrose, followed by purification and concentration, and the individual molecular species in the final product have not been separately quantified in the samples under consideration. The Applicant has also clarified that the chromatographic step does not isolate each FOS constituent into separate fractions, but principally removes non-FOS components while the remaining FOS constituents continue to be present together. 12.28 Consequently, the decision in Nutricia International Private Limited has to be considered as a relevant precedent in the context of the tariff provisions and the facts of the present case, rather than as a substitute for examining the composition and characteristics of FOS-95 independently. Examination of CTH 1702 and Chapter 17 Note 1(b) 12.29 Heading 17.02 covers: “Other sugars, including chemically pure lactose, maltose, glucose and fructose, in solid form; sugar syrups not containing added flavouring or colouring matter; artificial honey, whether or not mixed with natural honey; caramel.” 12.30 Note 1(b) to Chapter 17 provides that Chapter 17 does not cover “chemically pure sugars (other than sucrose, lactose, maltose, glucose and fructose) or other products of heading 2940”. 12.31 I find that the relationship between Headings 29.40 and 17.02 must be determined by applying the terms of the respective headings and the relevant Chapter Notes. Where goods are properly covered by Heading 29.40, Chapter 17 Note 1(b) excludes them from Chapter 17. Conversely, the exclusion in Chapter 17 cannot, by itself, establish that goods which do not satisfy the requirements of Heading 29.40 must necessarily be classified under that heading. 12.32 The form in which the subject goods are presented, namely powder or syrup, is a relevant characteristic but is not conclusive of classification. In particular, the fact that the syrup contains water and has a solids content of approximately 68-72% does not, by itself, determine whether the product falls under Heading 29.40 or Heading 17.02. The classification must be based on the composition of the goods and the applicable tariff provisions. Consideration of the additional submissions and supporting evidence 12.33 I have specifically considered the additional submissions dated 19.10.2026, wherein the Applicant has clarified that the individual FOS constituents are integral components of the product and are not impurities associated with any single principal FOS compound. I find that this clarification is consistent with the Applicant’s description of FOS-95 as a mixture of oligosaccharides. 12.34 The Applicant has stated that the reducing sugars, namely glucose, fructose and sucrose, collectively constitute less than 3% of the product on a dry basis and originate from the starting raw material or may be generated during the manufacturing process. It has further stated that some of these sugars are removed during chromatographic purification. However, the individual percentages of glucose, fructose and sucrose have not been separately furnished in the additional submissions. 12.35 I find that the presence of residual reducing sugars cannot be treated as permissible impurities solely on the basis of their aggregate concentration being below 3%. For the purpose of applying the relevant HSN provisions concerning impurities, the circumstances in which such substances are present, their origin in the manufacturing process and whether they arise solely and directly from the process must be considered. The Applicant’s explanation regarding their origin is relevant to this examination, but it does not dispense with the need for supporting technical evidence where required. 12.36 I have considered Annexure-A specifically. Report No. A2250000953101001E records total FOS of 95.8% on a dry basis for the tested FOS95 sample. Report No. A2250000953101002E records total sugars of 3.4 g/100 g, comprising reported fructose of 0.68 g/100 g and sucrose of 2.7 g/100 g, with glucose, maltose and lactose not detected at the stated quantification limits. These results must be stated as reported; in particular, the record does not support describing total sugars as below 3% without qualification. The reports do not quantify the individual GF2, GF3, GF4 and higher FOS constituents separately. 12.37 The Applicant has also clarified that the expression “100% FOS” used in certain documents refers to the FOS ingredient, while the technical specification declares the total FOS concentration to be greater than 95% and acknowledges the presence of multiple chemically distinct FOS molecular species. I find that, for the purpose of classification, the actual composition declared in the product specifications and established by the supporting technical records is material. The expression “100% FOS” cannot be interpreted as establishing that the product consists of only one chemically defined molecular species. 12.38 I find that Annexure-A supports the conclusion that the tested FOS95 powder sample is predominantly composed of fructo-oligosaccharides, with a reported aggregate FOS content of 95.8% on a dry basis. It does not establish the precise distribution of individual FOS molecular species, nor does it contain a separate test report for the syrup form. The Applicant’s explanation is that the syrup is the same FOS ingredient presented with water and has solids content of approximately 68-72%. I have considered that explanation alongside the product specifications and manufacturing description; the classification of the syrup is accordingly based on the declared identity and composition of the goods as proposed to be imported, rather than on a syrup-specific result in Annexure-A. Conclusion on classification 12.39 On a cumulative consideration of the product specifications, manufacturing process, additional submissions dated 19.10.2026, the relevant Chapter Notes, the HSN Explanatory Notes and the decision in Nutricia International Private Limited, I find that the subject goods are represented as a product predominantly comprising fructo-oligosaccharides, with a declared aggregate FOS content exceeding 95% on a dry basis. The individual FOS constituents are intended components of the product and are not merely incidental impurities. The classification question must therefore be determined with reference to the product as a whole and the applicable interpretative rules. 12.40 I further find that the HSN Explanatory Notes to Heading 29.40 expressly include oligosaccharides within the expression “sugars”. The fact that the subject goods comprise multiple oligosaccharide molecular species does not, by itself, conclude the matter against Heading 29.40. At the same time, the declaration of a high aggregate FOS content and the Applicant’s reliance on Chapter Note 1(c) cannot independently dispense with the requirements of the heading. The applicability of Heading 29.40 must rest on the nature and composition of the product, considered in conjunction with the relevant tariff provisions and the General Rules for the Interpretation of the Import Tariff. 12.41 Applying the relevant interpretative rules to the product as described and supported by the records, I find that the powder sample tested under Annexure-A is predominantly composed of fructo-oligosaccharides, with aggregate FOS reported at 95.8% on a dry basis. The separate sugar-profile report records total sugars at 3.4 g/100 g; therefore, I do not rely on the assertion that residual sugars are below 3% as an established fact. Nevertheless, the reported FOS content and the product specifications indicate that the FOS fraction imparts the essential character of the powder. In respect of the syrup, the conclusion is based on the Applicant’s declaration that it is the same FOS ingredient in aqueous form, with solids content of approximately 68-72%, and is subject to the goods being imported in conformity with that description. 12.42 Having regard to the terms of Heading 29.40, the relevant Chapter Notes and HSN Explanatory Notes, the product specifications and manufacturing process, the analytical evidence in Annexure-A, and the ruling in Nutricia International Private Limited considered above, I hold that “FOS-95 Powder (Dry)” and “FOS-95 Syrup”, when imported in conformity with the product description and composition examined in these proceedings, are classifiable under CTH 2940 00 00 of the First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, and not under CTH 1702. This conclusion is confined to the goods as described in the application and supported by the record; any material variation in composition or characteristics would require classification to be determined on its own facts. 12.43 The foregoing findings and determination have been arrived at on the basis of the application, the documents and submissions placed on record, the arguments advanced and information furnished during the personal hearing, and the additional clarifications and analytical reports submitted by the Applicant. The jurisdictional Customs authorities shall remain at liberty, at the time of import and in accordance with law, to call for the Certificate(s) of Analysis, test reports, technical literature or such other relevant evidence as may be considered necessary to verify the identity, composition and characteristics of the goods actually imported and their conformity with the product description examined in this ruling. The classification determined herein is subject to the goods being imported in conformity with the facts and material placed before this Authority. 13. I rule accordingly.
