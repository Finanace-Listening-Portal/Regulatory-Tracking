(Image Source: Pixabay)follow usfollow usGrowing overseas investment in India's non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) could bring additional capital, improve governance and widen funding options. However, the influx of foreign money may also encourage lenders to pursue faster loan expansion and accept greater credit risks in their efforts to deliver higher returns, potentially creating asset quality concerns over the longer term, according to Fitch Ratings.In its latest report, South and Southeast Asia Finance Companies Outlook 2027, the global credit rating agency highlighted the potential risks associated with increasing foreign participation in India's finance companies. While overseas investments could strengthen lenders' financial foundations, pressure to meet investor return expectations may influence lending strategies and weaken credit assessment standards."Rising foreign investment in Indian finance companies should support capitalisation, governance standards and funding diversification. However, the associated return expectations may also drive greater risk appetite and faster loan growth, which could weaken underwriting discipline and lead to higher asset-quality challenges in future years," the report said.Despite flagging these concerns, Fitch has retained its 'neutral' outlook for Indian finance companies in 2027. This contrasts with its broader assessment of the South and Southeast Asian finance company sector, for which it has changed its outlook from 'neutral' to 'deteriorating'.Higher funding expenses and mounting pressure on asset quality contributed to the downgrade in the regional outlook.For India, the rating agency believes economic growth will continue to support borrowing demand and help lenders manage some of the challenges arising from increasing funding costs. Nevertheless, it cautioned that aggressive credit expansion and greater exposure to riskier borrower categories could create difficulties.Fitch's projections, based on an assessment of 19 major Indian finance companies, suggest that lending growth will gradually lose some momentum.Gross loan growth is expected to ease to 17 per cent in the financial year beginning April 2027, followed by a further moderation to 16 per cent in the subsequent financial year. This compares with the estimated growth rate of 18 per cent for the financial year starting April 2026.Asset Quality Expected To Remain StableThe agency expects the gross non-performing loan (NPL) ratio of Indian finance companies to remain relatively steady at 2.3 per cent in 2027.A greater emphasis on secured credit products is expected to support this stability. Mortgage lending and loans backed by gold are among the categories that could help lenders manage credit risks, particularly as companies maintain stricter approval procedures.However, not all lending segments may experience similar conditions. Small businesses and rural borrowers could face additional financial pressure, potentially affecting repayment performance."We see scope for some near-term weakness in small business and rural-related lending due to the protracted geopolitical risks and weak monsoon rains in 2026," the report said.Higher Borrowing Costs Could Squeeze NBFC ProfitabilityApart from asset quality concerns, lenders may also have to contend with pressure on their earnings as funding becomes more expensive.Fitch expects borrowing costs to rise, while competition in secured lending products is likely to intensify. This could restrict the ability of finance companies to transfer higher funding expenses to customers through increased lending rates."Higher borrowing rates are likely to compress net interest margin (NIM) modestly, if Indian finance companies are unable to pass on the full impact to customers given intensifying price competition in secured lending segments," it said.Net interest margin is an important measure of lending profitability, reflecting the difference between the interest income earned on loans and the interest paid on borrowings.If lenders are unable to adjust customer rates sufficiently, their margins could come under pressure despite continued expansion in their loan portfolios.Major Foreign Investments Bring NBFC Sector Into FocusFitch's assessment comes amid a series of significant overseas investments in India's non-banking finance industry, reflecting international investor interest in the country's expanding credit market.In April 2026, Japan's MUFG Bank acquired a 20 per cent stake in Shriram Finance through an equity investment worth Rs 39,618 crore, according to the company's second-quarter investor presentation.Another major transaction involves Manappuram Finance. In March 2026, the company announced that it had secured all necessary statutory approvals for global investment firm Bain Capital's proposed investment of approximately Rs 4,385 crore. The transaction also involves Bain Capital acquiring joint control alongside the company's existing promoters.These investments represent substantial foreign participation in Indian finance companies and could support their capital requirements and long-term expansion strategies.Fitch, meanwhile, expects capitalisation and liquidity across the sector to remain adequate.
