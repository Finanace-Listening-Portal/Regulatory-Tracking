} } Listen to This ArticleThe margins of leading private banks in the second quarter this financial year came under pressure from the large mobilisation of deposits under Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank), or FCNR (B), mobilised under the Reserve Bank of India’s special window. However, lenders expect margins to recover over the coming quarters because the funds are being deployed and the turnaround in the interest-rate cycle supports lending yields. HDFC Bank, the country’s largest private lender, reported a 10-basis point decline in the net interest margin (NIM) to 3.16 per cent in the September quarter from 3.26 per cent in the preceding quarter. According to HDFC Bank Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer Sashidhar Jagdishan, the bank’s margins, which had faced pressure from a faster repricing of assets and a lag in the cost of funds during the declining interest-rate cycle, were positioned for a reversal as the rate cycle turned upwards. ICICI Bank has used a portion of the funds to repay existing deposits and support loan growth during the quarter, while the remaining amount will be deployed over the next two quarters, Batra said. Axis Bank, the country’s third-largest private lender, reported a 10-basis point decline in the NIM to 3.36 per cent in Q2 FY27 from 3.46 per cent in the previous quarter. The bank attributed the pressure, in part, to FCNR (B) deposits, but said it remained on track to meet its NIM guidance of 3.8 per cent. During the analyst call, the bank’s management said it had used a portion of the funds towards the end of the September quarter to retire higher-cost non-retail term deposits (NRTDs), with the benefit expected to flow through to margins in the current quarter. “Last quarter we utilised some portion of these funds to retire the more expensive NRTDs, which was done towards the end of the quarter.
