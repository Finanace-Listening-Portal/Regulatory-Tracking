Articles 2 min read Global banks seek safeguards as AI agents enter online commerce Six global banks including Bank of America and ING Group have proposed principles for the development of agentic commerce, calling for greater transparency, safety, data protection, consumer and merchant choice and interoperability as AI agents take on a larger role in shopping and payments. The paper says consumers are enthusiastic about the potential of AI agents but remain unclear about whether the technology will act in their interests.Consumers could face situations where AI agents buy the wrong product, spend more than intended or expose them to scams and fraud, the paper said. They may also be unclear about whether they are protected when something goes wrong or which party is responsible. Advt The banks identified five areas for the development of trusted agentic commerce: transparency, safety, privacy and data, choice, and interoperability. The principles are intended to give consumers and merchants greater choice, control and flexibility over how they pay and get paid, while ensuring transactions remain safe and secure.The risks with AI agentsThe paper highlights risks associated with AI agents handling payments, including situations where an agent could request a customer's card details and enter them directly into a website. It also raises concerns that AI agents could steer consumers towards payment methods that provide weaker protections.The banks said agentic commerce could increase the scope for fraud, scams and disputes. Bad actors could potentially compromise or impersonate AI agents or merchants and use new forms of social engineering, while uncertainty over responsibility could make it harder to resolve disputes.Transparency is one of the proposed principles, including greater clarity when an AI agent is involved in a transaction and greater visibility into how agents make decisions. The banks also propose safeguards for customer data as AI agents gain access to information needed to complete transactions.The principles also call for consumers and merchants to retain the ability to choose which AI-powered commerce services they use. Different systems should be capable of working with each other rather than creating fragmented ecosystems, the paper said. Advt The banks stressed that the paper is a set of high-level principles and is voluntary and non-binding. It does not prescribe conduct, commercial positions or a particular method or timetable for implementation.The group intends to engage with policymakers and other participants in the payments ecosystem as agentic commerce develops.
