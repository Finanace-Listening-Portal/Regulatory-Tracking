Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Gudla Vanaja And Others Vs Assistant Commissioner (ST) (Telangana High Court) Summary: The Telangana High Court, in Gudla Vanaja Vs Assistant Commissioner (ST), delivered a significant Full Bench judgment on 30 September 2026 concerning the validity of GST show cause notices and adjudication orders uploaded on the GST common portal without visible digital signatures. The three-judge Bench comprising Chief Justice Aparesh Kumar Singh, Justice N. Tukaramji and Justice G.M. Mohiuddin considered a large batch of writ petitions challenging electronically issued notices and orders under the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, and the corresponding State GST legislation. The principal question was whether the absence of a visible digital signature on documents downloaded from the GST portal rendered such notices and orders legally invalid, notwithstanding their electronic authentication through the GST Network. The petitioners contended that GST notices and adjudication orders must bear the signature or digital signature of the proper officer in accordance with Rule 26(3) of the CGST Rules, 2017, and the prescribed statutory forms. They argued that documents without visible signatures could not be treated as valid statutory communications and that merely uploading them on the GST portal did not establish proper authentication. Reliance was placed on the Information Technology Act, 2000, the applicable electronic signature framework and earlier judicial decisions holding unsigned GST notices or orders invalid. Certain petitioners also questioned the absence of Document Identification Numbers (DINs), the validity of service through the portal, and the legality of proceedings initiated without complying with mandatory procedural requirements. The State Government, Central GST authorities and Goods and Services Tax Network opposed these submissions, explaining that the GST common portal operates through a secure electronic authentication system. They submitted that proper officers access the system using authorised credentials and digital authentication mechanisms before generating and issuing statutory notices or orders. The respondents relied upon Sections 146, 160 and 169 of the CGST Act and Rule 26 of the CGST Rules to contend that electronic authentication through the common portal satisfies the statutory requirements. During the proceedings, GSTN conducted a live demonstration explaining the generation, authentication, uploading and verification of electronic documents. The authorities maintained that a visible digital signature on the downloaded PDF was not necessary when the document had already been authenticated through the prescribed electronic system. After examining the CGST Act, Chapter XVIII of the CGST Rules, the Information Technology Act, 2000, and the applicable rules governing electronic authentication, the Full Bench rejected the contention that every GST notice or order must display a visible digital signature. The Court distinguished between electronic authentication of a document and the appearance of a digital signature on its downloaded PDF copy. It held that documents covered under Chapter XVIII of the CGST Rules are valid when electronically authenticated by the proper officer and uploaded through the common portal. The Court further observed that statutory forms are subordinate to the governing rules and cannot introduce additional requirements inconsistent with the prescribed electronic procedure. Accordingly, the absence of a visible digital signature does not, by itself, invalidate an electronically authenticated GST notice or adjudication order. On the issue of DIN and Reference File Number (RFN), the High Court examined the applicable administrative instructions and Circular No. 249/06/2025-GST dated 9 June 2025. The Court recognised RFN as a valid identification and authentication mechanism for documents generated through the GST common portal. It clarified that where a document contains a verifiable RFN, a separate DIN is not necessary. However, the Court expressly held that the absence of both DIN and RFN would render the relevant document invalid. Examining the individual writ petitions, the Bench observed that the relevant notices, summaries, orders or their attachments contained either an RFN or DIN. Consequently, the impugned documents could not be declared invalid merely on the ground of defective document identification. The Full Bench also examined whether uploading GST notices and orders on the common portal constitutes valid service under Section 169(1)(d) of the CGST Act. Rejecting the petitioners’ argument that portal uploading alone was insufficient, the Court held that electronically authenticated notices and orders become validly served when made available on the GST common portal. It further clarified that the limitation period for filing statutory appeals or undertaking other prescribed compliances begins from the date of uploading the relevant notice or order on the portal. The Court disagreed with the contrary legal position adopted in earlier decisions, including Bigleap Technologies, insofar as those decisions treated the absence of a visible digital signature as sufficient to invalidate electronically authenticated GST communications. The judgment therefore established the legal validity of portal-based authentication and service, subject to compliance with applicable statutory requirements.
