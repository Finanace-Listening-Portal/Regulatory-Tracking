REC and L&T have each raised Rs500 crore, while IIFL Finance has raised Rs25 crore. The initial phase is restricted to institutional investors, with retail investors expected to be included later. Advt Demat 2.0 will connect tokenised securities with the RBI's wholesale central bank digital currency through its Unified Market Interface, allowing the securities and funds legs to be settled simultaneously. The framework also uses smart contracts to automate parts of asset servicing, including interest payments and redemptions.The initiative is aimed at reducing operational processes such as file sharing, reconciliation and validation among market intermediaries, while lowering settlement risk. It also seeks to preserve legal certainty around ownership as securities are recorded on a permissioned distributed ledger.From dematerialisation to tokenisationThe pilot represents a shift from simply holding securities electronically towards linking the security, settlement and servicing processes through a common technology framework.Under Demat 2.0, securities are recorded on a permissioned distributed ledger while the payment leg uses the RBI's wholesale CBDC. This allows delivery and payment to take place together rather than through separate processes. REC's first pilot issue used atomic delivery-versus-payment settlement under the framework.The framework also seeks to automate parts of the post-issuance process. Under the proposed arrangement, authorised institutions can access bondholder information through the shared ledger, while smart contracts can automate payments such as interest and redemption on their due dates. Advt That could reduce some of the reconciliation and manual processing involved in conventional bond servicing. The extent of those efficiency gains, however, will have to be established as the pilot develops.Three issuers test the frameworkREC completed India's first pilot issuance of tokenised corporate bonds under Sebi's regulatory sandbox framework, raising Rs500 crore through a 20-month bond carrying a 7.30 per cent coupon. The issue received bids worth about Rs795 crore.L&T subsequently raised Rs500 crore through a three-year tokenised bond at a 7.4 per cent coupon, while IIFL Finance raised Rs25 crore through a two-year issue at 9.1 per cent. The transactions take the total value of the three pilot issuances to Rs1,025 crore. Advt The transactions are significant because the technology is being tested through actual corporate bond issuances rather than only as a proof of concept. The immediate objective, however, remains a controlled pilot rather than a replacement for the existing corporate bond settlement infrastructure.Secondary market remains a testThe next challenge will be whether tokenised bonds can develop sufficient liquidity and integrate with the wider corporate bond market. The current framework does not yet have a mechanism for secondary trading of these tokenised bonds, according to market participants cited in recent reporting. Advt That means the success of Demat 2.0 will ultimately depend on more than faster settlement of individual issuances. Investors will need mechanisms to trade the securities, transfer holdings between tokenised and conventional infrastructure where required, and access sufficient liquidity for the technology to become relevant beyond pilot transactions.For now, Demat 2.0 is testing whether tokenisation, CBDC based settlement and smart contracts can reduce friction in the lifecycle of corporate bonds. If the pilot demonstrates measurable efficiency gains while maintaining legal and investor protections, the framework could eventually be extended beyond the current institutional transactions to a wider set of securities and investors.
