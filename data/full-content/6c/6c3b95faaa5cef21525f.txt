Summary: The article examines the November 2026 editions of ICAI’s Technical Guides on Form GSTR-9 and Form GSTR-9C, highlighting six important changes relevant to GST annual return preparation for FY 2025-26. It explains the structure and reporting requirements of both forms before analysing the revised computation of total turnover in Table 5N, which excludes supplies on which e-commerce operators discharge tax under Section 9(5), and the revised formula in Table 8I, which accounts for import-related IGST credit availed in the subsequent financial year. Through practical numerical illustrations, the author explains the reporting of input tax credit reclaimed under Rules 37 and 37A in Table 6H of the financial year of reclaim, along with the implications of pre-filled HSN details in Table 17 and changes in GST rates during the year. The article also discusses the withdrawal of Circular No. 212/6/2024-GST concerning post-sale discounts and the prospective amendment to Section 34(1). Changes in GSTR-9C include clarification of Table 7D1, revision of the taxable turnover formula in Table 7F and other reporting instructions. The author further examines the continuation of optional reporting requirements under Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax and its subsequent corrigendum. A practical compliance checklist assists taxpayers and professionals in updating reconciliation templates, reviewing e-commerce transactions, reporting reclaimed ITC and verifying HSN-wise supplies. The article emphasises that ICAI’s technical guidance must be read alongside the applicable statutory provisions, rules and notifications. It sums up the year: what was sold, what credit was claimed, what tax was paid. It is built mainly from the year’s GSTR-3B returns. The guide describes it as six parts and nineteen tables. GSTR-9C is the reconciliation statement. It sets the books (the audited financial statements) against the annual return and explains every difference. Under Rule 80(3) it is a self-certified statement for registered persons whose aggregate turnover exceeds ₹5 crore. GSTR-9 at a Glance Part and tables What it holds Part I (1 to 3) Year, GSTIN, name Part II (4 and 5) Supplies of the year. Table 4: tax payable. Table 5: tax not payable. 5N is total turnover Part III (6 to 8) Input tax credit: availed (6), reversed (7), comparison with GSTR-2B (8) Part IV (9) Tax paid, through cash and through credit Part V (10 to 14) Items of this year reported in the next year’s returns: amendments, reversals, credit availed, differential tax Part VI (15 to 19) Demands and refunds (15), composition and deemed supplies (16), HSN summaries (17 and 18), late fee (19) GSTR-9C at a Glance Part and tables What it reconciles Part I Basic details Part II (5 to 8) Turnover in the accounts against turnover in GSTR-9, with reasons Part III (9 to 11) Tax payable against tax paid, with any additional amount Part IV (12 to 15) Input tax credit in the accounts against GSTR-9, with reasons Part V (16) Tax payable on any credit difference that cannot be explained Change 1: Table 5N, Total Turnover (GSTR-9) Earlier: 4N + 5M – 4G. Now: 4N + 5M – 4G – 4G1. Table 4G1 is filled by the e-commerce operator. It shows supplies on which the operator itself pays tax under section 9(5), for example local delivery services, which the guide itself uses as an illustration. Those supplies belong to the suppliers, not to the operator. Worked Example and Practical Effect Example. An e-commerce operator has the following for the year: own taxable supplies ₹3,00,00,000, reverse-charge purchases ₹10,00,000 (4G), supplies on which it pays tax under 9(5) ₹2,00,00,000 (4G1), and non-taxable supplies ₹20,00,000 (5M). Table 4N comes to ₹5,10,00,000, assuming no credit notes or amendments. Under the old formula: 5,10,00,000 + 20,00,000 – 10,00,000 = ₹5,20,00,000. Under the new formula: 5,20,00,000 – 2,00,00,000 = ₹3,20,00,000. Effect: the operator’s own turnover is ₹3.2 crore. The old formula showed ₹2 crore more, and that figure would then have to be explained in GSTR-9C. Check any template carried over from last year. Change 2: Table 8I, IGST on Imports (GSTR-9) Earlier: G – H. Now: G – (H + H1). Worked Example and Practical Effect Example. A client paid ₹10,00,000 as IGST on imports in FY 2025-26 (G). It took ₹7,00,000 as credit in the year (H) and the remaining ₹3,00,000 in April 2026, within the time allowed (H1). Old formula: 10,00,000 – 7,00,000 = ₹3,00,000 difference. New formula: 10,00,000 – (7,00,000 + 3,00,000) = nil. Effect: the old way showed a difference although every rupee of credit had been taken in time. A preparer would have gone looking for an error that was not there. Change 3: Reclaim of Credit Under Rule 37 and 37A (GSTR-9) Under the second proviso to section 16(2) read with Rule 37, credit must be reversed if the supplier is not paid within 180 days, and it can be reclaimed once payment is made. Worked Example and Reporting in Table 6H Example. An invoice dated 10/03/2026 carries credit of ₹18,000. The supplier is not paid in time, so the credit is reversed in September 2026. The client pays in October 2026 and reclaims the ₹18,000. What the new edition says: the reclaim is credit of the year in which it is reclaimed. It is shown in Table 6H of the FY 2026-27 return. It is not shown in Table 8C or Table 13 of the FY 2025-26 return, even though the invoice belongs to that year. Effect: the older edition had a long set of advisory scenarios for this. Any notes made from them should be set aside. Change 4: Table 17, HSN Summary (GSTR-9) For FY 2025-26, Table 17 is pre-filled from Table 12 of GSTR-1/1A, and the GSTR-9 dashboard lets you download the details. Worked Example: Changes in GST Rates During the Year Example. The guide’s own apparel illustration shows garments above the value limit moving from 12% to 18% on 22/09/2025. A client selling such garments all year has the same HSN code at two rates. Effect: the code may have to appear twice in Table 17, once at 12% for supplies up to 21/09/2025 and once at 18% for supplies from 22/09/2025. Compare each line with the books. Change 5: Post-Sale Discounts (GSTR-9) The guide now records that Circular No. 212/6/2024-GST dated 26/06/2024 was withdrawn by Circular No. 253/10/2025-GST dated 01/10/2025. Earlier, a supplier had to show that the recipient had reversed the credit, through a certificate or declaration. That is no longer needed, so there is no reason to ask for one. The guide also mentions a discount clause inserted in section 34(1) by the Finance Act, 2026, and says it takes effect from a date still to be notified. Until the notification is out, I would not rely on it. Change 6: Table 7D1 (GSTR-9C) Earlier text: read like recipient-side reverse charge. Now: the supplier reports here supplies on which the e-commerce operator pays tax under section 9(5). Worked Example: Supplies Through E-Commerce Operators Example. A local delivery provider whose services are supplied through an operator that pays the tax under section 9(5) has ₹6,00,000 of such supplies in the year. The provider shows ₹6,00,000 in Table 7D1. Effect: a preparer reading the old wording could have placed reverse-charge purchases here instead. Table 7F also changed: taxable turnover is taken as (4N – 4G – 4G1) + (10 – 11), which follows the 5N change above. Two smaller points: Table 7B now gives un-denatured extra neutral alcohol or rectified spirit used to make liquor for human consumption as an example of a non-taxable supply, and the payment column of Table 11 now says cash or ITC, where it said cash earlier. Notification 05/2026-CT: Continuation of GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C Relaxations Both guides carry an extract of Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26/10/2026, which continues the optional reporting in certain tables of GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C for FY 2025-26 onwards. The GSTR-9 guide adds that the grouped instruction for Tables 17 and 18 does not make Table 17 generally optional. A corrigendum followed on 18/11/2026, so read the notification and the corrigendum together. What to Do This Week: Practical Compliance Checklist 1. Correct the 5N and 8I formulas in the templates. 2. For e-commerce operators, check 4G1 and carry the effect into 9C Table 7F. For suppliers selling through an operator that pays under 9(5), check 9C Table 7D1. 3. Show Rule 37/37A reclaims in 6H of the year of reclaim. 4.
