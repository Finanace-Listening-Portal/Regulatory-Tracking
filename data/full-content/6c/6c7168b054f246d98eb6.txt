Vs Commissioner of Customs (Chennai II) (CESTAT Chennai) Summary: The Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), Chennai, in Samsung India Electronics Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner of Customs (Chennai II), dismissed the importer’s appeal concerning the classification of imported split air conditioners under the Customs Tariff. Samsung India Electronics Pvt. Ltd. had imported split air conditioners from its group company, Samsung Electronics Co. Ltd., Korea, and declared their classification under Customs Tariff Heading (CTH) 84151010. The assessing authorities disputed this classification and, following adjudication through Order-in-Original No. 37449/2015 dated 11 May 2015, classified the goods under CTH 84158210, treating it as the specific applicable tariff entry. The Commissioner of Customs (Appeals-II), Chennai, upheld the classification, resulting in the appeal before the Tribunal. During the hearing, both parties brought to the Tribunal’s attention conflicting decisions in Daikin Air-Conditioning India Pvt. Ltd. Vs Principal Commissioner of Customs, Nhava Sheva, Raigad, reported at 2020 (374) E.L.T. 768 (Tri. Mumbai), and ETA General Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner of Customs, Chennai-II, reported at 2016 (341) E.L.T. 140 (Tri. Chennai). In view of the conflicting interpretations, the matter was referred to the President for constitution of a Larger Bench. By its decision dated 24 September 2026, the Larger Bench held that the imported 2-ton split air conditioners incorporating refrigerating units were appropriately classifiable under CTH 84158210. It rejected the classification under CTH 84151010 adopted in Daikin and also disagreed with the classification under CTH 84158310 adopted in ETA General, observing that the latter entry concerned split air conditioners not incorporating refrigerating units. The Larger Bench emphasised that a specific tariff entry must receive precedence over a generic entry. Following the Larger Bench ruling, the Division Bench comprising Shri P. Dinesha, Member (Judicial), and Shri Vasa Seshagiri Rao, Member (Technical), held that Samsung India’s declared classification was incorrect and that the appropriate classification was CTH 84158210. Accordingly, the Tribunal found no merit in the appeal and dismissed it through Final Order No. 41162/2026 dated 3 November 2026. The decision reinforces the application of the most specific tariff description when determining the classification of imported split air conditioners incorporating refrigerating units. Cases Discussed Daikin Air-Conditioning India Pvt. Ltd. Vs Principal Commissioner of Customs, Nhava Sheva, Raigad, 2020 (374) E.L.T. 768 (CESTAT Mumbai). The earlier decision classified split air conditioners of 2-ton capacity under CTH 84151010. The Larger Bench held that this classification did not lay down the correct law. ETA General Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner of Customs, Chennai-II, 2016 (341) E.L.T. 140 (CESTAT Chennai). The earlier decision classified the relevant goods under CTH 84158310. The Larger Bench rejected this approach because that tariff entry related to split air conditioners not incorporating a refrigerating unit. FULL TEXT OF THE CESTAT CHENNAI ORDER Brief facts of this Appeal are that the Appellant during the course of its business, had imported ‘Split Air Conditioners’ from its group Company viz. M/s. Samsung Electronics Co Ltd., Korea, and accordingly filed Bill of Entry for clearance of the same. The Appellant had classified the same under CTH 84151010 which was disputed by the Assessing Group, which resulted in the consequential issuance of Show Cause Notice and Order-in-Original upon adjudication vide Order-in-Original No.37449/2015 dated 11.05.2015. The Adjudicating Authority accordingly classified the items in question under CTH 84158210 since, according to the Revenue, the items in question viz. ‘Split Air Conditioners’ which has a specific entry. Aggrieved by the same, an Appeal was filed before Commissioner (Appeals) unsuccessfully, which has given rise to the Appeal before this Forum. 2. Heard Shri S.Ganesh Aravindh, Ld. Advocate for the Appellant and Smt. O.M. Reena, Ld. Departmental Representative for the Respondent. 3. When the matter was being heard, both the parties brought to the notice of the Division Bench that there were conflicting views expressed by different Benches of the Tribunal in the cases of — (i) M/s Daikin Air-Conditioning India Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Pr. Commissioner of Customs, Nhava Sheva, Raigad. [2020 (374) E.L.T. 768 (Tri. Mumbai)] (ii) M/s ETA General Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Commissioner of Customs, Chennai-II. [2016 (341) E.L.T. 140 (Tri. Chennai)] – as a result of which, the Bench felt it proper to refer the matter to the Hon’ble President for constituting a Larger Bench. The Larger Bench accordingly vide its decision dated 24.09.2026, concluded that the ‘Split Air Conditioners’ in question are more appropriately classifiable under CTH 84158210 since specific entry has to be given primacy over a generic entry and most specific entry needs to be applied. 4. Accordingly, this Appeal is taken up for disposal. The relevant portion of the order of the Larger Bench is reproduced for ready reference: “10. In view of the foregoing discussion, the reference is answered in the manner as below: “We hold that the decision in M/s Daikin AirConditioning Pvt. Ltd. (supra) does not lay down the correct law insofar as it classifies split air conditioners of 2- ton capacity under CTH 84151010. We further hold that the view taken in M/s ETA General Pvt. Ltd. (supra) classifying such goods under CTH 8415 83 10 is also not correct, as the said entry pertains to split air conditioners not incorporating a refrigerating unit. Since the impugned goods are undisputedly 2-ton capacity split air conditioners incorporating a refrigerating unit, their appropriate and specific classification is under CTH 8415 82 10. The reference is, accordingly, answered in the above terms.” 5. In view of the above decision of the Larger Bench, the classification declared by the Appellant is not correct and the correct classification is CTH 84158210 and hence, the Appeal filed by the Appellant lacks merit. Accordingly, the Appeal is dismissed.
