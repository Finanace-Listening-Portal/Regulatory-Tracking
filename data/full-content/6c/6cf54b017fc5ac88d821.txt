Advances grew 29% year-on-year to Rs 46,800 crore in the second quarter of FY27, with non-microfinance disbursements rising 31%.Small business loans, including housing finance, and vehicle finance together account for around 75% of the bank's loan book, providing a diversified secured lending base. The unsecured portfolio, primarily comprising microfinance loans, remains stable at around 12% of advances, with the bank aiming to keep its share below 15% in the medium term, according to Motilal Oswal Financial Services. Advt Vehicle finance and small business loan portfolios grew 16% year-on-year each in the September quarter, while housing finance and micro, small and enterprise (MSE) finance expanded 27% and 38%, respectively. The growth in these segments supported a 31% increase in non-microfinance disbursements, indicating continued momentum in the bank's core lending businesses.Deposit base strengthensThe bank's deposit mobilisation also strengthened, with deposits growing 19% year-on-year and 7% sequentially to Rs 52,500 crore. Deposit growth outpaced advances, bringing the credit-deposit ratio down to 89.1% in the second quarter from 91.3% in the previous quarter and around 92% in the March quarter. Deposits are estimated to grow at around 20% CAGR over FY26-28, ahead of the projected loan growth.Retail deposits accounted for around 60% of total deposits, supporting the bank's efforts to build a granular liability franchise. Its current and savings account (CASA) ratio improved 60 basis points sequentially to 25.7%, against management's guidance to maintain it at 28-30%. The bank also mobilised $110 million through the FCNR(B) window without leverage, adding to its surplus liquidity.However, surplus liquidity and higher funding costs affected margins. Net interest margin (NIM) contracted 55 basis points sequentially to 6.69% in the September quarter, while the cost of funds increased seven basis points to 7.12%. The bank expects a further 8-10 basis points increase in funding costs in the December quarter due to repricing of existing deposits. Advt Management expects surplus liquidity to be largely deployed during the December quarter, supporting an 18-20 basis point recovery in NIM by the March quarter. The bank has indicated a medium-term NIM target of around 7%.Asset quality continued to improve, with gross non-performing assets declining to 2.29% in the September quarter from 2.60% in the March quarter, while net non-performing assets fell to 0.70% from 0.72%. Credit costs moderated to 0.94% from 1.37% sequentially. The microfinance portfolio also showed signs of stabilisation, with its net slippage ratio improving to 2.64% from 2.76% in the previous quarter. Advt Operating leverage is expected to improve gradually as the bank's businesses scale up and productivity initiatives take effect. Motilal Oswal estimates the cost-to-income ratio will moderate to 66.4% by FY28 from 71.7% in FY26. The bank opened four branches year-to-date in FY27, compared with 66 in the whole of FY26, while expanding cross-selling of asset products through its liability branches.Management has raised its FY27 return on assets guidance to around 1.35% from 1.2% earlier and is targeting around 1.5% in FY28.
