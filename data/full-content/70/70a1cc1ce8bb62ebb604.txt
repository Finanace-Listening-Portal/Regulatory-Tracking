Summary: HDFC Life Click 2 Protect Ultimate is a live non-linked, non-participating individual term-insurance product under UIN 101N179V01. Its core benefit is death cover of ₹1 crore to ₹3 crore, with an accelerated terminal-illness benefit, an optional return-of-premium structure and a choice of lump-sum or instalment settlement. The insurer’s official brochure also places strict conditions around its “100% claim assurance” description: the policy must be in force, premiums must be fully paid, and contractual exclusions still apply. This guide separates those conditions from the marketing shorthand, explains the six-month terminal-illness waiting period, premium continuity, suicide exclusion, claim documentation, grievance route, GST exemption“current GST exemption for qualifying individual life policies and the Income-tax Act, 2025 treatment of policy proceeds. It is an informational review of the contract, not an endorsement or a personalised insurance recommendation. That identity matters because the similarly named Click 2 Protect Super is shown in the insurer’s withdrawn-products material. A prospective buyer should therefore match the exact product name and UIN on the proposal, benefit illustration and policy schedule instead of relying on the wider Click 2 Protect brand name. The plan is described as non-linked, non-participating and individual. Benefits do not depend on investment-unit values and the contract does not participate in bonuses. It is primarily a protection contract, although the optional return-of-premium feature can create a maturity payment. The insurer states that entry is from age 18 to 50, maturity can extend to age 85, the policy term can run up to 40 years and the basic sum assured ranges from ₹1 crore to ₹3 crore. Acceptance, pricing and medical requirements remain subject to underwriting. For single-pay policies, the sum assured on death is the highest of 125% of the single premium, the sum assured on maturity and the basic sum assured. For limited-pay and regular-pay policies, it is the highest of ten times the annualised premium, the sum assured on maturity and the basic sum assured. Taxes, rider premiums, underwriting extras and modal loadings are excluded from the defined annualised premium. The policy schedule remains decisive because it records the selected values and options. Conditions attached to the claim assurance label The brochure uses “100% claim assurance”, but its footnote is essential. The policy must be in force on the date of death, all premiums must have been paid and the exclusions in Part F continue to apply. The phrase therefore does not erase disclosure duties, exclusions, policy-status rules or the need for a valid claim. It should not be read as a guarantee that every submitted claim will be paid irrespective of the contract. Accurate proposal disclosures on health, occupation, tobacco use, existing insurance and other underwriting questions are particularly important. Terminal illness and return of premium choices Accelerated terminal illness benefit The product accelerates the death benefit on diagnosis of a specified terminal illness, subject to the policy definition. The official brochure imposes a six-month waiting period from the policy risk commencement date. If the illness is diagnosed during that period, the terminal-illness benefit is not payable. Acceleration means the amount paid reduces or exhausts the death cover rather than creating an unrelated second cover. The contract also states that acceleration is not available for diagnosis after age 80. Claimants should obtain the exact medical evidence and definition from the issued policy document. Maturity and return of premium Without the return-of-premium option, the sum assured on maturity is nil. If the option was selected and additional premium was paid, survival to maturity can produce a payment equal to 100% of total premiums paid as defined by the contract. That definition excludes taxes, rider premiums and underwriting extras. The maturity amount should not be described as a return on investment: it is a contractual refund measure attached to a protection policy, and the higher premium paid for the option must be considered separately. Premium continuity surrender and smart exit The grace period is 30 days for yearly, half-yearly and quarterly premiums and 15 days for monthly premiums. Risk cover continues during the grace period, but an unpaid premium for the policy year may be deducted from a valid benefit. The brochure distinguishes the value consequences of stopping premiums according to pay mode and whether return of premium was chosen. A return-of-premium policy may acquire paid-up or surrender value only after the stated premium conditions are satisfied; other structures can lapse without paid-up value. The brochure also describes a Smart Exit Benefit equal to total premiums paid, subject to strict timing and eligibility conditions. It can be exercised only after policy year 25 and not in the last five policy years, the policy must be in force and the option is unavailable where return of premium was selected. Revival may be requested within five years from the first unpaid premium and before the policy term ends, subject to overdue premiums, interest, evidence of continued insurability and the insurer’s underwriting policy. Because current interest rates can change under the stated formula, this article does not treat a displayed rate as a permanent charge. Exclusions disclosures and the free look period For death by suicide within 12 months from commencement of risk or revival, the policy states that the nominee or beneficiary is entitled to at least 80% of total premiums paid up to death or the available surrender value, whichever is higher, provided the policy is in force. This is a specific contractual outcome and not the full basic sum assured. Other claim conditions and definitions must be read in the policy bond, especially where death, terminal illness, policy revival or an optional benefit is involved. A policyholder who disagrees with the issued terms may return the policy within 30 days from receipt, whether received electronically or otherwise, stating the reasons. The applicable deductions and refund mechanics are governed by the policy and current IRDAI rules. The free-look period is best used to compare the policy schedule, proposal copy and customised benefit illustration with what was understood at sale. Any inconsistency in age, smoking status, medical history, nominee details, premium term or chosen option should be raised promptly in writing. Claim filing and grievance escalation A claimant should notify HDFC Life promptly and use the insurer’s current claim channel. Typical documentation includes the claim form, policy details, claimant identity and bank evidence, death certificate and additional records required for the cause and circumstances of death. The exact request depends on the claim; medical records, hospital papers, police documents or post-mortem records may be necessary in relevant cases. Submitting complete documents does not replace the insurer’s contractual review, and the claimant should retain acknowledgements and copies. The insurer’s grievance page permits a branch complaint, online submission, registered-email complaint and telephone contact. It states an initial regulatory turnaround time of 14 days, followed by internal Level One and Level Two escalation. The Grievance Redressal Officer can be contacted at the official channel shown on the insurer’s page. If the internal response remains unsatisfactory, the policyholder or claimant may use Bima Bharosa and, where eligible, approach the Insurance Ombudsman. Forum eligibility and limitation rules should be checked at the time of filing. A quote should still be checked for components that may fall outside the exemption, including any separately supplied service or product whose tax treatment differs. The proposal and premium receipt should show the actual charge. The GST position should not be assumed merely because the contract is sold by a life insurer. Income tax on premium and proceeds For tax year 2026–27, the Income-tax Act, 2025 is the current statute. Schedule II excludes qualifying sums received under a life-insurance policy from total income subject to its conditions, and preserves the special treatment of amounts received on death. A maturity or exit payment requires a separate check against policy issue date, premium thresholds, aggregation rules and the statutory conditions. The death benefit should not be casually grouped with a taxable maturity amount. Any premium deduction also depends on the taxpayer, the applicable deduction provision, payment facts and the chosen tax regime. Policyholders should use the current return instructions and obtain tax advice for high-premium, assigned, business-owned or non-resident cases. Frequently Asked Questions Is HDFC Life Click 2 Protect Super still the current product? No. The insurer’s withdrawn-products material lists Click 2 Protect Super V08 as withdrawn on 5 August 2025. This article covers the separately listed live Click 2 Protect Ultimate under UIN 101N179V01. Does 100% claim assurance mean every claim must be paid? No. The brochure ties the description to an in-force policy, fully paid premiums and the exclusions in the contract. Disclosure, policy status, evidence and claim validity still matter. What is the minimum cover under Click 2 Protect Ultimate? The insurer’s brochure lists a minimum basic sum assured of ₹1 crore and a maximum of ₹3 crore, subject to underwriting and the issued policy schedule. Is terminal illness an additional payment over the death benefit? It is an accelerated death benefit. Payment reduces or can exhaust the death cover, and a six-month waiting period plus the policy definition applies. Will the policy pay anything on maturity? Only if the return-of-premium option was selected. The defined maturity amount is 100% of total premiums paid; taxes, riders and underwriting extras are excluded from that definition. What happens if a premium is late? The contract provides a 30-day grace period for yearly, half-yearly and quarterly modes and 15 days for monthly mode. A valid claim during grace can be paid after deducting due premium. Are policy proceeds always tax-free? No blanket statement is safe. Death proceeds receive special protection, while maturity or exit proceeds must satisfy the Income-tax Act, 2025 conditions applicable to the policy and taxpayer.
