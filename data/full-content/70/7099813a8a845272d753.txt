Articles 4 min read Credit underwriting is moving from static scores to dynamic risk signals: Industry leaders India’s lending industry is moving beyond static bureau scores towards real-time cash-flow data, transaction behaviour and AI-led risk assessment, with fintech and NBFC leaders at GFF 2026 saying the shift could unlock credit for a much wider pool of borrowers. The panel also stressed that AI should strengthen human-led credit decisions rather than become a black box, while better data could help lenders price risk more accurately and turn rejected borrowers into future customers. Advt Cash flows add a new layer to credit assessmentSingh said merchant transaction data can give lenders a significantly deeper understanding of a business than a static credit score, particularly by capturing the velocity and consistency of transactions.“Velocity of transactions allows you to interpret merchants' capability and ability much faster than a static bureau. We see daily peak hours, transactions, seasonal variance, weekend effects and so on,” Singh said.According to him, transaction data also helps lenders identify early warning signals that may not immediately show up in conventional credit information.“If a merchant has taken a loan and gradually we see his transactions on the platform reducing, that itself is a much early warning signal that the customer will go into deep debt, right before even the person leaves you,” he said.Singh said bureau scores and cash flows serve different purposes in underwriting, with the former helping establish repayment intent and the latter providing a view of repayment capacity.“Bureau helps us identify willingness to pay by the customer, while cash flows help us understand the ability to pay. These are the two distinct parameters of basic underwriting. One cannot live without another,” he said. Advt AI moves from pilots to the core of lendingParagash said one of the biggest changes in the AI-led lending ecosystem is the need to move beyond experimentation and actually deploy models in balance-sheet and risk-management decisions.“AI has become cool. It is very widespread at the edges, but very cautious at the core. There are a number of pilots happening, but not many pilots are converting into a real balance sheet or risk-management decision,” Paragash said.She said Credit Saison India has focused on making technology more central to its operations, including using transformer models and machine-learning insights in customer-facing and risk-related processes. Advt According to Paragash, lenders also need to shorten the cycle between generating evidence from data and taking business decisions. That requires the right governance architecture and greater conviction around which data points can actually drive outcomes.She also stressed that risk discipline cannot be compromised as lenders adopt newer technologies.“Anything can be compromised, but risk cannot,” Paragash said, highlighting the importance of maintaining underwriting discipline through credit cycles.Technology is turning underwriters into pricing managersVarghese said the traditional underwriting process has changed significantly as technology has enabled lenders to process and analyse much larger volumes of data. Advt Earlier, credit managers would receive data, make sense of it and then apply their judgement. With technology increasingly handling data processing and risk signals, the role is shifting towards pricing and risk differentiation, he said.“The credit managers used to get all the data, try to make sense of the data and put the judgement on top of it. Now that has changed. The credit managers used to be called underwriters; now they are more of a pricing manager,” Varghese said.He said technology-led efficiency should ultimately benefit borrowers through better pricing rather than simply allowing lenders to charge more.“If technology has come and that also causes the pricing to be high, there is a little bit of a mismatch in the economy. Efficiency is improving, so we should be able to deliver at a reasonable price,” he said.Varghese added that technology is also allowing lenders to serve customers across multiple financial needs as their income and requirements evolve, rather than limiting them to a single credit product.AI should assist credit decisions, not become a black boxSandeep Singh said BharatPe Capital is not looking to hand over underwriting decisions entirely to AI. Instead, the company is using AI to extract relevant signals and reduce noise, while deterministic risk measures continue to drive credit decisions.“AI helps signal extraction and reduce noise, while deterministic risk measures drive credit decisions. That's our approach at the group level that we follow,” he said.The approach is also relevant to the growing focus on explainability in automated lending decisions.Singh said lenders are looking at ways to turn loan rejections into an opportunity to educate customers about what they need to improve before applying again.“If we are rejecting because over the last 30 days his transactions have not been to a certain level, we educate him on that. As he continues to interact and engage with the platform, he can deliver at the next level,” he said.This could eventually push lending towards a more continuous underwriting model, where a customer's eligibility is reassessed as fresh transaction and financial data becomes available.Click here to read our coverage of the Global FinTech Fest 2026.
