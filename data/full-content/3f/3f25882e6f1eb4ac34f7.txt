Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Vs Nitin Gupta (Karnataka High Court) Introduction An assessment cannot be reopened merely because the Assessing Officer wishes to make a larger disallowance on an issue already examined during scrutiny. In DCIT v. Nitin Gupta, the Karnataka High Court dismissed the Revenue’s appeal against the quashing of reassessment proceedings for AY 2015-16. The Division Bench relied on the Revenue’s concession recorded by the Supreme Court in Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal [2024] 469 ITR 46 (SC). Independently, it held that reopening the assessment to reconsider the same Section 54F claim constituted an impermissible change of opinion. Facts of the Case The assessee filed his return of income on 28 November 2015, declaring long-term capital gains of ₹16,60,20,182 arising from the transfer of shares. Against these gains, he claimed exemption of ₹2,24,21,692 under Section 54F of the Income-tax Act, 1961. In support of the exemption, the assessee stated that he had purchased land for ₹1,44,00,000 and made payments of ₹78,10,000 towards construction of a residential house. The return was selected for scrutiny. During the original assessment, the Assessing Officer examined the exemption claim, including the construction payments made to the assessee’s relatives. The assessment order itself contained a table identifying the recipients and their relationship with the assessee. The Assessing Officer considered the particulars relating to construction inadequate and denied the exemption to the extent of ₹80,69,490. The scrutiny assessment was completed on 29 December 2017. Reopening to Disallow the Entire Claim Subsequently, reassessment proceedings were initiated on the ground that the assessee had failed to furnish proper particulars concerning the acquisition of the residential property. This time, the Assessing Officer sought to add the entire exemption claim of ₹2,24,21,692, relying on the same ground on which a partial disallowance had already been made. The assessee challenged the order dated 29 July 2022 under Section 148A(d) and the consequential notice under Section 148. The Single Judge allowed the writ petition, relying on the concession recorded in Rajeev Bansal. The Revenue carried the matter in appeal. Revenue’s Argument on Limitation The Revenue contended that the six-year limitation period for AY 2015-16 expired only on 31 March 2022. Since the original notice had been issued on 30 June 2021, it argued that the proceedings were within time. The Revenue also referred to Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal [2022] 444 ITR 1 (SC) concerning the treatment of notices issued during the transitional period following the introduction of the new reassessment regime. According to the Revenue, the concession in Rajeev Bansal did not invalidate the notice in the present case. High Court Applies the Recorded Concession The Division Bench rejected this contention. It referred to the concession recorded in paragraph 19(f) of Rajeev Bansal, concerning the dropping of notices issued on or after 1 April 2021 for AY 2015-16 in the context considered there. The Court held that the Revenue’s attempt to sustain the proceedings by relying on the six-year period was unpersuasive. The significance of this finding must be understood in the context of the transitional reassessment notices, TOLA and the Supreme Court proceedings considered by the Court. The decision should not be read as a general exemption of AY 2015-16 from every conceivable reassessment situation. Same Deficiency Cannot Justify a Second Examination The Court gave a separate and decisive reason for invalidating the reopening. The Section 54F claim had already been examined during the original assessment. Indeed, the Assessing Officer had made a disallowance precisely because certain particulars were considered insufficient. Consequently, the same alleged deficiency could not be relied upon again to invoke the extended limitation period. The Court further held that the proposed reopening amounted to a change of opinion and an attempt to review the assessment order, which was impermissible. Thus, the original assessment order itself demonstrated that the issue had received conscious consideration. Decision of the Court The Division Bench dismissed the Revenue’s appeal and upheld the Single Judge’s conclusion. The Section 148A(d) order, Section 148 notice and other consequential notices for AY 2015-16 were quashed. The judgment did not grant the entire Section 54F exemption on merits or reverse the original partial disallowance. It invalidated the subsequent reassessment proceedings. Author’s Comments The ruling is particularly useful where the original assessment order records enquiries, discusses the assessee’s explanation and makes a partial disallowance. Such an order provides direct evidence that the Assessing Officer had already formed an opinion. A desire to increase an earlier disallowance does not, by itself, confer jurisdiction to reopen the assessment. Where the alleged inadequacy of disclosure was already identified and acted upon, repeating that allegation cannot convert a review into a valid reassessment. Practitioners should therefore compare the recorded reopening grounds with the original questionnaires, replies and assessment findings. In an appropriate case, the strongest defence against reopening may lie in the Department’s own earlier assessment order. Cases Discussed Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal — [2024] 469 ITR 46 (SC) — Revenue concession regarding AY 2015-16 notices. Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal — [2022] 444 ITR 1 (SC) — Transitional reassessment notices. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT 1. For the reasons stated in the application, I.A.No.1/2026, the same is allowed. The delay of 85 days in filing the present appeal is condoned. 2. The Revenue has filed the present appeal impugning an order dated 14.10.2025 passed by the learned Single Judge in Writ Petition No.19074/2022 (T-IT). 3. The respondent (Assessee) had filed the said petition impugning an order dated 29.07.2022 passed by the learned Assessing Officer [AO] under Section 148A(d) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 [the Act] for the Assessment Year [AY] 2015-16 as well as the notice issued under Section 148 of the Act for the said AY. 4. The learned Single Judge found that the notices were unsustainable in view of the concession made by the Revenue in Union of India and others v. Rajeev Bansal1. 5. The Assessee filed its returns of income on 28.11.2015. The same was picked up for scrutiny and the Assessment proceedings culminated in an order dated 29.12.2017. 6. The Assessee had declared a Long-Term Capital Gain [LTCG] of ₹16,60,20,182/- arising from transfer of shares and had claimed exemption of ₹2,24,21,692/- under Section 54F of the Act. 7. The Assessee claimed that it made payments for construction of a residential house amounting to ₹78,10,000/- and purchased land for ₹1,44,00,000/-. The Assessment Order indicates that the Assessee had disclosed that ₹78,10,000/- was paid to his relatives. The Assessment Order sets out a tabular statement setting out the persons to whom payments were made and their relation with the Assessee. 8. The AO held that the Assessee had failed to provide the necessary details regarding construction of the house and therefore denied the exemption to the extent of Rs.80,69,490/-. 9. The notice under Section 148 of the Act is also premised on the same ground: that the Assessee failed to provide proper particulars regarding the acquisition of a residential property. The AO now seeks to make an addition of the entire amount of ₹2,24,21,692/- on the same ground on which the addition was made in the assessment order. 10. The learned counsel appearing for the Revenue submits that the concession made by the Revenue in the case of Rajeev Bansal (supra) is not applicable to the present case, as the notice under Section 148A of the Act was issued on 30.06.2021, which was directed to be considered as a notice under Section 148(b) of the Act, in terms of the decision of the Supreme Court in Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal2. 11. The Revenue contends that the limitation of six years expired on 31.03.2022 and therefore, the Taxation and Other Laws (Relaxation and Amendment of Certain Provisions) Act, 2020 [TOLA] would not be applicable. The learned counsel contends that since the notice had been issued prior to 31.03.2022, it was within the period of limitation. 12. 12. It is relevant to refer to the concession made by the Revenue, as recorded in the Supreme Court’s decision in Rajeev Bansal (supra). The relevant extract is set out below: “19. Mr. N.Venkataraman, learned Additional Solicitor General of India, made the following submissions on behalf of the Revenue: a. ** ** ** b. ** ** ** c. ** ** ** d. ** ** ** e. The Finance Act, 2021 substituted the old regime for re-assessment with a new regime. The first proviso to section 149 does not expressly bar the application of TOLA. Section 3 of TOLA applies to the entire Income-tax Act, including sections 149 and 151 of the new regime. Once the first proviso to Section 149(1)(b) is read with TOLA, then all the notices issued between April 1, 2021 and June 30, 2021 pertaining to the assessment years 2013-2014, 2014-2015, 2015-2016, 2016-2017, and 2017-2018 will be within the period of limitation as explained in the tabulation below: Assessment year Within 3 years Expiry of limitation read with TOLA for (2) Within six years Expiry of limitation read with TOLA for (4) 2013-2014 31-3-2017 TOLA not applicable 31-3-2020 30-6-2021 2014-2015 31-3-2018 TOLA not applicable 31-3-2021 30-6-2021 2015-2016 31-3-2019 TOLA not applicable 31-3-2022 TOLA not applicable 2016-2017 31-3-2020 30-6-2021 31-3-2023 TOLA not applicable 2017-2018 31-3-2021 30-6-2022 31-3-2024 TOLA not applicable f. The revenue concedes that for the assessment year 2015-16, all notices issued on or after 1 April 2021 will have to be dropped as they will not fall for completion during the period prescribed under the Taxation and Other Laws (Relaxation and Amendment of Certain Provisions) Act, 2020;” 13. We may note that the concession made by the Revenue was that all notices issued on or after 01.04.2021 in respect of AY 2015- 16 would have to be dropped. The Revenue’s contention that the limitation of six years is applicable in the facts of the present case is also unpersuasive. 14. The Assessee’s claim for deduction under Section 54F was the subject matter of the examination by the AO during the assessment proceedings. The assessment was made on the basis of non-disclosure of certain particulars. Thus, the same ground will not be available for an extended period of limitation. It is also clearly a case of change of opinion and the AO seeking a review of the assessment order by reopening the assessment, which is impermissible. For this reason as well, the impugned notice is liable to be set aside. 15. In view of the facts noted above, we concur with the learned Single Judge’s conclusion that the impugned notice is unsustainable. Consequently, the order under Section 148A(d) of the Act, the notice under Section 148 and other notices issued pursuant thereto for the AY 2015-16 stand quashed. 16. The appeal is, accordingly, dismissed. Notes: 1. [2024] 469 ITR 46 (SC); 2. [2022] 444 ITR 1 (SC).
