} } Listen to This ArticleBy Saikat Das The power arm of Jindal Group has signed an offshore loan of about $470 million to help fund a power project in Botswana, according to people familiar with the matter, as one of India’s largest conglomerates boosts its footprint in the African nation. A group of three Indian lenders — Axis Bank Ltd., ICICI Bank Ltd. and Export-Import Bank of India — provided the nine-year door-to-door facility to Jindal Power Ltd., the people said, asking not to be identified because the information is private. The loan is being syndicated with some global institutional lenders expected to join, the people said. The financing highlights the growing overseas ambitions of Indian energy companies as they pursue opportunities in markets where rising electricity demand is driving investment in generation and transmission infrastructure. Jindal Power raised about $500 million in an earlier round of financing in February meant for its expansion abroad. Jindal Power built a 700-megawatt coal power plant in Botswana that is scheduled for commissioning next month, according to its website. Mining operations in the Southern African nation are big electricity consumers and Botswana has been hoping to use its coal resources, estimated at more than 200 billion tonnes, to build a petroleum fuels industry to reduce its reliance on imports from South Africa and Namibia.
