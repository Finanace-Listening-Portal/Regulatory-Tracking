The petitioner contended that the proceedings and consequential orders were defective because the authorities had failed to establish any fraudulent misstatement or suppression of facts warranting invocation of Section 74. It was further alleged that the impugned order had been passed without properly considering Sections 50, 65 and 122 of the CGST Act. The petitioner also submitted that more than 50% of the amount deposited in its bank account, representing an amount equivalent to 100% of the disputed demand, had been blocked, adversely affecting its business operations. The Department raised a preliminary objection regarding the maintainability of the writ petition, arguing that an efficacious statutory appellate remedy was available and that the taxpayer’s objections involved factual questions requiring examination of the relevant records by the Appellate Authority. For a related decision concerning the scope of bank-account restrictions under GST, see No Indefinite GST Bank Freeze Under Section 83: Gauhati High Court. The High Court, comprising Acting Chief Justice Sanjeev Prakash Sharma and Justice Maneesh Sharma, held that the grounds raised by the petitioner were predominantly factual and could be appropriately adjudicated by the statutory Appellate Authority. Taking note of the petitioner’s willingness to deposit 10% of the disputed demand, the Court permitted it to file an appeal against the order dated 28 May 2025. The Court directed that, upon compliance with the applicable statutory pre-deposit requirement, the Appellate Authority must consider the appeal on merits without raising the issue of limitation, provide an opportunity of hearing to both parties and dispose of the appeal within four months from 26 November 2025. Accordingly, the writ petition was disposed of without adjudicating the merits of the GST demand, the allegations concerning invocation of Section 74 or the legality of the bank account blocking. The decision reinforces that factual disputes arising from GST adjudication proceedings should ordinarily be resolved through the statutory appellate mechanism. Related decisions include Telangana HC Allows GST Appeal Against Section 74 Demand Despite Delay and Bombay HC Restores GST Appeal After Pre-Deposit Shortfall Was Cured. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF RAJASTHAN HIGH COURT 1. A preliminary objection has been raised by the Department with regard to the admissibility of the present petition. 2. Learned Additional Advocate General Ms.Mahi Yadav appearing for the respondent-State submitted that alternative efficacious statutory remedy is available under the Central Goods and Service Tax Act, 2017 (for short ‘the Act’) where factual aspects can be examined by the Appellate Authority in relation to action taken by the respondents under Section 74 of the Act. The case of the petitioner is that the proceedings under Section 74 of the Act are wholly laconic and even the orders do not reflect the reason for taking up a matter under Section 74 of the Act as there is no fraudulent misstatement or suppression on the part of the appellant. 3. It is also alleged that the order dated 28.05.2018 has been passed by the respondent without taking into consideration the provisions of Sections 50, 65 and 122 of the Act. 4. Admittedly, learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that more than 50% of the amount deposited in the account, which amounts to 100% of the demand, has been blocked in the account of the petitioner which has resulted the petitioner being suffered in the business. 5. Having noticed the preliminary objection of learned AAG and submission of the petitioner, we are of the firm view that so far as the grounds raised by the petitioner are concerned, the same are all factual in nature and can be best adjudicated after considering the record by the Appellate Authority. As the petitioner is ready to deposit the 10% of the demand amount, the appeal can be heard on merits. 6. Taking into consideration the aforesaid aspects, we allow the petitioner to file an appeal and if such an appeal is preferred against the order dated 28.05.2025, the Appellate Authority shall consider the same on merits without dwelling into the issue of limitation, subject to pre-deposit as required. The Appellate Authority shall give an opportunity of hearing to both the parties and decide the appeal within a period of four months from today. 7. The writ petition is disposed of accordingly.
