Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Shripal Sevantilal Morakhia Vs Bhrugesh Amin (NCLAT Delhi) Summary: The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, Principal Bench, New Delhi, dismissed the appeal filed by Shripal Sevantilal Morakhia challenging the approval of the resolution plan concerning Smaaash Entertainment Private Limited. The Tribunal held that the appeal had become infructuous following the rejection of a subsequent application raising substantially the same objections against the resolution plan. The appeal challenged the Adjudicating Authority’s order dated 7 May 2025 in I.A. No. 69 of 2024, through which the resolution plan had been approved. The appellant had initially filed an application objecting to the plan but subsequently withdrew it. Thereafter, another application, I.A. No. 1985/MB/2025, was filed challenging the plan approval. The subsequent application was rejected by the Adjudicating Authority on 17 February 2026. During the hearing on 16 March 2026, counsel for the respondents submitted that the rejection rendered the pending appeal infructuous. The appellant indicated that steps were being taken to challenge the subsequent order, and the matter was adjourned to 23 March 2026. At the hearing on 23 March 2026, the appellant sought permission to place additional documents on record. However, the NCLAT observed that the allegations proposed to be pursued in the appeal had already been raised in the subsequent application, which stood dismissed. The Tribunal concluded that the pending appeal need not proceed further and dismissed it as infructuous. It expressly preserved the appellant’s liberty to challenge the order dated 17 February 2026 in accordance with law. Counsel for the Resolution Professional also informed the Tribunal that the resolution plan had been fully implemented. The order was confined to the procedural status of the appeal. The Tribunal did not undertake a fresh examination of the merits of the approved resolution plan or the appellant’s substantive objections. FULL TEXT OF THE NCLAT JUDGMENT/ORDER Ld.
