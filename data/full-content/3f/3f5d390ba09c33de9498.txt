Purple Style Labs, Deepa Jewellers and Rays of Belief are among the mainboard offerings around the start of September, while Farm Peace, Phychem Technologies, Shanti Inorganics and Ashutosh Fibre are among the SME issues. The seven issues identified in the opening-week calendar together are looking to raise more than Rs 1,400 crore. Advt The biggest potential trigger, however, is Jio Platforms. The company has received Sebi approval for a proposed IPO of around Rs 37,700 crore, which would make it India's largest IPO if launched at that size. NSE is also considering a public issue of around Rs 30,000 crore, although its launch timing has not been confirmed.If either of the two mega issues comes to market in September, fundraising could rise sharply, with the month's total potentially approaching Rs 70,000 crore depending on the issue launched, its final size and the broader IPO pipeline.The possibility of a record September comes after a sharp revival in primary-market activity in July and August. Companies raised more than Rs 28,000 crore through IPOs in July, while 22 companies raised over Rs 23,000 crore in August. The August fundraising was the highest in a year in terms of the number of companies accessing the market.The revival follows a subdued first half of CY26, when market volatility, valuation concerns, foreign investor outflows and geopolitical tensions weighed on IPO activity. The Nifty 50 fell around 9% during H1 CY26, prompting several companies to defer their fundraising plans.Primary market boomMarket conditions improved in July and August, helping restore investor appetite. The Nifty 50 gained around 1.8% from the end of June through the first two months of the second half, while IPO listing performance improved substantially.The average listing-day gain of 25 IPOs that debuted between July 1 and August 19 stood at 20.9%, compared with 1.72% in H1 CY26. Across 47 mainboard IPOs during the year, the average listing gain was 11.9%. Advt The stronger listing performance has provided a more favourable backdrop for September's IPO pipeline. Companies that had held back their plans amid weaker market conditions now have greater incentive to proceed, while merchant bankers have a sizeable pool of approved and filed offerings to bring to market.The broader pipeline includes large companies such as Zepto, PhonePe and Manipal Health, among others. However, these companies should not be treated as confirmed September IPOs, as their launch dates have not been announced.The pipeline remains sizeable. Since March 2025, 146 companies have received Sebi approval, while 67 are awaiting regulatory approval, 65 have filed DRHPs and another 33 have filed confidential DRHPs. This gives the primary market a substantial pool of potential issuers beyond the current September calendar. Advt The surge in IPO activity will nevertheless test the market's ability to absorb a large supply of new equity. Large offerings from Jio Platforms and potentially NSE could compete for institutional and retail liquidity with smaller mainboard and SME issues coming to market at the same time.Investor returns also remain uneven despite the improvement in sentiment. The average gain from issue price across 407 IPOs stood at 66% as of July 31, but 17% of companies were trading 25-50% below their issue price and another 9% were more than 50% below the issue price.
