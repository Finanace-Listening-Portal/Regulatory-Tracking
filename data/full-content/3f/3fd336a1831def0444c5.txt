Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Hallegoudru Palakshireddy Vs ITO (ITAT Bangalore) Summary: The Bangalore Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), comprising Prashant Maharishi, Vice President, and Sandeep Singh Karhail, Judicial Member, admitted additional evidence under Rule 29 of the ITAT Rules, 1963, furnished by an agriculturist to substantiate the sources of bank deposits and investment in immovable property. The Tribunal restored the matter to the Assessing Officer (AO) for de novo adjudication of the assessment determining total income at Rs.1,90,08,978 for Assessment Year 2016-17. The assessee had not filed his return of income for the relevant assessment year. Based on information available on the Insight Portal regarding cash deposits of Rs.76,31,000 in his account with Pragathi Krishna Gramin Bank and purchase of immovable property for Rs.90,77,000, proceedings under Section 148A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, were initiated. Since the assessee did not respond to the show cause notice, a notice under Section 148 was issued on 28 March 2023. The assessee neither filed his return in response to the notice nor complied with subsequent statutory notices issued during the assessment proceedings. The AO obtained the assessee’s bank statement through verification proceedings involving issuance of a notice under Section 133(6). The statement revealed aggregate credits of Rs.99,31,978, including cash deposits of Rs.76,31,000. In the absence of explanations and documentary evidence regarding the bank deposits and purchase of immovable property, the AO completed the best judgment assessment under Section 147 read with Sections 144 and 144B on 29 December 2023. The entire bank deposits were treated as unexplained money under Section 69A, and the total income was assessed at Rs.1,90,08,978. The CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, dismissed the assessee’s appeal on account of a delay of 209 days. Before the Tribunal, the authorised representative submitted that the assessee was an agriculturist who was unaware of the electronic notices issued during the assessment and appellate proceedings. Although his email address had been furnished in Form No.35, the assessee was unaware of the hearing notices issued by the CIT(A) and consequently could not substantiate the reasons for delay in filing the appeal. The assessee contended that the bank deposits and investment in immovable property were sourced from agricultural income and furnished additional evidence, including a bank confirmation letter, bank statements and details of agricultural landholdings. The Departmental Representative submitted that these documents required factual verification because the assessee had remained non-responsive before both the lower authorities. The Tribunal observed that the additions had been made on account of the absence of documentary evidence and explanations from the assessee. Although adequate opportunities had been provided during the earlier proceedings, the documents subsequently furnished were relevant for adjudicating the disputed additions. Accordingly, after considering the assessee’s application under Rule 29 of the ITAT Rules, 1963, the Tribunal admitted the additional evidence. Since the documents required factual verification, the Tribunal considered it appropriate, in the interest of fair play, to restore the matter to the AO for fresh adjudication. The AO was directed to verify and examine the documents furnished by the assessee and provide a reasonable opportunity of hearing before passing a fresh order. The assessee was permitted to submit further evidence supporting his claims and was directed to furnish any additional details sought by the AO. Accordingly, the impugned order was set aside and the appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. The Tribunal did not conclusively determine that the disputed deposits and property investment were sourced from agricultural income; the assessee’s explanation remained subject to verification during the fresh assessment proceedings. FULL TEXT OF THE ITAT JUDGMENT/ORDER The assessee has filed the present appeal against the impugned order dated 29.10.2025, passed under section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (“the Act”) by the learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi [“learned CIT(A)”] for the assessment year 2016-17. 2. In this appeal, the assessee has raised the following grounds: – 1. The order of the Learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) is bad in law and on facts. 2. The Learned CIT(A) erred in dismissing the appeal in limine without adjudicating the issues on merits, which is contrary to the provisions of law and principles of natural justice. 3. The Learned CIT(A) erred in not condoning the delay in filing the appeal without appreciating the bona fide reasons and circumstances of the appellant. 4. The Learned CIT(A) failed to appreciate that the right of appeal is a substantive right and should not be denied on mere technicalities. 5. The Learned Assessing Officer erred in passing the assessment order under section 147 read with section 144 without providing adequate opportunity of being heard. 6. The Learned AO erred in treating the entire bank deposits of ₹99,31,978/- as unexplained income under section 69A without considering withdrawals, rotation of funds, and nature of transactions. 7. The Learned AO erred in treating the purchase of immovable property amounting to ₹90,77,000/- as unexplained investment under section 69 without appreciating the actual sources of funds. 8. The Learned AO failed to consider that additions cannot be made on gross receipts without determining real income. 9. The Learned AO erred in invoking provisions of section 115BBE without proper justification. 10. The Learned AO erred in initiating penalty proceedings without proper basis. 11. The reassessment proceedings initiated under section 147 are bad in law due to lack of proper application of mind and absence of tangible material. 3. We have considered the submissions of both sides and perused the materials available on record. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee is an individual and for the year under consideration did not file his return of income. Based on information available on the Insight Portal that the assessee deposited cash to the tune of Rs. 76,31,000/- in his bank account maintained with Pragathi Krishna Gramin Bank and purchased immovable property for consideration of Rs. 90,77,000/-, proceedings under section 148A of the Act were initiated. However, the assessee did not respond to the show cause notice, and accordingly, notice under section 148 of the Act was issued on 28.03.2023. In response to the said notice, the assessee did not file his return of income. Further, the assessee also remained non-responsive to the statutory notices issued during the assessment proceedings. During the assessment proceedings, reference for physical verification was made to the Verification Unit requesting to obtain the details from the bank. Accordingly, notice under section 133(6) of the Act was served on the Pragathi Krishna Gramin Bank. In response to the said notice, the bank submitted the bank statement. From perusal of the same, it was noticed that out of the total credit of Rs. 99,31,978/-, the assessee deposited cash to an extent of Rs. 76,31,000/-. As regards the transaction of purchase of immovable property, the assessee also did not submit any details. Accordingly, the Assessing Officer (“AO”) proceeded to complete the assessment on a best judgment basis and, vide order dated 29.12.2023 passed under section 147 r.w.s. 144 r.w.s. 144B of the Act treated the entire deposits in the assessee’s bank account as unexplained money under section 69A of the Act. Further, in the absence of any proof regarding the source of purchase of immovable property and credit in the bank account of the assessee, the AO assessed the total income of the assessee at Rs.1,90,08,978/-. The learned CIT(A), vide impugned order, dismissed the appeal filed by the assessee on the ground of delay of 209 days. Being aggrieved, the assessee is in appeal before us. 4. During the hearing, the learned Authorised Representative (“learned AR”) submitted that the assessee is an agriculturist and was completely unaware of the notices issued during the assessment proceedings, and therefore, could not file any response to the said notices. The learned AR submitted that although the assessee’s e-mail address was provided in Form No. 35, the assessee was not aware of the hearing notices issued by the learned CIT(A) and therefore failed to substantiate the reasons for delay in filing the appeal before the learned CIT(A). On merits, the learned AR referred to the application seeking admission of additional evidence filed by the assessee. In this regard, the learned AR submitted that since the assessee was not aware of the proceedings before the AO or the learned CIT(A), the details now furnished by the assessee by way of additional evidence could not have been furnished. The learned AR submitted that the investment in the immovable property and deposits in bank account were on account of assessee’s agricultural income. In this regard, the learned AR referred to the bank confirmation letter, bank account statement of the assessee and details of agricultural land holding of the assessee. 5. On the other hand, the learned Departmental Representative submitted that all the details now furnished by the assessee require verification as the assessee remained non-responsive before the AO as well as before the learned CIT(A). 6. Having considered the submissions of both sides and perused the materials available on record, we find that as the assessee did not respond to the statutory notices issued during the assessment proceedings, the AO proceeded to complete the assessment on a best judgment basis and made the impugned additions on account of deposits in the assessee’s bank account and purchase of immovable property, as the assessee failed to substantiate the source of deposits and purchase of immovable property. It is evident from the record that, even before the learned CIT(A), the assessee did not respond to any hearing notices despite ample opportunities. It is the plea of the assessee that he is an agriculturist and was completely unaware of the e-assessment and appellate proceedings before NFAC. In order to substantiate the source of deposits in the bank account and the source for purchase of immovable property, the assessee has now filed additional evidence along with an application under Rule 29 of the ITAT Rules, 1963, seeking admission of this additional evidence. Since the impugned additions were made only due to lack of documentary evidence and response from the assessee, we are of the considered view that the details/documents now furnished by the assessee are relevant for adjudication of the issue at hand. Therefore, considering the submissions made by the assessee in his application filed under Rule 29 of the ITAT Rules, 1963, the said additional evidences are admitted. As the details furnished by the assessee require factual verification, in the interest and fair play, we restore the matter to the file of the AO for de novo adjudication after verification/examination of the details furnished by the assessee. Needless to mention, no order shall be passed without affording reasonable opportunity of hearing to the assessee. As the matter is restored to the file of the AO for consideration afresh, the assessee shall be at liberty to furnish any other details in support of his claim and is also directed to provide other details as may be sought by the AO for complete adjudication of the issues involved. With the above directions, the impugned order is set aside, and the grounds raised by the assessee are allowed for statistical purposes. 7. In the result, the appeal by the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes. Order pronounced in the open court on 17-Nov-2026.
