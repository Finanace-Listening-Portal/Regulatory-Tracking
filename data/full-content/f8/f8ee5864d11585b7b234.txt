Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Sanjeev Kumar Vs Ward-1(1)(3) (ITAT Delhi) Section 148 Notice Dated 24 March 2022 for AY 2015–16 Is Time-Barred and the Reopening Must Be Quashed Summary: The Delhi Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held that the reopening for AY 2015–16 initiated by a notice under section 148 dated 24 March 2022 was barred by limitation. Applying the Supreme Court’s order in Income Tax Officer v. Sri Sai Kumar Mateti, read with Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal, the Tribunal quashed the reopening and allowed the assessee’s appeal. Once the notice initiating the reassessment was held invalid, the grounds concerning the merits of the assessment became academic. Facts of the Case The Assessing Officer initiated reassessment proceedings against Sanjeev Kumar for AY 2015–16 by issuing a notice under section 148 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, on 24 March 2022. The proceedings were under section 147 read with sections 144 and 144B. The assessee approached the Tribunal against the order dated 8 April 2026 passed by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi. There was an eight-day delay in filing the appeal before the Tribunal. The Bench condoned the delay in the larger interest of justice and heard both parties. It took up the validity of the reopening as the first issue because the answer would determine whether the reassessment could survive. Limitation Went to the Root of the Reassessment The issue before the Tribunal was whether the section 148 notice dated 24 March 2022 could validly confer jurisdiction on the Assessing Officer to reopen the assessment for AY 2015–16. The assessment year and the date of the notice were clear from the record. The question was the legal effect of those facts in light of the intervening Supreme Court order. The distinction between the two delays in the case is important. The delay in filing the appeal concerned whether the Tribunal would entertain the assessee’s challenge. The limitation governing the section 148 notice concerned the Assessing Officer’s authority to initiate reassessment. Condoning the former enabled the Tribunal to examine the latter. Supreme Court’s Ruling in Sri Sai Kumar Mateti In Income Tax Officer v. Sri Sai Kumar Mateti, arising out of SLP (Civil) No. 8682 of 2024, the Supreme Court passed its order on 4 May 2026 in a batch of 103 appeals. The cases had been separated from a larger group on the premise that they might relate to AY 2015–16. The Revenue accepted that reassessment notices issued or proposed to be issued for that year in the cases before the Court would be time-barred in light of Rajeev Bansal, reported in 2024 SCC OnLine SC 2693. The Supreme Court referred to the Revenue’s concession recorded in paragraph 19(f) of Rajeev Bansal and reiterated in Sri Sai Kumar Mateti. It remitted the matters to the jurisdictional High Courts to verify the assessment year. Where the year was confirmed as AY 2015–16, the High Court’s task was confined to declaring the notice time-barred. For matters concerning other assessment years, the Court directed adjudication in terms of its earlier order dated 10 April 2026. Why the Tribunal Quashed the Reopening In Sanjeev Kumar’s case, the appeal and the impugned notice concerned AY 2015–16. The Tribunal recorded that the legal position emerging from the Supreme Court decisions went unrebutted by the Revenue. It applied the Supreme Court’s treatment of notices issued as well as proposed to be issued to the notice already issued to the assessee on 24 March 2022. The Tribunal accordingly quashed the reopening and allowed the appeal. The remaining pleadings on the merits were rendered academic. The assessee thus obtained relief on the threshold legal ground of limitation, without requiring the Bench to examine the factual or substantive disputes arising from the reassessment. Author’s Comments The practical significance of the ruling lies in the sequence of adjudication. Where reassessment is challenged on limitation, the first documents to examine are the section 148 notice and the record identifying the assessment year. In a case materially similar to Sanjeev Kumar’s, stating the precise notice date, identifying AY 2015–16, and citing paragraphs 4 to 7 of Sri Sai Kumar Mateti bring the jurisdictional objection into focus. The decision also illustrates the value of raising that objection distinctly in the appeal so the appellate authority can address it at the outset. The assessment year is central to the application of the precedent. The Supreme Court expressly required verification of that fact and gave separate directions for matters involving other years. In Sanjeev Kumar’s appeal, the year was already established, so the Tribunal could apply the ruling directly. The resulting relief was quashing of the reopening itself, which disposed of the appeal without a finding on the merits of the reassessment.Cases Discussed: ITO Vs Sri Sai Kumar Mateti (Supreme Court); Civil Appeal No. 6922/2026 arising out of SLP (Civil) No. 8682/2024; 04/05/2026; [2026] 187 taxmann.com 225 (SC). FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI This assessee’s appeal for assessment year 2015-16 arises against the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)/National Faceless Appeal Centre (for short, “CIT(A)/NFAC”), Delhi’s DIN & Order No. ITBA/NFAC/S/25/2026-27/1088334073(1) dated 08-Apr-2026, involving proceedings u/s 147 r.w.s. 144 r.w.s 144B of the Income Tax Act, 1961; hereinafter referred to as, “the Act,” , Heard both the parties. Case file perused. 2. Delay of 8 days in filing of the assessee’s instant appeal is condoned in the larger interest of justice. 3. Next comes the first and foremost issue of validity of the impugned section 148 reopening itself initiated in the assessee’s case vide learned Assessing Officer’s notice dated 24.03.2022. It transpires during the course of hearing with the able assistance coming from both the parties that hon’ble apex court’s recent landmark decision in the Income Tax Officer & Ors. vs. Shri Sai Kumar Mateti in SPL (Civil) No. 8682 of 2024 has already settled all issues at rest so far as such a reopening for assessment year 2015-16; in light of Union of India vs Rajeev Bansal (2024) 469 ITR 46(SC) is concerned, reading as under: 1. Delay condoned. 2. Leave granted. 3. This batch of civil appeals comprising 103 cases is slightly different than those cases which came to be disposed of by a three Judge Bench of this Court, including both of us (Surya Kant, CJI. and Joymalya Bagchi, J.), vide order dated 10.04.2026 passed in Civil Appeal No. 4716 of 2026 and connected matters. 4. The instant cases were segregated through the above-mentioned order on the premise that they may be pertaining to Assessment Year 2015-16. It is fairly conceded by Mr. N. Venkataraman, learned Additional Solicitor General of India, representing the Revenue, that in the assessment cases pertaining to the year 2015-16, the notices issued/proposed to be issued for reassessment would stand barred by time in light of the view taken by this Court in Union of India & Ors. v. Rajeev Bansal, 2024 SCC OnLine SC 2693. 5. There is no quarrel that if the instant cases are found to pertain to Assessment Year 2015-16, then the impugned notices are liable to be struck down outrightly in terms of the concession on behalf of the Department recorded in paragraph 19(f) of Rajeev Bansal (supra) and reiterated before us by the learned Additional Solicitor General of India. 6. However, if it is found that these cases pertain to an assessment year other than 2015-16, the respondent-assessees shall be entitled to raise all the contentions that have been permitted by this Court vide order dated 10.04.2026. Ordered accordingly. 7. Consequently, keeping in mind the reasons set out in order dated 10.04.2026, the impugned judgment in each appeal is set aside and the instant appeals are disposed of by remitting the matters to the jurisdictional High Courts for redetermination of the issues. As observed above, the High Courts shall firstly determine whether the matters pertain to Assessment Year 2015-16. If it is found to be so, no further adjudicatory exercise shall be required to be undertaken by the High Court, except to declare the notices as being time-barred in light of Rajeev Bansal (supra). However, if it is found that the case does not pertain to Assessment Year 2015-16, then all the issues shall be resolved in terms of the order dated 10.04.2026 passed in Civil Appeal No. 4716 of 2026. 8. Pending application(s), if any, including application(s) for substitution and impleadment/intervention stand closed. 4. These clinching legal developments have gone unrebutted from the Revenue side. We thus conclude that once their lordships foregoing detailed discussion has covered both the notices “issued” as well as “proposed to be issued” in assessment year A.Y. 2015-16 as time-barred in light of Rajeev Bansal (supra), the impugned reopening initiated in the assessee’s case in quashed in very terms. All other remaining pleadings between the parties on merits stand rendered academic. 5. The assessee’s appeal is allowed.
