} })(); The trusted-person mechanism will be voluntary and will require explicit consent from both the customer and the person registered. It will apply to electronic fund transfers through physical and digital channels beyond a threshold set by the customer, but will exclude UPI payments, merchant payments and recurring payments through standing instructions, e-mandates or NACH mandates, the RBI says. Under the mechanism, the trusted person will receive transaction details, including the amount, beneficiary information and transaction narration, and will have one hour to approve or reject the transaction. A transaction will be executed only if the trusted person provides consent. If the transaction is rejected or no response is received within the stipulated period, it will be declined. Also Read | How UPI became India’s biggest digital payments system — and its next challenge The RBI has clarified that a trusted person will not become a joint account holder, nominee, authorised signatory or beneficiary of the account, nor will the person be allowed to independently access or operate the account. The role will be restricted to authenticating transactions, without access to the customer’s account balance, deposits, investments or insurance details. Customers will also be able to change or remove their trusted person, modify transaction thresholds or opt out of the mechanism, subject to authentication and a 24-hour cooling period. Banks may impose additional checks, including a branch visit or tele-verification, in cases involving customers considered more vulnerable to cyber fraud.Story continues below this ad Debit controls The proposed stop-switch facility will allow customers to immediately disable all outward future debits associated with their accounts. Once activated, customers will not be able to initiate debit transactions, create new payment mandates or standing instructions, or register their account on new third-party UPI applications. However, inward credits will continue to be permitted. Existing recurring mandates, standing instructions and certain bank-initiated debits, including recovery of charges and loan instalments, will remain outside its scope. Banks will also have to provide customers with the option of disabling individual digital payment channels, setting transaction limits for individual transactions, beneficiaries or daily aggregate payments, and restricting channel usage to specified time windows. Cardholders will be able to switch on or off transactions and set limits for domestic and international usage across ATMs, point-of-sale terminals, online and contactless payments.
