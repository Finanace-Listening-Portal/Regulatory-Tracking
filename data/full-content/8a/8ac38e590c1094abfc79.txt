Advt CASA represents deposits held in current and savings accounts, which generally cost banks less than fixed deposits and other forms of borrowing. A decline in the CASA ratio indicates that low-cost deposits are accounting for a smaller share of banks' total deposits.According to the brokerage's analysis of 36 banks, comprising 24 private sector and small finance banks and 12 public sector banks, total deposits grew 16 per cent year-on-year and 6 per cent sequentially during the quarter.The growth was primarily supported by term deposits, which increased 19.2 per cent year-on-year and 7.8 per cent quarter-on-quarter. Current account deposits also grew 13.2 per cent year-on-year.However, the report highlighted that despite strong FCNR(B) deposit mobilisation, banks continued to rely on other sources of funding as liquidity conditions tightened.Certificate of deposit issuances remained elevated at around Rs 1 trillion in October 2026, compared to approximately Rs 0.6 trillion in October 2025.Banking system liquidity also narrowed to around Rs 2.4 trillion in October, equivalent to 0.8 per cent of deposits, compared with 2.8 per cent in September 2026, the report noted.Highlighting the importance of attracting low-cost domestic deposits, the brokerage said, "While FCNR (B) deposits provide breather in the near-term, we believe retail deposits and CASA deposit accretion remains a key monitorable in the coming quarters." Advt The report also cautioned that rising funding costs could influence banks' profit margins during the second half of FY27, as several lenders have increased their term deposit interest rates.It stated, "While full impact of FCNR (B) deposits on cost of funds will be visible in 3Q, few banks have also raised their term deposit rates and thus the extent of NIM improvement in 2HFY27 will depend on the increase in the cost of funds and extent of rate hikes."
