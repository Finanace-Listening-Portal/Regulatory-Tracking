Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Norwest Venture Partners X-Mauritius Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Delhi Bench, in Norwest Venture Partners X-Mauritius Vs DCIT, ITA No. 2311/Del/2023, dated 19 March 2024, held that a Mauritius-resident investment company holding a valid Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) was entitled to exemption under Article 13(4) of the India-Mauritius Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) in respect of long-term capital gains arising from shares acquired before 1 April 2017. The assessee, incorporated in Mauritius on 4 September 2006, held a Category-1 Global Business Licence and was registered with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) as a Foreign Venture Capital Investor. During Assessment Year 2020-21, it disclosed short-term capital gains of ₹15,03,99,442 as taxable income while claiming exemption for long-term capital gains of ₹3,02,30,69,163 arising from investments made before the treaty amendment. The Assessing Officer rejected the exemption on the ground that the assessee was allegedly a shell or conduit company controlled by its ultimate holding company in the United States. The Revenue alleged that the assessee lacked employees, independent business premises, operational expenditure and substantial commercial activities in Mauritius. It was further alleged that important investment decisions were effectively taken outside Mauritius and that the Mauritius entity had been interposed to obtain treaty benefits that would otherwise not have been available under the India-USA DTAA. Accordingly, the Assessing Officer denied the benefit of Article 13(4) and brought the long-term capital gains to tax. The Dispute Resolution Panel (DRP) directed factual verification, but the Assessing Officer retained the addition in the final assessment order. The assessee argued that it had been carrying on genuine investment activities since 2007, held a valid TRC issued by the Mauritius Revenue Authority, possessed a Global Business Licence and had complied with the requirements of Section 90 read with Rule 21AB by furnishing Form 10F. It relied upon CBDT Circular No. 789 dated 13 April 2000, the Supreme Court’s decision in Union of India Vs Azadi Bachao Andolan and the Bombay High Court’s ruling in Bid Services Division (Mauritius) Ltd. Vs Authority for Advance Ruling. The assessee also demonstrated that it continued making substantial investments in Indian companies after 1 April 2017, including investments of US$376,886,444, even though gains from shares acquired after that date became taxable under the amended treaty. It submitted that the limitation-of-benefits provisions under Article 27A, applicable to Article 13(3B), could not be invoked to deny grandfathering protection under Article 13(4). The Tribunal found that the assessee had furnished the necessary documentary evidence establishing its Mauritius tax residency and treaty eligibility. The Assessing Officer had not produced conclusive evidence demonstrating that the company’s management and control were outside Mauritius or that it was a sham arrangement. The Tribunal observed that the Mauritius authorities had issued the TRC and Global Business Licence after examining the relevant requirements, while SEBI had registered the assessee as a foreign venture capital investor as early as 17 July 2007. Its continued investment activities, including substantial investments after the treaty amendment, further supported the genuineness of its business. Relying on the judicial principles concerning Mauritius tax residency and the grandfathering provisions of the treaty, the Tribunal held that the capital gains from shares acquired before 1 April 2017 were exempt from taxation in India. The Tribunal also found serious procedural defects in the assessment proceedings. It held that the DRP could not effectively restore the issue to the Assessing Officer for reconsideration instead of deciding the objections on merits under Section 144C. Such a direction deprived the assessee of an opportunity of being heard because the final assessment under Section 144C(13) had to be completed without providing a further hearing. The Assessing Officer had also substantially repeated the draft assessment order without properly implementing the DRP’s directions. Accordingly, the Tribunal directed deletion of the long-term capital gains addition of ₹3,02,30,69,163. The legal and jurisdictional grounds were kept open as academic, and the assessee’s appeal was partly allowed. Cases Discussed: 1. Bid Services Division (Mauritius) Ltd. Vs Authority for Advance Ruling (Income Tax), Writ Petition No. 713 of 2021; 453 ITR 461; 148 taxmann.com 215 (Bombay High Court); 08.03.2023. Followed. The Bombay High Court held that a valid Mauritius TRC could not be disregarded merely on allegations of treaty shopping without supporting evidence establishing fraud, illegality or an artificial arrangement. It also recognised the grandfathering protection available to investments made before 1 April 2017. 2. Veg & Table Vs DCIT, ITA No. 2251/Del/2022 (ITAT Delhi); 31.10.2023. Cited by the assessee. Relied upon in support of the applicability of Article 13(4) of the India-Mauritius DTAA to qualifying capital gains and the availability of treaty benefits to Mauritius-resident investment entities. 3. MIH India (Mauritius) Ltd. Vs ACIT, ITA No. 1023/Del/2022 (ITAT Delhi); 16.11.2022. Cited by the assessee. Relied upon for the proposition that qualifying capital gains arising from investments made before 1 April 2017 remain protected under the India-Mauritius DTAA. 4. CIT Vs JSH (Mauritius) Ltd., [2017] 297 CTR 275 (Bombay High Court). Relied upon. The Bombay High Court recognised the significance of Mauritius tax residency, a Category-1 Global Business Licence and a valid TRC in determining eligibility for treaty benefits. The Tribunal referred to this judgment while considering whether the assessee was a genuine investor or a shell company. 5. Serco BPO (P.) Ltd. Vs Authority for Advance Ruling, (2015) 60 taxmann.com 433 (Punjab & Haryana High Court). Cited by the assessee. Relied upon in support of the evidentiary value of a Mauritius TRC and the availability of India-Mauritius DTAA benefits. 6. Vodafone International Holdings B.V. Vs Union of India, (2012) 341 ITR 1; 17 taxmann.com 202 (Supreme Court). Considered. The decision examines the treatment of cross-border investment structures, commercial substance, treaty benefits and the circumstances in which a transaction may be investigated as an artificial device. The assessee relied upon the judgment to distinguish legitimate investment arrangements from fraudulent treaty-abuse transactions. 7. Union of India Vs Azadi Bachao Andolan, (2003) 263 ITR 706 (Supreme Court). Followed. The Supreme Court upheld CBDT Circular No. 789 and recognised the significance of Mauritius tax residency certificates and the operation of tax treaty provisions under Section 90 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Tribunal relied upon these principles in allowing the assessee’s claim. Relevant Circulars and Treaty Provisions: 1. CBDT Circular No. 789 dated 13 April 2000: Provides that a TRC issued by the Mauritius authorities constitutes sufficient evidence of residential status and beneficial ownership for applying the India-Mauritius DTAA, as interpreted in the judicial decisions considered by the Tribunal. 2. CBDT Press Release dated 1 March 2013: Clarified the Government’s position concerning the evidentiary value of a TRC and confirmed the continued operation of CBDT Circular No. 789. 3. Article 13(4) of the India-Mauritius DTAA: Provides the relevant treaty protection for capital gains arising from qualifying investments in shares acquired before 1 April 2017. 4. Article 13(3A) and Article 13(3B) of the India-Mauritius DTAA: Govern the source-based taxation of capital gains from shares acquired on or after 1 April 2017, including the transitional concessional tax treatment. 5. Article 27A of the India-Mauritius DTAA: Contains limitation-of-benefits provisions concerning the concessional treatment under Article 13(3B). The assessee contended that these restrictions did not apply to its grandfathered investments under Article 13(4). 6. Section 90 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, read with Rule 21AB and Form 10F: Governs the documentary requirements and application of treaty benefits. The Tribunal recorded that the assessee had furnished the relevant documents. 7. Section 144C of the Income-tax Act, 1961: Governs proceedings before the Dispute Resolution Panel and the passing of final assessment orders. The Tribunal held that the DRP could not effectively remand the matter to the Assessing Officer instead of deciding the objections on merits.
