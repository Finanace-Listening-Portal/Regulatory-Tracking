Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Imran Shareef Mohammed Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) Section 115BBE Tax Rate for AY 2017–18 Is 30% Even Where a Section 69A Addition Is Upheld Summary: The Hyderabad Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal upheld an addition of ₹38.70 lakh under Section 69A for unexplained cash deposits during demonetisation but directed the Assessing Officer to apply the 30% tax rate under Section 115BBE for AY 2017–18. Following the Rajasthan High Court in Deepak Maratha v. Union, the Tribunal held that the amended 60% rate applies from AY 2018–19 onwards. The assessee thus obtained relief on the rate of tax, and the entire addition stood confirmed. Cash Deposits Explained as Gift and Business Receipts The assessee carried on a gold jewellery trading business under the name M/s I.S. Jewels and returned a total income of ₹3,70,150. During scrutiny, the Assessing Officer noticed cash deposits of ₹38,70,000 in the assessee’s Syndicate Bank account between 11 November and 20 December 2016. The assessee attributed ₹20,50,000 to a cash gift from his brother-in-law, Taher Affendi, an NRI working in Kuwait. He attributed the remaining ₹18,20,000 to jewellery business receipts. The Assessing Officer found the gift and the donor’s creditworthiness inadequately substantiated. He also found that the assessee had failed to produce bills, invoices or vouchers supporting the business receipt explanation. He accordingly added the entire amount as unexplained money under Section 69A and applied the higher rate under Section 115BBE. Gift Declaration and Bank Withdrawals Examined Against the Timeline The assessee relied on the gift declaration, the donor’s passport and details of withdrawals from the donor’s bank account. The CIT(A) noticed that the donor had departed India on 29 August 2016, whereas the assessee claimed to have received the gift on 30 October 2016. The withdrawals by self-cheque also took place after the donor’s departure. The CIT(A) considered this chronology and the subsequent deposits during demonetisation, and relied on the Supreme Court’s decision in Sumati Dayal v. CIT [1995] 214 ITR 801 (SC). Related TaxGuru analysis. Before the Tribunal, the assessee argued that cash could be withdrawn using a self-cheque even during the donor’s absence. The Tribunal examined that explanation alongside the chronology and the availability of a bank account in India. It found that the assessee had failed to explain why the gift was made in cash when it could have been transferred through banking channels. Applying the test of human probabilities to the circumstances taken together, it sustained the rejection of the gift explanation. Section 44AD Declaration and Evidence of Cash Availability The assessee also contended that ₹18,20,000 represented trade receipts declared under Section 44AD, and that the Assessing Officer had accepted the returned business income. The Tribunal focused on the evidence connecting those receipts with the deposits. It found that the assessee had produced neither sales bills or invoices supporting the claimed cash receipts nor a cash book showing that the cash was available on the dates of deposit. Related TaxGuru discussion. The Tribunal therefore rejected the business receipt explanation as well. The finding turned on the absence of supporting evidence for the identified deposits. The declaration of turnover under Section 44AD did not establish, on the record in this case, that the deposited cash came from that turnover. The full ₹38.70 lakh addition under Section 69A was consequently upheld. Rajasthan High Court Ruling Followed on the Applicable Rate Having confirmed the addition, the Tribunal separately examined the rate chargeable under Section 115BBE. It referred to Deepak Maratha v. Union, Civil Writ Petition No. 3625/2020, decided on 27 May 2026, in which the Rajasthan High Court held that the amendment increasing the rate from 30% to 60%, effective 1 April 2017, applied from AY 2018–19 onwards. Related TaxGuru coverage. Following that ruling, the Tribunal set aside the CIT(A)’s order on the rate issue and directed the Assessing Officer to levy tax at 30% under Section 115BBE for AY 2017–18. The appeal was partly allowed. Author’s Comments This decision illustrates the value of raising an independent ground on the applicable tax rate in an appeal involving a Section 69A addition for AY 2017–18. Where the assessee disputes both the source finding and the rate, the alternative ground allows the appellate authority to grant relief on the computation even if it confirms the addition. Here, that ground reduced the applicable base rate by 30 percentage points. The evidentiary findings also merit attention. A withdrawal from the donor’s account establishes that cash was drawn. The circumstances of its receipt by the assessee and its availability for deposit require their own explanation. Likewise, an assessee who attributes bank deposits to presumptively taxed business turnover may need contemporaneous material that connects the receipts with the deposits. In this case, the Tribunal’s conclusions on those factual questions sustained the addition, while its separate examination of Section 115BBE secured relief on the tax rate. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT HYDERABAD This appeal filed by the assessee is directed against the order of the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi, dated 28.01.2026, and pertains to assessment year 2017- 18. 2. The assessee has raised the following grounds of appeal: 1. The learned CIT appeals ought to have noted that section 69 is not applicable in the present case since Cash deposit in a bank account is not an “investment, Section 69 applies only where there is an investment. 2. The learn CIT appeals ignored the fact that, Cash deposits to the extent of Rs. 18,20,000/-being trade receipts declared U/S 44 AD, explain the source, since the same is accepted as income by assessing officer. 3. The Commissioner of income tax Appeals, without disproved the evidences filed with reference to gift from NRI relative, has sustained the addition of Rs.20,50,000/-. 4. Objection that gift was received in four instalments – legally untenable, since the addition sustained purely on suspicion. 5. Time gap between withdrawal and deposit, without contrary evidence cannot justify and addition. 6. The learned commissioner of Income tax has wrongly presumed that physical presence of NRI donor is mandatory for cash withdrawals 7. The addition is not based on any incriminating material and primarily passed on conjecture, surmises and presumptions. 8. The Appellant craves leave to add, modify or withdraw any ground during the course of hearing 3. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee is an individual, filed his Return of Income for AY 2017-18 on 24.09.2018 admitting total income of Rs.3,70,150/-. The case was selected for scrutiny and during the course of assessment proceedings, the AO noticed that the assessee has made cash deposits of Rs.38,70,000/- into his bank account held with Syndicate Bank. The AO called upon the assessee to submit relevant details and explain source for cash deposits into bank account. In response, the assessee submitted that, he is in the business of trading in gold jewellery and is operating his business in the name and style of M/s. I.S. Jewels. He further submitted that, he had received a sum of Rs.20,50,000/- in cash as gift from his brother-in-law Shri Taher Affendi which is source for cash deposits into bank account. The assessee further submitted that the remaining amount of cash deposits of Rs.18,20,000/- was out of his business receipts. The AO after considering relevant submissions of the assessee and also taken note of pattern of cash deposits observed that the assessee has failed to explain the source for cash deposits into bank account with known source of income. Although, assessee claims to have received cash gift of Rs.20,50,000/- from his brother-in-law, but failed to substantiate the cash gift with supporting evidence and also failed to substantiate the creditworthiness of the donor. Further, the balance amount of cash deposits of Rs.18,20,000/- was also not substantiated by submitting bills/invoices/vouchers, therefore, held that in the absence of supporting evidence, explanation of the assessee cannot be accepted. Thus, rejected the explanation of the assessee and made addition towards cash deposits u/s.69A of the Income Tax Act, 1961 [in short “the Act”] and brought to tax under the provisions of Sec.115BBE of the Act. 4. Aggrieved by the assessment order, the assessee preferred an appeal before the Ld.CIT(A). Before the Ld.CIT(A), assessee has reiterated his submissions made before the AO and submitted that source for cash deposits into bank account was out of gift received from his brother-in-law for Rs.20,50,000/- and balance amount of Rs.18,20,000/- was out of trade receipts. To support his arguments, furnished copy of declaration of gift and pass-port of his brother-in-law. The Ld.CIT(A) after considering relevant submissions of the assessee and also taken note of declaration of gift and pass-port observed that the donor departed from India on 29.08.2016, whereas the assessee claims to have received gift in cash on 30.10.2016. Further, the cheques drawn from the bank account of Shri Taher Affendi were also drawn after his departure from India on 29.08.2016 which is not understandable. Moreover, these amounts got deposited during demonetization after keeping the cash in custody. Further, source for sale is also not proved with evidence. Therefore, by taking the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Sumati Dayal v. CIT reported in [1995] 214 ITR 801 (SC), rejected explanation of the assessee and sustained the addition made by the AO towards cash deposits into bank account. 5. Aggrieved by the order of the Ld.CIT(A), the assessee is now in appeal before this Tribunal. 6. The Ld. Counsel for the assessee, Sri R V Chalam, CA, submitted that the Ld.CIT(A) erred in sustaining the addition made towards cash deposits without appreciating the relevant facts that the assessee has explained source out of cash gift of Rs.20,50,000/- received form his brother-in-law, Shri Taher Affendi , for which submitted declaration of gift and supporting bank statement where a sum of Rs.20,50,000/- was withdrawn from the bank account of donor. The Ld. Counsel for the assessee further submitted that the AO & the Ld.CIT(A) are failed to appreciate the fact that assessee is into business and source for cash deposits of Rs.18,20,000/- was out of business receipts, for which, the assessee has furnished relevant supporting evidences. Although, assessee has filed various evidences but the AO & the Ld.CIT(A) rejected explanation of the assessee and sustained the addition only on the ground that there is a difference between date of departure out of India and the amount drawn from his bank account, even though, cash drawn from his bank account by self-cheque and the same can be done even in the absence of donor. Therefore, he submitted that the order of the Ld.CIT(A) should be set aside and the addition made by the AO should be deleted. 7. The Ld.Sr.AR for the Revenue, on the other hand, supporting the order of the Ld.CIT(A) & the AO submitted that, the assessee has failed to offer credible evidence with supporting evidence to substantiate and explain source for cash deposits into bank account during demonetization period. Although, the assessee claims to have received cash gift of Rs.20,50,000/- , but fact remains that there are various discrepancies in the declaration of gift and the date of departure of the donor. Further, the assessee had also failed to substantiate creditworthiness of the donor by filing supporting evidence. Further, in respect of trade receipts no supporting evidence, including bills and other details had been submitted by the assessee. In the absence of relevant details, the AO has rightly made addition towards cash deposits into bank account u/s.69A of the Act. The Ld.CIT(A) after considering relevant facts has rightly sustained the addition made by the AO. Therefore, he submitted that the order of the Ld.CIT(A) should be upheld and addition made by the AO should be sustained. 8. We have heard both the parties, perused the materials available on record and had gone through orders of the authorities below. There is no dispute about cash deposits into bank account and explanation of the assessee towards source for the said cash deposits. In fact, the assessee has made cash deposits of Rs.38,70,000/- in his bank account held with Syndicate Bank from 11.11.2016 up to 20.12.2016. The assessee explained source out of cash gift of Rs.20,50,000/- from his brother-in-law who is an NRI working in Kuwait and to support his arguments, furnished copy of declaration of gift and also details of cash drawn from the bank account of the donor. The assessee had also explained balance cash deposits of Rs.18,20,000/- out of his business receipts. In so far as gift from his brother-in-law, although the assessee has furnished declaration of gift and details of self-cheque drawn from the bank account of the donor before the date of demonetization, but fact remains that the AO & the Ld.CIT(A) has brought out clear facts to the effect that the donor left India on 29.08.2016. whereas the assessee claims to have drawn cash by self- cheque from the bank account of the donor, after he left India. Further, assessee had also failed to explain ‘as to why‘ cash gift has been received from his brother-in-law, when he was maintaining bank account in India and the donor could have given gift through proper banking channel. Since explanation of the assessee is contrary to the theory of human probability, in our considered view, the AO & the Ld.CIT(A) has rightly rejected the explanation of the assessee towards cash gift received from his brother-in- law to explain the source for the cash deposits. 9. Coming back to balance amount of cash deposit of Rs.18,20,000/- and explained by the assessee out of trade receipts. Although, the assessee explained the balance amount of cash deposits out of trade receipts, but has failed to file any supporting evidence, including relevant sales bills/invoices for receipt of cash from his business nor filed any cash book to explain and prove the availability of cash as on the date of cash deposits. In the absence of any supporting evidence, the explanation of the assessee towards cash deposits out of trade receipts cannot be accepted. Therefore, in our considered view, the assessee failed to explain the source for cash deposits into bank account of Rs.18,20,000/- into his bank account and thus, in our considered view, there is no error in the reasons given by the AO to make addition u/s.69A of the Act and the reasons given by the Ld.CIT(A) to sustain the addition made by the AO. Thus, the assessee failed to explain source for cash deposits of Rs.38,70,000/- into his bank account and hence, we reject explanation of the assessee and upheld the addition made by the AO u/s.69A of the Act. 10. Having said so, let us come back to application of provisions of Sec.115BBE of the Act. It is admitted fact that the tax rate as per provisions of Sec.115BBE of the Act was at 30% before amendment to Section 115BBE of the Act by the Finance Act, 2016 w.e.f. 01.04.2017. The Hon’ble Rajasthan High Court in the case of Deepak Maratha v. Union in Civil Writ Petition No.3625/2020 dated 27.05.2026 has clearly held that, amendment to Section 115BBE of the Act by the Finance Act, 2016 w.e.f. 01.04.2017 is applicable for AY 2018-19 onwards. Since the higher rate of tax @60% is not applicable for AY 2017-18, in our considered view, the AO was erred in levying tax @60% on additions made u/s.69A of the Act. The Ld.CIT(A) without appreciating the relevant facts simply sustained tax computed by the AO @60%. Thus, we set aside the order of the Ld.CIT(A) on this issue and direct the AO to levy tax @30% as per provisions of Sec.115BBE of the Act as application for the assessment year under consideration 11. In the result, appeal filed by the assessee is partly allowed. Order pronounced on the 18th day of November, 2026.
