Summary: Rule 14A of the CGST Rules offers genuine small traders a faster route to GST registration within three working days, subject to a monthly ₹2.5 lakh tax ceiling on supplies to registered persons. However, the simplified procedure also raises concerns about fraudulent registrations and the circulation of fake input tax credit. Aijaz Hussain Malik examines how Aadhaar OTP-based authentication, limited physical verification and the ability to obtain registrations across States may be exploited by organised fraud networks. Particular concern arises from the withdrawal procedure under Form GST REG-32, which permits eligible businesses to leave the simplified scheme after filing returns for only one tax period from April 2026. The article also considers changes to registration amendments and cancellation procedures introduced in October 2026, including automatic processing of certain applications. While these measures reduce compliance delays, the author argues that they may weaken opportunities to detect fictitious businesses. Proposed safeguards include compulsory biometric verification and premises inspection before withdrawal, a three-month operating history supported by transaction evidence, Aadhaar-linked nationwide restrictions and additional checks on address changes. The article supports faster registration for genuine traders while advocating stronger controls against fraudulent ITC generation. GST’s three-day registration needs a lock on its back door Rule 14A gets small traders a GST number fast, but a few gaps in it could also help fake firms sell tax credit. The facility comes from Rule 14A of the CGST Rules, inserted by Notification No. 18/2025-Central Tax of 31 October 2025 after the 56th GST Council meeting. The applicant simply declares that the tax on his sales to other registered businesses will not cross ₹2.5 lakh a month. No proof is needed. Sales to consumers and total turnover have no limit. Identity is checked through a one-time password (OTP) sent to the applicant’s Aadhaar-linked mobile. The rule provides for no visit to the premises before the number is issued. This is real relief for a genuine small trader. But speed helps fake firms too. Risk of Fake Firms and Input Tax Credit Fraud Under GST, a business can subtract the tax paid on its purchases from the tax due on its sales. This is called input tax credit, or ITC. Fake firms issue bills without supplying any goods. The buyer claims credit for tax that never reached the government, and the fake firm vanishes. One Rule 14A registration can pass on up to ₹2.5 lakh of credit a month, about ₹30 lakh a year. At 18 per cent GST, that means bills with a taxable value of nearly ₹13.9 lakh a month. The portal reportedly blocks reporting above this. But the limit is per registration, not per racket. Multiple Registrations and Aadhaar OTP Vulnerabilities One PAN cannot hold a second Rule 14A registration in the same State, but it can take one in other States. Rackets already work with many borrowed or stolen identities. In a case detected in Chennai in November 2025, one outfit had floated more than 95 bogus firms in two States. An OTP proves only that someone holds the phone linked to an Aadhaar, not who runs the business. In 2025-26, officers detected 30,162 ITC fraud cases involving ₹74,782 crore, the government told the Rajya Sabha in July 2026. No official figure links these to Rule 14A. Yet tax officers on the ground see the risk. A Haryana circular, reported in March 2026, made physical checks compulsory for auto-approved registrations. Uttar Pradesh ordered physical verification of all new registrations in May 2026. Withdrawal From Rule 14A Through Form GST REG-32 A Rule 14A firm can leave the scheme by filing an online form, REG-32. An officer approves it in REG-33 or rejects it in REG-05. Once out, the ₹2.5 lakh cap no longer applies. One-Month Return Requirement From April 2026 Since 1 April 2026, the firm needs returns for just one tax period, often a single month, down from three months earlier. And there is no turnover test. The firm need not show that its business has grown beyond ₹2.5 lakh. The form even accepts “Other” as a reason. Checks exist, but most are optional. Aadhaar OTP is compulsory. A biometric Aadhaar check, a look at original documents and a visit to the premises happen only if the risk system flags the case or the officer chooses. If the officer does not act in time, the application may be treated as approved by default. Registration Amendments and Automatic Approvals Recent steps loosen oversight further. In October 2026, the 57th GST Council recommended automatically accepting changes to registration details for Rule 14A firms, and cancellation requests from small taxpayers. From 1 December 2026, a new Rule 19(3) lets such a firm change its main business address through online checks alone. Each step saves honest traders time, but each removes a point where an officer might spot trouble. Safeguards Needed Against Fake GST Registrations Exit checks should be compulsory: a biometric Aadhaar check and a geo-tagged visit to the premises, with no approval by default. The wait before exit should go back to three months, with proof of real trade, such as bank receipts and e-way bills (transport permits) that match the returns. One Rule 14A registration should be allowed per Aadhaar across India, not per PAN per State. Address changes should need an officer’s nod until the premises have been seen once. Conclusion Rule 14A has a fair aim. A small trader should not wait weeks for a GST number. The task is to keep that fast lane open for him while shutting it to fake firms. ****** The author is a JKAS officer presently serving as State Taxes Officer, Circle- Budgam.
