Sebi has said it will examine the concerns raised by stockbrokers, while petroleum dealers have taken up the issue with the government.Under the new framework effective October 15, UPI payments above Rs 2,000 in essential and thin-margin sectors such as fuel will attract a flat Rs 5 MDR, while capital-market transactions involving mutual funds, securities, stockbrokers and dealers will attract 0.02 per cent MDR capped at Rs 300. The standard rate for specified merchant transactions above Rs 2,000 is 0.4 per cent. About 96 per cent of P2M transactions are expected to remain unaffected. Advt Petroleum dealers have argued that they cannot absorb the additional cost because fuel prices and dealer margins are regulated. The Federation of All Maharashtra Petrol Dealers Associations has sought a complete waiver from the Finance Ministry, Petroleum Ministry, RBI, NPCI and oil marketing companies, saying the charge would directly affect their margins. Dealers have also raised concerns over settlement delays, non-transparent deductions and account freezes linked to cyber-fraud investigations.The All India Petroleum Dealers Association has also held meetings with senior officials of the Petroleum Ministry over the issue. The dealers' concern is focused on the additional payment cost rather than UPI itself, with industry representatives saying they have supported digital payment adoption at fuel outlets.Stockbrokers' demandStockbrokers have raised a different concern over the 0.02 per cent MDR. They argue that clients can transfer money into their brokerage accounts without subsequently executing a trade, meaning the broker could incur a payment cost without generating corresponding brokerage revenue. The issue is particularly relevant because brokers are required to periodically return unused client funds to customers.The industry is seeking a lower, flat cap on the charge. New-age brokers including Groww, Zerodha, Upstox and Angel One are reported to be seeking a Rs 5 cap instead of Rs 300 and a higher threshold for zero MDR. A separate proposal reported by Business Standard has suggested replacing the 0.02 per cent charge with a flat fee of about Rs 5. Advt The potential cost is significant because the securities brokers and dealers category recorded 84.12 million UPI transactions worth Rs 63,667.21 crore in August. Business Standard reported that the industry could face an annual cost of about Rs 125 crore under the existing framework. Recurring UPI mandates such as mutual fund SIPs, however, will not attract MDR.Sebi Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said the regulator would examine the concerns raised by stockbrokers. The government, meanwhile, has maintained that MDR is a charge within the payments ecosystem rather than a tax or government levy, and that customers will not be required to pay it.
