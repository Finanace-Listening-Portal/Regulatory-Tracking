Short positions with a maturity of less than one year rose to $55.15 billion at the end of August from $47.66 billion in July, while positions with a maturity of more than one year nearly doubled to $176.57 billion from $91.54 billion. At the same time, the RBI's long dollar positions increased to $31.66 billion at August-end from $2.43 billion at July-end. All of these positions mature within one month. Consequently, after adjusting for long positions, the RBI's net short position in contracts maturing within one year fell to around $23.5 billion in August from $45.2 billion in July. “The short positions have increased on the back of FCNR(B) flows. The RBI has also let some positions to mature because looking at the data, if we include the amount of inflows, the short position in more than one year should have been around $218 billion,” said a market participant. “The RBI has been conducting sell/buy swaps too, and they have been conducting them in September as well, so technically the total short positions should have reduced,” the person added.
