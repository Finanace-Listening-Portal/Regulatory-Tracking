} } Listen to This ArticleIndia’s revised economic data series will help the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) analyse, forecast, and explain the drivers of growth and inflation, and support better-calibrated and more stable monetary policy, Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta said on Monday. Speaking at a data users’ conference organised by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (Mospi) in Mumbai, Gupta said the revised gross domestic product (GDP), Consumer Price Index (CPI), and Index of Industrial Production (IIP) series would help assess structural shifts and cyclical dynamics of the economy, and design policies that are more timely and responsive to its evolving state. “A base revision is not merely a routine technical update. It records important shifts in the structure of the economy: its sectoral composition, household consumption behaviour, organisation of economic activity, and the sources of growth,” she said. The new GDP series has a base year of 2022–23, replacing 2011–12. It uses double deflation for manufacturing and agriculture, and draws on a wider range of surveys and administrative data to strengthen the measurement of the informal economy. Gupta said the revised series reflected an economy that had become larger, more diversified, and more complex since the previous base revision. The new series has also brought GDP estimates from the demand and supply sides closer. Under the earlier series, the estimates did not always align, and the gap was reported separately as a discrepancy that could be large and volatile. “The new series brings the two together through the Supply and Use Table framework. As a result, nominal GDP from the two sides now aligns closely,” Gupta said. A small gap may still persist in real terms because the two sides are deflated differently, but it is much narrower than before, she said. This improves the ability to analyse, forecast, and explain the drivers of headline growth from both the demand and supply sides. Gupta said monetary policy decisions were based on an assessment of current conditions as well as projections. Early estimates, which draw on proxy indicators and provisional information, were, therefore, an important input into monetary policy. “The methodological improvements in the new series are expected The weight of food and beverages has declined to 36.8 per cent from 45.9 per cent, while the share of non-food items has increased. “As a corollary, food items, which have historically been more volatile, now carry lower weights. This should make headline inflation less volatile, and the monetary policy process less uncertain and easier to explain,” Gupta said. The new CPI follows the United Nations’ Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose (COICOP) 2018. Its more detailed classification allows core inflation to be defined more precisely. Social transfers, such as free public distribution system (PDS) items, are now excluded so that the index reflects prices actually paid by households. Better handling of missing data, particularly for seasonal items, and a revised methodology for compiling the house rent index have also improved the quality of the CPI. The new series addresses lumpy jumps in house rent inflation following pay commission revisions. Employer-provided and government accommodations are now excluded from the housing index, while house rent is also covered for rural areas. “These revisions should narrow the disconnect between measured and perceived inflation, and add value to the conduct of monetary policy under our inflation-targeting framework,” she said. The revised IIP assigns larger weights to technology and consumption-oriented sectors such as motor vehicles, pharmaceuticals, and electronics, while lowering the weights of refined petroleum products, basic metals, and mining. Gupta said incorporating emerging industries and production patterns should provide better insights into evolving industrial dynamics, and help capture turning points. “The expectation is that the recent refinements will bring the monthly IIP closer to the quarterly GVA (gross value added) for industry when a longer time series is available,” she said. Gupta said the revised GDP, CPI, and IIP series gave the RBI a clearer view of an economy that had changed considerably. She said the central bank valued a clear and predictable approach to revisions and continued dialogue on how they were being carried out. “As the new series have only a short history at this point in time, we are still learning about their features,” she said.
