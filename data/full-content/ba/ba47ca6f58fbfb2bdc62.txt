Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Bailor Raghvendra Shetty Vs State of Maharashtra (Bombay High Court) Flat Purchase Advance Does Not Become a Deposit Under the MPID Act Merely Because It Is Refundable With Interest on Non-Delivery — Bombay High Court Summary: The Bombay High Court has held that money paid specifically towards the purchase of flats does not become a “deposit” under Section 2(c) of the Maharashtra Protection of Interest of Depositors (in Financial Establishments) Act, 1999 merely because the developer agreed to refund it with 9% simple interest per annum if possession was not delivered. The Court looked at the purpose for which the money was received and the manner in which the agreements were acted upon. Facts of the Case M/s. Sai Siddhant Developers entered into agreements for the sale of flats in the redevelopment project of Suryakiran Co-operative Housing Society Ltd., D.N. Nagar, Mumbai. According to the prosecution, the developer received an aggregate of ₹6.51 crore from various purchasers. It was alleged that the developer failed to deliver possession within the stipulated period and misappropriated the amount. A criminal case was registered as C.R. No. 19 of 2023. The agreements provided that if the developer failed to hand over possession of the duly constructed flats, the amounts received would be refunded with simple interest at 9% per annum. The first informant, Bailor Raghvendra Shetty, approached the High Court under Article 226 of the Constitution, seeking a direction to the State to apply the provisions of the MPID Act to the criminal case. Purchaser’s Argument on the Refund Clause The petitioner argued that the failure to hand over possession within the agreed period made the refund obligation, together with interest, enforceable. According to him, this brought the money received by the developer within the definition of a deposit. He contended that the police ought to have invoked the MPID Act while filing the charge-sheet. The issue before the High Court was whether the contingent obligation to refund the advance with interest changed the character of the original receipt for the purposes of Section 2(c). High Court Examines How the Agreements Were Acted Upon The Division Bench noted that possession of two out of the five flats had already been handed over to the petitioner and/or other purchasers. This showed that the agreements had been partly acted upon. The Court observed that the consequence of incomplete performance was an entitlement to refund with 9% interest in terms of the agreed clause. The Bench also noted that the developer and the concerned respondents had given no assurance or guarantee of exorbitant interest on deposits contrary to RBI guidelines. It concluded that the money received under the five agreements was advance consideration specifically towards the purchase of flats and fell outside the definition of a deposit under Section 2(c) on these facts. Consequently, the failure to hand over the flats or alternatively return the money with agreed interest furnished no basis to invoke Sections 3 and 4 of the MPID Act in this case. The High Court dismissed the writ petition. Author’s Comments The important distinction is between the purpose for which money is received and the obligation that arises when a contract is breached. Here, the developer received money towards the sale of identified flats. The 9% interest clause came into operation if the developer failed to deliver possession. The clause spelled out what the purchaser could claim upon default while the original receipt remained attributable to the flat purchase. The delivery of two flats was significant. It gave the Court evidence of how the parties actually performed the agreements. In a similar dispute, the agreement, allotment particulars, receipts showing adjustment towards the sale price and records of possession can help establish the commercial purpose of the payment. The reference to exorbitant interest should be read in the context of the facts. As discussed in the Supreme Court’s interpretation of Section 2(c), the statutory definition can cover a deposit with or without interest or another benefit. The decision therefore provides no interest-rate threshold. Its reasoning turns on what the developer received the money for, the conditional nature of the refund clause and the partial performance of the agreements. The ruling is also limited to the relief sought. The petitioner asked the Court to direct the authorities to apply the MPID Act to the existing criminal case. The Court rejected that request and recognised the contractual consequence of refund with agreed interest. The allegations in the underlying criminal case and the enforcement of the refund obligation were outside the relief decided in this writ petition. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF BOMBAY HIGH COURT 1) By this Petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, Petitioner seeks a writ of Mandamus or any other appropriate writ, order or direction, directing the Respondent No.1 to apply the provisions of Maharashtra Protection of Interest of Depositors (in Financial Establishments) Act, 1999 (‘MPID Act’) to C.R. No.19 of 2023. 2) The short question for our determination in the present Petition is, whether an advance received by the developer towards sale of flat, which the agreement requires to be refunded with interest at 9% if the flat is not delivered, could be termed as ‘deposit’ under Section 2(c) of the MPID Act. 3) We have heard Mr. Mor, learned counsel for the Petitioner, Smt. Mhatre, learned APP for the State and perused the entire record. 4) The Petitioner is the first informant. The prosecution’s case is that, the Respondent No.3 entered into Agreement with informant and other persons for sale of the flats in the redevelopment project of Suryakiran Co-op. Housing Society Ltd., Building No.7, D.N. Nagar, Mumbai and received a total of Rs.6,51,00,000/- from various purchasers, who are witnesses in the said crime. It is alleged that, the Respondent No.3 failed to deliver possession of the flats within stipulated period and misappropriated that amount. On this basis, the crime was registered. 5) Mr. Mor submitted that, the Agreement itself contained a clause under which, if Respondent No.3 or its partners failed to hand over possession of duly constructed residential flat to the Petitioner and other purchasers, the developers would refund the amount with simple interest at the rate of 9% p.a. As the Respondent No.3 failed to hand over possession of the flats within stipulated period, the clause providing refund of amount along with interest thereon became enforceable and the provisions of the MPID Act were thereby attracted. He submitted that, the Police, while filing charge-sheet, failed to apply the MPID Act to the said crime and therefore the present Petition is filed seeking the above noted relief. 6) We are not in agreement with the learned counsel for the Petitioner. Perusal of record indicates that, as a matter of fact, out of five flats, possession of two flats have been handed over to the Petitioner and/or other witnesses by the Respondent No.3. Apparently therefore, the Agreements have been partially acted upon. At most, it would mean that the Respondent No.3 has partly performed its obligation under the Agreement and consequently, the Petitioner would be entitled to a refund of money with 9% interest as per the agreed terms. 7) In our considered view, failure to hand over flats and alternatively return money with agreed interest, does not constitute offence as contemplated under Section 3 or Section 4 of the MPID Act. Admittedly, the Respondent Nos.3 to 5, have not assured and/or guaranteed exorbitant rate of interest on deposits, contrary to the guidelines issued by the Reserve Bank of India, to the Petitioner. 8) According to us, the money received by the Respondent Nos. 3 to 5 under the five Agreements, was an advance payment specifically towards purchase of flats and cannot be termed as ‘deposit’ under section 2(c) of the MPID Act. 9) In view of the above, we find no merits in the Petition and is accordingly dismissed.
