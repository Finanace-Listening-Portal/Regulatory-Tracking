RBI Purpose Rules and Permitted Exchange Contracts RBI’s Risk Management and Inter-Bank Dealings framework, updated 1 October 2026, allows recognised exchanges to offer foreign-exchange futures and European call and put options in specified pairs. Permitted pairs listed in the direction are USD-INR, EUR-INR, GBP-INR, JPY-INR, EUR-USD, GBP-USD and USD-JPY. Availability through a particular broker and exchange must be checked at order time; regulatory permission does not mean every contract month or pair is continuously liquid or offered. INR contracts still require a valid contracted exposure For contracts involving INR, the purpose is hedging a contracted exposure. RBI permits users to take positions, long or short, up to USD 100 million equivalent across INR pairs and recognised exchanges without having to establish the exposure before taking the position. The user must nonetheless ensure that a valid underlying contracted exposure exists, is not hedged through another derivative and can be established if required. This is not a general permission for unsupported speculation. Contracts not involving INR may be offered without a purpose restriction under the direction, subject to all other law, broker and exchange controls. Margin and cash settlement NSE’s INR-pair specifications use SPAN-based initial margin and contract-specific extreme-loss margins. One futures contract represents USD 1,000, EUR 1,000, GBP 1,000 or JPY 100,000 for the corresponding INR pair. The contracts are cash settled in Indian rupees. NSE states daily settlement at T+1 and final settlement at T+2. Mark-to-market losses and margin calls can require funds before final settlement, and positions can be closed under broker or exchange risk controls if margin is insufficient. SMC’s trading help describes a withdrawal request from the broker to the registered client bank account and a transaction reference number. Withdrawal availability is not the same as gross ledger balance: unsettled obligations, blocked margin, risk holds, bank cut-offs and compliance review can affect the amount and timing. Clients should reconcile daily margin statements, contract notes, settlement entries and bank credits. Complaints, Limits and Tax Treatment The first complaint route is SMC customer support and its published escalation matrix, followed by the compliance and senior-management contacts. If unresolved, a client may use the exchange investor grievance mechanism and SEBI’s SCORES platform, subject to current procedure. Complaints should include UCC, order or contract details, screenshots, bank records and prior correspondence. Fraud or cyber incidents may also require immediate bank, broker and law-enforcement reporting. Income-tax and GST points Section 43(5)(d) provides that an eligible electronic derivative transaction on a recognised stock exchange, supported by a time-stamped contract note showing client identity and PAN, is not deemed speculative for that provision. That does not automatically decide whether a particular person’s profit or loss is business income, the availability of expenses, audit obligations or loss set-off; the total facts and tax law apply. GST at the applicable rate is levied on taxable brokerage and service components, while government levies have separate treatment. The actual contract note and tax invoice, not an illustrative web article, should control the charge reconciliation.
