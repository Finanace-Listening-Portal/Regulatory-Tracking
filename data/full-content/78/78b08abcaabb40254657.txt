Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Greater Noida Industrial Development Authority Vs Roma Unicon Designex Consortium & Others (NCLAT Delhi) Summary: The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT), Principal Bench, New Delhi, in Greater Noida Industrial Development Authority Vs Roma Unicon Designex Consortium & Others, considered three connected appeals arising from the corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP) of Earth Infrastructures Limited. The principal controversy concerned whether resolution plans approved for three stalled real estate projects could deal with land leased by the Greater Noida Industrial Development Authority (GNIDA) to subsidiary or special-purpose companies of the corporate debtor, without obtaining GNIDA’s consent and addressing outstanding land premium, lease rent and related dues. The appeals challenged the NCLT orders dated 05.04.2021 and 08.06.2021 approving the respective resolution plans, and the consequential order dated 07.12.2021 directing implementation of the Earth Towne plan. The NCLAT examined the distinction between ownership of land, leasehold rights and development rights, the legal personality of subsidiary companies, the binding effect of an approved resolution plan and the statutory duties of public authorities. In its judgment dated 30.01.2023, the Tribunal set aside all three orders and directed a fresh resolution process, subject to GNIDA’s approval for transfer of the relevant leasehold land and arrangements for payment of lawful dues. The Tribunal also directed GNIDA to waive penal interest and recalculate its claims. Subsequent development: The Supreme Court later set aside this NCLAT judgment and restored the resolution-plan approvals; accordingly, the NCLAT’s directions must be read as the historical outcome of the 2023 appeal, not the final prevailing disposition. Detailed Summary 1. Background of the Three Real Estate Projects: GNIDA, a statutory authority constituted under the Uttar Pradesh Industrial Area Development Act, 1976, had allotted and leased land for residential and commercial development in Greater Noida. Earth Infrastructures Limited was involved in developing three projects, namely Earth Towne, Earth TechOne and Earth Sapphire Court. The land for Earth Towne was leased to Earth Towne Infrastructures Private Limited, while the land associated with the other projects was leased to Neo Multimedia Limited and Nishtha Software Private Limited. These companies were separate legal entities associated with the developer. Development agreements and related contractual arrangements conferred construction, development and marketing responsibilities upon Earth Infrastructures Limited. A substantial number of homebuyers had invested in the projects, but construction remained incomplete, resulting in significant financial hardship for allottees. 2. Initiation of CIRP and Approval of Resolution Plans: Following an application by a financial creditor under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, CIRP commenced against Earth Infrastructures Limited on 06.06.2018. The Resolution Professional invited expressions of interest and resolution plans for the developer’s projects. Roma Unicon Designex Consortium submitted a plan for Earth Towne, which the Committee of Creditors approved on 26.08.2019 with 100% voting share. The NCLT approved that plan on 05.04.2021. Alfa Corp Development Private Limited submitted a separate plan covering Earth TechOne and Earth Sapphire Court, which received CoC approval on 11.11.2019 with 99.97% voting share and was approved by the NCLT on 08.06.2021. The plans contemplated implementation arrangements involving the underlying project lands, although the registered leases stood in the names of entities other than the corporate debtor. 3. GNIDA’s Objections to Transfer of Leasehold Land: GNIDA contended that the approved plans impermissibly dealt with property and contractual rights belonging to third parties. According to the Authority, the corporate debtor was neither the registered lessee nor the owner of the land, and its shareholding in the subsidiary companies did not confer ownership of their assets. GNIDA maintained that the relevant lease deeds imposed conditions governing transfer, assignment and payment of land dues, which could not be overridden through the approval of a resolution plan under Section 31 of the IBC. GNIDA had communicated a claim of ₹148,37,46,148 relating to Earth Towne and asserted that substantial additional amounts remained outstanding. It objected to provisions effectively requiring transfer of leasehold land to successful resolution applicants without satisfying the Authority’s contractual and statutory requirements. 4. Resolution Professional’s and Successful Applicants’ Submissions: The Resolution Professional and successful resolution applicants argued that the corporate debtor had financed the projects, made substantial payments towards land obligations and undertaken construction and marketing activities. They emphasised the close corporate and commercial relationship between the developer and its landholding subsidiaries. It was submitted that the subsidiaries functioned as special-purpose entities and that the development agreements conferred valuable rights upon the corporate debtor. The respondents further maintained that GNIDA knew about the insolvency proceedings and its outstanding dues had been disclosed in the Information Memorandum. They relied upon the binding nature of the approved plans and the need to revive stalled projects. The homebuyers’ associations supported implementation of the plans, stressing the prolonged delay in possession and the substantial investments made by allottees. 5. Separate Legal Identity of Subsidiaries and Ownership of Assets: A central issue was whether land leased to subsidiary companies could be treated as property of the corporate debtor merely because the developer held a controlling shareholding and had obtained development rights. The Tribunal examined the distinction between the assets of a company and those of its shareholders, together with the contractual arrangements governing the projects. It concluded that the separate legal personality of the landholding companies could not be disregarded merely because the corporate debtor exercised commercial control over their operations. The assets and leasehold interests of those companies could not automatically be included in the CIRP estate of Earth Infrastructures Limited. This distinction was important in determining the permissible scope of a resolution plan. 6. Development Rights Distinguished from Leasehold Ownership: The NCLAT considered the broad statutory definition of property under Section 3(27) of the IBC and the respondents’ contention that development rights constituted valuable assets capable of being dealt with in insolvency. Although development rights may constitute property under Section 3(27), the Tribunal distinguished such rights from ownership or leasehold interests held by separate companies. The development agreements did not, in the Tribunal’s view, authorise compulsory transfer of the underlying leasehold land contrary to the rights of GNIDA under the registered leases. The Tribunal therefore held that a resolution plan could not, without the requisite consent, compel transfer of land leased by GNIDA to entities other than the corporate debtor. 7. GNIDA’s Participation and Treatment of Outstanding Dues: The Tribunal also examined whether GNIDA’s rights and outstanding claims had been adequately addressed during the insolvency process. The Authority maintained that the plans had been approved without properly determining its dues and without giving it an effective opportunity to protect its interests. The respondents argued that GNIDA had knowledge of the CIRP and that its claims were disclosed. The NCLAT nevertheless considered that the statutory lessor’s rights required proper treatment where implementation of the plans depended upon the transfer of leasehold land. It held that GNIDA ought to be involved in the subsequent resolution process so that the legal and financial requirements for transfer could be examined before fresh plans were approved. The Tribunal’s approach reflected the need for effective implementation and statutory compliance in resolution plans. 8. Protection of Homebuyers and Recalculation of GNIDA Dues: While accepting GNIDA’s objections concerning transfer of land, the NCLAT recognised the serious hardship suffered by homebuyers whose investments remained locked in unfinished projects. It noted that hundreds of crores of rupees had been collected from allottees and that construction had remained stalled for years. The Tribunal also considered that GNIDA had not acted diligently in recovering its dues. Balancing the interests of the Authority and the homebuyers, it directed GNIDA to recalculate the amounts payable by the respective landholding companies without charging penal interest. The recalculated demands were to be communicated to the Resolution Professional and the flat buyers’ associations within 15 days. The Tribunal contemplated arrangements among GNIDA, the resolution applicants and homebuyers’ associations to facilitate payment of lawful dues and eventual transfer of the project land. 9. Fresh Form-G and Reconsideration of Resolution Plans: The Tribunal directed the Resolution Professional to issue a fresh Form-G inviting resolution plans. The invitation was required to specify that plans involving the project land would be placed before the Committee of Creditors for consideration only after obtaining GNIDA’s prior approval for transfer, subject to arrangements for payment of its dues. Roma Unicon Designex Consortium and Alfa Corp Development Private Limited were expressly permitted to submit fresh plans. The Resolution Professional was required to examine the plans and place them before the CoC for fresh consideration, followed by the necessary application before the Adjudicating Authority. The Tribunal extended the CIRP period by six months and directed completion of the specified steps within that period. These directions concerned the resolution-plan submission and approval framework under the CIRP Regulations. 10. Final Decision and Subsequent Judicial Development: By its common judgment dated 30.01.2023, the NCLAT set aside the NCLT orders dated 05.04.2021, 08.06.2021 and 07.12.2021. It directed recalculation of GNIDA’s dues without penal interest, participation of GNIDA in the continuing CIRP, issuance of a fresh Form-G, reconsideration of resolution plans and completion of the specified process within six months. The Tribunal sought to protect the contractual rights of the statutory lessor while creating a route for revival of the projects and delivery of flats to homebuyers. However, these directions did not remain the final judicial outcome. The Supreme Court subsequently set aside the NCLAT’s judgment and restored the resolution-plan approvals. Consequently, the NCLAT’s conclusions concerning mandatory prior consent and the setting aside of the approved plans must be understood in light of that subsequent Supreme Court ruling.
