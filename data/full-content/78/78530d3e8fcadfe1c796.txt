} } Listen to This ArticleThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has clarified that the ₹10 lakh threshold for filing an Export Declaration Form (EDF) by non-individual service exporters applies to each inward remittance, not their aggregate receipts, providing clarity on reporting requirements under the new foreign exchange framework. In frequently asked questions (FAQs) issued on Friday, the central bank said service exporters other than resident individuals need to file an EDF only when the value of an individual inward remittance exceeds ₹10 lakh or its foreign currency equivalent. The declaration must be submitted before the proceeds are credited to the exporter's account. The clarification follows the RBI's decision to simplify reporting requirements under the Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) (Second Amendment) Regulations, 2026, notified on November 2 and made effective from October 1. The RBI also clarified that service export invoices raised before October 1 will not require an EDF even if payment is received after that date. Resident individuals exporting services are exempt from filing a separate EDF; it will be deemed submitted once they provide the necessary information to their authorised dealer (AD) bank for crediting the inward remittance. Authorised dealer (AD) banks cannot insist on an EDF before crediting inward remittances of up to ₹10 lakh for non-individual service exporters or any amount received by resident individuals.
