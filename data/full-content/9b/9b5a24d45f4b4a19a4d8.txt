Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Tirupati Tubes Vs Commissioner of Central Excise & CGST (CESTAT Allahabad) Summary: The Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), Allahabad, in Tirupati Tubes Vs Commissioner of Central Excise & CGST, Ghaziabad, decided four connected appeals involving M/s Tirupati Tubes and M/s Tirupati Structurals Ltd., both manufacturers of PVC pipes. The principal dispute concerned whether removal of raw materials, particularly resin, in their original form after reversal of the CENVAT credit availed on those inputs constituted trading activity attracting the provisions of Rule 6(3) of the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004. The Department treated such clearances as trading and demanded an amount calculated at 6% of the value of the goods on the ground that the manufacturers had not maintained separate accounts for dutiable and exempted activities. The Commissioner confirmed demands of ₹3,69,49,846 against Tirupati Tubes and ₹1,43,82,927 against Tirupati Structurals Ltd. for the period commencing 1 April 2011, together with interest and equivalent penalties, while dropping the demands relating to the earlier period. Both manufacturers challenged the confirmed demands, whereas the Revenue appealed against the portions dropped. The manufacturers submitted that they had procured resin on payment of duty and, whenever inputs were transferred to sister units or independent buyers without being used in manufacture, they reversed the corresponding CENVAT credit under Rule 3(5). They maintained that such removal could not simultaneously be characterised as trading for the purpose of Rule 6(3). Tirupati Tubes further submitted that credit relating to telephone services had been reversed, while GTA, customs house agent and sales promotion services were not connected with the disputed removals. The assessees relied on Chandrapur Magnet Wires P. Ltd. v. Collector of Central Excise, Exide Industries Ltd. v. Commissioner of CGST & Central Excise, Salem and Mahaveer Cylinder. The Revenue argued that trading had been included as an exempted service with effect from 1 April 2011 and that the amendment was clarificatory and retrospective, justifying demands even for the preceding period. The Tribunal held that the manufacturers had removed inputs “as such” after reversing the credit availed on them in accordance with Rule 3(5). Such transactions could not be treated as trading activity merely because the inputs were transferred or sold without further processing. Relying particularly on the Chennai Bench decision in Exide Industries Ltd., the Tribunal observed that removal of inputs after the prescribed credit reversal could not be reclassified as an exempted trading service for invoking Rule 6(3)(i). The Tribunal also referred to the Supreme Court decision in Chandrapur Magnet Wires and the decision in Mahaveer Cylinder as supporting the same conclusion. Accordingly, the demands confirmed for the period from 1 April 2011 were held legally unsustainable, along with the consequential interest and penalties. Regarding the Revenue’s appeals, the Tribunal noted that trading was specifically declared an exempted service only from 1 April 2011. Consequently, the Revenue’s attempt to apply that classification to the earlier period was untenable. Independently, since the transactions themselves did not constitute trading for the purposes of the disputed CENVAT provisions, there was no basis to demand an amount under Rule 6(3) for either period. The Tribunal therefore allowed both manufacturers’ appeals with consequential relief, if any, in accordance with law, and dismissed both Revenue appeals. The decision confirms that compliance with Rule 3(5) on removal of inputs as such cannot, on the facts considered, be treated as a separate exempted trading activity to impose a further reversal obligation under Rule 6(3). FULL TEXT OF THE CESTAT ALLAHABAD ORDER Excise Appeal No.70629 of 2025 has been filed by M/s.Tirupati Tubes, Gaziabad, U.P.( herein after referred as the Appellant) against the impugned Order-in-Original No.11-14/COMMR./GZB/S.Tax/2024-25 dated 31.03.2025 passed by Commissioner of CGST, Ghaziabad, wherein Service Tax demand of Rs,3,69,49,846/- has been confirmed along with interest and equal amount of tax as penalty. 1.1. Excise Appeal No.70728 of 2025 has been filed by Commissioner of Central Excise & CGST, Ghaziabad (herein after referred as the Revenue) against the demand of Central Excise duty dropped by the adjudicating authority in the impugned Order-in-Original No.11-14/COMMR./GZB/S.Tax/2024-25 dated 31.03.2025. 1.2. Excise Appeal No.70739 of 2025 has been filed by M/s. Tirupati Structurals Ltd., Gaziabad, U.P. (herein after referred as the Appellant) against the impugned Order-in-Original No.02-10/COMMR./GZB/S.Tax/2024-25 dated 31.03.2025 passed by Commissioner of CGST, Ghaziabad, wherein Service Tax demand of Rs,1,43,82,927/- has been confirmed along with interest and equal amount of tax as penalty. 1.3. Excise Appeal No.70727 of 2025 has been filed by Commissioner of Central Excise & CGST, Ghaziabad (herein after referred as the Revenue) against the demand of Central Excise duty dropped by the adjudicating authority in the impugned Order-in-Original No.02-10/COMMR./GZB/S.Tax/2024-25 dated 31.03.2025 1.4. As the issue involved is common, all these appeals are taken up together for decision by a common order. 2. M/s. Tirupati Tubes, Gaziabad, U.P, the appellant in Appeal No. E/70629/2025, are engaged in manufacturing of PVC Pipes for which they were registered under central excise and claiming CENVAT Credit in terms of the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004(CCR). The appellant procured raw material mainly resin on payment of duty by way of import and domestic market. The imported raw material was always used for the manufacture of pipes. However, in certain cases, the appellant removed input (resin) as such, either on account of transfer to sister unit or to independent buyers. As removal of input ‘as such’ was covered under Rule 3(5) of CCR, the appellant reversed CENVAT credit taken thereon. 2.1. The appellant was also claiming credit on certain input services, which were otherwise not having any relation with the removal of inputs, except in the case of credit taken on telephone services. 2.2. During the course of audit of records of the appellant for the period 2010-11, it was observed that the appellant was claiming credit on input services under GTA, Telephone, CHA and Sales Promotion and cleared inputs as such, on which they were required to reverse amount in terms of Rule 6(3). On being enquired, it was informed by the appellant that they did not claim credit of input service which was commonly used. The Appellant reversed the entire credit on telephone service and GTA with regard to trading and informed the department accordingly. 2.3. The Department took a view that the clearance of inputs as such amounts to trading and hence considered the said clearance as clearance of exempted goods. Since, the appellant has not maintained separate accounts for availment of CENVAT Credit of inputs and input services used in dutiable and exempted goods, the Department contended that the appellant was liable to pay an amount equal to 6% of the value of exempted goods as provided under Rule 6(3) of the CCR, 2004. The appellant took the stand that removal of input ‘as such’ on reversal of duty could not be considered as ‘trading’. The appellant submitted that the removal of inputs as such was liable to State VAT and hence they were discharging the VAT liability. Under the VAT, removal of input as such was being treated as trading activity and, therefore, appellant was treating them as trading for the purpose of State VAT; that once a transaction is considered as ‘sale’ and VAT is discharged, then the same transaction cannot be considered as a ‘service’ for the purpose of service tax. Accordingly, the appellant held the view that the clearance of inputs as such cannot be considered as ‘trading activity’ for the purpose of service tax. However, the Department has not agreed to the views expressed by the appellant. 2.4. On the basis of the said allegations, various Show cause notices were issued which were contested by the appellant. The prime contention of the appellant was that removal of input ‘as such’ on reversal of duty could not be considered as ‘trading’. The appellant reversed the entire credit taken on telephone service during 2009-10 and 2010-11 without utilizing and after that they did not claim any credit on the same (para 20.8.ii) and services of GTA, CHA and sales promotion had no connection with removal of input ‘as such’. In support of their contention that the provisions of Rule 6(3) of the CCR are not applicable to such removals, the appellant relied on the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Chandrapur Magnet Wires P. Ltd. v. Collector of C. Ex. The Appellant also contested the demand on the ground of time bar. 2.5. The original adjudicating authority vide the impugned Order-in-Original No.11-14/COMMR./GZB/S.Tax/2024-25 dated 31.03.2025 dropped the demand for the period up to 01.04.2011 on the basis that trading was declared as exempted service w.e.f. 01.04.2011 only. However, for the period after 01.04.2011, the demand raised in the Notices have been confirmed in the impugned order dated 31.03.2025, by observing that removal of inputs as such was covered under trading and hence the appellant was required to reverse the amount under Rule 6(3). The impugned order is under challenge by the appellant as well as by the Revenue. 3. M/s. Tirupati Structurals Ltd., Gaziabad, U.P., appellant in Appeal No. E/70739/2025, are also engaged in manufacturing of PVC Pipes. The appellant was procuring raw material mainly resin on payment of duty by way of import and domestic market. The imported raw material was always used for the manufacture of pipes. However, in certain cases, the appellant removed input (resin) as such, either on account of transfer to sister unit or to independent buyers. As removal of input ‘as such’ was covered under Rule 3(5) of CCR, the appellant reversed CENVAT Credit taken thereon. The Department took a view that the clearance of inputs as such amounts to trading and hence considered the said clearance as clearance of exempted goods. Since, the appellant had not maintained separate accounts for availment of CENVAT Credit of inputs and input services used in dutiable and exempted goods, even in this case, the Department contended that the appellant was liable to pay an amount equal to 6% of the value of exempted goods as provided under Rule 6(3) of the CCR, 2004. On the basis of the said allegations, various Show cause notices were issued which were contested by the appellant, in respect of which the said appellant specifically contended that removal of inputs ‘as such’ on reversal of duty could not be considered as ‘trading’. 3.1. After due process, the said Notices were adjudicated vide the impugned Order-in-Original No.02- 10/COMMR./GZB/S.Tax/2024-25 dated 31.03.2025 passed by Commissioner of CGST, Ghaziabad, wherein Service Tax demand of Rs,1,43,82,927/- has been confirmed, being the demand w.e.f. 01.04.2011, along with interest and equal amount of tax as penalty. The demand for the period prior to 01.04.2011 has been dropped. The appellant is appeal against the demands confirmed against them and the Revenue is in appeal against the demands dropped. 4. During the course of hearing, the appellant inter alia submitted that the issue is no longer res-integra as various Tribunals have already decided the issue in favour of the appellant holding that removal of inputs ‘as such’ on reversal of CENVAT credit availed on such inputs cannot be considered as ‘trading’ activity and hence the provisions of Rule 6(3) of CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004 are inapplicable to these clearances. In support of this contention, the appellant relied on the decision in the case of Exide Industries Ltd. vs. Commissioner of CGST & Central Excise, Salem, 2025 (6) TMI 1960 – CESTAT CHENNAI, wherein it was held that removal of inputs ‘as such’ as per Rule 3(5) of the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004 on reversal of the credit availed, cannot be considered as trading activity. The Appellant also relied on the decision in the case of Mahaveer Cylinder, wherein it has been held that removal of inputs as such cannot be considered as trading activity. Thus, the appellant’s case is that there could be no scope for invoking Rule 6(3) even if the trading was specifically introduced in Service Tax as exempted service Accordingly, it is submitted that the provisions of Rule 6(3)(i) of the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004 are not applicable to this case and the demands confirmed in the impugned orders, along with interest and penalty, are not sustainable. 5. In their Grounds of Appeal, Revenue has inter alia submitted that trading has been declared as ‘exempted service’ w.e.f. 01.04.2011; that the amendment to the CENVAT Credit rules was clarificatory in nature and hence it has retrospective application. It is the Revenue’s contention that as the appellant did not maintain separate records, Rule 6(3) was invocable to demand 6% of the value of exempted goods cleared. Accordingly, it is submitted the Ld. adjudicating authority was in error in dropping the demands for the period prior to 01.04.2011 in the impugned orders. 5.1. The Ld. Authorized Representative of the Revenue reiterated the findings in the impugned orders. 6. Heard both sides and perused the records of the appeals. 7. After hearing the parties and going through the records, we find that the appellants have availed CENVAT credit of excise duty paid on the inputs and thereafter removed them “as such”, by reversing the CENVAT credit availed on the said inputs, as provided under Rule 3(5) of the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004. It is a fact that the said inputs, removed ‘as such’, cannot be considered a “trading” activity of the appellant. Hence, we observe that Rule 6(3)(i) of the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004 is not applicable to this case. 7.1. We find that the issue is no longer res-integra as the said issue has already been decided in favour of the appellants in the case of Exide Industries Ltd. vs. Commissioner of CGST & Central Excise, Salem, 2025 (6) TMI 1960 – CESTAT CHENNAI [Final Order No. 40064 of 2025 dated 26.06.2025 in Excise Appeal No. 40045 of 2017 – CESTAT, Chennai], wherein it has been held that removal of inputs ‘as such’ as per Rule 3(5) of the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004 on reversal of the credit availed, cannot be considered as trading activity. The relevant portion of the said orders are extracted below for ready reference: “17. We find it discombobulating that the same adjudicating authority has, despite holding that PPCP received by the appellant and cleared to the moulders are used in the manufacture of containers and lids which in turn are used by the appellant in the manufacture of batteries and thus an input falling under the definition of Rule 2(k) of Cenvat Credit Rules 2004, and further finding that its clearance to the moulders on sale after reversal of cenvat credit has been made by the appellant after rightly paying the amount in terms of Rule 3(5) of CCR on removal of inputs as such from the factory; yet gone on to treat the very same transaction of removal of inputs as such as trading and thus an exempted service. Moreover, it is also pertinent that the adjudicating authority has not controverted the appellant’s contention that the entire quantity of PPCP supplied by the appellant is only to make the parts meant for the appellant and thus no quantity of the PPCP is used by the moulders for any other use and the entire PPCP gets converted and supplied back in the form of battery parts and which was evidenced by the certificate of the moulders produced. The adjudicating authority also has failed to controvert the contention of the appellant that the price at which PPCP is sold to the moulders will ultimately become a cost to the Appellant and the Appellant does not stand to gain any profit, as the price at which PPCP is sold to moulders is built into the price of the containers and lids sold back by the moulders to the Appellant. We also note that the SCN itself does not categorically state that the Appellant is engaged in the business of trading in PPCP and is couched on an inferential basis based on a reply made by the appellant to an enquiry in which reply to the appellant has never stated that it is engaged in trading and on the contrary has stated the sequence of transactions that detail how the input is used for the manufacture of the containers which is further used in the manufacture of batteries. Thus, the show cause notice itself is woefully lacking in any evidence to show that the appellant is known in the market as a trader of PPCP or that the appellant sells PPCP to any other customers with a profit motive. The adjudicating authority has thus failed to appreciate that the Department has not let in any evidence that shows that the appellant is in the business of trading in PPCP. In fact, the concatenation of transactions as a whole clearly reveal that it is nothing but removal of inputs on payment of duty by reversal of credit taken, and is in the course of the appellant’s activity of manufacture of batteries. It is a settled principle in law that a party cannot approbate and reprobate on the same transaction. Therefore, having found the transaction of sale of PPCP to the moulders by the appellant to be removal of inputs as such from the factory, thereafter, treating the very same transaction of removal of inputs as such, as trading activity cannot be countenanced. We are constrained to fustigate such a dichotomous finding rendered by the adjudicating authority in the impugned order in original, which is appalling to say the least. We are therefore of the considered view that the demand made on the appellant consequent to the finding that the appellant’s clearances of Polypropylene Co- Polymer (PPCP) made tantamount to trading, along with applicable interest as well as consequent imposition of equivalent penalty on the appellant are wholly untenable and cannot sustain.” [Emphasis supplied] 7.2. We find that a similar view has also been expressed by the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Chandrapur Magnet Wires P. Ltd. v. Collector of C. Ex. 7.3. Similar was the view taken in the case of Mahaveer Cylinder, wherein it has been held that removal of inputs as such cannot be considered as trading activity. Thus, it was held that there could be no scope for invoking Rule 6(3) even if the trading was specifically introduced in service tax as exempted service. 7.4. Thus, by relying on the ratio of the decisions cited supra, we hold that the demands confirmed for the period after 01.04.2011, in the impugned Order-in-Original No.11-14/COMMR./GZB/S.Tax/2024-25 dated 31.03.2025 and the impugned Order-in-Original No.02- 10/COMMR./GZB/S.Tax/2024-25 dated 31.03.2025, are legally not sustainable and hence we set aside the same. As the demands are not sustainable, the question of demanding interest or imposing penalties does not arise and hence we set aside the same as well. 8. Regarding the appeals filed by the Revenue against dropping the demands for the period for the period prior to 01.04.2011 in the impugned orders, we take due note of the fact that ‘trading’ was declared as exempted service only w.e.f. 01.04.2011 and hence the question of consideration of ‘trading’ as an exempted service for the period prior to 01.04.2011 does not arise. Further, in view of the discussions in the preceding paragraphs, it has been held that the removal of inputs as such cannot be considered as ‘trading’ activity and hence the appellants were not required to reverse the amount under Rule 6(3) of the CCR, 2004. Thus, we hold that the Ld. adjudicating authorities have rightly dropped the demands made in the notices for the period prior to 01.04.2011. We, therefore, do not find any infirmity in the impugned orders dropping the demands for the period prior to 01.04.2011 in the impugned orders. Accordingly, we hold that there is no merit in the appeals filed by the Revenue and consequently, reject the same. 9. In the result, we allow the appeals filed by the appellants, with consequential relief, if any, as per law. The appeals filed by the Revenue are rejected.
