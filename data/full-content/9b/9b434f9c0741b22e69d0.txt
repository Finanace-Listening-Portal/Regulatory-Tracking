Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Vs Schaeffler India Limited (ITAT Ahmedabad) Section 292B Cannot Cure Assessment Framed in Name of Amalgamating Company After AO Was Informed of Merger: ITAT Ahmedabad Summary: The Ahmedabad Bench of the Incoe Tax Appellate Tribunal held that a valid scrutiny notice issued before amalgamation does not save a final assessment framed in the name of a company that has ceased to exist, where the Assessing Officer (AO) was informed of the merger. The Tribunal held that the defect went to the root of jurisdiction and could not be cured under Section 292B of the Income-tax Act, 1961. It upheld the CIT(A)’s orders quashing both assessments. Facts of the Case For AY 2016–17, LUK India Private Limited filed its return while it was still in existence, and the AO validly issued a notice under Section 143(2) on 13 September 2017. During the assessment proceedings, LUK India amalgamated with Schaeffler India Limited with effect from 1 January 2018 under a scheme approved by the National Company Law Tribunal. The Department was informed of the amalgamation, and the case was transferred from Hosur to Vadodara, where Schaeffler India was assessed. Schaeffler India made submissions as the successor and specifically informed the Vadodara AO of the amalgamation by letter dated 5 November 2018. The AO nevertheless passed the final assessment order dated 17 January 2020 under Section 144 read with Section 144C in the name of LUK India. The CIT(A) quashed the assessment. Valid Initiation of Scrutiny Did Not Save the Final Assessment The Revenue argued that the return and scrutiny notice were valid because LUK India existed when they were filed and issued, respectively. It contended that using the erstwhile company’s name in the final order was a procedural irregularity curable under Section 292B. The Tribunal rejected that argument. By the date of the final order, LUK India had ceased to exist, and the AO had been expressly informed of the amalgamation. Following the Supreme Court’s decision in PCIT v. Maruti Suzuki India Ltd. and the Gujarat High Court’s decision in Anokhi Realty Private Limited v. ITO, the Tribunal held that framing the assessment in the name of the non-existent company was a substantive jurisdictional defect. Section 292B could not validate the order. Same Principle Applied to INA Bearing India for AY 2017–18 The second appeal concerned INA Bearing India Private Limited, which also amalgamated with Schaeffler India with effect from 1 January 2018. The jurisdictional AO was informed of the merger, and the case was transferred from Pune to Vadodara. The Transfer Pricing Officer also acknowledged the merger. The final assessment under Section 143(3) read with Section 144C(3) was nevertheless framed in the name of INA Bearing India. The Tribunal followed its reasoning in the LUK India appeal and the coordinate bench’s ruling on the same issue in INA Bearing India’s case for AY 2016–17. It upheld the quashing of both assessments and dismissed both Revenue appeals. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT AHMEDABAD These two appeals are filed by the Revenue against the orders of Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeal) -13, Ahmedabad [hereinafter referred to as “CIT(A)”] dated 29.06.2026 and 30.06.2026 for the Assessment Years (A.Y.) 2016-17 and 2017-18 respectively. As the issues involved in the two appeals are identical, both the matters were heard together and are being disposed of vide this common order for the sake of convenience. We will first take up the appeal of the Revenue for A.Y. 2016-17. ITA No. 2907/Ahd/2026: A.Y. 2016-17 2. The ground taken by the Revenue in this appeal are as under: 1. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law, the learned Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals) [“Ld. CIT(A)” erred in quashing the assessment order solely on the ground that the assessment was framed in the name of LUK India Pvt. Ltd., a non-existent entity, without properly appreciating the factual and legal position relating to the issue of notice u/s 143(2) of the Act in the name of valid and existent entity. 2. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld CIT(A) erred in holding that the assessment order was void ab initio and without jurisdiction merely because the order was passed in the name of LUK India Pvt. Ltd., without appreciating that the return of income for AY 2016-17 had itself been filed in the name of LUK India Pvt. Ltd. on 30.11.2016, at a point of time when the said company was an existing and legally recognized entity and also that such error was rectifiable u/s 292B of the Act. 3. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld. CIT(A) erred in not appreciating that the assessment proceedings pertained to AY 2016-17 and were initiated on the basis of a valid return filed by LUK India Pvt. Ltd. when it was an existing entity, and therefore the subsequent amalgamation, with effect from 01.01.2018, ought to have been examined in the context of the statutory provisions governing succession, amalgamation and continuation of assessment proceedings. 4. That the appellant craves leave to add, amend, alter, vary or withdraw any ground(s) of appeal at the time of hearing. 3. Shri R. V. Aroon Prasaad, the Ld. CIT-DR, submitted that the assessment order in this case was passed on 17.01.2020 u/s 144 r.w.s. 144C of the Act, in the name of Luk India Private Limited, which was amalgamated with Schaeffler India Ltd., with effect from 01.01.2018. The Ld. CIT-DR explained that the assessee (erstwhile M/s. Luk India Private Limited) had filed its return of income for A.Y. 2016-17 on 30.11.2016 and also filed a revised return on 28.02.2017. A notice u/s 143(2) of the Act was issued on validly issued to the assessee on 13.09.2017. Luk India Private Limited amalgamated with Schaeffler India Ltd. with effect from 01.01.2018 in view of the scheme of amalgamation approved by the NCLT vide order dated 13.06.2018. According to the Ld. CIT-DR, the assessment proceeding in the case of the assessee was validly initiated by issuance of notice u/s 143(2) of the Act on 13.09.2017 in the name of the erstwhile company. It was only during the course of the assessment proceedings that Luk India Private Limited had got amalgamated with Schaeffler India Ltd. He submitted that the assessment order passed on 17.01.2020 in the name of Luk India Private Limited was a mere procedural irregularity, which was a curable defect u/s 292B of the Act. Therefore, the Ld. CIT(A) was not correct in quashing the assessment order only on the ground that the assessment was framed in the name of LUK India Private Limited, a non-existent entity. 4. Per contra, Ms. Amrin Pathan, the Ld. AR of the assessee, supported the order of the Ld. CIT(A). She explained that Luk India Private Limited was assessed with ACIT, Circle-1, Hosur, and the fact of amalgamation of Luk India Private Limited with Schaeffler India Ltd. was duly brought to the notice of the AO at Hosur. Thereafter, the jurisdiction of the assessee was transferred to Vadodara, where the case of Schaeffler India Ltd. was assessed. In spite of transfer of jurisdiction of Luk India Private Limited from Hosur to Vadodara pursuant to the amalgamation, the final order u/s 144 r.w.s. 144C(3) of the Act was passed in the name of Luk India Private Limited, which was no longer in existence. The Ld. AR submitted that the case was squarely covered by the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Maruti Suzuki India Ltd. v. PCIT reported in [107 taxmann.com 375] and the decision of the Hon’ble Gujarat High Court in the case of Anokhi Realty Private Limited V. ITO reported in [153 taxmann.com 275], as relied upon the ld. CIT(A). 5. We have considered the rival submissions and perused the material available on record. The controversy in the present appeal is whether the assessment order passed in the name of Luk India Private Limited, after its amalgamation with Schaeffler India Ltd., is legally sustainable or whether the defect is merely procedural and curable u/s 292B of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The relevant facts of the case are not in dispute. The erstwhile company, Luk India Private Limited, filed its return of income for A.Y. 2016-17 on 30.11.2016 and a revised return on 28.02.2017. Subsequently, a notice u/s 143(2) of the Act was validly issued to Luk India Private Limited on 13.09.2017. Thereafter, pursuant to the scheme of amalgamation approved by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) vide order dated 13.06.2018, Luk India Private Limited was amalgamated with Schaeffler India Ltd. with effect from 01.01.2018. It is also evident from the record that the fact of amalgamation was duly brought to the notice of the Assessing Officer. Consequent to the amalgamation, the jurisdiction of the case was transferred from the AO at Hosur to the AO at Vadodara, where Schaeffler India Ltd. was assessed. The subsequent replies and submissions before the AO at Vadodara were also filed by Schaeffler India Ltd. in its capacity as the successor of Luk India Private Limited. Further, the fact of amalgamation was specifically intimated to the AO at Vadodara vide letter dated 05.11.2018. Thus, the Department was fully aware of the amalgamation and the consequent cessation of the existence of Luk India Private Limited. Despite being aware of these developments, the AO had passed the assessment order dated 17.01.2020 u/s 144 read with section 144C of the Act in the name of Luk India Private Limited, which had ceased to exist pursuant to the amalgamation. 6. The contention of the Revenue is that since the assessment proceedings had been validly initiated by issuing notice u/s 143(2) of the Act on 13.09.2017, i.e., prior to the amalgamation, the subsequent assessment order passed in the name of the amalgamating company should be treated as a procedural irregularity curable under section 292B of the Act. We are unable to accept this contention. This issue is no longer res integra in view of the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Maruti Suzuki India Ltd. (supra). The Hon’ble Supreme Court has categorically held that an assessment order passed in the name of an amalgamating company, which had ceased to exist pursuant to amalgamation, constitutes a substantive illegality and not a mere procedural defect curable u/s 292B of the Act. The Hon’ble Court further observed that where the AO was duly informed about the amalgamation, the continuation of proceedings and passing of the assessment order in the name of the non-existent entity would render the assessment invalid. This legal position has also been followed by the Hon’ble jurisdictional Gujarat High Court in the case of Anokhi Realty Private Limited (supra). In the present case, the facts clearly establish that the AO was duly informed about the amalgamation, not only at the stage of transfer of jurisdiction but also through the specific communication dated 05.11.2018. Nevertheless, the assessment order was passed on 17.01.2020 in the name of the erstwhile company, Luk India Private Limited. We also note that the Coordinate Bench of the Tribunal in the case of INA Bearing India Ltd. [ITA No. 2021/Ahd/2025, order dated 22.04.2026] has taken an identical view and upheld the quashing of an assessment order passed in the name of a non-existent entity. 7. In view of the above facts and the settled legal position, we find no infirmity in the findings of the Ld. CIT(A) that the assessment order passed in the name of Luk India Private Limited, a non-existent entity, is bad in law and without jurisdiction. The plea of the Revenue that the defect is curable under section 292B of the Act is devoid of merit. Accordingly, we uphold the order of the Ld. CIT(A) quashing the assessment order and dismiss all the grounds raised by the Revenue. 8. In the result, the appeal of the Revenue is dismissed. ITA No. 2916/Ahd/2026, A.Y. 2017-18 9. We have considered the rival submissions and perused the material available on record. We find that the facts involved in the present appeal and the grounds raised by the Revenue are identical to those considered by us in ITA No. 2907/Ahd/2026 for A.Y. 2016-17. In the present case, the assessee, INA Bearing India Private Limited, was amalgamated with Schaeffler India Ltd. with effect from 01.01.2018 pursuant to the scheme of amalgamation approved by the NCLT vide order dated 08.10.2018. The fact of amalgamation was duly intimated to the jurisdictional Assessing Officer, pursuant to which the jurisdiction of the case was transferred from Pune to Vadodara. It is pertinent to note that the fact of amalgamation was also acknowledged by the Transfer Pricing Officer (TPO) in his order passed under section 92CA(3) of the Act dated 13.08.2021. Despite having acknowledged that INA Bearing India Private Limited had merged with Schaeffler India Ltd., the TPO proceeded to pass the order in the name of the erstwhile company. Subsequently, the Assessing Officer also passed the assessment order under section 143(3) read with section 144C(3) of the Act on 21.04.2021 in the name of INA Bearing India Private Limited, which had ceased to exist pursuant to the amalgamation. We further find that the Coordinate Bench of this Tribunal, in the assessee’s own case for A.Y. 2016-17 in ITA No. 2021/Ahd/2025, has already considered an identical issue and upheld the order of the Ld. CIT(A) quashing the assessment framed in the name of the non-existent entity. The relevant findings of the Coordinate Bench are reproduced below: “5. Heard rival submissions and perused the materials available on record. It is undisputed fact that the Scheme of Amalgamation order passed by NCLT, Mumbai was filed by the assessee before the Assessing Officer vide letter dated 31-10-2018 which was received and acknowledged by the A.O. at Pune on 01-11-2018 which is placed at Page Nos. 1 & 2 of the Paper Book. Further vide assessee’s letter dated 05-11-2018 on the same information which was received by DCIT, Circle-1(1)(2), Vadodara on 06-11-2018. In spite of the knowledge of the Amalgamation, the assessing officer passed the assessment order on a non-existing entity namely M/s. INA Bearing India Pvt. Ltd. This issue of passing order on a non-existing entity is no more res integra, even after intimation of the change of name by the assessee company to the concerned assessing officer, as held by Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Maruti Suzuki India Ltd., which was followed by Ld. CIT(A) in his appellate order. Thus the findings arrived by Ld. CIT(A) does not require any interference. Therefore the grounds raised by the Revenue are devoid of merits and liable to be dismissed.” 11. The aforesaid findings squarely cover the issue arising in the present appeal. We also find that in ITA No. 2907/Ahd/2026 for A.Y. 2016-17, we have followed the same legal position while adjudicating the identical issue in the case of the amalgamated entity. The material facts being indistinguishable, the reasoning adopted therein applies mutatis mutandis to the present appeal as well. Accordingly, respectfully following the decision of the Coordinate Bench in the assessee’s own case for A.Y. 2016-17 in ITA No. 2021/Ahd/2025, as well as our decision in ITA No. 2907/Ahd/2026 for A.Y. 2016-17, we find no reason to interfere with the order of the Ld. CIT(A). The grounds raised by the Revenue are accordingly dismissed. The appeal of the Revenue is dismissed. 12. In the final result, both the appeals of the Revenue are dismissed. Order pronounced in the Court on 18/11/2026 at Ahmedabad.
