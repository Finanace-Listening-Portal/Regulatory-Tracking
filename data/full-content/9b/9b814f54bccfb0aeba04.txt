Articles 5 min read RBI to make banks hold capital against CVA risk from April 2027 The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has introduced a new Credit Valuation Adjustment (CVA) framework requiring banks to hold capital against losses arising from changes in the creditworthiness of derivative counterparties. The framework will come into effect from April 1, 2027, with an alternate treatment available for banks with non-centrally cleared derivatives of up to Rs 10 lakh crore. It covers both domestic operations and overseas branches, subject to the conditions specified by RBI.CVA is an adjustment to the value of a derivative to account for the credit risk of the counterparty. Advt RBI defines CVA risk as the risk of losses arising from changes in CVA values in response to changes in counterparty credit spreads and market risk factors that drive derivative prices.The regulatory CVA under the framework may differ from accounting CVA, as it excludes the effect of the bank's own default and incorporates certain regulatory constraints.Which transactions will be covered?The framework will cover derivatives, except those transacted directly with qualified central counterparties and certain other transactions specified under RBI's capital adequacy directions.Banks will have to calculate CVA capital requirements on a standalone basis for covered transactions in both the banking book and trading book.The framework will cover eligible transactions undertaken through both domestic operations and overseas branches.However, trades pertaining to security financing transactions will be excluded from the CVA capital charge.What is the basic approach under the new framework?Under the new framework, banks will generally have to use the Basic Approach for Credit Valuation Adjustment, or BA-CVA, for calculating the CVA capital charge.However, RBI has provided an alternate treatment for banks with relatively smaller non-centrally cleared derivative portfolios.A bank whose aggregate notional amount of non-centrally cleared derivatives, on a consolidated group-wide basis, is less than or equal to Rs 10 lakh crore at the reporting date can opt not to use BA-CVA and instead adopt the alternate treatment. Advt The supervisory authority, however, can disallow the option if it determines that CVA risk arising from the bank's derivative positions materially contributes to its overall risk.If the bank crosses the Rs 10 lakh crore threshold at the reporting date, it will have to apply BA-CVA from that reporting date.How will the alternate treatment work?Under the alternate treatment, the bank's CVA capital requirement will be equal to 100 per cent of its capital requirement for counterparty credit risk, or CCR.Banks using this route will not be allowed to recognise CVA hedges.The alternate treatment will also have to be applied to the bank's entire portfolio of covered transactions. Advt What are the two versions of BA-CVA?Banks using BA-CVA can choose between the reduced and full versions.The reduced BA-CVA is designed to simplify implementation for banks that do not hedge CVA risk. It does not recognise the benefit of CVA hedges.The full BA-CVA, on the other hand, recognises eligible hedges against counterparty credit spread risk and is intended for banks that hedge CVA risk.However, even banks using the full version will have to calculate the reduced BA-CVA capital requirement, which RBI has prescribed as a conservative measure to limit recognition of hedging benefits.What hedges will be recognised under the full BA-CVA? Advt The full BA-CVA recognises certain instruments used to hedge counterparty credit spread risk.Eligible hedges include single-name credit default swaps (CDS), single-name contingent CDS and index CDS, although RBI said banks are currently not permitted to engage in single-name contingent CDS.Eligible single-name CDS can reference the counterparty directly, an entity legally related to the counterparty, or an entity belonging to the same sector and region as the counterparty.How will the capital requirement affect risk-weighted assets?RBI has prescribed that CVA risk-weighted assets will be calculated by multiplying the CVA capital charge by 12.5.The capital requirement will vary depending on factors including the bank's exposure to counterparties, their credit quality and sector, as well as the effective maturity of transactions.The framework uses exposure at default derived under the Standardised Approach for Counterparty Credit Risk (SA-CCR) for the calculation of the CVA capital requirement.RBI has also prescribed a one-year floor for effective maturity, subject to specified exemptions for fully or nearly fully collateralised short-term OTC derivatives with daily re-margining and other conditions.What will banks have to disclose?Banks will have to make disclosures on CVA risk as part of their Pillar 3 disclosure requirements.RBI has prescribed a qualitative disclosure template as well as separate templates for reporting under the reduced and full BA-CVA approaches.The disclosure requirements will also apply to banks using the alternate treatment.
