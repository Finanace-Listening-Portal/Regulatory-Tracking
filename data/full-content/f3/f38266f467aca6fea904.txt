Advt _________________________Union Bank of India has estimated a one-time impact of around Rs 55 billion from the transition to the expected credit loss (ECL) framework, against which it has already created provisions of about Rs 10 billion. The bank also created Rs2 billion of additional standard asset provision in the second quarter of FY27, while credit costs declined to 0.21% from 0.38% in the first quarter.The bank expects recoveries to exceed fresh slippages in FY27 and has retained its target of Rs 100-120 billion in total recoveries, including around Rs 30 billion from technical write-offs. Fresh slippages declined 11.5% quarter-on-quarter to Rs19.1 billion in the second quarter from Rs 21.6 billion in the first quarter, while gross and net NPA ratios improved to 2.42% and 0.42%, respectively, according to Motilal Oswal Financial Services.The provisioning transition comes even as Union Bank reported a 30% year-on-year increase in second-quarter net profit to Rs55.4 billion, beating the analyst estimate by 11%. The earnings growth was driven by strong net interest income and lower provisioning.Q2 performanceNet interest income rose 20% year-on-year and 5% quarter-on-quarter to Rs105.8 billion, beating the estimate by 8%. Net interest margin improved 6 basis points quarter-on-quarter to 2.86%, against the estimated 2.68%, largely due to lower cost of deposits.Pre-provision operating profit increased 19% year-on-year and 1% quarter-on-quarter to Rs81.1 billion, beating the estimate by 6%. Other income rose 1% sequentially but declined 7% year-on-year to Rs46.5 billion, while core fee income remained flat. Advt Union Bank's loan book grew 19.4% year-on-year and 5.6% quarter-on-quarter to Rs11.3 trillion, led by MSME, corporate and RAM segments. MSME advances rose 7.2% quarter-on-quarter, while corporate advances increased 3.8%. Within retail, vehicle finance grew 5.2% and housing loans rose 3.7% sequentially.Deposits grew 6.9% year-on-year and 2.8% quarter-on-quarter to Rs13.2 trillion, resulting in the credit-deposit ratio rising 230 basis points sequentially to 85.8%. Current-account deposits increased 10% quarter-on-quarter, although the CASA ratio declined 17 basis points to 34.9%.The bank has retained its guidance of growing the loan book in line with industry trends and expects to maintain its domestic credit-deposit ratio in the 80-84% range. It also mobilised $2.25 billion under the FCNR(B) scheme, with 70% leverage on these deposits. Advt Asset quality improved during the quarter, with fresh slippages declining and healthy recoveries and upgrades helping reduce gross and net NPAs. The provision coverage ratio remained stable at 82.9%.The bank has estimated the one-time ECL transition impact at around Rs55 billion, with about Rs10 billion of provisions already created. It also created Rs2 billion of additional standard asset provision in the second quarter. The bank expects recoveries to exceed slippages and has retained its FY27 recovery targets.Operating expenses rose 7% year-on-year and 2% quarter-on-quarter, pushing the cost-to-income ratio to 46.8% from 45.3% in the first quarter. Motilal Oswal raised its FY27 earnings estimate by 5%, reflecting the improving earnings trajectory, and projected FY27 return on assets and return on equity at 1.3% and 16.5%, respectively. It retained a Neutral rating and a target price of Rs200, based on 1.0 times March 2028 estimated adjusted book value.
