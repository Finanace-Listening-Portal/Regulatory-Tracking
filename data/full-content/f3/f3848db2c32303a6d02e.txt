Articles 2 min read Robust inflows, pullback in shorts lift rupee to seven-month peak The Indian rupee saw a notable rise, achieving its peak value in seven months following a surge in foreign equity investments. Closing at 93.47 per dollar, this upswing was driven by an impressive influx of $10 billion this month. Additionally, traders observed substantial movements in the dollar-rupee non-deliverable forward market as bearish positions were unwound. India's benchmark Nifty 50, meanwhile, rose 0.4%. Advt Foreign investors have poured money into local stocks over November so far with month-to-date flows tracking at a record $10 billion. The scale of inflows has boosted the rupee with the rally sparking an adjustment of trading positions.On Tuesday, three traders pointed to sizeable flows in the dollar-rupee non-deliverable forward market, likely linked to the unwinding of bearish rupee wagers.The flows pushed the rupee into a modest premium in the NDF market compared with the onshore forward market.The view on the rupee has "mostly turned neutral," but the outlook depends on whether the sell-AI theme persists and oil prices hold sustainably below $100 per barrel, a trader at a Singapore based hedge fund said.Elsewhere, currency markets were subdued with the dollar index hovering near 19-month highs on bets on more aggressive policy tightening by the Federal Reserve following last week's hot inflation data and hawkish central bank rhetoric.The focus is on earnings from chip giant Nvidia, whose results could set the tone for the AI trade."We still think further weakness in tech/AI stocks should make additional greenback appreciation less sustainable as it drives more dovish Fed repricing," ING said in a note.
