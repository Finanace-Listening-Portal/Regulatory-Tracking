Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Sonal Sumit Mehta Vs Chirag R Shah (NCLAT Delhi) Summary: The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, Principal Bench, New Delhi, dismissed IA No.5856/2026 filed by B.D. Agarwal, a shareholder and personal guarantor of Krystal Stone Exports Ltd., seeking recall of its judgment dated 17 July 2026. That judgment had set aside the NCLT’s direction requiring payment of the balance resolution amount of ₹14,60,56,036 within 45 days with interest at 12% per annum and excluded additional time required to vacate the Jaipur property from the approved resolution plan’s implementation period. The recall applicant contended that he had not been impleaded or heard, that the plan’s payment obligation was unconditional, and that the earlier appellate relief amounted to modification of the approved plan. The Tribunal found that the applicant was neither a financial nor an operational creditor and had not been made a party to IA No.2044/2026. His counsel’s appearance and request for time to intervene did not establish an order allowing intervention. The Tribunal also found that the recall application sought to obstruct implementation of an operative resolution plan. Despite the successful resolution applicant paying the entire upfront amount of ₹4,20,43,964 within 30 days of plan approval, possession of the subject property had not been handed over because of continued occupation by a company owned and controlled by the recall applicant and his immediate family. The subsequent NCLT direction to vacate within 15 days remained uncomplied with, without any stay. The Tribunal held that handing over possession upon receipt of the upfront payment was an obligation under the plan, rather than a concession requiring separate approval. It found that the successful resolution applicant could not be penalised with interest for delay arising from the applicant’s family-controlled company’s occupation. The sole financial creditor had been represented in the earlier appeal and had not opposed it. Relying on the principle reiterated in Ashok Dattaray Atre & Ors V State Bank of India & Ors, and referring to GP Global Energy Pvt Ltd Vs. Mr. Sandeep Mahajan & Anr, the Tribunal held that extension or exclusion of timelines for meeting financial obligations does not constitute modification of a resolution plan. Finding no legal injury to the applicant and no basis for recall or review, it dismissed the application. Mr. Sandeep Mahajan & Anr — NCLAT, Company Appeal (AT)(Ins) No.054/2021 as printed in the supplied order, decided on 06 May 2022 — Relied upon for the distinction between extending payment timelines and modifying an approved resolution plan. Heard. 11. It appears the present I.A. No. 5856 of 2026 is not an isolated, bona fide recall application, but it is a step to obstruct, delay and, if possible, unwind implementation of a Resolution Plan that was approved on 23.02.2026 by the Ld. NCLT Mumbai Bench and continues to remain operative and binding, there being no stay of its operation. Till date the possession of an inch of land has not been handed over to the Successful Resolution Applicant (Appellant) of Plot No. SP-2, RIICO Industrial Area, Kiliki Pura Village (also referred to in the records as Shivdaspura), Tehsil Shivdaspura, District Jaipur, Rajasthan, admeasuring 14,028 sq. mtrs. and is in the illegal possession of a tenant named “Countertops & Cabinets Pvt. Ltd.” (related party of B.D. Agarwal). 12. It is well settled a suspended board/erstwhile management has no unqualified right to participate in, or be heard on, matters concerning implementation of a resolution plan once the CoC’s commercial wisdom has been exercised. It is settled principle of law procedural rights of suspended directors are “subservient to the primary objectives of the IBC and are “not intended as a tool for the former management to delay or frustrate the process”. 13. Admittedly, the Applicant is neither a financial nor an operational creditor of the Corporate Debtor. Mr. B.D. Agarwal was not a party to I.A. 2044/2026; paragraph 13 of the order dated 01.06.2026 merely records his counsel appeared and sought time to intervene. No order allowing intervention was passed. Thus, without being impleaded as the proper party, his application to recall is non-est and devoid of any merit. 14. Mr.B.D. Agarwal’s subsequent I.A. No. 2336/2026 was expressly dismissed as a “frivolous application” on 09.06.2026, and he was directed to pursue the grounds in his appeal against the plan-approval order. 15. It is an evident fact on 09.01.2024, the suspended director Mr. BD Agarwal acting as Director of the Corporate Debtor executed a rent agreement leasing the entire subject property viz Plot No. SP-2, RIICO Industrial Area, Kiliki Pura, Village Shivdaspura, Tehsil Shivdaspura, District Jaipur, admeasuring 14028 sq. mtrs to a tenant namely M/s Countertops Device and Activities Pvt. Now on 03.05.2024 the corporate debtor was admitted into CIRP. Section 14 of IBC came into force on 20.11.2024. As per the Information Memorandum, the Resolution Professional secured possession of the subject property but with the approval of the CoC granted a short term lease of only 4028 sq. mtrs out of 14028 sq. mtrs viz a part of the property to M/s Countertops at the market rent of Rs.1,20,000/- per month. The balance property continued to be in possession of the Resolution Professional. On 29th March, 2025, the notices for vacation of such part were sent by the Resolution Professional to M/s Countertops but since it was also one of the resolution applicant it was allowed to stay till the decision on its plan was taken by COC. However, its plan was not approved and on 26.05.2025 a final resolution plan as was submitted by the Successful Resolution Applicant was approved by the CoC. Instead of vacating the premises i.e. 4028 sq mtrs, the tenant went to Rent Tribunal under the Rent Control Act 2001 and obtained certain orders from there to stick to possession without informing the Rent Tribunal of the ongoing CIRP. The Resolution Plan was approved on 23.02.2026. An appeal filed by the suspended management and M/s Counter Top is pending before this Tribunal. Admittedly as per the Resolution Plan, the Successful Resolution Applicant was to make an upfront payment of Rs.4,20,43,964/- and the Resolution Professional was to give possession of the subject property, i.e. 10000 sq. mtrs of land which remained with the erstwhile Resolution Professional and 4028 sq. mtrs which continued to be in unauthorized occupation of M/s Countertops. On 03.04.2024 when the Monitoring Committee and Resolution Professional went to the subject plot an obstruction was created by the applicant’s family as they took over possession of the entire piece of land. It was in this context an application was moved by the Successful Resolution Applicant to defer the balance payment, decided per impugned order dated 01.06.2026. Admittedly on 03.06.2026 the tenant was directed to hand over the vacant possession within 15 days but it has not handed over the land till date. 18. Now it is an undisputed fact despite prompt and complete payment of the entire upfront amount of Rs. 4,20,43,964/- within 30 days of Plan approval, the Subject Property is still not handed over to the Successful Resolution Applicant and the delay is occasioned entirely by M/s Countertop’s continued occupation and not upon any default of the Successful Resolution Applicant. Thus, the extension of time in implementation of Resolution Plan was necessarily to be granted in compliance with Schedule 2 Clause 7 of the Resolution Plan. 19. The same stance was recorded in the Minutes of the Monitoring Committee itself at the 2nd Meeting (27.03.2026), the 3rd Meeting (15.04.2026), and, most importantly, the Monitoring Committee’s own deliberations dated 26.05.2026, where the Monitoring Committee expressly acknowledged the delay in fulfilment of obligations on the part of the Resolution Professional /CoC in handing over possession after timely payment and recorded any extension/exclusion of time would follow the Plan’s own terms, without insisting on interest. 20. The exclusion of time granted on 17.07.2026 was made precisely because the Subject Property (which was not under any valid tenancy) and despite the Successful Resolution Applicant having promptly paid the entire upfront amount, could not be handed over due to the continued and unauthorized occupation by M/s Countertops; infact by applicant’s own family. 21. Hence the Applicant cannot now contend the Successful Resolution Applicant should be made liable for interest. In effect, the Applicant seeks to penalise the Successful Resolution Applicant for a delay arising from the continued occupation by a company, owned and controlled by the Applicant and his immediate family. 22. The sole financial creditor was represented in Appeal No. 1262/2026 and did not oppose the appeal. B.D. Agarwal, the applicant cannot assert a superior right to insist upon interest or a payment schedule that the beneficiary creditor itself did not seek to protect. 23. The recall application does not fairly address the subsequent NCLT order dated 03.06.2026 directing M/s Countertops to vacate within 15 days; the continued non-compliance, and the absence of a stay against that direction. Additionally, this is not the case of either the Suspended Management or any of the parties that the appellant is not ready and willing to make the balance payment and rather the implementation is being seriously hampered by the conduct of the Suspended Management of the Corporate Debtor i.e. the applicant. 24. The obligation to hand over possession does not require any concession from the Ld. NCLT or a statutory authority. It is an obligation of the RP/CoC arising upon receipt of the upfront payment. Paragraph 28(1) of the Impugned Order also does not reject the possession obligation. It merely made the respective rights of the Corporate Debtor and tenant subject to the decision in the pending application. Even in that pending application, vide order dated 03.06.2026 passed by Ld. NCLT Mumbai Bench, it directed M/s Countertops to hand over possession within 15 days. Thus, we are of the considered opinion the Applicant has no right on merit to seek recall of an order/judgement passed by us and/or to challenge the extension given by us. Even, otherwise, it is settled legal principle that the extension/exclusion of timelines for complying with financial obligations under the Resolution Plan is not modification of the Plan. In GP Global Energy Pvt Ltd Vs. Mr. Sandeep Mahajan & Anr, Company Appeal (AT)(Ins) No.054/2021 decided on 06.05.2022, this Tribunal rather set aside the order of the Ld. NCLT denying the extension, while stating it was not within its power to cause amends to approved Resolution Plan. This Tribunal held the Ld. NCLT has jurisdiction to grant extension of timeline in making the payment in a Resolution Plan, and the view of the Ld. NCLT that such extension would amount to modification was held to be not correct. 25. The same law was reiterated by this Tribunal in Ashok Dattaray Atre & Ors V State Bank of India & Ors, Company Appeal (AT)(Ins) No.221/222 of2024 decided on 08.04.2024 and it was further held the concurrence of the CoC is not necessary, and the Ld. NCLT can exercise its jurisdiction while allowing extension, even in cases where CoC’s express concurrence is not present. 26. Therefore, the plea urged by the Applicant is contrary to the settled legal principles and also, runs counter to the general rule that the extension/exclusion is between the party seeking such extension and the Court. The Applicant has been caused no legal injury, and the Applicant cannot seek review of the order passed by this Tribunal on 17.07.2026. 27. The application is thus dismissed.
