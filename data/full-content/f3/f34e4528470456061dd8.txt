Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Quickpaisa Fintech Private Limited Vs ACIT (ITAT Delhi) Section 153C Assessment Is Void If the Satisfaction Note Fails to Link Seized Material to the Assessee and Its Total Income Delhi ITAT Quashes Both Search Assessments The Delhi ITAT quashed two assessments under section 153C of the Income-tax Act, 1961 because the satisfaction note failed to record how the transactions in the seized material pertained to Quickpaisa Fintech Pvt. Ltd. or whether they had a bearing on the determination of its total income. The Department had recovered ledgers during a search of the Alankit Group and alleged that they recorded accommodation entries. The Tribunal held that the omission went to the Assessing Officer’s jurisdiction and declared both assessments void ab initio. Alankit Group Search Led to Proceedings Against Quickpaisa The Department conducted a search under section 132 in the Alankit Group cases on 18 October 2019. During the search, it obtained ledgers from a laptop found at the residence of Sunil Kumar Gupta. According to the Department, the ledgers contained transactions involving various beneficiaries, allegedly undertaken through an Axis Bank account against unaccounted cash during FYs 2016–17 and 2017–18. The Assessing Officer recorded a consolidated satisfaction note dated 24 June 2022, covering AYs 2014–15 to 2020–21, and initiated proceedings against Quickpaisa under section 153C. The assessments relevant to these appeals were made on 26 February 2024 for AYs 2017–18 and 2018–19. Following the first appellate order dated 3 November 2025, the company approached the Tribunal. Its first two grounds challenged the validity of the Assessing Officer’s assumption of jurisdiction. The Tribunal took up those grounds first because they went to the root of both assessments. Satisfaction Note Must Record the Statutory Connection For the books and documents at issue, the Tribunal examined whether the note recorded that the seized material pertained to the assessee, or that information contained in it related to the assessee. It also examined whether the note recorded that the transactions had a bearing on the determination of the assessee’s total income. These findings mattered because the material had been recovered in a search involving another person and was being used to initiate proceedings against Quickpaisa. The Tribunal found that the note failed to record either connection. A ledger said to cover several beneficiaries may provide a starting point for enquiry. The satisfaction note must still show why the material concerns the particular person against whom section 153C is invoked and why it bears on that person’s income. The alleged character of the entries did not supply the findings missing from the note. Identical Satisfaction Note Had Already Been Examined The Tribunal followed Amit Singla v. DCIT, ITA Nos. 882 and 883/Del/2026, decided on 11 August 2026. In that case, a coordinate Bench had examined an identical satisfaction note dated 24 June 2022. It found that the note did not discuss whether the seized material belonged to, pertained to or related to the persons assessed, as contemplated by clauses (a) and (b) of section 153C. The Revenue had not rebutted that factual position. The coordinate Bench had relied on Super Malls (P.) Ltd. v. PCIT (2020) 115 taxmann.com 105 (SC) in quashing the assessments for want of valid satisfaction. In Quickpaisa’s case, the Tribunal found the same defect and applied the coordinate Bench’s reasoning. The decision turned on what the Assessing Officer had recorded in the satisfaction note as the basis for initiating proceedings against the company. Assessments Quashed and Merits Left Open The Tribunal allowed grounds 1 and 2 in both appeals and held that the Assessing Officer had wrongly assumed jurisdiction under section 153C. It consequently quashed the assessments in full for AYs 2017–18 and 2018–19. The other grounds became academic and were expressly left open. Both appeals were formally recorded as partly allowed because the Tribunal disposed of them on the jurisdictional grounds alone. The operative result was that both assessments stood quashed. The Tribunal therefore had no occasion to decide whether Quickpaisa had actually participated in the alleged accommodation-entry transactions. Author’s Comments The point worth noting is that the assessee prevailed on the contents of an existing satisfaction note. A note had been prepared and covered seven assessment years. Its existence and period of coverage were evident from the record. The defect was the failure to record what connected the seized ledger entries to Quickpaisa and how they bore on its income. That distinction matters when seized records refer to several persons or transactions. Reading the note against the underlying material can show whether the Assessing Officer identified the relevant entries and recorded their significance for the assessee being proceeded against. In this case, the identical note examined in Amit Singla gave the Tribunal a direct precedent on the same jurisdictional omission. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. The appeals in ITA No.1066 & 1067/Del/2026 for AYs 2017-18 and 2018-19, arises out of the order of the ld Pr. Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)-25, New Delhi [hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. PCIT(A)’, in short] dated 03.11.2025 against the order of assessment passed u/s 153C of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’) dated 26.02.2024 by the Assessing Officer, ACIT, Central Circle-25, Delhi (hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. AO’). Identical issues are involved in these two appeals and hence they are taken up together and disposed of by this common order for the sake of convenience. 2. The Ground Nos. 1 and 2 raised by the assessee are challenging the validity of assumption of jurisdiction under section 153C of the Act. These go to the root of the matter and hence taken up first for adjudication. 3. We have heard the rival submissions and perused the materials available on record. A search action under section 132 of the Act was carried out in Alankit group of cases on 18/10/2019. In the course of the search command certain materials slash documents related to assessee have been found. Ledgers have been obtained from the laptop of Shri Sunil Kumar Gupta from his residence. The Ledger account allegedly contained transactions of various beneficiaries against unaccounted cash in the form of accommodation entries transacted during financial years 2016-17 and 2017-18 in Axis Bank. Accordingly, proceedings stood initiated under section 153C of the Act on the assessee after recording consolidated satisfaction note dated 24-6- 2022 for assessment years 2014-15 to 2020-21. 4. The satisfaction note recorded under section 153C of the Act does not mention the fact as to whether the transactions pertain to belong to or relate to the assessee herein and whether those transactions have bearing on determination of total income of the assessee. We find that under identical circumstances, the coordinate bench of this Delhi tribunal in the case of Amit Singla vs DCIT in ITA Nos. 882 and 883 /Del/ 2026 dated 11-8-2026 had an occasion to address the same dispute in respect of the identical satisfaction note recorded. The relevant operative portion of the said tribunal order is reproduced below:- “2.Itemergesattheoutsetthatboththeseassessees/appellantscanvasstheirfirst and foremost identical legal ground challenging validity of the Impugned section 153C proceedings initiated in their respective cases for want of a valid satisfaction notethereunder.Thatbeingthecase,learnedcounselplacesonrecordtheAssessing Officer’s satisfaction note(s) dated 24.06.2022 initiating the impugned proceedings wherein there is not even an lota of discussion as to whether the corresponding seized material in fact “belonged to” or “pertained to” or “related to” therein; as prescribed in clause (a) and (b) to section 153C of the Act. This clinching factual position has gone unrebutted from the Revenue side. We accordingly quote Super Malls (P.) Ltd. vs. Principal Commissioner of Income Tax, 8 New Delhi [2020] 115 taxmann.com 105 (SC) that these twin assessees’ impugned as many assessments framed u/s 153C of the Act are not liable to be sustained for want of a valid satisfaction recorded thereunder. The same are hereby quashed in very terms therefore. All other remaining pleadings between the parties on merits stand rendered academic. 3. Thesetwinassessees’appealinITANos.882&883/Del/2026areallowedin aboveterms.Acopyofthiscommonorderbeplacedintherespectivecasefiles. 5. Respectfully following the same, we hold that assumption of jurisdiction under section 153C of the Act is totally flawed and hence the consequential search assessments framed in the hands of the assessee under section 153C of the Act are hereby quashed declared void abinitio. Accordingly, the Ground Nos. 1 and 2 raised by the assessee are allowed. 6. Since the entire assessments are quashed on aforesaid legal aspect, the adjudication of other grounds raised by the assessee become academic in nature and they are left open. 7. In the result, both the appeals of the assessee are partly allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 18-Nov-2026. Order pronounced in the open court on 18-Nov-2026.
