Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Banarsi Devi Vs ITO (ITAT Jaipur) Reassessment Beyond Surviving TOLA Period Is Void: Seven-Day Balance Cannot Become a Fresh Limitation Period Background of the Case A survey under section 133A was conducted at the business premises of M/s Impulse Career Institute, Sikar. A register containing rough entries was impounded. According to the Department, those entries reflected payment of ₹6 lakh as rent to the assessee. When confronted with the information, the assessee denied receiving rental income during the relevant year. The Assessing Officer nevertheless added ₹6,00,000 as unexplained money under section 69A and applied section 115BBE. The CIT(A) confirmed the addition. Before the Tribunal, the assessee challenged both the validity of the reassessment and the substantive treatment of the alleged rental income. The appeal was ultimately decided on limitation, without examining the addition on merits. The Crucial Reassessment Dates The original notice under section 148, issued under the old reassessment regime, was dated 23 June 2021. Following the Supreme Court’s decision in Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal, a notice under section 148A(b) was issued on 26 May 2022, seeking a response by 14 June 2022. The assessee submitted her reply on 18 June 2022. The fresh notice under section 148 of the new regime was issued on 25 July 2022. The assessee contended that this fresh notice was beyond the time available to the Assessing Officer under the surviving-period principle explained in Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal, 469 ITR 46 (Supreme Court). Assessee’s Limitation Argument The assessee argued that the old-regime notice had been issued only seven days before 30 June 2021, the extended deadline under the Taxation and Other Laws (Relaxation and Amendment of Certain Provisions) Act, 2020, commonly referred to as TOLA. Accordingly, only seven days survived for completing the remaining reopening procedure once the limitation clock resumed. Since her reply was received on 18 June 2022, the Assessing Officer was required to consider the response, pass the appropriate order under section 148A(d), and issue the fresh section 148 notice by 25 June 2022. The notice actually issued on 25 July 2022 was therefore time-barred. The Revenue contested this contention. Supreme Court’s Surviving-Period Principle The Tribunal examined the reasoning in Rajeev Bansal, particularly paragraphs 108 to 113. It noted that Ashish Agarwal had created a legal fiction treating the relevant old-regime section 148 notices as show-cause notices under the new regime. The resulting exclusion of time did not give the Department an unrestricted fresh period for reopening. Instead, the balance time surviving between the original notice date and 30 June 2021 remained available for completing the subsequent steps, after accounting for the applicable exclusions. As explained in the passages relied upon by the Tribunal, the clock resumed after receipt of the assessee’s response. The Assessing Officer then had to complete the remaining procedure within that surviving period. A fresh section 148 notice issued beyond it would be barred by limitation, affecting the jurisdiction to reassess. Tribunal’s Application to the Facts The Tribunal found that only seven days survived in the present case because the original notice was issued on 23 June 2021. The assessee replied to the section 148A(b) notice on 18 June 2022. Applying the surviving period, the Tribunal held that the fresh notice should have been issued on or before 25 June 2022. Since it was issued on 25 July 2022, the Tribunal concluded that the notice was time-barred. Consequently, the reassessment order lacked valid jurisdiction and was quashed as void ab initio. The assessee’s appeal was allowed. Rental Income and Section 115BBE Left Undecided The assessee had alternatively argued that, even if the alleged receipt were accepted, it should be assessed as income from house property, with the applicable 30% deduction, rather than as unexplained money under section 69A. She also challenged the application of section 115BBE and the levy of interest. However, after quashing the reassessment, the Tribunal treated these grounds as academic and did not adjudicate them. Author’s Comments Conversion of an old notice into a deemed show-cause notice does not replenish the Department’s limitation period. The remaining statutory time must still be calculated and respected. This decision illustrates the practical importance of preparing a complete reopening chronology. The original section 148 notice, supply of information, response period, actual reply, section 148A(d) order and fresh notice must be examined together. A reopening that appears procedurally complete may nevertheless fail because the fresh notice was issued after the surviving deadline. The seven-day period is specific to this case. It should not be applied mechanically to every reassessment arising from Ashish Agarwal; the available balance and relevant exclusions must be worked out from the actual record. Cases Discussed 1. Union of India Vs Ashish Agarwal, [2022] 138 taxmann.com 64 (SC) — old-regime reassessment notices deemed show-cause notices under the new regime. 2. Union of India Vs Rajeev Bansal, [2024] 469 ITR 46 (SC) — surviving-period limitation after applicable exclusions. Ld. DR vehemently contested the pleadings of the assessee as above. 7. I have heard the rival contentions. The fact that in the present case notice issued u/s 148A(b) of the Act was issued in pursuance to the direction of the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Ashish Agarwal (Supra) is not disputed. Undeniably, the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Rajeev Bansal (Supra) interpreted the applicability of the new law relating to reassessment u/s 147-151 of the Act ,applicable w.e.f 01-04-2021 to notices issued in lieu of the direction of the Hon’ble apex court in Ashish Agarwal (supra) and laid down the concept of surviving period for issuance of notice u/s 148 of the Act of the new regime after receiving reply of the assessee to notice u/s 148A(b) of the Act. The Hon’ble apex court held that the surviving period so available with the AO was the period between the date of issuance of notice u/s 148 of the Act of the old regime(applicable upto 31-03-2021) and the 30th June 2021 i.e. the date upto which the limitation for compliances was extended by TOLA. The relevant portion of the order of the Hon’ble Apex Court contained at para Nos.108-113 of the order, laying down the concept of surviving period for issuance of notice under the new regime of reassessment is as under: ….. 108. The Income Tax Act read with TOLA extended the time limit for issuing reassessment notices under Section 148, which fell for completion from 20 March 2020 to 31 March 2021, till 30 June 2021. All the reassessment notices under challenge in the present appeals were issued from 1 April 2021 to 30 June 2021 under the old regime. Ashish Agarwal (supra) deemed these reassessment notices under the old regime as show cause notices under the new regime with effect from the date of issuance of the reassessment notices. The effect of creating the legal fiction is that this Court has to imagine as real all the consequences and incidents that will inevitably flow from the fiction. 163 Therefore, the logical effect of the creation of the legal fiction by Ashish Agarwal (supra) is that the time surviving under the Income Tax Act read with TOLA will be available to the Revenue to complete the remaining proceedings in furtherance of the deemed notices, including issuance of reassessment notices under Section 148 of the new regime. The surviving or balance time limit can be calculated by computing the number of days between the date of issuance of the deemed notice and 30 June 2021. 109. If this Court had not created the legal fiction and the original reassessment notices were validly issued according to the provisions of the new regime, the notices under Section 148 of the new regime would have to be issued within the time limits extended by TOLA. As a corollary, the reassessment notices to be issued in pursuance of the deemed notices must also be within the time limit surviving under the Income Tax Act read with TOLA. This construction gives full effect to the legal fiction created in Ashish Agarwal (supra) and enables both the assesses and the Revenue to obtain the benefit of all consequences flowing from the fiction, 164 110. The effect of the creation of the legal fiction in Ashish Agarwal (supra) was that it stopped the clock of limitation with effect from the date of issuance of Section 148 notices under the old regime (which is also the date of issuance of the deemed notices]. As discussed in the preceding segments of this judgment, the period from the date of the issuance of the deemed notices till the supply of relevant information and material by the assessing officers to the assesses in terms of the directions issued by this Court in Ashish Agarwal (supra) has to be excluded from the computation of the period of limitation. Moreover, the period of two weeks granted to the assesses to reply to the show cause notices must also be excluded in terms of the third proviso to Section 149. 111. The clock started ticking for the Revenue only after it received the response of the assesses to the show causes notices. After the receipt of the reply, the assessing officer had to perform the following responsibilities: (i) consider the reply of the assessee under Section 149A(c); (ii) take a decision under Section 149A(d) based on the available material and the reply of the assessee; and (iii) issue a notice under Section 148 if it was a fit case for reassessment. Once the clock started ticking, the assessing officer wasrequired to complete these procedures within the surviving time limit. The surviving time limit, as prescribed under the Income Tax Act read with TOLA, was available to the assessing officers to issue the reassessment notices under Section 148 of the new regime. 112. Let us take the instance of a notice issued on 1 May 2021 under the old regime for a relevant assessment year. Because of the legal fiction, the deemed show cause notices will also come into effect from 1 May 2021. After accounting for all the exclusions, the assessing officer will have sixty-one days (days between 1 May 2021 and 30 June 2021] to issue a notice under Section 148 of the new regime. This time starts ticking for the assessing officer after receiving the response of the assessee. In this instance, if the assessee submits the response on 18 June 2022, the assessing officer will have sixty-one days from 18 June 2022 to issue a reassessment notice under Section 148 of the new regime. Thus, in this illustration, the time limit for issuance of a notice under Section 148 of the new regime will end on 18 August 2022. 113. In Ashish Agarwal (supra), this Court allowed the assesses to avail all the defences, including the defence of expiry of the time limit specified under Section 149(1). In the instant appeals, the reassessment notices pertain to the assessment years 2013-2014, 2014-2015, 2015-2016, 2016-2017, and 2017-2018. To assume jurisdiction to issue notices under Section 148 with respect to the relevant assessment years, an assessing officer has to: (1) issue the notices within the period prescribed under Section 149(1) of the new regime read with TOLA; and (ii) obtain the previous approval of the authority specified under Section 151. A notice issued without complying with the preconditions is invalid as it affects the jurisdiction of the assessing officer. Therefore, the reassessment notices issued under Section 148 of the new regime, which are in pursuance of the deemed notices, ought to be issued within the time limit surviving under the Income Tax Act read with TOLA. A reassessment notice issued beyond the surviving time limit will be time-barred. ……. 8. In the facts of the present case admittedly, only seven days surviving period was available with the AO to issue notice u/s 148 of the Act, since the notice issued u/s 148 of the Act under the old regime was issued on 23rd June 2021 and the extension of limitation as per TOLA was 30th June 2021. The assessee had filed reply to the notice issued u/s 148A(b) of the Act of the new regime on 18th June 2022. Given seven days to issue notice therefore, the notice issued u/s 148 of the new regime ought to have been issued by the AO, as per the decision of the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Rajeev Bansal (supra), by the 25th of June 2022 however; the AO has issued notice in the facts of the present case on 25.07.2022. 9. It is abundantly clear, therefore, that the notice issued u/s 148 of the Act in the present case is barred by limitation. The order passed by the AO is therefore is clearly without valid jurisdiction, and is therefore quashed as void ab initio. 10. Ground of appeal No. 1 is allowed. 11. Since I have quashed the assessment order passed, the other grounds of appeal raised by the assessee are not being dealt with by me, as they are mere academic in nature. 12. The appeal of the assessee is accordingly allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 12.10.2026.
