(AI Generated Image)follow usfollow usThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is proposing a new set of customer-centric safeguards, including a 'kill switch' to instantly block all outward transactions, allowing bank customers to take greater control of their money and limit losses from digital banking fraud. The proposed measures also include self-imposed transaction limits, restrictions on payments to specific beneficiaries and a trusted-person approval mechanism for high-value transfers.Under the RBI's proposal, the customers would also be allowed to set their own limits on individual transactions, payments to specific beneficiaries and overall daily transfers across digital payment channels. RBI has additionally proposed a trusted-person mechanism under which customers can require another person’s approval for transactions above a self-set threshold.How will the proposed switch work? With the switch coming into effect, activation would block all future customer-initiated debits from an account, including creation of new mandates, standing instructions and registration of UPI on new third-party payment applications. RBI has also proposed that banks allow customers to disable and re-enable individual digital payment channels, while giving them the option to set transaction-level, beneficiary-level and daily limits. Further, the proposal allowscCustomers to specify time periods during which particular payment channels remain disabled. Under the trusted-person mechanism, customers may nominate an adult who would act as an additional authentication factor for electronic fund transfers above a threshold chosen by the account holder. The trusted person would receive transaction and beneficiary details and have one hour to approve the transaction; failure to approve within the prescribed period would result in rejection. The facility would be voluntary.What remains outside the kill switch? The mechanism will not apply to UPI transactions, merchant payments or recurring payments through standing instructions, e-mandates or NACH mandates.Existing recurring mandates, loan instalments and bank charges would, however, remain outside its scope, while inward credits would continue.Digital payment channels remain disabled by default when a new customer is onboarded and be activated only with explicit consent.
