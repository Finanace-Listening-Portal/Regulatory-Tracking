Summary: Form FC is the prescribed reporting form for financial commitments and disinvestment relating to overseas investments by persons resident in India under the Foreign Exchange Management Act framework. Reporting is undertaken through the designated Authorised Dealer (AD) bank, whether the investment follows the Automatic Route or requires regulatory approval. Financial commitment encompasses equity investments, loans, guarantees, pledges and charges, subject to the applicable overseas investment rules. Form FC contains seven sections covering investor identification, foreign entity particulars, transaction details, declarations, auditor certification, restructuring and disinvestment. Reporting obligations arise at different stages, including initial investment, further financial commitment, issuance or modification of guarantees, conversion of loans or deferred consideration into equity, restructuring of overseas investments and receipt of disinvestment proceeds. The form requires information about ownership, control, step-down subsidiaries, financial commitment limits, pricing compliance and applicable regulatory approvals. Particular attention is necessary where the investor has an NPA account, is classified as a wilful defaulter or is subject to investigation, since additional regulatory conditions may apply. The auditor’s certificate addresses investment eligibility, financial limits, valuation, reporting compliance and other prescribed matters. Investors should also monitor the Unique Identification Number allotted to the foreign entity, maintain accurate transaction records and ensure that changes are reported within the prescribed timelines. Delayed filings may attract the Late Submission Fee mechanism. Before filing, investors should reconcile their overseas investment records with the applicable FEMA Overseas Investment Rules, Regulations, RBI Directions and their designated AD bank’s requirements. Form FC is the reporting form for overseas investment under FEMA. Any person resident in India (a company, LLP, individual, trust or society) who invests in a foreign entity files it with their designated AD bank. It applies under both the Automatic Route and the Approval Route. Under the Approval Route, the AD bank scrutinises the form and then forwards it to RBI. “Financial commitment” is broader than money sent abroad. It covers equity, loans, guarantees (corporate, personal, performance, bank), and pledges or charges over assets, in India or abroad. When to file Form FC? New or further investment:File when you remit money or make the commitment, whichever happens first. Don’t wait for the money to move if the commitment is already made. Guarantees:Because a guarantee isn’t a cash outflow, the form can be filed before you give it. If the issue date or validity date later changes, tell the AD bank by the next working day. Any rollover or change must be reported again. Loan converted to equity:Reported in Section C, with the 15-digit transaction number RBI allotted when the original loan was reported. Deferred payment on equity:If you buy shares and pay part of the price later, the deferred part is reported as a non-fund-based commitment. When you finally pay, you report it again as conversion to equity. That second filing doesn’t count as fresh commitment against your limit. So there are two filings, and a Late Submission Fee facility exists if either is delayed. Restructuring (Section F):If the foreign entity restructures its balance sheet and the dues owed to you are written down, file within 30 days. Disinvestment (Section G):File within 30 days of receiving the sale proceeds. If proceeds arrive in instalments, each receipt is reported separately. The sections in plain terms in Form FC Section A: About the Investor Identity, PAN, LEI, industry code, net worth, and your total financial commitment across all foreign entities, including the current one. It also asks whether you have an NPA account, are a wilful defaulter, or are under investigation. That matters because an NOC is needed in those cases. Section B: About the foreign entity or Investee Name, country, incorporation date, LEI, business activity, and whether it’s a start-up, in a strategic sector or in financial services. It also covers the amount being committed, the shareholding split between you and foreign partners, whether you have control, and details of any step-down subsidiaries (SDS) and their levels. Section C: About the transaction The core of the form. It lists the method (banking channel, ECB, swap of securities, capitalisation, guarantees, pledges, conversions, mergers and so on), category, date and amount. The AD branch signs a confirmation that the transaction complies with FEMA and RBI directions. Section D: The required declaration(s) The investor confirms the NOC status, share certificates and reporting under Regulations 9 and 10, debt and pricing compliance, no pending reporting delays, gift compliance (FCRA) where relevant, and, for individuals, that the amount is within the LRS limit. Section E: Auditor’s certificate The statutory auditor certifies several things: the investment isn’t in a prohibited activity (Rule 19) the NOC has been obtained where needed the total commitment is within the limit, shown as a % of net worth pricing and valuation norms were followed the financial-services conditions are met no reporting delay is pending any group company guarantee is within its own limit The auditor signs with firm registration number and UDIN. Section F: Restructuring Shows your stake, commitment, the foreign entity’s accumulated losses, the dues outstanding, how much has been written down (equity, debt, receivables), and your position after restructuring. If the original investment is above USD 10 million, or the write-down is above 20%, a valuer’s certificate is needed, dated within six months of the restructuring. Section G: Disinvestment Covers the route, full or partial exit, date, stake, and method (sale, closure, buyback or one of the merger types). It also covers cumulative commitment, remittance history, fair value, any write-off, and amounts repatriated. Declarations include arm’s-length pricing, no outstanding dues for a full exit, a minimum one-year investment period, and that guarantees are novated or closed. Practical rules worth remembering Sections A and B: Fill them first, and skip them for later remittances unless details changed. UIN: RBI gives one 13-digit Unique Identification Number per foreign entity. If several Indian parties invest in it, each files separately but they all share that UIN. Group company guarantee: It uses up the group company’s own limit, and any fund-based exposure between it and the Indian entity is deducted from its net worth. Personal guarantee by a resident promoter: It counts towards the Indian entity’s limit. Loans: They need a written loan agreement with arm’s-length interest. AD bank’s role: It checks that you don’t breach the commitment limit on the actual date of the commitment. Presentation: Amounts are in actuals, currency codes follow SWIFT, dates are DD/MM/YYYY, and each page is signed, stamped and dated. Public disclosure: RBI may publish the information you furnish. ***** Disclaimer: This is an explanatory summary. For an actual filing, check the current RBI form, the FEM (Overseas Investment) Rules, Regulations and Directions, and your AD bank’s own requirements.
