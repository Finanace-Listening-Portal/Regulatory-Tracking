As of 9.14 AM, it was trading at 95.76, according to Bloomberg data.SI Reporter New Delhi 4 min read Last Updated : Oct 12 2026 | 10:03 AM IST @media (min-width:1100px) and (max-width:1280px) { .storymeta .meta-info { flex: 0 0 45%; } } Listen to This ArticleThe Indian rupee opened over 1 per cent higher on Monday after the central bank unveiled efforts over the weekend to curb dollar demand and provide support to the domestic unit, which has been among the worst-performing Asian currencies this year. The rupee opened at 95.73 against the greenback today compared with Friday's close of 96.76. As of 9.14 AM, it was trading at 95.76, according to Bloomberg data. The rupee has depreciated by over 7 per cent year-on-year and around 6 per cent since the West Asia conflict began on February 28, 2026, amid elevated crude oil prices, a strong dollar, high global bond yields and weak capital flows. The latest measures come as the currency faces renewed pressure near the 97-per-dollar mark. The RBI’s latest package is a clear near-term positive for the rupee, said Anindya Banerjee, Head of Commodity and Currency Research, Kotak Neo, as it tackles both a large recurring source of dollar demand and the tendency for expectations of rupee weakness to generate additional hedging demand, which can itself amplify depreciation. What are the measures announced by RBI?Among the measures unveiled by RBI to contain the rupee are a special oil window for daily dollar requirement of oil marketing companies (BPCL, HPCL and IOCL); this dollar demand therefore will not hit the interbank market. Banks are asked to park 20 per cent of notional rupee contract value (Foreign exchange risk Reserve i.e. FERR) with RBI for import contracts. Further, banks will pass on this cost to importers which makes hedging imports more expensive.
