Articles 3 min read Fresh loan rates rise 21 bps in H1 despite 125 bps repo cut; deposit rates diverge: RBI Bulletin The weighted average lending rate on fresh rupee loans rose 21 basis points in H1 FY27 despite the Reserve Bank of India’s cumulative 125 basis point repo rate reduction since February 2025, as strong credit demand outpaced deposit growth and increased funding pressure on banks. Over the entire easing cycle through September 2026, WALRs on fresh and outstanding rupee loans declined by 72 basis points and 91 basis points, respectively. Advt The RBI said transmission to lending rates moderated during H1 FY27, while transmission to deposit rates strengthened.On the deposit side, weighted average domestic term deposit rates (WADTDRs) of scheduled commercial banks moderated by 40 basis points for fresh deposits and 6 basis points for outstanding deposits during H1 FY27 till August, with the moderation led by bulk deposit rates.The RBI attributed the pressure on lending rates to strong credit demand and the widening gap between credit and deposit growth.“During H1 (till August), the WALR on fresh rupee loans increased by 21 bps, while it declined modestly by 3 bps for outstanding loans,” the central bank said. “During H1, the median 1-year marginal cost of funds-based lending rate (MCLR) rose by 21 bps.”The transmission across lending benchmarks has also remained uneven. The RBI said the share of external benchmark-linked loans in total outstanding floating-rate loans of scheduled commercial banks increased from 67.6 per cent at end-March 2026 to 68.2 per cent at end-June 2026.Consequently, the share of MCLR-linked loans declined from 30.2 per cent to 29.6 per cent during the period.According to the RBI, private sector banks have a higher share of EBLR-linked lending, while public sector banks have a significant share of MCLR-linked loans. Since MCLR and legacy lending rates are based on internal benchmarks and generally have longer reset periods, transmission to lending rates can be slower. Advt The RBI also said that private banks recorded higher transmission to WALRs than public sector banks, partly due to their higher share of EBLR-linked loans, while foreign banks recorded the maximum pass-through due to their larger EBLR-linked portfolio.On the deposit side, the central bank has also flagged the role of small savings rates in transmission. It said the relatively high rates on small savings instruments could limit transmission to banks’ deposit rates during an easing cycle by encouraging substitution away from bank deposits.The RBI said a large interest-rate differential in favour of small savings could lead to migration of deposits away from banks, potentially widening the credit-deposit gap in the banking system.
