Articles 3 min read ‘Expecting Bima Sugam To Bring More Uninsured People Into Insurance Is A Myth’: Nilesh Sathe Former IRDAI member Nilesh Sathe has questioned whether Bima Sugam can deliver the expansion in insurance coverage envisaged by the regulator, pointing to the limited adoption of similar online mutual fund marketplaces. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Oct 9, 2026 at 02:51 PM IST var news_msid = 134829019; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Bima Sugam alone is unlikely to bring a large number of uninsured Indians into the insurance ecosystem, former IRDAI member Nilesh Sathe said, calling the expectation that the proposed digital marketplace will automatically drive insurance penetration an “absolute myth”.Sathe questioned whether consumers, particularly those outside the digitally comfortable younger demographic, would move to an online insurance marketplace simply because it becomes available.“Who will buy online insurance from Bima Sugam? Most probably the youngsters in the age group 25 to 40. And youngsters don't have patience. If your Bima Sugam platform is not so robust, nobody will go to Bima Sugam. It is dynamic. Every new product which is launched by every insurance company has to be there. So it has to be dynamic,” he said. Advt Mutual fund experience offers a warningSathe drew a comparison with online mutual fund marketplaces, arguing that simply creating a digital platform does not guarantee that consumers will shift their purchasing behaviour.He said a similar marketplace for mutual funds was introduced by the Securities and Exchange Board of India in 2012, but online platforms still account for less than 8% of mutual fund purchases, according to his assessment.“What is the experience of mutual funds? Such a marketplace was introduced by SEBI in 2012. How many people are buying mutual funds from these platforms? Even today less than 8%. So expecting that as soon as the Bima Sugam platform is ready, more uninsured people will go to that platform and buy is a myth. Absolute myth,” Sathe asserted.Sathe said the success of Bima Sugam would depend heavily on the quality and robustness of the technology as well as its ability to keep pace with constantly changing insurance products and pricing.Cost savings need to reach customersSathe also questioned whether the proposed regulatory framework would actually deliver lower costs for policyholders.While the consultation paper talks about customer-centricity and efficiency, he said the framework does not explicitly require insurers to pass on savings from lower commissions and operating costs to customers through lower premiums.“The guidelines do not prescribe that insurance companies should proportionately reduce the premium rates. It is not mentioned in the guidelines. Then how do you say that it is customer-centric? The benefit of reduced cost should be passed on to the customer, but the guidelines are silent on that,” he added. Advt ‘Insurance for All by 2047’ needs more than regulationSathe was also sharply critical of the idea that the insurance industry alone can be held responsible for achieving the government's “Insurance for All by 2047” ambition.He said the government and IRDAI have to create the environment, roadmap and incentives required for deeper insurance penetration, particularly in rural and underserved markets.“It is not the responsibility of insurance companies. 2047 is a slogan and responsibility of the government and IRDAI. Creating a favourable climate is again a responsibility of the government and IRDAI. This is my blunt view on this,” he said. Advt Sathe argued that asking insurers to expand into rural areas while simultaneously reducing distribution economics creates a contradiction.“How can you say that it is the responsibility of insurance companies to go to rural areas and keep canvassing insurance when there is no commission or there are no agents?” he said.
