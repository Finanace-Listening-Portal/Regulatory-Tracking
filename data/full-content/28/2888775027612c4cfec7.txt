EconomyNomura Doubles RBI Rate Hike Forecast To 50 Bps Ahead Of December 4 Policy MeetEdited by: Priya RaghuvanshiUpdated: Nov 27, 2026, 18:09 ISTNomura expects the RBI to deliver a 50 bps rate hike in December, citing persistent food inflation, rising core prices and strong economic growth.Reserve Bank of India (Image Source: iStock)follow usfollow usThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) could deliver a sharper-than-anticipated interest rate hike at its December monetary policy review, with global brokerage Nomura now projecting a 50 basis point (bps) hike instead of its earlier estimate of 25 bps. The revised outlook comes amid persistent food price pressures, signs of rising underlying inflation and stronger-than-expected economic activity.If the projection materialises, the RBI would raise its benchmark repo rate to 6 per cent at its December 4 policy meeting. Nomura has assigned a 60 per cent probability to this outcome, while placing the likelihood of a smaller 25 bps increase at 40 per cent. The latter remains the prevailing market expectation.“We are changing our RBI call to a 50bp hike to a 6.00 per cent repo rate on 4 December, from our previous forecast of a 25bp hike. We assign a 60 per cent probability to a 50bp hike with a 5-1 vote split, and the remaining 40 per cent to a 25bp hike,” the report said.Nomura's revised assessment is largely linked to the possibility that retail inflation will remain above the RBI's comfort threshold for an extended period. The brokerage estimates that India's Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation could climb to 6.4 per cent in November, compared with 6.1 per cent recorded in October.Higher prices of cereals, pulses and other essential food products are expected to account for much of this increase. For the October-December quarter, Nomura projects average retail inflation of 6.3 per cent, exceeding the central bank's estimate of 6 per cent.The brokerage also flagged the possibility that inflationary pressures could become more widespread. Rising production and input expenses are reportedly being passed on to consumers by companies, indicating that price increases may no longer be confined to food commodities."Monetary policy should not respond to supply-driven food prices," the report said, adding that the combination of rising underlying inflation and strong economic growth could shift the balance in favour of an earlier and larger rate increase.Strong Economic Growth Adds To Rate Hike ExpectationsEconomic performance is another factor influencing Nomura's revised interest rate outlook. The brokerage expects India's GDP growth for the July-September quarter to surpass 7.5 per cent. The official figures are scheduled for release on November 30, while the RBI's existing growth projection stands at 7.2 per cent.The stronger growth outlook could also lead the central bank to revise its annual economic projections. Nomura anticipates that the RBI may increase its FY27 GDP growth estimate to approximately 7.5 per cent from the current 7.1 per cent.On the inflation front, the brokerage expects the central bank to raise its full-year retail inflation forecast for FY27 marginally to 5.3 per cent from 5.2 per cent.RBI May Keep Repo Rate at 6% After December MoveDespite anticipating a larger increase in December, Nomura does not currently expect the RBI to continue raising interest rates at every subsequent policy review.Its baseline assessment is that the repo rate will reach 6 per cent following the December decision and remain unchanged thereafter. This outlook is based on the assumption that inflation and economic growth pressures will begin to ease after the first quarter.Nevertheless, the brokerage has not ruled out another round of monetary tightening. It identified February and April as possible occasions for an additional 25 bps increase if economic conditions warrant further intervention.Apart from the benchmark interest rate, Nomura has outlined potential measures the RBI could consider to manage liquidity within the banking system.One possibility involves reducing the minimum daily Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) maintenance requirement for banks from the existing 99 per cent to 90 per cent.
