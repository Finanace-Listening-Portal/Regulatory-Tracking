The 10-year Indian G-sec yield is expected to remain in the 7.0-7.2 per cent range for the rest of FY27, as higher US yields, elevated crude and RBI liquidity absorption limit the scope for a meaningful decline in domestic bond yields, according to Motilal Oswal Financial Services.The US 10-year yield briefly crossed 5 per cent, while India's 10-year G-sec yield reached 7.05 per cent on September 17. Brent crude, meanwhile, eased to around USD103 a barrel from above USD108-110 earlier in the week but remained elevated, posing risks to inflation and the external balance. Advt August CPI remains manageable, but food inflation is close to 6 per cent and WPI inflation is near 10 per cent. The report expects the combination of elevated food and energy inflation to push CPI above 6 per cent in the third quarter of FY27, beyond the RBI's upper tolerance threshold. The FY27 CPI forecast has been retained at 5.1 per cent, slightly above the RBI's 5.0 per cent forecast, with risks tilted upwards.The RBI is already undertaking sizeable liquidity absorption through variable rate reverse repo operations and around Rs 1 lakh crore of open market sales. This suggests financial conditions could tighten even before a formal repo-rate hiking cycle, with liquidity absorption, higher bond yields and tighter global financial conditions reinforcing each other.October hike imminent?An October rate hike is considered a meaningful possibility if crude prices remain elevated and inflation expectations rise. Under a sustained oil-shock scenario, cumulative rate hikes of 75-100 basis points are possible in the current cycle, as the central bank may move pre-emptively rather than wait for the full impact of higher oil prices to appear in inflation data.Banks should initially be relatively better positioned than NBFCs as floating-rate assets reprice and stronger deposit franchises provide some protection against higher funding costs. NBFCs face greater exposure to wholesale funding and refinancing conditions, while real estate, autos, consumer durables and highly leveraged businesses are more sensitive to higher rates. Rural consumption could also face pressure from higher food inflation and weaker purchasing power. Advt Export-oriented IT and pharmaceutical companies could get some support from a weaker rupee, although IT companies remain exposed to slower global technology spending. More broadly, the shift from exceptionally cheap and abundant capital towards higher financing costs makes balance-sheet strength, refinancing requirements and pricing power increasingly important for businesses.
