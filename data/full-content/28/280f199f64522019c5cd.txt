Under the current GST returns framework, taxpayers are required to furnish details of their outward supplies in FORM GSTR-1. Based on such details, a statement of input tax credit (ITC) is made available to the recipients in FORM GSTR-2B for availment of input tax credit. To aid furnishing of return in FORM GSTR-3B of a tax period, tax liability of a supplier is being auto-populated on the portal based on the details furnished in his FORM GSTR-1, and available input tax credit is being auto- populated in his FORM GSTR-3B on the portal based on his FORM GSTR-2B. Currently, taxpayers can edit such auto-populated tax liability and available input tax credit in FORM GSTR-3B, before filing the same. Such editing may result in discrepancies between: a. tax liability reported in FORM GSTR-1 and tax paid in FORM GSTR-3B; and b. ITC available in FORM GSTR-2B and ITC availed in FORM GSTR-3B. 2. These discrepancies often lead to issuance of demand notices by tax officers and system generated intimations to taxpayers. In view of reducing the mismatches between the tax liability reported in FORM GSTR-1 and the tax paid in FORM GSTR- 3B, and between the ITC available in FORM GSTR-2B and the ITC availed in FORM GSTR-3B, various facilities have been introduced on the portal in the past. These facilities enable correct reporting of tax liability and ITC details in FORM GSTR-3B, including correction of such details, wherever required, in the source statements i.e. FORM GSTR-1 and FORM GSTR-2B, rather than direct editing in FORM GSTR-3B. These facilities include: a. FORM GSTR-1A, to enable taxpayers to amend their FORM GSTR-1 details before filing FORM GSTR-3B; b. Invoice Management System (IMS), to facilitate reconciliation of ITC available in FORM GSTR-2B with the ITC availed in FORM GSTR-3B; c. Electronic Credit Reversal and Re-claimed Statement, to facilitate accurate reporting of reversal and re-claim of ITC; and d. RCM Ledger/ITC Statement, to facilitate accurate reporting of ITC availment in respect of supplies liable to reverse charge www.taxguru.in Page 3 of 93 3. The above-mentioned facilities though made available on the portal, have, except for FORM GSTR-1A, not been notified in CGST Rules, 2017. It is seen that some taxpayers are either not using or not properly using these facilities and continue to manually edit auto-populated details in FORM GSTR-3B, resulting in continued issuance of demand notices by tax officers and system generated intimations to taxpayers on account of mismatches in tax liability and ITC, causing inconvenience to taxpayers while also weakening the ITC chain integrity. 4. Accordingly, there is a need to further strengthen the return-filing framework, so as to provide an improved alternate mechanism of amendment of tax liability and ITC details in return in FORM GSTR-3B, which reconciles with the details of outward supplies furnished in FORM GSTR-1 and the details of available ITC in FORM GSTR- 2B, thereby minimizing mismatches. Such a framework will improve the accuracy of tax liability and ITC reporting, enhance compliance, and strengthen the foundation for the effective implementation of other process reforms recommended by the GST Council. GST Council recommendations 5. In view of the above, the GST Council in its 57 th meeting held on 8 th October, 2026, took note of the various scenarios which require the taxpayers to edit auto- populated values in FORM GSTR-3B and recommended an Alternate mechanism of amendment of tax liability and ITC details in return in FORM GSTR-3B, building on the existing facilities and recommending certain new facilities as follows: i. Enhancement of FORM GSTR-1/1A/IFF for better reconciliation with FORM GSTR-3B and other statements ● The absence of supply-type-wise granularity in FORM GSTR-1/1A/IFF may lead to incorrect auto-population of the corresponding tables in FORM GSTR-3B, requiring manual correction by taxpayers. For instance, credit notes, debit notes, advances received, and advances adjusted are not categorised by the nature of supply, such as regular outward supplies, supplies under section 9(5), or zero-rated supplies. As a result, they may be reported under the table for taxable outward supplies in FORM GSTR- 3B, instead of the appropriate relevant table. ● Further, the existing format of FORM GSTR-1 does not enable taxpayers to reconcile supplies made through electronic commerce operators with the corresponding details furnished by the ECOs in FORM GSTR-8 and made available on the common portal. www.taxguru.in Page 4 of 93 ● Accordingly, enhancements to FORM GSTR-1/1A/IFF have been recommended to enable identification of the nature of the underlying supply in respect of credit notes, advances, and similar documents. This would ensure correct auto-population of tax liability in FORM GSTR-3B and obviate the need for amending the auto-populated liability in FORM GSTR-3B. ● Further, to facilitate the taxpayers in furnishing details in FORM GSTR- 1/1A/IFF on the portal, user interface improvements, such as tool tips and guided navigational paths on the portal have been suggested. ii. Expanding the scope of rectifications to be made through FORM GSTR-1A ● At present, FORM GSTR-1A cannot be used to add or amend details relating to earlier tax periods, or to correct the GSTIN of the recipient. This restricts taxpayers from rectifying any error regarding the earlier tax period or relating to change in GSTIN of the recipient before filing FORM GSTR-3B. Accordingly, it has been recommended that this restriction may be removed. iii. Rectification of details furnished in FORM GSTR-3B of an earlier tax period ● It was observed that certain taxpayers rectify tax liability for outward supplies of an earlier tax period directly in FORM GSTR-3B, without correcting the underlying details in FORM GSTR-1/1A. Accordingly, it has been recommended that such rectification be made only through the amendment tables of FORM GSTR-1/1A, instead of amending the auto- populated liability details in FORM GSTR-3B, directly. However, rectification of reverse-charge tax liability may be made directly through adjustments in the FORM GSTR-3B of a subsequent tax period. iv. Reporting of details of outward supplies in FORM GSTR-1 where tax had been discharged through FORM GST DRC-03 ● Where any amount of tax has been paid through FORM GST DRC-03 in pursuance of a proceeding such as the audit, inspection, scrutiny or enforcement activity by the tax authorities, and the taxpayer intends to pass on the ITC in respect of the same to the recipient, then if he reports the invoice or debit note details in FORM GSTR-1/1A, the liability gets auto-populated in FORM GSTR-3B. As the taxpayer has already paid the tax liability through FORM GST DRC-03, he may be resorting to editing liability in FORM GSTR-3B, so as to avoid duplication of payment of said tax liability. www.taxguru.in Page 5 of 93 ● To prevent such duplication, it has been recommended that FORM GST DRC-03 be enhanced to enable the taxpayer to declare, within seven days of payment, the details of the underlying invoice(s) or debit note(s). In such cases, when the said invoice or debit note details are reported in FORM GSTR-1/1A, the corresponding tax liability for the said invoice or debit note will not be added to the auto-populated liability in FORM GSTR-3B, thus avoiding any duplication of payment of tax. The details of such invoice or debit note will also be made available in corresponding FORM GSTR-2B of the recipient for availment of ITC. v. Correct reporting of reverse charge liability in FORM GSTR-3B ● To facilitate the taxpayers in correct reporting of tax liability and input tax credit in respect of supplies liable to reverse charge, RCM Liability and ITC Statement was introduced on the portal from August 2024 period onwards for monthly filers and July-September 2024 period onwards for quarterly filers. A facility was also provided to report cumulative closing balance, if any, upto that period. ● To further streamline correct reporting of such tax liability and ITC, it has been recommended to notify the said statement in CGST Rules, 2017 and to require that ITC claimed on inward supplies under reverse charge (other than import of goods) in FORM GSTR-3B of a tax period should not exceed the tax liability under RCM declared in the said FORM GSTR-3B and the closing balance available in the said statement. ● Accordingly, an Electronic Statement of tax paid on Reverse charge basis and input tax credit claimed has been recommended in FORM GST PMT-02B. Also, amendment has been recommended in FORM GST PMT-04, so as to allow a taxpayer to report discrepancies, if any, in the said statement. vi. Mandating and clarifying the usage of Invoice Management System (IMS) for better reconciliation of input tax credit details in FORM GSTR-3B ● IMS has been available on the portal since October 2024. It allows recipients to accept, reject, or keep pending inward supply details communicated by supplier. The salient features of IMS are as follows:  The taxpayer may keep documents pending on IMS, in respect of which the conditions of eligibility of ITC under section 16(2) of www.taxguru.in Page 6 of 93 CGST Act are presently not fulfilled, but which may get fulfilled subsequently. Upon fulfillment of the requisite conditions, such documents may be subsequently accepted by the recipient on IMS.  The taxpayer may reject a document on IMS, if any discrepancy in the document communicated on IMS is noticed or where the document is found to not pertain to him.  If no action is taken on a document communicated on IMS for generation of FORM GSTR-2B of a tax period before filing of FORM GSTR-3B for the said tax period, the same is considered to have been accepted by the recipient on IMS.  ITC pertaining to accepted or deemed accepted documents is made available in FORM GSTR-2B and accordingly auto- populated in Table 4(A) of FORM GSTR-3B for the corresponding tax period. Conversely, the ITC pertaining to documents kept pending or rejected in IMS is not made available in FORM GSTR- 2B and accordingly not auto-populated in Table 4(A) of FORM GSTR-3B for the corresponding tax period.  An action taken on any document on IMS by the recipient can be revised any number of times upto the filing of his FORM GSTR- 3B for the tax period for which FORM GSTR-2B is being generated. Based on the revised action taken on IMS, FORM GSTR-2B can be also revised, which will also revise the auto- populated ITC in FORM GSTR-3B. ● These above-mentioned features of IMS improve the reconciliation between ITC available in FORM GSTR-2B and ITC availed in FORM GSTR-3B. ● With a view to facilitate ease of taking action on IMS, offline tool has been made available on GST Portal, to enable taxpayers to undertake actions on both individual as well as bulk invoices in an efficient manner. ● To reduce inadvertent rejections or premature acceptance of credit notes, it has been recommended to extend the period for which a credit note may be kept pending on IMS: www.taxguru.in Page 7 of 93 a) By Monthly filers: Until filing of FORM GSTR-3B for next-to-next month, to the month in which the credit note was communicated to the taxpayer, but not later than the end of the month in which the due date for filing of the return in FORM GSTR-3B for the said next to next month falls. Thereafter, the credit note will be deemed accepted. Example: Say a credit note was communicated by the supplier in his FORM GSTR-1 filed on 11 th May, 2026, for the month of April 2026, to the recipient “A”. The same shall reflect in the IMS of “A” for the month April 2026. This credit note can be kept pending on the IMS by “A” till the date of filing of FORM GSTR-3B for the month of June, 2026 (which is next to next month of April 2026). However, if FORM GSTR-3B of June 2026 is not filed by “A” upto 31 st July 2026 (which is the last date of the month in which the return for the month June 2026 was due), then the said credit note shall be deemed to have been accepted by “A” on IMS. b) By Quarterly filers: Until filing of FORM GSTR-3B for the next quarter after the quarter in which the credit note was communicated to the taxpayer, but not later than the end of the month in which the due date for filing of the return in FORM GSTR-3B for the said next quarter falls. Thereafter, the credit note will be deemed accepted. Example: Say a credit note was communicated by the supplier in his FORM GSTR-1 filed on 11 th May, 2026 for the month of April 2026, to a recipient “A”,. The same shall reflect in the IMS of “A” for the quarter April–June 2026. This credit note can be kept pending on the IMS by “A” till the date of filing of FORM GSTR-3B for the quarter of July–September 2026 (which is next quarter to April–June 2026). However, if FORM GSTR-3B of July–September 2026 is not filed by “A” upto 31 st October 2026 (which is the last date of the month in which the return for the quarter July–September 2026 was due), then the said credit note shall be deemed to have been accepted by “A” on IMS. ● It has further been recommended to provide an option for the suppliers to link credit notes and debit notes with the underlying invoices in FORM GSTR-1/1A/IFF, on a purely voluntary basis, to help recipients take informed decisions and to reduce the need of keeping credit notes pending or rejecting them on IMS. www.taxguru.in Page 8 of 93 ● Since acceptance, rejection, and keeping documents pending on IMS improve ITC reconciliation, the GST Council has recommended that management of available ITC in FORM GSTR-3B should be carried through IMS instead of directly amending auto-populated ITC in FORM GSTR-3B. GST Council has also recommended notifying relevant amendment in GST Rules and FORMS for the same purpose. ● The Council also recommended issuance of a circular to guide the taxpayers in correct manner of taking action on IMS. vii. Facilitating taxpayers in correct reporting of reversal and reclaim of ITC in FORM GSTR-3B: ● In order to facilitate the taxpayers in correct and accurate reporting of reversal and reclaim of input tax credit, a statement namely, Electronic Credit Reversal and Reclaim Statement was introduced on the GST portal from August 2023 return period onwards, with a facility to report their cumulative closing balance of input tax credit, if any, which had been reversed but not re-availed upto that time. ● To further streamline correct reporting, it has been recommended to notify the said statement in CGST Rules, 2017 and to require that reclaimed input tax credit in FORM GSTR-3B shall not exceed the balance available in the said statement. ● Accordingly, an Electronic Credit Reversal and Reclaim Statement has been recommended in FORM GST PMT-02A. Also, amendment has been recommended in FORM GST PMT-04, so as to allow a taxpayer to report discrepancies, if any, in the said statement. ● Further, amendment has been recommended in Table 4 of FORM GSTR- 3B to provide for a separate Table 4(AA) in FORM GSTR-3B for reporting reclaimed ITC, so as to obviate the need for amending the auto-populated ITC details in Table 4(A)(5) of FORM GSTR-3B. viii. Streamlining the manner of rectification of errors made in reporting input tax credit in FORM GSTR-3B ● After suppliers furnish outward supply details, recipients act on IMS to generate FORM GSTR-2B and are generally required to manually report www.taxguru.in Page 9 of 93 RCM ITC, ITC reversals, and ITC re-claims in FORM GSTR-3B. Such manual interventions are prone to errors and omissions. ● As the temporary reversals made in Table 4(B)(2) of FORM GSTR-3B are credited to the Electronic Credit Reversal and Re-claim Statement, any excess temporary reversal done in Table 4(B)(2) can be rectified by reclaiming the excess amount so reversed in a subsequent return. However, no such systematic mechanism exists to rectify any excess reversal of ITC, reversed permanently in Table 4(B)(1) of FORM GSTR- 3B. ● Accordingly, it has been recommended to provide a systemic mechanism, by way of a new form, FORM GST PMT-10 to undertake rectifications of such excess reversals of permanent nature reported in Table 4(B)(1) of FORM GSTR-3B of an earlier tax period, on the basis of self-assessment. ix. Enhancement in FORM GSTR-2B in respect of ITC distributed by ISD ● ITC distributed by an Input Service Distributor (ISD) as ineligible in FORM GSTR-6 does not currently flow as available ITC in FORM GSTR- 2B and is therefore not auto-populated in Table 4(A)(4) of FORM GSTR- 3B. ● Accordingly, enhancements have been recommended in FORM GSTR- 2B and FORM GSTR-3B, to provide that ITC distributed by an ISD as ineligible need not be reversed again by recipients in Table 4(B)(1) of FORM GSTR-3B as such ITC will be auto-populated as ineligible ITC in Table 4(D)(2) of FORM GSTR-3B. ● Currently, ITC attributable to an ineligible ISD credit note is reduced from eligible ITC on the portal. Since such a credit note relates to ineligible ITC, its impact should instead flow to the ineligible ITC section, i.e., Table 4(D)(2) of FORM GSTR-3B. ● Accordingly, enhancements have been recommended in FORM GSTR- 2B to ensure that ITC relating to ineligible credit notes distributed by an ISD is not adjusted against eligible ITC distributed by the ISD. 6. Besides the above, the GST Council also recommended issuance of a circular to clarify the correct manner of reporting ITC availment and its reversal in FORM GSTR- 3B, in the context of IMS, the Electronic Credit Reversal and Re-claimed Statement, and the Electronic Statement of tax paid on reverse charge basis and ITC claimed. www.taxguru.in Page 10 of 93 7. The Council recommended that, with the above proposed measures, there may not be any need for directly rectifying/amending the details of liability (auto- populated from FORM GSTR-1/1A/IFF) and details of ITC (auto-populated from FORM GSTR-2B) in FORM GSTR-3B. Thus, any amendment/correction of liability will be done through FORM GSTR-1/1A/IFF instead of directly amending auto-populated values in FORM GSTR-3B. Further, any management of ITC will be done through IMS, Electronic Credit Reversal and Re-claimed Statement, and the Electronic Statement of tax paid on reverse charge basis and ITC claimed, FORM GST PMT-04 and FORM GST PMT-10, instead of directly amending the auto-populated details in FORM GSTR-3B. To allow stakeholders sufficient time to align their systems and practices, the Council recommended that this proposed Alternate mechanism of amendment of tax liability and ITC details in return in FORM GSTR-3B, and corresponding amendments to the CGST Rules, 2017, may be brought into force from the return period of April, 2027. In this context, in order to ensure uniformity in the procedure for correct availment and reversal of ITC in Table 4 of FORM GSTR-3B by the registered person, the Board, in exercise of its powers conferred under sub- section (1) of section 168 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (hereinafter referred to as the “CGST Act”), hereby clarifies the correct manner of furnishing information pertaining to ITC and reversal thereof in return in FORM GSTR-3B, in the succeeding paragraphs: 2.1 Manner of taking action on IMS: 2.1.1 The invoice, credit note, debit note or any other record (hereinafter referred to as “document”) saved in the statement of outward supplies before filing of FORM GSTR-1/1A/IFF/5, or filed by the suppliers in their FORM GSTR-1/1A/IFF/5, and the bill of entry filed against import of goods, or the goods brought in the domestic Tariff Area from Special Economic Zone unit or a Special Economic Zone developer shall be communicated to the concerned recipient on IMS. 2.1.2 It is further mentioned that the documents on which ITC is not available to the recipient on account of (i) limitation of time period as delineated in sub-section (4) of section 16 of the CGST Act, (ii) cases where the recipient of an intra- State supply is located in a different State / UT than that of place of supply, and (iii) ineligible ITC distributed by ISD, shall not be communicated to the concerned recipient on IMS. 2.1.3 The recipient can either accept, or reject, or keep pending a document received on IMS. It is to note that if the document is a bill of entry, the only options available are either acceptance or keeping the bill of entry pending on IMS, and the option to reject the same on IMS is not available, as bill of entry is filed by the recipient himself. In case of change in GSTIN in bill of entry, an ITC reversal entry will be shown in IMS of the original taxpayer where he can choose the amount of ITC to be reversed. By default, the system will show ITC reversal of the complete original value of the bill of entry. Also, the original taxpayer will only have the option to accept such ITC reversal entry. 2.1.4 In cases where in respect of a document communicated on IMS, the recipient ascertains that the conditions of eligibility of ITC under section 16(2) of CGST Act, such as on account of section 16(2)(a) (not being in possession of the said document), section 16(2)(b) (non-receipt of supply) of the CGST Act etc., are presently www.taxguru.in Page 85 of 93 not fulfilled but the same may get fulfilled subsequently, he should keep such documents pending on IMS. Upon fulfillment of the requisite conditions, such documents may be subsequently accepted by the recipient on IMS. 2.1.5 It is to note that an invoice or debit note communicated on IMS can be kept pending on IMS only upto the time limit under section 16(4) of CGST Act or section 16(6) of CGST Act, after which it shall no longer be available in IMS for availment of ITC. 2.1.6 Further, a credit note or any other document which reduces tax liability of the supplier, communicated on IMS, can be kept pending on IMS upto the filing of FORM GSTR-3B of the second succeeding month or the immediately next quarter, as the case may be, depending on whether the recipient is a monthly or a quarterly FORM GSTR-3B filer. But, if such FORM GSTR-3B for the said second succeeding month or the immediately next quarter is not filed upto the end of the month in which the FORM GSTR-3B of the said second succeeding month or the immediately next quarter is due to be filed, then such credit note or the other document shall be deemed to have been accepted on the IMS. Besides, the recipient also has an option to reject a document communicated on IMS, if the said document has any discrepancy affecting the availment of ITC on the same. If the document in question is an invoice, debit note or any other document which increases the tax liability of the supplier, upon rejection, the ITC in respect of the document shall not be available in his FORM GSTR-2B. Further, if the document in question is a credit note or any other document which reduces the tax liability of the supplier, upon rejection, the tax liability attributable to the document shall be added back to the tax liability of the supplier. Therefore, the recipient should be careful in making any rejections. 2.1.12 If no action is taken on a document communicated on IMS for generation of FORM GSTR-2B of the said tax period before filing of FORM GSTR-3B for the tax period, the same shall be deemed to have been accepted by the recipient on IMS. 2.1.13 ITC pertaining to documents kept pending or rejected in IMS shall not be made available in FORM GSTR-2B and accordingly would not be auto-populated in Table 4(A) of FORM GSTR-3B for the corresponding tax period. 2.1.14 An action taken on any document on IMS by the recipient can be revised any number of times but only upto the filing of his FORM GSTR-3B for the tax period for which FORM GSTR-2B is being generated. Table 4(A)(1), Table 4(A)(4) and Table 4(A)(5) of FORM GSTR-3B of the said tax period. 2.2.2 In respect of Table 4(A)(2) and Table 4(A)(3) of FORM GSTR-3B, which pertain to ITC on import of services and ITC on inward supplies under reverse charge other than import of services, respectively, the recipient should self-assess and furnish details of available ITC in such Tables, subject to payment of tax. It is mentioned that the sum of amount of ITC to be furnished under the Table 4(A)(2) and Table 4(A)(3) of FORM GSTR-3B cannot be more than the sum of the self- assessed tax paid on inward supplies under reverse charge in Table 3.1(d) of the FORM GSTR-3B of the said tax period and the balance available in the “Electronic Statement of tax paid on Reverse charge basis and input tax credit claimed" of the said recipient. 2.3 Procedure for reporting details of ITC reversals in Table 4(B) of FORM GSTR-3B: 2.3.1 The total ITC available in Table 4(A) of FORM GSTR-3B may be subject to, - (i) reversals of permanent in nature, such as on account of rule 38 (reversal of credit by a banking company or a financial institution), rule 42 (reversal on input and input services on account of supply of exempted goods or services), rule 43 (reversal on www.taxguru.in Page 89 of 93 capital goods on account of supply of exempted goods or services) of the CGST Rules and in respect of ineligible ITC under section 17(5) of the CGST Act, or (ii) reversals of temporary in nature, i.e. where ITC may become eligible to be re- availed in accordance with the provisions of the Act or the provisions of Chapter V of CGST Rules, such as on account of rule 37 of CGST Rules (non-payment of consideration to supplier within 180 days) and rule 37A of CGST Rules (non- payment of tax by the supplier) etc. 2.3.2 The reversals which are permanent in nature, as detailed above, shall be reversed by the recipient on their own assessment, in Table 4 (B)(1) of FORM GSTR- 3B. 2.3.3 ITC which is not available on account of, (i) limitation of time period as delineated in sub-section (4) of section 16 of the CGST Act; (ii) where the recipient of an intra- State supply is located in a different State / UT than that of place of supply; and (iii) in respect of ineligible ITC distributed by ISD, is auto-populated in Table 4(D)(2) of FORM GSTR-3B on the portal and is not included by the portal in Table 4(A) of FORM GSTR-3B. Accordingly, such ITC is not required to be again reported as reversals in Table 4(B) of FORM GSTR-3B. 2.3.4 The reversals which are temporary in nature, as detailed above, shall be reversed by the recipient on their own assessment, in Table 4 (B)(2) of FORM GSTR- 3B. It is to again highlight that where in respect of a document communicated on IMS, the recipient ascertains that the conditions of eligibility of ITC under section 16(2) of CGST Act are presently not fulfilled but the same may get fulfilled subsequently, he should keep such documents pending on IMS, as already mentioned in para 2.1.4 above, rather than accepting it on IMS, having its effect auto-populated in Table 4(A) and thereafter reversing it under Table 4(B)(2) of FORM GSTR-3B. 2.4 Procedure for reporting details of ITC reclaim in Table 4(AA) of FORM GSTR-3B: If any ITC which had been temporarily reversed in Table 4(B)(2) of FORM GSTR-3B of an earlier tax period, becomes eligible to the recipient on fulfillment of specific conditions, such ITC may be reclaimed in Table 4(AA)*, subject to the balance available in the “Electronic Credit Reversal and Reclaim Statement”. * It is to note that till such time the Table 4(AA) of FORM GSTR-3B is made operational on the portal, taxpayers may continue to report reclaimed ITC in Table www.taxguru.in Page 90 of 93 4(A)(5) alongwith being reported in Table 4(D)(1) of FORM GSTR-3B, as per the clarification issued through circular No. 170/02/2022-GST dated 06.07.2022. 2.5 Procedure for availment of ITC into Electronic Credit ledger (hereinafter referred to as “ECL”): The net ITC Available will be calculated in Table 4(C) which is as per the formula ([4(A) + 4(AA)*] - [4(B)(1) + 4(B)(2)]) will be credited to the ECL of the registered person. To avoid such reduction in ITC, recipients sometimes resort to rejection of such credit notes in respect of which no ITC was availed on the underlying invoices which causes inconvenience to the concerned suppliers. In order to prevent such undue reduction of ITC, without having to reject the said credit note on IMS, a facility has been made available on the IMS, which allows the recipient to accept a credit note and declare the amount of ITC which the taxpayer would like to reduce in respect of a credit note. Therefore, the recipient may use the said facility in such scenarios while providing the details of the underlying invoice in the designated place to provide remarks against such credit notes on IMS. 2.6.2 Inadvertent rejection on IMS: In case of any inadvertent rejections of a document on IMS, where corresponding FORM GSTR-3B has also been filed by the recipient, the recipient will be required to request the corresponding supplier to report the same document in the respective amendment table of subsequent FORM GSTR- www.taxguru.in Page 91 of 93 1/IFF, accept the same in IMS so that correct ITC is auto-populated in the corresponding FORM GSTR-3B. Where such document is a credit note, the said action of reporting the same document shall upon acceptance of such a document by the recipient even out the tax liability of the supplier which got added back on rejection of credit note in the first instance. 2.6.3 Inadvertent acceptance on IMS: In case of any inadvertent acceptance of a document on IMS, where corresponding FORM GSTR-3B has also
