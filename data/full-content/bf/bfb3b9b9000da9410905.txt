Ltd. (Central Consumer Protection Authority) Summary: The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) examined complaints concerning Agoda Company Pte. Ltd., an online travel booking platform, following allegations of inaccessible customer care, automated responses, booking cancellations, non-refunds and inadequate grievance redressal. A preliminary inquiry into 289 complaints registered on the National Consumer Helpline raised concerns about the platform’s compliance with the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, particularly the disclosure of grievance officer contact details. Agoda maintained that it operated as an intermediary between travellers and third-party travel suppliers, provided customer assistance through multiple digital channels and had resolved 216 of the original 289 complaints. The company subsequently submitted further grievance-resolution details, introduced additional India-specific support arrangements and agreed to participate in the National Consumer Helpline Convergence Programme. The CCPA observed that Agoda had not initially displayed its grievance officer’s contact details prominently on its platform, contrary to the requirements of Rules 4(2) and 4(4) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020. Although Agoda subsequently introduced a direct grievance-redressal hyperlink, an India-specific customer support number and a dedicated local team, the Authority emphasised the importance of accessible consumer support and effective complaint resolution. Exercising its powers under Sections 20 and 21 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, the CCPA directed Agoda to continue prominently displaying a direct link to its grievance officer’s name, designation, India-accessible email address and dedicated phone number, and to maintain an active India-based nodal team for handling National Consumer Helpline complaints. A compliance report was required within 15 days of the order. No monetary penalty was imposed in the supplied order. Detailed Analysis Origin of proceedings: A complaint dated 10 July 2024 from the Forum for Asserting Service Users Rights (FASUR) highlighted difficulties faced by consumers using online travel aggregators, including inaccessible customer care, automated replies, booking failures and cancellations, and inadequate grievance officer information. The CCPA sought a response from Agoda and examined complaints on the National Consumer Helpline. Preliminary inquiry and investigation: The preliminary inquiry concerned 289 consumer complaints. The CCPA noted deficiencies in the prominent display of grievance officer details and directed a detailed investigation. The DG investigation reported concerns regarding accessibility of customer support, grievance details, booking cancellations and refund communications. Agoda’s defence: Agoda stated that it facilitates bookings by third-party travel suppliers rather than providing the underlying hotel or travel services. It described its Help Centre, customer service chat and WhatsApp assistance, challenged several findings of the investigation, and reported resolving 216 of the initial 289 complaints. It also reported that 234 of the 289 cases could be located and processed, and explained its complaint resolution measures. Corrective action: During the proceedings Agoda introduced a prominently accessible grievance officer hyperlink and India-specific contact arrangements, worked towards National Consumer Helpline Convergence Programme onboarding, and arranged a dedicated India-based point-of-contact team. Decision: The CCPA focused on accessibility and prominence of grievance officer contact information under Rules 4(2) and 4(4) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020. It directed Agoda to continue maintaining a direct grievance officer hyperlink on its primary landing page and app home screen, and an active India-based nodal team for National Consumer Helpline complaints. Compliance was required within 15 days. The order did not impose a monetary penalty. Before delving into the specifics of the case, it is pertinent to examine the relevant legal framework that governs such transactions. 20. Grievances in NCH highlights deficiency in services, booking cancelled but money not refunded, booking cancellation without prior notice which violates the rights of consumers as class. ‘Consumer rights’ as defined under Section 2(9) of the Act, 2019 includes: (i) The right to be informed about the quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard and price of goods, products or services, as the case may be, so as to protect the consumer against unfair trade practices (ii) the right to be assured, wherever possible, access to a variety of goods, products or services at competitive prices; (iii) the right to be heard and to be assured that consumer’s interests will receive due consideration at appropriate fora; (iv) The right to seek redressal against unfair trade practices or restrictive trade practices or unscrupulous exploitation of consumers. (v) The right to consumer awareness. 21. Section 2 (11) “deficiency” means any fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in the quality, nature and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law for the time being in force or has been undertaken to be performed by a person in pursuance of a contract or otherwise in relation to any service and includes— (i) any act of negligence or omission or commission by such person which causes loss or injury to the consumer; and (ii) deliberate withholding of relevant information by such person to the consumer. 22.Section 2 (1) defines “advertisement means any audio or visual publicity, representation, endorsement or pronouncement made by means of light, sound, smoke, gas, print, electronic media, internet or website and includes any notice, circular, label, wrapper, invoice or such other documents”. 23. Subsequently, Section 2 (28) lays down “misleading advertisement” in relation to any product or service, means an advertisement, which— (i) falsely describes such product or service; or (ii) gives a false guarantee to, or is likely to mislead the consumers as to the nature, substance, quantity or quality of such product or service; or (iii) conveys an express or implied representation which, if made by the manufacturer or seller or service provider thereof, would constitute an unfair trade practice; or (iv) deliberately conceals important information; 24. Section- 2(47) of the Act, 2019 defines Unfair Trade Practice means a trade practice which for the purpose of promoting the sale, use or supply of any goods or for the provision of any service, adopts any unfair method or unfair or deceptive practice by falsely represents that the services are of a particular standard, quality or grade; and makes a false or misleading representation concerning the need for, or the usefulness of, any goods or services; 25. Rule 4 of Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 lays down Duties of e-commerce entities: (2) Every e-commerce entity shall provide the following information in a clear and accessible manner on its platform, displayed prominently to its users, namely:– (i) legal name of the e-commerce entity; (ii) principal geographic address of its headquarters and all branches; (iii) name and details of its website; and (iv) contact details like e-mail address, fax, landline and mobile numbers of customer care as well as of grievance officer. (3) No e-commerce entity shall adopt any unfair trade practice, whether in the course of business on its platform or otherwise. (4)Every e-commerce entity shall establish an adequate grievance redressal mechanism having regard to the number of grievances ordinarily received by such entity from India, and shall appoint a grievance officer for consumer grievance redressal, and shall display the name, contact details, and designation of such officer on its platform. 26.The CCPA observed that at the time of issuance of notice the Opposite party had not prominently displayed the contact details of Grievance Officer in a clear and accessible manner on the website /app of the platform as per the provisions contained in Rule 4(2) and Rule 4(4) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020. 27.The CCPA noted that in respect of the grievances lodged on the National Consumer Helpline concerning arbitrary cancellations, non-refund of bookings, and denial of services by unverified properties, the Opposite Party took action pursuant to the CCPA’s notice by redressing the reported matters, thereby restoring consumer confidence and securing equitable outcomes for users. 28.The steps taken by the Opposite Party following the issuance of notice and during the proceedings were duly noted by the CCPA: (a) The Opposite Party updated its platform architecture to place a direct hyperlink to the Grievance Officer’s information prominently on its landing page, ensuring transparency and ease of access for Indian consumers.
