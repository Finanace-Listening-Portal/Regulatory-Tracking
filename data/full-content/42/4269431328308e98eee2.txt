(Karnataka High Court) Summary: The Karnataka High Court, in Sanjay Garg Vs Commercial Tax Officer & Anr., quashed an ex parte GST adjudication order passed under Section 74 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and the Karnataka Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, after finding that the taxpayer’s serious illness, surgery and prolonged medical treatment constituted bona fide circumstances explaining his failure to respond to the show-cause notice. The petitioner, proprietor of M/s. Pankaj Steel, challenged the show-cause notice dated 17 May 2025, its accompanying summaries, the adjudication order dated 29 September 2025 and a subsequent recovery notice dated 2 March 2026. The proceedings related to the tax period April 2018 to March 2019. The petitioner had not submitted a response to the show-cause notice, resulting in the passing of the impugned adjudication order without his participation. Before the High Court, the petitioner submitted that he had been diagnosed with a serious illness on 13 March 2025, had undergone surgery on 1 April 2025 and had remained under prolonged medical treatment thereafter. These circumstances preceded the issuance of the show-cause notice on 17 May 2025 and explained his inability to participate in the adjudication proceedings. The Court examined the medical circumstances placed on record and accepted that the petitioner’s non-participation was bona fide. It considered that the petitioner should receive another opportunity to respond to the notice and its summary instead of allowing the ex parte adjudication order to stand. Accordingly, Justice B. M. Shyam Prasad allowed the writ petition in part and quashed the adjudication order dated 29 September 2025 along with its summary, identified as Annexures B and B1. The petitioner was granted liberty to file his response to the show-cause notice dated 17 May 2025 and its summary before the first respondent on or before 3 December 2026, or within such further time as that authority might permit, without requiring any additional notice. The Court did not decide the substantive GST liability or the merits of the allegations underlying the notice. The decision illustrates that genuine medical circumstances supported by the record may justify judicial intervention against an ex parte GST adjudication order to restore an opportunity of hearing. The operative relief was confined to quashing the adjudication order and its summary and permitting a fresh response. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT The question examined is should this Court interfere with the impugned Adjudication Order dated 29.09.2025 under Section 74 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017/ Karnataka Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 [for short, ‘the Act’] and the even dated Summary thereof [Annexures-B and B1], to extend an opportunity to the petitioner to respond to the Show Cause Notice dated 17.05.2025 and the Summary thereof. The Show Cause Notice dated 17.05.2025 and the Summary thereof are produced as Annexures-A to A2. The petitioner has not responded to the Show Cause Notice, and this has resulted in the Adjudication Order. Ms. Adithi S., the learned counsel for the petitioner, and Ms. Jyoti M. Maradi, a learned High Court Government Pleader, who accepts notice for the respondents, are heard for the disposal of the petition. The petitioner’s case is that just before the Show Cause Notice dated 17.05.2025 he was diagnosed with terminal illness and had to undergo surgery and that he was under treatment for a long period thereafter. In fact, Ms. Adithi S., points out from the records that the petitioner was diagnosed with the terminal illness on 13.03.2025; that he underwent surgery on 01.04.2025 and that he continued to be under prolonged treatment. These circumstances show bonafides for non-participation and hence this Court opines that there must be another opportunity to the petitioner to respond to the Show Cause Notice and the Summary thereof. In the light of the afore, the following: ORDER [A] The petition is allowed-in-part. [B] The impugned Adjudication Order dated 29.09.2025 and the Summary thereof [Annexures-B and B1] are quashed. [C] The petitioner is reserved with liberty to file Response to the Show Cause Notice dated 17.05.2025 and the Summary thereof with the first respondent, without further notice, by 03.12.2026 or such further time as the first respondent may allow.
