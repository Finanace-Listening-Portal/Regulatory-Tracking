The premium reflects the price the Canadian investor was willing to offer for control of a lender that has substantially improved its asset quality and profitability, although the precise reasons behind Fairfax's valuation have not been publicly established.The Rs 75.99 figure was the average of two independent fair-value assessments, not a ceiling on the price bidders could offer. The government used a market-multiple method based on public-sector banks and an asset-valuation method, drawing on audited accounts as of March 31, 2026. Fairfax's offer exceeded both individual assessments. The Finance Ministry said the offer was reasonable under the valuation framework, according to its October 14 disclosures. Advt The distinction matters because the government had earlier set a reserve price of Rs 94.42 per share for the initial bidding round. The initial offers fell short of that threshold and were not approved. The sale process was subsequently reopened for best offers after IDBI Bank's share price had corrected. The stock closed at Rs 70.38 on May 18, the last trading day before invitations for revised offers, making Fairfax's Rs 81 bid a premium of about 15 per cent to the market price.Bank's turnaround keyThe bank's turnaround provides a possible explanation for its appeal to a long-term investor. Its gross non-performing asset ratio fell from nearly 32 per cent in September 2018 to about 2.3 per cent in June 2026, while profitability recovered. Its existing branch network and retail and institutional customer base also offer a platform for expansion. These factors could support a valuation based on future earnings potential, although the extent to which Fairfax factored them into its bid has not been disclosed.The transaction will give Fairfax control without complete ownership. Following the sale, the government will retain a 15 per cent stake and Life Insurance Corporation of India will hold 19 per cent. Both are expected to withdraw their board nominees and become public shareholders. The acquisition is subject to the applicable regulatory approvals.Fairfax's premium over the independent fair-value benchmark therefore does not, by itself, establish that the government undervalued the bank. The benchmark was used to assess the reasonableness of bids, while the winning offer reflected the price Fairfax was prepared to pay. The investment's eventual returns will depend on IDBI Bank's ability to sustain profitability, grow its business and maintain asset quality.
