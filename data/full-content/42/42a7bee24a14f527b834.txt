} } Listen to This ArticleThe rupee depreciated against the US dollar on Wednesday to emerge as the weakest currency among its Asian peers as the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI’s) monetary policy outcome did little to boost sentiment, said dealers. With the hike of 25 basis points (bps) already priced in, and the policy statement offering no additional guidance, the rupee’s movement was driven by external pressures and weak underlying dollar inflows. The local currency settled at its second-weakest close of 96.78 per dollar, lowest since May 20 (96.86) this year. Its previous close was 96.43 per dollar. During the day, the rupee fell up to 96.86 per dollar. “The market was tracking crude and sentiment has been down because of outflows. The hike was priced in, but the market was hoping for some guidance from the RBI,” said a dealer at a state-owned bank. At the post-policy press conference, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said markets can be irrational in the short run but will eventually find the right value. As of August, REER of the Indian Rupee (INR) stood at 88.92, against 88.69 in July. REER adjusts the nominal effective exchange rate (NEER) to account for inflation differentials between India and its major trading partners. A REER value above 100 indicates an appreciation of the rupee relative to the base year. The rupee has depreciated by 2.03 per cent in the current financial year of 2026-27 (FY27), whereas it has weakened by 5.99 per cent since the onset of the US-Iran conflict in February. “Implicit in this is our belief that interest rate hikes are not being prioritised to shore up the rupee. Instead, they are being used for lowering inflation (which, in turn, may have a positive impact on the rupee). We do believe that other steps to attract inflows, such as FDI and tax incentives, may come into play if pressure on the currency continues,” said HSBC in a report. Meanwhile, dollar/rupee forward premiums rose sharply as traders braced for more sell/buy foreign exchange swaps by the RBI after the central bank announced no fresh liquidity-withdrawal measures in its policy. The implied one-year dollar/rupee forward premium climbed 20 bps to 3.66 per cent, its highest in six months, after jumping 14 bps on Tuesday on likely sell/buy swaps by the RBI. In his monetary policy statement, Malhotra said India’s current account deficit (CAD) remained modest and well below sustainable levels in the first quarter (Q1) of FY27 despite external shocks. He said that going forward, moderation in global trade growth, elevated energy prices, and persistent trade policy uncertainties pose upside risks to India’s CAD in FY27. At the same time, buoyant services exports, robust remittances, and the implementation of the India-UK trade deal, as well as other bilateral agreements, are expected to provide resilience to the external sector. India’s CAD stood at 0.5 per cent of gross domestic product (GDP), or $4.2 billion, in Q1FY27, compared with 0.4 per cent of GDP a year ago. The services trade surplus stood at $85.8 billion during April-August, while net transfers, primarily comprising worker remittances, stood at $53.9 billion during April-July. Malhotra said the balance of payments (BoP) was expected to record a healthy surplus in FY27, while foreign exchange reserves remained adequate, with import cover of around 11 months.
