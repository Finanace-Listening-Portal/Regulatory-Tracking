Motilal Oswal maintains a Neutral stance on Muthoot Finance and Manappuram Finance.Gold loan financiers could see elevated stress in their loan portfolios over the next few quarters as borrowers adjust to the Reserve Bank of India's revised gold lending regulations, with the shift from bullet repayments to monthly interest servicing beginning to affect reported asset quality across the industry. The increase in Stage 2 gold loan assets reported by major lenders in the second quarter could partly reflect difficulties faced by customers in adapting to the new repayment structure rather than a deterioration in the underlying collateral.The increase in Stage 2 assets has been observed across major gold financing NBFCs, including Muthoot Finance, IIFL Finance, Bajaj Finance and Manappuram Finance. Some of these loans could migrate into Stage 3 over the next couple of quarters, increasing the possibility of interest reversals, collection expenses and gold auctions, even though the collateral backing these loans remains adequate, Motilal Oswal Financial Services said in a report. Advt The revised RBI gold lending regulations, which took effect in April 2026, have tightened requirements around loan-to-value (LTV) computation and repayment structures. In response, lenders have introduced shorter-tenure loans and products requiring monthly interest payments to manage LTV requirements while preserving customers' borrowing eligibility.Traditionally, gold loan borrowers have preferred bullet repayment structures, under which the principal and accumulated interest are repaid at maturity. The transition to monthly interest servicing has created a behavioural challenge for customers accustomed to more flexible repayment arrangements. Borrowers who fail to meet their monthly contractual obligations can face delinquency recognition even when the underlying gold collateral remains adequate.While gold loans generally benefit from strong collateral protection and historically limited ultimate credit losses, the migration of accounts into Stage 3 can trigger interest income reversals, higher collection intensity and potential gold auctions. These developments could increase earnings volatility and affect the timing of cash flows, even if ultimate credit losses remain manageable.The hurdlesThe regulatory transition could also create a trade-off between loan growth and profitability. Shorter-tenure products and monthly interest repayment structures may help lenders sustain customer eligibility and disbursement growth. However, encouraging borrowers to shift to these products could require lenders to offer lower interest rates, putting pressure on lending yields. Advt Competition is emerging as another structural challenge for established gold financiers. Several well-rated NBFCs are entering the segment, either organically or through acquisitions. While incumbent lenders retain significant distribution advantages, increased competition for customers could challenge the profitability historically generated by established players.New entrants may also be willing to accept lower standalone returns because gold financing improves their secured lending mix and portfolio diversification. This could put pressure on the return on assets of established gold loan financiers and result in structural compression in industry profitability. Advt The industry's ability to sustain growth and profitability will depend on how successfully lenders transition customers towards periodic repayment structures while managing delinquencies and pricing pressure. Stage 2 cure rates, migration into Stage 3 and gold auction activity will be important near-term indicators, while product mix, lending yields, AUM growth and the ability to offset pricing pressure through scale and operating leverage will determine medium-term profitability.Motilal Oswal Financial Services maintained a Neutral stance on gold financiers Muthoot Finance and Manappuram Finance, citing rising competition, the need to adapt to evolving regulatory requirements and volatility in gold prices. While the brokerage remains constructive on the industry's long-term growth opportunity, it expects the sector to transition towards structurally lower profitability.
