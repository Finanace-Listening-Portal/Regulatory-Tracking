Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Ringlets Tradex Pvt Ltd Vs ACIT (ITAT Delhi) Summary: The Delhi Bench G of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal allowed the appeal of Ringlets Tradex Pvt. Ltd. in ITA No. 5731/Del/2025 for AY 2016–17. A search and seizure operation at the premises of the Sukhija Group of restaurants on 22 October 2016 yielded records that the Assessing Officer attributed to Ringlets Tradex, a non-searched person. In the assessment order dated 25 December 2019 under Section 143(3) read with Sections 153A and 153C of the Income-tax Act, 1961, the AO added ₹58,72,472 as notional gross profit at 70% of alleged suppressed sales of ₹83,89,182 and ₹11,50,000 as an unexplained cash receipt under Section 68. The CIT(A), in the appellate order dated 22 July 2025 under Section 250, granted partial relief but sustained the ₹11,50,000 addition based on a ledger found in the Sukhija Group search, which the AO treated as recording a receipt from Devender Khurana. The assessee denied receiving the cash and challenged the authenticity of the compilation drawn from the seized records. It contended that no incriminating material supported the addition, that the AO had neither sought confirmation from Khurana nor called for information under Section 133(6), and that it had received no proper show-cause notice. Before the Tribunal, its counsel challenged the validity of the satisfaction note dated 26 September 2018 because the AO had recorded that the seized documents “belong to” the assessee. The Departmental Representative relied on the orders of the AO and CIT(A). The Tribunal distinguished the two tests introduced by the Finance Act, 2015 with effect from 1 June 2015. Section 153C(1)(a) retains the “belongs to” test for seized assets such as money, bullion or jewellery. For books and documents, Section 153C(1)(b) requires satisfaction that they “pertain to” the other person or contain information that “relates to” that person. Relying on Prashant Premchand Bafana Vs ACIT, the Tribunal held that the AO’s operative satisfaction on ownership did not satisfy the statutory test for seized documents, even though the AO had recorded that the documents had a bearing on the assessee’s total income. The Tribunal held that the defective satisfaction note invalidated the AO’s assumption of jurisdiction. It quashed the notice and assessment order under Section 153C and allowed the appeal on that ground. It did not separately adjudicate the merits of the disputed cash receipt. The order records pronouncement on 24 August 2026 and carries 8 October 2026 below the signatures. Cases Discussed Prashant Premchand Bafana Vs Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax, Central Circle-1(1), Pune — [TS-462-ITAT-2025(PUN)], IT(SS)A Nos. 119 and 120/PUN/2022, AYs 2017–18 and 2018–19, decided on 8 April 2025 (ITAT Pune). The Delhi Bench relied on the Third Member’s conclusion that proceedings initiated without the satisfaction required by Section 153C(1)(b) were void ab initio. From the perusal of the above satisfaction note, the admitted fact in the instant case that emerges, is that the proceedings u/s 153C flowed from the search conducted on 22.10.2016 on Sukhija Group of cases and the assessee is a non-searched person. Another uncontroverted fact is that the AO of the assessee, the non-searched person, has recorded the satisfaction note on 26.09.2018 wherein he has recorded that the documents found and seized “belong to” the assessee, M/s Ringlets Tradex Pvt Ltd and have a bearing on the determination of total income of the assessee for the relevant assessment years. The question for determination is whether such satisfaction note enables the AO to assume a valid jurisdiction u/s 153C of the Act. 10. Prior to the amendment by Finance Act, 2015 in section 153C of the Act, strict threshold of “belong to” governed the validity of assumption of jurisdiction under Section 153C for both valuable assets and documents sized which led the Courts to hold that a document merely mentioning a third party’s name did not mean it belonged to them as concrete proof of ownership or property was mandatory. For searches conducted after June 1, 2015, the Finance Act, 2015 has significantly modified the language of Section 153C of the Income Tax Act to distinguish between physical assets and documentary evidence. For the Valuable Assets such as “money, bullion, or jewellery”, the qualifying expression “belongs to” was retained u/s 153C(1)(a) of the Act. Whereas for evidence such as “Books of Account/Documents”, the scope was widened by substituting “belongs to” in section 153C(1)(b) of the Act with the requirements that they “pertain or pertain to” or that any information contained therein “relates to” the other person. Legal interpretation of the phrase “Belong to” denotes that the assets are the absolute property of the non-searched entity whereas “Pertain to” or “relates to” denotes a functional nexus, subject matter link, or contextual reference to the entity’s transactions and the statute does not recognize that both the phrases are interchangeable. Thus the jurisdictional dictate, after 2015, of using “Belongs to” for seized documents, is that it does not accurately deploy the new statutory text. The judicial precedence, after 01.06.2015, requires that the satisfaction note under Section 153C must echo the specific statutory language assigned to the type of material seized. After June 1, 2015, where the satisfaction note solely records, u/s 153C(1)(b), that seized books/documents “belong to” a third party, it would lead to an invalid or defective assumption of jurisdiction. The Assessing Officer must explicitly record that the books of account or documents “pertain to” or contain information that “relates to” the third party, rather than relying on the pre-amendment “belongs to” test. The aforesaid distinction was clearly brought out in the case of Prashant Premchand Bafna V ACIT in ITA(SS)A 11 and 12/Pun/2022 for AY 2017-18 and 2018- 19 dated 08.04.2025, wherein the third Member held that where the requisite satisfaction was not recorded in terms of clause (b) of 153C(1), the initiation of proceedings u/s 153C is not in accordance with law and is void ab initio. 11. In such facts and circumstances, in the instant case, we find that the AO has recorded satisfaction based on the incorrect premise that seized documents “belong to” the assessee, a third party. The failure to state that the documents seized, “pertained to” or “related to”, as mandated in section 153C(1)(b), has rendered the aforesaid satisfaction note dated 26.09.2018 fundamentally vitiated. Consequently, we hold the said satisfaction note is legally defective rendering the assumption of jurisdiction under Section 153C unsustainable in the eyes of law. We, accordingly, quash the notice u/s 153C as well as order under section 153C of the Act. The appeal is allowed in aforesaid terms. 12. In the result, appeal filed by the assessee in ITA No.5731/Del/2025 is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 24th Aug 2026.
