The immediate lending impact will depend less on the 0.4% charge itself and more on whether merchants continue using UPI at broadly similar levels or change their payment behaviour in response to the additional cost.From October 15, a 0.4% MDR will apply to specified person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs2,000, with the charge capped at Rs300 for transactions of Rs75,000 and above. Person-to-person payments will remain free, while P2M transactions up to Rs2,000 and payments received by eligible small merchants under the zero-MDR framework will also remain free. The government estimates that around 96% of P2M transactions will remain unaffected. Advt The new charge is therefore concentrated on a relatively small portion of merchant transactions, but those transactions can be important for businesses that receive higher-value digital payments. A Rs 10,000 eligible transaction would attract Rs 40 of MDR, while a Rs 50,000 transaction would attract Rs 200. The charge reaches the Rs 300 cap at Rs 75,000. Transactions in specified essential and thin-margin sectors, including fuel, insurance, telecom and agricultural inputs, have a separate flat Rs 5 MDR for transactions above Rs 2,000.For lenders, the significance of the change is the growing use of UPI data as an indicator of business activity. State Bank of India is developing a lending solution that uses UPI transaction data as a proxy for sales to extend credit to small businesses without GST registration. The bank is seeking to build a profile of such businesses from their digital revenue and spending patterns, with UPI providing a digital trail where conventional financial information may be limited.If businesses continue using UPI at broadly similar levels and absorb the additional payment cost, the effect on the transaction data available to lenders could be limited. If merchants alter their payment behaviour, lenders could see changes in the digital sales patterns on which cash-flow-based lending models rely.Merchant behaviour keyThat makes merchant behaviour a key variable for lenders once the MDR comes into effect. A merchant could continue accepting UPI and absorb the cost within its existing margins. Alternatively, some businesses could seek to alter the mix of payment methods or the way higher-value transactions are collected. Any such shift would matter for lenders that use transaction histories to assess sales, determine borrowing capacity or monitor cash flows. Advt The impact would also differ across businesses because the same MDR rate can have different implications for merchants depending on their margins and transaction profile. A merchant with a higher proportion of transactions above Rs2,000 will have greater exposure to the new charge than one whose UPI receipts are predominantly below the threshold. Businesses operating on thinner margins could also have less capacity to absorb the additional payment cost.This does not mean that the MDR will automatically reduce merchants' cash flows or weaken lenders' underwriting models. The relevant measure for lenders will be whether the digital transaction trail changes relative to the underlying business activity. If a merchant's UPI receipts decline because customers shift to other payment methods, the digital data could show a weaker sales pattern even if the merchant's overall business remains broadly unchanged. Advt The distinction is particularly relevant as banks move towards cash-flow-based lending for smaller businesses. SBI has said its objective is to use digital transaction data to extend automated lending to businesses outside the GST system, where UPI can provide visibility into sales that may otherwise be difficult for a lender to verify.The new MDR also does not, by itself, imply that lenders will reprice loans. Any change in lending rates, credit limits or underwriting criteria would depend on how individual lenders assess the effect of the new cost on borrowers' cash flows and repayment capacity. There is currently no basis to treat loan repricing as an automatic consequence of the MDR framework. Advt Similarly, UPI-based automated repayments are already an established mechanism. NPCI's UPI AutoPay facility supports recurring payments including EMIs and loan payments, while its enhanced framework allows users to view active mandates and port them between UPI applications. The MDR framework does not itself mandate or establish a shift towards greater use of AutoPay for loan repayments.For lenders, the more immediate indicators to watch after the MDR takes effect will therefore be merchant UPI volumes, average transaction values, changes in payment-method preferences and the stability of digital sales patterns. These will show whether the new payment cost remains a marginal expense for merchants or begins to change the transaction data on which cash-flow-based lending increasingly depends.
