Banking system remains strong, but trade deficit widens Malhotra said the banking sector continues to remain strong, with scheduled commercial banks showing healthy capital adequacy, liquidity, asset quality and profitability. He added that the financial health of non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) also remained sound. On the external sector, Malhotra said India’s current account deficit (CAD) remained modest in the first quarter despite some pressures and was well below sustainable levels. "Notwithstanding robust services trade surplus and net remittance receipts, CAD widened in July due to the higher merchandise paid deficit," he said. India’s merchandise trade deficit rose to $58.7 billion in July and August, compared with $55.1 billion during the same period last year. "This is mainly driven by imports of electronic goods and crude oil. Going forward, moderation in global trade growth, elevated energy prices, and persistent trade policy uncertainties pose upside risks to India's current account deficit this year," Malhotra said during the MPC statement.
