The new framework requires customers to record which optional covers they want, while insurers must disclose the premium charged for each component separately.The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) has directed general insurers to adopt the new structure for private cars and two-wheelers, following a Supreme Court order in the case of National Insurance Company Ltd versus Thungala Dhana Laxmi and others. The framework, set out in a November 2 circular, prescribes standardised policy wordings, a customer option form and a mandatory Customer Information Sheet (CIS). It does not make all four sections compulsory. Third-party liability remains mandatory, while personal accident cover for the owner-insured is mandatory subject to applicable regulatory requirements. Legal liability towards occupants, additional personal accident protection and own-damage cover are optional.What are the four components? Advt The first component is third-party liability insurance, which is compulsory under motor vehicle law. It covers the vehicle owner's legal liability arising from death, bodily injury or property damage caused to a third party in an accident. A vehicle cannot be legally driven without this insurance. However, third-party cover does not pay for damage to the insured vehicle. Nor should vehicle owners assume that it automatically provides the separate legal liability protection towards passengers under the new structure.The second component is legal liability towards occupants or pillion riders, including employees travelling in the insured vehicle. This cover is optional and can be purchased by paying an additional premium. It protects the insured against legal liability towards covered occupants in the event of injury or death. The number of occupants covered cannot exceed the vehicle's registered seating capacity. The standard wording excludes the owner-insured and immediate family members, including parents, spouse and children. Buyers should therefore examine the policy wording rather than assume that every person travelling in the vehicle is covered under this section.The third component is personal accident protection. It includes mandatory personal accident cover for the owner-insured, subject to applicable requirements and exemptions under the existing rules. Additional personal accident protection for other occupants, pillion riders and drivers other than the owner-driver is optional. This is different from occupant liability cover. Personal accident protection provides specified benefits to the insured person in the event of accidental death or disability, subject to the policy terms. Occupant liability cover, by contrast, addresses the vehicle owner's legal liability towards covered passengers. Buying one does not automatically mean that the other has been purchased. Advt The fourth component is own-damage cover, which pays for loss of or damage to the insured vehicle, subject to the policy's terms, exclusions and deductibles. It remains optional. A customer who buys only compulsory third-party insurance will not receive compensation under the policy for damage to their own car or two-wheeler. Own-damage protection is relevant where the vehicle is expensive to repair or replace, or where the owner cannot readily bear the cost of a major repair. Insurers will retain flexibility to design and price these products according to market conditions.Will choosing fewer covers reduce the premium? Advt Separating the components and disclosing their premiums should allow customers to see how much they are paying for each type of protection. A buyer can compare the cost of occupant liability, additional personal accident protection and own-damage cover rather than assessing only the total premium.However, the extent of any saving from declining optional covers will depend on the insurer's pricing. IRDAI has not prescribed a uniform premium for all four components. Insurers retain flexibility to price own-damage cover and introduce additional coverage options, subject to the regulatory framework. Customers should therefore compare the premium saved with the financial exposure they are accepting by declining a cover. There is no basis to assume that the new structure will necessarily make motor insurance cheaper. Advt For example, a car owner who opts for third-party insurance but declines own-damage cover will have to bear the cost of repairing their own vehicle after an accident. Similarly, declining occupant liability protection could leave the owner having to meet the financial consequences of legal liability towards covered passengers, rather than relying on the insurer for indemnification under this section. This does not automatically mean that passengers have no personal accident protection: that is a separate cover which can be selected independently.What will change when buying or renewing a policy?Insurers will have to provide a customer option form setting out the four sections and record which covers the buyer has selected or declined. The form must be completed online or offline and will form part of the policy. Insurers must also issue a Customer Information Sheet at the point of sale explaining the coverage under each section. The policy schedule must separately disclose the actual coverage and premium charged for every component.This creates a documentary record of the customer's choices. It should help reduce disputes over whether a particular passenger, driver or vehicle-related risk was insured. Customers should check that the selected covers are correctly reflected in the policy schedule and that the option form matches their instructions. They should also read exclusions, deductibles and conditions rather than rely only on the premium breakup.Can existing policyholders add passenger cover?Yes. Customers who already have third-party motor insurance will be able to add legal liability cover for occupants or pillion riders during the policy period by paying an additional premium. They will not have to cancel their existing third-party policy. However, the additional cover must be obtained from the insurer that issued the third-party policy. It will take effect only from the date and time specified in the endorsement issued by the insurer, and will not retrospectively cover an accident that occurred before the protection became effective.The four-section structure and standard policy wordings will apply to policies issued from December 15. IRDAI has also directed insurers to integrate the framework with Bima Sugam by that date, so that the policies are available through the digital marketplace from the date of implementation.The practical test will be whether the new disclosures help customers distinguish between compulsory insurance and additional protection, and make choices based on the risks they can afford to retain rather than simply selecting the lowest premium.
