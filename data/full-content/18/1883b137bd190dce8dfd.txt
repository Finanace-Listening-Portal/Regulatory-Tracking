Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Ranjan Kare Vs State of Chhattisgarh (Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court considered the Special Leave Petition filed by Ranjan Kare challenging the order dated 18 July 2022 passed by the Chhattisgarh High Court at Bilaspur in MCRC No. 3624 of 2022, whereby his application for regular bail had been rejected. The proceedings arose from FIR No. 45 of 2021 dated 5 February 2021, registered at Police Station Saraipali, District Mahasamund, Chhattisgarh, for alleged offences punishable under Section 21 of the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985, and Section 25 of the Arms Act, 1959. According to the prosecution, the accused persons were allegedly found in unauthorised possession of cough syrup containing codeine phosphate, alprazolam tablets and Spas-Trancan Plus tablets containing tramadol hydrochloride. The prosecution also alleged recovery of an automatic knife and an air gun. These allegations formed the basis of the criminal proceedings and the successive applications seeking release on bail. Read HC Judgment in this case: Chhattisgarh HC Rejects Third NDPS Bail Plea Despite Trial Delay The proceedings before the High Court involved the petitioner’s third bail application. His first application had been dismissed as withdrawn on 1 July 2021, while the second application, MCrC No. 6105 of 2021, was rejected on merits on 27 October 2021. The applicants contended that they had been falsely implicated and had not committed the alleged offences. Their counsel submitted that they had remained in judicial custody since 4 February 2021 and that only three prosecution witnesses had been examined by the time the third application was considered. Relying principally on the delay in recording evidence and the continued deprivation of personal liberty, the applicants sought bail under Section 439 of the Code of Criminal Procedure. The State opposed the application. The High Court examined the case diary and the submissions advanced by both sides before determining whether the circumstances justified reconsideration of bail. The Chhattisgarh High Court declined to grant bail, observing that the second application had already been considered and rejected on merits. In particular, the earlier rejection had proceeded on the applicability of the stringent restrictions contained in Section 37 of the NDPS Act in cases involving commercial quantities of contraband substances. The High Court considered that the renewed application did not warrant a different conclusion and rejected the application under Section 439 CrPC. Although the applicants relied on prolonged custody and the examination of only three witnesses, the High Court did not regard these circumstances as sufficient to justify release in view of its earlier assessment of the statutory bail restrictions. The petitioner consequently approached the Supreme Court, challenging the High Court’s order dated 18 July 2022 and seeking relief from continued incarceration. Before the Supreme Court, the decisive circumstances were the petitioner’s prolonged detention and the absence of criminal antecedents. After considering the matter, the Court specifically recorded that the petitioner had been in custody for more than four years and had no previous criminal record. Taking these factors into account, the Supreme Court concluded that the petitioner should be released on bail, subject to such terms and conditions as the Trial Court might impose. The Court accordingly granted bail and disposed of the Special Leave Petition along with any pending applications. The decision illustrates that prolonged incarceration exceeding four years, coupled with the absence of criminal antecedents, may constitute significant grounds for granting conditional bail in an NDPS prosecution despite an earlier rejection by the High Court. The order does not contain an extended interpretation of Section 37 of the NDPS Act or determine the petitioner’s guilt or innocence; its operative direction is confined to granting bail on conditions fixed by the Trial Court. Cases Discussed: No separately identified judicial precedents are cited or discussed in the supplied Chhattisgarh High Court order or the subsequent Supreme Court order. The High Court refers to the earlier bail application, MCrC No. 6105 of 2021, rejected on 27 October 2021, as part of the procedural history of the same prosecution. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER The petitioner herein filed the captioned Special Leave Petition challenging the order dated 18.07.2022 passed by the High Court of Chhattisgarh at Bilaspur in MCRC No. 3624 of 2022 rejecting his application for bail, in connection with F.I.R.No. 45 of 2021, dated 05.02.2021, registered at Police Station SaraiPali, District Mahasamund, for the offences punishable under Section 21 of the NDPS Act, 1985 and Section 25 of the Arms Act, 1959. 2. Considering the fact that the petitioner has no criminal antecedents and has been in custody for more than four years, we are of the considered view that the petitioner be released on bail, subject to the terms and conditions to be imposed by the Trial Court. Ordered accordingly. 3. The Special Leave Petition is disposed of. 4. Pending application(s), if any, shall also stand disposed of.
