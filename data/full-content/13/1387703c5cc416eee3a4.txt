Summary : Punjab National Bank provides a foreign outward-remittance facility through internet banking and the PNB One mobile channel for eligible resident-individual customers. The bank is the executing Indian banking institution; this is not an offshore leveraged-forex platform and it does not authorise a resident to trade foreign exchange abroad. PNB’s published service material limits the digital route to specified purposes, twelve currencies and customer-level eligibility conditions. The selected account must be KYC-compliant and at least one year old, with PAN, mobile number and email recorded. The digital channel presently states a USD 10,000 equivalent single-transaction limit and USD 100,000 equivalent annual channel limit, both within the overall RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme ceiling of USD 250,000 per financial year. Larger or other permitted requests may require branch processing and additional documents. Costs include the bank’s stated service charge, SWIFT charge, the exchange-rate conversion, possible correspondent-bank deductions, GST and applicable tax collected at source. The final exchange rate is the rate applied when the bank confirms the transaction, not necessarily the indicative screen rate. This profile explains legal identity, permitted scope, documents, process, settlement dependencies, rejection grounds, grievance routes and current tax treatment without calling PNB the cheapest or safest provider. RBI’s current authorisation framework permits Authorised Dealer Category I banks to undertake permissible current-account transactions and permitted capital-account transactions. PNB’s own forex pages identify designated authorised-dealer branches, Trade Finance Centres and the International Banking Division that process foreign-exchange business. A customer should not assume that every branch handles every transaction; the relevant digital workflow, designated branch or Trade Finance Centre must accept the purpose and documents. The bank’s digital outward-remittance service is a customer-facing execution channel. PNB converts INR, routes the foreign currency through its correspondent network and debits the customer’s account for the remittance, charges and taxes. A beneficiary or overseas bank is not PNB’s agent merely because it receives funds. Intermediary and correspondent banks may apply their own deductions or compliance holds, so the amount credited and delivery time can differ from the remitter’s initial expectation. The selected account must be at least one year old and KYC-compliant. PAN, mobile number and email must be updated in the bank’s records. Minor accounts are excluded from this digital facility, even though RBI’s general LRS framework can permit a minor’s remittance through a guardian using Form A2. A minor or a customer outside the channel conditions should therefore use the appropriate branch process rather than treating the digital eligibility rules as the whole of LRS. Purposes Available in the Published Online Route PNB lists gift, donation, business travel, pilgrimage, medical treatment, education and maintenance of a close relative as the digitally supported purposes. The account-holder must select the correct RBI purpose code and provide accurate beneficiary information. A transaction outside these displayed purposes may still be permissible under FEMA but may require branch scrutiny, documentary evidence and a different workflow. Incorrect purpose coding can affect regulatory reporting, TCS treatment and the bank’s willingness to process the payment. A single digital remittance is capped at USD 10,000 equivalent, while the cumulative digital-channel amount is capped at USD 100,000 equivalent per financial year. These are PNB channel limits, not an expansion of the overall LRS ceiling. PNB expressly states that both remain within the USD 250,000 LRS annual limit. A customer needing another currency, a higher amount or another permissible purpose should contact a designated branch and should not split a transaction to avoid checks. Valid orders received by 4:00 p.m. IST on a forex working day are stated to be processed the same day; later requests move to the next forex business day. Saturday, Sunday and intervening holidays are excluded. Processing is not the same as beneficiary credit. Cut-off time, sanctions screening, correspondent routing, destination-country practices and beneficiary-bank controls can extend the timeline. Published Costs Exchange Rate GST and TCS PNB’s digital-service guide states a service charge of ₹500 plus SWIFT charges for remittances up to ₹20,000 and ₹1,000 plus SWIFT charges for amounts above ₹20,000. It states SWIFT charges of ₹500 plus GST. These figures are the bank’s published guide values and should be confirmed on the live transaction screen or with PNB before authorisation because schedules may change. The guide also warns that foreign or correspondent banks can deduct their charges. The exchange rate displayed while the request is entered is indicative; PNB applies the final rate at transaction confirmation. Above that threshold, the standard rate is five per cent for education or medical treatment remittances and twenty per cent for other LRS purposes; education funded by a qualifying loan under section 80E is excluded under the current guidance. TCS is not the final income tax on the remittance: it is creditable subject to the taxpayer’s records and return. GST is separate from TCS. Under GST valuation rules, foreign-currency conversion services can be valued using the RBI-reference-rate method or the prescribed optional slab method chosen by the supplier for the financial year. KYC Documents and Submission Sequence Information to Reconcile Before Authorisation Confirm the remitter name, PAN, residential status, selected debit account and cumulative LRS utilisation across authorised dealers. Match the beneficiary name, address, account or IBAN, bank name, SWIFT or BIC and destination country with the supporting invoice or declaration. Select the correct purpose and code; retain Form A2, declarations, invoices, admission or medical documents and source-of-funds evidence as relevant. Review the final INR debit, final exchange rate, service charge, SWIFT charge, GST, TCS and any correspondent-charge instruction before confirmation. After submission, the bank validates account balance, channel limits, beneficiary-country and beneficiary controls, FEMA permissibility and the completeness of details. Published rejection grounds include insufficient funds, exceeding channel or LRS limits, non-permitted country or beneficiary, unsupported or unauthorised transactions, incomplete beneficiary details, sending to one’s own overseas account through the restricted digital route, and overseas forex trading or margin purposes. A rejected request should be corrected through PNB; it should not be rerouted through an unverified foreign platform. Settlement Rejections Returns and Refund Questions A successful debit does not necessarily mean that the beneficiary bank has credited funds. The payment can pass through one or more intermediaries, each applying compliance checks and possibly charges. If beneficiary details are wrong, a bank may reject or return the transfer. The refund, if received, can be converted back at the then-applicable rate, so the INR amount may differ from the original debit. PNB’s published terms should be checked for current handling charges, and the customer should preserve the unique transaction reference, SWIFT copy if available and every communication. For delay or non-credit, the first contact should be PNB using the transaction reference. The bank’s forex helpdesk lists Trade Finance Centres and a dedicated International Banking Division grievance email for forex operations. General complaints can be raised with the branch or online grievance system, escalated to the Principal Nodal Officer and, after the bank’s process or thirty days as applicable, taken to the RBI Integrated Ombudsman. The Ombudsman route does not replace prompt correction of wrong beneficiary information. Resident Eligibility and Prohibited Forex Use RBI’s LRS direction permits resident individuals to remit for permitted current or capital-account transactions within the annual ceiling. It does not permit remittances for margins or margin calls to overseas exchanges or counterparties, and PNB’s own FAQ also lists overseas forex trading among prohibited uses. The absence of a foreign platform from RBI’s Alert List is not approval. A PNB account and an LRS declaration therefore cannot be used as a pathway to offshore leveraged forex, CFDs or another transaction prohibited under FEMA. Frequently Asked Questions Is PNB One an offshore forex-trading platform? No. It is Punjab National Bank’s channel for specified outward remittances. It does not authorise leveraged overseas forex or CFD trading. Who may use PNB’s digital outward-remittance facility? The published route is for eligible resident-individual customers with a qualifying PNB account, completed KYC, updated PAN, mobile and email, and channel registration. What is the online single-transaction limit? PNB’s current published digital instructions state USD 10,000 equivalent per transaction and USD 100,000 equivalent cumulatively per financial year, within the overall LRS ceiling. Is the displayed exchange rate final? No. PNB states that the rate shown during request entry is indicative and that the rate applied at transaction confirmation is final. Can correspondent banks deduct charges? Yes. Intermediary or beneficiary banks may levy charges, which can reduce the amount credited or increase the cost depending on the selected charge arrangement. Can LRS be used for an overseas margin call? No. RBI and PNB materials expressly prohibit remittances for overseas margins or margin calls and foreign-exchange trading abroad. Is TCS an additional permanent tax cost? TCS is collected at source and generally appears as tax credit. Final treatment depends on the taxpayer’s return, reconciliation and eligibility for credit. Where should a forex complaint be escalated? Start with PNB’s branch or grievance system and forex helpdesk, then escalate through the Principal Nodal Officer. Eligible unresolved complaints may be taken to the RBI Ombudsman. Key Takeaways Punjab National Bank is the executing Indian bank for its internet-banking and PNB One outward-remittance route; it is not an intermediary for offshore leveraged forex. The digital service has its own eligibility, purpose, currency and channel limits within the broader RBI LRS ceiling. The real cost combines the final exchange rate, service and SWIFT charges, possible correspondent deductions, GST and applicable TCS.
