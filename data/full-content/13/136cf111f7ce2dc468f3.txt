Articles 3 min read RBI’s rate hike likely to have moderate impact on home and auto loan demand For the last few years, banks and NBFCs have been betting big on retail loans, which have grown significantly compared to corporate and other loans. But with a 25 bps repo rate increase in the RBI’s October monetary policy, the retail loan book may be modestly impacted. A 25 bps hike alone might not make a huge impact immediately, but this is the beginning of the tightening cycle, which may impact retail credit, including home and auto loans. An increase of this size is modest when set against the long-term value of owning a home, and the exact change will depend on each loan's benchmark and reset cycle,” said Ajai Shukla, MD & CEO, PNB Housing Finance. He expects demand to continue during the festive season. Advt In the last almost 4 years, retail demand has picked up since lending rates remained steady. The certainty over the rates made lenders offer steady interest rates for home loans and auto loans.Home loan EMI increase?Since the majority of home customers' loans are connected to the External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR), the rates will be passed on immediately or in the next repayment cycle itself. On the other side, loans that are connected to the fixed format will continue in the same range.“Higher rates will naturally have implications for borrowers, particularly home loan customers, and small businesses. Banks will need to balance the transmission of higher rates while continuing to support productive credit demand,” said Ajay Kumar Srivastava, Managing Director & CEO, Indian Overseas Bank.According to a rough estimate by Andromeda loan distributor, with the rate hike, the current interest rate of 7.15% will become 7.40%. A borrower with a Rs. 1 crore home loan, currently paying Rs. 78,433 towards EMI, will have to pay more than Rs. 1,500, at Rs. 79,949, for a 20-year tenure loan.According to real estate research firm Knight Frank, the rate hike will have a moderate impact on home buying as lenders will certainly increase the rates for retail loans.“The rate hike will put pressure on consumer sentiment and discretionary spending – this has a direct correlation to housing demand. The festive season is a key period for housing demand, and an increase in borrowing costs will affect buyer sentiment,” said Anuj Puri, Chairman - ANAROCK Group. Advt Auto loans face testAuto loans are also likely to see a modest increase in borrowing costs, although the impact could be cushioned by strong festive demand. Automobile retail sales rose 31.82 per cent year-on-year to a record 25.37 lakh units in September, while passenger vehicle sales increased 32.10 per cent to 4.27 lakh units. Dealers are also seeing healthy bookings ahead of Navratri and Dussehra, with 75.57 per cent of dealers expecting growth in October. The 25 bps rate hike, however, could make some buyers more cautious if higher financing costs persist through the festive season.The RBI message is very clear that inflation is no longer the same, and food and fuel prices will not remain stable as last year. Hence, the question now is whether demand for housing or retail will remain stable or come down, and will credit growth be under pressure?
