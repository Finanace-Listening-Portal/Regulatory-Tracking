Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Yashraj Films Private Limited Vs Commissioner of CGST & Central Excise (CESTAT Mumbai) Summary: CESTAT Mumbai allowed the appeal of Yashraj Films Private Limited against an order confirming service tax of ₹46,40,92,070, interest, an equivalent penalty under Section 78 and a further penalty of ₹10,000 under Section 77(2) of the Finance Act, 1994. The dispute covered April 2013 to June 2017 and arose from foreign-currency expenditure on overseas film-related activities, including those concerning “Dhoom3”, “Gunday” and “Befikre”. Following an audit, the Department treated overseas Line Producers or Local Area Representatives (LARs) as independent providers of film-production services and demanded tax under the reverse charge mechanism. It also relied on foreign-government incentives received through those entities as supporting their status as film producers. The appellant maintained that the LARs merely facilitated procurement of services from third parties abroad, while creative decisions, production control, budgets and exploitation of the films remained with it. It relied on Rule 2(f) and Rule 9(c) of the Place of Provision of Services Rules, 2012, contending that the place of provision of the intermediary services was the overseas service provider’s location. Alternatively, it argued that reimbursements were outside taxable value before the amendment to Section 67 effective from 14 May 2015 and that the pure-agent exclusion under Rule 5(2) of the Service Tax (Determination of Value) Rules, 2006 applied thereafter. The Revenue reiterated that the overseas entities were not intermediaries. Examining the representative Production Assistance Agreement with FIRSTEP and supporting third-party agreements, the Tribunal found that the LAR’s role was ancillary and confined to arranging facilities, personnel and other services needed for overseas shooting. Yashraj Films retained exclusive creative control and final control over budgetary and business matters. Agreements concerning filming premises, church facilities and electrical arrangements demonstrated that the underlying suppliers provided their services to the appellant, while FIRSTEP acted between those suppliers and the appellant. Its fixed facilitation fee of €100,000 was distinct from the amounts used to procure the underlying services. Applying the criteria in the Ministry of Finance’s Taxation of Services Education Guide, the Tribunal held that the LAR could not alter the nature or contractual value of the underlying services, its own remuneration was separately identifiable, and the main services were distinct from its facilitation activity. The Tribunal consequently applied Rule 9, placing provision of the intermediary services at the overseas provider’s location in Paris, France. It further observed that Rule 14 would favour Rule 9 over the general Rule 3 even if both merited equal consideration. The services were therefore not exigible to service tax under Section 66B, and reverse charge under Section 68 read with Notification No. 30/2012-Service Tax dated 20 June 2012 did not apply. The Tribunal also rejected the inference drawn from foreign film incentives: receipt and passing on of a French tax rebate did not establish that the LAR itself supplied film-production services. Having decided the dispute on merits, it found it unnecessary to examine limitation and the other grounds. The impugned order dated 14 December 2022 was set aside and the appeal allowed. FULL TEXT OF THE CESTAT MUMBAI ORDER This appeal has been filed by M/s Yash Raj Films Private Limited, Mumbai (herein after referred to, for short, as “the appellants”) assailing the Order-in-Original No. MUM/CGST/MW/COMMR/AK/47/2022-23 dated 14.12.2022 (hereinafter referred to, for short, as “the impugned order”) passed by the Commissioner of Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) & Central Excise, Mumbai West, Mumbai. 2.1 The brief facts of the case are that the appellants herein inter alia, is engaged in the business of film production and distribution, primarily of ‘Hindi’ cinemas. In this business, their main activity consists of production of films, either directly by them or through their line producers on ‘work made for hire’ basis; such films are shot, either fully or partly, in India and/or in foreign countries. Besides production of films, they also undertake certain services viz., providing copyright right service, by temporary transfer/temporarily permitting use or enjoyment; selling of space or time slots for advertisement, advertising agency service; supply of tangible goods; business auxiliary services etc. For the purpose of payment of service tax on taxable services and for compliance with the service tax statute, the appellants are registered with jurisdictional Service Tax authorities holding Service Tax registration No. AAACY1176EST001. The appellants are also currently registered with jurisdictional authorities under Goods and Services Tax vide Registration No. 27AAACY1176E1ZF. 2.2 During the course of EA-2000 audit conducted by the department, upon scrutiny of balance sheets along with ledger accounts maintained by the appellants for the period 2013-2014 to June, 2017, the audit officers have noticed that the appellants have incurred expenditure in foreign currency for production, distribution, marketing and other expenditure for various movies produced and released by them. Some of such movies produced and released by the appellants during the relevant period, which are partly or wholly shot in foreign countries are “Dhoom3; Gunday; Befikre” etc. On further verification of ledgers and invoices, the audit officers had observed that the appellants have made payments in foreign currency, towards production of their films or part of their films, to many companies for the services of ‘Line production’ and/or for various other technical services. Through such analysis of accounting records, it was interpreted by the department that although the appellants had received services from various service providers located in foreign countries for the purpose of producing, post production of films and for other technical services, but they had not discharged appropriate service tax liability in terms of 66B, 68 (2) of the Finance Act, 1994 read with Rule 2 (1)(d)(G) of Service Tax Rules, 1994 and Notification No. 30/2012-S.T. dated 20.06.2012. 2.3 Further enquiry was made on the above issue by the department, in calling for agreements, contracts, purchase orders etc., entered by the appellants with service providers for their detailed examination. Based on ‘Production Assistance Agreement’ and other details submitted by the appellants, the department had interpreted that the overseas Line Producers or Local Area Representatives (LARs) are providing services of film production to the appellants on principal-to-principal basis, and therefore the appellants are liable to pay service tax on RCM basis. Further, the department noted that such overseas entities had received certain incentives from some of the foreign Governments like France, United Kingdom, Switzerland etc., which are given to the producers of films for having incurred expenses by foreign film makers in that country for production of international films, and the same has been passed on to the appellants. Therefore, the department had interpreted that such fact also supports that the services have been rendered by the overseas entities to the appellants, which are taxable under RCM basis. After providing opportunity for pre-notice consultation, the department had issued Show Cause Notice (SCN) dated 17.04.2019 for recovery of service tax payable on RCM basis under Section 73(1) of the Finance Act, 1994 along with applicable interest and for imposition of penalty under Sections 77 & 78 of the Act of 1994. The said SCN dated 17.04.2019, was adjudicated upon by the original authority in passing an Order-in-Original dated 14.12.2022, wherein he had confirmed the service demand of Rs.46,40,92,070/- for the disputed period 2013-2014 to 2017-2018 (upto June, 2017) and ordered for its recovery along with interest under Section 73(2) of the Finance Act, 1994. The original authority also imposed penalty equivalent to the service tax under Section 78 of the Act of 1994 and also imposed penalty of Rs.10,000/- under Section 77(2) ibid. Feeling aggrieved with impugned order dated 14.12.2022, the appellants have preferred this appeal before the Tribunal. 3.1 Learned Advocate for the appellants contends that the appellants as a film producing company in India, had entered into agreements with Local Area Representatives (LARs) situated abroad for undertaking certain activities depending upon creative requirements of scripts, cinematographic films. These included assistance to the appellants in hiring of junior artists, camera crew, arranging transportation, obtaining permission from local agencies, arranging lights, shooting related equipment, generator and studios from third parties and arranging support team from various agencies etc. He further submitted that LARs did not undertake film production on principal-to-principal basis. All creative control, production decisions, employment of lead actors, directors, technicians, post-production activities and exploitation of films vested exclusively with the appellants in India. He further stated that the true nature of the transaction between the appellants and LARs can be determined in light of the tenor of agreements and intention of the parties. Therefore, by citing various clauses of the agreement entered into between the appellants and LARs, he submitted that (i) the appellants alone are the producer of the films and retained exclusive control over all creative, budgetary, and business matters; (ii) the role of LAR was strictly limited to rendering assistance in procuring various services as required by the appellants; (iii) the engagement of LARs does not create any obligation on the appellants to produce the films or utilize the assistance. The LARs’ role was purely ancillary and subordinate, confined solely to facilitating the procurement of services, and had no connection whatsoever with the production of the film; (iv) the LARs acted purely as intermediaries, merely facilitating procurement of services from third parties on behalf of the appellants, without providing any independent service themselves; and (v) accordingly, the very foundation of the SCN and the Impugned Order, which presumes that the LARs provided production services of films, is factually incorrect and untenable. 3.2 Learned Advocate further stated that upon perusal several clauses agreement it would be evidential that LARs merely arranged and facilitated provision of services by third parties to the appellants, and LARs by themselves did not provide any service to the appellants. Therefore, he claimed that LARs squarely fall within the definition of “intermediary” under Rule 2(f) of the Place of Provision of Services Rules, 2012 (POPS Rules). Thus, he claimed that once the services rendered by the LARs are held to be intermediary services, the place of provision of such intermediary services in terms of Rule 9(c) of the POPS Rules, shall be the location of the LARs. Accordingly, the place of provision of the intermediary services, in terms of Rule 9(c) of the POPS Rules, is the location of the LARs, which is admittedly outside the taxable territory. Therefore, he submitted that the said services do not qualify as services provided in the taxable territory as contemplated under Section 66B of the Finance Act, 1994 in the impugned order. Accordingly, he claimed that no Service Tax is leviable on the appellants under the RCM. 3.3 He further stated that in terms of the agreements entered into by the appellants with the LARs, they are liable to pay only on a limited, fixed and pre-agreed facilitation fee as expressly provided; all other amounts paid to the LARs were strictly towards reimbursement of the costs, which were advanced by the appellants solely towards payment of third-party vendors. Therefore, in terms of Section 67 of the Finance Act, 1994, for the period prior to 14.05.2015, he claimed that expenses incurred by a service provider on behalf of the service recipient did not form part of the taxable value as the payments were made by appellants for reimbursement of amounts paid to the LARs. Further, he submitted that even after the amendment to Section 67 ibid with effect from 14.05.2015, though reimbursable expenditure is included within the scope of “consideration”, the expenses incurred by a person acting as a pure agent of the service recipient are specifically excluded from the taxable value in terms of Rule 5(2) of the Service Tax (Determination of Value) Rules, 2006. Therefore, he claimed that there is no legal requirement for payment of service tax by the appellants in this case. 3.4 In support of their stand, the learned Advocate has relied upon the following decisions of the Tribunal and the judgements of the Hon’ble Supreme Court, in the respective cases mentioned below: (i) Ramesh Sippy Vs. The issues for consideration before us are as follows: (i) whether the services received by appellants from overseas entities can be considered as ‘taxable service’ in terms of the definition provided under 65(B)(51) of the Finance Act, 1994; (ii) whether confirmation of service tax demand under proviso to Section 73(1) of the Act of 1994 read with Section 66B and 68(2) ibid, by invoking extended period of limitation is sustainable in terms of the Act of 1994; and (iii) whether penalty under Section 77, 78 of the Finance Act, 1994 can be imposed on the appellants in this case. 6.2. From plain reading of the above legal provisions, it transpires that during the disputed period i.e., post-negative list regime, the category of services hitherto defined under the erstwhile regime by itemizing it under Section 65(105) ibid were merged under a common phrase i.e., ‘service’ as defined under Section 65B(44) ibid. Further, the levy of service tax was provided under Section 66B ibid. On harmonious reading of these legal provisions, it transpires that there shall be an activity which should be in the nature of ‘service’, provided or agreed to be provided in the taxable territory, carried out by one person i.e., service provider, for another person viz., service receiver, involving a consideration in order to attract the levy of service tax. Further, where there is a levy of service tax under Section 66B ibid, and in specified situations as provided under Section 68(2) ibid and as provided under Notification No.30/2012-Service Tax dated 20.06.2012, service tax shall also be paid by the service receiver. 8.1 In the present case, the disputed transactions have been undertaken during April, 2013 to June, 2017. The LARs’ role is purely ancillary and subordinate, confined solely to facilitating the procurement of services, and had no connection whatsoever with the production of the film. Therefore, from the various clauses of the Agreement it is conclusively evident that the LARs have acted purely as intermediaries, merely facilitating procurement of services from third parties on behalf of the appellants, without providing any independent service by themselves. 8.3 In the impugned order, learned Commissioner in his findings recorded at para 49.3 & 50 of the impugned order, on the basis of above agreement has treated the LAR as ‘Line Producer’ of film on the basis of definition given in Wikipedia/online resource to categorize them as service provided on the basis of erstwhile Rule 3(iii) of the Taxation of Services (Provided from Outside India and Received in India) Rules, 2006 and under Rule 3 of the Place of Provision of Services Rules, 2012. As the period of dispute in the present case covers the period 01.04.2013 to 30.06.2017, the provisions of Rules of 2006 which superseded the Rule of 2012 by Notification No.28/2012-Service Tax dated 20.06.2012 brining into force the Place of Provision of Services Rules, 2012 (POPS) with effect from 01.07.2012 alone is relevant for determination of the place of provision of a service. In terms of the agreement dated 18.08.2016, it clearly brings out the fact that the services provided by FIRSTEP, Paris to the appellants are governed by the specific clauses of the agreement. On overall reading of the agreement, it transpires that recognizing the requirements of production of film, and the consequent activities that are needed to be undertaken viz., location of film shooting i.e., indoor and outdoor places; facilities and materials required viz., decorative items, costumes, plants & flower arrangement, sound recording systems, lighting arrangements etc.; manpower and specialists required viz., food stylists, decorators, technicians and specialists for filming of the events, actions by artists etc., the appellants have engaged FIRSTEP to provide the facility for engaging with the respective individuals concerned, by entering into a long form agreement, prior to rendering any services to the appellants. For all such activities, the Local Area Representative/ Line Producer (LAR) FIRSTEP shall be required to pay all the costs in connection with the activities performed by such individuals, in accordance with the budgetary allocation made by the appellants to the LAR. 8.4 In further examining such aspect, the learned Advocate for the appellants have produced the copy of the agreement entered into by the appellants with M/s BAZAR Decon Rental, 31/33 Rue du Temple, 75004, Paris & LAR FIRSTEP, as joint parties, along with English translation copy dated 23.03.2026, for providing the premises at ground floor of the ‘Aboudabi Bazar Boutique’ located at 125, Rue Vieille du Temple, 75003, Paris including storefront and display windows for the purpose of filming the movie “Befikre” for a specified period and timings as provided therein. The invoice dated 22.04.2016 indicate payment of Euro 3420 made by LAR on behalf of the appellant through LAR. Such amount paid for the above facility also included Value Added Tax (VAT)/ Taxe sur la Valeur Ajoutée (TVA) in French, of Euro 570 (VAT @ 20% on Euro 2850). Similarly, there were a number of agreements have been entered into by the appellant with various persons viz., agreement dated 11.05.2016 with Father Annequin, in his capacity as Parish Priest of Saint-Germain L’ Auxerrois Church situated as 2, Place du Louvre, 75001, Paris for use of parish hall, WC facilities, parking area for setting up canteen facility in the parking slot, installation of hair, makeup, costume in the parish hall; services provided by Mr. Jose’ Correia, 2, Rue Azais, 75018, Paris for providing electrical lines supply power to the Hair, Makeup, Costume at the premises along with generator position at the venue, for a specified period. On perusal of the above documents placed on record, we find that a number of persons operating from Paris, France have provided services or rendered certain activities as ‘service provider’ to the appellants for film shooting of ‘Befikre’ on principal-to-principal basis by specific agreements entered into between them and the LAR FIRSTEP had acted as an ‘intermediary’ between such persons and the appellants. 8.5 Therefore, the service provider in all such activities are the persons located abroad/in Paris and the service receiver is the appellants in India and such activity was for an agreed consideration paid through the LAR acting on behalf of the appellants. Thus, the services provided such persons placed in abroad in this case is covered under the scope of definition under Section 65(44) ibid as ‘service’ and is also covered under the definition of ‘taxable service’ in terms of Section 65(51) ibid, as the service in the present case is not covered under any specific exclusion in terms of negative list under 66D ibid. However, as the services are rendered from abroad i.e., Paris office of the service provider, we need to further examine the exigibility to service tax in terms of Section 66C ibid and the rules made thereunder. Thus, we need to look into the Rules framed in exercise of the powers vested with the Government in terms of Section 94(2)(hhh) ibid, i.e., Place of Provision of Services Rules, 2011 (POPS). 8.6 In terms of POPS, under Rule 3, generally the place of provision of service shall be taken as the location of the recipient of service. However, specific rules prescribe the place of provision of service in various circumstances such as performance-based services, services relating to immovable property, services relating to events, services provided from more than one location, service provider and service recipient located in taxable territory, provision of specified services, goods transport services, passenger transport services, services provided on board a conveyance etc. It is claimed by the appellants that the nature of services received from LARs situated at Paris is an intermediary service, for which the applicable rule under POPS is Rule 9; whereas the Department has claimed that the applicable rule under POPS is Rule 3. In order to determine the correct application of the Rule, we have also examined the definition of the phrase ‘intermediary services’ provided in Rule 2 ibid. Inasmuch as, the Paris office of FIRSTEP i.e., the service provider, had arranged for various activities related to shooting of film at Paris, France to the appellants, in order to completing the film production activity abroad, it is clearly proved that these services are covered under the definition ‘intermediary services’. 8.7 At the time of introduction of negative list regime of service tax, the Ministry of Finance had issued an Education Guide on ‘Taxation of Services”. The relevant paragraph dealing with ‘intermediary services’ the clarification providing on the scope of levy is extracted as follows: “5.9.6 What are “Intermediary Services”? Generally, an “intermediary” is a person who arranges or facilitates a supply of goods, or a provision of service, or both, between two persons, without material alteration or further processing. Thus, an intermediary is involved with two supplies at any one time: (i)the supply between the principal and the third party; and (ii)the supply of his own service (agency service) to his principal, for which a fee or commission is usually charged. For the purpose of this rule, an intermediary in respect of goods (such as a commission agent i.e. a buying or selling agent, or a stockbroker) is excluded by definition. Also excluded from this sub-rule is a person who arranges or facilitates a provision of a service (referred to in the rules as “the main service”), but provides the main service on his own account. In order to determine whether a person is acting as an intermediary or not, the following factors need to be considered:- Nature and value: An intermediary cannot alter the nature or value of the service, the supply of which he facilitates on behalf of his principal, although the principal may authorize the intermediary to negotiate a different price. Also, the principal must know the exact value at which the service is supplied (or obtained) on his behalf, and any discounts that the intermediary obtains must be passed back to the principal. Separation of value: The value of an intermediary’s service is invariably identifiable from the main supply of service that he is arranging. It can be based on an agreed percentage of the sale or purchase price. Generally, the amount charged by an agent from his principal is referred to as “commission”. Identity and title: The service provided by the intermediary on behalf of the principal is clearly identifiable.” 8.8 Accordingly, by applying the aforesaid three criteria in the present case, we find that firstly, LAR FIRSTEP cannot alter the nature of service for which they are engaging the persons abroad to enable provide film shooting requirements/services; secondly, the consideration is received in terms of the contractual arrangements which cannot be altered by LAR FIRSTEP. Thus, the nature and value of the services provided are known to both parties i.e., the service provider and a service receiver, and further to the LAR FIRSTEP. We also find that the consideration charged by LAR FIRSTEP in the form of fixed fees at 100,000 Euro is independent of the consideration involved in obtaining the services from various persons abroad by the appellants for shooting the film abroad. Thus, the principle of separation of value, is also fulfilled in the present case. The main transaction between the appellants and the persons situated abroad, is the services involved in obtaining various facilities viz., renting of location of film shooting i.e., indoor and outdoor places; facilities and materials required for decorative items, costumes, plants & flower arrangement, sound recording systems, lighting arrangements etc.; manpower and specialists required such as food stylists, decorators, technicians and specialists; for filming of the events, actions to be performed by artists for shooting the film. These services are distinct and is completely different from the services provided by LAR FIRSTEP. Thus, we find that all the three criteria laid down by the CBIC’s clarification is fulfilled in the present case to categorize the disputed services provided by the LAR, as ‘intermediary service’. 8.9 Thus, in terms of specific Rule of the POPS which would more appropriately applicable in this case, we find that Rule 9 of POPS is applicable to the ‘intermediary service’ as it is the more appropriate rule for determination of place of provision of the service. Further, in terms of Rule 14 of POPS in determining the order in which these rules shall be applied, even for argument sake, if we consider that both Rule 3 as claimed by the department and Rule 9 as claimed by the appellants are contending Rules in the present case, then notwithstanding the above various Rules, the place of provision of services shall be determined in terms of ‘later Rule’ among those merit equal consideration. Thus, even in such a situation, Rule 9 of POPS would be the appropriate rule to be applied in this case. Accordingly, the place of provision of service in the present case would be the location of the service provider i.e., Paris, France. Hence, in respect of ‘intermediary services’ provided by the LAR FIRSTEP from PARIS to the appellants who are located in India, would not be exigible to service tax under Section 66B ibid. Further, we are also of the view that for the above reasons, the levy of service tax on Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) basis under Section 68 ibid read with Notification No.30/2012-Service Tax dated 20.06.2012 is also not applicable in the present case. 9.1 In the impugned order, learned Commissioner has also considered the fact that the LAR FIRSTEP having received the refund of taxes provided by the Government of France, and thus have come to the conclusion that they should be treated as exporter of film to India, for charging service tax on the appellants as a recipient of service. On perusal of the extract of ledger accounts of foreign remittances for the period 01.04.2016 to 31.03.2017 maintained by the appellants, it transpires that an amount of 2,50,000 Euro had been received from LAR FIRSTSTEP. Similar incentives were also received by the appellants from other LARs viz., 15,361.41 GBP from Bollywood Limited. On further examination of this aspect with the details submitted by the learned Advocate of the appellants on the scheme of ‘Tax Rebate for International Production’ (TRIP) it transpires that it is a selectively granted tax rebate/incentive by the Centre National du Cinema et de I’image animee/French National Centre of Cinema (CNC). This is granted by the CNC to the French production services company who is in charge in compliance with a contract entered into with a non-French production company, for both supplying the artistic and technical means for making the feature film and of managing the material operations for its making and monitoring its proper execution at France. Further, such tax rebate is provided only to certain eligible project which fulfill the conditions of minimum project amount to be incurred viz., 250,000 Euro and having at least 5 days of shooting in France; the approved works should include elements related to the French culture, heritage, and territory, accordingly to a cultural test specific to each genre (live action or animation). On examining the nature of tax rebate/incentive, it transpires that this is being given to certain specific film projects fulfilling certain conditions specified therein and to the film production company as well as to the intermediary persons like the LAR who manages the material, operations, personnel required for shooting the film abroad, by a non-French production company. Hence, such grant of rebate/incentive to the LAR FIRSTEP as intermediary for the appellants being non-French film production company, does not provide any sufficient ground to conclude that the LARs are the service providers as film production company to the appellants. Therefore, we do not find any merits in the conclusion arrived by the learned Commissioner in the impugned order on this aspect to treat LARs as service providers for charging service tax in respect of services provided by them as film producers. 9.2 Since, we have examined the disputed issues on merits and have come to the conclusion that the disputed activities are not liable to payment of service tax on RCM basis, we do not find it necessary to go into the aspects of limitation of time and other grounds raised by the appellants against the adjudged demands confirmed in the impugned order. 10. Based on the foregoing discussions and analysis, we find that there are no strong grounds to hold that the services received by the appellants from LARs having their base in Paris, France or other foreign entities based in abroad, are liable for payment of service tax in terms of the provisions of Finance Act, 1994. Consequently, the adjudged demand of service tax and imposition of penalties confirmed in the impugned order is not legally sustainable. 11. In view of the above, we are of the considered view that the adjudged demands confirmed on the appellants in the impugned order dated 14.12.2022 is liable to be set aside. Therefore, by setting aside the impugned order, the appeal is allowed in favour of the appellants.
