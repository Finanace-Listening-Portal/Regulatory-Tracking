Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re NH Integrated Care Private Limited (NCLT Bengaluru) Summary: The National Company Law Tribunal, Bengaluru Bench, sanctioned the Scheme of Arrangement between NH Integrated Care Private Limited (demerged company) and Narayana Hrudayalaya Limited (resulting company) under Sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act, 2013. The scheme concerned transfer and vesting of the clinical services undertaking of NH Integrated Care, a wholly owned subsidiary, into listed Narayana Hrudayalaya. The order dated 30 October 2026 approved 1 April 2025 as the appointed date and made the scheme binding on the directors, shareholders and creditors of both companies. The second-motion petition followed the first-motion order dated 13 February 2026. The Tribunal recorded that the specified stakeholder meetings were convened on 2 April 2026 and the relevant classes approved the arrangement with 100% voting in favour. The petitioners also filed affidavits evidencing service on statutory authorities and newspaper publication, along with auditor certificates concerning the proposed accounting treatment and financial statements. Their boards had approved the scheme on 17 September 2025. The Regional Director, relying on the Registrar of Companies report, raised numerous compliance issues. These included significant beneficial ownership and Forms BEN-1/BEN-2, charges over transferred assets, SEBI and stock-exchange requirements applicable to the listed resulting company, share consideration and authorised capital, outstanding income-tax and GST dues, MSME and statutory payments, FEMA compliance, employee benefits and ESOPs, objects clauses, accounting standards, preservation of records, and separate identification of transferred and remaining assets and liabilities. The petitioners submitted detailed replies and undertakings. They maintained that no new shares would be issued because the demerged company was wholly owned, that stock-exchange intimation was sufficient under Regulation 37(6), and that the charge in favour of HSBC Bank relating to the undertaking would be transferred and necessary ROC forms filed. On tax liabilities, the Regional Director had noted disputed income-tax and GST dues of ₹266.87 million and other outstanding amounts. The companies undertook to settle disputed tax liabilities when crystallised. They stated that ₹169.75 lakh of undisputed statutory dues of the demerged company had been discharged and contested the characterisation of ₹258 million as undisputed dues of the resulting company. For MSME amounts, they submitted that payments would follow applicable contractual or statutory requirements. They also undertook compliance with FEMA/RBI requirements, employee continuity and benefit-fund transfers, applicable accounting standards and record-preservation obligations. These statements were the petitioners’ replies, not separate adjudications determining each underlying liability. The Income Tax Department requested compliance with Section 170A of the Income-tax Act, 1961; the petitioners undertook to contest proceedings relating to the transferred undertaking and pay demands upon crystallisation. The Reserve Bank of India stated that entities must themselves comply with FEMA and relevant RBI rules and that its regulatory role did not involve vetting individual schemes. The resulting company furnished a corresponding compliance undertaking. After reviewing the authorities’ observations and petitioners’ responses, the Tribunal concluded that the undertakings and certifications addressed the concerns and that no impediment remained to sanction. The Tribunal approved the scheme but expressly declined to treat its order as an exemption from stamp duty, taxes, statutory permissions, civil or criminal liability, or other legal compliance. It directed delivery of a certified copy to the Registrar of Companies, Karnataka within 30 days of receipt; compliance within the stipulated period with Section 314 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, identified in the order alongside former Section 170A of the 1961 Act; preservation of books under Section 239 of the Companies Act, 2013; and strict adherence to all accepted undertakings. It also directed issuance of orders in Form CAA-7 on filing affidavit-backed schedules of freehold and leasehold properties, permitted interested persons to seek further directions, and allowed the petition. REPLY BY THE PETITIONER: The Petitioner Companies have filed their reply to the RD report vide Diary No.9015 dated 29.09.2026, stating as under: – 1. Regarding the observation in Para No. 4 of the Regional Director Report, it is submitted that Petitioner Company No. 1 is a wholly owned subsidiary of Petitioner Company No. 2, and Petitioner Company No. 2 alone holds 100% of the equity share capital of Petitioner Company No. 1. In terms of Section 90 of the Act read with Rule 2(1)(h) of the Companies (Significant Beneficial Owners) Rules, 2018, an individual is regarded as indirectly holding a right or entitlement through a body corporate member of a reporting company only where such individual holds a “majority stake”, ie., more than fifty percent (50%) of the share capital, voting rights, or right to receive or participate in the distributable dividend, in such body corporate member. Upon examination of the shareholding pattern of Petitioner Company No. 2, being the sole member of Petitioner Company No. 1, it is submitted that no individual, including the largest individual shareholder holding 34.80% of the equity shares of Petitioner Company No. 2, holds a majority stake therein.
