The Nifty Bank index has risen 11.3% this month, with HDFC Bank gaining 17.9% and ICICI Bank 13.4% as of November 16. Lower valuations after a prolonged correction, strong credit growth and improved asset quality are supporting the rally, although foreign investors' latest bank-specific purchases are not separately available.Foreign portfolio investors pumped Rs 84,290 crore into Indian equities between November 2 and 13, reversing net withdrawals of Rs 35,861 crore in September and Rs 34,843 crore in October. Financial stocks gained between 11.5% and 13.5% in November, according to market data. The Nifty Bank index closed at 61,575.9 on November 16, up 11.3% over the month. Advt HDFC Bank has been among the biggest beneficiaries, rising 17.9% in November as of November 16, while ICICI Bank gained 13.4%. Yes Bank, Bank of Baroda and Canara Bank also advanced sharply. The gains reflect buying across large private and public sector lenders, although the available aggregate FPI flow data does not establish the amount invested specifically in banking shares.Valuations have improved after a prolonged correction. HDFC Bank was trading at 1.8 times book value in October, against its five-year average of 2.8 times. ICICI Bank was valued at 2.6 times book value, compared with its five-year average of 3 times. Axis Bank was also trading at 1.8 times book value against its five-year average of 2.02 times. The discounts have made established private lenders more attractive to investors seeking exposure to India's banking growth.The operating outlook provides another reason for the interest. Bank credit grew 18.13% year-on-year as of September 15, 2026, against deposit growth of 17.31%. The expansion was supported by lending to retail customers, services, industry and agriculture. The narrowing gap between credit and deposit growth, however, does not eliminate funding pressure on banks.The triggersAsset quality has strengthened considerably. The banking system's gross non-performing asset ratio fell to 1.67% in June 2026 from 2.22% a year earlier, while the net NPA ratio declined to 0.39% from 0.51%. The capital adequacy ratio stood at 17.87%, providing a substantial buffer against potential loan losses. Lower bad-loan ratios reduce provisioning requirements and improve the visibility of future earnings. Advt Profitability remains a more complicated part of the investment case. The banking system's net interest margin stood at 3.22% in June 2026, marginally below 3.26% a year earlier. Banks have faced pressure from elevated deposit costs and adjustments in lending rates. Investors are therefore looking for stronger loan growth and better deployment of deposits to offset margin pressure and support returns.Foreign investors' existing exposure to Indian private banks remains substantial. Their holdings in the segment stood at $144 billion at the end of September, out of total holdings of $717 billion in Nifty 500 companies. HDFC Bank accounted for $54.3 billion and ICICI Bank $50.1 billion. However, foreign investors actually reduced their allocation to private banks during the September quarter, and their overall BFSI allocation fell 130 basis points sequentially to 33.1%. The November rally therefore represents a recovery in share prices alongside a broader return of foreign money to Indian equities, rather than confirmed evidence of a fresh increase in bank ownership. Advt The global shift away from crowded artificial intelligence-linked trades and lower crude oil prices have also improved sentiment towards Indian equities. Foreign investors had withdrawn $31.41 billion from Indian equities through October. November's inflows have reversed part of that selling, but their sustainability will depend on earnings growth, deposit mobilisation and banks' ability to protect net interest margins.
