Articles 2 min read Faster lending cannot come at the cost of credit quality and customer trust: NBFC leaders NBFCs are using real-time data and AI to improve lending efficiency, but incomplete information, historical biases and the need for human oversight remain key challenges, Industry leaders said at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Published On Oct 12, 2026 at 05:13 AM IST var news_msid = 134879809; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The next competitive advantage in lending will come from translating existing customer data into timely and responsible credit decisions rather than simply collecting more information, NBFC leaders said at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026.Vinod Kumar, CDO, Shriram Finance; Gokul Rajan, CDO, Hinduja Leyland Finance; Suvayu K Chakraborty, National Head – Business Transformation Strategy (AI and Digital), Tata Capital; and Manju Dalvi, Head – Digital & Data, HDB Financial Services, discussed how lenders can use data and AI without compromising underwriting discipline or customer trust. Advt Getting data to the frontlineKumar said the challenge was ensuring that relevant customer information reached frontline employees when lending decisions were being made. Shriram Finance is using existing customer data and credit signals to identify pre-qualified borrowers, with one initiative eliminating around eight out of ten steps in certain application journeys.The company is also moving towards real-time data access and keeping AI capabilities separate from core lending systems, allowing models to evolve without extensive changes to underlying infrastructure.Connecting lending and collectionsRajan said fragmented information across sales, credit and collections often prevents lenders from building a complete picture of borrower risk. Hinduja Leyland Finance has been working to connect data-driven decisions across the lending lifecycle through consistent data models, automated data capture and risk-based business rules.Repayment trends across dealerships, vehicle models and variants can inform future lending terms and risk assessments. However, where information is incomplete or unreliable, decisions must be referred to credit officers.“We can only build decision systems to the extent we have correct data,” Rajan said, stressing the importance of explainability and accountability. Advt Speed cannot override underwritingChakraborty cautioned against treating faster approvals as the primary measure of digital lending success.“Are we targeting speed, or are we targeting the right decision and a faster decision?” he asked.He cited SME lending to illustrate how transaction inflows alone may not reflect a borrower's repayment capacity if other liabilities are overlooked. Lenders must retain human scrutiny for higher-risk cases and periodically review historical rejection patterns to ensure that AI models do not continue disadvantaging borrowers whose financial circumstances have improved. Advt Customer trust and human judgment remain criticalDalvi said access to customer data did not justify using every information point for marketing or personalised offers. HDB Financial Services uses workflows to assess whether potential customer opportunities warrant engagement, rather than automatically turning every lead into a sales campaign.“We may want to lose a lead rather than lose the trust of the customer,” she said.The company is also embedding task-management tools into existing systems to help its field sales workforce of more than 60,000 employees prioritise activities and improve productivity. Advt Kumar said data could help frontline employees identify suitable borrowers and make faster decisions, but local knowledge and relationships remained important, particularly in smaller towns.For NBFCs, the challenge is to build reliable and explainable decision-making systems that combine technological efficiency with sound underwriting, strong governance and human judgment.
