Summary : RBL Bank’s official rate page, checked on 6 November 2026, shows a defined callable retail fixed-deposit tenure band from 18 months to less than 60 months for deposits below ₹3 crore, effective 1 October 2026. The published annual rates are 7.00% for the general category, 7.50% for eligible senior citizens aged 60 to below 80, and 7.75% for eligible super senior citizens aged 80 or above. The corresponding effective annualised yields displayed by the bank are 7.19%, 7.71% and 7.98%, respectively, based on the bank’s stated compounding presentation. Premature full or partial withdrawal is allowed, but interest is recalculated at the original-date rate for the completed tenure and is generally reduced by a 1% penalty; the bank states that senior and super-senior citizens are exempt from that penalty. Interest remains taxable, and DICGC cover is limited to ₹5 lakh for principal and interest together per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity. This is a neutral verification guide, not a recommendation, ranking or return prediction. Callable Status and the Difference from ACE Non-Callable Deposits The selected product is callable: premature full or partial withdrawal is permitted under the published terms. RBL Bank separately lists non-callable deposits for specified higher minimum amounts and slightly different rates. Those ACE deposits cannot ordinarily be withdrawn before maturity and do not allow sweep-in, overdraft, letters of credit, bank guarantees or security liens. The two schedules should not be mixed when assessing liquidity or yield. For a standard callable booking, the application and deposit receipt should show principal, customer category, exact start and maturity dates, interest rate, cumulative or payout option, nomination, premature-closure status and maturity instructions. Monthly interest is paid at a discounted value. For cumulative deposits of 181 days or more, the bank’s terms provide quarterly compounding, while broken periods are handled under the stated calculation method. Effective annualised yield is therefore not the same as the nominal annual rate or a guaranteed cash amount for every payout option. Premature Closure Requires a Completed-Tenure Recalculation General Customer Penalty On premature full or partial withdrawal, RBL Bank states that interest is paid at the rate that applied on the original booking date for the period the deposit actually remained with the bank, subject to a 1% penalty on that rate. The contracted 7.00% does not simply continue until the early-closure date. The bank first identifies the original-date rate for the completed tenure, then applies the penalty under its terms. A pre-closure quote should show this calculation. Senior and Super-Senior Penalty Waiver The official callable schedule states that no premature-withdrawal penalty applies to senior and super-senior citizen fixed deposits. That waiver does not preserve the contracted long-tenure rate. Interest is still calculated using the rate that applied on the original booking date for the period actually completed. Eligibility should be confirmed on the receipt, because the additional rate and penalty waiver depend on the bank’s customer-category rules. Minimum Tenure and NRE Treatment For domestic and NRO fixed deposits, the minimum tenure is seven days and no interest is payable if a deposit is withdrawn before completing seven days. NRE deposits have a one-year minimum tenure, and no interest is payable when prematurely withdrawn within one year. The 18-to-under-60-month row can include eligible NRE/NRO deposits at the general rate, but senior-citizen additions do not apply to non-resident deposits. Tax deducted at source is only a collection mechanism. If no tax is deducted because a threshold is not crossed or a valid declaration is accepted, the income may still be taxable. Cumulative deposits can create yearly accrual and reporting differences even though cash is received only at maturity. Depositors should reconcile the bank’s interest certificate, Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement with their return or books. Section 393 Thresholds For transactions in Tax Year 2026-27, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies. Section 393 provides the relevant bank-interest threshold of ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen. These amounts are tested under the statutory aggregation rules; they are not exemptions. PAN status, the deduction rate, credit or payment timing and any higher-rate rule must be checked from current official instructions. Form 121 Is Not an Automatic No-TDS Form The Income Tax Department describes Form 121 as the declaration under section 393(6) for specified receipts without deduction when the statutory conditions, including estimated nil tax liability, are satisfied. It should ideally be furnished before credit or payment and does not make interest exempt. A depositor should not submit the form solely because interest falls below personal cash-flow expectations; eligibility must be assessed for the full estimated total income. DICGC Insurance Is Capped and Aggregated RBL Bank is a bank registered with DICGC. The statutory insurance ceiling is ₹5 lakh for principal and accrued interest together per depositor per bank in the same right and same capacity. Savings, current, recurring and fixed deposits at all RBL Bank branches are aggregated for that capacity. Opening several receipts or using digital and branch channels does not multiply the ceiling. Accounts held in genuinely different rights and capacities are treated under DICGC rules, but account naming should not be assumed to create separate cover without satisfying those rules. Insurance becomes payable through the statutory process when a covered event occurs; it is not a guarantee of immediate liquidity or recovery above ₹5 lakh. Any bank dues may be set off before the insured amount is determined.
