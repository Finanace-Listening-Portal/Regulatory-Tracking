Summary: Filing a second appeal before the Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT) under Section 112 of the CGST Act, 2017 involves a comprehensive electronic procedure extending beyond the preparation of FORM GST APL-05. Taxpayers challenging orders passed under Sections 107 or 108 must examine appealability and limitation, reconcile admitted and disputed tax liabilities, comply with statutory pre-deposit requirements, register on the GSTAT portal and prepare a properly indexed digital appeal record. The electronic filing framework is governed by Rule 110 of the CGST Rules and the GSTAT Procedure Rules, 2025. Importantly, electronic submission initially generates Part A of FORM GST APL-02A as provisional acknowledgement. The Registry may identify defects requiring correction and electronic re-submission. Part B of FORM GST APL-02A, containing the final appeal number, is issued after completion of the prescribed scrutiny process. The article provides a practical, step-by-step explanation of GSTAT account registration, professional representation, pre-deposit calculation, appeal fees, digital documentation, portal entries, electronic authentication and Registry compliance, enabling taxpayers and professionals to complete second-appeal filing systematically while avoiding procedural defects. The Entire Filing Process at a Glance Figure 1 — The thirteen-step roadmap, from the impugned order to the final acknowledgement Rule 110 presently requires FORM GST APL-05 with relevant documents to be filed electronically. Provisional acknowledgement is generated in Part A of FORM GST APL-02A, while the final acknowledgement carrying the appeal number is issued in Part B of FORM GST APL-02A after removal of defects, if any. The GSTAT’s present system is designed as an electronic filing system, and the earlier ordinary manual-filing route has been removed from Rule 110. The GSTAT Procedure Rules likewise provide for electronic uploading, electronic scrutiny and electronic filing through the GSTAT portal. 2 The Legal Architecture Behind the Filing Filing requirement Governing provision Practical effect Right to approach GSTAT Section 112(1) Second appeal against an order under section 107 or 108 Normal limitation Section 112(1) Three months from communication of the order, subject to the notified-date mechanism Condonation Section 112(6) Tribunal may admit a taxpayer appeal within a further three months on sufficient cause Form of appeal Section 112(7) + Rule 110 FORM GST APL-05 Mandatory pre-deposit Section 112(8) Full admitted dues plus statutory additional pre-deposit Automatic stay Section 112(9) Balance recovery deemed stayed after section 112(8) payment Electronic appeal Rule 110(1) APL-05 plus relevant documents, electronically Authentication Rule 110(3) read with Rule 26 Electronic authentication / signature Order copy and filing date Rule 110(4) The seven-day rule becomes crucial where the order is not available on the portal Appeal fee Rule 110(5) Minimum ₹5,000; Before starting the online form, four issues should be closed internally. A. Identify the appealable order The taxpayer’s appeal under section 112(1) lies against an order passed against him under section 107 or section 108. Therefore, keep ready at least: Document Purpose Order-in-Appeal / Revision Order Primary impugned order before GSTAT Order-in-Original Underlying adjudication record Show Cause Notice Origin of the dispute Relevant relied-upon documents To complete the record Proof / date of communication of OIA Limitation GST demand / payment records Pre-deposit reconciliation B. Check limitation separately The normal taxpayer limitation under section 112(1) is three months from communication of the impugned order, subject to the special notified-date provision. Section 112(6) gives the Tribunal power to admit the appeal within a further three months where sufficient cause exists. LEGACY ORDERS — A CAUTION FOR OCTOBER 2026 For legacy orders, the special general filing window notified for the initial GSTAT rollout has already expired. A legacy appeal should therefore not be filed merely on the assumption that the portal’s technical acceptance makes it within time. Wherever necessary, the appropriate condonation application with supporting affidavit and explanation should accompany the appeal. C. Separate admitted and disputed amounts This exercise drives the section 112(8) payment. The taxpayer should reconcile: Amount confirmed by OIA › Amount admitted › Amount disputed › First-appeal pre-deposit made › Additional GSTAT pre-deposit payable – DO NOT CONFUSE The statutory pre-deposit and the GSTAT filing fee are two entirely different payments. 4 Stage Two — Section 112(8) Pre-Deposit Before Filing For admission of the appeal, section 112(8) presently requires full payment of the admitted tax, interest, fine, fee and penalty, plus the prescribed additional pre-deposit in relation to the disputed tax. For the disputed component, the present law requires: First appeal: section 107(6) deposit + Second appeal before GSTAT: 10% of remaining tax in dispute = Statutory appellate deposit structure Figure 2 — The additional deposit rises with the remaining disputed tax and stops at the ₹20 crore ceiling The additional second-appeal deposit is subject to the statutory ceiling of ₹20 crore. Where the impugned order demands penalty without involving any demand of tax, the special proviso to section 112(8) governing the penalty deposit must be applied. Once the required amount under section 112(8) has been paid, section 112(9) provides a deemed stay of recovery for the balance amount during pendency of the appeal. A CURRENT-LAW CAUTION Some older portal guidance / FAQ material continued for some time to display the earlier 20% figure. That is no longer the statutory position. The amended section 112(8), as also reflected in the present filing architecture, uses 10%. 5 Stage Three — Create the Taxpayer’s Account on the GSTAT Portal The GSTAT e-filing portal maintains its own user-registration layer. Merely having a GST portal username does not mean that the GSTAT account has automatically been completed. The taxpayer should first choose the Taxpayer registration category on the GSTAT portal. Figure 3 — Taxpayer registration flow The GSTAT taxpayer-registration functionality fetches the registration on the basis of the GSTIN, sends OTP verification to the registered contact particulars and thereafter permits completion of the GSTAT user profile. The taxpayer login is linked to the GSTIN. Who should control this account? For a taxpayer appeal, the taxpayer account should remain the principal filing account. The professional should not ordinarily create an appeal by pretending to be the taxpayer. The GSTAT’s own filing advisory distinguishes the roles: Taxpayer — for filing the taxpayer’s appeal. Authorised Representative — for registration as representative and subsequent participation in the appeal in which he is appointed. This distinction is important. 6 Stage Four — Registration of Advocate, CA, CMA, CS or Other Authorised Representative Registration of the representative and appointment of the representative in a particular appeal are two different acts. Section 116 permits representation through, amongst others: Representative Broad statutory qualification Advocate Entitled to practise in any court in India and not debarred Chartered Accountant Certificate of Practice and not debarred Cost Accountant Certificate of Practice and not debarred Company Secretary Certificate of Practice and not debarred Regular employee / relative Subject to section 116 Specified retired tax officer Subject to statutory conditions GST Practitioner Where authorised in accordance with section 116 Figure 4 — Professional registration flow The present registration module seeks the representative category, name, registration / enrolment details and professional documentation, with OTP authentication of mobile / email. Registration does not itself authorise appearance After registering, the professional must still be added to the particular APL-05 and the appropriate authority document uploaded. For an Advocate, the current filing instruction requires the Vakalatnama. For a CA / CMA / CS or other tax professional, an authorisation in favour of the tax professional is required. The GSTAT Procedure Rules separately contemplate the Memorandum of Appearance in FORM GSTAT-04, together with the relevant Vakalatnama or letter of authorisation. The GSTAT President’s current filing instructions, extended up to 31 December 2026, specifically require the taxpayer / appellant to upload the professional’s authorisation or the Advocate’s Vakalatnama. 7 Stage Five — Prepare the Appeal as a Digital Paper-Book This is one of the most important changes in GSTAT practice. The underlying procedural discipline remains that of a properly prepared appellate paper-book: numbered paragraphs, separate grounds, A4 presentation, pagination, indexing and supporting documents. But Rule 115 shifts the filing environment to an electronic record. Practitioners should therefore think in terms of a digital paper-book, not a bundle of loose PDF attachments. The safest filing practice is to preserve traditional tribunal discipline even though the file is electronic. Aspect Practice A. Page size and text Prepare the appeal and written material in A4, with clear paragraphs and consecutively numbered grounds, placed under distinct heads. The GSTAT Procedure Rules prescribe formal presentation and indexing requirements. B. Pagination Use one continuous pagination structure within each electronic volume. If separate PDFs are uploaded, the index should identify the document description, internal page number and PDF / volume name. This saves enormous time at scrutiny and later at hearing. C. Bookmarking PDFs should be properly bookmarked, particularly large compilations. The current GSTAT filing manual expressly calls for proper PDF bookmarking in the upload process. D. Scanning quality Portal guidance recommends approximately 300 DPI, while managing file size; individual files ordinarily within about 20 MB, splitting large material into volumes. Do not scan a 300-page record as one uncontrolled image-heavy PDF. The upload dialogue displayed at the time of filing is the final technical limit, as different modules have at times displayed different ceilings. E. Searchable PDF Where documents originate electronically, upload the native / searchable PDF rather than printing and rescanning. For physical documents, scan clearly and, where possible, make the PDF text-searchable without altering the underlying image. F. Non-English documents The GSTAT Procedure Rules contemplate an appropriate English translation, duly authenticated / certified as required. VOL-1_APL05-SOF-GROUNDS.pdf Pages 1 – 75 VOL-2_OIA-OIO-SCN.pdf Pages 76 – 240 VOL-3_RUD-ANNEXURES.pdf Pages 241 – 410 A good naming and pagination structure (illustrative) A sensible bookmark structure would run: 01 FORM GST APL-05 · 02 Statement of Facts · 03 Grounds of Appeal · 04 OIA · 05 OIO · 06 SCN · 07 Reply to SCN · 08 Annexure A · 09 Annexure B, and so on. Is certification required on every document? No uniform rule should be applied mechanically to every PDF. The current instructions distinguish between documents that are already digitally generated and physical documents converted into scans. Digitally generated GST / GSTN documents need not be subjected merely to ritualistic re-certification, whereas scanned physical documents should carry the required signature / certification before upload. For the impugned order, Rule 110(4) has its own specific consequence, discussed in Section 12. 9 Stage Six — Start FORM GST APL-05 from the Taxpayer Account Before entering data online, the GSTAT itself recommends using its Offline Draft Filing Excel Sheet, which mirrors the principal portal tabs. This is good practice because portal filing should be a data-entry exercise from an already finalised appeal, not the place where facts and calculations are being decided. Verification & Signature Digital execution and final submission Tab 1 — Order Details The filing begins with the ARN / CRN or other identification particulars of the appellate / revisional order. Where the system successfully fetches the order, check the order number, date of order, date of communication, authority passing the order, GSTIN and demand particulars. The communication date deserves special attention because it drives limitation. Where portal data and the taxpayer’s evidence of actual communication do not coincide, the discrepancy should not simply be ignored: use the functionality available for entering the order details correctly and preserve documentary proof supporting the date relied upon. Tabs 2 and 3 — Basic Details and Case Details This stage creates the electronic metadata of the appeal: governing GST enactment, State / jurisdiction, nature / category of dispute, period, subject, brief case description, Statement of Facts, grounds and relief sought. The dropdown classification is essentially a system classification tool; it should not replace the carefully drafted Statement of Facts and Grounds uploaded with the appeal. If the matter involves place of supply, particular care is required in selecting the appropriate jurisdiction / Bench particulars. Tab 4 — Appellant Details Check the appellant’s legal name, GSTIN, address, email / mobile, legal constitution, authorised signatory and the signatory’s designation. The person selected for final verification should have valid authority to authenticate the appeal on behalf of the appellant, which is especially important for companies, LLPs, firms, societies and other juridical persons. Tab 5 — Who should be made respondent? The taxpayer should not mechanically enter the name of the officer who personally signed the Order-in-Appeal as the only respondent. Under the GSTAT Procedure Rules, in an appeal by a taxpayer, the concerned Commissioner is to be arrayed as respondent in accordance with the prescribed jurisdictional structure. Confirm the correct Central / State jurisdiction before final submission. Tab 6 — Add the Advocate / CA / other representative The registered professional’s name is selected from the portal’s representative database, and the taxpayer then uploads the representation document. Professional Filing document Advocate Vakalatnama + relevant memorandum of appearance CA / CMA / CS Letter / authority from appellant + FORM GSTAT-04 as applicable Other authorised representative Authority satisfying section 116 + prescribed appearance particulars Professional registers with GSTAT + Taxpayer authorises professional for the particular appeal = Valid representative linkage The representative’s portal registration is not the appellant’s authorisation. Both are required. The current filing directions make the authority / Vakalatnama an important scrutiny document. Tab 7 — Demand Details and Pre-Deposit The demand figures should be reconciled with the impugned order and GST records. Before moving ahead, the appeal file should internally contain a reconciliation such as: Particulars Amount Tax confirmed by impugned order ₹ ______ Interest ₹ ______ Penalty ₹ ______ Other amount ₹ ______ Amount admitted by appellant ₹ ______ Tax disputed ₹ ______ First-appeal pre-deposit ₹ ______ Additional section 112(8) deposit ₹ ______ Balance disputed amount ₹ ______ The GSTAT functionality interfaces with GST payment information for pre-deposit confirmation. If there is any mismatch between portal-fetched data and the taxpayer’s legally correct computation, do not conceal it: upload the calculation / payment evidence and supporting material through the functionality provided. 10 Stage Seven — Appeal Fee under Rule 110(5) Rule 110(5) presently prescribes the appeal / restoration fee at ₹1,000 for every ₹1 lakh of the relevant tax / ITC / difference in tax or ITC / fine / fee / penalty involved, subject to a minimum of ₹5,000 and maximum of ₹25,000. For an order not involving demand of tax, interest, fine, fee or penalty, the prescribed fee is ₹5,000. Figure 5 — The fee scales with the amount involved, between its floor and ceiling Type GSTAT appeal fee Appeal involving monetary demand As per Rule 110(5), minimum ₹5,000 and maximum ₹25,000 Order involving no demand of tax / interest / fine / fee / penalty ₹5,000 Rectification application under section 112(10) No fee under Rule 110(6) How the fee is paid The present GSTAT portal integrates appeal-fee payment with Bharatkosh. It supports online payment through the available gateway modes (Net Banking, Card, UPI, as available) and, where the permitted offline Bharatkosh route is used, uploading of the relevant payment receipt. APL-05 demand details completed › Portal computes / displays fee › Bharatkosh: online or permitted offline › Payment success; receipt reflected The taxpayer should download and preserve the challan / payment receipt even where the portal has automatically captured the successful transaction. 11 Stage Eight — Uploading the Digital Documents Once the payment / pre-deposit stages are complete, the appeal set is uploaded. A clean upload matrix may look like this: Portal document description Suggested file Appeal 01_APL05_SOF_GROUNDS.pdf Impugned order 02_OIA.pdf Original adjudication order 03_OIO.pdf SCN 04_SCN.pdf Appeal documents / annexures 05_ANNEXURES_VOL-I.pdf Further annexures 06_ANNEXURES_VOL-II.pdf Authorisation 07_AUTHORISATION_FORM04.pdf Pre-deposit 08_PREDEPOSIT.pdf Fee 09_APPEAL_FEE.pdf Condonation, if required 10_CONDONATION_AFFIDAVIT.pdf – AVOID GENERIC FILENAMES Do not upload scan1.pdf, new.pdf or final-final2.pdf. The Registry and the Bench should be able to understand the file from its name. 12 A Special Rule for the Impugned Order — Seven Days Matter Figure 6 — Effect of Rule 110(4) on the date of filing Situation What happens 1. Order already uploaded on the common portal The final acknowledgement is issued after removal of defects, if any, and the date of provisional acknowledgement is treated as the filing date. 2. Order not on the common portal; copy furnished in time The appellant uploads the prescribed self-certified copy within seven days from filing APL-05. The provisional-acknowledgement date remains relevant as the date of filing. 3. Self-certified copy furnished after seven days Rule 110(4) shifts the effective filing date to the date on which the copy is actually submitted / uploaded. The consequence can be serious where the appeal is close to limitation. – PRACTICAL RULE Never leave the impugned-order copy pending merely because APL-05 has been submitted. 13 Stage Nine — How Is the Appeal Signed? There are two levels of signatures that should not be confused. Level 1 — Signatures / certification on documents. An affidavit, Vakalatnama, authority, self-certified order or other document that legally requires execution should first be validly signed / executed in its own right, and then converted to PDF and uploaded. Level 2 — Final electronic verification of APL-05. Rule 110(3) refers back to Rule 26 for signing. Rule 26 recognises electronic authentication through Digital Signature Certificate, e-signature or other prescribed electronic verification modes, subject to the requirements applicable to the category of registered person. A company registered under the Companies Act is required to furnish documents / application verified through DSC, subject to the applicable rule. The current GSTAT filing interface provides digital-signing options including the portal-supported DSC / e-sign utilities. Who should perform the final signature? Although the Procedure Rules contain general provisions regarding execution through the appellant / authorised representative, the current GSTAT President’s filing instructions require one verification and digital signature of the appellant. As a filing practice, complete the appeal through the taxpayer account and have the appellant’s duly authorised signatory perform the final portal verification / digital execution. The professional separately executes his Vakalatnama, Memorandum of Appearance or authorisation documentation. Does every page of the paper-book require DSC? No. The present electronic system does not require a DSC to be cryptographically placed on every page merely because the paper-book is in PDF. The better distinction is: Document Signature approach Final APL-05 verification Portal digital signature / e-sign as applicable Vakalatnama Independently executed by client / Advocate CA / CMA / CS authorisation Signed by competent appellant signatory FORM GSTAT-04 Signed / executed as prescribed Affidavit Independently executed / attested as required Self-certified physical document Certification / signature before scanning Native GST portal-generated document Upload the authentic electronic document; unnecessary ritualistic certification should be avoided 14 Stages Ten and Eleven — Checklist, Final Preview and Submission This is the point to stop entering new information and begin verifying consistency. The appeal may first generate Part A of FORM GST APL-02A, but Rule 110 provides that the appeal is treated as filed only when the final acknowledgement indicating the appeal number is issued. Rule 24 contemplates rectification of defects within the prescribed filing process. Typical defects include: Typical defect Typical defect ▪ Missing OIA / OIO / SCN ▪ Unreadable scan ▪ Incomplete Statement of Facts or grounds ▪ Wrong respondent ▪ Missing authorisation / Vakalatnama ▪ Pre-deposit discrepancy ▪ Missing fee ▪ Absence of certification where required ▪ Illegible annexure ▪ Improper pagination / indexing ▪ Missing translation ▪ Mismatch between portal data and uploaded appeal ▪ Failure to upload the impugned order correctly Where a defect memo is generated: log in, open the defect / re-filing module, read each objection, correct the data, replace the PDF or upload the omitted document, respond to the defect and re-submit electronically for Registry re-scrutiny. RELAXATION, NOT LICENCE The current President’s instructions seek to avoid hyper-technical objections where the essential soft copies and requisite filing material have already been supplied; those directions presently stand extended up to 31 December 2026. This relaxation should not, however, be treated as permission to file an incomplete appeal. Final acknowledgement — when filing is complete Once scrutiny is cleared, Part B of FORM GST APL-02A containing the final appeal number is generated. At that stage, preserve a permanent electronic folder: 01_Final_Signed_APL05 06_Provisional_APL02A_PartA 02_Final_Digital_PaperBook 07_Defect_Memo_If_Any 03_Predeposit_Proof 08_Defect_Response_If_Any 04_Appeal_Fee_Receipt 09_Final_APL02A_PartB 05_Authorisation_Vakalatnama This is the correct closure point for the filing process 16 Taxpayer and Representative Accounts — The Correct Relationship A frequent conceptual error is to think that because the CA or Advocate is preparing the appeal, the appeal should originate entirely from the professional’s account. The better understanding of the present GSTAT system: Step Taxpayer Representative Outcome 1 Creates GSTAT account Registers separately on the portal Identity established 2 Initiates APL-05 appeal — Appeal begins in the taxpayer’s case record 3 Adds registered representative Advocate: Vakalatnama · CA: authority · CMA / CS / other: authority FORM GSTAT-04 / representative linkage 4 Appeal continues in the taxpayer’s case record Appears as authorised Valid appearance The GSTAT portal therefore separates identity, authority and filing. That separation should be maintained in office practice as well. Digital paper-book — a suggested professional standard For an appeal of reasonable size, the following electronic architecture works well: Volume I — Pleadings Volume II — Orders and proceedings Volume III onwards — Documentary record Cover · Index · FORM GST APL-05 · Synopsis / Chronology · Statement of Facts · Grounds of Appeal · Prayer · Verification · Authorisation / Vakalatnama · FORM GSTAT-04 Impugned OIA / Revision Order · Order-in-Original · Show Cause Notice · Reply to SCN · Relevant hearing submissions Relied-upon documents · Invoices / returns / reconciliations · Correspondence · Certificates · Statements · Other documents forming part of record – Clear filename › Index › Consecutive pagination › Bookmarks › Readable scan › Sensible file size Every volume should carry all six. A properly instituted second appeal now rests on six digital layers: Figure 8 — The six layers of a properly instituted GSTAT second appeal THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT PROCEDURAL POINT A practitioner who completes only the first four layers has prepared an appeal.
