Articles 2 min read 'An institution may outsource the computation, but it cannot outsource the consequence’: RBI DG Rohit Jain Emerging technologies can make finance faster, cheaper and more accessible, but institutions cannot outsource accountability along with technology, RBI Deputy Governor Rohit Jain said, flagging speed, concentration and opacity as key risks as AI and other technologies deepen their role in finance. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 9, 2026 at 04:13 PM IST var news_msid = 133964900; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India Deputy Governor Rohit Jain said the objective should not merely be to make finance faster or smarter, but to ensure that technology makes finance more useful, resilient and responsive to those it serves.“An institution may outsource the computation, but it cannot outsource the consequence,” Jain said while highlighting the accountability challenges posed by advanced models and third-party technology providers.He said AI is increasingly helping financial institutions interpret information, identify patterns, make predictions and support decisions, thereby augmenting human judgement. The larger opportunity, he said, lies in combining human creativity, judgement, empathy, ethics and purpose with the speed, scale and precision of AI and digital agents. Advt Jain also highlighted the broader transformation underway across financial technology, pointing to tokenisation, distributed technologies, quantum computing, digital identity and interoperable systems. According to him, these technologies are changing not just financial services but also the infrastructure through which finance is delivered.Technology is also reducing the cost of providing finance, which can expand the frontier of what is financially viable and feasible and, in turn, push financial inclusion, Jain said. He also stressed that productivity of the financial sector itself matters because a more productive financial sector contributes to productivity across the wider economy.At the same time, he cautioned that technology does not eliminate traditional financial risks.“Borrowers can still default, liquidity can still disappear, leverage can still magnify losses, and operational failures can still disrupt financial services,” Jain said, adding that technology can instead change the speed, scale and transmission of these risks.On AI and other emerging technologies, Jain identified three specific concerns — speed, concentration and opacity.Automated systems can analyse information and initiate actions far faster than humans, he said. At machine speed, resilience cannot depend only on preventing every error. Institutions must also be able to detect problems early, contain their effects and intervene before a small mistake becomes a much larger one. Advt The second concern is concentration. Financial institutions could increasingly depend on a relatively small number of technology vendors, cloud providers and common technological infrastructure. The risk, therefore, is not limited to the failure of one institution, but that a common dependency could transmit a disruption or error across several institutions at the same time.The third concern is opacity, particularly as advanced models make decisions through relationships that may be difficult to explain. “Greater sophistication, however, cannot mean weaker accountability,” Jain said, adding that customers affected by an important financial decision deserve something more meaningful than simply being told that “the model said so”. Advt He also pointed to quantum computing as an example of a technology where regulators and institutions need to prepare ahead of potential risks. While the technology holds significant promise, it could raise questions around the resilience of existing cryptographic systems.“Preparing in advance reflects a broader principle. We should not wait for a future vulnerability to become a present crisis before responding,” Jain said.
