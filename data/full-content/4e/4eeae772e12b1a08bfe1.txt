How the dispute began The proceedings originated from grievances raised by homebuyers who had invested in residential projects, primarily in Noida, Greater Noida and Gurugram, under subvention schemes launched between 2013 and 2015. Under these arrangements, banks and housing finance companies entered into tripartite agreements with buyers and developers. The lenders disbursed a substantial portion of the sanctioned home loans directly to developers, often upfront, while builders promised to service the EMIs until possession or an agreed date. When several developers defaulted on these payments in 2018 and 2019, lenders began demanding instalments from homebuyers even though many projects remained incomplete and possession had not been handed over. Some buyers also alleged that banks had released 70-80 per cent of the loan amounts to developers without adequately linking disbursements to construction progress, in violation of regulatory requirements. Aggrieved buyers initially approached the Delhi High Court, seeking, among other reliefs, directions to banks to recover the instalments from developers instead of from them. The High Court dismissed the petitions, citing the availability of alternative remedies before the Real Estate Regulatory Authority. The buyers subsequently approached the SC. On March 4, 2025, the SC observed that the circumstances indicated a possible nexus between banks and developers. It noted that despite repeated directions to furnish information about loan disbursements, project completion and recovery proceedings, only nine financial institutions and five developers had filed compliance affidavits at that stage. The court also restrained the execution of recovery certificates against homebuyers while allowing proceedings before debt recovery tribunals to continue. On April 29, 2025, after considering a report submitted by amicus curiae Rajiv Jain, a former Intelligence Bureau director, the court found a prima facie nexus between banks and housing finance companies, on the one hand, and developers, on the other. It directed the CBI to initiate preliminary enquiries and investigate the alleged collusion, including possible violations of Reserve Bank of India and National Housing Bank guidelines governing loan disbursements. The amicus report identified Supertech as a priority for investigation. It found that the developer had entered into subvention arrangements across 21 projects in six cities with 19 banks and housing finance companies, involving approximately 800 homebuyers. Eight institutions figuring prominently in these projects were Indiabulls Housing Finance, PNB Housing Finance, Dewan Housing Finance, HDFC Housing Finance, ICICI Bank, India Infoline Housing Finance, L&T Housing Finance and Aditya Birla Housing Finance. The report also highlighted separate lending exposures involving Corporation Bank, IDBI Bank, Punjab and Sind Bank, Indian Bank and Punjab National Bank. The court subsequently expanded the investigation to cover allegations concerning other developers and financial institutions. The SC’s April 2025 intervention led to the registration of CBI cases to investigate the alleged criminality and identify the roles of officials of financial institutions and developers. The proceedings have since expanded to cover hundreds of petitions involving thousands of homebuyers across several states.
