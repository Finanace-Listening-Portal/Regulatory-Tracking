Articles 2 min read Indian bonds brace for a rough week as US treasury selloff sends yields soaring At the onset of the week, Indian government bonds are likely to encounter significant pressure stemming from the rise in US yields. The noticeable increase in the US 10-year Treasury yield has shifted market sentiments regarding possible changes in monetary policy. Furthermore, with domestic retail inflation surging to 6.09%, which is above the Reserve Bank of India's target, concerns about imminent interest rate hikes in December are becoming prevalent. The sharp selloff in US government debt last week pushed its 10-year yield up nearly 30 basis points, as investors factored in the possibility of a bigger Federal Reserve rate hike.The yield on India's benchmark 6.94% 2036 bond is likely to trade between 7.29% and 7.35% on Monday, a private-bank trader told Reuters. The bond had settled at 7.3079% on Friday.The US 10-year treasury yield closed Friday at around 5.15%, marking a 29-basis-point increase over the week. This was its steepest weekly rise in seven months and came amid growing concerns that the Federal Reserve could announce a larger-than-usual rate hike. Advt The upward movement in US yields followed stronger-than-expected consumer price data for October and hawkish comments from some Federal Reserve members. These developments prompted markets to raise their bets on a bigger rate increase at the Fed's December 9 meeting.Traders are now assigning a 6% probability to a 50-basis-point hike on December 9, according to the CME FedWatch tool.The rise in US yields has also affected domestic bond market sentiment. India's 10-year benchmark yield increased 12 basis points last week, reversing half of the decline recorded during the first week of November."Rising US yields are likely to weigh on sentiment in the local market, especially after the sharp move witnessed in the previous week. Traders will be cautious, and the 10-year bond yield could test fresh highs," the trader said.The domestic inflation numbers have added another concern for the bond market. India's retail inflation accelerated to 6.09% in October, compared with 5.54% in September, crossing the upper tolerance band of the Reserve Bank of India's inflation target.According to STCI Primary Dealer, the latest inflation reading signals upward risks to food inflation, although fuel prices remain controlled for now.The increase in retail inflation has also strengthened expectations of another interest rate hike by the Reserve Bank of India in early December.
