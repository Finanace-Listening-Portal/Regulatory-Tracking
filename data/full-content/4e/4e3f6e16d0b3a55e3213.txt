Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); ITO Vs Baldev Prasad Patel (ITAT Raipur) CIT(A) Must Verify Additional Evidence Before Granting Relief When the AO’s Remand Report Addresses Only Admissibility: ITAT Raipur Raipur ITAT explains the scope of Rule 46A and Section 250 In ITO-2(1), Bilaspur Vs Baldev Prasad Patel, ITA No. 778/RPR/2026, decided on 20 November 2026 for AY 2019–20, the Raipur ITAT held that the CIT(A) could not grant relief on additional evidence that neither the Assessing Officer nor the appellate authority had verified. The Assessing Officer had received the material for examination but confined his remand report to objecting to its admission. The Tribunal set aside the appellate order and directed the CIT(A) to obtain a fresh remand report, make specific inquiries and pass a speaking order under Section 250. The remand report addressed the assessee’s earlier non-compliance The assessee produced additional evidence before the CIT(A)/NFAC. The appellate authority forwarded it to the Assessing Officer under Rule 46A(3) of the Income-tax Rules, 1962 to examine its genuineness and reliability. The Assessing Officer responded that the assessee had been given sufficient opportunities during assessment, had failed to comply with statutory notices and had shown no sufficient cause for withholding the material at that stage. He therefore recommended that the evidence be declined. The report contained no findings on the documents themselves. The CIT(A) recognised that the report had failed to serve its purpose. The appellate order also recorded that the Assessing Officer had earlier challenged the grant of relief without a remand report. Even so, the CIT(A) proceeded to grant relief by order dated 7 August 2026, without recording any specific inquiry or verification of the additional material. The Revenue appealed to the Tribunal. Admissibility and verification are distinct steps under Rule 46A Rule 46A(1) specifies the circumstances in which an appellant may produce evidence before the CIT(A) that was not produced during assessment. These include refusal by the Assessing Officer to admit evidence that ought to have been admitted, sufficient cause preventing its earlier production and inadequate opportunity to adduce relevant evidence. Rule 46A(2) requires the CIT(A) to record reasons in writing for admitting such evidence. Rule 46A(3) gives the Assessing Officer a reasonable opportunity to examine the evidence, cross-examine a witness where applicable, or produce material in rebuttal before the CIT(A) takes the additional evidence into account. The Tribunal held that the decision on admission belongs to the CIT(A). The Assessing Officer’s objection about earlier non-compliance addressed the conditions for admission. Once the documents were referred to him for verification, an objection on that issue alone left the factual inquiry unanswered. The distinction has a practical consequence. A remand report can put the Revenue’s objection to admission on record and also examine the documents on their merits. If the CIT(A) admits the evidence, findings on its genuineness, relevance and consistency with the assessment record give the appellate authority a basis for deciding what weight it deserves. The CIT(A) had the power to make further inquiries The Tribunal also examined the appellate authority’s own role. The CIT(A)’s powers are co-terminus with those of the Assessing Officer. Under Section 250(4), the CIT(A) may make further inquiry or direct the Assessing Officer to inquire and report. Section 250(6) requires the appellate order to state the points for determination, the decision on each point and the reasons for that decision. Here, the CIT(A) had identified the deficiency in the remand report, but the appellate order showed no specific inquiry that addressed it. The material remained untested at the assessment level, and the CIT(A) had recorded no independent verification before allowing the assessee’s appeal. The Tribunal held that the first appellate order therefore failed to satisfy Sections 250(4) and 250(6) in the circumstances of the case. The Revenue’s appeal was allowed for statistical purposes The Tribunal set aside the appellate order and restored the matter to the CIT(A)/NFAC. It directed the appellate authority to call for a remand report on the additional evidence, conduct specific inquiries and pass a reasoned order consistent with natural justice. It also directed the assessee to comply with the hearing notices issued by the first appellate authority. The Revenue’s appeal was allowed for statistical purposes, leaving the substantive tax dispute open for fresh adjudication. Author’s comments The ruling is useful where a remand report consists mainly of a chronology of missed notices. That chronology may help the CIT(A) decide whether the assessee had sufficient cause for producing the evidence late. It does little to answer whether a particular document is genuine or whether it establishes the fact for which the assessee relies on it. The appellate order should show what was verified and how the findings affected each disputed issue. If a document supports only part of the assessee’s explanation, the reasons should identify that part and explain the treatment of the balance. In Baldev Prasad Patel, the grant of relief was set aside because the necessary verification and findings were missing. The outcome now depends on what the additional evidence establishes when it is examined. FULL TEXT OF THE ORIGINAL JUDGMENT/ORDER The present appeal preferred by the Revenue emanates from the order of the Ld.CIT(Appeals)/NFAC, Delhi dated 07.08.2026 for the assessment year 2019-20 as per the grounds of appeal on record. 2. At the very outset, even without going into the merits of the matter, it is observed from the order of the Ld.CIT(Appeals)/NFAC that certain additional evidences had been filed by the assessee before the First Appellate Authority and the said additional evidences were forwarded to the A.O in terms with Rule 46A(3) of the IT Rules, 1962. However, though these additional evidences were submitted to the A.O for determining the genuinity and veracity of the additional evidences but the A.O instead of commenting on the genuinity of the additional evidences had written in the remand report that since the assessee had not complied before the A.O in spite of various opportunities and had not filed these evidences, hence, the same should not be admitted. The relevant findings of the A.O is extracted as follows: “5. The written submission and additional evidences for the A.Y. 2019-20 before your honour by the assessee were gone through. It is pertinent to n here that during the entire assessment proceedings, the assessee did made any response to the statutory notices issued to him. Further, it is mentioned here that Section 46A of the Act does not attract in this case as in spite of providing various opportunities, no proper compliance was made during the assessment proceedings, For the sake of clarification, the section u/s.46A is reproduced as under: “(1) The appellant shall not be entitled to produce before the before the Deputy Commissioner (Appeals) or as the case may be, the Commissioner (Appeals), any evidence. whether oral or documentary, other than the evidence produced by him during the course of proceedings before the Assessing Officer. Except in the following circumstances, namely:- (a.) where the Assessing Officer has refused to admit evidence which ought to have been admitted; or (b.) where the appellant was prevented by sufficient cause from producing the evidence which he was called upon to produce by the Assessing Officer; or (c.) where the appellant was prevented by sufficient cause from producing before the Assessing Officer any evidence which is relevant to any ground of appeal; or (d.) Where the Assessing Officer had made the order appealed against without giving sufficient opportunity to the appellant to adduce evidence relevant to any ground of appeal. (2) No evidence shall be admitted under sub-rule (1) unless the Deputy Commissioner (Appeals) or as the case may be, the Commissioner (Appeals) records in writing the reasons for its admission. (3) The Deputy Commissioner (Appeals) or as the case may be, the Commissioner (Appeals) shall not take into account any evidence produced under sub-rule (1) unless the Assessing Officer has been allowed a reasonable opportunity- (a.) To examine the evidence or document or to cross-examine the witness produced by the appellant, or (b.) To produce any evidence or document or any witness in rebuttal of the additional evidence produced by the appellant. (4) Nothing contained in this rule shall affect the power of the Deputy Commissioner(Appeals) or as the case may be, the Commissioner (Appeals) to direct the production of any document, or the examination of any witness, to enable him to dispose of the appeal, or for any other substantial cause including the enhancement of the assessment or penalty (whether on his own motion or on the request of the Assessing Officer under clause (a) of sub-section (1) of section 251 or the imposition of ,,malty under section 271.” 6. Sufficient opportunities were given to the assessee during the assessment proceedings. The assessee was not prevented by sufficient cause from producing the evidence which was called upon to produce. Therefore, additional evidence may not be accepted at this stage. Remand report is submitted for perusal and kind consideration.” 3. We are of the considered view that such kind of findings by the A.O defeats the purpose of law as per Rule 46A(3) of the IT Rules, 1962 and defeats the purpose of functioning of a quasi-judicial authority since as per the mandate of the various Higher Forums quasi-judicial authority needs to consider all the evidences and submissions of the assessee before coming to a certain conclusion. 4. In the present case, the additional evidences were provided to the A.O to find out veracity and genuineness of the same after due enquiry. Instead of doing the same, the A.O stated that since the assessee did not comply at the assessment stage, he does not get any further chance to file all these evidences before the First Appellate Authority. Law provides power to the First Appellate Authority to admit additional evidences in terms with Rule 46A(3) of the IT Rules, 1962. It is not for the A.O to comment whether the First Appellate Authority will admit such additional evidences or not and it is beyond his jurisdiction. In fact, the Ld. CIT(Appeals)/NFAC writes at Para 8 as follows: “8. The remand report submitted by the AO does not serve the purpose of this appeal. When the AO challenged the order of CIT (Appeal) before Hon’ble ITAT that the appellate authority granted relief without calling for remand report, he ought to have examined the additional evidence forwarded to the AO as per the direction of Hon’ble ITAT….” 5. Thereafter, the Ld. CIT(Appeals)/NFAC goes to provide relief to the assessee. But the fact of the matter is that those additional evidences furnished still remains to be examined at the ground level by the A.O. There is no finding on these additional evidences by the A.O on record. The substantive rights and liabilities of the parties herein are yet to be determined. The Ld. CIT(Appeals)/NFAC had passed order without conducting any enquiry or verification and its power is co-terminus with that of the A.O and nothing is emanating from its order that it had done specific enquiry and verification before coming to a particular reasoning allowing the appeal of the assessee. Therefore, order passed by the Ld. CIT(Appeals)/NFAC is not in terms with Section 250(4) & (6) of the Act. 6. Considering all these aspects in totality, we are of the considered view that the order of the Ld. CIT(Appeals)/NFAC has to be set aside back to its file for calling for a remand report from the A.O on the additional evidences, making specific enquiry in terms with Section 250(4) & (6) of the Act and then come out with a speaking order complying with the principles of natural justice. At the same time, the assessee shall comply with the hearing notices from the office of the First Appellate Authority. We order accordingly. 7. In the result, appeal of the Revenue is allowed for statistical purposes. Order pronounced in the open court on 20th November, 2026.
