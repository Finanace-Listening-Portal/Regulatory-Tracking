While lenders have expanded their reach beyond traditional markets and into new borrower segments, early repayment stress has begun to rise, according to a report by CRIF Credit Information Services Pvt Ltd. Gold loans outstanding grew 60.6 per cent year-on-year to ₹2.33 trillion as of September 2026, up from ₹1.45 trillion a year earlier and ₹1.07 trillion in September 2024. Active loan accounts grew at a slower pace, rising 14 per cent year-on-year to 102 million. Gold loans now account for 12.6 per cent of India’s ₹185.2 trillion retail credit market, a 3.3 percentage point increase over a year. The divergence between loan value and account growth points to borrowers pledging gold to secure larger amounts, even as credit access expands. The report also flags a sequential increase in early delinquencies, suggesting that lenders will need to monitor repayment behaviour as the market grows. Larger loans drive gold loan growth despite lower origination volumes Gold loan originations rose 27.2 per cent year-on-year to ₹81,500 crore in Q2 FY27, even as volumes fell 4.1 per cent to 36.94 million accounts. The average ticket size rose to ₹2.2 lakh, up 32.7 per cent year-on-year. The shift towards bigger loans is particularly visible in higher-ticket segments.
