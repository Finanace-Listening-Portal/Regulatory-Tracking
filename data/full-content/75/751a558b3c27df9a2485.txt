The potential shift in payment behaviour is likely to be more pronounced in sectors such as mobile and electronics retail, where most transactions are above the new threshold.The MDR will apply from October 15 to specified person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, with the charge capped at Rs 300 for transactions of Rs 75,000 and above. However, small merchants receiving up to Rs 1 lakh a month through UPI QR codes directly into their bank accounts will continue under the zero-MDR framework, including for individual transactions above Rs 2,000. About 96 per cent of P2M transactions will remain unaffected, according to the government. Advt The potential shift towards cash is already emerging as a concern among retailers ahead of the festive season. Retail industry bodies have warned that the additional cost could make merchants reconsider accepting UPI for larger purchases, with cash potentially becoming an alternative for transactions above Rs 2,000.The exposure is likely to vary sharply across retail categories. Around 97-98 per cent of transactions at mobile phone and retail stores are currently above Rs 2,000, according to industry estimates, while UPI accounts for around 35-40 per cent of transactions in FMCG. In the latter segment, payments above Rs 2,000 are largely associated with monthly shopping in tier-1 and tier-2 cities.Margin impactThe margin impact could also be significant for businesses operating on thin profitability. Industry estimates put net margins for FMCG kiranas at around 1.5-2 per cent, while the 0.4 per cent MDR would apply directly to the value of eligible UPI transactions rather than to the retailer's profit. This makes payment costs a more material consideration for merchants with a high proportion of eligible UPI sales.Retailers could therefore seek to reduce their MDR exposure by encouraging customers to make direct bank transfers or use cash for larger purchases. The extent of such a shift, however, will depend on customer preferences and the share of transactions that fall outside the zero-MDR categories. Advt Merchants cannot directly recover the MDR from customers as a separate UPI charge. Banks have been advised to ensure that the MDR is not passed on to customers, while UPI application providers are prohibited from imposing platform fees or hidden charges on users.The new framework therefore creates a distinction between merchants largely protected by the small-merchant exemption and businesses with larger UPI collections. For the latter, especially those with high-value transactions, the MDR could create an incentive to reconsider the mix of UPI, cash and bank-transfer payments from October 15.
