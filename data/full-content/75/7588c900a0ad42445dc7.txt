A suspected transaction is defined as one of Rs 1,000 or more that is flagged by the bank's monitoring systems, including AI and machine learning tools, as potentially involving proceeds of cyber-enabled financial fraud or money mule activity. The draft RBI (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026, issued following a Supreme Court order dated August 4, 2026, would take effect from April 1, 2027, or earlier if a bank chooses to implement them. Advt Under the proposed standard operating procedure, a bank can impose a temporary debit hold suo moto when its transaction monitoring systems identify a suspected money mule transaction. A suspected transaction is defined as one of Rs 1,000 or more that is flagged by the bank's monitoring systems, including AI and machine learning tools, as potentially involving proceeds of cyber-enabled financial fraud or money mule activity.The Rs 1,000 threshold does not mean that every transaction above that amount would be frozen. The transaction must first be identified as suspicious based on indicators such as being unusual or disproportionate to the customer's profile, or being linked to an account already reported as a mule or fraudulent account.Banks would have to immediately place a hold on the suspected transaction or, in cases involving a suspected mule account, the entire account. Account-level holds are to be used only as a last resort and in exceptional circumstances.The customer would have to be informed of the hold, the reasons for it, the process for its removal and the details of the designated officer handling the case. Banks would also have to seek an explanation or justification from the customer, who would get 20 days from the date of the hold to respond.Where an explanation is received, the bank would have to examine it and take a decision within 10 days of receiving it. If no explanation is received, the bank would have to take a decision within 30 days of the temporary debit hold. Advt If the bank is not satisfied with the customer's explanation, it would refer the matter to the jurisdictional police authority through the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal's Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System, along with the reasons for continuing the hold. The customer would also have to be informed.Law enforcement or another competent authority could direct the bank to continue the hold during the process. Banks would have to comply immediately with such directions received within the prescribed period.If no instruction requiring continuation of the hold is received within 30 days from the date of reference, the bank would have to remove the hold on the 31st day from the date of reference and notify the customer. In the absence of a contrary instruction, the overall temporary debit hold cannot continue beyond 60 days from the date on which it was imposed.Wider implementation Advt The proposed directions would apply to commercial banks, including small finance banks, payments banks, regional rural banks and local area banks, as well as urban cooperative banks.Banks would also have to continue filing suspicious transaction reports where required. The proposed SOP does not alter their existing obligations under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act and related KYC requirements. If an account is established to be a money mule account but the bank has not filed an STR, the draft treats this as non-compliance.Banks would be required to frame internal policies covering technology solutions, objective parameters for identifying suspicious activity, procedures for imposing and removing holds, customer communications, integration with the NCRP-CFCFRMS system and grievance redressal. They would also have to maintain centralised records of holds, customer communications, law enforcement references and subsequent decisions. Advt Records relating to temporary debit holds would have to be retained for at least five years from the date of the hold. Where an account is subsequently closed, the records would have to be retained for at least 10 years from the date of closure.The framework also provides for enhanced monitoring of accounts subject to such action and other active accounts or relationships held by the same customer. Banks would have to designate nodal officers at regional, zonal and head-office levels and resolve customer complaints relating to the process within 30 days.
