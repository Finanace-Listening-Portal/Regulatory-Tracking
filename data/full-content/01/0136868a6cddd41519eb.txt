Vasudevan said he resigned over persistent investor concerns around a perceived conflict involving his daughter’s NBFC, while his own holding was around 0.1 per cent and his daughter had earlier held around 1.5 per cent in the bank. The concerns relate to a perceived conflict arising from his daughter's business interests, with her housing finance company having received a Reserve Bank of India licence in 2023. Advt Vasudevan’s daughter, Varshini Vasudevan Pathangi, is the co-promoter/founder of Unico Housing Finance, an unlisted NBFC.The bank's management said the Nominations and Remuneration Committee (NRC) has been asked to start the process and the lender is evaluating both internal and external candidates. Agencies have also been contacted and asked to provide a list of potential candidates.“With regard to the timelines, we have contacted the agencies and asked for the list of candidates which possibly we can look at and, as I said earlier, the NRC has already been asked to start the process, put the process in place. So we don't expect it to take more than a month,” management said during the analyst call.“We in fact expect it to take a bit earlier, but nevertheless, we are taking it as one month as a normal part of the process,” management added.Vasudevan will continue to discharge his responsibilities until his successor takes over.As of now, the bank does not expect an overlap between Vasudevan and his successor, even if the eventual candidate is from outside the bank.“So far as the overlap is concerned, we will see. As of today, we don't see any need for an overlap, even if it's an external candidate. In case it comes to that, we will take a view at that point in time, but as of today, we don't see a need for an overlap,” management said. Advt Vasudevan also said he does not have a hard stop date for his exit and will continue until the transition is completed.No RBI nudge, says VasudevanVasudevan's resignation comes around three months after his reappointment, with investors having repeatedly raised concerns over a perceived conflict linked to his daughter's business.He said the issue had been discussed for the past two to three years and that there was no specific trigger behind his decision to step down.“This has been an issue which has been going on for the last two, three years now, so someday it had to happen, so it's happened today. So that's it. I mean, there is no particular trigger. Just that this has been going on for quite some time and there has been a lot of pushback from some of the investors that I have been meeting over a period of time on this issue of the perception,” Vasudevan said. Advt He said investors had repeatedly questioned how the perceived conflict could be managed, even though he believed there was no actual conflict.“Every time I meet investors, people have asked me. I try to explain my best, but at the end of it, it always ends up by the investors saying that while in reality there may be no conflict, the perception is still very strong and how do you handle that perception? How will you be able to manage that perception, which is difficult because you don't know who is thinking what in their mind,” Vasudevan said.Asked specifically whether there had been a regulatory nudge or whether he felt his services would be required by the NBFC promoted by his family, Vasudevan categorically ruled out regulatory pressure. Advt “There is no nudge or anything from the regulator. That's clear. And so, there's nothing as far as the regulator is concerned,” Vasudevan said.Vasudevan's reappointment and the licence for the housing finance company run by his daughter were legitimate and RBI-approved arrangements. Vasudevan, however, said the investor-perception issue had persisted despite that.Vasudevan holds around 0.1%; daughter had 1.5% in 2022On his own holding in Equitas, Vasudevan said his shareholding was “very little” and could be around 0.1 per cent, although he was not certain of the exact figure.“My shareholding will be very little. I don't really know. It may be 0.1 per cent or something like that, but actually I don't know,” he said.He added that his daughter had held around 1.5 per cent in Equitas in 2022, but subsequently exited the holding and put it into an MTSD.“My shareholding in the bank always was very little, but my daughter had around 1.5 per cent in the bank, which she had exited and put into an MTSD,” Vasudevan said.Vasudevan also said that he does not expect to return to the CEO role if the succession process faces difficulty this time.Asked whether he could come back and resume the role if a successor could not be appointed within the expected timeframe, he said, “No, I don't think that situation will arise this time.”Equitas SFB opened at Rs 66 on Thursday against the previous close of Rs 65.13 and ended at Rs 57.17, down 12.22 per cent; the stock hit the 20 per cent lower circuit at Rs 52.11 during the session.
