Summary: Filecoin is an active blockchain-based storage network in which storage providers commit capacity, prove that data has been replicated and remains stored, and use FIL for collateral, payments, rewards and network fees. The protocol is not a cloud-storage guarantee: a client still needs to choose providers, manage deal terms, preserve retrieval paths and protect wallet credentials. Filecoin mainnet completed network version 29, called Solstice, on 19 October 2026, confirming continuing protocol development after the May 2026 Fire Horse upgrade. This guide explains Proof of Replication, Proof of Spacetime, storage power, built-in actors, the Filecoin Virtual Machine, finality, FIL custody and the distinction between storing data and merely holding the token. For an Indian taxpayer, FIL is a crypto-asset within the virtual digital asset framework. Transfer income, withholding, loss restrictions, records and platform compliance must therefore be reviewed separately from the network technology. Nothing here predicts price, storage demand, provider profitability or investment returns. A storage provider pledges sectors, accepts or publishes storage commitments and submits cryptographic evidence. The chain can verify those commitments without storing every client file inside each block. This distinction matters: blockchain consensus records commitments and proofs, while the underlying data sits with storage infrastructure operated by providers. Buying FIL does not itself purchase a storage contract, create a copy of a file or ensure that a particular dataset can be retrieved. Roles for clients providers and applications A client chooses data, terms and provider arrangements. A storage provider supplies capacity and must satisfy protocol obligations. Applications can arrange deals, monitor sectors or use smart-contract logic through the Filecoin Virtual Machine. Retrieval may involve separate delivery mechanisms and operational choices. Users should therefore identify which layer is failing when a problem occurs: wallet signing, message inclusion, deal publication, sector sealing, proof submission, provider availability, retrieval transport or an application interface. A token transfer confirmation does not resolve a missing-data or retrieval dispute. Proof of Spacetime addresses whether that encoded data continued to be stored over a stated period. The concepts solve different questions and should not be collapsed into a generic claim that data is safe. A valid initial replication proof does not remove the need for later proofs, and later on-chain evidence does not replace the client’s own redundancy, encryption, access control or recovery plan. Window proofs faults and slashing The protocol divides each 24-hour period into proof windows and assigns a provider’s sectors across those windows. Providers must submit Window Proof-of-Spacetime for challenged sectors. A missed submission can register a fault, reduce power and expose pledged collateral to penalties. Winning Proof-of-Spacetime is used when selected providers produce blocks. These mechanisms create economic consequences for protocol non-performance, but the amount of collateral or a possible penalty should not be described as conventional deposit insurance, a sovereign guarantee or compensation automatically payable to an end user. FIL collateral fees and network economics Providers lock FIL as collateral for commitments and may earn block rewards and storage-related payments. Clients and applications pay FIL for network messages and may agree storage economics through relevant markets or contracts. Built-in actors account for balances, storage power, provider state and market functions. Gas is charged for computation and state changes. A storage provider’s business result depends on hardware, power, sealing, maintenance, deal flow, proof reliability, FIL price and protocol rules. The same token therefore supports several functions, but none creates a fixed yield or assured cash flow for a token holder. Collateral can be returned when obligations conclude correctly and reduced when specified promises are broken. That design aligns incentives at protocol level, yet users remain exposed to software defects, economic parameter changes, provider concentration, inaccessible keys, bridge or exchange failures and legal restrictions. An Indian resident who acquires FIL through a platform is not thereby becoming a storage provider. Conversely, a provider may face operational and tax questions beyond the treatment of a simple token transfer. Solstice confirms an active changing protocol October 2026 network version 29 Filecoin’s public status record shows that mainnet network version 29, Solstice, completed on 19 October 2026. That evidence supports treating Filecoin as active rather than an abandoned candidate. It also shows why wallet, node and application compatibility must be checked against current network versions. An upgrade can change supported message formats, actor versions, gas behaviour or implementation requirements. Users should not assume that a wallet build, custodial integration or operational guide from an earlier network version remains sufficient. Fire Horse and storage-aware smart contracts The prior NV28 Fire Horse upgrade went live in May 2026. Official project material describes storage-state visibility for smart contracts, passkey-related account capabilities and a revised gas mechanism. These features expand what applications can build, but they do not eliminate smart-contract or authentication risk. Passkeys can improve user experience, while recovery design and device security still matter. Storage-aware contracts can examine on-chain sector conditions, while data correctness, provider choice and the contract’s own logic remain separate questions. Wallet custody exchange and application risks FIL can be held in a self-custody wallet or through a custodian or exchange. Self-custody makes the user responsible for the seed phrase, signing device, address verification, backups and inheritance. Custodial holding adds counterparty, withdrawal, cyber-security and insolvency exposure. Network addresses and transaction messages should be checked carefully because a validly signed transfer is generally not reversed merely because the user copied the wrong address. A test transfer can reduce operational error but does not make a counterparty or smart contract safe. Bridged or wrapped representations are not automatically identical to native FIL. A bridge can add smart-contract, validator and liquidity risks, and a platform ticker may conceal which network is used for deposit or withdrawal. Users should verify the exact asset, native network, minimum amount, memo or address requirements and confirmation status. Storage applications add another layer: client data may require encryption before upload, multiple providers, monitoring and an independent backup. Confidential material should not be exposed merely because the storage proof is public and verifiable. Indian VDA tax reporting for FIL Transfer income and restricted deductions The Indian tax framework treats a crypto-asset represented on a cryptographically secured distributed ledger as a virtual digital asset. Income from transfer is taxed at the special 30% rate, plus applicable surcharge and cess, with only cost of acquisition allowed under the special computation rule. A loss from transfer of one VDA cannot be set off against income from another VDA and cannot be carried forward under that regime. Transaction-wise records should capture acquisition date, transfer date, units, rupee values, consideration, cost and the platform or wallet trail. Schedule VDA reports each transfer rather than only a single annual net number. One percent withholding and non-sale events A 1% withholding obligation can apply to consideration on transfer of a VDA under the current provision, subject to the statutory thresholds, responsible-person rules and exchange guidelines. TDS is not the final tax and does not establish profit. Storage payments, provider rewards, token distributions, swaps, transfers to another person and business receipts may require separate characterization. Moving native FIL between wallets beneficially owned by the same person may differ from a transfer for consideration, but records are still important. GST, business-income and FEMA questions depend on the exact service, counterparties and cross-border facts and should not be inferred from the income-tax rate alone. Frequently Asked Questions Does Filecoin store files directly inside every block? No. The blockchain records state, deals, messages and proofs. Providers operate the storage infrastructure holding the encoded data. What is the difference between PoRep and PoSt? Proof of Replication addresses creation of a unique encoded copy, while Proof of Spacetime provides continuing evidence that storage remains committed. Why must a storage provider lock FIL? Collateral supports the provider’s protocol commitments and can be exposed to penalties when specified obligations are not met. Is FIL ownership a claim on storage-provider revenue? No. FIL is a network token. Holding it does not create equity, a deposit, a fixed return or a contractual share of provider revenue. What happened on 19 October 2026? Filecoin mainnet completed network version 29, known as Solstice, according to the official network status record. Can an Indian taxpayer set off a FIL transfer loss against another VDA gain? The special VDA regime generally prohibits set-off of a transfer loss against VDA income and prohibits carry-forward of that loss. Does one percent TDS settle the final FIL tax? No. Withholding is a tax-credit and reporting mechanism. Final income is computed under the applicable VDA provisions using the taxpayer’s actual transactions.
