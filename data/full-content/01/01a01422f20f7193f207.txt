Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Satya Narayan Bairwa Vs ITO (ITAT Jaipur) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Jaipur Bench, allowed two appeals filed by Shri Satya Narayan Bairwa against the orders of the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), Kota, dated 1 March 2018, for Assessment Years 2008-09 and 2009-10. The dispute concerned reassessment proceedings initiated under Sections 147 and 148 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, and additions of ₹50,65,000 and ₹1,15,00,500, respectively, under Section 69A on account of unexplained cash deposits in savings bank accounts. The assessee, who claimed to derive income from agriculture and labour, had not filed income-tax returns on the ground that his income was below the taxable limit. The Assessing Officer issued reassessment notices dated 24 March 2015 and 29 March 2016 for the respective assessment years and subsequently completed ex parte assessments under Sections 144 and 147. The CIT(A) upheld the additions, following which the assessee challenged the reassessments before the Tribunal. The assessee principally contended that the reassessment notices were issued after the expiry of four years from the end of the respective assessment years without obtaining valid approval from the competent authority prescribed under Section 151(1). For AY 2008-09, the approval had admittedly been granted by the Additional Commissioner of Income Tax, Range-2, Jaipur, rather than the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner. For AY 2009-10, the assessee questioned the approval conveyed through a letter signed by the Income Tax Officer (Technical and Judicial), alleging that the prescribed authority had not independently recorded the required satisfaction. The assessee also objected to consolidated approval letters covering 70 and 56 assessees, respectively, and alleged mechanical sanction without application of mind. Reliance was placed on several judicial precedents, including Dhadha Exports v. ITO, CIT v. SPL’s Siddhartha Ltd., Ghanshyam K. Khabrani v. ACIT, Principal CIT v. N.C. Cables Ltd. and CIT v. S. Goyanka Lime & Chemicals Ltd., to argue that approval by an incompetent authority or mechanical satisfaction invalidated the reassessment proceedings. For AY 2008-09, the Tribunal examined the assessment records and the Assessing Officer’s report dated 9 April 2021, which confirmed that the notice under Section 148 had been issued after four years and that approval had been obtained from the Additional Commissioner rather than the authority specified under Section 151(1). The Tribunal observed that the statutory requirement of satisfaction by the Principal Chief Commissioner, Chief Commissioner, Principal Commissioner or Commissioner was mandatory in the circumstances of the case. Referring particularly to Principal CIT v. N.C. Cables Ltd., it emphasised that approval must reflect independent application of mind and cannot be treated as a mere procedural formality. Since the sanction had not been granted by the competent authority, the Tribunal held that the reassessment notice and consequential assessment were invalid. It accordingly quashed the reassessment proceedings for AY 2008-09 without adjudicating the remaining grounds relating to the cash deposit addition, opportunity of hearing and interest. Regarding AY 2009-10, the Tribunal considered the corresponding challenge to the reassessment notice dated 29 March 2016 and the disputed sanction under Section 151. The assessee had specifically relied on the approval communication issued by the Income Tax Officer (Technical and Judicial), the consolidated approval covering 56 assessees and judicial decisions concerning the mandatory nature of competent-authority satisfaction. The Tribunal applied the legal principles governing reassessment sanction and allowed the second appeal as well. Consequently, the reassessment proceedings for both assessment years were quashed, and the additions of ₹50,65,000 and ₹1,15,00,500 could not survive. The decision reinforces that compliance with the statutory approval requirements under Section 151 is a jurisdictional prerequisite for valid reassessment proceedings and that defective sanction cannot be overlooked merely because the Assessing Officer alleges unexplained cash deposits. It is important to mention here that Section 151 of the Act provides that no notice shall be issued U/s 148 of the Act by an A.O. after expiry of a period of four years unless the PCIT or CCIT or CIT is satisfied. For ready reference, we reproduce Section 151 of the Act as under: “151(1) No notice shall be issued under section 148 by an Assessing Officer, after the expiry of a period of four years from the end of the relevant assessment year, unless the Principal Chief Commissioner or Chief Commissioner or Principal Commissioner or Commissioner is satisfied, on the reasons recorded by the Assessing Officer, that it is a fit case for the issue of such notice. 151(2) In a case other than a case falling under section 151(1), no notice shall be issued under section 148 by an Assessing Officer, who is below the rank of Joint Commissioner, unless the Joint Commissioner is satisfied, on the reasons recorded by such Assessing Officer, that it is a fit case for the issue of such notice. 151(3) For the purposes of sub-section (1) and sub-section (2), the Principal Commissioner or the Chief Commissioner or the Principal Commissioner or the Commissioner or the Joint Commissioner, as the case may be, being satisfied on the reasons recorded by the Assessing officer about fitness or a case for the issue of notice under section 148, need not issue such notice himself.” From perusal of provisions of Section 151 of the Act, we observed that notice u/s 148 of the Act after four years can be issued only after obtaining satisfaction, sanction or approval u/s 151(1) from the CIT or Pr. CABLES LTD.(2017) 98 CCH 0018 Del HC, wherein it has been held as under: “that Reassessment—Issuance of Notice—Sanction for issue of Notice—Assessee had in its return for AY 2001-02 claimed that sum of Rs. 1 Crore was received towards share application amounts and a further sum of Thirty Five Lakhs was credited to it as an advance towards loan—Original assessment was completed u/s 143(3)—However, pursuant to reassessment notice, which was dropped due to technical reasons, and later notice was issued and assessments were taken up afresh—After considering submissions of assessee and documents produced in reassessment proceedings, AO added back a sum of Rs.1,35,00,000—CIT(A) held against assessee on legality of reassessment notice but allowed assessee’s appeal on merits holding that AO did not conduct appropriate enquiry to conclude that share inclusion and advances received were from bogus entities—Tribunal allowed assessee’s appeal on merits—Revenue appealed against appellate order on merits—Assessee’s cross appeal was on correctness of reopening of assessment— Tribunal upheld assessee’s cross-objections and dismissed Revenue’s appeal holding that there was no proper application of mind by concerned sanctioning authority u/s Section 151 as a pre-condition for issuing notice u/s 147/148—Held, Section 151 stipulates that CIT (A), who was competent authority to authorize reassessment notice, had to apply his mind and form opinion—Mere appending of expression ‘approved’ says nothing—It was not as if CIT (A) had to record elaborate reasons for agreeing with noting put up—At same time, satisfaction had to be recorded of given case which could be reflected in briefest possible manner—In present case, exercise appears to have been ritualistic and formal rather than meaningful, which was rationale for safeguard of approval by higher ranking officer—Revenue’s appeal dismissed.” 12.
