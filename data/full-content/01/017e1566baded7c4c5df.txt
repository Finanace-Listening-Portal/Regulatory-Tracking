Vs ACIT (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madras High Court, in a common judgment dated 29 December 2022, decided ten connected tax case appeals involving L&T Infrastructure Development Projects Ltd., formerly Narmada Infrastructure Construction Enterprises Ltd., and LTIDPL INDVIT Services Ltd., formerly L&T Western India Toll Bridge Ltd. The principal controversy concerned whether roads and bridges constructed, operated and maintained under Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) concession agreements with government authorities qualified for depreciation under Section 32 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The appeals covered assessment years 2001-02 to 2005-06 and involved competing claims for depreciation as plant and machinery, buildings or intangible assets. The assessees maintained that the infrastructure assets and commercial rights arising from the concession arrangements qualified for depreciation, while the Income Tax Department disputed their eligibility, particularly because ownership of the underlying public infrastructure remained with the Government. The two assessee companies had been incorporated as special purpose vehicles for developing and operating toll infrastructure in Gujarat. L&T Infrastructure Development Projects Ltd. entered into a concession agreement dated 21 November 1997 for constructing and operating a toll bridge across the Narmada River on National Highway 8 for a concession period of fifteen years, including construction. LTIDPL INDVIT Services Ltd. entered into another concession agreement dated 1 March 1999 for a two-lane toll bridge across the Watrak River near Kheda on the Ahmedabad-Vadodara stretch of National Highway 8 for ten years and eight months. Under these arrangements, the concessionaires incurred construction expenditure and recovered their investments, together with a potential commercial return, through toll collections during the stipulated concession periods. Although the expenditure was capitalised and amortised in their books, the companies claimed depreciation under the Income-tax Act at rates applicable to plant and machinery. The assessment proceedings resulted in different decisions concerning depreciation entitlement and applicable rates. In the appeals concerning L&T Infrastructure Development Projects Ltd. for assessment years 2001-02 to 2003-04, the Assessing Officer treated the bridge as a building and restricted depreciation to 10%, whereas the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) accepted its classification as plant and granted higher depreciation. The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal reversed the appellate decision, observing that a bridge forming an integral part of a national highway could not be treated as plant merely because it was used commercially. In the connected appeals relating to LTIDPL INDVIT Services Ltd., the Tribunal generally treated toll roads and bridges as buildings eligible for depreciation at 10%. Some proceedings also concerned reassessments under Sections 147 and 148 and revisionary jurisdiction exercised by the Commissioner under Section 263. Consequently, both the assessees and the Revenue challenged the Tribunal’s decisions before the High Court. On behalf of the assessees, it was argued that the toll bridges and roads constituted plant because they represented the principal income-generating apparatus of their infrastructure businesses. Alternatively, the rights acquired under the concession agreements, particularly the exclusive right to develop, operate and collect toll from specified infrastructure facilities, were claimed to be depreciable intangible assets in the nature of licences or business or commercial rights under Section 32(1)(ii), read with Explanation 3. Reliance was placed on decisions including Techno Shares & Stocks Ltd. Vs CIT, Mysore Minerals Ltd. Vs CIT, and decisions relating to depreciation on infrastructure assets. The assessees also challenged reassessment proceedings and revisionary orders by invoking judicial principles concerning change of opinion, ownership, examination of records and the jurisdictional conditions governing Section 263. They contended that where an Assessing Officer had adopted a permissible interpretation after enquiry, the Commissioner could not revise the assessment merely because another interpretation was possible. The Revenue contended that national highways, public roads and bridges constructed under BOT arrangements remained government-owned infrastructure and could not become depreciable assets of private concessionaires. The Government had merely permitted the companies to recover their construction and maintenance expenditure through toll collections over a contractually specified period. Such contractual permission did not confer ownership of the roads or bridges, nor did it necessarily create an independently depreciable commercial right. The Department relied particularly on North Karnataka Expressway Ltd. Vs CIT and CIT Vs West Gujarat Expressway Ltd., in which the Bombay High Court had rejected depreciation claims involving government-owned BOT infrastructure. It also disputed the classification of such infrastructure as buildings or plant and supported the Commissioner’s revisionary jurisdiction where the assessment had accepted an unsustainable depreciation claim. The High Court examined the statutory requirement of ownership under Section 32 and the legal character of BOT concession agreements. It held that roads and bridges forming part of national or state highways were public infrastructure and were never intended to be transferred to the concessionaires. The contractual right to collect toll represented a mechanism for recovering construction and maintenance costs, with the possibility of earning profit, rather than ownership of the infrastructure. The Court distinguished CIT Vs Podar Cement Pvt. Ltd., observing that the principles concerning beneficial ownership of property under arrangements contemplating eventual transfer could not be applied to BOT infrastructure that was never intended to become the private property of the concessionaire. The Court consequently concluded that the toll roads and bridges did not constitute tangible assets owned by the respective assessees for the purposes of depreciation. It expressly preferred the approach adopted by the Bombay High Court in North Karnataka Expressway Ltd. and West Gujarat Expressway Ltd. over the Delhi High Court’s contrary reasoning in Moradabad Toll Road Co. Ltd. Vs ACIT. Addressing the alternative claim concerning intangible assets, the High Court held that the rights granted under the concession agreements did not amount to depreciable licences or business or commercial rights contemplated by Explanation 3(b) to Section 32. The Court considered the nature of the concession arrangements and concluded that the toll collection privilege constituted consideration for developing and maintaining public infrastructure, rather than an independently acquired intangible asset qualifying for statutory depreciation. The assessees therefore could not claim depreciation on the infrastructure as plant and machinery, as buildings, or on the concession rights as intangible assets. The Court also rejected the Tribunal’s approach of allowing 10% depreciation by treating the relevant roads and bridges as buildings. Regarding revision under Section 263, it observed that the Commissioner had remitted the question of depreciation for fresh determination without finally deciding the merits, and that the Tribunal had erred in treating the original depreciation allowance as a legally plausible view. In its operative order, the Madras High Court dismissed all five appeals filed by the assessees and allowed all five appeals filed by the Income Tax Department. T.C.A. Nos. 868 to 870 of 2009, involving L&T Infrastructure Development Projects Ltd. for assessment years 2001-02 to 2003-04, were dismissed. The assessee’s appeals in T.C.A. Nos. 253 of 2013 and 206 of 2014 were also dismissed. Conversely, the Revenue’s appeals in T.C.A. Nos. 756 and 1201 of 2010, 339 of 2013, and 358 and 841 of 2014 were allowed, with the corresponding Tribunal orders set aside. No costs were awarded. The decision establishes, on the concession arrangements examined by the Court, that construction expenditure and the contractual entitlement to collect toll do not by themselves establish ownership of depreciable infrastructure or acquisition of qualifying intangible assets under Section 32. The ruling is particularly significant for infrastructure concessionaires claiming tax depreciation on public road and bridge projects developed under BOT arrangements.
