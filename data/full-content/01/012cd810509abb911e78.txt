Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Saffron Art Private Ltd (GST AAR Maharashtra) The Maharashtra Authority for Advance Ruling (AAR) examined the GST classification, applicable tax rates and valuation mechanism for various categories of second-hand goods, antiques, collectibles and memorabilia handled by Saffron Art Private Limited. The applicant conducts online and offline auctions, exhibitions and private sales of goods including paintings, sculptures, jewellery, furniture, carpets, watches, used cars, books, bags, cameras and other personal effects. It generally receives goods from owners on an approval or consignment basis, facilitates their sale to buyers and charges commission or buyer’s premium. The principal issues were whether the applicant could classify and determine GST rates for these goods, whether it could adopt the margin scheme under Rule 32(5) of the CGST Rules, and whether it could simultaneously follow different valuation methods depending on the nature of procurement and availability of input tax credit (ITC). The applicant submitted that it predominantly deals in second-hand goods and that GST should be charged on the difference between the selling price and purchase price under Rule 32(5), particularly where goods are procured from unregistered persons without ITC. It contended that the margin scheme was intended to avoid double taxation and ensure that GST was effectively imposed only on the value addition in transactions involving used goods. The applicant also sought classification and applicable GST rates for numerous product categories, including antiques exceeding 100 years in age, rare books, used jewellery, collectibles and old motor vehicles. It requested permission to apply the margin scheme where ITC was unavailable and normal valuation under Section 15 of the CGST Act where goods were purchased from registered suppliers with GST charged and ITC availed. On classification, the AAR held that old prints, engravings and lithographs fall under Heading 9702, while qualifying original sculptures and statuary fall under Heading 9703. Their applicable GST rates were examined with reference to the revised rate structure effective from 22 September 2025. However, the Authority declined to provide blanket classifications for several categories because the applicant had not supplied complete product descriptions, composition, specifications or supporting details. Old jewellery may fall under Chapters 7102, 7113, 7114 or 7116 depending on its nature and constituent materials. Books, furniture, carpets, watches, bags, pens, writing instruments and other articles must be classified according to their actual characteristics and relevant tariff headings. The Authority recognised that old or used status alone does not determine classification or the applicable tax rate. For used cameras, the Authority identified Heading 9007 and the applicable GST rate of 18%. In respect of old and used motor vehicles, Heading 8703 was held applicable, with concessional valuation governed by Notification No. 8/2018-Central Tax (Rate), dated 25 January 2018, and its amendments, subject to fulfilment of the prescribed conditions, including those relating to non-availment of ITC. The Authority also distinguished collectibles and memorabilia from ordinary articles on the basis of their essential character, historical or commemorative significance and commercial identity. Where the specific tariff description does not cover the goods and their predominant value arises from collectible or commemorative significance, the relevant residual tariff entries may apply. Antiques exceeding 100 years of age were held classifiable under Heading 9706, subject to satisfaction of the prescribed age condition. On the margin scheme, the AAR examined Rule 32(5), which applies to a person dealing in buying and selling second-hand goods, whether as such or after minor processing that does not change their nature, where no ITC has been availed on their purchase. The Authority observed that the scheme presupposes an actual purchase price, selling price and subsequent resale by the dealer. It distinguished transactions in which the applicant purchases goods and resells them on a principal-to-principal basis from transactions where the applicant merely facilitates auctions or sales between third-party owners and buyers. In the latter situation, ownership passes directly from the seller to the buyer, while the applicant receives commission or buyer’s premium. Such transactions constitute auctioneering or intermediary services taxable under SAC 9962, and the margin scheme cannot be applied to the underlying goods by the applicant. Accordingly, the Authority ruled that the applicant may adopt valuation under Rule 32(5) only where it actually purchases and resells second-hand goods on a principal-to-principal basis, the goods are sold as such or after permissible minor processing, no ITC has been availed on their purchase, and proper purchase and sale records are maintained. The Authority further clarified that the applicant may simultaneously use the margin scheme for eligible transactions and normal valuation under Section 15 for goods purchased from registered suppliers where GST is charged and ITC is availed. Such transactions must be separately identified and supported by appropriate documentary records. The ruling therefore establishes a distinction between the GST treatment of a second-hand goods dealer and that of an auction facilitator, while permitting transaction-specific valuation where statutory conditions are satisfied.
