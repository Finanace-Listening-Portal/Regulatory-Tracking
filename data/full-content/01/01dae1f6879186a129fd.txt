Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Shiva Traders Vs PCIT (ITAT Agra) Section 263 Revision Is Valid Where the Assessing Officer Accepts Sundry Creditors Without Completing Material Verification Summary: The Agra Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal upheld revision under Section 263 where the Assessing Officer raised specific queries about sundry creditors but accepted the balances despite material gaps in the assessee’s replies. The assessment order recorded that the liabilities had been verified. The Tribunal examined the underlying record and found that the evidence the Assessing Officer himself had considered necessary was incomplete. It therefore upheld the partial setting aside of the assessment and left the genuineness of the creditors open for fresh examination. Why the assessment was taken up for revision Shiva Traders, engaged in the liquor trade, returned an income of Rs. 37,63,500. Its case was selected for complete scrutiny, inter alia, because of high liabilities in comparison with income or receipts. Sundry creditors had increased from Rs. 75,63,659 as on 31 March 2021 to Rs. 3,72,87,428 as on 31 March 2022. The Assessing Officer completed the assessment under section 143(3) read with section 144B at Rs. 51,22,050 after disallowing certain expenses. On the issue of high liabilities, he recorded that the assessee had furnished documents, that these had been verified and that no adjustment was warranted. The Principal Commissioner considered the enquiry incomplete and invoked Explanation 2(a) to section 263, which covers an assessment passed without enquiries or verification that ought to have been made. He set aside the assessment to the extent of the sundry creditors issue. The assessee relied on scrutiny notices and Form 26AS The assessee argued that the Assessing Officer had issued a notice under section 142(1) specifically seeking purchase particulars, outstanding balances and creditor confirmations. It relied on the audited accounts, ledgers, purchase particulars and Form 26AS, and contended that the Assessing Officer had taken a permissible view after examining the material. The assessee also explained that liquor purchases could be made only from suppliers authorised by the Uttar Pradesh Excise Department. It submitted that the purchase figures matched Form 26AS and attributed the higher outstanding balances to increased business volumes following the COVID-affected year. Relying on CIT v. Sunbeam Auto Ltd. (332 ITR 157) and other decisions, it argued that a difference over the extent of enquiry could not by itself justify revision. What the assessment record actually showed The Tribunal compared the specific queries in the notice with the replies and documents on record. The Assessing Officer had called for confirmed copies of creditor accounts. There were only three creditors, yet Agra Wines and Mittal Traders had furnished no confirmations through the assessee. The confirmation for Royal Associates, which accounted for Rs. 3,06,12,139 of the total balance, was undated and did not disclose the capacity in which the signatory had signed. The ledger for Royal Associates for the relevant financial year, along with the particulars of purchases and payments for that year, was also missing. The ledger showing subsequent payment did not supply those details. In the case of Agra Wines, the ledger showed Rs. 62,20,561 as an opening balance, with apparently no transaction during the year. The Tribunal also found gaps in the supporting purchase documents and in the response explaining the commercial basis for the outstanding liabilities. Why the Tribunal upheld revision The Tribunal concluded that the Assessing Officer’s statement that he had verified the liabilities was unsupported by the assessment record. The requisitions had been complied with only in part, yet the Assessing Officer accepted the balances without following up on material particulars directly relevant to the scrutiny issue. The Bench followed the jurisdictional Allahabad High Court’s decision in CIT v. Bhagwan Das (142 Taxman 1), which applied the Supreme Court’s ruling in Malabar Industrial Co. Ltd. v. CIT (243 ITR 83). An assessment made without application of mind can satisfy the requirement that it be erroneous and prejudicial to the interests of the Revenue. The Tribunal dismissed the appeal against revision and expressly allowed the assessee to urge its submissions on genuineness before the Assessing Officer in the fresh proceedings. Author’s Comments The practical significance of this ruling lies in the distinction between raising a query and carrying the enquiry through. When the Assessing Officer identifies particular evidence as necessary and the record shows that material requisitions went unanswered, a general statement of verification in the assessment order carries little weight. The strength of a defence against revision will depend on what the replies and supporting documents actually establish. The remand also brings out the importance of a creditor-wise reconciliation. Form 26AS and the regulated nature of liquor purchases support the assessee’s explanation, while the ledger entries, confirmations and payment records address how each closing balance arose. The opening balance of Agra Wines calls for separate factual examination because it was apparently carried forward without a current-year transaction. The Tribunal’s decision concerns the validity of revision. Whether any amount can ultimately be added, and for which year, remains to be determined in the fresh assessment. Because under the facts and circumstances of the case, the Ld. PCIT erred in holding that the Assessing Officer (“AO”) failed to make independent enquiry or verification with regard to the sundry creditor liabilities, when the record clearly demonstrates that: (a) the case was specifically selected for scrutiny inter alia on the issue of “high liabilities as compared to low income/receipts”; (b) the AO issued notices, called for and examined detailed documentary evidence including audited accounts, ledger accounts, purchase details, and Form 16 records; and (c) the AO consciously recorded in the assessment order that the sundry creditors of Rs. 3,72,87,428/- were examined and no variation was warranted – the order is accordingly neither erroneous nor the product of any lack of enquiry. 3. Because under the facts and circumstances of the case, the Ld. B. Adequate Enquiry and Applied His Mind (No “Lack of Enquiry”) 3.1 The cornerstone of the Ld. PCIT’s order is the allegation that the AO failed to independently verify the genuineness and creditworthiness of the sundry creditors. This finding is factually incorrect and is directly contradicted by the following undisputed facts of record: (a) The case was specifically selected for scrutiny under CASS inter alia on the issue of “High liabilities as compared to low income/receipts”, the precise issue of high sundry creditors. The AO was therefore alive to this issue from the very commencement of the assessment proceedings. (b) The AO issued Notice u/s 142(1) dated 02.08.2023, raising specific queries at Paras 3, 7, and 8 regarding the increase in sundry creditors. (c) The Appellant duly responded vide its submission dated 30.01.2024, furnishing audited financial statements, ledger accounts of all sundry creditors, Form 26AS, purchase details, and explanations for the increase in liabilities. (d) The AO specifically recorded in the assessment order that sundry creditors of Rs. 3,72,87,428/- as on 31.03.2022 were examined; details/documents were verified; and after examination of the evidence, no variation was proposed on the issue of high liabilities. This is a conscious, reasoned, and informed conclusion of the AO, not a passive acceptance without any inquiry. 3.2 It is therefore abundantly clear that this is not a case of “no enquiry” or “lack of enquiry”, it is, at best from the Revenue’s perspective, a case where the AO conducted enquiries and arrived at a conclusion that the Ld. PCIT disagrees with. The distinction between “lack of enquiry” and “inadequate enquiry” or “difference of opinion” is crucial, as has been repeatedly affirmed by the Hon’ble Courts: CIT v. Sunbeam Auto Ltd. [2010] 332 ITR 157 / 189 Taxman 436 (Delhi) “If the Assessing Officer, while making an assessment, has made inadequate enquiry, that would not by itself give occasion to the CIT to pass an order under Section 263 merely because he has a different opinion on the matter. Only in a case of lack of enquiry that such a course of action would be open. Where the view taken by the AO was one of the possible views, the assessment order passed by the AO cannot be held to be prejudicial to the interest of the revenue.” CIT v. Vikas Polymers [2010] 194 Taxman 57 (Delhi) “If a query was raised during the course of scrutiny by the Assessing Officer, which was answered to the satisfaction of the Assessing Officer, but neither the query nor the answer was reflected in the assessment order, that would not, by itself, lead to the conclusion that the order of the Assessing Officer called for interference and revision.” Kumar Rajaram v. ITO [2019] 110 taxmann.com 109 (Madras) “After perusal of the documents, the Assessing Officer has taken a stand and passed the order. Therefore, it cannot be stated that the Assessing Officer did not apply his mind to the issue; after all, the Assessing Officer cannot be expected to write a judgment. Admittedly there was an inquiry conducted by the Assessing Officer and it is not the case of the Commissioner that there was a lack of inquiry or inadequate inquiry.” 5. On the other hand, the learned CIT DR supported the order of the Ld. PCIT. The Ld. CIT DR submitted that no confirmations were obtained from two parties, i.e. Mittal Traders and M/s. Agra Wines and only ledger copies were obtained. The ld. CIT DR also relied upon the observations of the Ld. PCIT that complete information about the sundry was not furnished by the assessee even before him during the proceedings under section 263 of the Act. 6. In rejoinder, the learned AR submitted that there were only three creditors and the details of the same were available in the balance sheet filed by the assessee on page No. 32 of the paper book. It was further submitted that in the immediately preceding year, which was the Covid year, the sale was only Rs.57.60 crores, which increased to Rs.86.06 crores during A.Y. 2022-23 showing growth of approximately 49%. It was further submitted that higher business volumes necessarily require greater purchases from authorized suppliers, resulting in correspondingly higher outstanding creditor balances. 7. We have heard both the parties and perused the material on record. As noted above, during the year, the assessee was carrying out liquor business and the case was selected for complete scrutiny inter alia to verify ‘High liabilities as compared to low income/receipts’. On perusal of the records, we notice that during the year there were three sundry creditors, namely, (i) M/s Agra Wines – Rs. 62,20,561/- (ii)M/s Royal Associates – Rs.3,06,12,139/- and (iii) M/s Mittal Traders – Rs. 4,54,728/-. In this regard, the AO vide notices u/s 142(1) of the Act (placed at page nos 8-13 of the paper book) had raised the following queries at serial number 3,6,7and 8 which are reproduced as under ; “3. List of parties from whom TCS is collected in following format – S.No. Name & current address of the Party with email Nature of Receipts TCS Amount 1 2 3 4 5 6. Furnish a comparative list of direct and indirect expenses in ITR which have increased by 20% from preceding year in following format – S.No. Head of Expenditure Amount (F.Y. 21-22) Amount (F.Y 20-21 1 2 3 4 a. Give a note justifying increase of expenditure in table above compared to preceding year with supporting documentary evidence/s; b. File proof of genuineness of expenses in (a) above 7. Furnish complete details in respect of purchases debited during the year under consideration in the following format. S.No. Name and complete current addresses with email of the party from whom the purchases have been made PAN Total Amount of Purchases made during the year Amount paid during the year Item purchased Amount outstanding at the end of the accounting year 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 a. Confirmed copy of account of top 10 creditors as per table above; b. Proof of genuineness of purchases debited for goods or services exceeding Rs.5 lakh made to any party such as proof of third party transportation invoices for delivery of goods or similar such documents for services; 8. Give a note justifying commercial expediency of liabilities other than Capital in the Balance sheet, in reference to revenue from operations during the year;” 7.1 On perusal of the above queries and the replies filed by the assessee before the AO (duly placed at page no. 16-37, 38,40-41and 42-53 of the paper book filed by the assessee), we notice that no reply was furnished in respect of the query at serial No. 6, even though we note that the assessee in its written submission before us had stated that the appellant’s turnover rose substantially from Rs. 57.60 crores during F.Y. 2020-21 to Rs. 86.06 crores during F.Y. 2021-22. In this query, the AO had also asked the assessee to give a note justifying increase of the expenditure by 20% of direct expenses (which includes the purchases of goods sold during the year) as compared to preceding year with supporting documentary evidences and about its genuineness, which apparently was not submitted as no details of the same is filed in paper book submitted by the assessee before us. The AO in the assessment has also not mentioned that the said details were filed by the assessee. Similarly, regarding the query at serial number 7, it appears that only an undated confirmation in respect of M/s Royal associates was furnished wherein the capacity of the person signing the confirmation on behalf of M/s. Royal Associates is not spelt out. In respect of the other two parties i.e. M/s Agra Wines and M/s Mittal Traders we notice that no confirmation was filed even though the AO had asked for the confirmed copy of account of top ten creditors which would have included M/s Agra Wines and M/s Mittal. Further, as per records the address in respect of M/s Royal Associates was only made available before the AO and the address in respect of the other two sundry creditors namely M/s Agra Wines and M/S Mittal Traders were not made available by the assessee before the AO. Further, the AO had also asked the assessee to furnish the email of the above sundry creditors but the same also appears to have not been filed by the assessee before the AO. Further, the other details as called for in the table in the said query, i.e., details of ‘total amount of purchases made during the year’, ‘amount paid during the year’ and ‘amount outstanding at the end of the accounting year’ was not furnished in the format asked for even though ledger copy showing the purchase, payment and the outstanding amount in respect of M/s. Mittal Traders was furnished. Further, in respect of M/s. Agra Wines, a copy of the ledger was filed wherein the amount of Rs.62,20,561/- was stated to be the opening balance as on 01.04.2021 and apparently there was no transaction with this party during the year but the amount remained outstanding during the entire F.Y. 2021-22. Moreover, no ledger account of M/s. Royal Associates for the F.Y. 2020-21 was furnished and only the ledger for F.Y. 2022-23 was furnished wherein the outstanding sundry creditors balance of Rs. 3,06,12,139/- was paid. Thus, details of ‘total amount of purchases made during the year’ and ‘amount paid during the year’ for the F.Y. 2021-22 in respect of M/s. Royal Associates was not filed by the assessee before the AO during the course of assessment proceedings. Further, we notice that at Sr no 7(b) the AO had asked for the proof of genuineness of purchases debited for goods exceeding Rs.5,00,000/-to any party and proof of third party transportation invoices for delivery of goods which appears to have not been filed by the assessee before the AO during the course of the assessment proceedings. Further, the AO at Serial No. 8 had asked for a note justifying the commercial expediency of the liabilities in reference to the revenue from operation during the year for which again, the assessee has not demonstrated before us that any reply was furnished by the assessee before the AO during the course of assessment proceedings, even though in its reply before the learned PCIT during the proceedings u/s. 263 of the Act, it has been claimed by the assessee that the same was filed before the AO during the course of assessment proceedings. 7.2 Thus, we notice that though the AO made the requisite queries about the outstanding sundry creditors balance of Rs.3,72,87,428/- as on 31.03.2022 as per the various queries of the notice u/s. 142(1) of the Act dated 02.08.2023, as reproduced above, but the same was only partly complied by the assessee. In fact, the confirmation in respect of M/s. Agra Wines and M/s. Mittal Traders were not filed despite requisitioned by the AO. Similarly, the confirmation of M/s. Royal Associates was undated and the capacity of the person signing the confirmation on behalf of M/s. Royal Associates is not spelt out. As noted above, this case was picked up for scrutiny, inter alia, to verify the high liabilities as compared to low income/receipts and therefore, the minimum enquiry by the AO was expected to have the confirmation from all the three sundry creditors as referred above. Further, in respect of M/s. Royal Associates, the details of the total purchases and total payments made during the F.Y. 2021-22 was not furnished by the assessee before the AO during the course of assessment proceedings. Moreover, the ledger of this party for the F.Y. 2021-22 was also not furnished. Further, we have also noted that other details as called for by the AO were also not furnished. Thus, when these details despite being requisitioned by the AO, which were not furnished by the assessee, the observation of the AO in the assessment order that ‘the assessee has submitted details/documents in respect of liabilities and the same has been verified. On perusal and examination of the documents submitted by the assessee, no variation is proposed in the case on the issue of high liabilities’is not justified. When the details called for as discussed above were not filed by the assessee, the question of examination of the documents and its verification does not arise. In this regard we agree with the observation of the Ld. PCIT in the impugned order u/s 263 of the Act that ‘Information was given only about one sundry creditor, whereas there were three sundry creditors. Apparently, this complete information was also not available to the AO. Hence, the AO could not and did not make any independent and complete verification’. Therefore, in the given facts of the case, we are of the considered view that the AO has accepted the explanation of the assessee regarding the genuineness of the sundry creditors balance of Rs.3,72,87,428/- without application of mind. In this regard, the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in the case of CIT vs. Bhagwan Das (2005) 142 Taxman 1 (Allahabad) has held that an assessment order passed without application of mind by the AO will fall under the expression ‘erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of revenue’. In the case before the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court, the learned CIT had passed an order u/s. 263 of the Act in respect of an assessment order passed by the AO, wherein he noticed that the AO had granted exemption of income from agriculture amounting to Rs.30,446/- and income from poultry farming amounting to Rs.33,849/- without making any enquiry or investigation as regards the nature, source or extent of income derived from agriculture and poultry farming or investment from which these income arose. The relevant substantial question of law and extract of the order of the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court upholding the order u/s. 263 of the Act is reproduced as under : “1. The Income-tax Appellate Tribunal, Allahabad has referred the following question of law under section 256(1) of Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as Act) for opinion of this Court. “Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the order of the CIT was rightly set aside by the Tribunal ? 2. Briefly stated facts giving rise to the present Revision are as follows :— The respondent assessee is an individual. For the assessment year 1973-74, the relevant previous year being 1st April, 1972 to 31st March, 1973, the respondent filed his return of income showing an income of Rs. 10,450. Notice under section 143(2) of the Act was issued. In response the respondent appeared and after discussion, the Income-tax Officer, Circle II (9) Kanpur vide order dated 27th November, 1976 assessed a total income of Rs. 24,750. He granted exemption of income from Agriculture amounting to Rs. 30,446 and income from Poultry Farming amounting to Rs. 33,849. The Commissioner of Income-tax initiated proceedings under section 263 of the Act on the ground that in making the assessment the Income-tax Officer did not make any enquiry or investigation as regards the nature, source or extent of income derived from agriculture and poultry farming or investment from which these income arose. Further the Income-tax Officer also did not take any steps to verify whether the income from these sources as claimed were fully exempt. In response to the notice issued under section 263, the respondent appeared before the Commissioner of Income-tax and stated that he has maintained proper accounts for these sources of income and will be in a position to produce evidence and proof to substantiate his claim. The Commissioner of Income-tax passed an order setting aside the assessment made by the Income-tax Officer with a direction to reframe the assessment after making full and proper investigation in the nature, sources and extent of the income earned from these sources and the source of the investment made for earning these incomes. In doing so, the Income-tax Officer will give full and proper opportunity to the assessee to present his side of the respondent. The respondent feeling aggrieved, preferred an appeal before the Income-tax Appellate Tribunal. The Tribunal vide order dated 27th November, 1979 had allowed the appeal filed by the respondent assessee and had set aside the order of the Commissioner of Income-tax passed under section 263. The Tribunal, while doing so had held as follows:— “In our view, there is case for interference in the order of the Commissioner of Income-tax. The requirement of the provisions of section 263 of the Act are that the order of the Income-tax Officer should be erroneous in so far as it could be prejudicial to the interest of the revenue. The Commissioner nowhere applied his mind in respect of the order of the Income-tax Officer being erroneous or prejudicial to the interest of the revenue. Besides the above, we have noted that during the original assessment proceedings, the assessee, in support of his claim for exemption of agricultural and poultry farm income from tax, the assessee filed copies of certain agreements and after considering the same, the Income-tax Officer allowed exemption to the assessee. Thus, it is a case of change of opinion on the part of the Commissioner which is not warranted under the provisions of section 263 of the Act. In view of this matter, the order of the Commissioner is reversed and the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed.” 3. We have heard Shri A.N. Mahajan, learned Standing Counsel appearing for the revenue. In spite of service of notice, nobody has put in appearance and the Court vide order dated 11th February, 2004 held the services to be deemed sufficient under the Rules of the Court and directed for hearing of the revision. Shri A.N. Mahajan, learned Standing Counsel submitted that from the perusal of the assessment order, it would be seen that the Income-tax Officer had not applied his mind as to whether the income which has been claimed by the respondent assessee has been earned or whether it is exempted in full or in part. Thus, the order granting exemption to income from Agricultural and Poultry farming has been without any application of mind. The Commissioner of Income-tax was, therefore, fully justified in taking re course to proceedings under section 263 of the Act and after giving an opportunity of hearing to the assessee, had rightly set aside the assessment order as the same was erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of revenue. He relied upon the decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Malabar Industrial Co. Ltd. v. CIT [2000] 243 ITR 831 . 4. Having heard the learned counsel for the revenue, we find that in the assessment order, there is no discussion regarding the question as to whether the amount of income shown by the assessee which is being claimed to be exempt has actually been earned by him or not and, further, whether the entire amount of income from Agriculture and Poultry farming is exempt from tax. The Commissioner of Income-tax has rightly initiated proceedings under section 263 of the Act as exemption has been granted without any application of mind. The Apex Court in the case of Malabar Indus trial Co. Ltd. (supra) while interpreting section 263 of the Act held as follows :— “A bare reading of this provision makes it clear that the prerequisite to the exercise of jurisdiction by the Commissioner suo motu under it, is that the order of the Income-tax Officer is erroneous insofar as it is prejudicial to the interests of the Revenue. The Commissioner has to be satisfied of twin conditions, namely, (i) the order of the Assessing Officer sought to be revised is erroneous; and (ii) it is prejudicial to the interests of the revenue. If one of them is absent-if the order of the Income-tax Officer is erroneous but is not prejudicial to the revenue or if it is not erroneous but is prejudicial to the revenue recourse cannot be had to section 263(1) of the Act. There can be no doubt that the provision cannot be invoked to correct each and every type of mistake or error committed by the Assessing Officer, it is only when an order is erroneous that the section will be attracted. An incorrect assumption of facts or an incorrect application of law will satisfy the requirement of the order being erroneous. In the same category fall orders passed without applying the principles of natural justice or without application of mind.” [Emphasis supplied] 5. Thus an order, which has been passed without application of mind, will also fall under the expression erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of Revenue.Since the Income-tax Officer has granted exemption to the assessee in respect of income from agriculture and poultry farming without any discussion and with out any application of mind, respectfully following the aforesaid decision, we are of the opinion that the Tribunal had committed error in holding that the assessment ordering so far as it grant ed exemption to income from Agriculture and Poultry farming was not erroneous or prejudicial to the interest of Revenue. We, therefore, answer the question referred to us in the negative, i.e., in favour of the Revenue and against the assessee. Since nobody has put in appearance on behalf of the assessee, there shall be no order as to costs.” (emphasis supplied by us) 7.3 In the present case before us, we have noted as to how the order has been passed by the AO without bringing on record the documents called for vide notice u/s. 142(1) of the Act dated 02.08.2023, which was considered necessary by him to establish the genuineness of the sundry creditors balance of Rs.3,72,87,428/-. Even though in the case of our present assessee, the AO mentions that the details filed in respect of the sundry creditors balance of Rs.3,72,87,428/- have been verified and no variation is proposed by him but as observed by us that said observation by the AO is not justified in this case. 7.4 Therefore, in view of the above facts and discussion and respectfully following the order of the Hon’ble jurisdictional High Court in the case of CIT vs. Bhagwan Das (supra), we uphold the order dated 24.03.2026 of the learned PCIT setting aside the issue of sundry creditors to the file of the AO as discussed by the learned PCIT in para nos. 2, 3 and 6 of his order. Ground Nos. 1 to 5, 8 & 9 of the appeal challenging the correctness of the order dated 24.03.2026 passed by the learned PCIT u/s. 263 of the Act are dismissed. Ground Nos. 6, 7 & 10 of the appeal are on the merits regarding the claim on the genuineness of the claim regarding the sundry creditors of Rs.3,72,87,428/-. Since, we have affirmed the order of learned PCIT in respect of remanding the matter back to the AO on the issue of sundry creditors balance of Rs. 3,72,87,428/-, the remaining grounds of appeal Nos. 6, 7 & 10 raised on the merit of the addition on the same issue become academic and are left open in this case and the assessee is at liberty to plead it before the AO during the set aside assessment proceedings. 8. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is dismissed. Order pronounced in the open court on 19/11/2026.
