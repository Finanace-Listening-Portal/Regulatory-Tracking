Kamthe Infrastructure Developers Private Limited (NCLT Mumbai) Summary: NCLT Mumbai admitted SREI Equipment Financial Limited’s petition under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 against R S Kamthe Infrastructure Developers Private Limited, concerning a claimed default of ₹7,28,54,918 as on 12 August 2022 under two equipment-financing arrangements. The petition had initially been dismissed on 2 November 2023 as barred by Section 10A. NCLAT set that dismissal aside on 28 January 2026, holding that the application was not barred, and the petition was restored on 20 February 2026. The Tribunal subsequently considered the loan documents, statements of account, correspondence concerning delivery of machinery and NeSL records of default. The first loan, sanctioned on 1 July 2019, was for ₹2.95 crore. The corporate debtor admitted receiving the money but contended that it had returned the amount through a demand draft the next day and independently purchased the equipment from Rithwik Projects Private Limited. The Tribunal rejected this defence because no document or bank statement establishing the corresponding debit had been produced. The creditor’s statement of account, showing subsequent instalment payments up to 21 August 2020, remained unrebutted. For the second loan of ₹2.745 crore, sanctioned on 28 September 2019, the corporate debtor disputed disbursement, execution of Schedule VII and transfer of the equipment, also pointing to invoices issued only in August 2021. The Tribunal found that Schedule VII bore the corporate debtor’s stamp and signature, correspondence established delivery and acknowledgment of machinery, and the account reflected part-payments up to 28 September 2020. It therefore rejected the non-disbursement defence. A letter concerning correction of a negative CIBIL entry was held not to extinguish the underlying debt or default. The creditor relied on default from 15 April 2021, after the protected period under Section 10A. Form D records issued by NeSL recorded that default date and amounts of ₹2,06,88,616 and ₹1,50,24,312 for the respective loan accounts, with deemed authentication as on 10 October 2022. The Tribunal found that these records corroborated the statements of account and established debt and default. It also found that the default exceeded the statutory threshold and that the petition was within limitation. The corporate debtor relied on its asserted asset base of approximately ₹57 crore, workforce of about 600 employees, ongoing infrastructure projects and commercial viability, invoking Vidarbha Industries Power Ltd. v. Axis Bank Ltd. The Tribunal rejected that defence in light of the Supreme Court’s clarification in the Vidarbha review proceedings and its subsequent decisions in M. Suresh Kumar Reddy v. Canara Bank and Power Trust v. Bhuvan Madan. It held that, on the facts before it, the admission inquiry concerned the existence of financial debt and default and did not extend to a broader assessment of business prospects or ability to pay once those requirements were established. The petition was allowed, Megha Agrawal was appointed Interim Resolution Professional, and the financial creditor was directed to deposit ₹5 lakh towards initial CIRP costs. The Tribunal declared the Section 14 moratorium, directed an immediate public announcement, vested management in the IRP/RP and required the suspended directors and employees to cooperate. The Present Company Petition has been filed under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (‘IBC’/ ‘the Code’) read with Rule 4 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016 by SREI Equipment Financial Limited (‘the Financial Creditor/Petitioner’) for initiating Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (‘CIRP’) against R S Kamthe Infrastructure Developers Private Limited (‘the Corporate Debtor/Respondent’) for a default amount of 7,28,54,918/-(Rupees Seven Crore Twenty-Eight Lakh Fifty-Four Thousand Nine Hundred Eighteen Only) as on 12.08.2022. 2. It is important to note that the present Petition was dismissed by order dated 02.11.2023 on the ground that it was barred under Section 10A of the Code. The Petitioner challenged the order dated 02.11.2023 by filing an appeal, being CA (AT) (Ins) No. 116 of 2024, before the Hon’ble NCLAT. Vide order dated 28.01.2026, the Hon’ble NCLAT set aside the dismissal order dated 02.11.2023 holding that application is not barred by Section 10A, and revived the present Petition for afresh consideration by this Authority. Accordingly, the Petitioner filed Restoration Application No. 17 of 2026, seeking restoration of the Company Petition. Thereafter, vide order dated 20.02.2026, this Tribunal restored the Company Petition. Brief Facts as per the Petition: 3. Further, as per the Statement of account for the Loan Agreement No. 180822 as on 12.08.2022, the particulars of the loan are as follows: 5. Thereafter, upon the Respondent’s default, the Petitioner filed the present petition under Section 7 of the Code on 28.09.2022, seeking initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process against the Respondent. Objection Regarding Loan Account 178737 6.It is submitted that the loan agreement dated 01.07.2019 is incomplete and lacks details of the borrower and guarantor as well as lacks the consent letter to be obtained from the borrower/guarantor for disclosure of information to CIBIL. It is submitted that this loan was sanctioned to the Respondent for the purchase of equipment owned and operated by M/s. Rithwik Projects Private Limited (‘RPPL’). 7. It is submitted that the sanctioned amount of Rs. 2,95,00,000/- was received by the Respondent in its Bank Account on 10.07.2019 and thereafter Respondent returned the amount back to the Petitioner vide Demand Draft dated 11.07.2019, and instead directly purchased the said equipment from M/s RPPL. It is contended that return of the sanctioned amount by the Petitioner has not taken into consideration and adjusted by the Petitioner in the statement of account. Further, the transfer of the machine to the Respondent never took place and was invoiced at a belated stage. ii. Objection regarding loan account No. 180822 8. It is submitted that under the loan agreement dated 28.09.2019, the amount of Rs. 2,74,50,000/- was sanctioned for purchasing equipment owned by the Petitioner itself. It is submitted that the Petitioner has neither disbursed the sanctioned amount to the Respondent in its account for the period from 28.09.2019 to 15.12.2022, nor entered into a specific agreement for sale/purchase of the equipment owned by the Petitioner. Therefore, it is submitted that there is no default on the part of the Respondent, and the money cannot be considered to be disbursed till the time the underlying asset is transferred. 9. It is further submitted that as per Clause 2.1 titled ‘facility’ of the loan agreement, the agreement was to come into effect after the request made by the Respondent in Schedule VII and upon the Petitioner signing the Schedule VII. It is submitted that the Petitioner has not signed the Schedule VII and therefore no agreement can be said to have come into effect and therefore no financial debt exists. 10. It is submitted that the Petitioner has issued invoices dated 16.08.2021, much after signing of the loan agreement dated 28.09.2019. It is submitted that the invoices have only been issued in respect of 8 out of 9 assets as were required to be transferred by the Petitioner to the Respondent, thereby indicating that the Petitioner itself is in default as it not only failed to disburse the amount but also failed to transfer all the assets to the Respondent, hence, there is no default on the part of the Respondent. 11. It is submitted that the Petitioner classified the loan accounts as Non-Performing Assets before December 2021, the date of default in the Petition is wrong. It is submitted that the Petitioner, vide letter dated 03.05.2021, stated that the Respondent has paid the instalments and the negative record created by the Petitioner on the CIBIL record of the Respondent would be resolved as the same was a technical glitch. 12. It is submitted that the CIBIL Report of the Respondent reflects a strong credit history, with a credit line of approximately Rs. 75 Crores, which has been duly repaid by the Respondent. In view of the aforesaid submissions, it is submitted that the present Petition be dismissed with costs. Rejoinder of the Petitioner: 14. It is submitted that there was no transaction between the Petitioner and RPPL. The Respondent had approached the Petitioner for a loan to facilitate the purchase of machinery from RPPL. Accordingly, the transaction under the MFAs was solely between the Petitioner and the Respondent, and RPPL was not a party to the said transaction. 15. Further, contrary to the contention of the Respondent, the Petitioner submits that the Respondent has been in possession and use of the said assets since the execution of the MFAs. It is further submitted that the interest charged is in accordance with the terms agreed under the MFAs. 16. It is submitted that the amounts under the MFAs were disbursed for the specific purpose of financing the purchase of machinery by the Respondent from RPPL. The transaction was structured in such a manner that the funds were directly disbursed to RPPL towards adjustment of the amounts payable by the Respondent for the machinery financed under the MFAs. The sale transaction between the Respondent and RPPL was completed upon such adjustment of the amounts due and the Respondent taking possession of the machinery from RPPL. Further, the email dated 30.08.2019 sent by RPPL to the Petitioner, as well as the Petitioner’s email dated 21.10.2021 addressed to the Respondent, establish that the Respondent had taken delivery of the machinery and that the Petitioner had only facilitated and financed the purchase. With regard to the contention that the Agreements are incomplete, it is submitted that the Agreements have been annexed in their entirety and contain all the essential terms and information agreed upon between the parties. 18. With regard to the Respondent’s contention that the sanctioned amount of Rs. 2,95,00,000/- was returned back to the Petitioner vide Demand Draft dated 11.07.2019, the Petitioner submitted that, under Clause 2.9 of the MFAs, the Respondent could prepay the entire loan amount only after obtaining the prior approval of the Petitioner and upon payment of a premium of four percent, along with any pending taxes, surcharges, cess, etc. Therefore, the Respondent’s claim that the facility was repaid by way of a demand draft is contrary to the terms of the MFAs and cannot be considered a valid prepayment. 19. With regard to the loan account bearing No. 180822, it is submitted that the underlying transaction involved the purchase of certain equipment by the Respondent from the Petitioner on a finance basis. Accordingly, the Respondent was required to make payments to the Petitioner from the amounts disbursed by the Petitioner. The equipment was handed over to the Respondent by RPPL between August 2019 and December 2019. The invoices were raised only in August 2021, solely at the request of the Respondent. 20. It is submitted that, while considering Review Petition (Civil) No. 1043 of 2022 against the order dated 12.07.2022 passed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India in Vidarbha Industries Vs. Axis Bank, the Hon’ble Supreme Court clarified that the said order was made in the context of the facts of that particular case and should not be interpreted as laying down a general rule or as a statute. Analysis & Findings 22. Heard Ld. Counsel for the parties, and perused the record. We have given our thoughtful consideration to the arguments advanced and material placed on record. 23. The Petitioner has filed the present Petition on the basis of default committed by the Respondent on 15.04.2021. The Petitioner claims a total outstanding default of Rs. 7,28,54,918/- as on 12.08.2022, contends the equipment was duly delivered to and used by the Respondent, and seeks initiation of CIRP on the ground that debt and default are established. 24. Per contra, the Respondent contends that, having regard to its financial position, the present case is not fit for initiation of CIRP. The Respondent further contends that, in respect of Loan No. 178737, the sanctioned amount was returned by way of a demand draft and the purchase of the equipment was independently made from RPPL, accordingly, no debt remains outstanding. In respect of Loan No. 180822, the Respondent contends that the amount was never disbursed and that Schedule VII was not signed by the Petitioner, and therefore, the Agreement never came into effect. 25. It is an admitted position that under Loan Agreement No. 178737 dated 01.07.2019, a sum of Rs. 2,95,00,000/- was received in Respondent’s bank account on 10.07.2019. The Respondent has contended that it returned the loan amount to the Petitioner on the next day by Demand Draft dated 11.07.2019, however, no document or bank statement evidencing a corresponding debit from the Respondent’s account has been placed on record. On the contrary, the Petitioner has placed on record the Statement of Account of the Respondent, showing that the Respondent has been paying instalments upto 21.08.2020, as per the sanctioned loan agreement, which has not been rebutted by the Respondent. Therefore, in the absence of any documentary evidence, the Respondent’s contention of repayment cannot be accepted, particularly in view of its conduct of continuing to make instalment payments thereafter. 27. Insofar as Loan Agreement No. 180822 is concerned, the Respondent contends that it neither signed Schedule VII of the Agreement nor received any disbursement. However, Schedule VII, placed at page 101 of the Petition, bears the stamp and signature of the Respondent. Under the said Agreement, the Petitioner was to provide funding on behalf of the Respondent. Further, the email dated 30.08.2019 sent by RPPL to the Petitioner records the list of machinery supplied to the Respondent on a purchase basis, which was acknowledged by the Respondent. Thereafter, at the Respondent’s request, the Petitioner issued invoices dated 16.08.2021, nearly two years after the assets had been delivered by RPPL. The delivery of the assets and the said correspondence have not been disputed by the Respondent. The Statement of Account also reflects part-payments made by the Respondent up to 28.09.2020. In these circumstances, the contention that there was no disbursement under Loan Agreement No. 180822 cannot be accepted. 28. The Respondent has relied upon the Petitioner’s letter dated 03.05.2021, to contend that the instalments had been paid and that the negative CIBIL record was due to a technical glitch. However, the said letter, issued after the recorded date of default, in our view, merely relates to correction of the credit record and does not amount to an acknowledgment that the underlying debt or default stood extinguished. 29. The Petitioner has mentioned the default amounts which occurred after expiry of the Section 10A period, i.e., from 24.03.2021 until filing of the present Petition on 28.03.2022, amounting to Rs. 1,82,88,000/- and Rs. 1,33,87,200/-, respectively. Moreover, the Hon’ble NCLAT in its order dated 28.01.2026 observed that: “8. … The financial creditor has relied on date of default 15.04.2021 which is subsequent to Section 10A period and default by the corporate debtor has been claimed from 15.04.2021 thereafter and the amount which is in default after 15.04.2021 is much beyond the threshold period.” The aforesaid facts have not been rebutted by the Respondent. 30. At this juncture, it would be apposite to refer to Sections 7(3) and 7(4) of the Code, which are reproduced below: “7. Initiation of corporate insolvency resolution process by financial creditor. (3) The financial creditor shall, along with the application furnish – record of the default recorded with the information utility or such other record or evidence of default as may be specified; the name of the resolution professional proposed to act as an interim resolution professional; and any other information as may be specified by the Board. (4) The Adjudicating Authority shall, within fourteen days of the receipt of the application under sub-section (2), ascertain the existence of a default from the records of an information utility or on the basis of other evidence furnished by the financial creditor under sub-section (3).” [Bold for Emphasis] 30. In the present case, the Financial Creditor has relied upon the statements of account furnished along with the Application, wherein the default amounts mentioned in Part IV of the Application correspond with the amounts reflected in the said statements. Further, pursuant to the order dated 25.06.2026 passed by this Tribunal, the Financial Creditor has placed on record Form D, i.e., the record of default issued by NeSL, in respect of both Loan Agreements. The Form D records the date of default as 15.04.2021 and was deemed authenticated as on 10.10.2022, with the default amount being Rs. 2,06,88,616/- under Loan Agreement No. 178737 and Rs. 1,50,24,312/-under Loan Agreement No. 180822. Thus, the record of default issued by NeSL corroborates the default amounts and the date of default stated in the Application. 31. Section 7(4) mandates the Adjudicating Authority to ascertain the existence of default from the records of the Information Utility, which, in the present case, is evidenced by the Form D issued by NeSL, along with the other evidence furnished by the Financial Creditor. Taking into account the aforesaid material on record, we are of the considered view that the Financial Creditor has established the debt and default in respect of the amounts claimed in the Application. 32. The Respondent has further relied upon the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Vidarbha Industries Power Ltd. v. Axis Bank Ltd., Civil Appeal No. 4622 of 2021, to contend that this Tribunal, apart from being satisfied as to the existence of debt and default, is also required to examine the commercial viability of the Respondent, its ongoing projects, workforce, and asset base, before admitting a petition under Section 7 of the Code. 33. This contention no longer survives in view of the subsequent pronouncements of the Hon’ble Supreme Court. While considering the Review Petition arising from Vidarbha, the Hon’ble Supreme Court itself clarified that the observations therein were made in the context of the facts of that particular case and are not to be read as laying down a general rule of law of universal application. This clarification has been reaffirmed in Suresh Kumar Reddy v. Canara Bank & Ors., (2023) 8 SCC 387, wherein the Hon’ble Supreme Court observed as under: “12. A Review Petition was filed by the Axis Bank Limited seeking a review of the decision of Vidarbha Industries on the ground that the attention of the Court was not invited to the case of E.S. Krishnamurthy. While disposing of Review Petition by Order dated 22nd September 2022, this Court held thus: The elucidation in paragraph 90 and other paragraphs were made in the context of the case at hand. It is well settled that judgments and observations in judgments are not to be read as provisions of statute. Judicial utterances and/or pronouncements are in the setting of the facts of a particular case. To interpret words and provisions of a statute, it may become necessary for the Judges to embark upon lengthy discussions. The words of Judges interpreting statutes are not to be interpreted as statutes. 13. Thus, it was clarified by the order in review that the decision in the case of Vidarbha Industries was in the setting of facts of the case before this Court. Hence, the decision in the case of Vidarbha Industries cannot be read and understood as taking a view which is contrary to the view taken in the cases of Innoventive Industries and E.S. Krishnamurthy. The view taken in the case of Innoventive Industries still holds good” 34. Furthermore, it is relevant to take note of the judgment dated 18.02.2026, passed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Power Trust (Promoter of Hiranmaye Energy Ltd.) v. Bhuvan Madan, IRP of Hiranmaye Energy Ltd. and Ors., Civil Appeal No(s). 2211/2024, wherein, while distinguishing between the insolvency processes under Sections 7 and 9 of the Code, the Hon’ble Supreme Court held as under: “35. …when the financial creditor initiates the insolvency process for the purposes of admission, the Adjudicating Authority is only to ascertain the existence of a default from the records of the information utility or the evidence furnished by the financial creditor within fourteen days from the receipt of such application. At this stage, neither is a corporate debtor entitled nor is the Adjudicating Authority required to examine any dispute regarding the existence of such debt. This significantly reduces the scope of enquiry at the stage of a time-bound admission of an insolvency process by a financial creditor which has been succinctly summed up in Innoventive: “30… in the case of a corporate debtor who commits a default of a financial debt, the adjudicating authority has merely to see the records of the information utility or other evidence produced by the financial creditor to satisfy itself that a default has occurred. It is of no matter that the debt is disputed so long as the debt is “due” i.e. payable unless interdicted by some law or has not yet become due in the sense that it is payable at some future date. It is only when this is proved to the satisfaction of the adjudicating authority that the adjudicating authority may reject an application and not otherwise” …” 35. From the aforesaid, it is evident that the scope of enquiry before the Adjudicating Authority under Section 7 is confined to ascertaining the existence of a financial debt and whether a default has occurred, once these ingredients stand established, the Adjudicating Authority at the stage of admission, cannot delve into a broader enquiry into the viability, business prospects, or ability of the corporate debtor to pay. 36. In view of the foregoing discussion and the judgments of the Hon’ble Supreme Court, it stands established that financial debt exists under Loan Agreement Nos. 178737 and 180822 and that the Respondent committed default in repayment thereof on and after 15.04.2021. The amount in default exceeds the threshold prescribed under Section 4(1) of the Code, and the Petition is within limitation. The Respondent’s reliance on Vidarbha Industries Power Ltd. v. Axis Bank Ltd. does not support the Respondent on the ground of commercial viability. Accordingly, the Company Petition is admitted in terms of the following order: ORDER A.
