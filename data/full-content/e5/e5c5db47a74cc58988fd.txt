Articles 3 min read AI must strengthen human judgment, not replace empathy in lending: Industry leaders NBFCs are using AI and data-driven personalisation to improve customer acquisition, servicing and collections, but responsible data use, regulatory compliance and human oversight remain critical to building customer trust, industry leaders said at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Published On Oct 10, 2026 at 03:15 PM IST var news_msid = 134851537; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Artificial intelligence (AI) can help NBFCs lower customer acquisition costs and personalise lending, but sustainable customer relationships will depend on responsible engagement, transparent practices and human judgment, industry leaders said at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026.Ashish Chandak, Group Chief Compliance Officer, Manappuram Finance, said lenders must move beyond basic Know Your Customer (KYC) checks to understand borrowers’ financial needs and structure products around their circumstances.“KYC is really knowing your customer in the truest sense,” Chandak said, citing agricultural borrowers whose loan tenures and repayment schedules could be aligned with crop cycles and expected harvest income. Advt Personalisation can lower acquisition costsSanjay Shamnani, Chief Liabilities Officer, Kissht, said AI-powered tools could identify customers dropping out of digital loan applications and provide timely assistance, reducing friction in onboarding.He said Kissht's merchant-led acquisition network of around 5.5 lakh merchants generated customer acquisition costs of approximately ₹50–150. Digital marketing costs, however, could rise to ₹1,800–2,000 during high-competition periods such as major sporting events and festive seasons, compared with ₹250–300 during less competitive periods.Kissht's overall customer acquisition cost was around ₹600–700, equivalent to approximately 1.8–2% of its lending volume, Shamnani said, emphasising that acquisition strategies must remain commercially viable.Ram Kolli, Head of Business, Credit Saison, said the shift towards direct customer relationships gave lenders greater opportunities to understand borrowers' broader financial needs. Credit Saison had built assets under management of approximately ₹25,000 crore over seven years, he said.Kolli said lenders often lose opportunities to serve existing customers across product categories, forcing them to spend again on acquisition. Better customer intelligence could help lenders identify relevant products for existing borrowers while ensuring that additional credit remains aligned with repayment capacity. Advt Digital lending must retain human oversightChandak said technology could make repeat lending more efficient once an initial physical interaction had taken place. Around 92–95% of Manappuram Finance's gold loan book was being serviced digitally through its online gold loan platform, he said.However, he cautioned against excessive reliance on automated customer interactions, particularly bot-led calls, which could frustrate borrowers rather than improve their experience.“AI should augment the human judgment and not eliminate human empathy,” Chandak said, arguing that lenders must establish clear accountability when an employee's assessment differs from an AI-generated credit recommendation. Advt He also outlined four principles for responsible AI systems: explainability, auditability, reliability and stability. Compliance, he said, should be treated as integral to profitable growth rather than as a competing priority.Shamnani said Kissht had implemented technology controls to monitor customer calls, restrict contact frequency and enforce permitted calling hours. The company also had safeguards governing access to confidential customer information and oversight of outsourced collection agencies.He stressed that responsibility for customer treatment remained with the regulated lender even when servicing and collections were outsourced. Advt Data and technology must strengthen trustKolli said branch-led lenders faced growing pressure on productivity as competition intensified in Tier-III and Tier-IV markets. AI-enabled document processing, automated KYC data entry, optical character recognition and electronic signatures could reduce manual workloads and allow relationship managers to focus more on customers.However, technology gains alone may not offset rising distribution costs and competition, making deeper engagement with existing borrowers increasingly important.The panellists said AI could improve underwriting, customer servicing and operating efficiency, but its long-term value would depend on how effectively lenders combine data-driven insights with responsible communication and human judgment.For NBFCs, customer loyalty will increasingly depend not simply on faster loan approvals, but on offering relevant products, protecting borrower information and maintaining trust throughout the lending relationship.
