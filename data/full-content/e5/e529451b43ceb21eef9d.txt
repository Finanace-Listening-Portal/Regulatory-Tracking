Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 11, 2026 at 04:48 PM IST var news_msid = 134062982; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Minister of Finance and Corporate Affairs Nirmala Sitharaman on Friday said India's distinctive contribution to the global artificial intelligence ecosystem could be the way it deploys AI for public good, with a focus on inclusive, equitable and affordable access to services.Speaking at the Global FinTech Fest 2026 in Mumbai, Sitharaman said India would build its own AI models and applications, but the larger opportunity was to demonstrate how the technology could be used to create public value.“What I believe will uniquely give to the world, is how AI has been put to use for public good. How AI must be deployed? How AI can be leveraged for inclusive, equitable, affordable, broad access services for all,” she said. Advt She compared the potential global relevance of India's AI deployment to the way UPI is being discussed internationally.Sitharaman also pointed to the use of emerging technologies beyond payments, including accelerating credit to farmers and small businesses, providing insurance and speeding up financial journeys, including through vernacular languages.She also said that large language models could potentially influence public opinion without people even realising they had been influenced.“To the extent that we, being a minister, being in politics, I'm clearly seeing it capable of influencing elections,” Sitharaman said.She said the rapid development of agentic AI and other technologies had prompted questions around whether regulation could be designed in a way that did not undermine innovation.“Is there a way in which we can do a soft touch regulation? Is there a way in which, without affecting the innovative pressure, is there a way in which we can do something?” she said.AI safety needs industry accountabilitySitharaman also questioned whether responsibility for AI safety should rest primarily with governments and regulators, saying the global AI industry itself needed to demonstrate that safety mechanisms were keeping pace. Advt She referred to concerns raised within the frontier AI ecosystem and asked who from the global AI industry would stand up and reassure the public about safety, and where the structured collective effort was to provide credible and transparent answers.“Technology alone can engineer the solutions to the vulnerabilities that technology creates,” she said, adding that safeguards would have to arrive quickly and be continuously updated.She also warned that AI, fintech and digital platforms were increasingly operating across borders, creating challenges for regulators as technology could be developed in one country, run on infrastructure in another, use a model from a third jurisdiction and serve customers elsewhere. Advt AI a double-edged sword for fraudSitharaman said the same technology that can improve financial-sector efficiency can also amplify risks.“AI is a double edge sword. It helps us spot clusters and fraud faster. But it also allows the attackers to automate larger, more sophisticated cyber attacks,” she said.‘Fast yet accountable, autonomous yet reversible’Sitharaman said the objective should neither be to block innovation out of fear nor allow technological speed to compromise safety.Instead, she said institutions need to distinguish between operational friction that can be removed and safeguards that are essential to the stability of the system. Advt “The built-in checks, human oversight, and circuit breakers that protect the system, we should keep all this in mind,” she said.She asked whether systems could be built that are “fast yet accountable” and “autonomous yet reversible” when mistakes occur.“Efficiency and safety should not be competing objectives. Good institutional design must make them mutually reinforcing,” she said.
