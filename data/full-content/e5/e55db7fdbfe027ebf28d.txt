Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 10, 2026 at 12:55 PM IST var news_msid = 133998296; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Country’s largest lender State Bank of India (SBI) Chairman Challa Sreenivasulu Setty said the large inflows mobilised through the FCNR(B) route are unlikely to result in abnormal lending, expecting banks to deploy the funds responsibly over time.“At the end of the day, it is going to be responsible lending. Don't think FCNR(B) will lead to abnormal lending,” Setty said on Day 02 of Global FinTech Fest in Mumbai, adding that it would take around three to four months for the banking system to deploy the FCNR(B) flows.Banks mobilised USD 127.22 billion through FCNR(B) deposits by August 31, according to provisional RBI data. Including overseas foreign currency borrowings (OFCBs) and external commercial borrowings (ECBs), total foreign currency mobilisation under the special schemes stood at USD 136.37 billion. Advt The FCNR(B) window was originally scheduled to remain open until September 30, but the RBI advanced the deadline to August 31 following the strong response to the facility. Swaps against eligible FCNR(B) deposits can, however, be undertaken with the RBI until September 11.The scale of mobilisation has raised questions over how quickly banks can deploy the additional liquidity without creating excessive pressure on credit growth.A day prior at the same platform, Global FinTech Fest 2026, third largest private lender Axis Bank MD & CEO Amitabh Chaudhry cautioned that the sharp inflows could put pressure on banks to deploy the funds.“I think this 18 per cent, 19 per cent also has a base effect coming in. Actually, now it could lead to some abnormal lending. We’ll have to deploy this. I hope they don’t do that, but my view is it will end up at some level,” Chaudhry said.SBI’s FCNR(B) mobilisationAs of August 7, SBI had already mobilised nearly USD 6 billion through FCNR(B) deposits. While the bank did not have a formal target, Setty said the traction at that point indicated that mobilisation could reach around $10 billion.“On the FCNR(B), we do not have any specific target. I think we mobilised almost USD 6 billion now on the FCNR(B) deposits, but the way we look at it, the traction indicates that we should be mobilising around USD 10 billion,” Setty had said. Advt SBI Chief had also said that the lender expects the FCNR(B) flows to reduce its reliance on bulk and wholesale deposits rather than materially add to the bank’s overall funding pool.ICICI Bank is said to have mobilised about USD 17.88 billion through FCNR(B) deposits by August 31, followed by HDFC Bank at around USD 12 billion.
