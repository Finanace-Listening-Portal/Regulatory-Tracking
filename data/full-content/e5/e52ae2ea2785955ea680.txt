Summary: An inadvertent error while reporting Futures and Options (F&O) trading losses in an Income Tax Return can result in assessment adjustments, penalty proceedings and prolonged litigation. In Pallavi Sethi v. ACIT/DCIT [2026] 192 taxmann.com 131 (ITAT Agra), decided on 4 November 2026, the assessee reported an F&O loss of ₹19,50,335 instead of the actual loss of ₹19,09,647, resulting in an excess claim of ₹40,688. During assessment proceedings, she voluntarily disclosed the computational mistake and offered the difference for taxation. Nevertheless, the Assessing Officer imposed a penalty of ₹24,410 under Section 271AAB(1A) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, treating the difference as undisclosed income. The CIT(A) upheld the penalty. The ITAT held that an inadvertent computational error voluntarily disclosed during assessment could not automatically constitute undisclosed income under Section 271AAB, particularly when no incriminating document discovered during the search established the alleged undisclosed income. Accordingly, the Tribunal deleted the penalty and allowed the assessee’s appeal. The ruling highlights the importance of distinguishing genuine reporting mistakes from search-related undisclosed income. It also reminds F&O traders to reconcile broker statements, trading turnover, expenses and tax computations before filing their returns. While most participants focus on their trading profits and losses, equal attention is required when reporting these transactions in their Income Tax Returns (ITR). An inadvertent calculation or reporting error while declaring F&O income or losses may result in tax adjustments, scrutiny, penalty proceedings and even prolonged litigation. Even if the taxpayer ultimately succeeds in establishing that the error was unintentional and the penalty is deleted, the experience can be stressful, time-consuming and expensive. A recent decision of the Agra Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) in Pallavi Sethi v. ACIT/DCIT, [2026] 192 taxmann.com 131 (Agra – Trib.), dated 4 November 2026, highlights precisely this concern. It serves as a useful reminder that taxpayers dealing in F&O must exercise due care while computing and reporting their income or losses in their ITR. A Small F&O Loss Calculation Error That Led to Penalty Proceedings Ms. Pallavi Sethi reported an F&O loss of ₹19,50,335 in her ITR, whereas the actual loss was ₹19,09,647. Consequently, an excess loss of ₹40,688 was claimed. During the assessment proceedings, she herself brought the discrepancy to the Assessing Officer’s notice and requested that the excess amount be added back to her income. The Assessing Officer treated the amount as undisclosed income and levied a penalty of ₹24,410 under Section 271AAB(1A), calculated at 60% of the amount. The Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) [CIT(A)] upheld the penalty. What Is the Penalty under Section 271AAB(1A)? Section 271AAB of the Income-tax Act, 1961, provides for the levy of a penalty in specified cases where a search has been initiated and undisclosed income is found. Under Section 271AAB(1A), applicable to searches initiated on or after 15 December 2016 but before 1 September 2024, the penalty was prescribed as follows: 30% Penalty on Undisclosed IncomeWhere the assessee admits the undisclosed income in a statement made during the search, specifies and substantiates the manner in which such income was earned, and fulfils the prescribed conditions regarding payment of tax and interest and filing of the return declaring such income. 60% Penalty on Undisclosed IncomeIn cases not covered by the above conditions. Thus, this is a stringent penalty provision applicable to specified search cases, and not an ordinary penalty applicable to every error or incorrect claim in an Income Tax Return. Does Reporting a Substantial F&O Loss Invite an Income Tax Search? Before proceeding further, it is important to clarify that reporting a substantial loss from Futures and Options (F&O) transactions in an Income Tax Return does not, by itself, invite a search by the Income Tax Department. A search is an investigative action undertaken when the prescribed legal conditions are satisfied. It is not initiated merely because a taxpayer has declared a high trading loss or a substantial business loss. In the present case, the judgment records that a search was conducted in connection with the Ghanaram & Others Group and that the jurisdiction over Ms. Pallavi Sethi’s case was subsequently assigned to the DCIT, Central Circle, Agra. However, the judgment does not explain her precise connection with the group or suggest that her reported F&O loss triggered the search. What Did the ITAT Agra Decide? The matter eventually reached the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Agra Bench. The Tribunal examined whether the excess F&O loss of ₹40,688, which had been inadvertently reported in the ITR, could be treated as ‘undisclosed income‘ so as to attract penalty under Section 271AAB(1A). The Tribunal observed that the excess loss was the result of a clerical computational error. Importantly, the assessee herself had brought the error to the Assessing Officer’s notice during the assessment proceedings and offered the differential amount to tax. The Tribunal also noted that the excess loss was not found recorded in any document discovered during the search. The Assessing Officer had not disputed the assessee’s claim in this regard. Further, her offer to bring the differential amount to tax could not be termed false. The Tribunal made an important distinction between an entry found to be false during search proceedings and an inadvertent mistake voluntarily brought to the notice of the Assessing Officer, even during post-search assessment proceedings. Accordingly, it held that the excess F&O loss claimed by the assessee did not qualify as ‘undisclosed income‘ within the meaning of Section 271AAB(3)(c) of the Act. Consequently, the penalty of ₹24,410 imposed under Section 271AAB(1A) was held to be unsustainable and deleted. The assessee’s appeal was allowed. Important Takeaway: Don’t Let a Reporting Error Turn into a Tax Dispute! The decision in Pallavi Sethi v. ACIT/DCIT serves as an important reminder for taxpayers dealing in Futures and Options (F&O). While the Tribunal granted relief to the assessee, the case also highlights the unnecessary hardship that can arise from an inadvertent error in reporting income or losses in an Income Tax Return. The assessee had to go through assessment proceedings, penalty proceedings and appellate litigation before finally obtaining relief. F&O traders should therefore exercise due care while preparing their Income Tax Returns. They should carefully reconcile their broker statements, trading records, profit and loss statements and other relevant details before reporting their income or losses. The lesson is simple: Winning a tax appeal may bring relief, but avoiding an unnecessary tax dispute in the first place is always better. Practical Checklist for F&O Traders Before Filing ITR Before filing the Income Tax Return, ensure the following: (a) The F&O profit or loss reported in the ITR agrees with the broker’s profit and loss statement. (b) Trading turnover has been correctly calculated. (c) Brokerage, transaction charges and other allowable expenses have been properly accounted for. (d) The figures reported in the ITR are consistent with the books of account and supporting documents. (e) The correct ITR form and applicable schedules have been used. (f) The final computation is carefully reviewed before submitting and verifying the return. ***** Disclaimer: This article is intended for general educational and informational purposes only.
