Pranav Bansal and CS Suman Gupta Summary: RoDTEP and RoSCTL scroll generation reportedly resumed on ICEGATE from 17 November 2026 after an interruption lasting nearly two months, providing relief to exporters whose shipping bills had been processed but corresponding duty credit scrolls remained pending. The authors highlight that both export remission schemes continued to operate during the interruption, with their stated validity extending until 31 December 2026. They refer to DGFT Notification No. 41/2026-27 dated 30 September 2026 and the corresponding Ministry of Textiles extension, along with CBIC Instruction No. 05/2026-Customs dated 23 April 2026 prescribing timely scroll generation. The article examines the financial consequences of delayed credits, including additional working capital requirements, borrowing costs and uncertainty in export pricing. It also discusses a possible connection between the interruption and reduced budgetary allocation, expressly identifying this as the authors’ interpretation rather than an officially established reason. Exporters are advised to reconcile pending shipping bills, verify Export General Manifest filings, monitor scroll generation and create electronic duty credit scrips promptly. The authors emphasise that continuation of notified remission schemes must be supported by adequate funding, predictable processing timelines and transparent communication to protect exporters’ liquidity and international competitiveness. From 17 November 2026, RoDTEP and RoSCTL scrolls have started getting generated on ICEGATE after nearly two months in which shipping bills sat processed but unpaid. The relief is real, but the episode deserves a hard look. A benefit that the Government had notified, and that was legally in force throughout, was held back with no notification, no advisory and no explanation. That should not happen again. What Happened For roughly two months, exporters found that their RoDTEP and RoSCTL claims were simply not moving. The shipping bills carried the claim, Let Export Order was given, the Gateway EGM was filed and the goods had left the country. Everything the exporter had to do was done. The scroll, which is the step where Customs actually sanctions the credit, did not come. This was not an IEC-specific problem. Exporters in Panipat and in other export centres across the country were reporting the same thing. CHAs had no answer. ICEGATE helpdesks had no answer. No public notice from DGFT and no advisory from CBIC or ICEGATE was issued during this period. The Schemes Were in Force All Along This is the core point. DGFT Notification No. 41/2026-27 dated 30.09.2026 continued the RoDTEP Scheme up to 31.12.2026 for exports by DTA units, Advance Authorisation holders, EOUs and SEZ units, at the rates and value caps of Appendix 4R and 4RE as they stood on 30.09.2026. On the same date, the Ministry of Textiles extended RoSCTL up to 31.12.2026 at existing rates. Neither extension was withdrawn, suspended or amended. So every eligible shipment made in this period earned the benefit at the notified rate. The exporter’s right to the credit arose on export. What was missing was only the administrative act of generating the scroll. CBIC’s Own Instruction Was Not Followed CBIC, by Instruction No. 05/2026-Customs dated 23.04.2026, directed all field formations to generate RoDTEP and RoSCTL scrolls within three days, in line with the timeline already laid down for duty drawback under Instruction No. 21/2020-Customs. That instruction came after audit observations on delays in RoSCTL processing. Seven months later, the same delay returned, and on a far larger scale. Why Were the Scrolls Held Back? No official reason has been given, so what follows is our reading of the circumstances, not a confirmed fact. The budget for RoDTEP for 2026-27 is Rs. 10,000 crore, against about Rs. 18,233 crore in 2025-26. At the same time, the 50% restriction on RoDTEP rates imposed earlier in the year was withdrawn and full rates were restored from 23 March 2026. Full rates on a budget cut by nearly half would run out well before the year ends. Scroll generation is the point where the money actually goes out. When funds are short, that is the tap that gets turned off. If this reading is right, the problem is not the delay alone. It is that a budget shortfall was managed by quietly stopping payment of a notified benefit, instead of telling exporters openly. Why This Cannot Be Treated as a Routine Delay RoDTEP Is a Remission, Not a Subsidy The scheme refunds taxes and levies already paid by the exporter that are not refunded anywhere else, such as duty on fuel, electricity duty and mandi tax. Holding it back means the exporter ends up exporting those taxes, which is exactly what the scheme exists to prevent. The Loss Is Real, Not Notional Every exporter builds RoDTEP or RoSCTL into the costing sheet before quoting a price. The orders shipped in September and October were booked months earlier on that basis. The gap is funded by bank borrowing, and interest runs every day. Unlike drawback, where Section 75A of the Customs Act provides interest on delayed payment, there is no comparable interest provision for RoDTEP or RoSCTL. The exporter simply absorbs the cost. Uncertainty Hurts More Than a Lower Rate An exporter can plan around a reduced rate if it is announced. He cannot plan around a benefit that may or may not be paid. This is especially damaging when Indian exporters are competing with countries whose exporters get their dues on time. What Exporters Should Do Now 1. Check Every Pending Shipping Bill Ask your CHA to pull the scroll status for all shipping bills from the halt period and match it against your export register. Do not assume everything has flowed through. 2. Generate Scrips Promptly Once the scroll is generated, the credit sits in the ICEGATE ledger. The e-scrip has to be generated by the exporter, and its validity of one year runs from the date of generation. 3. Check the EGM If a particular shipping bill is still not scrolled, confirm that the shipping line or airline has filed the Gateway EGM correctly. Nothing scrolls without it. The Bigger Question: What After 31 December? Both schemes are notified only up to 31.12.2026. Reports from 16 November say the Commerce and Textiles Ministries are making a case to the Department of Revenue for a longer extension. That is welcome, but the last two months show that an extension on paper is not enough. Exporters need a scheme that is funded for the full period it is notified for, and an assurance that scrolls will not be stopped without public notice. The Government has repeatedly said that taxes should not be exported. RoDTEP and RoSCTL are the tools for that. They work only when they are paid on time.
