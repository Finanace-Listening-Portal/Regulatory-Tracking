Summary : A loyalty point is not automatically a marketing expense and it is not automatically deferred revenue. Under Ind AS 115, the issuer first asks whether the customer’s option to obtain future goods or services provides a material right that the customer would not receive without the current contract. If it does, the option is a separate performance obligation. Part of the current transaction price is allocated to that right using relative stand-alone selling prices, adjusted for the discount and the probability of exercise. Revenue allocated to the points remains a contract liability until the entity supplies the future reward, the option expires, or qualifying breakage is recognised in the pattern required by the Standard. If a third party supplies the reward, the issuer must determine whether it is principal or agent for that promise. Accounting estimates must connect points issued, expected redemption, actual redemption, expiry, cancellations and programme changes to the ledger. This article gives a control model for Ind AS reporters. It does not revive the withdrawn Ind AS 18 loyalty appendix or treat the face value of points as their stand-alone selling price. An option can include loyalty points, renewal rights, future discounts or free products. The question is whether the option gives a benefit incremental to the discount typically available to that class of customer in that market. If the customer receives such a material right only because it entered the current contract, the entity has promised a future good or service and must account for that promise as a separate performance obligation. A routine promotional price open to all customers may not create a material right. Conversely, an apparently small percentage discount may be material when it can be accumulated, transferred, used on high-margin products or combined with other benefits. The assessment should consider the programme terms, customer class, normal selling practices, redemption restrictions, expiry, minimum thresholds and the economic value of access. Management should document the conclusion at programme launch and when terms change. Cohort history and constraint analysis A Material Right Creates a Separate Performance Obligation When the option is a material right, the customer effectively pays in advance for future goods or services. The entity identifies the current product and the loyalty option as separate performance obligations. It does not recognise all consideration as revenue on the first sale and then accrue the expected reward cost. That cost-accrual model would miss the fact that part of the customer’s payment relates to an unfulfilled promise. The contract liability should be presented and tracked consistently with the entity’s other Ind AS 115 balances. When the option is not a material right If the option does not provide an incremental benefit, it is not a separate performance obligation under the customer-option guidance. The entity then accounts for any future purchase when it occurs and evaluates current promotional costs under the relevant Standards. This conclusion must be supported; a policy that declares all points immaterial because individual points have low face value ignores accumulation and redemption economics. Estimate the Option’s Stand-Alone Selling Price The option’s stand-alone selling price is rarely directly observable because the entity does not sell the points separately. Ind AS 115 permits estimation based on the discount the customer would obtain when exercising the option, adjusted for any discount that the customer could receive without the option and for the likelihood that the option will be exercised. The estimate should reflect the actual award catalogue, conversion ratios, exclusions, thresholds and expiry terms. Expected redemption is therefore not merely a provision input; it affects allocation. A cohort model can segment customers by programme tier, channel, geography, age of points and redemption behaviour. The entity should avoid using a blended historic rate when a programme redesign or a new customer segment makes that history unrepresentative. Overrides should identify the evidence, approval and sensitivity. The model should reconcile to the operational points database before it feeds the revenue entry. Allocation using relative values The transaction price is allocated between the current goods or services and the loyalty option in proportion to their stand-alone selling prices. The result is not necessarily the cash face value or the cost of the future reward. For example, if a product has a stand-alone selling price of ₹1,000 and the estimated stand-alone selling price of the points is ₹50, a ₹1,000 transaction price is allocated in the ratio 1,000:50. The arithmetic should use the contract’s actual consideration, including any variable consideration and constraint, rather than a standard ticket price when the customer paid another amount. Recognise Deferred Revenue on Redemption or Expiry Revenue allocated to the loyalty performance obligation is recognised when the entity satisfies that obligation. If the entity itself supplies the reward, satisfaction normally occurs when the promised reward is transferred. The amount recognised for a period should reflect points redeemed relative to the points expected to be redeemed, updated for current estimates. Redemption entries must not exceed the remaining contract liability for the cohort, and programme amendments should preserve an audit trail from old to new conversion rules. When points expire, the entity determines whether the right has lapsed and whether any remaining allocated amount can be recognised. Legal enforceability, published terms, customer communications, extensions and reinstatement practices matter. A system expiry does not establish legal expiry if the entity routinely honours points afterward. Finance should reconcile expired points to the terms and exception approvals before releasing the liability. Breakage Requires an Entitlement and a Constrained Estimate Breakage is the portion of a customer’s non-refundable prepayment that the entity does not expect to be exercised. If the entity expects to be entitled to breakage, it recognises the expected amount as revenue in proportion to the pattern of rights exercised. If it is not highly probable that a significant reversal will not occur, recognition is deferred until exercise becomes remote. This prevents an optimistic non-redemption assumption from accelerating revenue at programme inception. The breakage model should be refreshed at each reporting date. Points can remain outstanding for long periods, and redemption may rise before expiry or during campaigns. The control should compare actual and expected redemption by cohort, investigate deviations, update the stand-alone selling-price and breakage estimates where required, and record the catch-up effect in accordance with Ind AS 115. Unclaimed-property or consumer-law requirements may restrict entitlement and require separate legal analysis. Third-Party Rewards Need a Principal-versus-Agent Decision Where another entity supplies the reward, the issuer determines whether it controls the promised good or service before transfer to the customer. Indicators include primary responsibility for fulfilment, inventory or fulfilment risk and discretion in setting the exchange or price, but the conclusion rests on control of the specified promise. If the issuer is principal, revenue presentation and reward cost differ from an agency conclusion. Settlement paid to the reward partner should not automatically be booked as the measure of the contract liability. The operating file should tie the customer promise to the partner agreement. It should address unavailable rewards, substitutions, refunds, partner failure, point conversion, customer support and who must make the customer whole. Programme descriptions used by marketing should match the enforceable obligations used in the accounting analysis. Monthly Reconciliation and Disclosure File Reconcile opening points, points issued, adjustments, redemptions, expiries and closing points by cohort. Reconcile the operational points population to the contract-liability subledger and general ledger. Retain the material-right memo and stand-alone selling-price model for each programme version. Back-test redemption and breakage estimates and approve evidence-based overrides. Separate issuer-supplied rewards from third-party rewards and document principal-agent conclusions. Explain contract-liability movements and significant judgements in the financial-statement disclosures. Ind AS 115 requires disclosure of contract balances, performance obligations and significant judgements and changes in judgements. Material loyalty programmes may require an explanation of when the performance obligation is satisfied, significant payment or expiry terms and the methods, inputs and assumptions used to estimate stand-alone selling price and breakage. The disclosure should agree to the model and ledger; generic wording that points are “provided for” can obscure the revenue-allocation policy. Frequently Asked Questions Are all loyalty points separate performance obligations? No. They are separate when the customer option provides a material right that the customer would not receive without entering the current contract. Is the face value of points their stand-alone selling price? Not necessarily. The estimate considers the incremental discount, discounts otherwise available and the probability that the option will be exercised. Can the issuer recognise all sale revenue and accrue the expected reward cost? Not when the points create a material right. Part of the transaction price is allocated to the separate option performance obligation and deferred. When is revenue allocated to points recognised? It is recognised as the promised reward is transferred, the option expires, or qualifying breakage is recognised under the Ind AS 115 pattern and constraint. How is expected breakage recognised? When entitlement is expected and a significant reversal is highly improbable, expected breakage is recognised in proportion to rights exercised. Otherwise it remains deferred until exercise becomes remote. Does payment to a reward partner determine the liability? No. The contract liability comes from transaction-price allocation. The partner arrangement also requires a separate principal-versus-agent and cost analysis. Should redemption estimates be updated after initial recognition? Yes. Estimates and the resulting revenue pattern are updated from current cohort evidence, with the accounting effect recorded under Ind AS 115. Key Takeaways Assess whether the option gives an incremental material right before deciding the accounting model. Allocate transaction price using relative stand-alone selling prices, not point face value or reward cost. Recognise the allocated amount on redemption, valid expiry or constrained breakage. Document principal-versus-agent conclusions for third-party rewards. Reconcile points, cohorts, contract liability and disclosures at every reporting date. ****** Disclaimer: This article is a general educational discussion of customer options and loyalty programmes under Indian Accounting Standard 115, Revenue from Contracts with Customers, based on official material checked on 11 November 2026.
