Articles 4 min read HUDCO looks to raise over USD 3 billion through RBI forex window, expects lower cost of funds Housing and Urban Development Corporation (HUDCO) has already raised USD 2 billion under the RBI’s concessional forex swap window and is targeting total mobilisation of slightly over USD 3 billion, as it looks to ease borrowing costs and protect margins amid elevated domestic funding rates. The disbursements are expected to be completed before the window closes on December 31, 2026, HUDCO management said during its Q2 FY27 earnings call. Advt The effective borrowing cost after hedging for the funds raised under the window is around 6.5-6.75%, according to the management. HUDCO expects around Rs 35,000 crore of its total borrowings to come at this cost, which it said would ease pressure on the borrowing side.The lender’s investor presentation showed that it had raised USD 2 billion under the RBI window, with another USD 500 million tied up and USD 1 billion under negotiation. Separately, HUDCO raised USD 1.742 billion equivalent through five-year external commercial borrowings (ECBs) in H1 FY27 at a weighted average cost of 6.16%.HUDCO's overall cost of funds stood at 7.07% in H1 FY27, compared with 7.04% in the year-ago period. Its average cost of foreign currency borrowings was 6.16% during the half year, according to the presentation.The lender is also looking to pass on higher borrowing costs to borrowers through loan repricing. HUDCO has increased its marginal cost of funds-based lending rate (MCLR) by 15 basis points after a gap of two years. Around Rs 20,000 crore of loans are expected to be reset during the second half of the current financial year, which management said would support its bottom line.HUDCO currently follows an annual reset mechanism for its loans, with each tranche being repriced after completing one year. It is also in discussions with some state borrowers to move from annual to quarterly resets to ensure faster transmission of changes in borrowing costs. Advt Margins, PPP lending in focusHUDCO reported an interest spread of 1.76% and a net interest margin (NIM) of 2.64% in H1 FY27. Excluding Rs 20,000 crore of fully serviced government of India bonds, which carry a spread of around 40 basis points, the spread stood at 1.96% and NIM at 2.93%.The management said it was confident of maintaining a spread of around 2% and NIM of 3% through the year. The impact of the 15-basis-point MCLR increase and the ECB borrowings is expected to reflect in the Q3 FY27 results.HUDCO's yield on loans stood at 8.83% in H1 FY27, while its cost of funds was 7.07%. The company has been looking at multiple measures to protect its margins, including tapping the RBI forex window and improving the repricing frequency of loans. Advt On the lending side, HUDCO has sanctioned around Rs 9,000 crore of projects under the public-private partnership (PPP) route, but disbursements have not yet commenced. These projects include the Dharavi redevelopment project in Mumbai, road and highway projects, and a renewable energy project.The Dharavi redevelopment project is in an advanced stage of documentation, and the company expects disbursements to begin in Q3 FY27. HUDCO said it has been working on its PPP financing guidelines for around one-and-a-half years and is cautious about the risks associated with lending to private entities. Advt The company has made investment-grade status a prerequisite for funding such projects and is also looking for strong concession agreements or regulatory arrangements. Its safeguards include third-party appraisal and due diligence, a project monitoring agency and a trust and retention account.The risk premium on PPP lending would be higher than that on loans to state governments, management said, adding that HUDCO does not have a fixed target for PPP financing and would not compromise on asset quality to expand the segment.HUDCO has also been classified as an upper-layer non-banking financial company (NBFC) by the RBI. Management said the impact of the classification on its operations would be limited as the company had been following the relevant requirements from the outset.The classification would entail additional compliance requirements and monitoring of exposure limits, including board approval for exposures beyond prescribed thresholds. However, management does not expect this to create any bottleneck in lending.
