} } Listen to This ArticleThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) does not see any concerns over the asset quality of non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), Governor Sanjay Malhotra said on Wednesday. "We don't see any issue regarding asset quality for NBFCs amid high liquidity conditions in the banking system," Malhotra said at the post-Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) press conference. The comments came after the RBI's six-member MPC unanimously raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.5 per cent from 5.25 per cent on Wednesday. The central bank also changed its policy stance from 'neutral' to 'calibrated tightening' at its October 5-7 meeting. Malhotra said NBFC growth has remained robust, with bank credit to NBFCs growing by about 37 per cent. NBFC credit growth itself has also been strong, although it remains lower than that of banks. He added that NBFC credit to the real economy is also lower than that of banks. "With regard to NBFCs, we are always alert to risks getting developed, not only from an asset quality point of view, but also on demand and price pressures getting built. No rate cut in near term The RBI Governor also clarified that the change in policy stance means a rate cut is not being considered in the near term. "We have clarified by changing the stance that there is no rate cut in the near term. What we are contemplating is either a pause or a rate hike. How deep the rate hike or how shallow the rate hike will be will depend on the evolving macroeconomic conditions and the growth-inflation dynamics." The RBI's decision on future rates will continue to depend primarily on domestic growth, inflation and the broader macroeconomic outlook, Malhotra said. The Governor also explained the meaning of the RBI's revised policy stance. He said 'tightening' refers to a monetary policy approach involving higher interest rates that can constrain or curb economic activity. 'Calibrated' means that the tightening would be milder, with decisions taken in a measured manner based on evolving macroeconomic conditions. He said 'accommodation' refers to a loose monetary policy with low interest rates, while 'neutral' means the policy is neither accommodative nor tightening. He further added that the RBI continues to focus on bringing headline inflation to its 4 per cent target. "While headline inflation is our target, as given to us by the government, and we continue to focus on trying to bring headline to the target of 4 per cent."
