Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Rajasthan Horticulture and Nursury Society Vs ITO (ITAT Jaipur) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Jaipur, condoned a delay of 473 days in the appeal filed by Rajasthan Horticulture and Nursury Society for AY 2016-17 and restored the dispute concerning interest income of ₹1,12,76,940 to the Assessing Officer for fresh examination. The society, functioning under the Ministry of Agriculture, Government of Rajasthan, promotes agriculture and horticulture by providing saplings and seedlings grown in its nurseries. It filed its return declaring nil income. During scrutiny, the AO noted agricultural gross profit of ₹59,62,460 and interest receipts comprising ₹42,94,657 as interest income and ₹69,82,283 as interest on FDRs. In the absence of an explanation supporting exemption, the AO assessed the entire interest amount as income from other sources. The CIT(A) sustained the addition in appellate proceedings in which the society did not appear. Explaining the delay, the society relied on a notarized affidavit and office note sheets showing administrative processing, internal communications and efforts to appoint a tax professional. It stated that it initially believed income-tax litigation formed part of the statutory auditors’ engagement, but both auditors subsequently clarified that such work was outside their scope. The society also referred to its lack of prior tax-litigation experience and its understanding that its public-service activities did not attract income tax. The Revenue opposed condonation, alleging laxity. Applying the substantial-justice principles stated in Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag & Anr. v. Mst. Katiji & Ors., (1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC), the Tribunal accepted that the combined administrative circumstances, auditor-engagement confusion and contemporaneous records sufficiently explained the delay. On merits, the society’s case was that the interest arose from unutilized Central or State Government grants earmarked for specific projects and did not belong to it independently. It contended that the interest had to be used for the relevant schemes, returned to the Government or adjusted against future grants. The order discussed the Tribunal’s earlier decision for AY 2015-16, in which this plea had been raised for the first time and the matter had been remanded for factual verification. That earlier proceeding referred to Ministry of Agriculture Instruction No. 20-12/2007 dated 11.08.2009 and decisions concerning interest on government funds. For the present year, however, the CIT(A) found that the relevant circulars, grant-sanction letters and particulars establishing the source and conditions of the FDRs had not been furnished. The CIT(A) also questioned the claimed treatment under section 56, the set-off of agricultural loss and the availability of expenditure deduction under section 57(iii). The Tribunal recorded that the earlier-year proceedings remained pending before the AO. Since the present dispute likewise required documents establishing that the interest arose from project-specific government grants and was subject to corresponding utilization conditions, it restored the issue to the AO for decision in accordance with the AY 2015-16 directions after verification of all facts and documents furnished by the society. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. The Tribunal did not finally determine that the interest was exempt or direct unconditional deletion of the addition. Ld. Counsel for the assessee stated before us that assessee is a society functioning under the Ministry of Agriculture, Government of Rajasthan, and is engaged in activities relating to the disbursement of funds to agriculturists for the development of crops, nurseries and allied agricultural activities. Being an entity functioning under the State Government machinery, the assessee society is required to follow various administrative procedures, checks and balances before taking any substantive decision, including the appointment of tax professionals and the initiation of appellate proceedings under the Act. Consequently, the process of deciding upon and initiating tax litigation involves internal consultations, administrative approvals and necessary communications among the concerned officials and professionals. It is further explained that several communications took place within the assessee’s organization and with the concerned professionals in connection with the handling of tax matters and the appointment of an appropriate tax professional for filing the appeal. The delay occurred, inter alia, on account of an ambiguity and misunderstanding regarding the scope of work entrusted to the chartered accountant firm engaged for the preparation of financial statements and conduct of the statutory audit. The assessee was under the bona fide impression that the work relating to income-tax appeals and other tax litigation was also covered within the scope of the engagement of the statutory auditor. However, upon subsequent communication with the statutory auditors, it was clarified by both the auditors that representation and other work relating to tax litigation did not fall within their respective scopes of work. This necessitated further deliberations and efforts for identifying and appointing a suitable tax professional to undertake the appellate proceedings. 4. The relevant office note sheets placed on record indicate that the matter was being processed at various levels for the purpose of addressing the outstanding tax demand and arranging for professional assistance. The assessee has also submitted that detailed charts of pending tax demands were prepared on several occasions by its office, which is evident from the notings and documents maintained in the official file. These circumstances lend support to the assessee’s explanation that the matter was not deliberately ignored and that the delay arose in the course of administrative processing and the efforts made to determine the appropriate professional assistance required for filing the appeal. 5. Ld. DR, on the other hand, vehemently opposed the condonation of delay contending that the reason brought out by the assessee reflected laxity on his part in pursuing the remedy of filing appeal and therefore the delay ought not to be condoned. 6. We have heard both the parties. 7. The law governing condonation of delay is now well settled. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag &Anr. v. Mst. Katiji & Ors.(1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC) laid down that the expression “sufficient cause” occurring in the Limitation Act should receive a liberal construction so as to advance substantial justice. The Court held that when substantial justice and technical considerations are pitted against each other, the cause of substantial justice deserves to be preferred. The Court further observed that there is no presumption that delay is deliberate or occasioned on account of culpable negligence or mala fides and that ordinarily a litigant does not stand to benefit by lodging an appeal belatedly. 8. Having regard to the entirety of the facts and circumstances, it is evident that the delay was attributable to a combination of administrative procedures, internal communications, confusion regarding the scope of work of the statutory auditors, the subsequent requirement to appoint a tax professional, and the assessee’s bona fide understanding concerning the taxability of its activities. The material available on record, particularly the office note sheets and the details of the pending tax demands, lends support to the assessee’s contention that the matter remained under consideration within its administrative machinery and that steps were being taken to address the outstanding demand and arrange for filing the appeal.We accordingly condone the delay of 473 days in the filing of the present appeal before us. The assessee was asked to as to why the interest income also be treated as exempt. The matter was carried in appeal before the Ld. CIT(A) where none appeared for the assessee. 14. The Ld. AR of the assessee has submitted that the interest income in question is not taxable in the hand of the assessee because the same is not the income of the assessee but has to be returned to Central or State Government or has to be adjusted against future grant to be given by the Central or State Government. In support of his contention, he has referred to the instruction no. 20-12/2007 dated 11.08.2009 issued by the department of Agriculture and Cooperation of Horticulture Division Government of India Ministry of Agriculture and submitted that the interest earned on the Central share and other unspent funds available as on 1st April of previous financial year will added to the respective scheme fund as the case may be and the interest amount will be adjusted accordingly for the future release of installment. Since, this plea was not raised before the authorities below and it requires verification of the fact whether it is received on the amount of aid deposited with the bank which has not been spent by the assessee or interest was earned on some other received of the assessee on account of its activity therefore, we set aside this issue to the record of the Assessing Officer for verification and afresh adjudication after giving an opportunity of hearing to the assessee.” 16.0 The contention made by the appellant before the ITAT needs the scrutiny and the same cannot be taken into consideration on its face value. That is the very reason due to which the Hon’ble ITAT had directed the AO to examine the issue of taxability of interest income earned from the surplus funds invested in FDRs. It is not known whether the said circular issued by the Ministry of Agriculture is applicable to the facts of the appellant’s case. One more issue comes up for interpretation is whether the circular issued by the Ministry of Agriculture overrides the law enacted by the Parliament. If so, the CBDT could have given a clarification in the form of circular or notification not to tax the interest earned from the grants received from the Govt. either state or central Govt. Be it as that may be, the govt. agencies and boards runs for the public interest and the grants issued were sanctioned in the budgets of the legislature and/or parliament. Such budget will be confined to a financial year alone. If the funds are un-utilised, the same must be surrendered to the Govt. at the end of the financial year by the grantees. The carry forward of the budget allocations and adjustment to the future grants is against the fiscal policy followed in India. Generally, the grants allocated to the corporations or Govt. agencies/boards etc., after utilization of the grants, the balance funds would be left with the entities for its future financial needs and while allocating the grants in future the amounts unutilized will be reduced from the current year grants by the Govt. The funds allocated to a particular purpose are akin to the corpus donations to a trust. Here also, the assessee is a trust but its activities are not covered under definition of charitable purpose. Though the assessee had relied on some decision of the Karnataka state jurisdiction, but how the ratio decidendi is applicable to the appellant’s case is not presentedby the appellant before the AO or the CIT(A). Further, allocation letters of grants are not referred by the appellant. Nothing is prevented the appellant to produce the sanction letter of grant and conditions under which the grant is carried forward along with the accrued interest. Suppose considering but without acceptance, even if the circular is speaking about the interest accrued on FDRs to be adjusted against the future grants, it has to be interpreted that the interest mentioned therein is the interest after tax or interest before tax. Hence, the tax issues have to be decided based on the facts specific to the case rather applying blindly when the facts are not known completely. 17.0 The purposes for which the income derived would not determine the nature and head of the income. The interest earned from the idle funds of the appellant would undoubtedly falls under the income from other sources unless the same is allowed as a deduction or exemption. Unless the Union legislature allows a particular item or source of income under the Income Tax Act as not taxable or any other Act passed by the Union legislature having the overriding power on the provisions of the Income Tax Act nothing will prevent taxing a receipt under the Income tax Act. In my view, without sanction of the Parliament or circulars issued by the CBDT, allowing an interest receipt as exempted without sanction of the parliament is illegal and unjustified. 18.0 Without prejudice to the above, the appellant has not provided any details in respect of sources of the Fixed Deposit Receipts (FDRs) and the interest amount earned out of such FDRs, the claim of the appellant cannot be decided under the category of receipts falling under the tenets of circulars of Ministry of Agriculture, Uol. Whether the grants which are basis for the deposits are received from Union Govt. or State Govt. or any other external agency is not known to AO or CIT(A). Irrespective of the above discussion, how the assessee had claimed set off the loss from agriculture against the interest income is not at all explained by the appellant. As per the scheme of the Act, the agricultural income including loss if any is exempted u/c III of the Act. That means, the income or loss generated from the activities provided in the Chapter III of the Act shall not enter in to the income computation under any head of the income. Due to all the facts and law as discussed above, in my view, the interest earned by the appellant for the AY 2016-17 is taxable under sec.56 of the Act. Consequently, the action of the AO is sustained in taxing the interest income fully without allowing any expenditure u/s 57(iii) of the act since no expenditure is exclusively incurred for earning the interest income from the FDRs. With this discussion, all effective grounds raised against the addition(s) made by the AO are dismissed. …….. 17. Counsel for the assessee was asked to submit the status of the proceedings before the AO.The ld. Counsel for the assessee submitted that case was still pending and had not been adjudicated. 18. In effect, the appeal of the assessee is accordingly allowed for statistical purposes.
