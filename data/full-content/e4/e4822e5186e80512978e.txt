Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Mangal Sponge And Steel Private Limited Vs Commissioner of State Tax (GSTAT Raipur) Summary: The GSTAT Raipur dismissed the appeal of Mangal Sponge And Steel Private Limited and sustained the penalty imposed under Section 129(1)(a) for transportation of MS billets after expiry of the e-way bill. The dispute arose from a consignment dispatched on 12 September 2025 to JTL Industries Ltd. over an indicated distance of approximately 79 kilometres. The vehicle was intercepted on 16 September 2025, about four kilometres short of its destination. The goods had a taxable value of ₹16,56,675, with CGST and SGST of ₹1,49,101 each. Penalty of ₹2,98,202 under each enactment, aggregating ₹5,96,404, was deposited to secure release. The first appellate authority dismissed the appeal by order dated 19 January 2026 in First Appeal No. 640/GST/2025. The appellant argued that the vehicle had broken down three times between 12 and 14 September 2025 and that the delay was beyond its control. It relied on repair documents, vehicle-tracking records and a reference to breakdown in DRC03. It emphasised that the driver had produced the invoice, transport bilty and e-way bill, and that no discrepancy existed in the goods, quantity, value or identities of the supplier and recipient. It contended that absence of tax-evasion intent and the principles underlying CBIC Circular No. 64 dated 14 September 2018 precluded the penalty. The Revenue disputed the breakdown explanation, questioned the documents and GPS report, and argued that expiry of the e-way bill constituted a substantive contravention for which proof of intention to evade tax was unnecessary. The Tribunal held that Section 129(1) did not expressly make intention to evade tax a prerequisite for detention or penalty. Applying principles of strict statutory interpretation, it distinguished the language of that provision from the intent-related language referred to in Section 130. It relied on the statutory-interpretation principles reproduced from Patanjali Ayurved Ltd. and Doba Steel Rolling Mills, the discussion of civil penalties in SEBI v. Cabot International Capital Corp., and the observations in Asian Switchgear concerning mens rea. On that basis, it concluded that the Revenue was not required to establish facts or material showing an intention to evade tax to invoke Section 129(1). The Tribunal distinguished the appellant’s authorities on their facts. It noted the traffic obstruction and intervening Sunday in Satyam Shivam Papers, generation of a fresh e-way bill before the order in Trimble Mobility Solutions, and shorter periods of expiry in Amit Mines and Appollo Plywood. It considered Nezone Tubes factually different and held that the Himachal Pradesh appellate decisions in Bhushan Power & Steel and Godrej Consumer Products were not binding. It also referred to Shree Radhemani Multitrade, in which the Chhattisgarh High Court had distinguished Satyam Shivam Papers on the facts. On evidence, the Tribunal found that the GPS report referred to vehicle CG-04-IM-0317, whereas the intercepted vehicle was CG-04-LM-0317. Although it considered rejection solely on the service provider’s disclaimer incorrect, it held that the vehicle-number mismatch made the report irrelevant. It also noted that the report was generated on 25 September 2025. The repair papers were rejected because they comprised estimates, an unsigned credit memo and other documents which the Tribunal did not accept as authentic repair bills. The breakdown reference in DRC03 was made nine days after interception, did not mention three breakdowns and did not explain the intervening silence. The parties also disputed whether the driver had mentioned any breakdown. The Tribunal rejected the appellant’s contention about non-supply of his statement, observing that there was no material showing a request for its copy or an application under the Right to Information Act, 2005, and that the appellant could have filed the driver’s affidavit. Referring to Rule 138(10), the Tribunal noted the provision for extending validity within eight hours of expiry. It held that the circular’s specified exceptions did not cover this case and did not provide blanket protection for expired e-way bills. The question of law was answered in favour of the Revenue and the appeal was disallowed. FULL TEXT OF THE ORIGINAL JUDGMENT/ORDER 1. The present appeal has been preferred under Section 112(1) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and the Chhattisgarh Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (hereinafter referred to as the “GST Act, 2017”), against the order dated 19.01.2026 passed by the Appellate Authority and Joint Commissioner (Appeal), State Tax, Raipur, in First Appeal No. 640/GST/2025, in the case of M/s Mangal Sponge & Steel Private Limited, Bilaspur (C.G.), whereby the appeal preferred by the appellant was disallowed. 2. Due to involvement of legal issue, this appeal has been heard and decided by Division Bench. 3. The brief facts of the case are that the appellant supplied MS Billets, in transport vehicle no-CG-04 LM-0317 on 12.09.2025 with e-way bills to JTl Industries Ltd. from Bilha, Bilaspur to village- sarora Raipur Chhattisgarh. According to e-way bills approximate distance between these two places are 79 kilometres. The vehicle was checked at Metal Park Rawabhata by Mrs. Ashish Shukla State Tax Officer on 16.09.2025 at 4.00 A.M. The officer asked documents from in charge of vehicle. Mrs. Jitendra Kumar driver presented invoice, e-way bills and billty. Driver gave the statement that MS billets is sent by Mangal Sponge and Steel Bilha to JTL Industries Ltd Raipur. The State Tax Officer found that the e-way bills have been expired on13.09.2025 at 3.59 P.M. but goods are being transporting. He issued GST FORM MOV -09. He assessed taxable value of goods Rs. 16,56,675/- on the basis of invoice. The State Tax Officer assessed CGST Rs. 1,49,101/- and SGST Rs. 1,49,101/- and demand of penalty CGST Rs. 2,98,202/- and SGST Rs. 2,98,202/-. According to provision of section-129(1)(a) GST Act.DRC-07 has been issued on 24.09.2025. Appellant deposited above penalty in compliance of above order therefore vehicle has been released. Aggrieved by the aforesaid order, the appellant preferred a first appeal, which was dismissed as stated above. 4. The main grounds of appeal, as advanced by the appellant, are that the learned Appellate Authority failed to consider and deal with all the submissions made by the appellant during the course of personal hearing. It is further contended that the learned Appellate Authority erred in confirming the imposition of penalty equivalent to twice the amount of tax under Section 129(1)(a) of the GST Act, solely on the ground that the e-way bills had expired. The appellant submits that the e-way bills had expired due to repeated breakdown of the vehicle and not on account of any intention to evade payment of tax. It is contended that, at the time of inspection, the driver produced the GST invoice, transport bilty and e-way bill before the Inspecting Authority. The vehicle had broken down on three occasions, namely, first on 12.09.2025 at 7:22 P.M., again on 13.09.2025 at 5:49 A.M., and for the third time on 14.09.2025 at 2:34 P.M. During the course of repairs, the vehicle was inspected on 16.09.2025 at about 4:00 P.M., when it was only about four kilometres short of the destination. It is further submitted that the appellant relied upon C.B.I.C. Circular No. 64 dated 14.09.2018, wherein guidelines have been issued regarding proceedings under Section 129 of the GST Act in cases where the goods are accompanied by the requisite documents and e-way bill. According to the appellant, the said circular supports the contention that proceedings under Section 129 ought not to be initiated merely on account of an expired e-way bill in the absence of any intention to evade tax. The appellant also relied upon the Global Positioning System (GPS) records of the vehicle to establish its actual movement and to substantiate the explanation regarding the breakdown and subsequent repair of the vehicle. It is contended that the learned Appellate Authority failed to properly appreciate this material evidence in its correct perspective. The appellant has also relied upon various judicial precedents in support of the grounds raised in the appeal. If considered necessary, the same shall be dealt with at the appropriate stage of this order. On the aforesaid grounds, the appellant contends that the impugned order is unsustainable in law and on facts and that the appeal deserves to be allowed. 5. The respondent denied all the material statements of fact and grounds of appeal raised by the appellant. According to the respondent, the Appellate Authority passed a speaking and reasoned order after duly considering the material available on record, and all findings were recorded on the basis of the documents and evidence produced in the proceedings. It was submitted that the vehicle was found to be in movement after the expiry of the e-way bill, and therefore, the appellant had failed to comply with Rule 138(10) of the CGST Rules, 2017. The vehicle-repairing bills dated 13.09.2025 to 15.09.2025 do not provide any assistance to the appellant, as they do not establish that the vehicle had actually broken down during the course of transportation. The respondent further denied the allegation that the impugned order was passed without application of mind. It was contended that the intended distance of transportation was approximately 79 kilometres from Bhilai to Sarora, Raipur. The e-way bill was valid from 12.09.2025 to 13.09.2025 up to 23:59 hours. However, the vehicle was found at Rawanbhata on 16.09.2025, i.e., nearly three days after the expiry of the e-way bill, and only about four kilometres short of the consignee’s premises. The respondent submitted that, in the event of any genuine breakdown of the vehicle, the appellant or the transporter could have approached the proper officer, intimated the alleged breakdown through the common portal, or generated a fresh or extended e-way bill, as permissible under the law. However, no contemporaneous evidence has been produced to substantiate the alleged breakdown. There is no diary entry, police intimation, FASTag/toll record, insurance claim, or roadside-assistance record evidencing such breakdown. It was further submitted that, in his statement, the driver did not state that the vehicle had suffered any breakdown. He merely stated that the goods were being transported from Bhilai to Raipur and produced the invoice along with the expired e- way bill. According to the respondent, the plea of vehicle breakdown was an afterthought and a subsequent invention made only to explain the movement of the vehicle after expiry of the e-way bill. respondent also contended that the GPS report relied upon by the appellant is not a reliable piece of evidence, particularly in view of the express disclaimer contained therein. The judgments and decisions relied upon by the appellant are distinguishable on facts, and the respondent has specifically pointed out the distinguishing features of the present case in his reply with reference to each of the authorities cited by the appellant. It was further submitted that Circular No. 64 dated 14.09.2018 does not extend any benefit to the appellant, as the present case relates to movement of goods after the expiry of the e-way bill and does not fall within the circumstances contemplated by the said Circular. The respondent further submitted that the Hon’ble Supreme Court and various High Courts have held that the intention to evade tax is a relevant consideration while exercising powers under Section 129 of the GST Act. However, such intention cannot be treated as an inflexible or mandatory requirement in every case, particularly where the contravention is not merely a minor or technical error but a substantive violation, such as transportation of goods after the expiry of the e-way bill. According to the respondent, the provisions of Rule 138(10) of the CGST Rules, 2017 and Sections 68 and 129(1)(a) of the GST Act are clear and unambiguous. Since the appellant was found transporting goods after the expiry of the e-way bill and failed to establish any genuine or contemporaneous circumstance justifying such movement, the action taken by the authorities is legally sustainable. Accordingly, the respondent prayed that the appeal, being devoid of merit, be dismissed. 6. The argument of the learned C.A. appearing on behalf of the appellant is that at the time of inspection of vehicle by Tax Officer the driver had invoices, transport bilty, e way bill i.e. all necessary documents only e way bill was expired due to vehicle breakdown thrice. There was Global Positioning System fitted in that vehicle. Appellant Submitted bills of vehicle repairing. When Tax Officer inspected vehicle, it was only 4 Kilometre away from his its destination. Union of India (2025) 54 GSTJ 596 (All) division bench of Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in para 26 held that; – 26. Upon sifting through the various judgments quoted above, one may pen down the salient rules to be applied for interpretation of all statutes especially taxing statutes: – (a) A statute is designed to be workable, and the interpretation thereof by a court should be to secure that object, unless crucial omission or clear direction makes that end unattainable. (b) In a Taxing Act, one has to look merely at what is clearly said. There is no room for intendment. There is no equity about a tax. There is no presumption as to a tax. Nothing is to be read in, nothing is to be implied. One can only look fairly at the language used. A Taxing statute, if it Professes to impose a charge, its intention must be expressed in clear, unequivocal and unambiguous language. The charging section is to be construed strictly regardless of its consequences that may appear to the judicial mind. (c) However, in construing the machinery provisions for assessment and collection of the tax to make the machinery workable ut res valeat potius quam pereat, that is, the court should avoid construction that would defeat the purpose of legislature behind enacting the particular legislation on the presumption that it was to bring about an effective result. (d) Even while applying the strict rules of interpretation, the court may interpret the construction of a provision in harmonious manner by reading all the provisions together to gather the intention from the language employed, its context, and give effect to the intention of the legislature. Ingenious attempt to avoid tax is to be thwarted. The Supreme Court in State of Tamil Nadu Vs. M. K. Kandaswami, (1975) 4 SCC 745 has categorically stated that where a particular section is a charging as well as remedial provision its main object is to plug leakage and prevent evasion of tax and in such circumstances in interpreting such a provision, a construction which would defeat its purpose and, in effect, obliterate it from the statute book should be eschewed. 11. The Supreme Court in case of Commissioner of Central Excise, Chandigarh Vs. M/s. Doba Steel Rolling Mills 2011 Vol-7 Supreme Court Reports 934 has observed that a taxing statute should be strictly construed. It has also observed that the intention of the legislature is primarily to be gathered from the words used in the statute and once it is shown that the assesses falls within the letter of the law, he must be taxed howsoever great the hardship may appear to the judicial mind to be. 12. Section-129(1) of GST Act, is as under: – Section- 129(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, where any person transports any goods or stores any goods while they are in transit in contravention of the provisions of this Act, or the rules made thereunder, all such goods and conveyance used as a means of transport for carrying the said goods and documents relating to such goods and conveyance shall be liable to detention or seizure and after detention or seizure, shall be released, – (a) On payment of penalty equal to two hundred per cent, of the tax payable on such goods and, in case of exempted goods, on payment of an amount equal to two per cent, of the value of goods or twenty-five thousand rupees, whichever is less, where the owner of the goods comes forward for payment of such penalty: (b) On payment of penalty equal to fifty per cent of the value of the goods or two hundred per cent, of the tax payable on such goods, whichever is higher, and in case of exempted goods, on payment of an amount equal to five per cent, of the value of goods or twenty-five thousand rupees, whichever is less, where the owner of the goods does not come forward for payment of such penalty:] (c) Upon furnishing a security equivalent to the amount payable under clause (a) or clause (b) in such form and manner as may be prescribed: Provided that no such goods or conveyance shall be detained or seized without serving and order of detention or seizure on the person transporting the goods. 13. If we consider and interpret the section 129 (1) of GST Act, in the light of above rules of interpretation of taxing statute, we find that it is not mentation in this section that the intent to evade tax is mandatory for invoking of this provision or imposing penalty under this section as mention in section 130 (1) (4) of GST Act. 14. So far as the mens ara is concern in case of SEBI Vs. Cabot International Capital Corp. (2004) SCC Online Bombay 180 Division Bench of Hon’ble Bombay High Court held in para-47 as under: – (A) Mens rea is an essential or sine qua non for criminal offence. (B) A straitjacket formula of mens rea cannot be blindly followed in each and every case. The scheme of a particular statute may be diluted in a given case. (C) If, from the scheme, object and word used in the statute, it appears that the proceedings for imposition of the penalty are adjudicatory in mature, in contradistinction to criminal or quasi-criminal proceedings, the determination is of the breach of the civil obligation by the offender. The word ‘penalty’ by itself will not be determination to conclude the nature of proceedings being criminal of quasi-criminal. The relevant considerations being the nature of the functions being discharged by the authority and the determination of the liability of the contravene or and the delinquency. (D) Mens rea is not an essential element for imposing a penalty for breach of civil obligations or liabilities. (E) There can be two distinct liabilities, civil and criminal, under the same Act. (F) Even the administrative authority impowered by the act to adjudicate have to act judicial and follow the principals of natural justice to the extent applicable. 15. In case of of Asian Switchgear (P) Ltd. v. STO, (2024) 123 GSTR 175: 2023 SCC OnLine Cal 4760: (2023) 79 GSTL 417 division bench of Hon’ble Calcutta High Court held in para 27 as under; – 27. Relevancy of motive in respect of imposition of tax liability to the extent that it results in civil liability has been examined by the Supreme Court in Chairman SEBI vs. Shriram Mutual Fund and Another 2006 volume 5 SCC 361 (Supra), Guljag Industries vs. Commercial Tax Officer 2011 Volume 7SCC 934(Supra) and in State of Gujrat vs. Saw Pipes limited 2003 SCC Online 428 (Supra). The Supreme Court has held in such authorities that, mens rea is not an essential ingredient for contravention of the provisions of a civil act. It has also held that, penalty is directed since the contravention of the statutory provisions as contemplated by a statute is established and therefore, the intention of the parties committing such violation becomes immaterial. The breach of civil obligations which attracts penalty under the provision of a statute would immediately attract levy of the penalty irrespective of the fact whether the contravention has been made by the defaulter with any guilty intention or not. In none of these three authorities the Supreme Court had the occasion to consider the provisions of the Act of 2017. 16. In case of Asian Switchgear (supra) the point for consideration before hon’ble High Court was regarding necessity of intent to evade tax for imposing penalty under section 129 of GST Act, therefore, it is an important verdict on this point. It is held in this case that intent to evade tax is not necessary for imposing penalty under this section and it is a civil liability therefore mens ara is also not required for imposing penalty under this section. 17.
