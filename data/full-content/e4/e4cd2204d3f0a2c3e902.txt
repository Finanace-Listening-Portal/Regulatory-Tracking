Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Cherupushpam Films Private Limited (NCLT Kochi) Summary: The NCLT Kochi Bench admitted Phoenix ARC Private Limited’s petition under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, against Cherupushpam Films Private Limited, which had furnished guarantees and mortgaged its land to secure credit facilities aggregating ₹8.60 crore extended by South Indian Bank to Kerala Chamber of Commerce and Industry for the Kerala Trade Centre project. Phoenix ARC, as assignee of the debt under the deed dated 17 March 2017, claimed a default of ₹14,50,78,158 and identified 30 September 2013 as the NPA and default date. Following the NCLAT’s remand of the matter on 3 July 2026, the Tribunal reconsidered objections relating to the assignment deed, trust deed, alleged forged guarantees, limitation, pending proceedings, settlement, and possible double recovery. The Tribunal found the alleged forgery defence unsubstantiated, treated the Section 13(2) SARFAESI demand notice dated 16 December 2013 as invocation of the guarantee, and held that written acknowledgements in the principal borrower’s financial statements, binding on the guarantor under the guarantee terms, along with the COVID-19 limitation exclusion, rendered the petition within time. It also held that the settlement among creditors in the principal borrower’s CIRP did not expressly release the corporate guarantor, that simultaneous insolvency proceedings are permissible, and that any recovery from the principal borrower must be accounted for. Finding financial debt and default above the statutory threshold, the Tribunal admitted the petition, imposed a Section 14 moratorium, appointed Mr. Jossy Steephen Kattur as IRP, and directed a ₹2 lakh expense deposit by the financial creditor. The Petitioner submitted that the South Indian Bank Limited, the Original Lender, had sanctioned various credit facilities aggregating to Rs.8,60,00,000/- to M/s. The Petitioner stated that the Principal Borrower committed defaults in repayment and the loan accounts were classified as Non-Performing Assets (NPA) on 30.09.2013. Thereafter, the entire debt, together with all rights, title, interest and security interests of the Original Lender, was assigned in favour of the Petitioner pursuant to the Assignment Deed dated 17.03.2017, whereby the Petitioner stepped into the shoes of the Original Lender and became entitled to enforce the debt and securities against the Principal Borrower and the Corporate Debtor. 8. The Petitioner further submitted that CIRP was initiated against the Principal Borrower by order dated 22.02.2022 passed by this Adjudicating Authority in CP(IB)/33/KOB/2021. Since the only substantial asset of the Principal Borrower is the building constructed on the land belonging to the Corporate Debtor, the resolution of the Principal Borrower is affected by the Corporate Debtor’s interest in the underlying property. The Petitioner therefore contended that initiation of CIRP against the Corporate Debtor is necessary for an effective resolution of the debt and the connected assets. 9. The Petitioner stated that, as on 05.10.2022, a sum of Rs.14,50,78,158/- (Rupees Fourteen Crores Fifty Lakhs Seventy-Eight Thousand One Hundred Fifty-Eight Only), inclusive of interest and other charges, is due and payable, comprising outstanding amounts under the three aforesaid credit facilities, with further interest and applicable charges until realisation. The Corporate Debtor is liable to discharge the said financial debt in terms of the guarantees executed by it in favour of the Original Lender. 10. With regard to limitation, the Petitioner submitted that the liability was duly acknowledged by the Principal Borrower in its Annual Financial Statements for the years ended 31.03.2015, 31.03.2017 and 31.03.2018, and the Corporate Debtor had also reflected the said liability as a contingent liability in its Annual Financial Statements. The Petitioner further relied upon the orders of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Suo Motu Writ Petition (Civil) No.3 of 2020, including the order dated 10.01.2022, whereby the period from 15.03.2020 to 28.02.2022 was directed to be excluded for computation of limitation. The Petitioner accordingly contends that the present Petition has been filed within the period of limitation. 11. We have heard the learned Counsel appearing for both sides and have carefully gone through the pleadings, records, documents and written submissions placed on record by the parties. 37. The present Company Petition under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 has been filed by the Financial Creditor against the Corporate Debtor, in its capacity as a guarantor in respect of the financial facilities availed by M/s. Kerala Chamber of Commerce and Industry, the Principal Borrower. It is the case of the Financial Creditor that the Principal Borrower committed default in repayment of the said financial facilities and that, by virtue of the guarantee and security documents executed by the Corporate Debtor, the Corporate Debtor is liable for the financial debt and the consequent default. 38. It is not in dispute that the Financial Creditor had earlier initiated proceedings under Section 7 of the Code against the Principal Borrower in CP(IB)/33/KOB/2021, which came to be admitted by this Adjudicating Authority by order dated 22.02.2022, and the CIRP of the Principal Borrower is presently at an advanced stage. It is also relevant to note that the present Company Petition filed against the Corporate Debtor was earlier dismissed by this Adjudicating Authority vide order dated 15.02.2023, inter alia, in relation to the objections concerning the Assignment Agreement, the non-production of the complete schedules thereto, the Trust Deed of Phoenix Trust FY17-8 and the issue relating to stamp duty/registration of the Assignment Agreement. 39. The said order was challenged by the Financial Creditor before the Hon’ble National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, Chennai Bench. The Hon’ble NCLAT, vide order dated 03.07.2026 in C.A.(AT)(CH)(Ins) No.81 of 2023, remanded the matter to this Adjudicating Authority for fresh consideration. In the appellate proceedings, certain additional documents, which were not available before this Adjudicating Authority at the time of the original consideration of the Petition, were also placed on record and taken into consideration by the Hon’ble NCLAT. Before proceeding further, it would be appropriate to refer to the relevant observations of the Hon’ble NCLAT in its order dated 03.07.2026, which have a bearing on the scope of the present adjudication. The relevant portion of the order is extracted hereunder: 31. The impugned decision rendered by the Ld. Tribunal rejecting the Section 7 application filed by the Appellant was solely on the ground of the aforesaid two procedural anomalies, and as of now the said anomalies stand rectified by the Appellant by supplying the registered Assignment Deed, which ultimately got registered in compliance with orders passed by the Hon’ble High Court, and which can be read in evidence and similarly by supplying the Trust Deed. 32. Since the Ld. Tribunal had not ventured on merits of the application under Section 7 of the Code, and it has proceeded to decide the same, on these limited questions of restrictions, which now stand rectified because of satisfying the aforesaid lacunas, we are of the view that the application under Section 7 of the Code requires consideration, on merits. 33. Thus, the impugned order dated 15.02.2023 is hereby quashed. The application preferred by the Appellant under Section 7 of the I & B Code is relegated back to the Ld. Tribunal which is to be decided on merits afresh, without drawing the embargoes of the Assignment Deed having been not registered or non-presentation of the Trust Deed. 34. Thus, the Respondent agreed to the terms of the Guarantee Agreements. Therefore, any acknowledgement of the debt or liability by the Principal Borrower under those agreements would also be binding on the Corporate Debtor as guarantor. There is also no specific factual denial by the Respondent about the acknowledgements of debt made by the Principal Borrower, as relied upon by the Financial Creditor. 50. In the case of a guarantor, the question of limitation has to be examined with reference to the invocation of the guarantee and the liability arising therefrom. In the present case, the demand notice issued under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act, 2002, calling upon the Corporate Debtor, as guarantor, to discharge the outstanding liability, constitutes invocation of the guarantee. The Hon’ble NCLAT has also held that a demand notice issued under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act can constitute invocation of the guarantee for the purpose of computing limitation in Ujwal Gupta v. Union Bank of India and Anr., (2026) in 18 NCLAT. The relevant portion of the order is as under: 32. Thus, what has been highlighted by the aforesaid judgment is that it would be the terms and conditions of the agreement executed between the parties with regard to the guarantee which would be relevant to assess as to whether the guarantee has been sufficiently invoked or not. Therefore, whether a guarantee may be invoked by giving notice under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act depends on the terms of the guarantee and the content of the notice. If the notice clearly demands payment from the personal guarantor in terms of the guarantee, it can be treated as an invocation of the guarantee. The facts and the wording of the notice are crucial in this determination. 33. We have already observed herein before that notice dated 29.02.2016, given by the financial creditor under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act has sufficiently indicated the Appellant to discharge his liability for the amount mentioned in the notice of which the credit facilities were extended to the CD and simply by the fact that word ‘director’ has been suffixed after the name of the Appellant/Personal Guarantor, the same will not be sufficient to change the character of the Appellant from the guarantor of the CD and therefore, in our considered opinion, the personal guarantee has been rightly considered by the Tribunal to have been invoked by issuance of this notice given under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act and we do not find any illegality therein. 51. It is relevant to examine the issue of limitation in its proper sequence. The loan accounts of the Principal Borrower were classified as NPA on 30.09.2013, which is pleaded as the date of default. The demand notice under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act, 2002, i.e., invoking the guarantee of the Corporate Debtor, was issued on 16.12.2013. However, the limitation period is not to be reckoned merely with reference to the date of issuance of the said demand notice. The Respondent has relied upon certain judgments and contended that the present petition is barred by limitation. However, the material circumstance is that, subsequent to the default and within the subsisting period of limitation, the Principal Borrower acknowledged the subsisting debt and liability in its financial statements for the financial years ending 31.03.2015, 31.03.2017 and 31.03.2018. In terms of Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963, and the guarantee agreement, an acknowledgement of liability made in writing before expiry of the prescribed period gives rise to a fresh period of limitation from the date of such acknowledgement. Thus, the acknowledgement made on 31.03.2018 is required to be taken into consideration for determining the subsisting period of limitation. Thereafter, the period from 15.03.2020 to 28.02.2022 stands excluded in view of the orders passed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Suo Motu Writ Petition (Civil) No. 3 of 2020, and the balance period of limitation available as on 15.03.2020 became available from 01.03.2022. The Hon’ble Supreme Court, in Babasaheb Raosaheb Kobarne & Anr. v. Pyrotek India Private Limited & Ors., Civil Appeal arising out of SLP (C) No.2522 of 2022, 2022LiveLaw (SC) 520, decided on 09.05.2022, has also reiterated that the period from 15.03.2020 to 28.02.2022 is liable to be excluded for the purposes of limitation in respect of proceedings governed by general or special laws. Accordingly, upon giving effect to the aforesaid acknowledgement and the statutory exclusion of the COVID-19 period, the limitation has to be computed afresh, and the present application is within the period of limitation. Accordingly, we find no merit in the contention of the Respondent that the present proceedings are barred by limitation. 52. It is pertinent to note that the aforesaid proceedings before the Hon’ble High Court of Kerala arose out of disputes between the members of the Committee of Creditors in the CIRP of the Principal Borrower. The settlement arrived at between the members of the Committee of Creditors in relation to such disputes, therefore, cannot, by itself, have any bearing on the merits of the present proceedings, particularly when the very same property which is the subject matter of the CIRP of the Principal Borrower continues to remain mortgaged with the Financial Creditor as security for the financial debt, the said property being owned by the present Corporate Debtor. 54. Any order or finding which has the effect of relieving the present Corporate Debtor from its obligations under the Guarantee Agreements, in the absence of any specific term to that effect in the settlement, would give rise to legal and factual complications and may result in a conflict with the rights of the Financial Creditor to enforce the guarantee and security furnished by the Corporate Debtor. Therefore, where the Financial Creditor otherwise satisfies the requisite ingredients and requirements for admission of a Petition under Section 7 of the Code, the settlement referred to above, which pertains to the CIRP of the Principal Borrower, cannot by itself constitute a bar to the present proceedings against the Corporate Debtor. Accordingly, we find no merit in the contention raised by the Respondent on the aforesaid ground. 55. It is also pertinent to note that there is no legal bar to initiation or continuation of insolvency proceedings against a Corporate Debtor in its capacity as a guarantor merely because the CIRP of the Principal Borrower is pending. The liability of the guarantor arises from the contract of guarantee and is co-extensive with that of the Principal Borrower. The pendency of the CIRP of the Principal Borrower, by itself, does not extinguish or suspend the liability of the Corporate Debtor under the Guarantee Agreements. 56. The Respondents have mainly argued in their written submissions that the settlement reached by the Committee of Creditors in the CIRP of the Principal Borrower affects the maintainability of the present petition, in view of the order passed by the Hon’ble High Court of Kerala in Writ Appeal No. 2079 of 2025. However, any arrangement or settlement among the members of the Committee of Creditors concerning their inter se rights and obligations cannot, by itself, affect the rights of the Financial Creditor or extinguish the obligations of the Respondent under the Guarantee Agreements. Unless the settlement expressly releases the Corporate Debtor from its guarantee obligations, it cannot bar the present proceedings. 57. The defence taken by the Respondents that the present proceedings would result in double recovery of the same dues is not tenable. Firstly, proceedings under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code are not in the nature of recovery proceedings. It is settled law that proceedings against the Principal Borrower and the Guarantor can proceed simultaneously. Any amount recovered in the CIRP of the Principal Borrower would, of course, be duly accounted for and given effect to while determining the liability in the present proceedings. Accordingly, we find no merit in the contentions raised by the Respondent in this regard. 58. Having considered all the issues, we find that the amount of financial debt involved is more than Rs.1 Crore and, therefore, there is no bar under Section 4 of the Code to entertain the present Company Petition. The relationship of creditor and debtor between the Financial Creditor and the Corporate Debtor stands established, and the date of default is also evident from the documents placed on record. We further find that there is no legal impediment to the Financial Creditor maintaining the present application and that the Financial Creditor has satisfied the requirements of Section 7 of the Code for seeking appropriate relief against the Corporate Debtor. 60.
