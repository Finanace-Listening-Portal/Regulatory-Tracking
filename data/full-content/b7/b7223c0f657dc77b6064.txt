Summary: The 57th GST Council Meeting held on 8 October 2026 recommended wide-ranging reforms aimed at simplifying GST compliance, improving cash flow, reducing disputes and making tax administration more business-friendly. The proposed measures cover rationalisation of penalties, prosecution and appeal requirements, faster and system-driven refunds, expanded eligibility for input tax credit, and simplified registration and return filing. Important recommendations include automated refund processing, 90% provisional refunds for eligible claims, accumulated ITC refunds on specified input services and capital goods, rationalisation of blocked credits under Section 17(5), and a revised reconciliation mechanism proposed from the April 2027 return period. The Council also recommended changes relating to export services, smoother interstate goods movement, restrictions on transit interception and simplified procedures for small businesses and e-commerce sellers. Sector-specific proposals cover electric vehicle transport, delivery services, agricultural products, intellectual property and scrap transactions. Businesses should evaluate the potential implications for compliance systems, refund planning and working capital. For businesses, these recommendations signal a shift toward a more automated, transparent, and taxpayer-friendly GST ecosystem. This article extracts the key strategic highlights and actionable policy shifts that every business should track. What Changed at a Glance The Council’s recommendations are designed to make GST easier through: Simpler compilation and filing processes Faster refunds Fewer disputes and penalties Enhanced input tax credit availability Sector-specific rate changes Relief for small businesses Easier registration and returns Smoother goods movement across states 1. Appeals, Penalties, and Prosecution: A Lighter Touch The Council has proposed significant relief in the area of penalties and prosecution: Appeal deposit capped at 40% or more in total where the order involves only a penalty. In non-fraud cases, if tax and interest are paid within the time allowed after the order, penalties are reduced. Small pending cases closed: Where the tax involved is below ₹10,000, no notice will be issued. Maximum general penalty reduced from ₹25,000 to ₹10,000. Prosecution threshold raised from ₹1 crore to ₹35 crore. The power to arrest is removed entirely in certain cases. 5% reduced penalty applies in non-fraud cases where tax and interest are paid within the allowed time. These measures are expected to reduce litigation and encourage voluntary compliance. 2. Faster Refunds and Improved Cash Flow A major thrust of the 57th Council Meeting is on unlocking working capital: Accumulated credit now refundable, with timelines: 1 November 2026 – Credit on input services (inverted duty cases) 1 April 2027 – Credit on capital goods, spread over 60 months Automatic cash ledger returns – Excess balance is returned by the system with no officer involvement. 90% upfront refund for exporters – Exporters and inverted-duty businesses get 90% upfront. Less paperwork – System-readable refund forms; no scanned uploads required. These changes are expected to significantly ease cash flow challenges for exporters and businesses with accumulated credits. 3. More Input Tax Credit (ITC) Availability The Council has proposed expanding ITC availability across several sectors: No longer blocked: Outdoor catering Health and life insurance Telecom towers Pipelines outside the factory Goods destroyed or expired as required by law Limited credit, same line of business: Restaurants and outdoor catering Hotels (room tariff up to ₹7,500 per day) Gyms and fitness services Second-hand vehicle dealers: Can claim credit on spares, repairs, rent, and marketing. Only the tax on the vehicle itself remains restricted. 4. Export-Related Clarifications Delivery in an SEZ or free zone and services to a foreign branch can now count as exports, making refunds possible. Clarity is coming on how export payments can be received — in rupees or foreign exchange. 5. Smoother Goods Movement To reduce harassment and delays in transit: No confiscation in transit – Confiscation powers will not apply to goods and vehicles in transit. No checks in transit between states. Stops need intelligence – Vehicles can be intercepted only on specific intelligence, with approval of a Joint Commissioner or above. Keep the e-way bill and invoice anywhere – Greater flexibility for transporters. 6. Easier Registration and Returns Returns: A new mechanism for making amendments for liability and credit mismatches, reducing notices. Starts with the April 2027 return. The new mechanism will go to public consultation before finalization. Registration: Clearer forms and a user-friendly portal, reducing restrictions. Automatic acceptance of amendments (except for principal place of business). Cancellation on request is automatic once returns are filed and dues paid. Small online sellers can sell in other states using e-commerce operators’ warehouses. 7. Relief for Small Businesses Late fee waived on delayed returns filed by the end of the month in which they were due. Optional annual return scheme – Annual return with quarterly payment, approved in principle for businesses selling only to consumers (B2C) with turnover of ₹35 crore or less in the preceding year. 8. Sector-Specific Rate Changes and Exemptions Area Change Electric vehicle passenger transport and rentals Rate aligned with new tractor tyres E-commerce delivery services 5% GST without input credit Retreated tractor tyres Option to pay 5% GST with restricted credit Psyllium (isabgol) seeds Change in rate Intellectual property transfers Always treated as a supply of services Reverse charge on purchases from unregistered suppliers Extended to reverse charge purchases and import of services (turnover ₹75 crore and above) 2% TDS on B2B supplies Applicable as proposed 9. What Businesses Should Do Now 1. Plan refund cash flow – Exporters and inverted-duty businesses should prepare for the new refund timelines. 2. Keep invoice data clean – For automated processes and system-readable forms. 3. Reconcile returns and purchases regularly – The new mismatch system starts with the April 2027 return. 4. Check scrap and waste purchases – Review reverse charge and TDS requirements. 5. Wait for official notification – These are recommended duties, not yet law. Conclusion The 57th GST Council Meeting proposes a comprehensive set of reforms that promise to make GST more business-friendly. From faster refunds and expanded input tax credit to reduced penalties and smoother goods movement, the recommendations aim to reduce compliance friction and improve cash flow. Businesses should begin preparing now — particularly in the areas of refund planning, invoice data hygiene, and return reconciliation — while awaiting official notifications. Source: PIB, Ministry of Finance, 8 October 2026. These are Council recommendations and take effect only through notifications, circulars, and law amendments.
