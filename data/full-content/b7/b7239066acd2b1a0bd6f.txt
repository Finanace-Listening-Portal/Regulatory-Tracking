(Karnataka High Court) Summary: The Karnataka High Court, in Santosh C J Vs Assistant Commissioner of Commercial Tax & Ors., partly allowed a writ petition challenging GST adjudication orders and consequential recovery notices relating to financial years 2020-21 and 2021-22. The proceedings had been initiated under Section 73 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, based on information available on the MGNREGA website. The petitioner submitted that he had already discharged tax liabilities of Rs.75,000 for FY 2020-21 and approximately Rs.3,50,000 for FY 2021-22. He explained that an accountant managing his registered email account had failed to communicate the statutory notices and adjudication orders, and that he became aware of the proceedings only after receiving summons in Criminal Miscellaneous No. 34/2026. The petitioner further contended that the authorities had already recovered 100% of the tax demanded for FY 2020-21 and approximately 11.76% for FY 2021-22. He sought an opportunity to produce work orders and supporting documents to reconcile the disputed liabilities. His counsel also questioned the timing of adjudication and contended that notices uploaded under the GST portal’s additional notices and orders section were not effectively communicated. The Revenue opposed the petition, maintaining that notices had been emailed and uploaded to the portal and that sufficient opportunities had been provided. The High Court considered the particular circumstances of the case, especially the amounts already recovered and the petitioner’s willingness to substantiate his tax payments through documentary reconciliation. It held that a further opportunity should be granted to prevent enforcement of the demands without consideration of all relevant circumstances. Accordingly, the Court quashed the adjudication orders dated 20 January 2025 and 10 March 2025, their corresponding summary orders and the recovery notices issued under Section 79 of the CGST Act. The petitioner was permitted to submit replies, documents and reconciliation statements to the concerned authorities by 2 December 2026, without requiring further notice. The amounts already recovered were directed to remain subject to the outcome of the restored adjudication proceedings. The Court also permitted the petitioner to produce a certified copy of its order before the jurisdictional Magistrate in the pending criminal miscellaneous proceedings for closure, without preventing the authorities from initiating appropriate proceedings depending on the outcome of the fresh adjudication. The decision grants relief on the specific facts without conclusively deciding the broader objections concerning the GST portal’s notice-upload mechanism or the alleged premature completion of adjudication. Mr. Y. Pranay Sharma, the learned counsel for the petitioner, submits that this Court may consider intervening as afore in the light of the following circumstances. [a] The proceedings are commenced based on the data available on the MGNREGA website. [b] The petitioner has discharged liability for these two financial years in offering Rs.75,000/- and about Rs.3,50,000/- for these two corresponding years. [c] The petitioner had availed the assistance of an Accountant who was managing the petitioner’s registered e-mail account and there are lapses on the part of the accountant in not furnishing the necessary details as and when the notices and the impugned orders are passed. [d] The petitioner was not aware of the impugned Adjudication Orders until service of summons in the proceedings commenced in Criminal Miscellaneous No.34/2026 on the file of the Additional Civil Judge and JMFC, Kunigal. [e] The Authorities have recovered 100% of the tax covered under the impugned order for the financial year 2020-2021 and about 11.76% of the tax covered under the demand for the financial year 2021-2022. [f] The petitioner can produce work orders and other documents to justify the tax already offered and to reconcile any difference that they could be. 3. On the other grounds, Mr. Y. Pranay Sharma submits that the Authorities could not have completed the adjudication proceedings before the expiry of three months from the date of issuance of the Show Cause Notice, a ground that has been considered by this Court in granting interim order in W.P.No.17853/2026. However, the learned counsel emphasizes that this Court could, because the petitioner pleads bonafides in not participating in the proceedings and because amounts are recovered, extend an opportunity to the petitioner to produce documents to justify reconciliation. 4. Mr. K. Hema Kumar, the learned Additional Government Advocate who accepts notice for the respondents, submits that the petitioner has been extended every opportunity with the notices/intimation being sent on the e-mail apart from being uploaded on the Portal. In response, Mr. Y. Pranay Sharma submits that the upload on the Portal will not be effective because it was on the page which enabled view of additional notices and orders and not on the page which enabled the view of notices and orders, a page that an assessee would normally see. 5. Be these contentions as they are, in the peculiarities where 100% of the tax for one financial year and 11.76% of the tax for the other assessment year are recovered with the petitioner contending that he has discharged his liabilities in offering Rs.75,000/- for the financial year 2020-2021 and about Rs.3,50,000/- for the financial year 2021-2022 that documents can be produced to ensure reconciliation, this Court is of the view that there must be another opportunity lest a demand be enforced without considering all the circumstances. Therefore, the following. ORDER [A] The petition is allowed-in-part. [B] The following orders/notices are quashed. Sl.
