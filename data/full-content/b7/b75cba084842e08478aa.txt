The consultation received approximately 2.18 lakh responses to 32 questions from over 13,000 persons by the extended deadline of 31 October 2026. The Committee will examine proposed reforms relating to insurance distribution, expenses of management and commissions, market conduct, transparency and digital infrastructure. Its terms of reference include balancing the interests of policyholders, insurers and distributors, mitigating potential adverse effects on employment and insurance agents’ income, recommending suitable transition mechanisms for smaller insurers and distributors, developing a framework for determining commission limits applicable to smaller premium policies, expanding insurance coverage in smaller towns, rural areas and underserved segments, and assessing enhanced transparency and a gradual transition towards fee-based services for large commercial risks. The reforms aim to reduce insurance costs, improve policyholder outcomes, widen coverage and promote a sustainable and professional distribution ecosystem that rewards quality advice, service and long-term customer relationships. IRDAI Chairman Ajay Seth emphasised that the Committee would help make the framework robust, clear and effective while complementing the regulator’s ongoing engagement with the insurance industry. The Committee comprises Suresh Goyal, A.V. Girija Kumar, Praveen Vashishta and Mahesh Kumar Sharma as members, with IRDAI Executive Director Dr. Ayyaj Tamboli as Convenor. The Committee may submit its report with specific recommendations on each term of reference by 31 December 2026. IRDAI will provide secretariat support, data and analytical assistance. The order constitutes an advisory committee and does not itself prescribe revised commission limits or implement the proposed distribution reforms. Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India Press Release | 23rd November 2026 IRDAI constitutes Expert Committee to strengthen the design and implementation of its distribution reforms On 23 September 2026, IRDAI issued a public consultation paper titled “Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution”. The paper sets out a comprehensive framework of reforms covering the structure of insurance distribution, expenses, commissions, market conduct, transparency and the use of digital infrastructure. The public consultation received an overwhelming response. By the extended deadline of 31 October 2026, about 2.18 lakh responses to the 32 questions had been received from over 13,000 persons. These responses reflect the breadth of the insurance ecosystem and the seriousness with which participants have engaged with the proposals. The objectives of the reforms remain to lower the cost of insurance, improve policyholder outcomes, widen coverage, and support a sustainable and professional distribution ecosystem. Insurance should be accessible, affordable and fairly sold, and distribution should reward quality advice, service and long-term customer relationships. These goals are central to IRDAI’s mandate of protecting policyholders’ interests and promoting the orderly growth of the sector, in line with the national vision of insurance for all. IRDAI, today, constituted an Expert Committee of independent economists and insurance professionals to suggest on measures to strengthen the design and implementation of its distribution reforms. Mr. Ajay Seth, Chairman, IRDAI, said, “The Committee’s role is to help make the framework as robust, clear and effective as possible. Its work will complement the ongoing engagement IRDAI maintains with all segments of the industry.” Drawing on the depth of expertise available across the sector and beyond, the Committee is expected to advise on how the proposals can be further refined so that they are implemented smoothly and deliver their intended benefits. The Committee will be chaired by Dr. Indu Bhushan, IAS (Retd.), former CEO, Ayushman Bharat Mission. Its members are Shri Suresh Goyal, Director General, National Council of Applied Economic Research (NCAER); Shri A.V. Girija Kumar, former CMD, The Oriental Insurance Co. Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India Order Ref: IRDAI/INT/ORD/MISC/157/11/2026 Dated: Date: 23.11.2026 Re: Constitution of an Expert Committee to examine the responses received on the consultation paper “Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution” 1. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (“the Authority”) issued a public consultation paper titled “Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution” on 23 September 2026. The paper proposed reforms covering the structure of insurance distribution, expenses of management and commission, market conduct, transparency and the use of digital infrastructure. Stakeholders’ views on these proposals were sought through 32 questions. 2. The public consultation received an overwhelming response. By the extended last date of 31 October 2026, about 2.18 lakh answers to the 32 questions were received from over 13,000 persons. The responses reflect the breadth of the insurance ecosystem and the seriousness with which participants have engaged with the proposals. 3. The reforms’ objectives remain to lower the cost of insurance, improve policyholder outcomes, widen coverage, and support a sustainable and professional distribution ecosystem. Insurance should be accessible, affordable and fairly sold, and distribution should reward quality advice, service and long-term customer relationships. These goals are central to IRDAI’s mandate of protecting policyholders’ interests and promoting the orderly growth of the sector, in line with the national vision of insurance for all. 4. IRDAI hereby constitutes an Expert Committee of independent economists and insurance professionals to advice further on strengthening the design and implementation of its distribution reforms. The Committee’s role is to help make the framework as robust, clear and effective as possible. Drawing on the depth of expertise available across the sector and beyond, it will advise on how the proposals can be further enhanced and refined, so that they are implemented smoothly and deliver their intended benefits. Its work will complement IRDAI’s ongoing engagement with all segments of the insurance industry. 5. The Terms of Reference (ToR) of the Committee are proposed as under: (i) To examine the proposed reforms and recommend additional measures or refinements, if any, that may be incorporated to achieve a fair and sustainable balance between the interests of policyholders, insurers and distributors. (ii) To assess whether the proposed measures carry the potential of adverse impact on employment in the insurance sector and on the income of individual agents and other distribution persons and, if so, to suggest suitable refinements in the proposed reform measures to mitigate such impact. (iii) To suggest appropriate transition pathways, including phasing, glide paths and supportive measures, to address the concerns of smaller distributors and smaller insurers. (iv) To recommend an appropriate framework, including principles, parameters and a review mechanism, for determining the commission limits applicable to smaller premium policies. (v) To examine whether additional measures are required to expand insurance coverage in smaller towns, rural areas and underserved segments, and to recommend such measures. (vi) To examine whether the large commercial risk segment would be better served by enhanced transparency and a gradual shift towards fee-based services, and to recommend the manner and pace of such transition, if found appropriate. (vii) Any other matter incidental or related to the above, as the Committee may consider relevant, or as may be referred to it by the Competent Authority. 6.
