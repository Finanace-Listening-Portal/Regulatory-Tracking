Gains of around Rs 10,000 crore booked by large public sector banks from stake sales and the NSE IPO boosted reported earnings. Adjusted profit also declined 4.5% sequentially, indicating a weaker underlying performance.Higher net interest income, supported by around 19% credit growth, was the main driver of bank earnings. Net interest income grew 15.4% year on year, while a weak base also supported headline profit growth, as banks' net profit had increased just 4.2% in Q2FY26, according to CareEdge Ratings. Advt Public sector banks reported 15.9% profit growth, while private sector banks recorded a 25.1% increase. Both groups grew operating profit at a similar pace, with the difference in net profit growth largely attributable to provisions. Provisions rose at some public sector banks as they began setting aside funds ahead of the expected credit loss (ECL) framework. At other private banks, provisions fell sharply as stress in microfinance and unsecured lending eased.Banks' return on assets (RoA) rose seven basis points year on year to 1.39%, its highest level since Q2FY25. However, excluding one-time items, the improvement was just one basis point. Sequentially, reported RoA rose nine basis points, but declined 10 basis points after adjusting for one-time items, reflecting higher provisions and operating expenses at some large public sector banks.Other private banks recorded the strongest improvement in profitability, with their RoA rising 47 basis points year on year as credit costs eased. The gap between their RoA and that of large private banks narrowed to around 50 basis points from 100 basis points a year earlier. Their median return on equity (RoE) increased 240 basis points, the highest gain among bank groups, although it continued to trail public sector banks.FCNR(B) impactThe impact of foreign currency non-resident (bank) [FCNR(B)] deposits on profitability varied across bank groups. Banks mobilised around USD 133 billion under the Reserve Bank of India's FCNR(B) swap window between June 8 and August 31, 2026. Public sector banks used the inflows to replace costlier bulk deposits, supporting their net interest income relative to assets. Large private banks had not yet deployed much of the funds, weighing on their margins and resulting in a four-basis-point year-on-year decline in RoA. They expect margins to improve after Q3FY27 as the funds are deployed. Advt Banks' median RoE rose 79 basis points year on year to 13.56%, although the increase was smaller at 38 basis points after excluding one-time items. The median Common Equity Tier 1 ratio increased 87 basis points to 15.8%, while the median capital adequacy ratio rose 71 basis points to 17.86%. All banks in the sample remained above the 11.5% capital adequacy requirement.Profit growth is expected to moderate in the second half of FY27 as one-time gains fade, treasury income remains weak and banks build provisions ahead of the transition to ECL norms. Higher provisions could also weigh on returns, while large private banks expect some margin improvement as FCNR(B) funds are deployed.
