Articles 6 min read KCC, MUDRA credit gets UPI push, bringing sanctioned loans closer to point of purchase UPI is moving deeper into the credit ecosystem, with Kisan Credit Card and PM MUDRA borrowers now able to use their sanctioned credit limits for merchant payments. The move extends the existing Credit Line on UPI framework into two large government-backed lending programmes, bringing formal credit closer to the point at which farmers and small businesses actually spend it. The facility was announced by Department of Financial Services Secretary Sanjay Lohiya at the Global Fintech Fest 2026 in Mumbai on Wednesday.The significance of the move is less about creating a new form of lending and more about changing how an already sanctioned loan can be used. Instead of requiring borrowers to access their credit through conventional account-based channels, the available facility can be used when making an eligible merchant payment through UPI. Advt This effectively places UPI closer to the point at which credit is actually utilised. For farmers and small businesses, where borrowing is often linked directly to the purchase of inputs, inventory or other business requirements, that could make formal credit more convenient and potentially increase the usefulness of existing sanctioned limits.Extending credit line on UPIThe development builds on the Reserve Bank of India's Credit Line on UPI framework introduced in 2023. Under the framework, pre-sanctioned bank credit lines can be linked to UPI and used for transactions with merchants, subject to the terms and conditions of the lending bank.The framework therefore predates the latest KCC and MUDRA announcement. What is new is its extension into two large government-backed credit programmes, potentially bringing UPI-based utilisation to a much wider base of farmers and micro and small entrepreneurs.The distinction is important. The facility does not mean that a customer is receiving a fresh loan every time a UPI transaction is made. The underlying credit has already been sanctioned. The UPI transaction provides a digital channel through which the borrower can draw against the available limit.That makes the development better understood as credit utilisation at the point of purchase, rather than the creation of a new point-of-sale lending product.A large existing credit base Advt The potential scale is substantial. There are more than 7.7 crore operative KCC accounts, with sanctioned limits exceeding Rs 10 lakh crore, according to data cited by the Department of Financial Services.The PM MUDRA Yojana, meanwhile, has crossed 57 crore loans with cumulative lending of more than Rs 40 lakh crore, according to the same data.These figures should not be interpreted as the amount of credit that will move through UPI. KCC figures relate to operative accounts and sanctioned limits, while the MUDRA figure represents cumulative lending over the life of the scheme. Actual UPI utilisation will depend on borrower eligibility, participating lenders, merchant acceptance and the specific terms attached to each facility. Advt But the size of the existing customer base explains why the expansion matters. Rather than having to build an entirely new lending market, banks and the payments ecosystem can potentially use an established digital payment network to make an existing pool of formal credit easier to deploy.From payments infrastructure to financial-product infrastructureThe move also fits into a broader evolution of India's digital financial infrastructure. UPI's original role was largely to move money between bank accounts, but the platform has progressively been connected to other financial products, including credit. Advt RuPay credit cards were linked to UPI in 2022, while the RBI subsequently permitted pre-sanctioned bank credit lines to be operated through UPI. The broader direction has been towards making UPI an interface through which customers can access different forms of regulated finance, rather than merely a mechanism for transferring deposits.The KCC and MUDRA expansion takes that process into agriculture and micro-enterprise lending, two areas where the timing and purpose of expenditure can be closely connected to the availability of working capital.For a small trader buying inventory, or a farmer purchasing eligible inputs, the ability to pay a merchant directly against an available credit limit could reduce the friction between having access to sanctioned credit and actually using it.Why point-of-purchase access mattersThe conventional lending journey often separates the act of borrowing from the act of spending. A borrower receives or draws funds, the money sits in an account and is subsequently used for a purchase or business expense.UPI-linked credit can bring those two stages closer together. The borrower can make an eligible merchant payment against the sanctioned facility, making the transaction itself the point at which the available credit is utilised.That could be particularly relevant for small borrowers, for whom access to working capital can be as important as the size of the sanctioned facility. The more seamlessly credit can be deployed for legitimate business expenditure, the greater the potential value of the existing borrowing relationship.For banks, the model also creates a closer connection between credit and digital transactions. But the extent to which that improves underwriting or monitoring will depend on how transaction data can legally and appropriately be used, including applicable consent and data-protection requirements.UPI could become part of the credit cycleThe latest move comes as regulators and the government seek to build a broader digital architecture around credit.RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra, speaking at GFF 2026, highlighted the role of the Unified Lending Interface (ULI) and Account Aggregator framework in enabling data-driven lending, particularly to MSMEs. The objective is to allow lenders to use better-quality financial information and cash-flow data to assess borrowers who may have limited conventional credit histories.This creates the possibility of a more connected digital credit cycle. Infrastructure such as Account Aggregator can help make financial information available, ULI can help lenders access relevant data and streamline lending processes, while UPI can provide a familiar channel through which sanctioned credit is ultimately used.That does not mean UPI transaction histories will automatically become credit scores. Lending decisions will continue to be governed by individual lenders' underwriting processes, regulatory requirements and the rules governing use of financial data.The more immediate change is simpler: the same digital infrastructure that records and facilitates a merchant payment can increasingly sit alongside the credit facility funding that payment.Convenience will have to be balanced with credit disciplineThe expansion also raises a question around responsible use. Making borrowed money as easy to access as a UPI payment could reduce the psychological and practical distinction between a customer's own funds and credit.That makes transaction limits, clear disclosure of the source of funds, appropriate end-use controls and fraud monitoring important as the system expands. These safeguards will be particularly relevant for smaller borrowers, where relatively modest additional borrowing can have a meaningful impact on cash flows.The RBI's Credit Line on UPI framework keeps such credit within the regulated banking system rather than turning UPI itself into a lender. The bank remains responsible for the underlying credit facility, while UPI provides the transaction infrastructure through which it can be used.For the payments ecosystem, however, the direction is significant. UPI is increasingly becoming not just the infrastructure through which Indians move money, but an interface through which different forms of regulated financial services can be accessed.
