Articles 7 min read PNB Housing Finance targets 18-20% growth, 300 affordable segment branches in a year: CFO Vinay Gupta PNB Housing Finance targets annual growth of 18 to 20 percent over the next two to three years. The company plans to expand its affordable branch network from 235 to around 300 branches in this time. Loan assets have grown by 19 percent in the last quarter, and affordable loans are driving significant growth. The shift towards affordable and emerging markets is a strategic focus for the company. The housing finance company, whose book is close to Rs 1 lakh crore, grew its loan assets 19 percent in the September quarter. “In terms of medium term target, we are targeting to grow somewhere between 18 to 20 percent over the next two to three years,” Gupta said.To support the push, the company plans to take its dedicated affordable branch network from 235 today to around 300 by first half of FY28. At the end of quarter two of financial year 2026-27, the company had 404 total branches across the country spanning affordable, emerging and prime markets. Advt Edited excerpts: The rupee has been very volatile over the last year and a half. How has treasury navigated it?Our liability profile is well diversified and we are not dependent on one set of instruments. We are a deposit-taking HFC, with about 40 percent in bank loans and the rest spread across NCDs, ECBs, NHB refinance and deposits.There was a time when the rupee was depreciating and ECBs were quite lucrative because the dollar cost was low. We increased our ECB borrowing then, and even after hedge accounting it worked out cost-effective. Over the last year we also got three rating upgrades, from ICRA, CARE and now CRISIL, so we are AAA from all four agencies. That is another tailwind. Our cost of borrowing has come down by 40 to 50 basis points over the last year, though that was also a falling interest rate cycle.The rate cycle has turned. How does the 25 basis point hike change things for your cost of borrowing and margins?A good part for us is that our asset side is fully floating. We have no fixed-rate book, so when our cost of borrowing rises we can pass it on to customers.Transmission is also never 100 percent on day one. About two-thirds of our borrowing pool is linked to external benchmarks and the rest to bank lending rates, so there is a timing effect. For the 25 basis point hike we have seen, I estimate our cost of borrowing would go up by 12 to 15 basis points. We have therefore decided to raise our asset pricing by 15 basis points from November 1. We are keeping a close watch, and whatever increase we see in our cost of borrowing we will pass on to customers. Advt What is your asset mix today, and how will it change over the next few years?This is an area where we have worked very hard. Historically we operated largely in the prime segment. Over the last three years we launched the affordable vertical, and then an emerging vertical that sits between prime and affordable. Together they are now about 41 percent of our asset base, and prime is the other 60 percent.We have guided to 18 to 20 percent growth, and we grew 19 percent in Q2. With a higher mix from affordable and emerging, we should be able to post even better growth. Our target is to take the share of emerging and affordable to 50 percent within the next two years, and then we will prepare a strategy for the following three years. Over the long term it could go higher. Advt HFCs should also lean on the self-employed segment, where our expertise lies. On salaried customers you can’t compete with banks, which have a better cost of borrowing. For self-employed borrowers we are better placed to structure loans, meet customer requirements and keep turnaround times short.Loans in the affordable segment is growing fastest. What is driving it and what are the tailwinds?In Q2, affordable grew around 45 percent, emerging grew 22 percent and prime grew 12 percent. That is by design. We want to grow in that order.We have invested a lot in affordable. We have 235 dedicated branches, and we have added people there. We had 700 at the start of this financial year and now have more than 1,100. We also enabled our prime and emerging branches to sell affordable products, which gave us an uplift without adding any manpower or infrastructure. All 400 branches can now do affordable business, and almost 20 percent of our affordable business came from prime and emerging branches. Going forward we expect about 25 percent to come from them and 75 percent from the dedicated branches. We expect 40 to 45 percent growth in affordable this year and at least 30 to 40 percent over the medium to long term. Advt What is the branch expansion plan?We always add branches between Q3 and Q4 so they are up and running by year-end and become productive the following year. This year we will again add around 30 to 40 branches, and almost all the investment goes into affordable, with some in emerging. By first half of FY28, we aim to reach around 300 branches, with more investment depending on the potential. What does the funding strategy look like as the book grows?We use all instruments, and money has no colour, so we go with whichever offers the best rate. Because of the rating upgrades, we can renegotiate some of our existing bank loans, and we will work very hard on that.Second, now that we are rated AAA by all agencies, we should command a better rate in the capital market for new issuances. Third is the National Housing Bank (NHB) refinance route, which is an important source of funding for HFCs. The government has increased the allocation for lending to HFCs on the affordable side from about Rs 10,000 crore to around Rs 30,000 crore. These three will be our focus areas.Where are you seeing stronger housing credit demand, urban or rural?Most mortgage-focused HFCs are not in rural housing. They are largely in urban housing, and affordable is also mostly done in urban areas.Demand is fairly good across all segments. Large and medium HFCs have posted healthy growth of 18 to 23 percent, and some as much as 25 percent, including companies with books of Rs 1 lakh crore or more, on both prime and non-prime. The second half is generally better than the first, with the festive season in Q3 and Q4 always the best quarter for the industry because of year-end targets and promotional schemes. With turmoil in equity and gold, housing is a sustainable asset class, and the macro situation should support it.What is your experience with AI and technology so far?We are not at a very advanced stage. We started this year and have a few proofs of concept running. One is pre-delinquency modelling, where bots call customers with a higher propensity to default. That has given very good results and we are now deploying it.Another targets sanctioned but undisbursed cases, which sit with you for a long time unless you chase the customers. That showed good conversion. We have also launched an internal app called Infinity that makes loan onboarding paperless and mobile. It is at 95 percent plus penetration across businesses. We have integrated e-sign and e-stamping, with about 65 percent penetration, and the whole journey should be paperless by the end of this financial year. We are still running proofs of concept on underwriting, and I think next year is when we can measure productivity gains. Cyber threats are rising. How are you positioned?We have not seen any attack or impact so far. We have a security operations centre that scans every email and activity, and we are upgrading it to the latest version, hopefully by the end of Q3. Every year we run a red teaming exercise, where experts try to penetrate our systems, and any vulnerability they find is fixed immediately. We are also implementing a single sign-on and centralised access control.How far along are you on DPDP compliance?We started about six months ago. We have a steering committee that reviews progress regularly, we have onboarded an implementation partner, and we have procured the consent management contracts. Contracts are being amended, and we have identified where consent management has to be deployed, across digital and physical onboarding as well as our deposit touchpoints. We plan to be ready by March, run in parallel for two months to find gaps, and go full flow from Q1.I haven’t hit anything that isn’t doable, but it is a learning curve because suppliers, vendors and employees are all involved. Data masking at source and approvals for sharing outside the norm are being worked out.
