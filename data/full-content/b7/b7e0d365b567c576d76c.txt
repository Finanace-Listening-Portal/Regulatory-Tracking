Introduction and Legal Concept The concept of Input Service Distributor [hereinafter referred to as “ISD”] is a distinct and sui generis compliance mechanism envisaged under the Goods and Services Tax law, to cater to the business exigencies of entities having multi-locational presence and operating under a single Permanent Account Number [PAN]. Under the GST framework, an entity having registration in more than one State or having multiple registrations within a State is treated as “distinct persons” in terms of Section 25 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 [hereinafter referred to as “CGST Act”]. In such a business model, it is a common commercial practice that certain common Input Services, inter alia, audit fees, legal consultancy, advertisement, IT services, rent, insurance etc., are procured at a centralized level, generally at the Head Office or Corporate Office, for administrative convenience and economies of scale, but the said services are actually utilized at multiple locations. In such circumstances, the invoice for such common Input Services is raised by the supplier in the name of the Head Office, and the Head Office is not the ultimate consumer of such services, nor can the branch offices avail the Input Tax Credit [ITC] directly, as they are not the recipient in terms of Section 2(93) of the CGST Act and the invoice is not in their name. To mitigate the said hardship and to ensure seamless flow of credit, the Legislature has introduced the concept of ISD under Section 2(61) read with Section 20 of the CGST Act, 2017 and Rule 39 and Rule 54 of the CGST Rules, 2017. An ISD, by definition, is an office of the supplier of goods or services or both, which receives tax invoices towards receipt of Input Services and issues a prescribed document for the purpose of distributing the credit of central tax, State tax, Union territory tax or integrated tax paid on the said services to its distinct persons having the same PAN. It is humbly submitted that ISD is not a final consumer or user of Input Services, but acts merely as a facilitator, conduit and post office for distribution of ITC of common Input Services procured at a centralized level, without any value addition or consideration, and for the sole purpose of ensuring compliance and preventing cascading effect of taxes. Legal Provisions Governing ISD a) Section 2 of CGST Act, 2017– Defines ISD as an office of the supplier of goods or services or both which receives tax invoices for Input Services and issues a prescribed document for the purpose of distributing the credit of central tax, State tax, integrated tax or Union territory tax paid on the said services to a supplier of goods or services or both having the same PAN as that of the said office.[61] b) Section 20 of CGST Act, 2017– Enunciates the mechanism of distribution of credit by ISD. c) Rule 39 of CGST Rules, 2017 read with Rule 54– Lays down the procedure, method and manner of distribution and invoicing. d) Form ISD-01 and GSTR-6 / GSTR-6A– Prescribed for declaration and returns. 2. Eligibility and Essential Conditions for Acting as an Input Service Distributor The term Input Service Distributor has been defined under Section 2(61) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. To act as an ISD, a person must satisfy the following mandatory Statutory Conditions Statutory Conditions Separate Registration for the ISD As per Section 25(4) and (5) of CGST Act, 2017, an office of a supplier having the same PAN as that of other units is treated as a distinct person. Such office must obtain separate registration as ISD under Section 20 read with Rule 39. Without ISD registration, no distribution is permissible. Same PAN as the Recipient Branches The ISD and its recipient units/branches must have the same Permanent Account Number [PAN]. ISD is essentially a mechanism for distribution within the same legal entity having multiple GSTINs. Receipt of Tax Invoices for Input Services Only ISD can receive invoices only for Input Services. As per the latest amendment vide Finance Act, 2023 w.e.f. 01.10.2023, it is clarified that ISD can distribute ITC only in respect of Input Services. Credit of goods and capital goods cannot be distributed via ISD. Distribution of Credit to Other Units or Branches The credit received must be distributed to other units which are suppliers of goods or services having the same PAN. The distribution must be done through a prescribed document i.e. ISD Invoice under Rule 54(1). Nature of ISD Activities ISD is merely a distributor of ITC, not a final consumer. It cannot charge any consideration or value addition for distribution. ISD does not make outward supply of its own. Restrictions on ISD Activities i) ISD cannot distribute credit of goods – only services. ii) ISD cannot avail ITC for its own consumption and then distribute remaining. If ISD itself is providing outward supply, it must take separate regular registration. iii) ISD cannot deviate from the method of distribution declared in Form ISD-01 during a financial year. Mandatory Compliance After Finance Act, 2023, obtaining ISD registration and filing of Form ISD-01 before availing credit as ISD is mandatory for all persons having multiple registrations under the same PAN and centralized procurement of services. Clarification: ISD cannot charge any consideration / amount from other units for distribution of ITC. It is a credit distribution mechanism, not a supply of service. 3. Nature of Services Covered under ISD All Input Services used by ISD for its other units/branches are covered. Illustratively: a) Advertising and Marketing Services b) Legal, Audit & Professional Consultancy Fees c) Banking, Financial and Insurance Services d) Rent-a-cab / Travel Services e) IT Services and Software Services f) Insurance Services g) Repairs and Maintenance h) Hiring of vehicles, Plant & Machinery, etc. and other common services used by all units. 4. Mechanism – ITC Flows Through ISD Step 1: Service Provider Service Provider issues Tax Invoice for input service to ISD. ISD receives invoice, pays GST under Reverse Charge Mechanism [RCM] if applicable, and takes ITC in its GSTR-3B. Step 2: Input Service Distributor ISD issues an ISD Invoice for Distribution of ITC to eligible distinct persons having the same PAN. Step 3: Recipient Units or Branches Receiving units receive ISD Invoice, take ITC in their GSTR-3B and utilize the same for payment of their output tax liability. 5. Statutory Obligations of an ISD The statute casts the following mandatory obligations upon an Input Service Distributor, as envisaged under Section 20 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 39 and Rule 54 of CGST Rules, 2017: A) Obtaining Separate ISD Registration Every person acting as an ISD is mandatorily required to obtain a separate GST registration as Input Service Distributor under Section 20, distinct from its regular GST registration, even though having the same PAN. B) Receipt of Tax Invoices for Input Services ISD shall receive tax invoices issued by the service providers towards supply of Input Services for common use of its distinct persons / branches / units. C) Payment of Tax under the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM), Where Applicable Where the Input Services received by ISD are covered under the reverse charge mechanism pursuant to Section 9(3) and 9(4) of CGST Act, the ISD shall be liable to pay GST through its GSTR-3B return. D) Availment of Input Tax Credit ISD shall avail the Input Tax Credit [ITC] of such Input Services through its monthly GSTR-3B return, based on invoices reflected in its GSTR-2B. E) Distribution of ITC to Eligible Units ISD shall distribute the said ITC to its eligible units / branches having the same PAN, by issuance of a prescribed document i.e. ISD Invoice under Rule 54(1) of CGST Rules, 2017, within the prescribed time limit. F) Reporting of ISD Invoices in GSTR-6 All details of ITC received and distributed shall be duly reported in Form GSTR-6. G) Filing of Statutory Returns ISD is required to file: i) GSTR-6 – on or before 13th day of succeeding month; and ii) GSTR-3B – for RCM payment, on or before 20th day of succeeding month. H) Maintenance of Books of Account and Records ISD shall maintain proper Books of Account and records of invoices received and ISD invoices issued for distribution, for a period of 72 months from the due date of filing of Annual Return as mandated under Section 35 of CGST Act, 2017. 6. Manner of Distribution of ITC ISD distributes common credit to its units based on any one of the following methods, which is required to be declared in Form ISD-01 and cannot be changed during a financial year: a) Proportion of turnover of each unit for preceding financial year. b) Proportion of number of employees in each unit. c) Proportion of total area occupied by each unit. d): Equally among all eligible units. e): As may be specified by CBIC. Once a method is chosen in Form ISD-01, it is irrevocable for that financial year. Form Isd-01 – Declaration Before Availing Credit as an ISD It is a one-time filing / on becoming ISD. Details to be furnished: Method of distribution Basis / formula iii. List of units to which credit will be distributed Date from which distribution will be effective Any other prescribed details Due Date: On or before availing ITC as ISD. 7. Returns to Be Filed by ISD a) GSTR-6: For furnishing details of ITC received and distributed to units. Due Date: 13th of the month succeeding the month. b) GSTR-3B: For payment of tax under RCM and availing ITC. Due Date: 20th of the month succeeding the month. 8. ISD Invoice – Key Mandates ISD issues invoice to units for distribution of ITC only; No consideration shall be charged in an ISD invoice; iii. Must be issued within 30 days from end of month in which credit is received; Must contain details of invoice, amount of tax, ITC distributed, proportion, etc.; Receiving units shall claim credit based on ISD Invoice details. 9. Advantages and Benefits Arising Out of the Input Service Distributor Model The Input Service Distributor mechanism, as envisaged under Section 20 of the CGST Act, 2017, has been introduced as a business-friendly and revenue-neutral measure with a view to achieve the fundamental objective of seamless credit flow under GST. The benefits accruing therefrom are delineated hereunder: Facilitates Centralized Procurement of Input Services: The ISD model enables an entity having multiple registrations under the same PAN to procure common Input Services such as audit, legal, advertisement, security, software and insurance at a centralized level from its head office, thereby availing economies of scale, better negotiation of rates and administrative convenience. Ensures Efficient and Optimal Utilization of Input Tax Credit: Since the ISD itself does not have any outward supply liability, the entire credit of common Input Services procured centrally is distributed to its distinct persons / branches where such services are actually utilized, resulting in optimum utilization of credit and preventing accumulation of unutilized ITC at head office. iii. Prevents Cascading Effect of Taxes: By allowing distribution of credit to the actual place of consumption, the ISD mechanism eliminates the cascading effect of taxes and upholds the principle that tax should be a pass-through and not a cost to the business. Ensures Statutory Compliance and Transparency: The mandatory filing of Form ISD-01 declaring the method of distribution and monthly reporting through Form GSTR-6 creates a complete electronic audit trail on the GSTN portal, from supplier’s GSTR-1 to recipient’s GSTR-2B. This ensures transparency, uniformity and defensibility during departmental audit and assessment. Simplifies Tax Credit Flow Across Multiple Geographical Locations:The ISD model provides a legally sanctioned and system-driven route for distribution of credit across multiple states and units, thereby obviating the need to resort to alternative methods such as cross-charge which are prone to valuation disputes and litigation under Section 15 read with Schedule I of the Act. 13. Obligation as to Maintenance of Books of Account and Other Records under Section 35 In terms of Section 35 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 read with Rule 39 and Rule 56 of the CGST Rules, every person registered as an Input Service Distributor is statutorily obligated to maintain true, correct and complete accounts and records at its principal place of business as declared in the registration certificate, for a period of seventy-two months from the due date of furnishing of annual return for the relevant financial year. The said obligation includes but is not limited to maintenance of the following documents for production before the Proper Officer as and when demanded during the course of audit, scrutiny, assessment or investigation: a) All original tax invoices, debit notes, credit notes and other duty paying documents received from the suppliers of Input Services on the basis of which Input Tax Credit has been availed, including invoices where tax is payable under Reverse Charge Mechanism; b) All Input Service Distributor invoices issued in terms of Rule 54(1) of the CGST Rules, 2017 for distribution of Input Tax Credit to the eligible distinct persons having the same PAN; c) Acknowledgement of filing of Form ISD-01 declaring the method and basis of distribution, along with any subsequent amendments, if any; d) Copies of Form GSTR-6 and Form GSTR-3B filed from time to time along with challans evidencing payment of tax, late fees and interest, if any; and e) Detailed working sheet for distribution of ITC, showing the methodology adopted, computation of proportion, turnover / employee / area data of each recipient unit for preceding financial year and other corroborative supporting documents to justify the distribution in consonance with the declaration made in Form ISD-01. Failure to maintain the aforementioned records shall attract penal consequences under Section 35(6) read with Section 122 of the Act and may lead to adverse inference during departmental proceedings. 14. Penal Consequences of Non-Compliance with ISD Provisions Any contravention or non-adherence to the statutory obligations envisaged under Section 20 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 39 and Rule 54 of the CGST Rules, 2017 shall attract penal consequences as under: a) Consequence for Delayed Filing of Form GSTR-6:In the event of failure to furnish Form GSTR-6 within the statutory time limit prescribed under Section 20 read with Rule 39, i.e., on or before the 13th day of the succeeding month, the Input Service Distributor shall be liable to pay late fees in terms of Section 47 of the CGST Act. The late fee shall be levied at the rate of Rs. 50 per day of delay, comprising Rs. 25 towards CGST and Rs. 25 towards SGST, subject to a maximum ceiling of Rs. 5,000 per return. It is pertinent to note that such late fee is auto-populated and payable before filing and the delay also results in non-availability of ITC to the recipient distinct persons, causing undue hardship and cash flow impediment. b) Consequence for Incorrect Distribution or Excess Availment of Credit:Where it is found upon scrutiny, audit or assessment that the Input Service Distributor has distributed the input tax credit in contravention of the method declared in Form ISD-01, or has distributed excess credit than available, or has distributed credit in respect of ineligible Input Services including goods and capital goods, or has failed to distribute in accordance with Section 20 and Rule 39, then such excess or irregular credit shall be liable to be demanded and recovered from the recipient units along with applicable interest under Section 50 of the CGST Act, 2017. Furthermore, the said act shall constitute an offence under the Act and shall attract penal provisions under Section 122 for wrongful availment and utilization of ITC, Section 125 for contravention of any provisions where no separate penalty is prescribed, and Section 126 for general discipline, as the case may be, in addition to initiation of proceedings under Section 73 or Section 74 of the Act, depending upon existence of fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression of facts. Conclusion ISD is a mandatory compliance system for companies having multiple GSTINs under the same PAN to distribute ITC of common Input Services. It is not a supplier but only a distributor of credit without charging any fee. Filing Form ISD-01 before starting distribution and filing GSTR-6 by 13th every month with a fixed method for the full financial year ensures fair, transparent and trackable flow of credit from head office to branches, prevents tax cascading, avoids interest and penalty, and keeps the business litigation-free. ****** Disclaimer: This article is for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice.
