Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); CIG Realty Fund Vs Deputy Director (Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA) Tribunal Permits Sale of Attached CIG Realty Properties to Secure Rs. 284.92 Crore Through Escrow Summary: The Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA at New Delhi permitted CIG Realty Fund to sell properties attached under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA), subject to depositing the sale consideration through an escrow arrangement until Rs. 284.92 crore is secured. The amount comprises Rs. 189.92 crore identified as proceeds of crime and an additional Rs. 95 crore directed by the Tribunal. Complete lifting of the attachment was made conditional on deposit of the entire amount. The order was passed by Chairman Justice Munishwar Nath Bhandari on October 12, 2026, in M/s CIG Realty Fund v. Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement, New Delhi, Appeal No. FPA-PMLA-5526/DLI/2023. Background of the attachment The appeal challenged the Adjudicating Authority’s order dated December 2, 2022, confirming a provisional attachment order dated June 16, 2022. The proceedings arose from allegations of cheating investors, misappropriation, forgery and criminal breach of trust involving Unitech Limited, associated entities and individuals connected with CIG Realty Fund. According to the investigation recorded in the order, Rs. 244.92 crore from Unitech’s homebuyer and loan funds was diverted to CIG Realty Fund through interconnected transactions. Investments were channelled through Auram Asset Management Private Limited to acquire interests in landholding companies. After accounting for approximately Rs. 55 crore relating to properties already sold, ED identified a balance of Rs. 189.92 crore as proceeds of crime remaining with CIG Realty Fund. The attachment covered interests in land parcels and approximately 777 flats, villas, plots, shops, office spaces and other properties, including assets under construction. The appellant also referred to attachment of a bank balance of Rs. 1,23,69,335. Fund highlights interests of other investors CIG Realty Fund argued that its schemes contained investments from numerous bona fide investors, besides Unitech and its subsidiaries. It submitted that investors held units representing proportionate beneficial interests in the schemes, while underlying property interests were held through investee companies. Attaching the entire property pool therefore affected investors whose funds were unrelated to the alleged diversion. According to the scheme-wise position presented by the appellant, Unitech’s remaining investment represented 17.68% in Scheme I, 27.16% in Scheme II and 80.32% in Scheme IV. Across these schemes, the balance corpus was Rs. 684.35 crore. Unitech’s share was stated to be Rs. 189.92 crore, while other investors accounted for Rs. 494.43 crore. The appellant also relied on SEBI’s order dated December 29, 2023, directing liquidation of investments, redemption of units and return of investors’ money. Proposal to substitute monetary security The fund initially argued that attachment should have been confined to Unitech’s units rather than the underlying properties. It proposed selling the properties and depositing the amount attributable to Unitech’s proportionate interest in the net proceeds. It sought an arrangement that would protect ED’s identified amount while enabling other investors to recover their investments. The appellant also submitted that the properties had remained attached for a considerable period and that possible encroachments could complicate their sale. ED acknowledged that attachment of the entire properties affected other investors. It agreed to an appropriate arrangement securing Rs. 189.92 crore, but sought an additional amount because the funds had remained with the appellant for a long time. Tribunal directs additional Rs. 95 crore The Tribunal observed that investments in the schemes came from several entities and that attachment of all the properties affected their rights. It considered an arrangement under which money could be secured and placed in fixed deposits, avoiding complications associated with eventual realisation through property sales. Although the appellant expressed willingness to accept a reasonable addition of between 10% and 20%, the Tribunal directed an addition of 50% to Rs. 189.92 crore. That addition worked out to Rs. 94.96 crore, which the Tribunal rounded to Rs. 95 crore. The total amount required to be secured consequently became Rs. 284.92 crore. Escrow mechanism governs property sales The appellant sought immediate release of the properties, followed by phased deposits. ED instead proposed an escrow mechanism linking release to actual sales and receipt of consideration. The appellant accepted that proposal. The Tribunal permitted sales after opening an escrow account in the names of the appellant and ED. The entire sale consideration must enter that account until the stipulated total is deposited. Attachment would stand lifted to the extent of each sale, and ED would release the documents relating to the property sold. ED would transfer the money received through escrow to its account and create fixed deposits, subject to the final outcome of the trial. Complete release after deposit On deposit of the full Rs. 284.92 crore, the provisional attachment would be lifted entirely without further condition. ED was permitted to monitor sales to secure the amount at the earliest, within an outer limit of one and a half years from receipt of the order. The appeal was disposed of on these terms. The order establishes a conditional sale-and-deposit arrangement while preserving the deposited funds pending the trial’s outcome. Brief facts: 2. The impugned order was passed by the Adjudicating Authority against 31 defendants Page 1 issue pertaining to the appellant and accordingly, the facts related to the appellant would be referred. It is a fact that several FIRs were registered against M/s Unitech Limited and its group companies. The relevant FIR in the present case is FIR No. 473/2017, registered at Police Station, Saket, Delhi. The allegations were made against Shri Ramesh Chandra, Shri Sanjay Chandra and Shri Deepak Bajaj, Managing Director of CIG Realty Fund, Unitech Realty Investors Private Limited (now known as “Auram Asset Management Private Limited”) in association with unknown co-accomplices, for criminal conspiracy to cheat the investors by way of misappropriation of money through forgery and criminal breach of trust. 3. Based on the FIR disclosing the predicate offence, an ECIR was recorded by the respondent. The investigation was thereupon conducted under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (in short “the Act of 2002 ”). 4. In the investigation, it was revealed that, out of the funds of the Homebuyers/ loan funds of Unitech Limited, Rs.244.92 Crore were criminally diverted to CIG Realty Fund on the directions of Shri Sanjay Chandra and others. The diversion of funds was made under a complex web of interconnected transactions. The fund aforesaid was invested through M/s Auram Asset Management Private Limited (in short “M/s AAMPL”) to acquire various land holding companies. M/s AAMPL was having S/Shri Ramesh, Sanjay and Ajay Chandra as their directors and beneficial owners. They were designated as Advisor and Manager of CIG Realty Fund. On the investment in CIG Realty Fund, the units were allotted to M/s Unitech Limited in proportion to their total investment. 5. The CIG Realty Fund had launched five different schemes out of which scheme I, II & IV are still active. These schemes are running beyond their prescribed time-limit and therefore SEBI vide its order dated 28.03.2019 has taken adverse view to the activities of M/S AAMPL and has imposed penalty on them. SEBI vide letter dated 17.06.2022 has informed that 139 investors have lodged complaints against CIG Realty Fund. 6. During the course of the investigation, respondent recorded the ‘reasons to believe’ that ‘proceeds of crime’ to the tune of Rs.189.92 Crore exist with the CIG Realty Fund, out of Rs.244.92 Crore received by them. The funds were used to acquire the immovable properties. T he provisional attachment of properties has been confirmed by the Adjudicating Authority. Findings of the Tribunal: 28. I have considered the rival submissions of the parties and perused the record carefully. Detail facts pertaining to the FIR No. 478/2017 has been given in the opening paras. At this stage, it would be necessary to clarify that apart from the aforesaid FIR, many other FIRs were registered against the accused company and its promoters. In the FIR against CIG Realty Fund, Unitech Realty Investors Private Limited (now known as M/s AAMPL), the main allegation was for cheating the investors. It was by way of diverting their funds instead of using it for the purpose for which it was taken. Money was for investment purpose in different schemes floated by the appellant company. The present appeal is arising out of FIR No. 478/2017, thus, fact relevant to the aforesaid has been narrated. The initial diversion of the funds was of Rs.244.92 Crore in the appellant company, CIG Realty Funds. However, it was found that out of aforesaid funds, an amount of Rs.55 Crore was earned out of sale of the properties allotted by CIG Realty to the subsidiaries companies and has been accounted leaving the balance of amount of Rs.189.92 Crore. 29. I am not required to go deep in regard to the allegation other than to take note of the fact that the investment under different schemes floated by the company was not made by one entity but several other entities and therefore prayer was made for causing interference in the provisional attachment of all the properties on a proportionate basis. On account of attachment of all the properties, rights of the other entities invested in the funds of appellant company under different schemes have also been affected. Though, for the aforesaid, those entities could have approached the respondent – ED or this Tribunal. However, now the issue can be settled in view of the fact that the appellant companies are willing to deposit a sum of Rs.189.92 Crore with addition of appropriate amount, so that the properties can be released. The amount so deposited by the appellant can be kept under FD to safeguard the interest of the victims who invested their money with the accused company and otherwise it would be difficult to recover the amount for the benefit of the victims, as the sale of the properties may cause complication and therefore the ED has agreed to accept this proposal. However, with the request to make addition on the amount of Rs.189.92 Crore as funds aforesaid was lying with the appellant company for long time. Ld. Counsel for the appellant opposed the prayer, however, accepted it with the reasonable addition. It may be 10% to 20% over and above the amount to be deposited. In that case, the deposit of amount would be made in the phases, to which initial contest was made by the respondent but ultimately left on the Tribunal to pass an appropriate order. 30. In the light of the discussions made above and finding an agreement between the parties to secure the amount of proceeds of a sum of Rs.189.92 Crore with the addition of an appropriate amount over and above the proceeds, though, initially, a contest to the aforesaid was made by the Ld. Counsel for the appellant, however, to make the proposal equitable, they had agreed to add a reasonable amount between 10% to 20% on Rs.189.92 Crore. Ld. Counsel for the respondent submitted that aforesaid amount is on the lower side. On the aforesaid, both the parties prayed for passing an appropriate order by the Tribunal. Thus, the issue was left open for an appropriate order. 31. In view of the above, and as prayed by the parties, I allow addition of an amount to the extent of 50% to the original amount of Rs.189.92 Crore. The amount aforesaid comes to Rs.94.96 Crore (say Rs. 95 Crore). The appellant would accordingly deposit an amount of Rs.189.92 Crore with addition of Rs.95 Crore. 32. Ld. Counsel for the appellant submitted that the property was acquired long back and remained under the provisional attachment, thus even encroachment may exist on the land parcels. The appellant prayed for immediate release of the properties with the agreement that amount of Rs.189.92 Crore would be deposited within six months from the date of release of property and remaining amount of Rs.95 Crore in another six months. The proposal aforesaid was opposed by the Ld. Counsel for the respondent and submitted that let the appellant sell the properties by opening an Escrow Account in the name of the appellant and the ED with an arrangement that the proceeds on sale of the properties would first come in Escrow Account till it match the amount of Rs.189.92 Crore + Rs.95 Crore. The provisional attachment would stand lifted to the extent of sale as and when it takes place. On deposit of the complete amount, as given above, the respondent would make a formal order for the release of the properties. On receipt of the amount referred above in the Escrow account, it would be taken in the account of ED to be kept in the FD, making it subject to final outcome of the trial. The respondent-ED would release property documents on sale of the property concern. 33. The proposal aforesaid was accepted by the Ld. Counsel for the appellant and accordingly the appellant would be at liberty to sell the properties under attachment, after opening an Escrow Account with an arrangement that entire sale consideration would be kept in the Escrow Account in the name of the appellant and the ED, till the entire amount is deposited i.e. Rs.189.92 Crore + Rs. 95 Crore. On the receipt of the aforesaid amount in the Escrow Account, the ED would take it in their account and will create FD making it subject to final outcome of the trial. 34. On the deposit of the amount given above, the provisional attachment would be lifted entirely without any condition. The ED would however be at liberty to monitor the sale of the properties to secure the required amount at the earliest which would not be later than a year and half, from the date of receipt of the copy of this order. 35. With the aforesaid, the appeal is hereby disposed of. The provisional attachment of the properties and its confirmation would be governed by the directions given above.
