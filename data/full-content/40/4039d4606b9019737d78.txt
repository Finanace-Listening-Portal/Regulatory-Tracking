Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Baidyanath Ayurved Bhawan Private Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Kolkata) Summary: The Kolkata Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal adjudicated cross-appeals of Shree Baidyanath Ayurved Bhawan Private Limited and the Revenue for Assessment Years 2020–21 and 2021–22. The dispute covered alleged bogus purchases, sales promotion expenditure involving gold and silver coins, foreign travel, consultancy payments to related persons, cash expenditure and employee remuneration. The Tribunal condoned a 140-day delay in the Revenue’s appeals and examined whether additions made on an estimated basis could survive where the assessee’s audited books were not rejected under Section 145(3) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. For AY 2020–21, the Tribunal upheld deletion of the estimated 12.5% purchase disallowance of approximately ₹91.25 lakh. The assessee had furnished purchase invoices, banking evidence, GST particulars and transport documents; the mere non-response of three suppliers to notices under Section 133(6) was insufficient to reject the purchases. Relying on judicial precedents including CIT v. Nikunj Eximp Enterprises (P.) Ltd. and PCIT v. R.G. Buildwell Engineers Ltd., it held that unsupported ad hoc disallowances were impermissible on the facts. The Tribunal also directed deletion of the remaining 25% disallowances of ₹26,94,891 on sales promotion and ₹41,80,765 on foreign travel, finding the expenditure connected with business activities. In the assessee’s appeal for AY 2020–21, the Tribunal deleted estimated disallowances of ₹8,40,000 relating to consultancy payments and ₹84 lakh relating to cash expenses. It found no demonstrated defects sufficient to sustain these additions. However, it rejected the assessee’s ground concerning Section 14A, Section 80G and club expenses arising from adjustments in the Section 143(1) intimation, holding that the asserted merger with the scrutiny assessment did not assist the assessee. The Tribunal also disposed of the sales promotion and foreign travel grounds consistently with its findings in the Revenue’s cross-appeal. For AY 2021–22, the Tribunal considered disallowances of ₹33,63,43,852 for sales promotion involving gifts to customers and dealers, and ₹7,52,011 for foreign travel. The CIT(A) had restricted the disallowances to estimated percentages despite acknowledging the commercial purpose of the expenditure. The Tribunal held that the estimates were unsupported by identified defects and directed deletion of the additions. It similarly rejected estimated salary disallowances involving employee remuneration, noting the absence of evidence demonstrating that the payments were excessive or unrelated to business requirements. In the consolidated result, the Tribunal allowed the assessee’s appeals and dismissed the Revenue’s appeals. The decision emphasises the need for specific adverse material rather than suspicion or arbitrary percentages when disallowing business expenditure. Its conclusions are fact-specific, including the acceptance of audited books and documentary support; it does not establish that rejection of books is invariably necessary for every disallowance under the Act. After hearing the rival contentions and perusing the materials available on record, we find that the AO has made an ad-hoc addition of Rs.91,25,518/- at the rate of 12.5% of the total purchases from 3 parties for the only reason that the said parties have not filed their responses to the notices issued under section 133(6) of the Act. We find that the Assessee has furnished complete evidences and details before the AO as stated hereinabove and thus in our opinion, the addition cannot be made merely because the parties have not responded to the notices issued u/s 133(6) of the Act. We find from the documents placed before us from paging 4 to 101 comprising bills, account confirmations, Transport Challans etc.and all these evidences were also filed before the AO. We note that the books of accounts of the assessee were subjected to audit and the auditors have not pointed out any defect or deficiency in the books of accounts or drawn adverse inference. Moreover, the disallowance made is on adhoc basis by applying a percentage of 12.5% on the total purchases from 3 parties which in our opinion is completely wrong and cannot be sustained. In our opinion,if the AO was not relying the books of account then the ld. AO should have invoke the provisions of Section 145(3) of the Act by pin pointing defects in the books of accounts and only then the estimate income or disallowance of expenses should have been done. The Hon’ble Calcutta high court in case of PCIT vs. Ganesh Steel & Alloys Ltd, in ITAT 130/2025 order dated 22 July 2025 on issue of sec 69C and alleged bogus purchases, has held as under:- “The learned Tribunal in the impugned order has held that the provision of Section 69C will not be attracted as the assessee has duly disclosed the total purchases in their books of accounts and the payments made to M/s. Chakradhari Industries were also from and out of the books of accounts with explained sources and, therefore, the source of expenditure was also found. That apart, the source of expenditure was also fully explained and, therefore, the addition made by the Assessing Officer under section 69C of the Act cannot be sustained. Section 69C of the Act states that where in any financial year an assessee has incurred any expenditure and he offers no explanation about the source of such expenditure or part thereof, or the explanation, if any, offered by him is not, in the opinion of the 52 [Assessing] Officer, satisfactory, the amount covered by such expenditure or part thereof, as the case made be, may be deemed to be the income of the assessee for such financial year. It is evidently clear that the said section 69C would stand attracted if the source of expenditure is not satisfactorily explained by the assessee before the Assessing Officer and only then it will be deemed to be income of the assessee. The Assessing Officer alleged the purchases to be bogus. However, the Assessing Officer did not dispute the source of expenditure to be not genuine. Therefore, the assessee was fully justified in contending that the sales are accepted, purchase cannot be treated as bogus. The CIT(A) was of the view that the notices issued under section 133(6) of the Act to the other party remained non-complied and the inspector deputed from the department for verification has reported that the said party is not found. Admittedly, this fact was never brought to the notice of the assessee and the assessee came to know of it only from the assessment order” iii. The series of judgments referred to hereinabove clearly allude to the settled position of law that the books of account have to be necessarily rejected before the AO proceeds to the best judgment assessment upon fulfilment of conditions mentioned in the Act. The underlying rationale behind such an action is to meet the standards of correct computation of accounts for the purpose of a more transparent and precise assessment of income. After considering rival contentions and the material on record, we note that during the assessment proceedings the AO disallowed the foreign travel expenses on adhoc basis without any valid basis. The ld. CIT(A) partly sustained the addition as made by the AO despite recording a finding to the effect that expenses were incurred for the purpose of business of the assessee and connected with exports of the assessee. Considering these facts,we are of the view that no part of such foreign travel expenses can be disallowed in such a manner. In our opinion, if the department was not relying the books of account, then they should had invoked the provisions of Section 145(3) of the Act to reject the books and only then the estimated disallowance of expenses could be made, however, that was not done. The case of the assessee is squarely covered by the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in case of Principal Commissioner of Income Tax vs. R.G. Buildwell Engineers Ltd. [2018] 99 taxmann.com 284 (SC). The relevant portion of the judgment is already reproduced above and the said ratio will apply this issue also. The case is also squarely covered by the decision of the co- ordinate Bench in the case of Mukund Oil Mills Pvt. Ltd. Vs DCIT (ITA No.2534/Kol/2025) dated 12.01.2026. Considering the facts of the case in the light of the above decisions, we are inclined to set aside the order of CIT(A) on this issue and direct the AO to delete the addition. The ground no. 3of revenueappeal becomes infructuous as we have deleted the entire addition made by the Assessing Officer and is accordingly dismissed. The ground no. 6 of assessee appeal is allowed. 23. The Appeal of the Revenue is dismissed. A.Y. 2020-21 ITA No.1032/KOL/2025 – Assessee Appeal 24. It was submitted that 117 employees were paid salary between 1.00 to 2.00 lacs and 110 employees were paid less than 1.00 salary. Particularly, in the case of the appellant, it could be noted that they have a pan-national presence in multiple states, UTs, and cities, and therefore, the quantum of petty cash expenses is likely to be substantial. As long as the expenses are incurred wholly and exclusively for the purpose of business and are not in violation of Sec. 40A (3) of the Act, it does not entitle the tax authorities to take an adverse view. The AO has accepted the fact that the Assessee was maintaining day-to-day books of accounts and the same were subject to tax audits. The sales and purchases of the assessee were duly supported by the bills and vouchers. No expense in cash were incurred in violation of the provisions of section 40A (3) of the Act. In the present case, the AO has not rejected the books of the appellant by invoking the provision of section 145(3) of the Act the Ld. CIT(A) noted that no disallowance was warranted in the probability of expenses being unvouched unless it has been established that the expenditures claimed by the assessee are unvouched. The ld. CIT(A) has also accepted the fact that the accounts were duly audited and that the cash expenses were justified on the grounds of business expediency. The Ld. ld. CIT(A) despite factual findings recorded in the appellate order, chose to restrict the said addition to Rs.84,00,000, being 10% of total alleged cash expenses of Rs.8.44 crores although it was established that cash expenses were in fact cash withdrawals and for the purpose of business of the assessee. 33. After considering rival contentions and the material on record, we are of the view that no part of such cash expenses can be disallowed on ad hoc basis. We note that the books of account were not rejected by invoking the provisions of Section 145(3) of the Act and therefore the estimate of disallowance of cash expenses is without any basis and cannot sustained. The case of the assessee is squarely covered by the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in case of Principal Commissioner of Income Tax vs. R.G. Buildwell Engineers Ltd. [2018] 99 taxmann.com 284 (SC). Considering the facts in the light of above decision, we are inclined to set aside the order of ld. CIT(A) and direct the AO to delete the disallowance. The ground no.3 is allowed. 34.
