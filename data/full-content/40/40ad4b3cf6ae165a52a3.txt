The next 25 bps increase is more likely in February 2027 than December, unless incoming inflation or growth data surprise the Monetary Policy Committee on the higher side.The RBI unanimously raised the repo rate by 25 bps to 5.5% in its October meeting and changed its stance from neutral to calibrated tightening. While the decision initially appeared hawkish, the post-policy press conference provided more clarity and tempered expectations of back-to-back hikes, according to Barclays India. Advt “Calibrated tightening” should mean milder or more measured increases, with the MPC remaining data dependent and without a predetermined terminal rate. The report expects one more 25 bps hike in FY27, but said the timing will depend on incoming data, with February 2027 more likely than December.The RBI raised its FY27 GDP growth forecast by 40 basis points to 7.1%, while its CPI inflation forecast was increased by 20 basis points to 5.2%. The inflation forecast was raised for every quarter through Q1 FY28, with CPI inflation projected at 4.9% in Q2 FY27, 6% in Q3, 5.7% in Q4 and 5.6% in Q1 FY28.Inflation concernsThe report said the RBI's decision to raise rates reflects concerns that inflation is no longer as benign as last year, although there are still limited signs of demand-side pressures. The central bank is also assessing core CPI, inflation diffusion, base effects and whether supply-side pressures are becoming embedded in pricing behaviour.At the same time, economic activity remains resilient. Twelve of 20 high-frequency indicators for the July-September quarter are growing faster than their Q2 averages, with power, fuel, vehicle and two-wheeler sales, personal-loan credit and GST collections showing strong growth. Industrial production in sectors such as coal, cement and manufacturing, as well as credit to industry, has also accelerated. Advt The report said the RBI also faces a large liquidity surplus following larger-than-expected FCNR inflows, although the central bank expects the surplus to fade before the end of the financial year. The RBI is likely to continue using its existing operations to absorb liquidity, while a CRR increase remains its least preferred option.The report expects the repo rate to reach 5.75% after one more hike, but sees a risk that the terminal rate could be higher if FY28 inflation remains above its current forecast of 4.5%. The timing and extent of further tightening will depend on incoming inflation and growth data.
