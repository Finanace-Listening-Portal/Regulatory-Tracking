Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Akhil Bhartiya Pashwachandra Jain Charitable Trust Vs CIT (Exemption) (ITAT Rajkot) Summary: The Rajkot Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal considered the trust’s appeal against the CIT (Exemption)’s order dated 25 August 2023 rejecting its application for registration under Section 12AB. The trust had received provisional registration on 24 March 2022 and filed Form 10AB on 3 February 2023. The order refers to Section 12A(1)(ac)(iii) in describing the dispute and to clause (ii) in recounting the filing. The Commissioner sought the trust deed, evidence of commencement of activities, the expiry date shown on Form 10AC, any earlier registration under Section 12A or 12AA and details of donations. The Commissioner’s show-cause notice cited missing annual accounts under Rule 17A(2)(g), the absence of a translated consent letter, possible benefit confined to a religious community, a potential profit element in the guest house and dining facility, and a lack of expenditure on the trust’s objects. The trust said its facilities were available without discrimination and that it had spent ₹4,06,455 of ₹6,61,437 in receipts on establishment costs and registration charges. The Commissioner questioned wording added to the trust’s explanation of the Gujarati deed, treated the financial records as showing expenditure on bank charges, travel and office expenses, and rejected the application. The Revenue defended the rejection under the current registration procedure and argued that the trust had failed to show genuine charitable activity. The Tribunal reviewed the Gujarati trust deed and English translation, the audited financial statements and Form 10B for AY 2022–23, donation details, the provisional registration order, bank records, documents relating to the property and trustees, and the replies filed before the Commissioner. It found the activities genuine and the objects open to the general public. In applying clause (d) of the Explanation to Section 12AB(4), it rejected the contention that the trust was established solely for a particular community or caste. It treated the Atithigruh as a noncommercial facility for attendees and the Bhojansala as a meal service for poor and needy members of the public. The Tribunal also took note of the establishment and registration expenditure and found no infirmity in the trust’s objects and activities. The Tribunal directed the CIT (Exemption) to examine the objects and activities and grant registration in accordance with law. It held that the CIT (Exemption), Ahmedabad, had jurisdiction because the trust was situated in Gujarat. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT RAJKOT Captioned appeal filed by the assessee, is directed against the order passed by the Learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Exemption), [in short ‘Ld.CIT(E)’], dated 25.08.2023, wherein the Ld.CIT(E) rejected the assessee’s provisional registration granted on 24.03.2022 u/s 12AB r.w.s. 12A(1)(ac)(iii) of Income Tax Act, 1961 (in short ‘the Act’), holding that assessee trust has failed to substantiate the charitable nature of the trust as well as the genuineness of its activities.2. The grounds of appeal raised by the assessee are as follows: “1. That the learned CIT(Exemption) has wrongly rejected the application filed in form no.10AB for granting registration u/s 12A of the I.T. Act, 1961. 2. That, the learned CIT(Exemption), Pune has wrongly passed the order without 3. that, the findings of the learned CIT, Exemption are not justified in law as well as facts of the case and required to be deleted. 4. The appellant craves leave to add, alter, amend or delete any of the above grounds of appeal.” 3. Brief facts of the issue in dispute are stated as under. The assessee, before us, is a charitable trust and has filed application in Form No.10AB under clause (ii) of section 12A(1)(ac) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, on 03/02/2023 before the CIT(Exemptions), Ahmedabad. With a view to verify the genuineness of activities of the assessee a notice was issued by the CIT (Exemptions), Ahmedabad through ITBA portal on 11/07/2023 requesting the assessee to upload certain information/clarification such as: (i) Copy of trust deed. (ii) Date of commencement of activity with proof. (iii) Date of expiry of provisional registration u/s 12AB (as per Form No.10AC) (iv) Registration under section 12A or 12AA of the Income Tax Act, 1961 prior to 01/04/2021. If yes, copy of such registration order. (v) List of all (v) donations received, etc. 4. In response, the assessee furnished its reply before ld.CIT(E), on 21/07/2023. The information submitted by the assessee along with the application was verified by ld CIT(E), wherein various discrepancies were noticed, therefore, a show cause notice was then issued to the assessee on 14/08/2023 and the discrepancies were duly communicated to the assessee, which are reproduced below: “(i) As per the provisions of Rule 17A(2)(g) of the Income Tax Rules, 1962, the application in Form No.10AB shall be accompanied by the self certified copies of annual accounts relating to earlier three years or since inception, whichever is later. Thus, the annual accounts for the F.Y. 2019- 2020, 2020-21 and 2021-22 were required to be submitted along with the application itself. However, it is seen that you have not furnished the same. You have failed to comply with the provisions of Rule 17A(2)(g) of the Income Tax Rules, 1962. (ii) It is seen that (a) to construct and maintain a crematorium for monks and mentioned as the objects of the trust in the trust deed. Said objects appear to be for the benefit of particular religion, community. (ii) Further, objects like construction of canteen, guest house are prima facie of commercial nature with involvement of profit element therein. (iii) You have not furnished translated copy of ‘Sammati Patra’ for perusal. (iv) From the financial statements for the AY 2022-23, it is seen that no expenditure has been incurred on the objects of the trust and almost entire receipts have been transferred to the balance sheet without actual utilization.” 5. In response to the above notice, the assessee furnished its reply on 18/08/2023. The assessee’s reply to the notice and other information furnished by assessee had been duly considered by ld CIT(E) and the issues are dealt with by ld CIT(E), as under: (i) The assessee contended that the objects mentioned in the Trust Deed under clause 4 are meant for the benefit of all individuals, irrespective of their caste, colour, or creed. The Trust further contended that the construction of a crematorium and the maintenance of religious establishments are aimed at providing facilities for Sadhu Saints from diverse backgrounds, emphasizing that these activities are not exclusive to any particular religion or community. The assessee trust further contended that the terms “Atithigruh” and “Bhojansala” are distinct from “Guest House” and “Canteen,” respectively. It stated that these facilities are intended to serve the needs of attendees of various activities without any discrimination based on caste, colour, or creed. (ii) However, the above contention of the assessee is not acceptable and is devoid of any merit. Upon careful examination of the Trust’s contentions and the original Trust Deed, it is evident that certain phrases and clarifications provided by the Trust in its response were not present in the original Gujarati Trust Deed. The assessee trust introduced additional elements, such as “irrespective of their religion and/or caste from all walks of life” to object ‘construction of a crematorium and the maintenance of religious establishments’ and ‘who are seeking food, cloth, and shelter to make their pious life comfortable and careful,” to object ‘maintain and manage temple, gurumandir, upashraya’ which were not part of the original text. (iii) Similarly, the Trust’s interpretation of “Atithigruh” and “Bhojansala” contradicts their commonly accepted meanings. The words “Atithigruh” and “Bhojansala” indeed translate to “Guest House” and “Canteen” respectively, as confirmed by various dictionaries. Also, the assessee has not clarified that these activities are not intended to be carried out in commercial manner. (iv) Considering the above findings, it is clear that the assessee’s contention, specifically the additional phrases and explanations added in its response, deviates from the original intent and wording of the Trust Deed. These considered as valid representations of the Trust’s objectives. The assessee could not prove that the objects of the trust are not for a particular caste, creed, religion, or community. It also failed to substantiate that the object of construction and maintenance of canteen and guest house is not of commercial nature involving any profit motive in it. In the light of the discrepancies identified and the assessee’s deviation from the original Trust Deed, the contentions of the assessee are hereby rejected. (v) On the query of no expenditure incurred on the objects of the trust, the assessee contended that the trust has applied Rs. 4,06,455/- out of total receipt of Rs. 6,61,437/- by way of establishment expenses and registration charges. However, said contention of the assessee is also not acceptable. In reply dt. 21/07/2023, the assessee has stated that major activities of the trust are of education, medical and other activities and its application of fund for last year was Rs. 14,585/-. However, as seen from the I&E account (Gujarati and English) said expenditure is made only on bank charges, travelling and office expenses. No expenditure is made by the trust on any of its objects as has been contended by it. In I&E account, surplus of Rs. 6,42,852/- is shown which is further reflected in the balance sheet. It is, thus, clear that the trust has not incurred any expenditure on any of its objects during the FY 2021-22. As such, the trust has not carried out any activity since its inception in 2019. 6. In view of the above discussion, the ld CIT(E) noted that the assessee has failed to substantiate the charitable nature of the trust as well as the genuineness of its activities and compliance to requirements of any other law for the time being in force by the trust / institution as are material for the purpose of achieving its objects. Therefore, the application filed by the assessee was rejected by ld CIT( E). 7. Aggrieved by the order of Ld. CIT(E), the assessee is in appeal before us. 8. Shri Kalpesh Doshi, Learned Counsel for the assessee, argued that the objects of the trust under consideration are not for particular community and caste The Ld. Counsel took us through paper book page no.7 and explained that most of the objects are for general public purposes. The ld Counsel stated that objects mentioned in the Trust Deed are meant for the benefit of all individuals, irrespective of their caste, colour, or creed. The establishments are aimed at providing facilities for Sadhu Saints from diverse back grounds, emphasizing that these activities are not exclusive to any particular religion or community. The “Atithigruh” and “Bhojansala” are facilities for attendees of various activities without any discrimination based on caste, colour, or creed. The Ld. Counsel also took us through paper book page no.37 and stated that assessee has submitted all documents and evidences before the Ld. CIT(E), therefore, the registration should be granted to the assessee. 9. The Ld. Counsel also stated that assessee has submitted enough documents and evidences to prove the genuineness of its activities and objects, however Ld. CIT(E) rejected the same without considering the documents and evidence in right perspective, which is not acceptable. 10. On the other hand, the Learned Commissioner of Income Tax – Departmental Representative (Ld. CIT-DR) submitted that first of all the assessee has failed to prove the genuineness of its activities. The assessee trust has not been carried out any activities after granting provisional registration. The Ld. CIT-DR also pointed out that some of the objects of the trust are commercial in nature, as the assessee trust is running “Atithigruh” and “Bhojansala” which are commercial activities. The Ld. CIT-DR also pointed out that there are lot of difference between old provisions and new provisions, for granting registration. Under old provisions, the registration used to be granted u/s 12AA of the Act. However, under the new provisions, the registration is granted to a trust provided the assessee trust fulfils the conditions mentioned in section 12AB read with Rule 17A of the Income Tax Rules. Therefore, since the assessee trust has not carried out any charitable activities, since its incorporation and some of the objects are commercial in nature, therefore under Clause (iii) of Section 12A(1)(ac)(iii) of the Act. Therefore, registration should not be granted to the assessee. 11. We have carefully considered the facts of the case, the submission of the Learned Counsel for the assessee and ld DR for the Revenue and evidences on record. We note that before us, the assessee submitted the following documents and evidences, viz: (i) Copy of Trust Deed in vernacular language and English translation of the same, (ii) Copy of Audit Report for FY.2021-22 relevant AY 2022-23, (iii) Copy of Form No.10B for AY.2022-23, (iv) Details of donation received, (v) Copy of existing order u/s 12A of form no.10AC, (vi) Copy of bank statement, (vii) Copies of Aadhar Card of trustees, (viii) Consent letter by well- wisher and revenue record of property, (ix) Undertaking by trustee, (x) Audited financial statements for FY.2021-22, (xi) Copies of reply filed by assessee before Ld CIT(E). 12. We have gone through the above documents and evidences, and noticed that assessee has satisfied the genuineness of its activities and the nature of activities. After going through the objects of the assessee- trust, we find that objects of the assessee -trust are for general public benefit and not restricted to a particular community or caste. We also find that as per Clause (d) of Explanation of Section 12AB of the Act, the specified violation is stated, which reads as follows: “12AB procedure for fresh registration ………………. (d) the trust or institution established for charitable purpose created or established after the commencement of this Act, has applied any part of its income for the benefit of any particular religious community or caste; or…..” We have gone through the above Clause (d) of Explanation of sub-section (4) of section 12AB of the Act and noted that the assessee -trust, under or cast, it is open for the benefit of the general public, as evident from the object of the assessee- trust, therefore we do not accept the contention raised by the Ld. CIT-DR for the Revenue, to the effect that the assessee- trust was created only for the benefit of particular persons or community or caste. As per the objects of the trust, the assessee- trust under consideration, is open for benefit for all sections of society and general public. That is, the facilities provided by the assessee- trust are intended to serve the needs of attendees of various activities without any discrimination based on caste, colour, or creed. Hence, assessee- trust deserve for registration. 13. So far the activities of running “Atithigruh” and “Bhojansala” are concerned, we find that these are not commercial activity. The “Atithigruh” facilities are intended to serve the needs of attendees of various activities without any discrimination based on caste, colour, or creed, hence, it is not a commercial activity. Besides, “Bhojansala” is only for providing food to the poor and needy persons of the general public without any discrimination. It is open only for poor and needy persons to provide them food. No any specific community and caste is taking benefit, exclusively, for the “Atithigruh” and “Bhojansala” these”Atithigruh” and “Bhojansala” are needed to run the charitable activities. Therefore, none of the objects of the assessee-trust, are restricted to any particular community and caste. 14. So far the expenditure incurred on the objects of the trust, are concerned, we find that assessee -trust has applied Rs. 4,06,455/- out of total receipt of Rs. 6,61,437/- by way of establishment expenses and registration charges. In future, the assessee-trust would spend the amount construction of a crematorium and the maintenance thereof is for the objects of the trust, which is not for particular caste, or community. Hence, we do not find any infirmity in the objects and activities of the trust. Therefore, we direct the ld CIT(E) to examine the objects and activities of the trust and grant the registration, in accordance with law. 15. In ground number-2 the assessee raised the objection the learned CIT(Exemption), Pune has wrongly passed the order without assuming proper jurisdiction under the law. We find that assessee -trust is situated in Gujarat, therefore learned CIT (E) -Ahmedabad, would have jurisdiction, over the assessee, who, will pass the order in accordance with law. 16. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes.
