Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Essel Mining and Industries Limited (NCLT Mumbai) Summary: The NCLT Mumbai Bench-II allowed a first-motion company scheme application filed by Aditya Birla Renewables Limited and four transferor companies for directions concerning a composite scheme involving Essel Mining and Industries Limited. The scheme contemplated transfer of Essel Mining’s renewable energy undertaking to Aditya Birla Renewables through a slump sale, amalgamation of Electrotherm Renewables Private Limited into Aditya Birla Renewables, and merger of three wholly owned EPC subsidiaries—ABREL EPCCO Services Limited, ABREL Renewables EPC Limited and ABREL EPC Limited—into the same transferee company. The application was presented under Sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act, 2013 and the applicable arrangement and amalgamation rules. Essel Mining, whose registered office was in West Bengal, had separately approached the Kolkata Bench, which had passed its first-motion order on 28 April 2026. The applicant companies’ boards approved the scheme on 29 December 2025, and its appointed date was stated as 1 April 2025. The applicants submitted that the restructuring would consolidate renewable energy generation and ancillary services, simplify the corporate structure, reduce duplication and improve operational efficiencies. The proposed consideration for Electrotherm’s amalgamation was 187070 fully paid equity shares of Aditya Birla Renewables of INR 10 each for every 100 fully paid equity shares of Electrotherm of INR 10 each. The slump-sale consideration comprised 39,90,75,667 fully paid equity shares of the transferee company of INR 10 each. No consideration was proposed for the merger of the three wholly owned EPC subsidiaries. A joint valuation report and fairness opinion supported the proposed consideration. The order also recorded BSE’s observations, the applicants’ responses, the debenture trustee’s no-objection certificate and statutory auditors’ certificates addressing debt-servicing capacity and the proposed accounting treatment. The applicants stated that the listed non-convertible debentures would continue on their existing terms without reduction or extinguishment of liability. The Tribunal dispensed with equity shareholder meetings of all five applicant companies because every equity shareholder had consented to the scheme through affidavits. It also dispensed with the relevant secured and unsecured creditor meetings, recording the submissions that the scheme offered no compromise to creditors, did not reduce, diminish or extinguish their liabilities, and contemplated payment in the ordinary course of business. The transferee company had five secured creditors with dues of Rs. 74,875.77 lakhs and 134 unsecured creditors, including NCD holders, with dues of Rs. 3,17,501.47 lakhs as on 31 December 2025. Its expected excess of assets over liabilities after the scheme was Rs. 1,67,272.40 lakhs, based on that date. Electrotherm and the third and fourth applicants had no secured creditors; their unsecured creditor meetings were dispensed with. Meetings of both creditor classes of the fifth applicant were also dispensed with. The applicants were directed to serve notices in Form CAA.3 with enclosures on the specified governmental, corporate, securities, stock exchange, income-tax, GST and other relevant authorities under Section 230(5) of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule 8 of the CAA Rules. The authorities were given 30 days from receipt to make representations; absence of a response would attract the stated presumption of no objection. A copy of the scheme was also required to be served on the Official Liquidator, High Court of Bombay, for the transferor companies. The applicants were directed to host the notices on their websites, if any, and file an affidavit of service within 15 days of service on the regulatory authorities. The application was allowed and disposed of with these directions. The order concerns first-motion procedural directions and meeting dispensation; it does not finally sanction the scheme. The Rationale of the proposed Scheme is stated as under: The Scheme is being proposed to create a consolidated renewable energy platform along with ancillary services, under the Transferee Company to simplify the management and operational structures of the Companies and to increase efficiencies by reaping benefits of economies of scale and synergies. Further, the Scheme would result in distinct value creation opportunities for the Transferee Company and EMIL. The Slump Sale, the ERPL Merger and the EPC Merger would be in the best interest of EMIL, ERPL, the EPC Companies and the Transferee Company and their respective shareholders and creditors for the reasons set out below: a. In case of the Slump Sale: i. The management of EMIL believes that the EMIL Renewable Energy Undertaking (as defined below) has minimal synergies with its commercial mining, Noble Ferro Alloys and Pellets business and accordingly, each of the two business segments require dedicated management focus and business strategies to ensure that the respective businesses are given the right impetus for growth. ii. The Slump Sale will streamline EMIL’s business by carving out the renewable energy assets owned / leased by EMIL, resulting in a focused renewable energy platform under the Transferee Company. It will also allow the management of EMIL to focus on its core capabilities in relation to commercial mining of various minerals, where it has expertise and goodwill, thereby enhancing the ability of the Transferee Company and EMIL to independently explore and leverage strategic opportunities for their respective businesses/undertakings, including fund raising prospects, with the aim of maximizing stakeholders value. iii. The Slump Sale will aid the Transferee Company in its goal of being a leading renewable energy company in India, which would also benefit the stakeholders of EMIL in the form of wealth creation from the potential appreciation in value of its stake in the Transferee Company. iv. The Slump Sale will strengthen organisational capabilities around operational and financial areas, driving benefits of scale through leveraging resources and enabling streamlining of the renewable energy operations. b. In case of the ERPL Merger: i. The management of ERPL and the Transferee Company believe that the financial, managerial and technical resources, personnel, capabilities, skills, and expertise of ERPL pooled with the Transferee Company will lead to increased competitiveness. ii. Integration of ERPL and Transferee Company under a single entity through the ERPL Merger will result inter alia in focused management attention, operational efficiencies, optimization of capital, operational expenditure, and simplification of corporate structure. iii. The ERPL Merger would bring about synergy of operations and benefit of scale and additionally, the legal and regulatory compliances of both the entities will be unified and streamlined. iv. The ERPL Merger would also result in overheads reduction and better optimal utilization of various resources by avoiding duplication of administrative functions. c. In case of the EPC Merger: i. As the EPC Companies are wholly owned subsidiaries of the Transferee Company providing services solely to the Transferee Company and its subsidiaries (having business of generation of renewable energy), the management of the Transferee Company believes that merging the EPC Companies within the Transferee Company would result in a simplified corporate structure by streamlining the number of subsidiaries under the Transferee Company, resulting in a more efficient management and administrative structure. ii. The EPC Merger would lead to the integration and consolidation of all the renewable energy engineering, procurement and construction activities of the Transferee Company into one single entity for better utilization of resources and expertise, cost reduction, more effective decision making and better coordination. iii. The EPC Merger would also result in synergy creation by leveraging the complementary strengths of the respective EPC. 8.
