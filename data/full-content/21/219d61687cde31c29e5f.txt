Larger retailers, online platforms and restaurants, among others, may choose to absorb the cost.The new UPI merchant discount rate (MDR) framework will apply to a relatively small share of merchant transactions but a disproportionately large portion of payment value, with transactions above ₹2,000 accounting for around 4% of P2M transaction volume but nearly 66% of its value, according to estimates by Grant Thornton Bharat.The framework, which comes into effect from October 15, introduces a standard 0.4% MDR on eligible P2M UPI transactions above ₹2,000, while payments up to ₹2,000 and qualifying small-merchant transactions remain zero-MDR. The concentration of value in the higher-ticket segment means that the new framework is effectively targeting a narrow portion of UPI merchant transactions that accounts for a much larger share of the money flowing through the payment rail. Advt “UPI transactions above ₹2,000 are primarily driven by salaried households, borrowers, affluent consumers, travellers, and small businesses using the platform for recurring obligations, financial commitments, discretionary spending, and business-related payments,” said Dharmender Jhamb, Partner & FinTech sector leader, Grant Thornton Bharat.The analysis also pointed to a similar concentration on the P2P side. Transactions above ₹2,000 contributed more than 86% of total P2P transaction value, highlighting how a relatively small number of higher-ticket payments account for a substantial share of UPI's overall value.“As UPI continues to gain trust as a primary payment rail, it is increasingly being used for larger-ticket financial commitments that were traditionally routed through cards, net banking, or bank transfers,” Jhamb said.While exact merchant category code-wise data is not available on the NPCI website, Grant Thornton Bharat identified several categories where higher-value UPI payments are increasingly relevant.These include rent and housing, utilities, insurance, financial services, government payments, healthcare, travel, education and consumer electronics.The customer base behind these payments is also broader than just affluent consumers. Salaried households use UPI for recurring monthly commitments and larger household payments, while borrowers and investors use it for EMIs, insurance premiums, SIP contributions and loan repayments. Advt Affluent consumers and frequent travellers use UPI for flights, hotels and larger retail purchases. Small businesses and independent workers also use the rail for supplier payments, business settlements, rent and other large-value expenses, including payments made by professionals, gig workers, tenants and families.The shift reflects UPI's expansion from a predominantly small-ticket payment mechanism towards a broader payment rail for recurring and financial commitments.40 bps sits at top end of Ambit’s revenue estimateThe 40-bps standard MDR is also significant in the context of an estimate by Ambit Capital in its recent fintech thematic report, which had modelled the potential impact of a reintroduced MDR on higher-value UPI merchant transactions. Advt Ambit had estimated that a 20–40 bps MDR applied to FY26 P2M gross merchandise value of ₹61 trillion for transactions above ₹2,000 could unlock an aggregate annual revenue pool of ₹122 billion to ₹245 billion, which is around ₹12,200 crore to ₹24,500 crore, for the UPI ecosystem. The notified 40-bps rate therefore sits at the upper end of Ambit's sensitivity range.However, the ₹24,500 crore figure should not be treated as a direct revenue forecast under the final framework. Ambit's calculation was based on applying a 20–40 bps levy to the relevant FY26 P2M value pool, whereas the notified framework includes several exclusions and differentiated rates. These include the ₹300 per-transaction cap for transactions of ₹75,000 and above, a flat ₹5 MDR for specified categories such as railways, telecom, insurance, fuel and agricultural inputs, and a 0.02% MDR for specified capital-market transactions. Eligible small P2PM merchants also remain exempt. Advt As a result, the actual revenue generated by the framework would depend on the composition of eligible transactions, the impact of the caps and category-specific rates, as well as the growth of high-value UPI payments after implementation.The report had also estimated that the zero-MDR regime introduced in 2020 resulted in UPI ecosystem participants absorbing operating costs, which it put at around 25 bps based on RBI estimates, with government subventions partially offsetting those costs. The new framework therefore introduces a direct monetisation mechanism for a segment that is relatively small in transaction count but disproportionately significant in payment value.For UPI players, the immediate focus will now shift from the introduction of MDR to the actual revenue pool created after exemptions, caps and category-specific rates, as well as how that revenue is distributed across ecosystem participants.
