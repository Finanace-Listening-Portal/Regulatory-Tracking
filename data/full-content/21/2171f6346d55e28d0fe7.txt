Summary: CBDT extended the AY 2026-27 return-filing date for the specified audit-case class from 31 October to 21 November 2026 through Circular No. 7/2026. Uploading the return did not by itself complete the filing process: the return must be verified within 30 days through an eligible electronic method or by timely receipt of signed ITR-V at CPC. Where verification occurs within that window, the upload date remains the filing date. Where it occurs later, the verification or CPC-receipt date becomes the filing date and late-filing consequences follow; an unverified return is treated as invalid, subject to discretionary condonation for genuine delay. This guide converts that rule into a return-by-return post-filing control for taxpayers, finance teams and advisers. The same circular moved the corresponding specified date for the audit report from 30 September to 21 October 2026. The extension addressed filing of the return; it did not replace the separate verification requirement. The Income Tax Department’s current ITR-V FAQ applies a 30-day period from the date of uploading the return. A team that treated 21 November as the end of the assignment can therefore miss the final act required to make each uploaded return valid. The control should begin with the actual upload date and time recorded in the portal acknowledgement, not with the statutory due date or the date on which the computation was approved. The CPC-receipt date controls a physical ITR-V submission For a taxpayer using physical ITR-V, the relevant date is when the duly signed form is received at Centralised Processing Centre, not when it is printed or posted. Postal transit must be built into the plan. The form should be signed as instructed and sent to the address displayed on the official acknowledgement or current portal guidance. The dispatch record and delivery evidence should be retained, but they do not replace confirmation of CPC receipt. Choose a verification method that matches the taxpayer and signatory Electronic verification options are not interchangeable in every case The official e-Verify user manual lists verification through Income-tax Act, 1961” Digital Signature Certificate, Aadhaar OTP, an Electronic Verification Code generated through a pre-validated bank account or demat account, EVC through a bank ATM, and net banking. Availability depends on the taxpayer, authorised signatory, portal profile, pre-validation and the return’s circumstances. The preparer should not select a method only because it worked for another entity. Where a DSC is mandatory or selected, confirm that the certificate is valid, registered on the portal against the correct signatory and usable on the configured system. A board-approved or legally authorised signatory should be mapped to the return before the deadline. For Aadhaar OTP, the relevant Aadhaar and mobile linkage must operate. For bank or demat EVC, pre-validation and contact details should be confirmed. A last-minute method change can fail if the alternate channel has not been activated. Verification credentials must not be shared casually An adviser may prepare or upload a return, but verification is an authenticated act by the taxpayer or authorised person. OTPs, DSC tokens and portal credentials should be handled under documented authority and information-security controls. Retain evidence of authorisation and the final verification result, not passwords or reusable authentication secrets. The compliance file should show who performed the act and when without creating a credential-security risk. Late verification changes the filing date and can change legal outcomes The Department’s FAQ states that where a return is uploaded within the due date but verified, or the signed ITR-V is received, after 30 days, the verification or receipt date is treated as the date of furnishing the return. All applicable consequences of late filing then follow. This is more serious than a procedural warning: it may affect whether the return is considered timely, the availability of provisions linked to timely filing, interest, fee, loss carry-forward positions or other statutory consequences, depending on the taxpayer’s facts and the law governing AY 2026-27. The relevant return concerns financial year 2025-26 and Assessment Year 2026-27. The Department’s transition FAQ confirms that such return and audit-report compliance continues under the Income-tax Act, 1961 even though the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from tax year 2026-27 for current-year income. The post-filing memorandum should therefore cite the correct legacy provision and circular for the AY 2026-27 return instead of substituting a similarly numbered provision from the 2025 Act. Advisers should separately assess whether other remedies or a valid belated or updated-return route exists under the law and facts; the condonation screen does not decide those questions. Continue to monitor the request and return status Submission of a condonation request is not proof that the return has become valid. Preserve its transaction ID, check the portal outcome, complete any required verification, respond to communications and record the final filed-return status. If the delay changes the furnishing date, quantify every affected consequence rather than limiting the review to whether a refund is visible. Close every AY 2026-27 audit-case file with an evidence-based review A robust close-out pack contains Circular No. 7/2026, the applicable return acknowledgement, computation and tax-payment evidence, audit-report linkage, successful verification page or CPC receipt, and a screenshot or exported portal status. The reviewer should match PAN, assessment year, form, filing section, acknowledgement number and dates across the documents. A success message for a different return or assessment year cannot close the item. Where multiple group entities were filed, use one row per return and require an independent reviewer to clear each row. Exception reports should identify returns approaching their deadline, methods that failed, signatories whose DSCs expire, physical ITR-V awaiting CPC receipt and condonation requests awaiting decision. This control is more reliable than a single email stating that all returns were filed, because it distinguishes upload, verification and legal furnishing date. Export the filed-return status after verification and retain it with the acknowledgement. Reconcile the portal population to the original audit-case filing list so that no PAN is omitted. Document the governing Act as the Income-tax Act, 1961 for AY 2026-27 return compliance. Escalate any post-30-day item for consequence analysis and supported condonation without delay. Keep authentication evidence securely and never archive reusable credentials in the tax file. Frequently Asked Questions on AY 2026-27 Audit-Case ITR Verification Did Circular No. 7/2026 extend the verification period itself? No. It extended the specified audit-case ITR due date to 21 November 2026. The separate verification rule remains 30 days from the actual upload date. Does a return uploaded on 21 November 2026 use 21 November as its filing date? Yes, if it is verified within the applicable 30-day period. If verification is late, the verification or CPC-receipt date is treated as the furnishing date. Is emailing or posting ITR-V within 30 days sufficient? The Department states that the date on which duly verified ITR-V is received at CPC is considered for the 30-day period. Allow for delivery time. Can every taxpayer use Aadhaar OTP? No. Method availability depends on the taxpayer, signatory and portal conditions. The official e-Verify workflow should be checked for the specific return. What happens if the return is never verified? The Department’s FAQ states that it is treated as invalid, although a condonation request may be submitted for genuine delay. Does a condonation request automatically preserve the original upload date? No. Condonation is discretionary and its legal effect depends on acceptance and the applicable law. Monitor the decision and obtain advice on consequences. Which Act governs the AY 2026-27 audit-case return? The Income Tax Department’s transition FAQ states that the FY 2025-26 return for AY 2026-27 is governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961 and the old ITR forms, notwithstanding the 2025 Act’s application to tax year 2026-27. Key Takeaways Circular No. 7/2026 moved the specified audit-case AY 2026-27 ITR due date to 21 November 2026, not the separate verification rule. Calculate 30 days from each return’s actual upload date and track physical ITR-V by CPC receipt. Verification within 30 days preserves the upload date; late verification shifts the furnishing date and can trigger late consequences. Use an eligible DSC, OTP, EVC or net-banking method with the correct authorised signatory and secure evidence.
