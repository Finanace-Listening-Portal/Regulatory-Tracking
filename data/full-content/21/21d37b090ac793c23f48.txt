Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Biogen Pharmachem Industries Limited Vs ITO (ITAT Rajkot) A Section 148 Notice Issued Without Jurisdiction Cannot Be Cured by the Jurisdictional Officer Completing the Reassessment Reassessment Quashed and ₹5.40 Crore Addition Deleted on Merits The Rajkot ITAT quashed a reassessment because the officer who issued the section 148 notice lacked jurisdiction over the assessee on the date of issue. The subsequent completion of the reassessment by the jurisdictional Assessing Officer could not cure that defect. The Tribunal also independently deleted the ₹5.40 crore addition under section 68, holding that the assessee had substantiated the inter-corporate deposit and that the Revenue had produced no specific evidence linking it to an accommodation-entry arrangement. Tribunal Condoned Delay of 703 Days The company supported its condonation application with a director’s affidavit. It explained that its name had changed from Sun Techno Overseas Ltd. to Biogen Pharmachem Industries Ltd., that a new director had created a new email address, and that notices and orders continued to go to the old address. The director’s prolonged illness and death in January 2024 contributed to the communications failing to reach the management’s attention. The Tribunal found sufficient cause and condoned the 703-day delay, applying Collector, Land Acquisition v. Mst. Katiji (1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC). It then proceeded to decide the appeal on the jurisdictional grounds and on merits. Ahmedabad Officer Issued Notice to a Rajkot Assessee The notice under section 148 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, dated 13 March 2019, was issued by the ITO, Ward 4(1)(2), Ahmedabad. The assessee’s Rajkot address appeared on the notice itself, and the record indicated that jurisdiction lay with the ITO, Ward 1(2)(4), Rajkot. The Revenue produced no operative order under section 120 or section 127 vesting jurisdiction in the Ahmedabad officer on the date of issue. The Tribunal held that the relevant date for testing jurisdiction was the date on which the notice was issued. The section 127 order dated 22 December 2025 referred to in the proceedings came years after the reassessment order of 29 December 2019. The Rajkot officer’s subsequent conduct of the proceedings and the assessee’s participation could not confer jurisdiction retrospectively. The Tribunal relied on the Gujarat High Court decisions in Omkareshwar Mines and Minerals Pvt. Ltd. v. ITO, R/Special Civil Application No. 13085 of 2019, and Hynoup Food & Oil Industries Ltd. v. ACIT (2008) 307 ITR 115 (Guj.). Reopening Reasons Failed to Examine the Specific Loan The Assessing Officer relied on departmental information alleging that Malabar Trading Company Ltd. was a shell company engaged in providing accommodation entries. The Tribunal found that the ₹5.40 crore inter-corporate deposit was recorded in the assessee’s audited books and reflected in its financial statements. To the extent the recorded reasons proceeded on a contrary assumption, they were inconsistent with the record. The Tribunal also found no meaningful independent examination of the assessee’s particular transaction before the notice was issued. Following Mumtaz Haji Mohmad Memon v. ITO (2018) 408 ITR 268 (Guj.), it allowed the challenge to reopening on this ground as well. Documentary Evidence Discharged the Burden Under Section 68 The lender responded directly to the notice under section 133(6) and furnished its confirmation, ledger account, income-tax return, financial statements and bank statement. The parties’ records showed the transfer through banking channels. The lender’s financial statements showed substantial shareholders’ funds and availability of funds. The Tribunal observed that limited trading turnover did not determine the lending capacity of a company principally engaged in investment. It also considered the subsequent repayment through banking channels alongside the other evidence. The Tribunal found that the assessee had established the lender’s identity, creditworthiness and the genuineness of the transaction. The Revenue identified no cash trail, circular movement of funds, commission payment or statement implicating the assessee in an accommodation-entry arrangement. Subsequent restrictions on trading in the lender’s shares likewise supplied no evidence about this particular deposit. The Tribunal therefore deleted the ₹5.40 crore addition on merits, independently of quashing the reassessment, and allowed the appeal. Author’s Comments The jurisdictional finding turns on the authority of the officer on the date of the notice. The order completing the reassessment showed which officer ultimately handled the case. The missing document was an operative statutory order showing that the Ahmedabad officer had authority to initiate it. On the merits, the lender’s direct response under section 133(6) mattered because its confirmation could be tested against its own ledger, financial statements and bank records. Those documents addressed the particular deposit that the Assessing Officer sought to tax. The allegation about the lender’s wider activities supplied a reason to examine the transaction, while the Tribunal’s decision turned on the evidence available after that examination. As per the affidavit, the order of the Ld. CIT(A) was passed on 30.11.2023, against which the appeal before the Tribunal was required to be filed within the prescribed period. The assessee has, therefore, pleaded that the delay was neither deliberate nor intentional and arose due to the aforesaid circumstances. 03. We have also considered the objections raised by the Department against the condonation of such a substantial delay. However, having regard to the facts and circumstances of the case, particularly the change in the name of the company, the subsequent change in its management, the creation of a new e- mail ID and the unfortunate demise of the concerned director, we find that the assessee has furnished a plausible explanation for the delay in filing the appeal. 04. In our considered view, the explanation furnished by the assessee constitutes a reasonable cause for the delay. After considering the explanation furnished by the assessee and the documents placed on record, we are of the view that the delay in filing the appeal was not intentional or deliberate. The assessee has placed sufficient cause which affected the ability to pursue the appellate remedy within the prescribed time. It is a settled principle of law that a litigant should not be denied an opportunity of hearing on technical grounds when sufficient cause has been shown for the delay While considering a similar issue, the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Collector, Land Acquisition v. Mst. Katiji reported in 1987 taxmann.com 1072/[1987] 28 ELT 185/66 STC 228 (SC)/(167 ITR 471) laid down six principles. For the purpose of convenience, the principles laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court are reproduced hereunder: (1) Ordinarily, a litigant does not stand to benefit by lodging an appeal late. (2) Refusing to condone delay can result in a meritorious matter being thrown at the very threshold and cause of justice being defeated. As against this, when delay is condoned, the highest that can happen is that a cause would be decided on merits after hearing the parties. (3) ‘Every day’s delay must be explained’ does not mean that a pedantic approach should be made. Why not every hour’s delay, every second’s delay? The doctrine must be applied in a rational, common-sense and pragmatic manner. (4) When substantial justice and technical consideration are pitted against each other, the cause of substantial justice deserves to be preferred, for the other side cannot claim to have vested right in injustice being done because of a non-deliberate delay. (5) There is no presumption that delay is occasioned deliberately, or on account of culpable negligence, or on account of mala fides. A litigant does not stand to benefit by resorting to delay. In fact, he runs a serious risk. (6) It must be grasped that the judiciary is respected not on account of its power to legalise injustice on technical grounds but because it is capable of removing injustice and is expected to do so. The Hon’ble Supreme Court has repeatedly observed that refusal to condone delay in appropriate cases may result in perpetuating injustice on purely technical grounds, even when the appellate authority is otherwise competent to correct the error. Hence, if the application for condonation of delay is rejected despite the existence of reasonable cause, it would amount to allowing an injustice to continue merely on account of technicalities. Considering the facts of the present case explained by the assessee, we are satisfied that the assessee was prevented by reasonable cause from delay of 703 days in filing the present appeal is condoned and the appeal is admitted for adjudication on merits. 05. AR, the expression “Assessing Officer” is specifically defined in section 2(7A) of the Act and means an officer who is vested with the relevant jurisdiction by virtue of directions or orders issued under section 120 of the Act or under any other provision of the Act. The power under sections 147 and 148 of the Act is therefore required to be exercised by the Assessing Officer having jurisdiction over the assessee. A non-jurisdictional Assessing Officer cannot assume such jurisdiction merely on the basis of information received from another authority. 08. The Ld. Subsequently, notices under section 142(1) of the Act were issued by the ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot Assessing Officer and the assessment order was ultimately passed by the ward-1(2)(4) Rajkot. Therefore, the very initiation of reassessment proceedings by the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad was no valid order under section 127 of the Act transferring the jurisdiction from ward- 1(2)(4), Rajkot to ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad on the date on which the notice under section 148 of the Act was issued. It was argued that if, for the sake of argument, the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer was considered to have jurisdiction to issue the notice under section 148 of the Act, then the entire reassessment proceedings ought to have been completed by that Assessing Officer only. AR submitted that a jurisdictional defect in the issuance of notice under section 148 of the Act cannot be treated as a mere procedural irregularity. The notice under section 148 of the Act constitutes the foundation of reassessment proceedings, and where such notice is issued by an officer having no jurisdiction over the assessee, all subsequent proceedings are vitiated. Therefore, the technical objection raised by the Ld. AR regarding the jurisdiction of the Ahmedabad Assessing Officer was not sufficient to invalidate the reassessment proceedings. He, further submitted that the transfer of jurisdiction and administrative allocation of cases between Assessing Officers are matters governed by the departmental orders and instructions. According to him, the mere fact that the assessee’s address was mentioned as Rajkot in the notice under section 148 of the Act would not, by itself, establish that the Ahmedabad Assessing Officer lacked jurisdiction to initiate reassessment proceedings. He accordingly opposed the contention that the subsequent proceedings were without jurisdiction. On the validity of the reasons recorded for reopening, the Ld. The Ld.DR further contended that the financial capacity of the lender and the proper examination. We have carefully considered the rival submissions of the Ld. AR and the Ld. DR and perused the material available on record, including the assessment order, the reasons recorded for reopening, the notice issued under section 148 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (“the Act”), the approval under section 151 of the Act and the documents placed in the paper book. The assessee has challenged the impugned reassessment proceedings both on jurisdictional grounds as well as on merits. Since the legal ground challenging to the jurisdiction goes to the root of the matter, we deem it appropriate to first examine the validity of assumption of jurisdiction under sections 147 and 148 of the Act. It is an admitted position that the notice under section 148 of the Act was issued on 13.03.2019 by ITO, Ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad. The assessee, however, was admittedly situated at Rajkot and its address at Rajkot was available in the records of the Department. The notice under section 148 of the Act itself contains the Rajkot address of the assessee. Section 147 of the Act empowers the Assessing Officer to assess or reassess income which has escaped assessment. The jurisdiction to exercise such power is therefore vested in the Assessing Officer having jurisdiction over the assessee. Section 2(7A) of the Act defines “Assessing Officer” as an officer who is vested with the relevant jurisdiction by virtue of directions or orders issued under section 120 of the Act or under any other provision of the Act. Thus, the expression officer of the Income-tax Department. It means the officer who, on the relevant date, was vested with jurisdiction over the assessee in accordance with the statutory scheme. In the present case, the Department has not brought on record any valid order under section 120 or section 127 of the Act demonstrating that, on 13.03.2019, the ward- 4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer was vested with jurisdiction over the assessee. On the contrary, the material placed before us indicates that the assessee’s jurisdiction lay with the ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot Assessing Officer. The subsequent conduct of the Department also supports this position. After issuance of the notice under section 148 of the Act by the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer, the proceedings were subsequently conducted by the ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot Assessing Officer, including issuance of notice under section 142(1) of the Act and completion of the assessment. 14. In the present case of assessee, the Ld. AR has specifically pointed out that the transfer order under section 127 of the Act relied upon by the Department was passed only subsequently, on 22.12.2025, whereas the reassessment order in the present case had already been passed on 29.12.2019. The Revenue has not brought before us any earlier order under section 127 of the Act which was operative on 13.03.2019 and which could have vested jurisdiction over the assessee in the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer. We therefore find that, on the date on which the notice under section 148 of the Act was issued, the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer was not shown to be the Assessing Officer having jurisdiction over the assessee. The notice under section 148 of the Act is not an empty formality. It is the foundation upon which the entire reassessment proceedings are built. Before assuming jurisdiction under section 147 of the Act, the Assessing Officer is required to possess the statutory jurisdiction to do so and thereafter validly issue notice under section 148 of the Act. If the foundational notice itself is issued by an officer who has no jurisdiction over the assessee, the subsequent proceedings cannot acquire validity merely because another defect in the present case is therefore not merely procedural. It goes to the root of the assumption of jurisdiction. We also find force in the submission of the Ld. AR that subsequent participation of the assessee in the reassessment proceedings cannot confer jurisdiction upon an officer who did not possess such jurisdiction in the first place. Consent, acquiescence or absence of objection cannot create statutory jurisdiction where none existed. We further find that there was no valid order under section 127 of the Act transferring the case from the ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot Assessing Officer to the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer before issuance of the notice under section 148 of the Act. Section 127 of the Act provides a statutory mechanism for transfer of cases from one Assessing Officer to another Assessing Officer in accordance with the prescribed procedure. Such transfer cannot be presumed merely because information was available with, or proceedings were initiated by, another Assessing Officer. In the present case, no such contemporaneous order transferring jurisdiction to ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad has been brought on record. More importantly, the subsequent transfer of the case to ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot cannot retrospectively validate the notice under section 148 of the Act which had already been issued by the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer. We accordingly hold that the notice under section 148 of the Act dated 13.03.2019 was issued by an Assessing Officer who was not shown to have jurisdiction over the assessee on the relevant date. The reassessment proceedings founded upon such notice are consequently unsustainable. The Revenue contend that the assessment order was ultimately passed by the ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot Assessing Officer and, therefore, the assessment was completed by the officer having jurisdiction over the assessee. The validity of the reassessment proceedings has to be examined from the stage at which jurisdiction under section 147 of the Act was assumed and the notice under section 148 of the Act was issued. The subsequent involvement of a jurisdictional Assessing Officer cannot ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer had no jurisdiction to issue the notice under section 148 of the Act, the proceedings initiated by him could not be validated merely because the case was subsequently dealt with by the ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot Assessing Officer. Even assuming that the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer had jurisdiction at the stage of issuance of notice, the Revenue has not demonstrated the legal basis under which the ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot Assessing Officer subsequently assumed jurisdiction and completed the reassessment in the absence of a contemporaneous order under section 127 of the Act. Thus, from either perspective, the reassessment proceedings suffer from a jurisdictional infirmity. Accordingly, we following the judgement of the Hon’ble Jurisdictional High Court as in the case of Omkareshwar mkareshwar Mines and Minerals Pvt. Ltd v. ITO R/SCA NO. 13085 of 2019 (Guj. HC), held as: The issuance of a valid notice u/s 148 of the Act by the Assessing Officer having jurisdiction over the assessee is a condition precedent to the assumption of jurisdiction under Section 147 of the Act, and is not a mere procedural formality. A notice under Section 148 of the Act issued by an officer who is not the Assessing Officer of the assessee is a notice issued without authority of law; it is non-est, and no valid reassessment can be founded upon it. The Court relied upon Hynoup Food & Oil Industries Ltd. Vs. The ITO, Ward 3(3), Kolkata, was not the “Assessing Officer ” of the petitioner within the meaning of Section 2(7A) of the Act, the jurisdiction over the petitioner having stood transferred to the Income Tax Officer, Ward 3(1)(4), Ahmedabad by the order dated 07.12.2015 passed under Section 127 of the Act, and that the impugned Notice u/s 148 of the Act issued by the ITO, Ward 3(3), Kolkata is without jurisdiction and cannot be sustained. Wherein the issue was decided in favour of the assessee. Accordingly, we allow this 15. We have also examined the ground raised by the assessee challenging the reasons recorded for reopening. The reasons proceed, inter alia, on the basis that the assessee had received an unsecured loan of Rs.5,40,00,000/- from M/s Malabar Trading Company Ltd. and that the transaction was not satisfactorily reflected in the assessee’s records. However, the assessee has demonstrated before us that the amount was duly recorded in its regular books of account as an unsecured loan/borrowing and was reflected in the financial statements accompanying the return of income. The assessee had maintained regular books of account which were audited. The loan transaction was recorded in the books and was also supported by the corresponding ledger and bank records. Therefore, the factual premise forming the basis of the reopening, to the extent it proceeds on the assumption that the loan was not recorded in the books, is contrary to the material available on record. It is well settled that law the belief contemplated under section 147 of the Act must be based on relevant and tangible material and cannot be founded upon an incorrect factual assumption. Where the very information forming the basis of reopening is factually incorrect or incomplete, the consequential exercise of jurisdiction becomes legally vulnerable. We have also examined the assessee’s contention that the reopening was based on borrowed satisfaction. The reasons recorded substantially rely upon information received from Investigation Wing concerning M/s Malabar Trading Company Ltd. and its alleged involvement in accommodation entries. However, there is a distinction between information which constitutes tangible material and the mechanical reproduction of such information without independent examination of the assessee’s specific transaction. In the present case, the material before us does not demonstrate that, before issuing notice under section 148 of the Act, the Assessing Officer carried out any meaningful independent enquiry concerning the assessee’s particular receipt of Rs.5.40 crore. There is no material demonstrating that the assessee had paid cash to the lender, that cash had been or that the amount received by the assessee represented its own undisclosed money. There is also no statement of any person from M/s Malabar Trading Company Ltd. admitting that the particular amount advanced to the assessee was an accommodation entry. The mere receipt of information that the lender company was allegedly involved in accommodation entries could not, without further examination of the specific transaction, automatically lead to the conclusion that the loan received by the assessee represented escaped income. The Assessing Officer issued notice under section 133(6) of the Act to M/s Malabar Trading Company Ltd. The lender responded to the notice and confirmed the transaction. The assessee has placed the response of the lender on record along with the relevant supporting documents. The lender furnished its confirmation, ledger account, return of income, financial statements and bank statement. The bank statement also reflected the payment made to the assessee. Therefore, the Revenue had before it direct documentary evidence from the lender confirming the transaction. If the Assessing Officer nevertheless intended to treat the transaction accommodation entry, it was incumbent upon him to bring some contrary material on record demonstrating why the lender’s confirmation and supporting documents unreliable. There is no finding that the assessee paid cash to M/s Malabar Trading Company Ltd. against the loan. There is no evidence of cash withdrawal by the assessee corresponding to the impugned transaction. There is no material establishing circular movement of funds between the assessee and the lender. The transaction was routed through banking channels and was duly recorded in the books of both parties. In these circumstances, the mere characterization of the lender as a shell company or an accommodation-entry provider cannot, by itself, establish that the particular transaction entered into with the assessee was bogus. Coming to the addition made under section 68 of the Act, it is settled that the assessee is required to establish the identity of the creditor, the creditworthiness of the creditor and the genuineness of Court as in the case of Mumtaz Haji Mohmad Memon vs. ITO (408 ITR 268) (Guj. Besides that, the assessee furnished substantial documentary evidence in support of all three ingredients. The identity of M/s Malabar Trading Company Ltd. was not in dispute. The assessee furnished its PAN, corporate details, ROC particulars, income- tax records and other statutory documents. The genuineness of the transaction was supported by the confirmation, ledger account and bank statements of both parties. The amount was transferred through normal banking channels and was duly recorded in the books of account. As regards creditworthiness, the assessee furnished the financial statements of the lender demonstrating its capital, reserves/net worth and availability of funds. The lender was an existing corporate entity having substantial financial resources. Thus, the assessee discharged the primary onus cast upon it under section 68 of the Act. Once the assessee furnished such primary evidence, the burden shifted to the Revenue to rebut the same by bringing cogent material on record. As the allegation that the lender lacked capacity to advance the amount also does not find adequate support from the material placed before us. The financial statements of M/s Malabar Trading Company Ltd. demonstrated substantial shareholders’ funds/net worth and availability of funds. The lender had also undertaken corporate transactions through which funds were raised. The fact that the lender’s principal activity was investment and that it had limited conventional business turnover cannot, by itself, establish lack of creditworthiness. An investment company may deploy its capital in securities, loans and advances and earn income in the form of interest, dividends or capital gains. Therefore, the absence of substantial trading turnover is not determinative of its capacity to grant an inter-corporate deposit or loan. We also find that the observation of the Assessing Officer regarding non- compliance or unsatisfactory compliance by the lender does not, on the facts before us, adequately address the relevant financial and banking documents. The Revenue has not demonstrated any contradiction between the confirmation and the corresponding bank entries. Thus, the adverse conclusion drawn by the Assessing Officer is not supported by any specific discrepancy in the documents furnished by the lender. The entire addition substantially rests upon the allegation that M/s Malabar Trading Company Ltd. was involved in providing accommodation entries. However, even if the lender had been subject to investigation or adverse findings in respect of certain transactions, the same cannot automatically lead to the conclusion that every transaction undertaken by the lender was non-genuine. The Revenue was required to establish a nexus between the assessee and the alleged accommodation-entry mechanism. No such nexus has been established. There is no statement of the lender admitting that it provided an accommodation entry to the assessee. There is no statement of any intermediary. There is no cash trail. There is no evidence of commission paid by the assessee. There is no material demonstrating that the assessee’s own unaccounted money was routed through the lender. Therefore, the allegation remains at the level of suspicion and general information rather than specific evidence relating to the assessee’s transaction. The Revenue’s reliance suspension/delisting or restriction of trading in the scrip of M/s upon the subsequent Malabar Trading Company Ltd. also does not, in our considered view, establish the nature of the loan transaction in question. The impugned transaction pertains to financial year 2013-14 relevant to A.Y. 2014-15. Any subsequent action by the stock exchange, without specific material connecting it with the loan transaction in question, cannot retrospectively establish that the transaction was an accommodation entry. The Revenue has not demonstrated that, at the time when the loan was advanced, the assessee was part of any alleged accommodation-entry arrangement. We also take note of the fact that the loan was subsequently repaid through normal banking channels. While repayment by itself cannot conclusively establish genuineness, it is certainly a circumstance when audited financial statements and banking trail already available on record. The Revenue has not shown that the repayment was fictitious or that the assessee had received back its own undisclosed money through any circular arrangement. Moreover, assessee had specifically requested copies of adverse material, statements and other documents relied upon by the Revenue and had sought an opportunity to cross-examine the concerned persons, if any adverse statement was proposed to be relied upon. No such specific incriminating statement or material implicating the assessee has been demonstrated before us. An assessment cannot be sustained merely by referring to generalized information or investigation reports without bringing the material relied upon against the assessee on record and establishing its nexus with the particular transaction. On an overall consideration of the facts and material available on record, we find that the reassessment proceedings suffer from a fundamental jurisdictional defect. The notice under section 148 of the Act dated 13.03.2019 was issued by the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer, whereas the assessee was within the jurisdiction of the ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot Assessing Officer and no valid order transferring jurisdiction to ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad on the relevant date has been brought on record. The subsequent transfer of the case or subsequent conduct of the ward-1(2)(4), Rajkot Assessing Officer cannot retrospectively validate the assumption of jurisdiction by the ward-4(1)(2), Ahmedabad Assessing Officer. Accordingly, the notice under section 148 of the act and the reassessment proceedings pursuant thereto are held to be invalid and the impugned reassessment order is liable to be quashed. Even otherwise, on merits, the addition of Rs.5,40,00,000/- under section 68 of the Act cannot be sustained. The assessee established the identity of the lender, furnished evidence of its financial capacity and demonstrated the genuineness of the transaction through confirmation, ledger accounts, audited financial statements and bank statements. The lender itself confirmed the transaction in response to notice specific material establishing that the amount represented the assessee’s own undisclosed money or that the transaction was an accommodation entry. No cash trail, statement, intermediary evidence or other direct material connecting the assessee with any accommodation-entry arrangement has been brought on record. Therefore, even on merits, the addition under section 68 is unsustainable. 17. In view of the foregoing discussion, we hold that the reassessment proceedings initiated pursuant to the notice under section 148 of the Act dated 13.03.2019 are invalid and the consequent reassessment order is quashed. Further the addition of Rs.5,40,00,000/- made under section 68 of the Act is also deleted on merits. Accordingly, the grounds raised by the assessee are allowed.
