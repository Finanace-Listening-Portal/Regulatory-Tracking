The MDR will be capped at Rs 300 for transactions of Rs 75,000 and above, while consumers will not be charged for using UPI. Person-to-person transactions will also remain outside the levy. Advt For fintechs, the significance of the change lies in the creation of a transaction-linked revenue pool around a payment system that has generated enormous volumes but limited direct monetisation for its participants. The government said the MDR framework is aimed at supporting infrastructure, innovation, cybersecurity and customer service within the payments ecosystem.The benefit will not automatically translate into 0.4% revenue for payment apps. The MDR is a charge on the merchant side of the transaction and the economics of a UPI payment involve multiple participants, including issuing and acquiring banks, payment service providers and fintechs. How the revenue is ultimately shared will determine the amount that accrues to individual payment companies.This makes the new framework particularly relevant for fintechs that have invested heavily in merchant acquisition, QR infrastructure and payment processing. Companies such as PhonePe, Google Pay and Paytm have built large merchant networks around UPI, but the zero-MDR structure meant that transaction scale by itself did not provide a conventional payment-processing revenue stream. Industry analysis has previously identified banks, fintechs and other participants across the transaction chain as potential beneficiaries of a revived MDR.The opportunity is nevertheless narrower than the headline 0.4% rate suggests. More than 95% of P2M UPI transactions by volume are below Rs 2,000, according to the new framework, leaving the bulk of everyday UPI payments outside the MDR regime. Eligible small P2PM merchants will also remain protected from the charge. Advt That means the biggest monetisation opportunity is likely to be concentrated among larger merchants, e-commerce companies and businesses handling higher-value transactions. For fintechs, this could improve the economics of merchant acquisition by allowing part of the cost of providing QR codes, payment infrastructure and transaction services to be recovered from eligible payment flows.Two-tier economyThe structure also varies by merchant category. General eligible P2M transactions above Rs 2,000 will attract 0.4%, subject to the Rs 300 cap. Railways, telecom, insurance and fuel are among categories that will face a flat Rs 5 MDR on eligible transactions, while some capital-market transactions have a separate rate structure. Advt The policy therefore creates a two-tier UPI economy. The mass-market layer of low-value payments remains free, preserving UPI's role in everyday transactions, while higher-value merchant payments provide a mechanism for participants in the ecosystem to recover some of the cost of operating and maintaining the network.For fintechs, the change could also reduce their dependence on indirect monetisation models such as selling financial products, merchant services and other offerings to users acquired through UPI. But the extent of that benefit will depend on the eventual revenue-sharing arrangement rather than the headline MDR itself. Advt The shift marks a significant change in UPI's economics. The government has preserved free consumer payments and protected small merchants while allowing a charge on a defined segment of merchant transactions. For payment fintechs, the question is no longer whether UPI can be monetised, but how much of the new MDR pool will ultimately reach the companies that built and operate the customer and merchant interfaces.
