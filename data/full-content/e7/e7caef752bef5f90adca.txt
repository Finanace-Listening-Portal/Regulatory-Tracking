The sharp increase reflects NBFCs' growing reliance on bank loans as bond-market borrowing becomes more expensive. Festive-season funding requirements and borrowings advanced ahead of the October rate hike have also contributed to the surge.Banks added Rs 6.6 lakh crore of credit to NBFCs over the past year, around 1.5 times the Rs 4.4 lakh crore added in the previous three years combined. NBFCs now account for 9.8% of gross bank credit, up from 8.3% a year earlier. Bank credit to the sector has expanded nearly six-fold since 2016, at a compound annual growth rate of 19.8%, compared with 11.9% for overall bank credit, according to CareEdge Ratings. Advt The shift is also visible in the allocation of incremental bank lending. Of every Rs 100 added to non-food bank credit in the 12 months to September 2026, around Rs 18 went to NBFCs, compared with about Rs 3 a year earlier. Although NBFCs account for less than a tenth of total bank credit, their share of fresh lending has risen sharply.The trend is particularly pronounced in services lending. NBFCs account for around a third of bank credit to the services sector and nearly half of the incremental services credit extended over the past year. This highlights their growing role in credit intermediation and their increasing weight in banks' loan books.Bank lending to NBFCs had slowed after the RBI raised risk weights on such exposures in November 2023. The measure continued to weigh on credit growth even after the central bank rolled it back in February 2025. Growth briefly turned negative in May 2025 before recovering. The latest surge is partly attributable to this low base, with NBFC credit now growing at more than twice the pace of overall bank credit.The relative cost of funding has also encouraged NBFCs to shift towards bank loans. Bond-market borrowing has become more expensive than bank funding. NBFCs have also increased their borrowing to finance festive-season demand for vehicle, consumer durable and gold loans. Some lenders may have advanced their funding plans ahead of the RBI's October rate hike. Advt The pace of growth, however, may moderate in the coming months. Festive-season demand and front-loaded borrowing are likely to normalise, while the low-base effect will begin to fade from October 2026. The year-on-year growth rate could therefore ease even as the absolute level of bank credit to NBFCs remains higher.Concentration in fresh lendingThe growing share of NBFCs in incremental bank lending makes funding concentration an important area to monitor. Although they account for less than a tenth of total bank credit, NBFCs absorbed around 18% of the additional non-food credit extended by banks in the year to September 2026. Advt Their share of bank lending to services and of incremental services credit is also substantial. This growing reliance on bank funding increases the importance of tracking how credit is distributed across financial intermediaries.
