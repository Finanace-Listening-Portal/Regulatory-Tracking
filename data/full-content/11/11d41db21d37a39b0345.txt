Poonawalla Fincorp, Cholamandalam Investment and Finance Company, HDB Financial Services and Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services also have significant exposure. L&T Finance has the highest exposure among the NBFCs, with insurance commission accounting for about 26% of FY26 profit before tax and 80 basis points of average assets, according to an assessment by JM Financial. Advt IRDAI's consultation paper proposes lower expense of management caps for life and general insurers and segment-level commission caps, which had been eliminated from FY24. The regulator has sought feedback on the proposals until October 25, 2026.The proposed changes could have a greater impact on lenders with multiple insurance tie-ups and incentive-led payouts. Multi-tie-up banks earned an average total payout of 33% of life new business premium, compared with 13% for banks with a single tie-up.Impact on banksAcross the banks covered, insurance distribution income increased from 3.5% of PBT in FY23 to 5.1% in FY26. Relative to balance sheet size, it rose from 4 basis points to 7 basis points of average assets. Life insurance accounted for 4.3% of PBT, or 84% of total insurance income, while non-life insurance remained at about 0.9% of PBT.Among large private banks, Axis Bank has the highest exposure to insurance income, which accounted for 12.5% of PBT and 18 basis points of average assets in FY26. ICICI Bank had the lowest exposure at 0.6% of PBT and about 1 basis point of assets.Most mid-sized and smaller private banks, except Federal Bank and CUB, have insurance income exposure of 13-21 basis points of assets. PSU banks have lower exposure, with insurance income accounting for 1-3% of PBT and 1-3 basis points of assets.Small finance banks are the most exposed segment relative to their balance sheets, with insurance income at 13-25 basis points of assets and 14-71% of PBT. SBI is expected to see less disruption as SBI Life is already within the EOM norms. Advt The proposed cuts to life commissions, particularly on credit life and short-tenure savings products, could affect the largest component of banks' insurance distribution income. There could also be some shift in bank distribution away from ULIPs towards mutual funds.NBFCs have emerged as a rapidly growing channel for life insurance distribution. New business premium sourced through NBFCs nearly tripled from Rs 3,600 crore in FY23 to Rs 10,300 crore in FY25, while payouts to them reached Rs 4,300 crore, or about 42% of the premium.About 93% of this business is loan-linked group credit life. Commission on this business increased from 5% to 28% in two years and to about 45% including other payouts. Advt NBFCs also earn the highest commissions in general insurance, averaging 40%, compared with 26% for banks and 17-18% for agents and brokers.The proposed reforms could affect both insurance volumes and payouts. Forced bundling of insurance with loans would be banned, while lenders would have to show loan pricing both with and without insurance. Incentives for NBFC staff would also be barred, and all forms of payment would count as commission.Group credit life payouts of 13-67% would face caps. For vehicle financiers, commission on new-vehicle own-damage, personal accident and legal liability covers would fall to 5%, while third-party cover would carry zero commission for distributors. Advt Large distributors would also face cost audits and commission clawbacks for mis-selling.Among NBFCs, L&T Finance has the highest exposure, with insurance commission accounting for about 26% of FY26 PBT and 80 basis points of average assets. Poonawalla Fincorp, Cholamandalam Investment and Finance Company, HDB Financial Services and Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services also have significant exposure.Their income comes mainly from credit-linked and motor insurance, which are among the areas targeted by the proposed changes. Poonawalla Fincorp's insurance income accounted for 38.4% of PBT in FY25.For Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services, Home First Finance and Poonawalla Fincorp, the share of insurance income increased sharply over FY24-26.Shriram Housing Finance, Aditya Birla Finance, Aavas Financiers, CreditAccess Grameen and Bajaj Housing Finance are relatively better placed, as insurance is only a small part of their profits. Bajaj Finance, Tata Capital, PNB Housing Finance and Fedfina are in the middle, with the impact expected to be manageable.Group-linked NBFCs such as Bajaj Finance, Aditya Birla Finance, Tata Capital, HDB Financial Services and CIFC could face additional scrutiny because of conflicts of interest arising from common directors between insurers and their promoter NBFCs.IRDAI has sought feedback until October 25, 2026. The final impact on lenders will depend on the guidelines eventually issued.
