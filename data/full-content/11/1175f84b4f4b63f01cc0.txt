Summary: Algorand is an active layer-one blockchain whose native unit, Algo (ALGO), pays transaction fees, supports minimum-balance requirements and supplies stake for Pure Proof-of-Stake consensus. Current protocol documentation describes cryptographic sortition through verifiable random functions, short-lived participation keys, immediate transaction finality and a base fee of 0.001 Algo when the network is not congested. Since protocol version 4.0, eligible consensus participants may receive block-proposer rewards, but this is operational participation rather than a passive guarantee: node availability, account registration, reward eligibility, online stake and protocol rules matter. For an Indian reader, ALGO remains a privately issued virtual digital asset, not legal tender or an RBI-backed instrument. Transfers can attract section 115BBH tax and section 194S withholding, while service providers carrying on covered VDA activities in India face FIU-IND reporting-entity obligations. The analysis below separates protocol facts from price or investment claims and focuses on custody, transaction construction, staking and compliance records. Key Takeaways Show all 18 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Algorand and ALGO in the Current Protocol Algorand is a public, permissionless blockchain designed to settle transactions through a Pure Proof-of-Stake consensus protocol. Algo is the chain’s native currency. It is distinct from Algorand Standard Assets, which are user-created assets recorded by the protocol, and from application tokens issued by smart contracts or other projects. That distinction matters for custody, tax records and technical due diligence: a wallet balance labelled ALGO represents the native unit, while another asset using the Algorand network may have a separate issuer, supply policy and legal character. The Algorand Foundation’s August 2026 report stated that circulating ALGO had reached 9.04 billion, or 90.4% of the fixed 10 billion maximum supply. Circulating-supply figures are time-sensitive and should not be treated as permanent. The cap does not prevent market volatility, protocol changes in distribution schedules, custody loss or changes in the proportion held by institutions, the Foundation or community participants. Supply information is useful for identifying the asset and understanding dilution mechanics; it is not a valuation forecast. For a round, a verifiable random function privately determines whether an eligible account is selected to propose a block or serve on a voting committee. A selected proposer publishes the block with cryptographic proof of selection; voting steps narrow the choice and certify the block. The probability of selection rises with participating stake, but the account’s spending key is not used for consensus messages. This architecture reduces the incentive to target a known proposer in advance because selection is privately known until a participant speaks. Participation keys are not spending keys Algorand separates transaction-authorising spending keys from time-bounded participation keys. Participation material and ephemeral keys are used for consensus rounds, with old ephemeral keys deleted to support forward security. Compromise of a node holding participation keys is therefore a serious operational event, but it is not automatically the same as compromise of the offline spending key. A node operator must still protect systems, monitor key validity, keep software current and take an account properly offline when retiring participation. Wallet users who do not operate a node should not describe ordinary token holding as direct validation. Transactions, Assets and Account Controls Fees, validity windows and atomic groups A standard transaction carries a validity window measured in rounds and identifies the sender, fee and transaction-specific fields. The minimum fee is currently 0.001 Algo for common Ed25519, multisignature and logic-signature authorisation when uncongested. Network congestion, oversized notes or post-quantum signatures can change the required amount. Algorand also permits groups of up to 16 transactions to execute atomically: either all valid group transactions succeed or none does. Fee pooling can allow one group member to pay more while another pays less, provided the group as a whole meets the protocol requirement. Users should review the entire group before signing, not only the line that appears to transfer value to them. Minimum balance, opt-ins and rekeying Every account starts with a protocol minimum-balance requirement, and opting into Algorand Standard Assets or applications can increase the required locked balance. Closing an asset holding, application state or an account requires correctly unwinding those resources. Rekeying allows an account address to keep its identity while changing the authorised signing address. That is useful for custody migration and institutional controls, but an unintended rekey can transfer practical control without moving funds. Hardware-wallet screens, multisignature policies and application calls should therefore be verified against the expected sender, authorised address, group identifier and asset ID. Staking Rewards Require Active Consensus Participation Protocol version 4.0 added proposer rewards. Official instructions state that an account can register participation keys online and opt into reward eligibility by paying a 2 Algo registration fee. A self-hosted node generally needs at least 30,000 ALGO of eligible online stake to receive block-proposer rewards directly, and the current mechanism pays eligible proposers rather than distributing a fixed yield to every holder. The Foundation also describes a supplementary per-block bonus that decays under stated rules. These parameters can change through protocol updates and funding decisions. Pooled staking services may let smaller holders participate indirectly, but they introduce a separate operator, smart-contract, fee, withdrawal and custody analysis. A quoted annualised rate from an exchange or pool is not a protocol guarantee. Rewards can be irregular because proposals are probabilistic; downtime and missed challenges can suspend eligibility. Indian taxpayers should preserve the date, quantity, rupee fair value, wallet address, transaction ID, operator statement and any fee for each reward. The later transfer of rewarded ALGO can create a separate tax computation from any income character attributed when the reward was received. Indian Tax, TDS and VDA-Service Compliance For current Indian income-tax purposes, a cryptographic token such as ALGO will ordinarily fall within the virtual digital asset definition. Section 115BBH taxes income from transfer of a VDA at 30%, with applicable surcharge and health and education cess. The provision allows the cost of acquisition specified by law but generally denies other deductions and does not permit a transfer loss from one VDA to be set off against other income or carried forward under that section. Classification of a staking receipt before a later transfer can depend on facts and accounting treatment, so a taxpayer should not simply treat every wallet credit as a tax-free event. Section 194S generally requires 1% tax deduction on consideration paid to a resident for transfer of a VDA, subject to statutory thresholds and special mechanics for consideration in kind or exchanged VDAs. The Income Tax Department’s current guidance states thresholds of ₹50,000 for a specified person and ₹10,000 for other payers. TDS is a credit mechanism, not the final 30% tax. Taxpayers should reconcile exchange certificates, Form 26AS or the Annual Information Statement, wallet records and their own transaction ledger. Transfers between self-controlled wallets should be documented so that an internal movement is not confused with a sale. India’s anti-money-laundering framework covers specified VDA activities. FIU-IND’s January 2026 AML/CFT/CPF guidelines identify FIU-IND as the regulator for reporting entities providing covered VDA services, and current registration directions impose documentation and cyber-audit requirements on applicants. An exchange’s or custodian’s registration is not an endorsement of ALGO, a guarantee of solvency or confirmation that every service it offers is lawful for every customer. Users should verify the platform’s exact legal entity, FIU-IND status where applicable, custody terms and withdrawal functionality. No. ALGO is the native unit. Algorand Standard Assets and application-issued tokens can have different asset IDs, issuers, contracts and risks. Verify the exact asset rather than relying on a ticker or logo. Does simply holding ALGO make a wallet a validator? No. Direct consensus participation requires an online account with valid registered participation keys operated by a synchronized node. Ordinary custody without that setup is not direct validation. Is every Algorand transaction fee exactly 0.001 ALGO? No. That is the current base minimum for common transactions when uncongested. Transaction size, signature type, congestion and fee pooling can change the amount a signer must authorize. Can an account spend its complete displayed ALGO balance? Not necessarily. The base minimum and additional minimums for asset and application resources must remain until those resources are closed or cleared. Wallets should distinguish total from spendable balance. Are Algorand staking rewards guaranteed? No. Eligibility, online stake, successful block proposals, node operation, protocol parameters and any pool terms determine receipts. A displayed rate is not a sovereign or contractual guarantee unless the relevant contract expressly says so. How should an Indian taxpayer track ALGO transactions? Maintain wallet addresses, transaction IDs, quantities, fees, purpose, counterparty or platform, INR value and method, cost records, reward statements and section 194S credits. Reconcile self-transfers separately. Key Takeaways ALGO is Algorand’s native unit; it is not interchangeable with every asset issued on the chain. Pure Proof-of-Stake uses privately selected proposers and committees, while participation keys separate consensus operations from spending authority. The base fee is 0.001 Algo when uncongested, but transaction design and signatures can increase fees and minimum-balance requirements. Current staking rewards are tied to active, eligible consensus participation rather than passive holding and are not a fixed return.
