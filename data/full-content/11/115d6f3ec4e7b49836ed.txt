GST Input Tax Credit Under Sections 16(2)(aa) and 16(2)(b): Supplier Reporting and Receipt of Goods Summary: The article examines input tax credit eligibility under Sections 16(2)(aa) and 16(2)(b) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, focusing on supplier reporting and the receipt of goods. It explains that Section 16(2)(aa), introduced with effect from 1 January 2022, links a recipient’s claim to the supplier furnishing invoice or debit-note particulars in outward-supply statements and their communication to the recipient under Section 37. The authors refer to Rule 59(4), Form GSTR-1 and the practical consequences of missing invoices in Form GSTR-2B. They argue that a genuine purchaser should have an opportunity to substantiate a transaction with invoices, banking records and evidence of receipt instead of facing an automatic denial solely because the supplier failed to report an invoice. The discussion cites the Madhya Pradesh High Court decision in Shree Kishna Chemicals and a Gauhati High Court decision involving MCLEOD Russel India Limited, alongside CBIC circulars addressing earlier implementation difficulties. Turning to Section 16(2)(b), the article describes the statutory explanation under which delivery to another person at the registered purchaser’s direction may constitute deemed receipt. It also considers principal-agent supplies, deliveries away from the purchaser’s premises and ex-works contracts. Particular attention is given to CBIC Circular No. 241/35/2024-GST dated 31 December 2024, including delivery to transporters at the supplier’s premises. The authors conclude that supplier reporting obligations and deemed-receipt principles should inform departmental scrutiny of ITC claims and question notices that disregard those considerations. These are the authors’ interpretations, not a finding that every disputed credit or notice must succeed. Purpose and Effect of Section 16(2)(aa) And the Hon’ble Gauhati High Court in the case of M/s MCLEOD Russel India Limited Vs Union of India, the Hon’ble High court categorically adjudged that the purpose of inserting Section 16(2)(aa) into the CGST Act is to mandate a stricter condition for availing ICT. It actually ties the recipient’s ability to claim ITC directly to the supplier’s compliance with their GST filing obligations. This amendment was introduced to address tax evasion and improve the transparency and integrity of the GST system. Thus, the purpose and rationale is to prevent fraudulent ITC claims, to promote supplier compliance and eliminate provisional ITC, which has proved to be effective in curbing fraudulent claims. The object and purpose of GST Act is to avoid any cascading effect of taxation. Proof of Receipt of Goods Under Section 16(2)(b) Section 16 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 governs the eligibility of a registered person to claim input tax credit on GST paid on inward supplies of goods or services used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business. Section 16 establishes the statutory framework for claiming ITC. Eligibility must be examined with reference to the applicable provisions, supporting documents, actual receipt of supplies, supplier reporting, tax payment and prescribed time limits. CBIC Circular 241/35/2024-GST on Third-Party Delivery The Explanation to Section 16(2)(b) expressly provides for deemed receipt where goods are delivered by the supplier to any other recipient or person on the direction of the registered purchaser, whether acting as an agent or otherwise, before or during movement of the goods, by transfer of documents of title or otherwise. Further, CBIC Circular No. 241/35/2024-GST dated 31 December 2024 clarifies at clarification No 3.2.1 The said Explanation provides that where goods are delivered by the supplier to any other person, whether acting as an agent or not, upon the direction of the registered person, and where such delivery occurs either through transfer of documents of title to goods or otherwise, the registered person is deemed to have “received” such goods for the purpose of the clause (b) of sub-section (2) of section 16 of CGST Act. Accordingly, in cases where goods are delivered by the supplier to the registered person, either directly or to any other person on the directions of the said registered person, the registered person shall be considered to have “received” the said goods for the purpose of clause (b) of sub-section (2) of section 16 of CGST Act. EXW Contracts and Delivery to Transporters And at clarification No 3.4 of above circular, The same principle is applicable in respect of supply of other goods also where the contract between the supplier and recipient is an EXW contract, and as per terms of the contract, the goods are to be delivered by the supplier to the recipient, or to any other person (including a transporter) on behalf of the recipient, at his (supplier’s) place of business and the property in the goods stands transferred to the recipient at the time of such handing over. In such cases, the said goods can be construed to have been “received” by the said recipient at the time of handing over the said goods to the recipient or to the transporter, as the case may be, as per provisions of clause (b) of sub-section (2) of section 16 of CGST Act.
