Articles 4 min read Kotak says FCNR to put further pressure on Q3 margins; repo repricing, deployment to help Kotak Mahindra Bank’s net interest margin fell to 4.31 per cent in Q2 FY27 from 4.53 per cent in the previous quarter, with FCNR(B) deposits and related advances accounting for around 15 bps of the decline. The bank expects the full-quarter FCNR impact in Q3, while deployment of the Rs 55,344 crore FCNR pool and repricing of repo-linked loans could support margins. CFO Devang Gheewalla said FCNR(B) deposits and advances against those deposits contributed around 15 basis points to the sequential decline in NIM, while the remainder was largely due to quarter-specific factors including day-count variation and repayment of IBPC borrowings. Advt For Q3, however, the bank will see the full-quarter impact of the FCNR(B) mobilisation, while the deployment of these funds into customer assets is expected to happen progressively.“We will see the full quarter impact of the FCNR(B) mobilisation in Q3,” CFO said during the media call, adding that the bank expects the progressive deployment of excess FCNR liquidity and repricing of repo-linked and other floating-rate advances to provide an offset.Kotak had mobilised around Rs 55,344 crore of FCNR(B) deposits under the RBI’s swap facility. Of this, around Rs 16,110 crore had been deployed as advances against FCNR(B) deposits through the bank’s international branches.The bank has said it will not deploy the entire FCNR pool in a single quarter. Instead, the remaining liquidity will be deployed over several quarters into customer assets, depending on the rates, segments and tenures that management is comfortable with.FCNR mobilisation changes liability mixKotak’s deposits grew 23 per cent year-on-year to Rs 6.51 lakh crore as of September 2026, while net advances rose 25 per cent to Rs 5.77 lakh crore. Customer assets, including advances and credit substitutes, increased 23 per cent year-on-year to Rs 6.27 lakh crore.However, the sharp increase in FCNR deposits has also changed the deposit mix. The CASA ratio declined to 38.2 per cent in September 2026, from 40.3 per cent in June and 42.3 per cent a year earlier. At the same time, the bank’s term deposits increased to Rs 4.02 lakh crore from Rs 3.42 lakh crore in June. Advt The bank’s reported cost of funds, however, improved to 4.52 per cent in Q2FY27 from 4.70 per cent a year earlier, although it was higher than 4.46 per cent in Q1.Kotak’s management has indicated that the large FCNR pool gives it flexibility to choose where and when to deploy funds rather than chasing loan growth purely to deploy the liquidity.The bank’s CD ratio stood at 88.6 per cent at the end of September, compared with 89.4 per cent in June and 87.5 per cent a year earlier.Repricing expected to aid marginsApart from deploying FCNR liquidity, Kotak expects the repricing of its floating-rate loan book to provide another offset. Advt Around 60 per cent of the bank’s advances are repo-linked, according to management. The bank expects the benefit from repricing to build progressively, with the full impact of the recent rate changes expected in Q4.This would provide some relief to margins after the immediate pressure from the FCNR mobilisation.Despite the margin compression, Kotak’s net interest income rose 16 per cent year-on-year to Rs 8,459 crore in Q2FY27, while other income increased 23 per cent to Rs 3,194 crore. Net total income rose 18 per cent to Rs 11,653 crore.Fee and services income rose 21 per cent to Rs 2,926 crore, with the bank receiving around Rs 158 crore of processing fees during the quarter on advances against FCNR(B) deposits. Advt The bank also reported around Rs 116 crore of fees and other expenditure on borrowings mobilised by its international branches, while provisions on standard assets included around Rs 120 crore related to advances against FCNR(B) deposits and loans transferred from Kotak Mahindra Investments during the quarter.Kotak reported a 29 per cent year-on-year increase in standalone PAT to Rs 4,212 crore in Q2FY27. Return on assets improved to 1.92 per cent from 1.88 per cent a year earlier, while return on equity stood at 11.65 per cent.The bank’s credit metrics also remained stable, with net NPA at 0.24 per cent, credit cost at 0.35 per cent and CET-I capital adequacy at 21.4 per cent at the end of September.
