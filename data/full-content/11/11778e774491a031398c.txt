Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Ratnalaya Jewellers Vs Assistant/DCIT (Patna High Court) Summary: The Patna High Court dismissed the writ petition filed by Ratnalaya Jewellers challenging a notice dated 29 March 2025 issued under Section 148 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, for assessment year 2021–22. The partnership firm, constituted with effect from 1 April 2020, had obtained two PANs—ABBFR2514C and ABBFR3082R—in June and July 2020. It filed its return under the first PAN, declaring income of ₹1,23,64,150 and audited sales of approximately ₹27.78 crore. The firm claimed that the second PAN had been surrendered through an application dated 27 January 2021 and that reopening under the second PAN concerned a non-existent entity. The controversy arose from substantial cash deposits in bank accounts linked to the second PAN. During an inquiry under Section 133(6), the firm maintained that the deposits were reflected in its audited accounts and arose from sales of gold and silver ornaments. An Income-tax Officer of the Intelligence and Criminal Investigation Wing recorded in a preliminary verification report that the verification issues stood explained because the information value had been considered against the first PAN. Nevertheless, the Department stated that the Final Verification Report showed ₹4,59,91,700 as estimated income escaping assessment, flagged on the Insight Portal under the High-Risk e-Verification Scheme. Approval was obtained from the specified authority before the Section 148 notice was issued. The Revenue also stated that both PANs had been applied for separately, with different descriptions of the source of income; the second PAN remained active and was used for HDFC and IDBI Bank accounts in subsequent financial years. It referred to cash deposits in those accounts and the prohibition on holding more than one PAN under Section 139A(7). The petitioner did not file a rejoinder disputing these counter-affidavit statements. Its counsel relied on reassessment precedents and argued that there was no jurisdictional information, particularly in view of the earlier verification report. The Department relied on the statutory reassessment process and decisions restricting writ interference where an effective alternative remedy exists. The High Court observed that the petitioner admittedly obtained and used the second PAN but had not filed a return under it. Although the preliminary verification report noted the information against the other PAN, the audit report before the Court did not identify the second PAN, and the financial statements did not specify the accounts maintained under it. Deciding whether the deposits were already included in the declared sales would require a factual exercise that the Court could not undertake under Article 226. It also referred to the Directorate of Income Tax (Systems) communication dated 11 March 2025 concerning dissemination of high-risk e-verification cases and the estimated Value at Risk shown in final verification reports. Considering Commissioner of Income Tax v. Chhabil Dass Agarwal and Sanand Properties Private Limited v. Joint Commissioner of Income Tax, the Court held that the sufficiency of the Assessing Officer’s reasons was not for determination in the writ proceedings. On the material before it, it could not conclude that the officer’s belief was mala fide or founded on vague, irrelevant or non-specific information. The Court accordingly declined to quash the reassessment notice and dismissed the petition. The judgment does not finally adjudicate the taxability of the cash deposits or determine the resulting reassessment liability. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF PATNA HIGH COURT This writ application has been preferred seeking the following reliefs:- “i) For issuance of writ of certiorari or any other appropriate writ quashing the notice under Section 148 of Income Tax Act, 1961 (“the Act”) dated 29.03.2025 for Assessment Year (“A.Y.”) 2021-22 issued by the Deputy Commissioner of Income Tax, Circle-4, Patna herein and also referred to as the “Assessing Officer” hereinafter) initiating proceedings for reassessment against the Petitioner as being wholly without jurisdiction and consequently, is illegal and bad in law as the impugned notice has been issued on a non-existent entity on PAN No.ABBFR3082R allotted by the department which has been surrendered by the petitioner way back on 27.01.2021. (ii) For issuance of a writ of certiorari or any other appropriate writ(s), order(s) or direction(s) quashing the recorded reasons by erroneously concluding that the financial transaction of Rs.4.59 crores has escaped assessment within the meaning of Section 147 and by overlooking the report of Income Tax Officer, Intelligence and Criminal Investigation, Patna wherein a finding has been recorded that the transaction of Rs.4.59 crores on account of cash deposit in Bank has duly been declared by the petitioner in its financial statements filed along with the return of income on entity in existence on PAN No. ABBFR2514C for A.Y. 2021-22 and that the cash deposit of Rs.4.59 crores is duly accounted for in the books of the petitioner and closing balance of all the Bank Account including the Bank account with HDFC Bank in which the said cash was deposited is duly reflected in the financial statements and is covered by the sales of the petitioner during the year under consideration amounting to Rs.27.78 crores. iii) For issuance of a writ of mandamus or any other appropriate writ(s), order(s) or direction(s) to bring on record the approval of specified authority as mentioned in the notice under Section 148 dated 29.03.2025. iv) For granting any other relief(s) to which the petitioner is otherwise found entitled for.” Brief Facts of the Case 2. It is the case of the petitioner that the proprietorship firm of one of its partners, namely Nagendra Prasad Keshri, was converted into a partnership firm vide partnership deed executed on 01.03.2020. The partners of the firm are (1) Nagendra Prasad Keshri, (2) Saket Keshri, son of Nagendra Keshri and (3) Sadhana Keshri, daughter of Harishchandra Prasad Keshri, all residents of 104, Pushpanjali Plaza, Basant Bihar Colony, Patna. 3. The partnership firm came into effect on 1st April, 2020 in the name and style of M/s Ratnalaya Jewellers. 4. It is the case of the petitioner that the petitioner was allotted two Permanent Account Number (PAN) bearing number ABBFR2514C and ABBFR3082R which were allotted on 21.06.2020 and 03.07.2020 respectively. According to the petitioner, the first PAN allotted on 21.06.2020 is the correct PAN whereas the second PAN allotted on 03.07.2020 is incorrect PAN. The petitioner filed a petition dated 27.01.2021 (Annexure ‘P/4’ series) before the Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax, Circle 4(1), Patna for cancellation of PAN No. ABBFR3082R. The petitioner claimed that the petitioner was regularly using PAN No. ABBFR2514C. 5. It is stated that the petitioner filed its first return in the status of partnership firm for the Assessment Year 2021-22 under PAN No. ABBFR2514C under the jurisdiction of Income Tax Officer, Ward Number 5(1), Patna declaring total income of Rs. 1,23,64,150/- on 28.01.2022 along with audited set of accounts and had paid tax of Rs. 43,20,528/-. It is his case that the audited set of accounts takes into account the entire financial transactions including deposit of cash in HDFC Bank amounting to Rs. 4.59 crores, source of which is covered out of declared sales of Rs. 27.78 crores of gold and silver ornaments. 6. It is further submitted that the return filed on 21.08.2022 for Assessment Year 2021-22 under PAN No. ABBFR2514C was accepted under Section 143(1) vide intimation under Section 143(1) dated 10.05.2022. In the subsequent Assessment Year 2022-23 and Assessment Year 2023-24 also, the petitioner firm filed its return on the same PAN, which were accepted by the Department. Institution of an inquiry under Section 133(6) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 7. It is stated that the Intelligence and Criminal Investigation Wing of the Department instituted an inquiry under Section 133(6) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as the ‘Act of 1961’ or the ‘IT Act’). The petitioner was served with a notice whereby it was asked to explain the cash deposit of Rs. 8.33 crores during Financial Year 2020-21 in HDFC Bank Account No. 50200028168974. The petitioner submitted a reply stating that the entire cash deposit is duly accounted for in the books of the petitioner. The petitioner claimed that the cash deposit is covered under the total sale of Rs. 27.78 crores, declared in the audited set of accounts filed with the return of income. 8. It is stated that the Income Tax Officer, Intelligence and Criminal Investigation had instituted another inquiry under Section 133(6) vide notice dated 01.05.2024 in respect of cash deposit in another bank account with HDFC bearing Account No. 50200052342649 whereby the petitioner was asked to explain the cash deposit. In compliance thereof, the petitioner submitted a reply stating that it has already filed an application on 27.01.2021 for surrender of duplicate PAN i.e. ABBFR3082R. The petitioner claimed that it was regularly using PAN No. ABBFR2514C. The petitioner reiterated its stand that the entire cash deposit is duly accounted for in the books of the petitioner and the bank account with HDFC Bank, all these have been duly disclosed in the balance sheet and income tax return. It is the stand of the petitioner that in support of his averments made in the reply, the petitioner had submitted copy of ITR, copy of audited set of account, details of bank account (Annexure ‘P/8’ series). 9. It is stated that the Income Tax Officer, Intelligence and Criminal Investigation, after conducting inquiry and verification and after taking note of the replies filed by the petitioner pursuant to the aforementioned notices, recorded a finding as to cash deposit in bank of Rs. 4.59 crores to be out of explained sources i.e. sale of gold and silver ornaments of Rs. 27.78 crores. It is the stand of the petitioner that the Income Tax Officer observed that the verification issues stood explained as information value was taken into consideration against other PAN of the firm ABBFR2514C. Issuance of notice under Section 148 read with Section 147 of the IT Act 10. The petitioner is aggrieved by the issuance of notice under Section 148 dated 29.03.2025 (Annexure ‘P/1’ series) under the I.T. Act by the respondent under PAN No. ABBFR3082R, after obtaining prior approval of specified authority alleging therein that income chargeable to tax amounting to Rs. 4.59 crores on account of cash deposit in bank has escaped assessment for the Assessment Year 2021-22 within the meaning of Section 135A, 147 and 148 of the Act. The notice required the petitioner to file it’s return of income within 90 days in prescribed form in respect of PAN No. ABBFR3082R. Submissions on behalf of the petitioner 11. Mr. Ajay Kumar Rastogi, learned Senior Counsel representing the petitioner, has taken a plea that the notice under Section 148 of the IT Act has been issued upon a non-existent entity vide PAN No. ABBFR3082R. The petitioner had already submitted an application surrendering the said PAN. The petitioner was using PAN No. ABBFR2514C allotted to it on 21.06.2020 and had filed its return of income for Assessment Year 2021-22 with the correct PAN. 12. Learned Senior Counsel for the petitioner further submits that it is in the exclusive domain of the revenue authorities to deactivate/cancel the PAN allotted to a non-existent entity. According to him, non-discharge of statutory obligation of deactivating/cancelling the PAN has led to issuance of notice under Section 148 of the IT Act on an entity which does not exist. 13. It is submitted that the allegation of escapement of income is contrary to materials on record and is bereft of any ‘information’ as required under Clause (iv) of Explanation 1 to Section 148 read with Section 135A of the IT Act, thus, the impugned notice is liable to be quashed by this Court as the jurisdictional condition for reopening of assessment being ‘information’ is absent in the present case. Since the preconditions under Section 135A, 147 and 148 of the IT Act are not satisfied as there is no entity in existence of PAN ABBFR3082R, the very assumption of jurisdiction for reassessment is wholly illegal and without jurisdiction. 14. Learned Senior Counsel has relied upon the judgments of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Chhugamal Rajpal vs. S.P. Chaliha and Ors. reported in (1971) 79 ITR 603 : (1971) 1 SCC 453, Commissioner of Income Tax vs. Kelvinator of India Ltd. reported in (2010) 320 ITR 561 : (2010) 2 SCC 723, Siemens Financial Services Private Ltd. vs. Deputy Commissioner of Income Tax and Ors. reported in (2023) 457 ITR 647 : 2023 SCC OnLine BOM 2822 and a recent judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Sanand Properties Private Limited vs. Joint Commissioner of Income Tax and Ors. reported in (2026) 488 ITR 337 : 2026 SCC OnLine SC 851. In the present case, the notice under Section 148 of the IT Act is the bone of contention between the parties. So far as the facts are concerned, it is not in dispute that the petitioner made two separate applications for obtaining PAN. In one of the applications, the petitioner declared it’s source of income as “other sources” whereas in the another application, the petitioner declared it’s source of income as “business”. There is no denial of the fact that the petitioner used the second PAN ABBFR3082R in it’s bank account maintained with HDFC Bank as well as IDBI Bank not only in the Financial Year 2020-21 but also in Financial Year 2021-22 and 2022-23. There is also no denial of the fact that the petitioner had deposited cash of Rs. 8.33 crore during the Financial Year 2020-21 in its HDFC Bank account number xxxx8974. The petitioner filed its return of income under the first PAN, which was issued on 21.06.2020. No return of income has been furnished by the petitioner under the second PAN issued on 03.07.2020. Since the petitioner was using the second PAN in the HDFC and IDBI Bank accounts, it came to the notice of the Department of Income Tax and as stated in the counter affidavit, an inquiry was instituted under Section 133(6) of the IT Act, whereunder the petitioner was asked to explain the cash deposit of Rs. 8.33 crore during the Financial Year 2020-21 in its one of the HDFC Bank accounts. 23. The scope and ambit of interference with a notice under Section 148 of the Act has been succinctly laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Chhabil Dass Agarwal (supra). We reproduce paragraphs ‘15’, ‘16’ and ‘17’ of the judgment in case of Chhabil Dass Agarwal (supra) hereunder for a ready reference:- “15. Thus, while it can be said that this Court has recognised some exceptions to the rule of alternative remedy i.e. where the statutory authority has not acted in accordance with the provisions of the enactment in question, or in defiance of the fundamental principles of judicial procedure, or has resorted to invoke the provisions which are repealed, or when an order has been passed in total violation of the principles of natural justice, the proposition laid down in Thansingh Nathmal case22 , Titaghur Paper Mills case3 and other similar judgments that the High Court will not entertain a petition under Article 226 of the Constitution if an effective alternative remedy is available to the aggrieved person or the statute under which the action complained of has been taken itself contains a mechanism for redressal of grievance still holds the field. Therefore, when a statutory forum is created by law for redressal of grievances, a writ petition should not be entertained ignoring the statutory dispensation. 16. In the instant case, the Act provides complete machinery for the assessment/reassessment of tax, imposition of penalty and for obtaining relief in respect of any improper orders passed by the Revenue Authorities, and the assessee could not be permitted to abandon that machinery and to invoke the jurisdiction of the High Court under Article 226 of the Constitution when he had adequate remedy open to him by an appeal to the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals). The remedy under the statute, however, must be effective and not a mere formality with no substantial relief. In Ram and Shyam Co. v. State of Haryana40 this Court has noticed that if an appeal is from “Caesar to Caesar’s wife” the existence of alternative remedy would be a mirage and an exercise in futility. 17. In the instant case, neither has the writ petitioner assessee described the available alternate remedy under the Act as ineffectual and non-efficacious while invoking the writ jurisdiction of the High Court nor has the High Court ascribed cogent and satisfactory reasons to have exercised its jurisdiction in the facts of the instant case. In light of the same, we are of the considered opinion that the writ court ought not to have entertained the writ petition filed by the assessee, wherein he has only questioned the correctness or otherwise of the notices issued under Section 148 of the Act, the reassessment orders passed and the consequential demand notices issued thereon.” 29. In the light of the aforesaid judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court, this Court refused to quash the notice under Section 148 of the IT Act in case of Alkem Laboratories Limited (supra). 30. Section 147 of the IT Act provides for initiation of a proceeding in respect of such income which has escaped assessment and which comes to the notice of the Assessing Officer subsequently in course of proceedings under this section. The Assessing Officer is competent to recompute the loss or the depreciation allowance or any other allowance, as the case may be, for the assessment year concerned. In a recent judgment in the case of Sanand Properties Private Limited (supra), the Hon’ble Supreme Court has reviewed the previous case laws on the subject in detail. In that case, the assessee had undergone scrutiny assessment under Section 143(3) whereafter the Income Tax Department found certain materials during a survey under Section 133A of the IT Act in the business premises of the assessee. Certain books of account and documents were seized and impounded. Statement of one of the directors of the assessee was also recorded under Section 131 of the IT Act whereafter it was held by the Assessing Officer that these materials indicated that the income received by the assessee was not a share of its profit but a share of its revenue. Based on this reason, the Department issued notice under Section 148 of the IT Act. The Department was of the view that the income had escaped assessment within the meaning of sub-clause (iv) of clause (c) of Explanation 2 to Section 147 of the IT Act. 32. In the context of the aforesaid case, the Hon’ble Supreme Court went into its earlier judgment in case of CIT vs. Kelvinator of India (supra), Calcutta Discount Co. Ltd. vs. Income Tax Officer, Companies District Calcutta and Another reported in (1961) 41 ITR 191, Phool Chand Bajrang Lal vs. Income Tax Officer and Anr. reported in (1993) 4 SCC 77 and on a review of the case laws, the Hon’ble Supreme Court held in paragraph ‘71’ of its judgment as under:- “71. Moreover, as pithily put by this court in Phool Chand Bajrang Lal v. ITO19, one must understand that acquiring fresh information relating to a concluded assessment which goes to expose the falsity of a statement made by the assessee at the time of original assessment is different from drawing a fresh inference from the same facts and material which was available with the Assessing Officer at the time of original assessment proceedings. Where the transaction itself on the basis of subsequent information, is found to be a bogus transaction, the mere disclosure of that transaction at the time of original assessment proceedings, cannot be said to be disclosure of the “true” and “full” facts in the case, and the Income-tax Officer would have the jurisdiction to reopen the concluded assessment in such a case. The relevant observation of this court in Phool Chand Bajrang Lal v. ITO19 reads thus21 : “19…. Acquiring fresh information, specific in nature and reliable in character, relating to the concluded assessment which goes to expose the falsity of the statement made by the assessee at the time of original assessment is different from drawing a fresh inference from the same facts and material which were available with the Income-tax Officer at the time of the original assessment proceedings. The two situations are distinct and different. Thus, where the transaction itself on the basis of subsequent information, is found to be a bogus transaction, the mere disclosure of that transaction at the time of original assessment proceedings, cannot be said to be disclosure of the ‘true’ and ‘full’ facts in the case and the Income-tax Officer would have the jurisdiction to reopen the concluded assessment in such a case. It is correct that the assessing authority could have deferred the completion of the original assessment proceedings for further enquiry and investigation into the genuineness of the loan transaction but in our opinion his failure to do so and complete the original assessment proceedings would not take away his jurisdiction to act under section 147 of the Act, on receipt of the information subsequently. The subsequent information on the basis of which the Income-tax Officer acquired reasons to believe that income chargeable to tax had escaped assessment on account of the omission of the assessee to make a full and true disclosure of the primary facts was relevant, reliable and specific. It was not at all vague or non-specific…. 25. From a combined review of the judgments of this court, it follows that an Income-tax Officer acquires jurisdiction to reopen an assessment under section 147(a) read with section 148 of the Income- tax Act, 1961 only if on the basis of specific, reliable and relevant information coming to his possession subsequently, he has reasons which he must record, to believe that by reason of omission or failure on the part of the assessee to make a true and full disclosure of all material facts necessary for his assessment during the concluded assessment proceedings, any part of his income, profit or gains chargeable to income-tax has escaped assessment. He may start reassessment proceedings either because some fresh facts had come to light which were not previously disclosed or some information with regard to the facts previously disclosed comes into his possession which tends to expose the untruthfulness of those facts. In such situations, it is not a case of mere change of opinion or the drawing of a different inference from the same facts as were earlier available but acting on fresh information. Since the belief is that of the Income-tax Officer, the sufficiency of reasons for forming the belief, is not for the court to judge but it is open to an assessee to establish that there in fact existed no belief or that the belief was not at all a bona fide one or was based on vague, irrelevant and non-specific information. To that limited extent, the court may look into the conclusion arrived at by the Income-tax Officer and examine whether there was any material available on the record from which the requisite belief could be formed by the Income-tax Officer and further whether that material had any rational connection or a live link for the formation of the requisite belief. It would be immaterial whether the Income-tax Officer at the time of making the original assessment, could or, could not have found by further enquiry or investigation, whether the transaction was genuine or not, if on the basis of subsequent information, the Income-tax Officer arrives at a conclusion, after satisfying the twin conditions prescribed in section 147(a) of the Act, that the assessee had not made a full and true disclosure of the material facts at the time of original assessment and, therefore, income chargeable to tax had escaped assessment. The High Courts which have interpreted CIT v. Burlop Dealers Ltd.22 as laying down the law to the contrary fell into an error and did not appreciate the import of that judgment correctly. 26. We are not persuaded to accept the argument of Mr. Sharma that the question regarding the truthfulness or falsehood of the transactions reflected in the return can only be examined during the original assessment proceedings and not at any stage subsequent thereto. The argument is too broad and general in nature and does violence to the plain phraseology of sections 147(a) and 148 of the Act and is against the settled law laid down by this court. We have to look to the purpose and intent of the provisions. One of the purposes of section 147, appears to us to be, to ensure that a party cannot get away by wilfully making a false or untrue statement at the time of original assessment and when that falsity comes to notice, to turn around and say ’you accepted my lie, now your hands are tied and you can do nothing’. It would be travesty of justice to allow the assessee that latitude.” (emphasis8 supplied)” 33. The Hon’ble Supreme Court has held in paragraph ‘87’ of its judgment in the case of Sanand Properties (P) Ltd. (supra) as under:- “87. Thus, it is settled law that the validity of a reopening must be tested solely on the basis of the reasons recorded at the time of issuing the notice under section 148. A document not referred to in those reasons recorded under section 148 cannot be used to justify the validity of the reopening, as the assessee must not be deprived of a fair opportunity to dispute such grounds. In the present matter, while we exclude the extraneous documents relied upon by the High Court, our inquiry into the reassessment’s validity, when tested strictly against the reasons recorded, reveals existence of “tangible material” to form a bona fide belief that income has escaped assessment. Accordingly, Civil Appeal No. 9107 of 2012 stands dismissed.” 34. In the facts of the present case and keeping in view the judgments of the Hon’ble Supreme Court laying down the scope and ambit of scrutiny at this stage, this Court is of the considered opinion that this Court cannot go into the sufficiency of reasons aspect, which is in the sole domain of the Income Tax Officer. We cannot come to a conclusion in this writ petition that the belief of the Assessing Officer/Intelligence Officer is not at all a bona fide one or is based on vague, irrelevant and non-specific information. The limited extent within which the Court may look into the conclusion arrived at by the Income Tax Officer would not permit this Court to travel beyond and take a view otherwise. 35.
