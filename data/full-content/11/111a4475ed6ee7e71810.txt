Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); PCIT Vs Sunit Sudhirbhai Chokshi (Gujarat High Court) Summary: The Gujarat High Court, in a common judgment dated 6 July 2026, considered seven connected tax appeals filed by the Revenue arising from search assessments involving Sunit Sudhirbhai Chokshi. The proceedings followed a search conducted under Section 132 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 on 11 April 2017, with assessments framed under Section 153A and related provisions for Assessment Years 2012-13 to 2018-19. The Revenue challenged the relief granted by the appellate authorities in respect of additions relating to alleged on-money receipts from real estate transactions, unexplained cash credits, unsecured loans, interest expenditure and other disallowances. The principal controversy concerned the evidentiary value of seized documents and the permissible scope of additions in completed or unabated assessments. The Revenue contended that loose papers, digital records and WhatsApp communications recovered during the search constituted incriminating material indicating unaccounted receipts from property transactions. It disputed the Tribunal’s treatment of certain seized records as dumb documents and challenged the deletion of substantial additions arising from extrapolation of alleged on-money receipts across construction projects. In Tax Appeal No. 140 of 2024, concerning Assessment Year 2014-15, the disputed additions included ₹1,29,60,000 towards on-money receipts, ₹19,69,49,246 under Section 68 towards unexplained cash credits and ₹77,21,384 towards interest expenditure. The Court admitted the substantial question of law concerning deletion of the on-money addition and the treatment of seized documents, leaving that issue for consideration rather than finally deciding it in this judgment. Regarding unsecured loans and related interest expenditure, the Revenue argued that once incriminating material was found during a search, the Assessing Officer could determine total income by considering other available material, even in respect of completed assessments. The assessee opposed this interpretation, relying upon the decisions in PCIT v. Saumya Construction Pvt. Ltd., CIT v. Kabul Chawla and PCIT v. Abhisar Buildwell Pvt. Ltd. The High Court examined the distinction between pending assessments, which abate upon initiation of search proceedings, and completed or unabated assessments, where additions under Section 153A are subject to the requirement of incriminating material. It rejected the Revenue’s attempt to use the Supreme Court’s observations in Abhisar Buildwell as authority for unrestricted reassessment of completed matters. The High Court held that the Supreme Court’s decision in Abhisar Buildwell must be read as a whole. Although incriminating material discovered during a search may confer jurisdiction to assess or reassess total income in a completed assessment year, this does not permit additions unrelated to such material merely because search proceedings have been initiated. The Court emphasised that the statutory scheme does not authorise a general reopening of completed assessments under Section 153A in the absence of the necessary search-related foundation. It also recognised that, where applicable, the Revenue retains its separate remedy under Sections 147 and 148, subject to fulfilment of the prescribed statutory conditions. Consequently, the Court found no substantial question of law concerning the deletion of unsecured-loan additions and related interest disallowances that were not based on incriminating material. The judgment therefore distinguishes between the admitted controversy over alleged on-money receipts supported by seized records and the Revenue’s unsuccessful challenge to deletions involving non-incriminating material. In Tax Appeal No. 140 of 2024, the High Court admitted the substantial question concerning the evidentiary treatment of seized documents and dismissed the appeal insofar as the proposed questions relating to unsecured loans and interest expenditure were concerned. The ruling reinforces the limitation on additions in completed search assessments while preserving adjudication of the admitted on-money issue. It should not be understood as a final determination of every disputed addition or as a blanket dismissal of all seven connected appeals. Cases Discussed CIT Vs H.M. Esufali H.M.
