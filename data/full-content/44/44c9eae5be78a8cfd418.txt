An estimated 1.35 crore hectares of seasonal cropped area could face production-equivalent impact across the two seasons, according to a scenario-based assessment by SBI Research. Advt The estimates are based on district-level Kharif and Rabi rainfall deviations and agricultural production data. Potential production losses are estimated at 0.89 crore tonnes for Kharif and 2.97 crore tonnes for Rabi. The estimated gross value of production at risk has been calculated using indicative market-arrival-weighted commodity prices.The district-level assessment maps agricultural credit exposure against potential production vulnerability. Tamil Nadu has approximately Rs 43,548 crore of agricultural credit in districts classified as highly exposed, followed by Andhra Pradesh at Rs 20,256 crore and Maharashtra at Rs 12,115 crore. Together, the three states account for Rs 75,919 crore of agricultural credit in these districts.The report's Climate–Credit–Production Exposure Index identifies concentrations of potential agricultural vulnerability across Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, West Bengal, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh. Madhya Pradesh accounts for nine of the 20 most exposed districts in the Rabi assessment, with Ujjain, Dhar, Vidisha, Rajgarh and Narmadapuram emerging prominently. These districts combine a substantial share of national Rabi production with severe rainfall deviations.The impactOther districts identified in the assessment include Solapur in Maharashtra, Guntur, Bapatla, West Godavari and Sri Potti Sriramulu Nellore in Andhra Pradesh, and Nalgonda and Nizamabad in Telangana. Purba Bardhaman in West Bengal also ranks among the prominent Rabi-exposed districts, reflecting its contribution to national agricultural production. Advt The geographical distribution of potential losses differs between the two crop seasons. Kharif production losses are concentrated in districts across Rajasthan, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra, including Barmer, Jalore, Bathinda, Muktsar, Ferozpur, Ludhiana, Devbhumi Dwarka and Banaskantha. The Rabi assessment points to substantial exposure in Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal, with Dhar, Ujjain, Rajgarh, Vidisha, Purba Bardhaman, Hooghly, Agra and Kannauj among the districts recording potential production losses.The report also highlights the potential fiscal implications for states facing high agricultural climate-credit exposure. Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu could together face an estimated fiscal burden of approximately Rs 33,000 crore. The drought-related resource requirement for these five states is estimated at around Rs 13,776 crore over the award period. Advt SBI Research has cautioned states against resorting to farm loan waivers and suggested that banks be approached for restructuring agricultural loans, with state governments bearing part of the cost. It cites the experience of the droughts of 2014 and 2015, followed by El Niño conditions in 2018, when agricultural NPAs at the systemic level rose to 10.1% in FY20 from 5.4% in FY17.Farm loan waiver schemes announced by Maharashtra, Karnataka, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu during 2014–18 amounted to approximately Rs 1.26 lakh crore, as reported by the Fifteenth Finance Commission. During the 2014–15 drought, five states sought National Disaster Response Fund assistance, following which the Centre approved a combined Rs 3,347.06 crore for Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka, Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh.
