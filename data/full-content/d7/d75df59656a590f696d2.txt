Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Venu Gopal Reddy Challa Vs DCIT (ITAT Hyderabad) Peak Cash Deficit Must Be Verified: Conflicting Figures and Possible FD Duplication Warrant Fresh Examination The legal principle The Hyderabad Tribunal held that an addition based on a peak negative cash balance required fresh examination where the amount recorded by the CIT(A) did not match the assessee’s cash statement and a substantial fixed-deposit entry appeared to have been inadvertently included. The Tribunal restored the ₹20,89,050 addition under section 69A to the Assessing Officer for verification of the cash statement, fixed-deposit records and the alleged cash received from the assessee’s wife. It also restored the consequential penalty proceedings. Neither the addition nor the penalty was finally deleted on merits; both appeals were allowed for statistical purposes. Background of the dispute The assessee had not filed a return for Assessment Year 2013–14. The Department received information regarding cash deposits of ₹1,87,71,400 in a savings bank account and salary receipts of ₹9,04,243. The Assessing Officer initiated reassessment proceedings and issued a notice under section 148 on 30 March 2021. The assessee neither filed a return in response nor complied with the assessment notices. Consequently, the Assessing Officer passed an ex parte assessment order on 26 March 2022, under section 147 read with sections 144 and 144B. The entire cash deposits were treated as unexplained money under section 69A. Together with the salary addition, total income was assessed at ₹1,96,75,643. CIT(A) restricted the deposit addition to a cash deficit Before the CIT(A), the assessee furnished submissions and supporting material, which were examined through remand proceedings. The CIT(A) substantially reduced the section 69A addition from the entire deposits of ₹1.87 crore to ₹20,89,050, describing this as the peak negative cash balance on 4 October 2012. The salary addition of ₹9,04,243 was sustained. Before the Tribunal, the controversy was confined to the surviving ₹20,89,050 cash-deficit addition. The Tribunal’s remand therefore did not amount to reopening every component of the assessment. Cash statement showed a different figure The assessee pointed out that his daily cash statement reflected a negative balance of only ₹3,02,965 on 4 October 2012, rather than ₹20,89,050. The Departmental Representative also acknowledged the discrepancy between the CIT(A)’s recorded figure and the amount appearing in the cash statement, while submitting that factual verification was necessary. The Tribunal independently examined the statement and confirmed the mismatch. This was material because the addition rested on the identified peak deficit. The figure adopted for taxation had to be reconciled with the very statement used to support it. Possible duplication of a ₹26 lakh FD entry The assessee further explained that an entry concerning the maturity of a ₹26 lakh fixed deposit, together with withdrawal of the same amount on 18 January 2013, had been inadvertently taken again in the cash statement. According to counsel, the erroneous treatment produced a negative balance of ₹22,27,152 on that date. After correction, the peak negative balance would allegedly be only ₹3,02,965. The Tribunal found that the entry appeared to have been included inadvertently in the manner suggested. However, it did not accept the corrected computation conclusively. It held that the alleged duplication and its effect on the running balance and peak deficit had to be verified against the bank and fixed-deposit records. Family-source explanations required supporting evidence Alternatively, the assessee claimed that some deposits came from cash received from his wife and father-in-law. The Tribunal rejected the father-in-law explanation on the material then available. No confirmation had been furnished, and the circumstances in which he allegedly provided cash for the assessee’s deposits had not been explained. The wife’s alleged contribution required further verification. Her bank statement showed withdrawals but also deposits. It did not establish how much cash remained available after those transactions and household expenditure. The Tribunal therefore required her cash-flow statement, rather than accepting bank withdrawals alone as proof that the money was available and transferred to the assessee. Scope of the remand The Assessing Officer was directed to verify the cash-balance statement, the fixed-deposit entry dated 18 January 2013 and the claim concerning cash received from the wife. The assessee was required to furnish bank statements, fixed-deposit records, a reconciled cash statement, his wife’s cash-flow statement and supporting confirmations or evidence. The Tribunal expressly stated that it expressed no final opinion on the amount, if any, assessable under section 69A. The connected penalty matter was also restored for fresh adjudication in accordance with law, depending on the outcome of the quantum proceedings. Author’s comments A peak calculation is only as reliable as its underlying entries. Duplication of a substantial transaction or adoption of an incorrect balance can distort the result, even where the daily statement appears arithmetically detailed. Equally, an FD maturity credited to a bank account and its subsequent cash withdrawal must be recorded according to their actual movement. The same funds cannot be counted twice. The family-source discussion is practically useful. A relative’s withdrawal establishes withdrawal; it does not automatically establish available cash or its transfer to the assessee. A dated cash flow and transaction-specific confirmation provide the necessary link. The order grants an opportunity to establish the correct position. It does not endorse either the CIT(A)’s ₹20.89 lakh figure or the assessee’s proposed ₹3.03 lakh deficit. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee is an individual, had not filed his return of income under section 139 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (“the Act”) for the A.Y. 2013­14. On the basis of information regarding cash deposits of Rs. 1,87,71,400/- in his savings bank account and salary receipts of Rs. 9,04,243/-, the Assessing Officer (“AO”) initiated proceedings under section 147 of the Act and issued notice under section 148 of the Act dated 30.03.2021. The assessee neither filed any return in response to that notice nor responded to the any of the notices issued by the AO during the assessment proceedings. The AO, therefore, passed an ex parte assessment order dated 26.03.2022 under section 147 read with sections 144 and 144B of the Act by treating the entire cash deposits of Rs. 1,87,71,400/- as unexplained money under section 69A of the Act and making addition on account of salary income of Rs. 9,04,243/-, determining total income of the assessee at Rs. 1,96,75,643/-. 4. Accordingly, the addition is restricted to the extent of Rs.20,89,050/-, being the peak negative cash balance representing unexplained cash deposits, and the balance amount is directed to be deleted. 8. Further, the assessee’s explanation regarding cash allegedly received from his father-in-law cannot be accepted on the material presently available. No confirmation from the father-in-law has been filed, and the circumstances in which he would deposit or provide cash for deposit in the assessee’s bank account have not been explained. Accordingly, the said claim is rejected. Further, the claim concerning cash received from the assessee’s wife also requires verification. Her bank statement, by itself, does not establish how much cash remained available with her after other deposits, withdrawals and household expenditure. The assessee has not furnished her cash flow statement to substantiate the amount allegedly provided to him. 11. In these circumstances, we set aside the addition of Rs. 20,89,050/- sustained under section 69A of the Act and restore this issue to the file of the AO for fresh examination. The AO shall verify the cash balance statement, the entry relating to the fixed deposit on 18.01.2013, and the assessee’s claims concerning cash received from his wife. The assessee is directed to furnish the relevant bank statement and fixed deposit records, a reconciled cash statement, his wife’s cash flow statement, and such confirmation and supporting evidence as he relies upon. The AO shall afford the assessee a reasonable opportunity of being heard and decide the issue afresh in accordance with law. We express no final opinion on the amount, if any, liable to be added under section 69A of the Act. 12. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes.
