NBFCs accounted for 43% of retail loan originations by volume and 30% by value in the quarter ended June 2026, highlighting their growing role in new credit even though banks continue to dominate the overall market.The shift is being driven by segments where NBFCs have traditionally had an advantage in distribution, underwriting and servicing, including gold loans, consumer durable finance, vehicle loans and credit to borrowers outside the prime banking franchise. RBI data show that NBFC retail credit has been growing faster than bank retail credit, with gold loans emerging as the strongest growth category. Advt NBFC retail loans grew 21.4% year-on-year in July, with loans against gold jewellery rising 68.5% and consumer durable finance increasing 51.5%, according to RBI data. NBFCs' gold-loan book reached nearly Rs3.54 lakh crore by the end of July.The growth is not, however, evidence of a broad retreat by banks from retail lending. Bank retail credit continues to expand rapidly, with lenders retaining a dominant share of the market. The more significant change is occurring within the composition of incremental lending, with NBFCs capturing a disproportionate share of originations in products where smaller ticket sizes and specialised distribution models matter more than balance-sheet scale.The difference between NBFCs' share of originations by volume and value is particularly revealing. Their 43% share by volume compared with 30% by value indicates that NBFCs are originating a larger proportion of smaller-ticket loans than banks.This gives non-bank lenders a growing role in parts of the market where traditional bank distribution may be less suited to the economics of small-ticket lending. Merchant-led consumer finance, vehicle finance, gold loans and other specialised products can be originated through focused distribution networks and processed using underwriting models designed for specific borrower segments. Advt Gold lending on riseGold lending has become the clearest example of the change. NBFC gold-loan originations have been gaining share steadily, with TransUnion CIBIL data showing the NBFC share of gold-loan originations rising from 44% in 2022 to 51% in 2025. Their share by value increased from 26% to 38% over the same period.The expansion has been supported by the sharp rise in gold prices, which has increased the collateral available to borrowers and allowed lenders to extend larger loans against jewellery. But the same dynamic also creates a risk: growth in loan values can be driven partly by higher collateral values rather than a comparable increase in underlying borrowing capacity. Advt NBFCs are also gaining ground in consumer durable finance. RBI data show that NBFC lending to the segment grew 51.5% year-on-year in July, while growth in bank lending to consumer durables was considerably weaker. The divergence illustrates how specialised lenders can continue expanding in categories where banks have become more selective about the economics and risk of small-ticket lending.The broader retail-credit market remains strong. TransUnion CIBIL data show that retail credit originations by value increased 20% year-on-year in the quarter ended June 2026, with personal-loan growth supported by higher volumes and smaller ticket sizes. Advt That makes the competitive shift more significant. NBFCs are not necessarily taking business away from banks in absolute terms. Instead, they are expanding faster in selected parts of a growing market and increasing their share of new lending.The strategy also gives larger NBFCs an opportunity to build scale in areas where banks have a structural advantage in funding but may not always have the same specialised distribution infrastructure. The trade-off is that NBFCs generally face a higher cost of funds, making asset selection and pricing critical to sustaining returns as competition increases.Gold loans demonstrate both sides of the equation. Rapid growth has made the segment an increasingly important source of NBFC expansion, but asset-quality indicators are beginning to warrant closer monitoring. Shriram Finance, one of the country's largest retail-focused NBFCs, reported a rise in stress in its gold-loan portfolio in the September quarter, while its overall FY27 growth outlook was subsequently reduced amid higher credit costs and pressure on borrowers in some rural markets.The distinction between growth and quality is becoming increasingly important because the segments attracting NBFCs are also those in which underwriting can deteriorate quickly when lenders chase market share. The RBI has previously flagged rising fresh NPA accretions and increasing write-offs across the NBFC sector even as headline gross NPA ratios remained contained.For banks, the competitive threat is therefore selective rather than systemic. Their scale, low-cost deposits and broad customer relationships continue to give them an advantage across mortgages, personal loans, credit cards and other mainstream retail products. But NBFCs are building stronger positions in pockets where speed, local distribution and specialised underwriting can outweigh the funding advantage enjoyed by banks.The next phase of NBFC growth is consequently likely to be less about replacing banks and more about taking a larger share of incremental credit in specialised retail markets. Gold loans, consumer durable finance, vehicle finance and other smaller-ticket products are providing the clearest openings.For NBFCs, however, the test will be whether faster origination growth can translate into sustainable returns without a corresponding deterioration in credit quality. Their growing share of retail originations shows that they are becoming more important in the flow of new credit. The next test is whether they can retain that share as banks compete more aggressively and regulators remain focused on underwriting standards and consumer-credit risks.
