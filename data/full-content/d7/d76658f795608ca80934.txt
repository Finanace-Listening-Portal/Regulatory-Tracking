Summary: The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), through Press Release No. 75/2026 dated November 23, 2026, has issued an advisory concerning the publication, circulation and dissemination of unregulated Pre-Listing Premium, commonly known as Grey Market Premium (GMP), for upcoming Initial Public Offers (IPOs). SEBI has observed that GMP figures are increasingly circulated through websites, digital platforms, newspapers, social media and certain registered intermediaries, often creating expectations regarding listing prices and investment returns. The advisory emphasises that such figures are unofficial, unverified and derived from transactions conducted outside the recognised stock exchange framework, without regulatory oversight, settlement guarantees, investor protection or formal price-discovery mechanisms. SEBI has advised discontinuation of the publication and dissemination of GMP figures and urged registered intermediaries and market participants to refrain from circulating such information through websites, emails, SMS, social media and other communication channels. Investors have been cautioned against treating GMP as an indicator of listing prices or anticipated returns and advised to avoid GMP-linked transactions and use authorised platforms. The advisory further clarifies that disputes arising from such transactions are not covered by SEBI or stock exchange investor protection benefits, exchange-administered grievance redressal mechanisms or the Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) portal. Registered intermediaries should review their promotional materials, digital communication practices and internal compliance mechanisms to prevent misleading investor communications. The advisory reinforces the importance of transparency, accountability, credible disclosures and informed investment decisions within India’s regulated securities market. The advisory comes against the backdrop of increasing instances of GMP figures being circulated through websites, digital platforms, newspapers and even by certain SEBI-registered intermediaries and market participants. Such figures are frequently projected as indicators of the likely listing price or anticipated returns from an IPO, potentially influencing investment decisions. Through this advisory, SEBI has highlighted the risks associated with reliance on unofficial and unregulated GMP figures and emphasised the importance of maintaining transparency, investor protection and integrity in the securities market. 1. Understanding Grey Market Premium (GMP) Grey Market Premium refers to the unofficial premium at which shares of a company are reportedly traded before their formal listing on a recognised stock exchange. In simple terms, GMP represents the difference between the expected unofficial trading price of an IPO share and its issue price. For instance, if an IPO is offered at an issue price of ₹100 per share and its reported GMP is ₹40, the implied unofficial price would be ₹140. However, it is important to understand that GMP is not an officially recognised indicator of an IPO’s listing price. It is based on unofficial market activity and does not necessarily reflect the actual demand, valuation or listing performance of the securities. 2. SEBI’s Observations Regarding Unregulated GMP SEBI has observed that GMP figures relating to upcoming IPOs are being widely published and circulated across different platforms, including: Websites and digital platforms; Newspapers and other publications; Social media and online communication channels; and Certain SEBI-registered intermediaries and market participants. Such figures are often presented as indicators of anticipated listing gains or expected returns, which may create misleading expectations among investors. SEBI has specifically highlighted that these transactions take place outside the recognised stock exchange framework and are: Unofficial and unverified; Not subject to regulatory oversight; Without any settlement guarantee; Not covered by investor protection mechanisms; and Not governed by any formal price-discovery process. Consequently, reliance on such figures may expose investors to financial risks and undermine transparency and accountability in the securities market. 3. SEBI’s Advisory on Publication and Dissemination of GMP One of the most significant aspects of the advisory is SEBI’s direction concerning the publication and dissemination of unregulated GMP figures. SEBI has stated that the publication, display, circulation or dissemination of unregulated Pre-Listing Premium or Grey Market Premium figures through any mode or platform may be discontinued across the securities market. Further, SEBI has advised all SEBI-registered intermediaries and market participants to refrain from: 1. Publishing GMP figures for upcoming IPOs on their websites or digital platforms; 2. Displaying or broadcasting GMP figures through any communication channel; 3. Circulating such information through emails and SMS; and 4. Disseminating GMP-related information through social media or other communication platforms. 4. Guidance Issued by SEBI for Investors SEBI has also outlined certain important precautions that investors should follow while participating in the securities market, particularly in relation to IPO investments. Investors are advised to: a. Not Treat GMP as an Indicator of Listing Price Investors should not consider GMP figures as a reliable indicator of the actual listing price or expected returns from an IPO. b. Avoid GMP-Linked Transactions Investors should refrain from participating in transactions linked to GMP, as such transactions operate entirely outside the established regulatory framework. c. Use Authorised Platforms IPO-related transactions should be undertaken exclusively through SEBI-registered intermediaries and authorised platforms. These precautions are intended to promote informed investment decisions and discourage investors from relying on speculative market information. 5. Non-Availability of Investor Protection and Dispute Redressal Mechanisms Another important aspect highlighted by SEBI is the absence of regulatory protection in the event of disputes arising from GMP transactions. Since such transactions take place outside the recognised stock exchange framework, investors should be aware that the following remedies will not be available in relation to disputes arising from these transactions: Investor protection benefits under SEBI or the jurisdiction of Stock Exchanges. Investor grievance redressal mechanisms administered by Stock Exchanges. The Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) portal administered by Stock Exchanges and Depositories. Accordingly, investors participating in GMP-linked transactions may have limited avenues for seeking regulatory assistance or resolving disputes. 6. Implications for SEBI-Registered Intermediaries and Market Participants The advisory has significant implications for SEBI-registered intermediaries and other market participants who publish or disseminate IPO-related information. Intermediaries should carefully review their existing communication practices, including their websites, mobile applications, research publications, marketing materials and social media platforms. Particular attention should be given to: Removal or discontinuation of GMP-related content; Review of IPO-related promotional material; Monitoring of digital communication channels; Ensuring that investor communications do not project unofficial GMP figures as indicators of expected listing prices or returns; and Strengthening internal compliance and monitoring mechanisms. The advisory also highlights the responsibility of market participants in ensuring that information disseminated to investors does not create misleading expectations or encourage speculative investment behaviour. 7. Conclusion SEBI’s advisory dated November 23, 2026, represents an important step towards strengthening investor awareness and maintaining the integrity of India’s securities market. Although Grey Market Premium figures have become a widely followed aspect of the IPO ecosystem, their unofficial and unregulated nature makes them an unreliable basis for investment decisions. The advisory reinforces a broader regulatory objective: ensuring that investment decisions are based on credible disclosures, authorised market mechanisms and informed financial analysis rather than speculative information.
