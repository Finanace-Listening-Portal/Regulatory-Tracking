Articles 3 min read AA-enabled lenders’ home and property loan books grow 32.9% in two years: Report According to the latest joint report by CRIF Credit Information Services and Sahamati, lenders that predominantly use the AA framework recorded a 2-year portfolio CAGR of 32.9% in home loans and loans against property as of June 2026, compared with 14.3% for non-AA lenders. AA-enabled lending is estimated at ₹3.82 lakh crore across 3.68 crore loans in FY26. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Published On Oct 15, 2026 at 06:37 PM IST var news_msid = 134968639; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Lenders that predominantly use the Account Aggregator (AA) framework for underwriting grew their home loan and loan against property (LAP) portfolios more than twice as fast as non-AA lenders over two years, according to the report released on 15 October by CRIF Credit Information Services and Sahamati.As of June 2026, the 2-year CAGR in portfolio outstanding was 32.9% for AA-enabled lenders against 14.3% for non-AA lenders, while active loans grew 26.6% against 10.2%. The report said the findings are based on data reported to the CRIF Retail Bureau, with AA lenders defined as those using AA for at least 30% of underwriting in a product. It cautioned that growth differences cannot be attributed solely to AA adoption. Advt Personal and Business LoansIn personal loans, AA lenders posted a 2-year portfolio CAGR of 30.9% against 26.8%, while their active loans rose 7.9% and those of non-AA lenders fell 2.0%. On a year-on-year basis, however, portfolio growth was 46.4% for AA lenders against 57.1%.In business loans of up to ₹10 lakh, data in the report showed AA lenders ahead on 2-year portfolio growth (19.8% vs 16.5%) and year-on-year growth (25.2% vs 10.1%), though active-loan growth was lower. In ₹10-25 lakh business loans, both cohorts grew about 25% over two years, but active loans fell 8.4% for AA lenders and rose 8.6% for non-AA lenders.First-Time Borrower Share RisesFor the 12 months ended June 2026, new-to-credit (NTC) borrowers made up 28.5% of originations in ₹10-25 lakh business loans at AA lenders, against 8.6% at non-AA lenders. In personal loans, the shares were 9.0% and 3.8%, and in business loans up to ₹10 lakh, 16.7% and 16.3%. In home loans and LAP, the share was lower for AA lenders at 19.0% against 23.2%, the report noted.Six-month PAR 31-90 for these borrowers was lower for AA cohorts in business loans and in home loans and LAP (0.04% vs 0.14%), but higher in personal loans at 5.2% against 4.5%, with the report noting that risk levels there are trending upward. Advt Loan-Level Growth and TicketsA separate analysis of loans disbursed between October 2023 and April 2026 by about 10 selected lenders compared AA and non-AA loans. The report said the sample may not capture all AA loans. Sanctioned home loan value grew 8.17x year-on-year for AA-enabled loans against 1.72x for non-AA loans, with volume at 6.16x against 1.59x. Two-wheeler loan value grew 3.82x against 0.32x.Average ticket sizes were higher for AA-enabled personal loans (₹2,67,500 vs ₹1,81,100) and auto loans (₹8,14,270 vs ₹6,84,800), but lower for home loans (₹18,39,600 vs ₹18,70,400). Advt Delinquency Mostly Lower for AAThe report highlighted, as of June 2026, PAR 90+ was lower for AA-enabled loans in two-wheeler loans (0.5% vs 5.3%), auto loans (0.2% vs 0.7%), personal loans (2.0% vs 2.2%), and business loans up to ₹10 lakh (4.1% vs 4.4%). Used car loans were higher at 2.4% against 1.7%, which the report described as slightly elevated risk reflecting asset-specific dynamics.Pincode Study Links ExposureIn pincode-level analysis, described as preliminary research by CAFRAL researchers, greater exposure to AA-participating banks was associated with stronger AA-linked lending in housing, personal and auto loans, and higher lending to NTC, Tier 3 and beyond, and women borrowers. Repayment, measured by 12-month 90+ DPD, showed no significant deterioration in housing and business loans, while AA-tagged auto loans showed a modest relative deterioration that the report said may need monitoring. Advt Higher predicted AA exposure between March 2025 and March 2026 was associated with about 2.11% growth in night-time light intensity at the pincode level and 3.03% at the district level. The report noted this is a proxy for local economic activity and not an estimate of GDP growth.
