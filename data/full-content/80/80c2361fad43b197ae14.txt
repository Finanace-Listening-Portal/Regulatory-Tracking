Articles 3 min read NBFCs must balance growth, governance and technology to build sustainable lending models: NBFC Leaders NBFCs must align lending growth with funding costs, asset quality and risk appetite as regulatory scrutiny and competition intensify, industry leaders said at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026. Technology-led efficiency, digital MSME lending and affordable housing emerged as key opportunities for sustainable expansion. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Updated On Oct 10, 2026 at 04:34 PM IST var news_msid = 134850602; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook India’s NBFCs must move beyond aggressive loan growth and focus on profitability, stronger governance and disciplined risk management to remain competitive, industry leaders said at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026.At a panel discussion NBFC leaders highlighted how technology, changing customer expectations and regulatory requirements are reshaping lending models across microfinance, affordable housing and MSME financing.Lending models must deliver sustainable returnsAnuj Pandey, CEO, UGRO Capital, said smaller NBFCs must identify underserved borrower segments where they can generate adequate returns, particularly as larger lenders benefit from lower funding costs and greater leverage. Advt Smaller NBFCs may need returns on assets of around 3–3.5% to attract capital for expansion, compared with approximately 2% for larger, parent-backed lenders, Pandey said. However, pursuing higher yields must not come at the cost of excessive credit risk.Deo Shankar Tripathi, Executive Vice Chairman, Aadhar Housing Finance, said lenders should build their businesses around their borrowing costs, target customers and risk appetite rather than compete with established players in segments where they lack scale.“Regulation always sets the right path for doing the right business. Regulation is always supportive. Regulation is never an obstruction,” Tripathi said, highlighting the importance of liquidity safeguards, asset-liability management and fair lending practices.Jayaraman Balakrishnan, Whole-Time Director & CEO, Belstar Microfinance, said rising compliance requirements could create operational challenges for microfinance institutions, particularly where requirements do not fully reflect the characteristics of small-ticket lending.Technology can improve efficiency and credit assessmentAnooj Mishra, Business Transformation Leader – Applications, Oracle India, said the next phase of NBFC growth would depend less on capital mobilisation and disbursement speed and more on smarter customer acquisition, risk management and productivity.He identified fragmented technology systems as a key challenge, with loan origination, loan management, collections and regulatory reporting often operating on separate platforms. Advt “The challenge is not the availability of data. It is the fragmented data which is not getting translated into crucial, critical intelligence for the organisation,” Mishra said, advocating integrated systems or a common data layer to improve analytics and AI adoption.Balakrishnan said automated underwriting, credit scoring, early-warning systems and data analytics could strengthen microfinance operations. However, human engagement would remain important for customer relationships and collections. Belstar Microfinance conducts around 60% of its collections digitally, while the remaining 40% require more intensive engagement, including field visits. Advt Ritesh Jain, Co-Founder, FlexiLoans, said digital verification and alternative data could help lenders expand access to MSME credit. FlexiLoans' adoption of video-based KYC had not led to a sustained decline in approval rates, he said.UPI transactions, GST records and other digital information could help lenders assess business cash flows and repayment capacity, particularly for smaller enterprises with limited conventional collateral. Jain also said around 60% of FlexiLoans' loans were in Tier-II, Tier-III and Tier-IV towns, where differentiated distribution and technology-led cost efficiency could support growth. Advt Affordable housing and MSME lending offer opportunitiesTripathi said affordable housing demand remained significant beyond major cities, particularly in smaller towns where self-construction on owned or purchased land is common.He said loans below ₹15 lakh accounted for around 40% of Aadhar Housing Finance's portfolio, down from approximately 65% in 2022, reflecting rising construction costs and changing ticket sizes. Uttar Pradesh was the company's largest market by loan count, while Maharashtra led by loan value.Jain highlighted the potential for digital lending to improve access to finance for smaller businesses by using transaction data to assess repayment capacity and reduce dependence on collateral.Pandey said consolidation could create opportunities for lenders with differentiated business models and sustainable returns, while institutions unable to manage credit costs or maintain profitability could face pressure to merge or exit.The leaders said NBFC growth will depend on combining disciplined underwriting and resilient funding structures with technology that improves operating efficiency and customer understanding. Lenders will need to expand without compromising credit quality, governance or customer trust.
