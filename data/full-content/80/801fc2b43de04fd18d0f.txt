Summary: SBI Nifty Midcap 150 Index Fund is an open-ended passive equity index scheme that seeks returns closely corresponding to the Nifty Midcap 150 Total Return Index, subject to tracking error, without any guarantee of achieving the investment objective. Its stated inception date is 3 October 2022. SBI Mutual Fund’s official factsheet dated 31 August 2026 reported scheme AUM of ₹1,293.91 crore, monthly average AUM of ₹1,262.00 crore, Regular-Growth NAV of ₹20.1258 and Direct-Growth NAV of ₹20.5275. The same dated factsheet disclosed a Very High scheme Riskometer, total expense ratios of 0.88% for Regular Plan and 0.46% for Direct Plan, and one-year tracking errors of 0.0491% and 0.0433%, respectively. The stated minimum initial purchase was ₹5,000, with ₹1,000 additional purchases, and the disclosed exit load was 0.25% on redemption on or before 30 days from allotment, nil thereafter. Its August portfolio included BSE Ltd, Federal Bank, MCX and other index constituents. For tax year 2026-27, qualifying equity-oriented mutual-fund redemptions are generally subject to 20% short-term capital-gains tax or 12.5% long-term tax on eligible aggregate gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh, subject to the statutory holding period, STT, surcharge and cess rules. The figures in this article are dated August 2026 rather than presented as live October quotations; investors and publishers must check subsequent AMC disclosures for changes. Its stated objective is to provide returns that closely correspond to the total returns of securities represented by the underlying index, subject to tracking error. The benchmark is Nifty Midcap 150 TRI, which includes dividends on the index constituents rather than only changes in share prices. The scheme does not promise to beat the index or to deliver positive returns over a specified holding period. The Nifty Midcap 150 index represents a rules-based segment of listed Indian mid-cap companies. Index constituents and weights change with index-provider rebalancing and corporate actions. The scheme seeks to replicate the relevant index holdings rather than selecting companies primarily through a discretionary stock-picking process. The fund manager still makes operational decisions on cash flows, rebalancing, corporate actions and execution, so exact index matching is not possible. The portfolio may have small cash or cash-equivalent exposures arising from subscriptions, redemptions, dividends, expenses and pending trades. Index rebalancing, security suspensions, corporate actions, market liquidity, taxes and transaction costs can all create a difference between scheme returns and index returns. Tracking error is a measure of variability of the difference between a fund’s returns and the benchmark over an observation period. Tracking difference measures the realised gap between the scheme return and the benchmark return over a stated horizon. A small tracking error does not mean that the fund cannot lose money: the benchmark itself can fall substantially. Nor does it mean that a fund’s return is guaranteed to equal the index. These metrics should be read with the methodology and observation date. August 2026 tracking metrics The official 31 August 2026 factsheet reported one-year tracking error of 0.0491% for Regular Plan and 0.0433% for Direct Plan. The one-year tracking difference in the ready-reckoner was approximately minus 0.81 percentage points for Regular Plan and minus 0.36 percentage points for Direct Plan. The numbers have different meanings: the first is variability of tracking deviations, while the second is the realised return gap. The scheme’s TER is an important contributor to tracking difference, but cash drag, timing and trading costs can also matter. The portfolio is exposed to mid-cap market risk, sector cycles and liquidity conditions. A 150-company index can diversify single-company exposure but cannot eliminate market-wide losses or sector concentration. Corporate actions, index reconstitution and changes in free-float market capitalisation can alter weights. Therefore, a portfolio table dated 31 August 2026 should not be treated as the composition on 14 October 2026 unless a later official AMC disclosure confirms it. Regular and Direct plans participate in the same underlying portfolio but have different expense structures and therefore different NAV histories. Purchase, SIP and redemption terms The August factsheet states a minimum initial investment of ₹5,000 in multiples of ₹1 and an additional purchase minimum of ₹1,000. It describes a monthly SIP starting at ₹1,000 for at least six months or ₹500 for at least one year, with other permitted frequencies and conditions. The exit load disclosed is 0.25% if units are redeemed on or before 30 days from allotment and nil thereafter. Each SIP instalment has its own allotment date for determining the load period; exit load is distinct from capital-gains tax and STT. The scheme is operated within the mutual-fund structure of an asset management company, trustees, custodian and registrar, with mandatory disclosures including SID, KIM, portfolio, NAV, TER and Riskometer. SEBI regulation does not constitute a guarantee of principal or any specific rate of return. The AMC’s SID and KIM are the legal product documents for investment strategy, permissible assets, investment restrictions, purchase/redemption, risk factors and charges. AMFI is an industry association and publishes relevant scheme and NAV data; it is not a guarantor of the fund. Where the AMC’s dated factsheet and a more recent SID/addendum differ on an operational term, the later applicable disclosure must be checked before acting. Income-tax Treatment for Tax Year 2026-27 For an equity-oriented mutual fund satisfying the statutory definition and securities transaction tax conditions, units held for not more than 12 months generally give rise to short-term capital gains on redemption, taxed at the special 20% basic rate. Qualifying long-term gains after more than 12 months are generally subject to 12.5% basic tax on aggregate eligible equity gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh in the tax year, subject to applicable conditions. Surcharge and health and education cess can apply. The threshold is not a separate ₹1.25 lakh exemption for each mutual-fund scheme or each redemption. An investor must distinguish redemption proceeds, cost of acquisition, eligible grandfathering for old holdings where relevant, exit load and securities transaction tax. Switching from Regular to Direct Plan or from one scheme to another is ordinarily treated as redemption and purchase for tax purposes, even when cash is not paid to the bank account. SIP instalments are separate acquisition lots. IDCW distributions are not tax-free merely because the scheme has equity exposure; distributions are taxable under the applicable provisions and withholding rules. Capital losses, NRI withholding and return reporting Eligible short-term capital losses can generally be set off against short-term or long-term capital gains; eligible long-term capital losses can generally be set off only against long-term capital gains. Carry-forward depends on the statutory return-filing conditions and prescribed period. NRI investors may face withholding on redemption or distribution under non-resident rules, and their final tax can differ from the amount withheld. Treaty relief, if claimed, requires appropriate residency and beneficial-entitlement documentation. Investment through NRE/NRO accounts and repatriation is separately governed by FEMA. For the current tax framework, see TaxGuru’s verified capital-gains overview under the Income-tax Act, 2025; individual transactions must be tested against the enacted law and the current return form. Accounting and Audit Implications for Business Investors A corporate investor applying Ind AS generally evaluates mutual-fund units under Ind AS 109, with recognition, subsequent measurement and fair-value changes determined by the instrument’s contractual characteristics and the relevant classification rules. Fair value through profit or loss is common for fund units, but a classification conclusion must follow the applicable standard and facts. Entities applying non-Ind AS accounting should assess AS 13 and related presentation requirements. This article does not prescribe one accounting classification for all investors. Auditors should reconcile unit statements to the registrar, bank payments and redemptions, NAV used for measurement, realised gains, TDS and STT, and the related-party/financial-instrument disclosures where relevant. Where investments are pledged, subject to lien, held through nominees or included in treasury portfolios, additional control and disclosure questions arise. The fund’s underlying holdings should not be confused with securities directly owned by the investor.
