Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); ITO Vs Lalit Kumar (ITAT Jaipur) Cash Deposits in Employer’s Bank Account Cannot Be Taxed U/s 69A in Employee’s Hands Merely Because They Were Reported Against His PAN The Jaipur Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held that cash deposits in a co-operative society’s bank account could not be assessed as unexplained money in the hands of its employee merely because the reporting system picked up his PAN. The employee was an authorised signatory, and the Assessing Officer himself confirmed during remand that the current account belonged to the society. The Tribunal upheld the deletion of the entire ₹2,58,02,460 addition for Assessment Year 2017–18. Case reference — Income Tax Officer, Ward-1, Beawar v. Lalit Kumar, ITA No. 1509/JPR/2025 and CO No. 56/JPR/2025, Jaipur ITAT, order dated 18 November 2026. How the Cash Deposits Came to Be Assessed Lalit Kumar, a salaried employee of Adarsh Credit Co-operative Society Ltd., had not filed his return of income for the relevant assessment year. Information extracted from the Non-Filers Monitoring System on the Income Tax Business Application portal showed cash deposits of ₹2,58,02,460 against his PAN. The data also reflected salary of ₹1,17,126, interest income of ₹12,711 and withdrawals of ₹87,40,000. The Assessing Officer initiated reassessment proceedings under sections 147 and 148 and sought information under section 142(1). The assessment was completed under section 147 read with sections 144 and 144B. Treating the reported cash deposits as belonging to Lalit Kumar, the Assessing Officer added ₹2,58,02,460 as unexplained money under section 69A, read with section 115BBE. Bank Statement and Board Resolution Established Account Ownership During the first appellate proceedings, the assessee produced the SBI current account statement and the society’s board resolution. The Assessing Officer examined these documents during remand and confirmed that the account belonged to Adarsh Credit Co-operative Society Ltd. The resolution established that Lalit Kumar was one of the persons authorised to operate the account in his capacity as an employee. The Assessing Officer’s remand report expressly accepted that the deposits in the account could not be considered in the employee’s hands. Relying on that finding, the CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, deleted the entire addition. The Revenue challenged the deletion before the Tribunal. Revenue Questioned the Source and Beneficiary of the Deposits The Revenue argued that the remand report established only the nominal ownership of the account. According to the Department, the source, character and actual beneficiary of the deposits still required verification. It also argued that the transactions appeared against the employee’s PAN and that the CIT(A) had given insufficient weight to the assessment findings concerning the absence of supporting evidence at that stage. The Tribunal upheld the CIT(A)’s order. It found that the cash had been deposited in the society’s current account and that the assessee’s connection with that account arose from his authority to operate it. The Tribunal attributed the PAN linkage to a system error and recorded that the society received cash in the course of transactions with its members and deposited it in its own account. It found no perversity or illegality in the deletion and dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. Reopening and Alleged Double Counting Left Undecided The assessee’s cross-objection challenged the reopening on the ground that the Assessing Officer had relied on NMS and SFT information without independently verifying account ownership. He also contended that the original and corrected SFT filings had been combined, resulting in the same amount of ₹1,29,01,230 being counted twice. After the Tribunal upheld the deletion on merits, the assessee did not press the cross-objection. It was dismissed as withdrawn. The Tribunal therefore left both the validity of the reopening and the alleged duplication undecided. The deletion rested on the evidence showing whose account received the deposits and in what capacity the employee operated it. Author’s Comments Ownership of the money is central to section 69A. A transaction reported against a PAN gives the Assessing Officer a basis to enquire. Where the bank statement identifies the employer as the account holder and the board resolution establishes the employee’s signing authority, any allegation that the money actually belonged to the employee requires evidence connecting him personally to the funds. In this case, the Assessing Officer’s own enquiry confirmed the employer’s ownership of the account and the employee’s limited role. The distinction matters for employees, office-bearers and other persons authorised to operate institutional accounts. A person may sign deposit slips, issue cheques or operate a current account on behalf of an institution. Those acts are consistent with the authority conferred by the institution. If a cash deposit appears against that person’s PAN, the bank statement, account mandate and authorising resolution can establish whose account was operated and explain why the individual’s particulars appear in the records. The decision also shows why the remand report deserves close attention in an appeal. Here, the Assessing Officer examined the very documents that were unavailable at the assessment stage and accepted the factual position on which the deletion ultimately turned. Once that verification established the employee’s role, the PAN entry alone could not sustain the addition. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT JAIPUR The Appellant- Income Tax Officer by filing aforesaid appeal, sought to set aside the impugned order dated 27.08.2025 passed by the National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [hereinafter referred to as the ‘CIT(A)’] qua assessment year 2017-18 on the grounds inter- alia that:- ITA No. 1509/JPR/2025 (Revenue’s grounds) “1. Whether on the facts & the cirmcumtances of the case and in the law the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in deleting the addition of Rs. 2,58,02,460/- made under section 69A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, ignoring that the transactions giving rise to such addition were duly reflected against the PAN of the assesse and their nature and source remained unverified and unexplained? 2. Whether on the facts & the circumtances of the case and in the law, the Ld. CIT (A) has erred in relying solely on the remand report wherein the Assessing officer stated that the bank account No. 35316716971 belonged to Adarsh Credit Co-operative Society Ltd, without appreciating that such observation only established nominal ownership of the account, but not the true source, character, or beneficiary of the impugned cash deposit. true 3. Whether on the facts & the circumtances of the case and in the law, the Ld.CIT(A) has erred in not appreciating that the Assessing officer in the assessment order dated 29.03.2022 had given a detailed finding on the unexplained nature of cash deposits, including the failure of the assesse to produce any credible evidence regarding the ownership of funds or linkage with the said society, and hence the deletion of the entire addition was unjustified. 4. Whether on the facts & the circumtances of the case and in the law, the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in holding that the addition amounting to Rs. 2,58,02,460/- is not sustainable merely because that the impunged bank account does not stand in the name of the assesse, over looking the fact that the information regarding cash deposits and other transactions were directly reported under the assessee’s PAN, thereby attracting the statutory presumption? 5. The Revenue craves leave to add, amend, modify, or withdraw any of the above grounds of appeal at the time of hearing.” 2. The appellant, Cross Objector Lalit Kumar by filing Cross Objection also challenge the impugned order passed by the Ld. CIT(A) on the grounds inter-alia that:-. ITA No. 56/JPR/2025 (Assessee’s ground) “1. The Ld. AO, NFAC has erred on facts and in law, in initiating the assessment proceedings U/s 147 solely on the basis of NMS/SFT information, without conducting any independent inquiry, without verifying the correctness or ownership of the bank account reported therein, and without establishing any nexus between such information and any income alleged to have escaped assessment. Moreover, failure to provide the assessee with the information and reasons recorded forming the basis for opening an assessment U/s 147 of the Income Tax Act renders the reassessment proceedings invalid and void ab initio. 2. The Ld. AO, NFAC has erred both on facts and in law in completing the assessment U/s 147 r.w.s. 144 by completely disregarding the assessee’s replies, sworn affidavits, RTI correspondence, documentary evidence and explanations submitted during the proceedings which unequivocally established that the impugned bank account and the related SFT reporting did not pertain to the assessee. 3. The addition of Rs. 2,58,02,460 U/s 69A is wholly unsustainable as the impugned SBI Current A/c No. 35316716971 does not belong to the assessee but to Adarsh Credit Co-operative Society Ltd. and even the Ld. AO, in the Remand Report, has categorically admitted it. The Ld. CIT(A) has therefore rightly deleted the addition, and no part of it can survive. 4. The Ld. AO, NFAC wrongly considered cash deposits Rs. 1,29,01,230 twice (i.e. Rs. 2,58,02,460) by combining original and correction SFT filings. Such mechanical, non-application of mind vitiates the entire assessment. 5. The respondent craves to alter, amend and modify any ground Rs. 0 of cross-objection.” 3. Briefly stated, facts necessary for consideration and adjudication of the issues at hand are : Assessee has not filed return of income for the year under consideration. On the basis of information extracted from multiyear NMS data on the AIMS module of ITBA portal that during the year under consideration the assessee found to have entered into transactions viz deposited cash of Rs. 2,58,02,460/-, the assessee has received salary of Rs. 1,17,126/-, the assessee has earned interest income of Rs. 12,711/- and withdrawal of Rs. 87,40,000/-. Consequently reopening proceedings were initiated u/s 147 and 148 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as the “Act”) and the assessee was called upon to furnish necessary information by giving a notice u/s 142(1) of the Act. On failure of the assessee to furnish the necessary information, the AO proceeded to make addition of Rs. 2,58,02,460/- as unexplained money u/s 69A r.w.s. 115BBE of the Act and thereby framed the assessment u/s 147 r.w.s. 144 r.w.s. 144B of the Act. 4. Assessee carried the matter before the Ld. CIT(A) by way of filing appeal, who has partly allowed the appeal. Being aggrieved with the impugned order passed by the Ld. CIT(A), Revenue has come up before the Tribunal by way of filing the present appeal. 5. We have heard Ld. ARs for the assessee and Ld. DR for the Revenue and perused the record available on file. 6. Undisputedly, the assessee being a salaried employee of Adarsh Credit Co-operative Society Ltd. has not filed any return of income for the year under consideration. It is also not in dispute that the Adarsh Credit Cooperative Society Limited is managing its current account No. 35316716971 with State Bank of India and the assessee being its employee was authorized signatory to the said account. It is also not in dispute that the entire cash amount of Rs. 2,58,02,460/- was deposited in the assessee’s account. 7. In the backdrop of the aforesaid undisputed fact, the Ld. CIT(A) has deleted the addition on merits made by the AO u/s 69 of the Act to the tune of Rs. 2,58,02,460/-, by returning following findings as under:- “4.5 On merits of the additions, the AO has in the remand report stated that “In view of the above, during the course of assessment proceedings the assessee could not produce the documentary evidence to prove that the bank account No. 35316716971 not belongs to him whereas as per the information available on record the bank account No. 35316716971 belongs to assessee. In absence of documentary evidence the Bank account no. 35316716971 was considered to belong to assessee i.e. Shri Lali Kumar and accordingly the addition was made. During the course of Remand Report proceedings as well as documents produce before the Ld. CIT (A) i.e. statement of Bank account No. 35316716971 and Board Resolution of Adarsh Credit Co. Operative Society Ltd., it is established that the Bank account No 35316716971 belong to Adarsh Credit Co. Operative Society, and not to the assessee. The assessee Shri Lalit Kumar is only a signatory authority alongwith with other three officials of bank. Hence, after carefully examination of the documents produced by the assessee during the course of Remand report proceedings and before the Ld. CIT (A), it is emerged that the Bank account No. 35316716971 belong to Adarsh Credit Co. Operative Society and the assessee is only acted as a signatory authority. Hence, the deposit in the said bank account could not be considered in the hand of the assessee.” 4.6 Thus, the AO has accepted the appellant’s contention that the bank account in which the impugned deposits were made do not belong to the appellant and hence, the additions of Rs. 2,58,02,460/- is not sustainable. Accordingly, the same is deleted. AO is directed to delete the same. Ground no. 6, 7, 8, 10 and 11 of the appeal are allowed.” 8. During remand proceedings when the assessee is proved to be employee of the Adarsh Credit Cooperative Society Ltd. in whose account No. 35316716971 cash amount of Rs. 2,58,02,460/- was deposited, and it is fault of the system that while dealing with the said account of the society, assessee’s PAN was picked up and addition has been made in the name of the assessee. Adarsh Credit Cooperative Society limited being situated in the rural areas and is transiting with its member was getting the cash, which was deposited in the account of the society. The Assessing during the remand proceedings established that the assessee Lalit Kumar was mere authorized signatory to run the bank account in question and as such said deposit in bank account could not be considered in the hands of the assessee. In these circumstances, findings no perversity or illegality in the deletion of addition of Rs. 2,58,02,460/- by the Ld. CIT(A), the present appeal filed by the Revenue is dismissed. 9. In view of the aforesaid findings the Cross Objector has not pressed his Cross Objection, hence dismissed as withdrawn. 10. Resultantly, the appeals filed by the Revenue and Cross Objection are stands dismissed. Order pronounced in the open court on 18-11-2026.
