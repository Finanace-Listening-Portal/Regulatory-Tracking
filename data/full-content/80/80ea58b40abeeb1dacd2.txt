Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Commissioner State Tax (Through Krishna Kumar Mishra) Vs Jindal Hardware (GSTAT Agra) Summary: The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), State Bench Agra, comprising Ajeet Singh, Judicial Member, and Vivek Kumar, Technical Member, upheld the deletion of a GST demand of Rs.1,91,781 raised against Jindal Hardware on account of an alleged mismatch between input tax credit (ITC) claimed in GSTR-3B and the credit reflected in GSTR-2A for FY 2022-23. The Tribunal accepted the findings of the First Appellate Authority that the discrepancy was attributable to unclaimed ITC pertaining to FY 2021-22, which had subsequently been availed in September 2022. During scrutiny of returns for FY 2022-23, the Assistant Commissioner, State Tax, Sector-8, Agra, noticed that the taxpayer had claimed ITC in GSTR-3B in excess of the amount reflected in GSTR-2A. A notice seeking reversal of the allegedly excess ITC was issued. Since the taxpayer did not respond, the proper officer passed an order under Section 73 reversing IGST credit of Rs.1,03,936.99, SGST credit of Rs.23,922.10 and CGST credit of Rs.23,922.10. Penalties of Rs.20,000, Rs.10,000 and Rs.10,000, respectively, were also imposed, resulting in an aggregate demand of Rs.1,91,781. The taxpayer challenged the demand before the Additional Commissioner (Appeals), Agra, who allowed the appeal through an order dated 2 August 2025 and set aside the demand. Aggrieved by this decision, the Department preferred a second appeal before the Tribunal, contending that the taxpayer had availed ITC which was not available as per GSTR-2A and that the First Appellate Authority had erroneously allowed the claim. The taxpayer explained that ITC pertaining to FY 2021-22, which had remained unclaimed during that financial year, was subsequently availed during FY 2022-23. It was submitted that while adjudicating the appeal for FY 2021-22, the appellate authority had already undertaken a detailed scrutiny of GSTR-2A and GSTR-3B for the period from FY 2017-18 to FY 2022-23. The taxpayer had clarified during those proceedings that ITC amounting to Rs.1,65,550 pertaining to FY 2021-22 had been availed in September 2022. The explanation had been accepted by the appellate authority while allowing the appeal for FY 2021-22. The taxpayer also raised a preliminary objection regarding maintainability of the departmental appeal, arguing that the disputed tax amount was below the monetary threshold of Rs.20 lakh prescribed for filing appeals before the GST Appellate Tribunal. Reliance was placed on the State Tax Commissioner’s circular dated 5 July 2024 and CBIC Circular No.207/1/2024-GST dated 26 June 2024. The Tribunal examined Clause 4 of the State Tax Commissioner’s circular, which prescribed exceptions to the monetary limits applicable to departmental appeals. In particular, Clause 4(vi) permitted appeals irrespective of the monetary threshold where, in the Commissioner’s opinion, contesting the matter was necessary in the interest of justice or revenue. The Tribunal observed that where an appeal was filed on the basis of such an opinion, the Commissioner was not required to state the grounds or reasons for forming that opinion. Consequently, the taxpayer’s preliminary objection regarding maintainability was rejected. On merits, the Tribunal found that the only objection raised by the proper officer concerned the difference between ITC claimed in GSTR-3B and the amount reflected in GSTR-2A. The taxpayer had satisfactorily explained before the First Appellate Authority that the difference arose from ITC pertaining to FY 2021-22 which had been claimed in the GSTR-3B return for September 2022. The First Appellate Authority had accepted the claim after detailed examination of the returns for both FY 2021-22 and FY 2022-23. The Tribunal observed that the Department had failed to establish any inconsistency in these findings or produce any material warranting interference with the appellate order. Accordingly, although the preliminary objection concerning the monetary threshold was rejected, the departmental appeal failed on merits. The Tribunal affirmed the order of the First Appellate Authority setting aside the GST demand and dismissed the Department’s appeal without any order as to costs. The decision recognises the importance of financial-year-wise reconciliation of ITC before treating differences between GSTR-2A and GSTR-3B as excess credit, particularly where the discrepancy arises from eligible credit pertaining to an earlier financial year. FULL TEXT OF THE GST APPELLATE TRIBUNAL ORDER 1. The present appeal has been preferred by the Commissioner, State Tax, through Shri Krishna Kumar Mishra, Assistant Commissioner, State Tax, Sector-8, Agra, against order dated 02.08.2025 passed by Shri Paras Nath Yadav, Additional Commissioner, Grade-II (Appeal-II) State Tax Agra, in Appeal No. 2425AAGR001338/2025 ARN No. AD0901250661828/2025, whereby by allowing first appeal learned first appellate authority set aside the O.I.O and consequential demand on respondent herein. For the sake of convenience, in this order, the appellant shall hereinafter be referred to as the “Department” and the respondent, M/s Jindal Hardware, Belan Ganj Agra, shall be referred to as the “Taxpayer”. Brief Facts of the case- 2. Proper Officer Assistant Commissioner sector-8, Agra while scrutinizing the returns filed by the tax payer for the financial year 2022-23 found that the taxpayer has claimed excess ITC in return 3B than what is available in return 2A. Hence, a notice for ITC reversal was issued to the taxpayer by the proper officer. 3. As the taxpayer did not respond to the notice, the proper officer passed an order under section 73 and reversed ITC of Rs. 103936.99 of IGST, Rs. 23922.10 of SGST and Rs. 23922.10 of CGST and imposed penalty of Rs. 20000, Rs. 10000 and Rs. 10000 respectively, thus creating a demand of Rs. 191781. 4. Being aggrieved by the said order of the proper officer, the taxpayer filed an appeal before the Additional Commissioner Appeal, Agra. Additional Commissioner Appeal, Agra vide his impugned order dated 02/08/2025 allowed the appeal filed by the taxpayer and set aside the demand created by the adjudicating authority. 5. Being aggrieved by the said order passed by appellate authority, department has filed this second appeal before the Tribunal. 6. Shri Mukesh Kumar, learned departmental representative, appearing for Revenue, reiterated the grounds of appeal and submitted that the impugned order passed by the First Appellate Authority suffers from errors of fact and law. 7. He further stated that the taxpayer availed ITC which was not available to him as per his GSTR-2A but the first appellate authority without any basis allowed the appeal filed by the taxpayer. 8. Shri Amit Singhal, Learned advocate, appearing for the tax payer supported the order passed by the first appellate authority stating that the ITC claimed is as per Law. He said that ITC which could not have claimed in the year 21-22 has been claimed in the corresponding year 2022-23 which is permissible as per rules. 9. He further pointed out that during the appellate order of the year 2021-22, the appellate authority had done a detailed scrutiny of GSTR-2A and 3B from the year 2017-18 to the year 2022-23 and it was clarified by the taxpayer that the ITC of the year 21-22 of Rs. 165550 was taken in September 2022 in which ITC of Rs. 107734 of IGST and Rs. 28907 of SGST and Rs. 28907 of CGST were taken. The first appellate authority accepted this statement and the appeal for the year 2021-22 was allowed. 10. The taxpayer has also questioned the admissibility of the appeal, stating that the amount of tax in dispute in this case is less than Rs. 20 lakhs, and therefore, as per the Commissioner’s own circular dated 05-07-2024 and CBIC’s Circular No. 207/1/2024-GST dated 26 June 2024, the appeal filed by the appellant is liable to be rejected as not admissible. 11. We have heard the learned authorized representatives of respective parties, perused the material on record and perused the relevant provisions of the law applicable to the facts of the case in hand. 12. As far as the question of admissibility of appeal on the basis of monetary threshold limit mentioned in the circular dated 05-07-2024 is concerned, the exceptional circumstances in which this monetary limit will not be applicable are mentioned in Paragraph 4 of this circular itself. Clause 4 of the said circular is as follows- 4- EXCLUSIONS Monetary limits specified above for filing appeal or application by the department before GSTAT or High Court and for filing Special Leave Petition or appeal before the Supreme Court shall be applicable in all cases, except in the following circumstances where the decision to file appeal shall be taken on merits irrespective of the said monetary limits: i. Where any provision of the CGST Act or UPGST Act or IGST Act or GST (Compensation to States) Act has been held to be ultra vires to the Constitution of India; or ii. Where any Rules or regulations made under CGST Act or UPGST Act or IGST Act or GST (Compensation to States) Act have been held to be ultra vires the parent Act; or iii. Where any order, notification, instruction, or circular issued by the Government or the Board has been held to be ultra vires of the CGST Act or UPGST Act or IGST Act or GST (Compensation to States) Act or the Rules made there under; or iv. Where the matter is related to – a) Valuation of goods or services; or b) Classification of goods or services; or c) Refunds; or d) Place of Supply; or e) Any other issue, which is recurring in nature and/or involves interpretation of the provision of the Act /the Rules/ notification/circular/order/instruction etc; or v) Where structures/adverse comments have been passed and/or cost has been imposed against the Government/Department or their officers; or vi) Any other case or class of cases, where in the opinion of the Commissioner, it is necessary to contest in the interest of justice or revenue. 13. So, the subclause vi of clause 4 of the said circular permits to file the appeal in the cases where in the opinion of the Commissioner, it is necessary to contest in the interest of justice or revenue. In cases where the appeal is filed on the basis of the opinion of the Commissioner, same cannot be challenged and the Commissioner is not required to state the grounds or reasons for his opinion. Hence the objection raised by the respondent on the maintainability of the present appeal is not sustainable and rejected accordingly. 14. Now we come to the merit of the case. In the present case in hand the only objection raised by the proper officer is that the taxpayer has claimed excess ITC in his GSTR-3B as compared to the ITC available in GSTR-2A. Taxpayer has explained this objection before the first appellate authority and said that he has claimed some unclaimed ITC of the year 2021-22 in the return GSTR-3B of September 2022. After detailed examination in the year 21-22 and 22-23, first appellate authority has accepted the ITC claimed by the taxpayer. 15. The Department has failed to establish any inconsistency in the conclusions reached by the First Appellate Authority. The Department has not presented any facts in the grounds of appeal or during the hearing that warrant any interference with the decision reached by the First Appellate Authority. 16. In the light of the above discussion, we are of the considered opinion, that the departmental appeal does not contain any material fact that would suggest any infirmity in the impugned order. Therefore, the order passed by the first appellate authority is affirmed, and the departmental appeal is dismissed. 17. No order as to cost. 18. No interim application is pending in present appeal. 19. Order Pronounced today on 19-11-2026 in open court. 20. Order be communicated to all concerned, as per rules.
