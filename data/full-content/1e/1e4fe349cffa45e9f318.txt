Summary: Section 16(4) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, restricts the availment of input tax credit (ITC) on invoices and debit notes pertaining to FY 2025-26 beyond 30 November 2026 or the date of furnishing the relevant annual return in Form GSTR-9, whichever is earlier. Consequently, taxpayers filing their annual returns before 30 November may inadvertently lose otherwise eligible input tax credit. The article explains the statutory time limit, the importance of invoice reflection in GSTR-2B under Section 16(2)(aa), and the implications of delayed reporting by suppliers. Through a practical illustration involving IGST credit of ₹2,16,000, the author demonstrates how delayed supplier compliance and premature annual return filing can result in loss of credit. Particular attention is given to businesses dealing with small suppliers, year-end purchases and outstanding debit notes. The article recommends supplier-wise reconciliation of purchase registers with GSTR-2B, written follow-up with defaulting suppliers, timely availment of eligible credit through GSTR-3B and appropriate accounting treatment of unrecoverable credit before furnishing GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C. It also discusses the annual return exemption for taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to ₹2 crore, the reconciliation statement requirement for taxpayers exceeding ₹5 crore and the changes attributed to Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026 and its subsequent corrigendum. The author emphasises that taxpayers should prioritise ITC reconciliation and availment before filing their annual returns to avoid unnecessary financial losses. It is a fair request. Rule 80 gives that date, and nobody wants to be filing on the last evening. The trouble is that s.16(4) does not care about the due date of the annual return. It cuts off input tax credit on the earlier of two events: 30 November following the end of the financial year, or the date the annual return for that year is furnished. A return filed on 10 November is therefore a cut-off on 10 November. In practice most returns are filed after 30 November, so the point rarely bites. But where a client wants to file early, or a supplier is late, it bites hard and without any notice. This article is about that gap, and about the checks I would want done before the return is submitted. What the Law Says About the GST ITC Deadline Section 16(4) of the CGST Act provides that a registered person shall not be entitled to take input tax credit in respect of any invoice or debit note for supply of goods or services or both after the thirtieth day of November following the end of the financial year to which the invoice or debit note pertains, or furnishing of the relevant annual return, whichever is earlier. I read this on the CBIC Tax Information Portal (consolidated CGST Act text). Three Important Conditions for Availing Input Tax Credit Three points are worth keeping in view: 1. The test is the financial year to which the invoice pertains, not the date the credit appears in GSTR-2B. An invoice dated 28/03/2026 belongs to FY 2025-26 even if the supplier reports it months later. 2. “Annual return” here means the GSTR-9 for that year. For a taxpayer with aggregate turnover up to ₹2 crore who is exempt from filing it under Notification 15/2025-CT (17/09/2025), there is no return date to trigger the cut-off, and 30 November alone operates. 3. Credit must also satisfy the other conditions of s.16(2), including reflection of the invoice in GSTR-2B (s.16(2)(aa)). The cut-off is an additional gate, not a replacement. Particulars Detail Supplier invoice date 28/03/2026 Taxable value ₹12,00,000 IGST at 18% ₹2,16,000 Supplier reports it in GSTR-1 December 2026 Appears in our client’s GSTR-2B January 2027 Goods were received in March 2026 and paid for. The invoice pertains to FY 2025-26, so the last date to take the credit was 30/11/2026. By the time it shows in GSTR-2B, the window has closed. ₹2,16,000 of credit is lost unless the supplier’s delay is resolved earlier or the credit is recovered from the supplier commercially. Impact of Filing GSTR-9 Before 30 November 2026 Now change one fact: the client files GSTR-9 on 20/10/2026. From that date the window is shut for every FY 2025-26 invoice not yet claimed, including any the supplier reports in November. That is why I do not like a quick annual return filed “to get it out of the way”. Who Is Exposed to the Risk of Losing GST Input Tax Credit? Situation Why it matters Clients with many small suppliers who file GSTR-1 late Invoices land in GSTR-2B after the cut-off Year-end purchases (February and March) Closest to the cut-off, most often reported late Clients above ₹2 crore who want an early GSTR-9 The return itself ends the window Clients up to ₹2 crore No annual return, so 30 November is the only date Credit notes and debit notes of the year Debit notes pertaining to FY 2025-26 sit under the same cut-off The Routine We Follow Before Filing GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C Match the purchase register with GSTR-2B for the whole year. Not month by month; a single sheet, supplier-wise. List every FY 2025-26 invoice that is in the books but not in GSTR-2B. Write to those suppliers in writing and keep the copies. Claim all eligible credit in the last GSTR-3B that can still carry it. Do not wait for the annual return to “pick it up”. The annual return reports what was claimed; it does not claim anything. Decide what to do with credit that will not make it. Either reverse it in the books and treat it as a cost, or hold it as a recoverable from the supplier. Record the decision. Only then furnish GSTR-9, and GSTR-9C where applicable. GSTR-9C Applicability and Annual Return Due Date Under Rule 80 Under Rule 80(3), a self-certified reconciliation statement in Form GSTR-9C goes with the annual return for registered persons whose aggregate turnover exceeds ₹5 crore. The annual return is due on 31 December following the end of the financial year (Rule 80(1)). What Notification 05/2026-CT Changed for GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C Notification 05/2026-CT, published as G.S.R. 919(E) on 26/10/2026, is the Central Goods and Services Tax (Amendment) Rules, 2026, amending the CGST Rules, 2017. On the CBIC portal I read the amendments to the instructions for filling GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C. They extend the existing relaxations to FY 2025-26 onwards. For GSTR-9, the relaxation now covers tables 5H to 5K, 6B to 6D, 15A to 15D, 15E to 15G, 16A to 16C, and 17 and 18, for FY 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26 onwards. For GSTR-9C, it covers tables 5B to 5F, 5H to 5N and table 14. Corrigendum Dated 18 November 2026 A corrigendum to the notification was issued on 18/11/2026. Anyone preparing a return this season should read the notification together with the corrigendum and not rely on summaries. Closing Thought: Claim Eligible ITC Before Filing the Annual Return The rule is old. What changes each year is the habit around it. My own order of work is credit first, return second. If the books are clean on 30 November, the date of the annual return hardly matters. If they are not, an early return only makes a bad position final. For FY 2025-26 the date is 30/11/2026. For FY 2026-27 it will be 30/11/2027, and the same sheet will be needed. References Section 16(4), Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (CBIC Tax Information Portal).
