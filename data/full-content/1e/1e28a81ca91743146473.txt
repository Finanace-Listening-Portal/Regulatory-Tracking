Summary : Invesco India Mid Cap Fund is an open-ended equity scheme that predominantly invests in mid-cap companies. Official scheme material identifies an allotment date of 19 April 2007, benchmark BSE 150 Midcap Total Return Index and a Very High Riskometer. The scheme page’s 30 September 2026 snapshot reported equity exposure of 98.74%, cash and cash equivalents of 1.26% and AUM of ₹15,779.55 crore. Those figures are dated portfolio disclosures, not live values or future allocation promises. The August 2026 factsheet reported base expense ratios of 1.44% for Regular and 0.47% for Direct, while current TER must be checked because it can change. The exit-load rule permits up to 10% of units allotted to be redeemed without load within one year; redemption above that limit within one year attracts 1%, and redemption after one year has nil load under the current disclosure. Normal redemption proceeds are stated to be dispatched within three business days, subject to applicable rules and exceptional circumstances. A mid-cap mandate carries material market, liquidity, valuation, concentration and manager risks. Qualifying equity-oriented-fund gains are generally taxed by holding period, while IDCW is separately taxable. This article is an official-source scheme guide, not a recommendation, ranking or return forecast. Direct and Regular are not different underlying schemes Direct and Regular plans invest in the same underlying portfolio. The Regular plan can include distribution expenses, while the Direct plan does not include distributor commission. Their expense ratios and NAVs therefore differ. Growth and IDCW choices also create different cash-flow and tax outcomes, but a plan or option label should not be counted as a separate fund for duplicate editorial coverage. September 2026 Portfolio and AUM Snapshot The official scheme page’s 30 September 2026 snapshot reported equity exposure of 98.74% and cash and cash equivalents of 1.26%, with AUM of ₹15,779.55 crore. It listed Prestige Estates Projects, Max Healthcare Institute, Meesho, The Federal Bank and Manipal Health Enterprises among the larger positions at that date. These are historical month-end facts. Holdings, weights, cash and AUM can change with market movements, subscriptions, redemptions and manager decisions. A dated holding is not an endorsement Portfolio disclosure lets an investor assess concentration, sector exposure and liquidity, but it does not convert a holding into a recommendation. Mid-cap companies can have narrower trading depth, greater earnings sensitivity and larger price gaps than established large-cap companies. A large position can amplify company-specific events. The current October 2026 factsheet and monthly-holdings file should be checked before any decision because they supersede an older snapshot. Expense Ratios, Minimums and Option Choices The August 2026 official factsheet reported base expense ratios of 1.44% for the Regular plan and 0.47% for the Direct plan. Expense ratios are charged through NAV and can change under regulation and scheme assets. The latest AMC TER disclosure, not an archived factsheet, controls for a current transaction. A lower Direct-plan ratio does not reduce market risk or establish that Direct is suitable for a person who needs advice. Minimum investment is an access rule, not a suitability test The factsheet reported a minimum investment of ₹100 and multiples of ₹1 thereafter. Platforms may impose operational minimums, mandate rules and cut-off requirements. Growth retains distributable surplus in the scheme, while IDCW may distribute when declared and reduces NAV to the extent of the payout and applicable adjustments. IDCW is neither guaranteed interest nor an additional return over Growth. Investors should verify the plan, option, folio, nominee, bank mandate and transaction cut-off. A switch between plans or options usually operates as a redemption and new purchase, potentially creating exit load and tax consequences. Platform labels and model portfolios do not replace the SID, KIM, addenda and AMC confirmation. Exit Load, Redemption and Lot-Level Records The current disclosed exit-load rule allows up to 10% of units allotted to be redeemed or switched out without exit load within one year from allotment. Units above that 10% limit redeemed within one year attract 1%. After one year, the exit load is nil. Each purchase and SIP instalment has its own allotment date, so the rule is applied to transaction lots rather than the age of the folio as a whole. Redemption timing and exceptional events The SID states that redemption proceeds are normally dispatched within three business days from receipt of a valid request, subject to applicable rules. Invalid bank details, lien or pledge, transmission, KYC mismatch, market holidays and permitted exceptional restrictions can affect processing. Nil exit load is not a promise of same-day liquidity, a fixed NAV or a tax-free redemption. Mid-Cap Riskometer and Portfolio Risks The scheme’s Very High Riskometer reflects predominant equity exposure and the volatility and liquidity characteristics of mid-cap shares. Earnings disappointment, valuation compression, sector cycles, interest rates, regulation and corporate governance events can reduce NAV. In stressed markets, mid-cap trading volumes can fall and transaction costs can rise. Concentration in a company or sector can amplify losses even if the portfolio holds many names. Benchmark and past returns do not cap downside The BSE 150 Midcap TRI is a comparator, not a capital guarantee, allocation floor for each constituent or risk ceiling. Active selection can cause meaningful deviation from the index. Fund-manager decisions, cash levels and portfolio turnover create additional risk. AUM growth can make the fund commercially prominent but does not prove that future liquidity, execution or performance will be superior. Mutual-fund units are not bank deposits and are not insured by DICGC. NAV can decline and redemption can crystallise a loss. An investor’s horizon, emergency needs, existing equity exposure and capacity to tolerate drawdowns matter more than a ranking or recent return. This article does not assess personal suitability. Equity-Fund Capital Gains, IDCW and Switch Tax Where the scheme qualifies as an equity-oriented fund and the statutory securities-transaction-tax conditions are met, units held for not more than 12 months are generally short-term. Current law generally taxes qualifying short-term capital gains at 20% plus applicable surcharge and cess. Units held for more than 12 months are generally long-term, and aggregate qualifying long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh are generally taxed at 12.5% plus surcharge and cess. The exemption is aggregate across relevant assets, not a separate allowance for each scheme. IDCW and switches require separate analysis IDCW is generally taxable in the investor’s hands at the applicable rate and can attract withholding under current rules. A switch from Regular to Direct, Growth to IDCW or one scheme to another is generally a redemption followed by a purchase for tax purposes. Exit load, securities transaction tax, cost, grandfathering and holding period must be computed from the actual statement. Non-residents, minors, trusts, businesses, investors using special regimes and persons with pre-2018 grandfathered costs may have different results. The law applicable to the transfer date controls. Records should include allotment confirmation, units, NAV, acquisition cost, plan and option, exit load, STT, redemption consideration, realised gain, IDCW and tax deduction.
