Summary : Karnataka Bank Limited’s domestic term-deposit schedule effective 8 June 2026 includes a special 555-day callable tenure for deposits below ₹3 crore. The published annual rate is 7.00% for the general category and 7.40% for eligible senior citizens. The rate row must be read with the bank’s governing deposit and interest-rate policies, the chosen payout option, booking date, completed tenure on premature closure and tax instructions. A headline rate is not a promised net return: interest may be taxable, TDS can apply under section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, and an early closure can change both the applicable tenure rate and the penalty. Karnataka Bank is a bank covered by the deposit-insurance framework, but DICGC protection is capped at ₹5 lakh for principal and interest together per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity. This neutral guide explains the verified rate, documentation, premature-closure mechanics, tax treatment and insurance aggregation. It does not rank the deposit or recommend it to any person. Eligibility and Booking Evidence Senior-Citizen Status Must Be Recorded Correctly The additional senior-citizen rate is available only when the depositor satisfies the bank’s eligibility and documentation rules. Age, residential status, ownership pattern and the relevant product conditions should be confirmed at booking. A joint deposit should not be assumed to qualify merely because one holder is a senior citizen; the bank’s rule for the first or primary holder and the precise account constitution should be checked. The receipt should display the applied rate so any mismatch can be corrected promptly. Callable Status Means Early Closure Is Possible, Not Costless The cited special tenure is callable, so premature closure is permitted subject to the bank’s policy and the actual deposit mandate. Callable does not mean the contracted 555-day rate continues if the deposit is broken early. The bank identifies the rate for the completed tenure and applies the applicable penalty framework. A depositor who may need liquidity should obtain a bank-generated premature-closure quotation rather than estimating the amount from the original maturity value. Premature Closure Uses the Completed-Tenure Rate Karnataka Bank’s interest-rate policy states that premature interest is based on the rate applicable for the period for which the deposit actually remained with the bank or the contracted rate, whichever is lower, with a penalty. For term deposits below ₹2 crore, the policy states a 0.50 percentage-point penalty on the applicable rate. For deposits of ₹2 crore and above, it states a 1 percentage-point penalty. Because this article covers the below-₹3-crore rate table, customers between ₹2 crore and ₹3 crore must not assume the lower penalty. Interest Is Taxable Even When the Bank Does Not Deduct TDS Interest on a bank fixed deposit is generally taxable according to the depositor’s applicable income-tax method and status. For an individual following the usual cash basis for such income, interest received or credited may be reported accordingly; other taxpayers or accounting methods may require accrual. The bank’s interest certificate, Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement should be reconciled with the return. The deposit principal, accumulated interest and TDS should not be conflated. Section 393 Thresholds for Tax Year 2026-27 For Tax Year 2026-27, section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 sets the threshold for the relevant bank-interest category at ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen, subject to the statutory conditions and aggregation rules. Where TDS applies and a valid PAN is available, the ordinary rate is 10%; a higher rate may apply if PAN or other compliance requirements are not met. Threshold testing applies to the payer-level interest covered by the provision, not separately to each receipt or branch. Form 121 Does Not Make Interest Exempt Eligible persons expecting nil tax liability may examine Form 121, which replaces the earlier self-declaration architecture under the new income-tax framework. Acceptance of a valid declaration affects deduction at source; it does not convert taxable interest into exempt income. The depositor must satisfy the statutory eligibility, estimate total income correctly and submit the prescribed form through the available channel. If tax is deducted, credit should be claimed only after matching the tax statement and bank certificate. DICGC Cover Is Aggregated at the Bank Level Deposits with Karnataka Bank are within the DICGC framework applicable to insured banks. The maximum insurance amount is ₹5 lakh for principal and interest together for each depositor at one bank in the same right and capacity. All eligible balances held in the same right and capacity across branches of Karnataka Bank are aggregated; opening multiple deposits or using different branches does not multiply the cover. Balances in genuinely different rights and capacities are treated under DICGC’s rules, and the legal constitution must be real and properly documented. DICGC insurance is not a guarantee of market liquidity, uninterrupted access or recovery above the ceiling. The statutory mechanism is triggered by specified bank events and operates through the prescribed process. Interest included in the insured claim counts toward the same ₹5 lakh cap. Deposit splitting should therefore be evaluated using the depositor-bank aggregation rule, not the number of receipts. DICGC question Correct treatment Are principal and interest separately insured up to ₹5 lakh each? No. The ₹5 lakh ceiling covers principal and interest together. Does each branch receive a separate limit? No. Eligible deposits across branches of the same bank are aggregated in the same right and capacity. Do multiple FD receipts multiply cover? No. Receipt count does not change the depositor-per-bank aggregation rule. Is an NBFC-style no-insurance warning applicable here? No. Karnataka Bank is a bank; bank deposits fall within DICGC rules, unlike public deposits accepted by NBFCs. Neutral Decision Checks Before Booking A depositor should verify the effective rate, available payout options, callable status, liquidity needs, tax bracket, aggregation of uninsured balances and renewal instructions. Those checks do not establish that the product is suitable. The 555-day tenure may align or conflict with the depositor’s cash-flow needs, and breaking it early can reduce interest materially. Neither the bank’s published rate nor DICGC insurance removes reinvestment, liquidity, tax, operational or concentration considerations.
