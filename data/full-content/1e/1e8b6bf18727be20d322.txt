A bear-case scenario for NBFCs factors in slower loan growth, higher credit costs, a 50% reduction in insurance fee income from FY28 and an increase in the risk-free rate assumption to 7.25% from 6.25%, according to a report by Nomura. Advt The south-west monsoon ended with a 13% deficit at the all-India level, while the South Peninsula and East and North-East regions recorded deficits of up to 27%. The north-east monsoon has also started poorly, with rainfall 54% below normal across the country between October 1 and 24. The South Peninsula recorded a 31% deficit during this period.Although farmers closed the kharif sowing season with an overall shortfall of just 1% in the area covered, rice sowing was 3.5% lower year on year. The rabi season, which began in October, has started slowly, with the area under coverage of rabi crops down 37% year on year.Reservoir levels as of October 22 were 18% below normal across the country. The deficits were higher in parts of South India, Punjab, Rajasthan and Assam, ranging between 31% and 62%. Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Telangana, Maharashtra, Gujarat and Rajasthan have declared droughts.These conditions could affect agricultural earnings and repayment capacity among rural borrowers. However, participation in government rural employment programmes could provide some cushion. States that have declared droughts have historically recorded high participation in these schemes, potentially offering alternative sources of income to households affected by weaker farm activity. Advt Food inflation presents another risk, particularly for urban borrowers, as higher household expenditure on essentials could reduce disposable income and repayment capacity. The combination of rural income pressures and tighter household budgets could affect collections and credit costs across different NBFC segments.The impact on the rural economy and food inflation is expected to become clearer from November-December 2026 onwards. Investors have been advised to remain selective until actual trends in rural incomes and household spending emerge.Urban-focused NBFCs with a higher share of housing and business loans are seen as better placed than lenders more exposed to rural borrowers and consumption-linked credit, including personal and microfinance loans. Mortgage and business lending portfolios are considered relatively better positioned to withstand the current uncertainties. Advt Besides economic conditions, an extended interest-rate increase cycle and regulatory changes affecting flexi loans and insurance distribution pose additional headwinds. Higher interest rates could weigh on borrowing costs and loan demand, while regulatory changes could affect product economics and fee income.The bear-case scenarioUnder the bear-case assumptions, loan growth estimates have been reduced and credit costs increased for the NBFCs under coverage. The scenario also assumes that insurance fee income will be halved from FY28 and raises the risk-free rate assumption by 100 basis points to 7.25% from 6.25%. Advt The resulting 12-month bear-case fair values indicate downside of 11% to upside of 27% to current market prices of the covered NBFCs, pointing to considerable variation in their ability to absorb the potential stress.Piramal Finance is the preferred pick, followed by Tata Capital, Aadhar Housing Finance and Bajaj Finance, all of which carry Buy ratings. L&T Finance, Mahindra Finance, Shriram Housing Finance and HDB Financial Services were trading close to their respective bear-case fair values. These lenders could see positive triggers if asset-quality deterioration in the second half of FY27 is less severe than assumed in the stress scenario.CreditAccess Grameen was trading at valuation multiples below those seen during the previous crisis, despite the absence of a comparable crisis on the ground so far.
