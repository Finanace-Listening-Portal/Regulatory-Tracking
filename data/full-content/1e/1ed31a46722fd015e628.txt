Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Host Mercantiles Private Limited Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Reassessment U/S 147 Cannot Sustain an Addition on a Different Issue When No Addition Is Made on the Ground for Reopening Gurgaon Plot Triggered Reopening, but Share Application Money Was Added The Assessing Officer reopened the assessment of Host Mercantiles Private Limited for Assessment Year 2009–10. In the concluding portion of the recorded reasons, the officer expressed the belief that the company had spent crores of rupees on the purchase and construction of a plot in Gurgaon and that the expenditure had escaped assessment. This was the issue on which the officer invoked the reassessment provisions and proceeded under section 148 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. When the reassessment was completed on 27 December 2016 under section 143(3) read with section 147, the Assessing Officer made no addition in respect of the Gurgaon plot or the alleged expenditure on its purchase or construction. The disputed addition of ₹3 crore under section 68 concerned share application money. The Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre, dismissed the company’s appeal on 11 February 2026, following which the company approached the Tribunal. Notice Under Section 148 Was Served Only by Affixture The company challenged the reassessment on the ground that the notice under section 148 had been served only by affixture, without first attempting ordinary modes of service such as speed post or email. Its counsel argued that the approaching limitation deadline had prompted the officer to resort directly to affixture. The Revenue relied on the findings of the authorities below. The company cited Heaven Distillery (P) Ltd. v. ITO [2017] 88 taxmann.com 825, a Mumbai Tribunal decision concerning a notice under section 143(2). That decision held that the nearing expiry of the limitation period did not justify direct service by affixture where ordinary service had not been attempted and the circumstances did not show that the assessee was avoiding service or that ordinary service was impracticable. The Delhi Tribunal recorded that the notice in the present case had been served only by affixture without exhausting other modes of service. Its express reliance in quashing the reassessment was on the Delhi High Court decisions concerning the absence of an addition on the original reopening issue. Power to Assess Other Escaped Income Under Section 147 The principal issue was whether the Assessing Officer could sustain a standalone addition on a different issue when the reassessment produced no addition on the issue identified in the recorded reasons. The company relied on the jurisdictional Delhi High Court decisions in Ranbaxy Laboratories Ltd. v. CIT and CIT v. Software Consultants [2012] 341 ITR 240 (Delhi). It also cited PCIT v. Sunlight Tour and Travels (P) Ltd. in support of its challenge. Under the reassessment provision applicable to the case, the power to assess other escaped income that came to the officer’s notice during the proceedings depended on assessing the escaped income that formed the basis of reopening. The recorded reasons identified the alleged expenditure on the Gurgaon plot. Since the officer made no addition on that issue, the addition relating to share application money could not independently sustain the reassessment. Applying Ranbaxy Laboratories and Software Consultants, the Tribunal quashed the reassessment order and allowed the assessee’s appeal. The ₹3 crore addition consequently fell with the reassessment. Other Grounds Were Left Open The company had separately challenged the reopening on grounds of borrowed satisfaction, absence of a rational reason to believe that income had escaped assessment, non-quantification of the alleged escaped income and mechanical approval under section 151. It also alleged that the Assessing Officer had completed the reassessment without disposing of its objections, relying on G.K.N. Driveshafts (India) Ltd. v. ITO [2003] 259 ITR 19 (SC). On the merits of the section 68 addition, the company claimed it had furnished the share application forms and the applicants’ names, PANs and addresses. It also challenged the adequacy of the opportunity given to it. Having quashed the reassessment, the Tribunal expressly left the remaining issues undecided. The order therefore contains no finding on whether the company discharged its evidentiary burden under section 68. Author’s Comments The practical question in a reassessment appeal of this kind is whether the final assessment actually makes an addition on the issue that triggered reopening. That can be tested by comparing the concluding portion of the recorded reasons with the findings and additions in the final assessment order. Here, that comparison was decisive. The recorded reasons concerned alleged expenditure on immovable property, while the disputed addition concerned a credit in the company’s books. The result also shows why a jurisdictional ground can determine the appeal before the evidentiary dispute is reached. The Tribunal could decide whether the officer had power under the applicable section 147 to make the share application money addition without deciding whether the documents produced by the company established the identity and creditworthiness of the applicants or the genuineness of the transactions. Service by affixture is governed by section 282(1)(b) of the Act, which provides as follows: “282. (1) The service of a notice or summon or requisition or order or any other communication under this Act (hereafter in this section referred to as “communication”) may be made by delivering or transmitting a copy thereof, to the person therein named,— … (b) in such manner as provided under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (5 of 1908) for the purposes of service of summons; or …” 8. The alleged affixture does not satisfy the mandatory requirements of Order V Rule 17 or Rule 20 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. The purported affixture report, prepared on a standard proforma, is placed at page 297 of the Paper Book. The report suffers from the following material defects: (i) It does not name any independent or local person who identified the premises; (ii) It bears only the signatures of departmental personnel; (iii) It contains neither the name, address nor signature of any independent witness; (iv) It is unsupported by an affidavit of the Inspector who allegedly effected service; and (v) It refers jointly to Plot Nos. 372 and 377, although these are two distinct premises, and does not specify the particular premises at which the notice was allegedly affixed. 9. More fundamentally, the assessment record does not disclose any prior unsuccessful attempt to effect service through an ordinary mode before resorting to affixture. Nor does it disclose the requisite satisfaction that the Appellant was avoiding service or that service could not, for any other reason, be effected in the ordinary manner. Affixture is an exceptional mode of service governed by section 282 of the Act read with Order V of the CPC and cannot be resorted to mechanically. 10. In the absence of compliance with the mandatory statutory requirements governing service by affixture, the alleged service of notice under section 148 is invalid. The issue is squarely covered by the judgment of the jurisdictional Hon’ble Delhi High Court in Ranbaxy Laboratories Ltd. v. CIT, 336 ITR 136 (Delhi). The expression “assess or reassess such income and also any other income” occurring in section 147 was construed to mean that the Assessing Officer may assess another item of escaped income only where he assesses or reassesses the income in respect of which jurisdiction was originally assumed. 16. The same principle was applied in CIT v. Software Consultants, 341 ITR 240 (Delhi), where the assessment was reopened on one issue, no addition was ultimately made on that issue, and the Assessing Officer instead sought to make an addition relating to share application money. 17. More recently, in Pr. CIT v. Sunlight Tour and Travels (P.) Ltd. [2024] 169 taxmann.com 673 (Delhi), the Hon’ble Delhi High Court reaffirmed the principle laid down in Ranbaxy Laboratories even after considering Explanation 3 to section 147. As in the present case, no addition was made in respect of the income forming the recorded reason for reopening, while an independent addition under section 68 was sought to be sustained. The Hon’ble High Court upheld the quashing of the reassessment. 18. The Ld. Counsel of the assessee also brought on record various case laws like Heaven Distillery (P) Ltd. vs. ITO [2017] 88 taxmann.com 825 wherein it was clearly held that merely because service of notice was becoming time barred, service of notice by affixation without exhausting ordinary process, was not proper. In this case the notice u/s.143(2) of the Act had been directly served through an affixture. It was held that there was nothing to suggest that the assessee was intentionally hiding from the authorities purpose of avoiding service notice or there was any good reason to conclude that the notice could not be served in an ordinary way. The reason to delay on the part of the AO in issuing notice u/s. 143(2) of the Act cannot be a ground to straightway effect service by affixture. Thus the ordinary process not having been exhausted or carried out by the AO, he was not justified indirectly restoring to service of notice by an affixture. As it was further held that in this case the AO had assumed wrong jurisdiction by restoring notice by affixture without exhausting other means of issuance of notice. We find that the notice was served on the assessee only by affixture without exhausting other modes of service of notice. Further we find that there was no addition made on the issue for which the case was reopened u/s.148 of the Act rather additions were made on different issues. 6. Keeping in view the jurisdictional High Court order in Ranbaxy Laboratories Ltd. (supra) and CIT Vs. Software Consultants (supra) the assessment order deserves to be quashed. 7.
