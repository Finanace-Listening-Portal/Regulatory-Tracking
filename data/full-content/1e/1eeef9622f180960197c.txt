Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Usha Gupta Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Section 50C Tolerance Applies Retrospectively Where the DVO’s Valuation Is Within 10% of the Declared Sale Consideration Tribunal Accepts Declared Sale Consideration of ₹18.25 Lakh The Delhi ITAT held that the assessee’s declared sale consideration of ₹18,25,000 should be accepted for computing capital gains because the Departmental Valuation Officer (DVO) valued the property at ₹18,59,200, a difference of approximately 1.87%. The Tribunal followed the Mumbai Bench decision in Maria Fernandes Cheryl v. ITO, which treated the 10% tolerance under section 50C as retrospective. It set aside the determination of long-term capital gain of ₹18,03,083 and directed acceptance of the assessee’s computation, which showed a long-term capital loss. Stamp Duty Value Was More Than Double the Sale Price The assessee filed her return for AY 2017-18, declaring total income of ₹2,16,980. During the year, she sold immovable property for ₹18,25,000. The value adopted for stamp duty purposes was ₹36,90,000, resulting in a difference of ₹18,65,000. In her return, the assessee computed the result of the transfer using the declared consideration and the indexed cost of acquisition, arriving at a long-term capital loss. The assessee objected to adoption of the stamp duty value under section 50C of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The assessment record described the valuation reference as having been made under section 55A. The DVO determined the property’s value at ₹18,59,200, substantially below the stamp duty value and only ₹34,200 above the stated consideration. Reassessment Determined a Long-Term Capital Gain The Assessing Officer completed the reassessment on 25 May 2023 under section 144 read with section 147, determining a long-term capital gain of ₹18,03,083. The assessee’s case was that this computation ignored the valuation obtained from the DVO. The CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, upheld the assessment by order dated 11 January 2025. No one appeared for the assessee before the Tribunal despite issuance of notice. The Tribunal heard the Departmental Representative and decided the appeal on the available record. The operative dispute concerned the capital gains computation on the transfer, and the existence and amount of the DVO’s valuation were undisputed. DVO’s Valuation Changed the Relevant Comparison The difference between the stamp duty value and the declared price had led the assessee to object to the proposed substitution under section 50C. The valuation report supplied a materially different figure. The Tribunal compared the DVO’s value of ₹18,59,200 with the declared consideration of ₹18,25,000. The resulting difference of ₹34,200 was approximately 1.87% of the consideration. A 10% margin on that consideration was ₹1,82,500. The Tribunal held that the difference fell within the tolerance margin. Consequently, it accepted the declared consideration itself for the computation. This was significant because even substituting the much lower DVO value would have increased the consideration by ₹34,200. Applying the tolerance preserved the computation made using the actual sale price. Mumbai ITAT Precedent Supported Retrospective Application The Tribunal followed Maria Fernandes Cheryl v. ITO (2021) 123 taxmann.com 252 (Mumbai ITAT). It relied on that decision for the proposition that the enhanced 10% tolerance was retrospective in operation and applied it to the transfer under consideration for AY 2017-18. The Delhi Bench held that the long-term capital gain of ₹18,03,083 could not be sustained and that the assessee’s computation should be accepted. The assessee’s grounds were allowed, and the Tribunal allowed the appeal in full. Author’s Comments The decision is useful for two distinct points. First, a substantial gap between the stamp duty value and the sale price may narrow considerably once a valuation report is obtained. Here, the stamp duty value exceeded the declared price by more than 100%, while the DVO’s estimate exceeded it by only 1.87%. Second, the Tribunal applied the tolerance to that narrower difference and accepted the declared consideration, preserving the capital loss computed in the return. The statutory references deserve attention when citing the order. The Tribunal describes the tolerance by reference to section 50C(2). The 110% threshold appears in the third proviso to section 50C(1), while section 50C(2) deals with a reference to the Valuation Officer. The proviso is worded by reference to the stamp valuation authority’s value. The particular significance of Usha Gupta is that the Tribunal applied the tolerance by comparing the DVO’s valuation with the declared consideration and extended that treatment to an earlier assessment year by following Maria Fernandes Cheryl. Cases Discussed Maria Fernandes Cheryl Vs ITO (ITAT Mumbai); ITA No. 4850/Mum/2019; 15/01/2021; AY 2011-12; (2021) 123 taxmann.com 252. The Mumbai Bench held that the enhanced tolerance under the third proviso to Section 50C(1) was curative and retrospective. The Delhi ITAT expressly followed this decision in accepting the consideration declared by Usha Gupta. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. The appeal in ITA No. 505/Del/2025 for AY 2017-18, arises out of the order of the ld. National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. CIT(A)’, in short] dated 11.01.2025 against the order of assessment passed u/s 144 r.w.s 147 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’) dated 25.05.2023 by the Assessing Unit, NWR (hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. AO’). 2. None appeared on behalf of the assessee despite issuance of notice. Hence I proceed to dispose of this appeal on hearing the learned DR and based on materials available on record. 3. The only issue to be decided in this appeal is as to whether the learned CITA was justified in upholding the addition of ₹18,03,083 made by the learned AO on account of investment in immovable property in the facts and circumstances of the instant case. 4. I have heard the learned DR and perused the materials available on record. The assessee filed her return of income for the assessment year 2017-18 declaring total income of ₹2,16,980. During the year under consideration, the assessee sold immovable property for Rs 18,25,000. The value of said property for the purpose of stamp duty was ₹36,90,000 in terms of section 50C of the Act. The assessee reported long term capital loss in the return of income after applying the cost inflation index. A reference for valuation of the property was made under section 55A of the Act since the assessee objected to adoption of value determined in terms of section 50C of the Act. The valuation officer determined the value at ₹18,59,200. The assessment was completed under section 144 read with section 147 of the Act determining the long term capital gain at ₹18,03,083. 5. It was submitted that the learned AO ignoring the value determined by the learned DVO had proceeded to determine the long term capital gains at ₹18,03,083 in the reassessment. This action of the learned AO was upheld by the learned CITA. 6. It is not in dispute that the learned AO had referred the valuation of the property to learned Departmental Valuation Officer (DVO) who had determined the value at ₹18,59,200 as against the reported consideration of ₹18,25,000. This difference in valuation is less than 10% of the tolerance margin in terms of section 50C(2) of the Act. This amendment brought in section 50C(2) of the Act in respect of tolerance margin has been held to be retrospective in operation by the coordinate bench of Mumbai Tribunal in the case of Maria Fernandez Cheryl vs ITO reported in 123 taxmann.com 252 (Mumb ITAT). Respectively following the same, I hold that determination of long term capital gains at ₹18,03,083 on the transfer of immovable property in the hands of the assessee is not in order and assessee’s computation is required to be accepted. The grounds raised by the assessee are hereby allowed. 7. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 18-Nov-2026.
