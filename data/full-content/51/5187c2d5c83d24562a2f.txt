Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Nitin Spinners Limited Vs ITO (ITAT Jodhpur) Export Commission Paid to HUFs Cannot Be Disallowed U/s 37(1) Merely Because They Have No Salary Expense: ITAT Jodhpur Summary: The Jodhpur Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal deleted the disallowance of ₹24.23 lakh in export sales commission paid to six Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs). The Tribunal held that an HUF can render services through its Karta or members and that the absence of salary expenditure, by itself, does not establish that no services were rendered. Facts of the Case Nitin Spinners Limited claimed a deduction for commission paid to six HUFs on export sales. The Assessing Officer doubted the genuineness of the payments because the recipients were HUFs and had claimed no salary expenditure. The Assessing Officer inferred that they had no personnel to render the services and disallowed the commission under Section 37(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The CIT(A) upheld the disallowance. The assessee submitted that an HUF is recognised as a person under Section 2(31) and can act as a commission agent through its Karta. It also pointed out that the payments were supported by bills and vouchers, that tax had been deducted at source, and that the recipient HUFs had disclosed the commission income in returns accepted by the Department. An HUF Can Render Services Through Its Karta or Members The Tribunal held that an HUF is a distinct assessable entity legally competent to carry on business. The status of the recipients as HUFs did not, by itself, cast doubt on the genuineness of the commission payments. Section 37(1) does not require a commission agent to employ salaried personnel or claim salary expenditure. An HUF may render services through its Karta or members without paying them a salary. Genuineness and Business Purpose Govern Deductibility The Tribunal explained that the test under Section 37(1) is whether the expenditure was incurred wholly and exclusively for the purposes of the assessee’s business. The tax authorities cannot substitute their commercial judgment for the assessee’s on whether the commission should have been incurred. The assessee must establish the genuineness of the expenditure and its business purpose. On the facts, the Tribunal found that the bills and vouchers, TDS compliance and disclosure of the commission in the recipients’ returns, taken together, supported the assessee’s explanation. The assessee had discharged its initial burden, and the Revenue produced no specific material showing that the documents were fabricated, the payments were returned, or the transactions were accommodation entries. The ITAT accordingly deleted the ₹24.23 lakh disallowance and allowed the assessee’s appeal. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT JODHPUR 1. This appeal by the assessee for Assessment Year (AY) 2017-18 is directed against an order dated 16.10.2025 as passed by the Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [CIT(A)] in the matter of an assessment as framed by Ld. AO u/s 143(3) on 27.12.2019 disallowing commission expenses on export sales for Rs.24.23 Lacs. 2. The relevant facts are that the assessee paid commission to six HUF entities as detailed in the assessment order. The Ld. AO held an opinion that since the payment was to artificial entities, the expenses would not appear to be genuine and was to be disallowed u/s 37(1). The assessee, inter-alia, stated that HUF could carry on business or act as commission agent through its Karta. The HUF is included as person u/s 2(31). The assessee also stated that other parameters of Sec.37(1) were duly satisfied and the payment was supported by bills / vouchers. The payments were subjected to TDS and income was disclosed by HUF entities which stood accepted by the department. However, Ld. AO observed that no salary was claimed by HUF entities which would show that no such person was available who could render services on behalf of HUF. Accordingly, the claim was denied. The assessment stood confirmed by Ld. CIT(A) against which the assessee is in further appeal before us. 3. In our considered view, the aforesaid reasoning of the Ld. AO is not sustainable in law. It is an admitted position that a Hindu Undivided Family is recognised as a ‘person’ u/s 2(31) of the Act. An HUF is a distinct assessable entity and is legally competent to carry on business activities through its Karta or other members. Merely because the recipient entities are HUFs, the same cannot be a ground to characterise them as artificial or non-genuine entities. The fact that an HUF does not have a separate physical existence in the manner of a company does not deprive it of its legal status or its capacity to carry on business. We also find merit in the contention of the assessee that there is no statutory requirement under the provisions of Section 37(1) of the Act that a commission agent must necessarily employ salaried personnel or claim salary expenditure in its return of income. An HUF may render services through its Karta or members and such services need not necessarily involve payment of salary by the HUF. Therefore, the mere absence of salary expenditure in the hands of the recipient HUFs cannot, by itself, lead to the conclusion that no services were rendered by them. It could further be noted that the payments are duly supported by bills / vouchers and subjected to deduction of tax at source. The corresponding commission income has duly been disclosed by the recipient HUF entities in their respective returns of income which stood accepted by the Department. These facts, when considered cumulatively, lend support to the assessee’s explanation regarding the genuineness of the transactions. The Ld. AO has not brought any specific material on record to demonstrate that the bills and vouchers were fabricated or that the payments were returned to the assessee or that the transactions were merely accommodation entries. The test u/s 37(1) is whether the expenditure has been incurred wholly and exclusively for the purposes of the assessee’s business. The revenue authorities cannot substitute their own commercial judgment for that of the assessee and determine whether the assessee ought to have incurred such expenditure or whether the business could have been carried on without it. At the same time, the assessee must establish the genuineness of the expenditure and its business purpose. In the present case, the assessee has discharged this initial burden through the documentary evidence placed on record, whereas the revenue has not brought any cogent material to rebut the same. On these facts, the impugned disallowance could not be sustained. We order so. 4. The appeal stand allowed.
