Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Rathindra Rabindra Dutta Vs ITO (ITAT Ahmedabad) BSNL VRS Compensation: ITAT Allows Section 10(10B) Exemption Despite No Claim in Original Return The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Ahmedabad, in Rathindra Rabindra Dutta v. Income Tax Officer, ITA No. 708/Ahd/2026, allowed a retired BSNL employee’s appeal seeking exemption under Section 10(10B) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, for compensation received under BSNL Voluntary Retirement Scheme, 2019. The assessee had initially offered the compensation to tax and subsequently claimed exemption during appellate proceedings. The order, pronounced on 22 October 2026, concerned Assessment Year 2021–22. Following coordinate Bench decisions dealing with the same scheme, the Tribunal found no change in the factual matrix or legal position and decided the appeal in the assessee’s favour. Background of the Compensation Received The assessee was employed with Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited, a Government of India enterprise. BSNL introduced its Voluntary Retirement Scheme, 2019, through a notification dated 4 November 2019. The assessee opted for the scheme and received compensation according to its terms. He submitted that regular salary had remained unpaid for several months before he opted for retirement, creating financial hardship and professional uncertainty. On this basis, he argued that the payment was, in substance, compensation for involuntary retrenchment, despite being described as compensation under a voluntary retirement scheme. This formed the factual background to his claim for exemption under Section 10(10B). Compensation Initially Offered to Tax The assessee explained that he was unaware of the exemption when filing his return. Consequently, he offered the compensation to tax, and the employer also deducted tax at source from the payment. No exemption under Section 10(10B) was claimed in either the original return or a revised return. The Centralised Processing Centre, Bengaluru, processed the return under Section 143(1) without allowing the exemption. The order records that no rectification application or appeal was filed at that stage. The assessee subsequently pursued the exemption claim before the appellate authority. His appeal was dismissed through an order dated 28 January 2026, passed by the Additional/Joint Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), Lucknow, under Section 250. Objection to Rejection of the Fresh Claim Before the Tribunal, the assessee challenged the rejection of his claim merely because it had not been made in the return. He argued that appellate authorities possess jurisdiction to entertain fresh legal claims, relying on Jute Corporation of India Ltd. v. CIT, NTPC Ltd. v. CIT, and CIT v. Pruthvi Brokers & Shareholders Pvt. Ltd. He further contended that reliance on Goetze (India) Ltd. v. CIT was misplaced because that decision restricted the Assessing Officer’s powers and did not curtail appellate jurisdiction. These propositions appeared in the grounds of appeal. The Tribunal ultimately granted relief by following coordinate Bench precedents rather than separately examining each cited authority in detail. Reliance on Harish Kumar Decision The assessee submitted that he became aware of the exemption after learning of Harish Kumar v. ITO, ITA No. 42/CHD/2025, decided by the Chandigarh Tribunal on 30 May 2025. According to his submission, that decision recognised exemption under Section 10(10B) for compensation received under the same BSNL VRS-2019, subject to the compliance requirements described in that case. He maintained that his circumstances were identical and that the CIT(A) should have examined the claim on merits. The assessee also alleged denial of a reasonable hearing and non-issuance of notice under Section 250. However, the Tribunal did not record a separate determination of that allegation. Tribunal Follows Coordinate Bench Rulings At the hearing, both parties submitted that the issue stood covered by Goverdhan D Bhambhani v. ITO, concerning Assessment Year 2020–21, decided on 28 July 2025. The Tribunal also reproduced relevant portions of Pravinkumar Ramibhai Patel v. ITO, decided on 9 July 2026, and Jayeshkumar Tulsidas Sutaria v. ITO, decided on 17 February 2026. The reproduced reasoning in these decisions addressed compensation received under BSNL VRS-2019 and claims raised after the receipts had initially been offered to tax. In Jayeshkumar Tulsidas Sutaria, the Tribunal had accepted the claim presented before the CIT(A), observing that the compensation was not taxable and that the assessee was eligible for refund of TDS. Significance of the Earlier Decisions The precedent in Pravinkumar Ramibhai Patel recognised exemption for the entire amount of retrenchment compensation, although described as VRS compensation. The present order also reproduced discussions concerning leave encashment from earlier cases. However, the assessee’s grounds and factual narrative in this appeal principally concerned exemption of BSNL VRS compensation and the ability to raise that claim during appellate proceedings. The Tribunal applied the cited precedents after finding no material difference in the facts or legal position. Its decision therefore rested on the treatment already accorded to comparable claims under the same retirement scheme. Final Decision The Tribunal allowed the assessee’s appeal, following the coordinate Bench rulings. The result permitted relief on the Section 10(10B) claim despite its omission from the original and revised returns. The decision demonstrates the practical importance of appellate consideration of an otherwise sustainable exemption claim, where comparable compensation under the same scheme has already received favourable judicial treatment. The assessee has raised the following grounds of appeal: 1.
