Articles 4 min read NBFCs can unlock Maharashtra’s next phase of growth through risk innovation and MSME financing: Aman Mittal Maharashtra is exploring data-driven lending, parametric insurance and innovative risk-sharing instruments to expand credit access for MSMEs and support its $1 trillion economy ambition. Aman Mittal, Joint CEO, Maharashtra Institution for Transformation (MITRA), said NBFCs could play a bigger role in financing emerging risks, climate resilience and industrial transition. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Updated On Oct 10, 2026 at 12:52 PM IST var news_msid = 134849467; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) can play a bigger role in Maharashtra’s economic transformation by developing specialised credit products, financing emerging risks and expanding access to capital for micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs), according to Aman Mittal, Joint CEO, Maharashtra Institution for Transformation (MITRA), Government of Maharashtra.Delivering the keynote address at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026, Mittal said the state’s ambition to become a $1 trillion economy would require greater mobilisation of private capital and new approaches to understanding and managing financial risks. Advt An assessment undertaken by MITRA around three years ago had estimated an investment requirement of approximately $1.5 trillion over a three-to-four-year period. Public resources were expected to contribute only 2–3% of the requirement, making private investment critical to the state’s growth ambitions, he said.“Who are the experts in underwriting unknown risks? I think this is where the NBFCs play the best role,” Mittal said, highlighting the sector’s ability to develop specialised financial products for emerging business needs.High-quality data key to expanding MSME creditMaharashtra has approximately 64–65 lakh MSMEs registered on the Udyam portal, but only around two to three lakh are insured against risks such as fire, Mittal said. Insurance coverage remains concentrated among businesses already accessing formal credit, highlighting the limited penetration of risk protection among smaller enterprises.He said better data could help lenders assess businesses more accurately and develop financing products suited to their needs and operating environments.“While we realise that we need to provide access to credit, what will lead to that access to credit? It is high-quality data,” he said.Maharashtra is developing State Business Registers to build more comprehensive information on enterprises across sectors. The initiative, being pursued through the State Data Authority established within MITRA, aims to make shareable, non-personal public data available to financial institutions and other stakeholders. Advt The state is also developing vulnerability maps covering climate-related risks such as floods and extreme heat. Mittal said additional data points from these initiatives were targeted for March–April.For NBFCs, the data infrastructure could create opportunities to collaborate with technology companies and use artificial intelligence to develop specialised lending and risk-assessment solutions.Parametric insurance and risk pooling can unlock new financingMittal identified parametric insurance as another potential tool for expanding financial protection. Unlike conventional insurance, which typically requires an assessment of actual losses, parametric products trigger payouts when predefined conditions are met. Advt Such products could help businesses and workers manage climate-related disruptions, including extreme heat that affects productivity and earnings. Mittal said MITRA had explored heat-related insurance and was working towards developing a product for the next heat season, with insurance-sector participants involved in discussions on underwriting the risks.He also pointed to the potential of transaction-level information, such as salary-credit patterns, to help assess income disruptions and design insurance products.Beyond insurance, Mittal said risk pooling and innovative debt instruments could help lenders manage exposures and finance borrowers who lack conventional collateral. He also highlighted the potential of smart contracts and tokenisation to automate clearly defined contractual obligations and distribute financial risks among multiple participants. Advt Transition finance offers another opportunity for NBFCsClimate resilience, industrial transition and sustainability-related financing could generate significant demand for specialised credit, particularly among MSMEs facing new environmental and supply-chain requirements, Mittal said.He cited the European Union’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) as one factor that could increase transition pressures on export-oriented businesses, requiring investments in new technologies and processes.NBFCs could help finance these requirements by developing products tailored to businesses adapting to climate risks and changing market conditions. Mittal cautioned that traditional banks could take time to develop the capabilities and frameworks needed to serve these emerging financing needs.He also highlighted financing challenges in Maharashtra’s self and cluster redevelopment segment, where restrictions on lending structures and real estate exposure have complicated access to capital. Innovative debt instruments and risk-sharing arrangements could help address some of these constraints, although discussions with NBFCs remained at an early stage.Mittal said rising public-sector borrowing requirements could also put pressure on the availability of conventional bank credit for smaller businesses, increasing the importance of alternative financing channels.For NBFCs, the opportunity lies in using specialised underwriting, better data and innovative risk-transfer mechanisms to finance businesses that may otherwise struggle to access capital.He argued that India’s next phase of economic growth would require financial institutions to move beyond increasing lending volumes and develop the ability to assess, price and distribute emerging risks.For Maharashtra, expanding MSME credit, financing industrial transition and building resilience against climate-related risks will be important to its economic ambitions. NBFCs, Mittal said, can help bridge these financing gaps by combining specialised lending capabilities with new financial products and stronger data infrastructure.
