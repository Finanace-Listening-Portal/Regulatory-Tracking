Summary : Mirae Asset Sharekhan is the current brand used for services provided through Sharekhan Limited. The legal entity is a SEBI-registered stock broker and an exchange member whose disclosed segments include currency derivatives. This profile explains the narrow scope that those registrations support: recognised Indian exchange-traded currency products made available to eligible clients. It does not treat the broker as authority for offshore spot-forex, leveraged CFD or unrecognised electronic trading. The guide also covers onboarding, margins, costs, settlement, complaints and tax documentation without endorsing trading. What the Verified Authorisation Does—and Does Not—Cover A stock-broker registration and active exchange membership permit intermediation in the segments for which the broker and client are enabled, subject to exchange, SEBI and RBI rules. They do not authorise an Indian resident to trade any currency pair, offshore leveraged forex contract, binary option or contract for difference found on the internet. Electronic forex transactions by residents must occur through authorised persons and permitted electronic trading platforms or recognised exchanges, as applicable. Absence from the RBI Alert List is not evidence of approval, and an overseas licence does not establish Indian-resident eligibility. Recognised exchange products remain rule-bound Indian exchanges specify available currency futures and options, contract specifications, trading hours, margins and settlement. RBI directions govern permissible foreign-exchange derivatives and the underlying-exposure framework. Certain exchange-traded contracts may be used without establishing an underlying exposure up to the regulatory threshold, while positions beyond applicable limits or in other circumstances may require evidence of contracted exposure. A client should verify the current exchange circular, pair, contract month, permitted purpose and broker availability before placing an order. A generic “currency trading” label is not enough. Account Opening, KYC and Segment Activation A resident client generally needs a PAN, verified identity and address, bank linkage, mobile number, email address and completed KYC. Currency-derivatives activation may require financial information or income proof under the broker’s current policy, risk disclosures and exchange requirements. The client should confirm that the currency segment is actually activated rather than assuming that an equity account automatically enables it. The Unique Client Code, bank account and contact details should match the investor’s records, and any change should follow authenticated broker procedures. Order authorisation and platform security Orders may be entered through the broker’s approved web, mobile or dealer-assisted channels. Clients should use only the current exchange-reported applications and official domains, protect credentials and one-time passwords, review login alerts and never share remote-access control. An order confirmation is not the final accounting record. The contract note, trade confirmation, margin statement, funds ledger and exchange messages should be reconciled. Discrepancies should be reported promptly using a durable written channel. Costs, Margins and the Difference Between Brokerage and Total Cost The published pricing page displays currency-options brokerage of ₹5 per lot on each side and futures brokerage, including currency futures, of 0.02% of transaction value on each side. These are headline rates from the current page, not a complete tariff for every account or order type. The executed account tariff prevails. Total transaction cost can also include exchange transaction charges, SEBI turnover fees, GST on taxable brokerage and service charges, stamp duty and other statutory levies. Bid-ask spread, slippage, rejected orders and forced position reduction can materially exceed a small stated brokerage amount. Cost or funding item How to evaluate it Brokerage Check the signed tariff and current official pricing; distinguish per-lot and percentage rates. Exchange/statutory levies Review the contract note for exchange charges, SEBI fees, GST, stamp duty and applicable taxes. Initial and exposure margin Exchange/SPAN and broker risk requirements can change with volatility and positions. Mark-to-market obligation Adverse futures movement can require funds before normal settlement. Options premium and risk A buyer pays premium; a writer can face substantial margin and open-ended adverse exposure. Spread and slippage Market liquidity can make execution differ from the displayed quote or intended exit. Trading, Settlement, Withdrawals and Position Risk Currency futures are marked to market under exchange clearing rules, and options have premium and exercise or assignment consequences defined by the contract. The broker can impose risk controls in addition to exchange minimums, including position limits, margin calls, product restrictions or square-off. A client who fails to maintain required margin can have a position reduced without the preferred timing or price. Settlement credits do not necessarily become withdrawable immediately; ledger posting, clearing obligations, bank holidays, unsettled positions and broker cut-offs can affect availability. Withdrawal and refund expectations A withdrawal request should be made only through the authenticated broker process and to the registered bank account. Available cash is different from the headline ledger balance when margin is blocked or settlement is pending. If an account is closed or a transaction is reversed, the applicable broker, clearing and banking process determines timing. The client should retain the request acknowledgement and ledger trail. No profile should promise a universal withdrawal or refund time that the current broker documents do not guarantee. Tax, GST and Record-Keeping for Indian Clients Profit or loss from exchange-traded currency derivatives is not taxed under the virtual-digital-asset regime merely because the contract references a currency. Its Indian income-tax character depends on the taxpayer’s facts and the statutory treatment of eligible exchange-traded derivatives, commonly analysed as non-speculative business transactions when the conditions are met. Turnover computation, expense deduction, audit thresholds, loss set-off and carry-forward require a fact-specific review. GST appearing on brokerage and service charges is a transaction cost; it is not GST on the entire notional contract value. Clients should retain contract notes, daily margin statements, funds and securities ledgers, bank entries, exchange messages, profit-and-loss reports and expense invoices. Broker reports are useful but do not replace reconciliation to the return and books. A person trading for business, hedging an exposure or undertaking occasional transactions may have different documentation and tax outcomes. FEMA purpose and exposure records can also be important even when the tax computation is identical. Grievance Escalation and Investor Controls The first step for an operational or accounting complaint is a written submission to Sharekhan Limited using the designated investor-grievance channel, with order numbers, dates and supporting statements. If unresolved, the investor can use the relevant exchange investor-service mechanism and SEBI’s SCORES platform, following the applicable sequence and limitation periods. Arbitration or online dispute-resolution mechanisms may be available for eligible broker-client disputes. Complaints should state the requested correction precisely and preserve acknowledgement numbers. Verify legal entity, SEBI number, exchange member code and currency-segment activation. Check that the exact pair and contract are currently permitted and exchange listed. Read the account tariff, risk disclosure and exchange contract specification before trading. Maintain margin buffers without treating them as protection from loss. Reconcile every contract note, margin statement, exchange message and bank movement. Escalate promptly in writing; never transfer funds to an employee or third-party account.
