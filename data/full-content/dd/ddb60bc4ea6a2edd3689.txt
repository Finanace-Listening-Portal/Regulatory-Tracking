Summary: Input Tax Credit (ITC) reversal is an essential component of GST compliance, requiring registered taxpayers to reverse or restrict credits in specified circumstances. This article comprehensively explains the statutory provisions governing ITC reversal under the CGST Act, 2017 and CGST Rules, 2017, supported by practical examples and calculation tables. It examines Rule 38 applicable to banking companies, financial institutions and NBFCs, Rule 42 governing common inputs and input services, and Rule 43 dealing with capital goods used for taxable and exempt supplies. The article discusses blocked credits under Section 17(5), proportionate reversal under Rule 37 for non-payment to suppliers within 180 days, and reversal under Rule 37A where suppliers fail to furnish their GSTR-3B returns within the prescribed timeline. It also explains the time limit for availing ITC under Section 16(4), statutory relaxations under Sections 16(5) and 16(6), reversal upon switching to the composition scheme or making wholly exempt supplies under Section 18(4), and payment obligations arising upon cancellation of GST registration under Section 29(5). Particular attention is given to the conditions governing ITC eligibility under Sections 16 and 17 and the appropriate reporting of permanent reversals, temporary reversals, reclaimed credits and restricted credits in Table 4 of GSTR-3B. The article concludes with a practical compliance checklist covering purchase register reconciliation, GSTR-2B verification, supplier compliance, payment monitoring, common credit calculations and documentary evidence. Proper monthly reconciliation and periodic review can help businesses minimise incorrect ITC claims, interest exposure, penalties and GST litigation. What is ITC Reversal under GST? Input Tax Credit (ITC) reversal means reversing the GST credit that was previously claimed or availed by a registered taxpayer. A business may purchase goods or services and claim the GST paid on such purchases as ITC. However, GST law specifies certain situations in which the taxpayer is required to reverse the credit, either fully or proportionately. ITC reversal may be required due to: Purchases used for exempt or non-business purposes. Blocked credits under Section 17(5). Non-payment to suppliers within the prescribed period. Non-compliance by suppliers in filing GST returns. Change in the nature of business or GST registration status. Common use of inputs, input services and capital goods for taxable as well as exempt supplies. The important provisions governing ITC reversal are explained below with practical examples. 2. Rule 38 – ITC Reversal by Banking Companies and Financial Institutions Rule 38 applies to specified banking companies, financial institutions and NBFCs engaged in providing services by way of accepting deposits or extending loans or advances. Such eligible entities may exercise the option to avail only 50% of the eligible ITC on inputs, capital goods and input services, subject to the prescribed exceptions. Example: A bank receives eligible GST credit of ₹10,00,000 during a month. If it opts for the 50% ITC method: Total eligible ITC: ₹10,00,000 ITC available: ₹5,00,000 ITC not availed: ₹5,00,000 Certain specified credits, including credit on supplies between registrations of the same legal entity, are subject to separate treatment under the rule. 3. Rule 42 – ITC Reversal on Inputs and Input Services Rule 42 applies where inputs and input services are used commonly for: 1. Taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies. 2. Exempt supplies. 3. Non-business purposes. If a business uses common purchases for both taxable and exempt supplies, it cannot retain the entire ITC. The proportionate credit attributable to exempt supplies and non-business purposes must be reversed. Practical Example Suppose a hospital has common input service ITC of ₹1,00,000. Its turnover is as follows: Taxable supplies: ₹20,00,000 Exempt supplies: ₹80,00,000 Total turnover: ₹1,00,00,000 For illustration, assuming the relevant exempt turnover ratio is 80%: Particulars Amount Common ITC ₹1,00,000 Exempt turnover ratio 80% ITC required to be reversed ₹80,000 Balance ITC attributable to taxable supplies ₹20,000 Therefore, the hospital would reverse ₹80,000 and retain ₹20,000, subject to the complete Rule 42 computation and other applicable conditions. Important: Rule 42 requires a detailed statutory calculation. The above example is simplified for understanding. Directly attributable exempt ITC must be reversed separately, while directly attributable taxable ITC may be retained if otherwise eligible. 4. Rule 43 – ITC Reversal on Capital Goods Rule 43 applies to capital goods used commonly for taxable and exempt supplies. Capital goods include assets such as: Medical equipment and machinery. Computers and servers. Furniture and fixtures. Plant and equipment. Other capital assets used in business. For common capital goods, the useful life is deemed to be 60 months for the purpose of the prescribed ITC reversal calculation. Practical Example A hospital purchases an MRI machine for ₹50,00,000 plus GST of ₹9,00,000. Assuming the entire GST of ₹9,00,000 is otherwise eligible: Particulars Amount GST paid on MRI machine ₹9,00,000 Deemed useful life 60 months Monthly ITC attributable to capital goods ₹15,000 Exempt turnover ratio (illustrative) 80% Monthly ITC reversal ₹12,000 Balance monthly eligible portion ₹3,000 Accordingly, the proportionate ITC attributable to exempt supplies is reversed over the applicable period, subject to Rule 43. Important: The actual calculation depends on the category of capital goods, date of receipt, eligibility of credit and prescribed Rule 43 formula. 5. Section 17(5) – Blocked ITC Section 17(5) specifies certain goods and services on which ITC is not available, even if they are used for business purposes, unless a specific statutory exception applies. Some common examples are: Particulars General ITC Treatment Passenger motor vehicles with approved seating capacity of up to 13 persons, including the driver Generally blocked, subject to specified exceptions Food and beverages Generally blocked, subject to specified exceptions Outdoor catering Generally blocked, subject to specified exceptions Health insurance and life insurance Generally blocked, subject to specified exceptions Club membership Blocked Works contract services for construction of immovable property Generally blocked, subject to statutory exceptions Goods or services used for construction of immovable property on own account Generally blocked, subject to the plant and machinery exception Goods lost, stolen, destroyed or written off Blocked Goods disposed of by way of gifts or free samples Blocked Personal consumption Blocked Practical Example A company purchases furniture for ₹1,00,000 and pays GST of ₹18,000. If the furniture is used for business purposes and the credit is otherwise eligible, ITC may generally be claimed. However, if the company purchases goods and distributes them as gifts to employees or customers, ITC may be blocked under Section 17(5), depending on the facts and applicable provisions. Important: Section 17(5) contains several exceptions. Therefore, ITC should not be reversed merely because an expense falls under a broad category such as food, insurance, vehicles or construction. The specific statutory provision must be examined. 6. Rule 37 – Non-Payment to Suppliers Within 180 Days Under Rule 37, where a recipient fails to pay the supplier the value of supply along with the applicable tax within 180 days from the date of invoice, the proportionate ITC attributable to the unpaid amount is required to be reversed or paid, along with applicable interest. The ITC can generally be re-availed after payment is made to the supplier, subject to the prescribed conditions. Practical Example A company purchases goods worth ₹1,00,000 plus GST of ₹18,000. The company claims ITC of ₹18,000 but does not pay the supplier within 180 days. If the entire invoice remains unpaid: ITC originally claimed: ₹18,000 ITC required to be reversed or paid: ₹18,000 Applicable interest: As prescribed under the GST law If the company subsequently pays the supplier, it may reclaim the eligible ITC in accordance with the applicable provisions. Important: The reversal is proportionate where only part of the invoice remains unpaid. Specified transactions, including certain reverse-charge supplies and deemed supplies under Schedule I, have separate treatment. 7. Rule 37A – Supplier Has Not Filed GSTR-3B Rule 37A applies where the supplier has reported an invoice or debit note in GSTR-1 or through the Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF), but has not filed the corresponding GSTR-3B. In such cases, the recipient may be required to reverse the ITC if the supplier does not file the corresponding GSTR-3B within the prescribed timeline. For a financial year, the recipient is required to reverse the relevant ITC by 30 November following the end of that financial year if the supplier has not filed the corresponding GSTR-3B by 30 September following that financial year. The recipient may re-avail the ITC after the supplier files the corresponding GSTR-3B, subject to the applicable provisions. Practical Example A company receives an invoice of ₹1,00,000 plus GST of ₹18,000. The supplier reports the invoice in GSTR-1 but does not file the corresponding GSTR-3B. If the supplier fails to file the return within the prescribed timeline: ITC claimed by recipient: ₹18,000 ITC required to be reversed: ₹18,000 Re-availment: Permitted after the supplier files the corresponding GSTR-3B, subject to applicable conditions. Practical Suggestion: Businesses should regularly reconcile GSTR-2B with the purchase register and monitor suppliers who have not filed their GSTR-3B. 8. Section 16(4) – Time Limit for Availing ITC Section 16(4) prescribes the time limit for claiming ITC against an invoice or debit note. Generally, ITC cannot be availed after 30 November following the end of the relevant financial year or the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier. There are specific statutory relaxations under Sections 16(5) and 16(6), where applicable. Practical Example A company receives an invoice dated 15 March 2025 relating to FY 2024-25. The general deadline for claiming ITC would be 30 November 2025 or the date of filing the annual return for FY 2024-25, whichever is earlier. If the company fails to claim the ITC within the applicable time limit, the credit may become time-barred. 9. Section 18(4) – Reversal on Switching to Composition Scheme or Exempt Supply Where a registered person switches to the composition scheme or where the supply of goods or services becomes wholly exempt, the taxpayer is required to pay an amount equivalent to the ITC attributable to: Inputs held in stock. Inputs contained in semi-finished goods. Inputs contained in finished goods. Capital goods, after applying the prescribed reduction for the period of use. Practical Example A business has the following ITC-related balances on the date it switches to the composition scheme: Particulars ITC Amount Inputs lying in stock ₹20,000 Inputs contained in finished goods ₹15,000 Inputs contained in semi-finished goods ₹10,000 Capital goods (after prescribed calculation) ₹25,000 Total amount payable/reversible ₹70,000 The business is required to reverse or pay the applicable amount in accordance with Section 18(4) and Rule 44. 10. Section 29(5) – ITC Reversal on Cancellation of GST Registration Where GST registration is cancelled, the registered person is required to pay an amount equivalent to the ITC attributable to inputs held in stock, inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods, and capital goods, as prescribed. For capital goods, the calculation is made after the prescribed reduction. Practical Example A company decides to close its business and applies for cancellation of GST registration. On the relevant date, it has: Raw material stock carrying ITC of ₹20,000. Finished goods carrying ITC of ₹30,000. Capital goods requiring reversal of ₹15,000 after the prescribed calculation. The company is required to discharge the applicable amount under Section 29(5), subject to the statutory computation. 11. Other Important Situations Where ITC May Be Reversed or Restricted Apart from the provisions explained above, businesses should also monitor the following: 1. Section 16(2)(b) – Receipt of Goods or Services ITC is subject to the condition that the registered person has received the goods or services. Where goods are received in lots or instalments, credit is generally available upon receipt of the last lot or instalment. 2. Section 16(2)(c) – Tax Actually Paid to Government ITC is subject to the condition that the tax charged on the supply has actually been paid to the Government, either in cash or through utilisation of eligible ITC, subject to the statutory framework. 3. Section 17(2) – Common Credit Where goods or services are used partly for taxable supplies and partly for exempt supplies, ITC is restricted to the portion attributable to taxable and zero-rated supplies, subject to the prescribed rules. 4. Section 17(1) – Non-Business Use ITC attributable to goods or services used for non-business purposes is not available. 5. Section 17(5) – Blocked Credit ITC on specified goods and services is blocked, even where the expenditure is incurred in the course of business, unless a statutory exception applies. 6. Section 16(2)(aa) – Supplier Reporting The invoice or debit note details must be furnished by the supplier in the prescribed manner and communicated to the recipient, subject to the applicable statutory conditions. 12. Reporting of ITC Reversal in GSTR-3B ITC reversals are required to be reported in the appropriate tables of GSTR-3B. GSTR-3B Table Particulars Table 4(B)(1) Permanent reversals, including reversals under Rules 38, 42 and 43 and blocked credit under Section 17(5) Table 4(B)(2) Other ITC reversals, including reversals under Rules 37 and 37A Table 4(D)(1) Disclosure of ITC reclaimed which was reversed earlier under Table 4(B)(2) Table 4(D)(2) Disclosure of ITC restricted under Section 16(4) and specified place-of-supply restrictions Important: Table 4(D)(2) is a disclosure field and does not represent an additional ITC reversal. The actual reporting should be done in the appropriate ITC tables in accordance with the applicable GST instructions and circulars. 13. Practical GST Compliance Checklist for Businesses To avoid excess or incorrect ITC claims, businesses should regularly perform the following checks: 1. Reconcile GSTR-2B with the purchase register. 2. Identify invoices appearing in GSTR-2B but not recorded in the books. 3. Identify purchase invoices recorded in the books but not appearing in GSTR-2B. 4. Verify whether suppliers have filed their GSTR-3B. 5. Monitor payments to suppliers within 180 days. 6. Identify blocked ITC under Section 17(5). 7. Calculate common ITC reversal under Rule 42. 8. Calculate capital goods ITC reversal under Rule 43. 9. Review RCM transactions and ensure applicable tax is paid. 10. Verify the time limit for claiming ITC under Section 16(4). 11. Review ITC reversal and reclaim entries in GSTR-3B. 12. Perform annual Rule 42 and Rule 43 reconciliation. 13. Maintain supporting workings and documentary evidence for all ITC claims and reversals. 14. Conclusion ITC reversal is an important part of GST compliance. Incorrect availment or non-reversal of ineligible ITC may result in additional tax liability, interest, penalties and departmental notices. Businesses should establish a proper monthly ITC reconciliation system covering GSTR-2B, purchase registers, supplier compliance, blocked credits, common credit reversals and payment status. A properly maintained Excel-based ITC reconciliation and reversal working can significantly reduce the risk of GST errors and future litigation.
