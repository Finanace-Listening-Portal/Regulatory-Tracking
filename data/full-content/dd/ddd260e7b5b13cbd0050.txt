Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) Summary: ITAT Delhi directed the Assessing Officer to recompute the disallowance for AY 2014–15 under Section 14A read with Rule 8D using only investments that yielded exempt dividends during the relevant previous year. Mahabhadra Constructions Ltd. earned exempt dividend income of ₹1,15,20,605 and had voluntarily disallowed ₹50,87,743 in its computation. In the assessment order dated 29 December 2016, the Assessing Officer increased the disallowance to ₹2,21,36,173 under the administrative expenditure component. The CIT(A)/NFAC upheld that amount by its order dated 21 August 2023. Relying on the Delhi High Court decision in Caraf Builders and the ITAT Delhi Special Bench decision in Vireet Investment, the Tribunal found that both lower authorities had overlooked the restriction on which investments could enter the calculation. It accepted the assessee’s sole substantive ground in principle and left the resulting amount to be worked out by the Assessing Officer. No other ground was pressed. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. Cases Discussed PCIT-2 Vs Caraf Builders & Constructions Pvt. Ltd., (2019) 414 ITR 122 (Delhi High Court), ITA No. 1260/2018, decided on 13 November 2018. The Tribunal cited the Delhi High Court decision alongside the Special Bench ruling when directing the Assessing Officer to restrict the investment base to dividend-yielding investments. ACIT Vs Vireet Investment Pvt. Ltd., [2017] 165 ITD 27 (ITAT Delhi Special Bench), ITA No. 502/Del/2012 and CO No. 68/Del/2014, decided on 16 June 2017. The Tribunal relied on the Special Bench rule that the average value of investments for the Rule 8D computation takes into account only investments that yielded exempt income during the year. Heard both the parties at length. Case file perused . It transpires during the course of hearing that the assessee/appellant herein is aggrieved against both the learned lower authorities’ action invoking section 14A r .w . Rule 8D disallowance amounting to Rs .2,21,36 ,173; in assessment order dated 29.12.2016 as upheld in the lower appellate discussion. We next notice with the able assistance coming from both the parties that there is no dispute about the assessee having derived exempt income in the relevant previous year amounting to Rs .1,15,20 ,605/- in the nature of dividends. And that it had further suo motu disallowed a sum of Rs .50,87 ,743/- in the computation which has been enhanced to Rs .2 ,21,36 ,173/- as administrative expenditure thereunder, in the Assessing Officer’s assessment as upheld in the lower appellate discussion . That being the case , the Revenue could hardly dispute that case law PCIT Vs. Caraf Builders and Construction Pvt. Ltd. (2019) 414 ITR 122 (Del.) and ACIT Vs. Vireet Investment Pvt. Ltd., ITA No. 502/Del/2012 and CO No . 68/Del/2014 dated 16.06 .2017 (Special Bench) have already settled the issue that only dividend yielding investments ought to be considered whilst computing such a disallowance u/s 14A r .w. Rule 8D o f the Income Tax Rules, 1962. Both the learned lower authorities have admittedly not considered this clinching proposition in their respective findings. We thus accept the assessee’s instant sole substantive ground in principle and dire ct the learned Assessing Officer to frame his consequential computation in very terms. No other ground or argument has been pressed before us. This assessee’s allowed for statistical purposes. Order Pronounced in the Open Court on 08/01/2026 .
