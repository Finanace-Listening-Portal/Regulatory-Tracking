Global supply chains are being reconstituted. Trade agreements are opening markets. Technology is changing the location and organization of production. India has an opportunity to move from being primarily a large market to becoming an important production and innovation hub,” Mishra said while speaking at 13th SBI Banking and Economic Conclave. Advt Mishra said the changing global environment also required India to strengthen domestic manufacturing and reduce its dependence on imports.“Second, our dependence on imports and not just on energy alone. We have a significant merchandise trade deficit. We must make things at home and competitively,” he said.He also addressed the broader changes taking place in global trade and supply chains, including the increasing use of supply chains as a strategic tool and the return of trade barriers.“First, alliances are fragmented. Supply chains are being used as weapons. Capital can be switched on and off. Trade barriers, both tech and non-tech, are rising again. We cannot count on a friendly trade agreement,” Mishra said.He said efforts were being made to improve India's manufacturing performance and the skilling of its young people.“Third, the pressure on our external accounts. In a world where capital is stricken, we need to focus on foreign direct investment. That calls for stability of tech policy contracts that states enter, logistics that can be relied on and clearances that actually clear,” Mishra said.Mishra said production-linked incentives had helped concentrate investment across sectors, but argued that manufacturing could not be the final objective for India.“Production-lead incentives, for example, have helped centralize investment across 14 sectors. But manufacturing cannot be the end of ambition. We must progressively move towards greater domestic value-addition, stronger components, ecosystem, design capabilities, intellectual property and globally competitive Indian brands,” he said. Advt He said India's ambition should ultimately move beyond manufacturing towards building design, innovation and globally competitive capabilities from within the country.“The journey must move from make in India to design in India, innovate in India and ultimately lead from India,” Mishra said.
