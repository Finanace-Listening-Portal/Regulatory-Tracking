Articles 7 min read Piramal Finance CEO says new co-lending norms may impact upfront loan monetisation models Piramal Finance’s co-lending book stands at around Rs 5,000 crore and is growing at an annualised 20–25 per cent. While pure origination arrangements will continue to work as is under the revised framework, the model of originating loans to monetise them upfront through direct assignment or co-lending to investors may not survive, MD & CEO Jairam Sridharan told ETBFSI. Hear him on funding costs, growth priorities, regulatory changes and AI-led lending. The NBFC is currently participating purely as an investor and is not originating much business for banks.The CEO believes arrangements in which originators monetise loans upfront by taking the net present value of future cash flows as profit may not survive the new co-lending framework.The lender’s cost of borrowing stands at 8.8 per cent, while fresh borrowings are being raised at 8.4 per cent, expects its overall funding cost to remain stable or decline even if interest rates rise.Revolving credit exposure is around Rs 2,000 crore, or 2 per cent of its Rs 1 lakh crore book. The gross impact of the proposed insurance distribution norms is estimated at 18–24 basis points of return on assets, but the company has a natural hedge through its 50 per cent ownership in an insurance business.AI-led disbursements stood at Rs 102 crore, while collections driven entirely by AI without human intervention amounted to Rs 172 crore.Edited Excerpts:Q. With interest rates moving up, how do you see the funding environment and the transmission to borrowers?Jairam Sridharan: If you look at our borrowing costs, they have been steadily coming down over the last two quarters, even in a rising rate environment.This is happening because we got a rating upgrade two quarters ago. The new money that we are raising is coming at a significantly cheaper cost. That impact is going to continue for at least another year and a half because it takes time for old debt to get repaid and new debt to take over.Today, our cost of borrowing on the portfolio is 8.8 per cent. But the new money we are raising is at 8.4 per cent. Since our incremental cost of borrowing is significantly lower than our existing cost, we are better positioned than many others in the market. Even in a rising rate environment, we can see our cost of borrowing remain stable or even decrease. Advt Over the next 12 months, we have to repay a significant amount of debt, and that debt is at 9.1 per cent. Against that, we are borrowing new money at 8.4 per cent. Even if the cost of new borrowing goes up a little because of further rate increases, it will still be much lower than the debt we are retiring. On a net basis, we will be in a much better position with respect to our overall cost.Q. How much of your loan book is on floating rates? Will customers see an increase in lending rates?Jairam Sridharan: About 50 per cent of our assets or loans are on floating rates. All our housing and loan against property products are on a floating rate basis.But if my rates do not increase, I am not going to pass on anything to my customers. Our customers are not going to see a rate increase. Unlike some other NBFCs, where their cost of borrowing might be increasing and they may have to increase customer rates, we will not have to do that because our rates are not increasing.Q. The Rs 3,850 crore QIP and preferential allotment will create how much incremental lending capacity?Jairam Sridharan: About Rs 20,000 crore.Q. What kind of mix do you want to maintain between wholesale and retail, and secured and unsecured lending?Jairam Sridharan: We want wholesale to be about 15 per cent of our overall portfolio. It is currently at 14 per cent, below that level. We want unsecured lending to be about 25 per cent of our overall portfolio. Today, it is 21 per cent. Advt We want to increase it to 25 per cent of the overall portfolio. Those are the main shifts that you will see, and that reduction will largely happen in mortgages.Q. Where will the growth in unsecured lending come from?Jairam Sridharan: That depends on the opportunities available quarter to quarter. Right now, the opportunities are broad-based. They are across self-employed and salaried customers, and also in digital lending. Different opportunities arise in different quarters, so I do not want to be definitive about it.Q. Piramal Finance has an exposure to revolving credit. How much is the book, and are you looking at recalibrating it in anticipation of the RBI's final guidelines? Advt Jairam Sridharan: It is about Rs 2,000 crore. We have all given our feedback on the challenges and some of the unforeseen side effects of the draft as it was written. The RBI has been clear that it will take into account the feedback it has received.We have done nothing so far. We are waiting for the final guidelines. Our book is very small. It is about Rs 2,000 crore in a Rs 1 lakh crore book, so it does not really move the needle in any major way. Even if we have to take some action overnight, we can do it.Q. What is the estimated impact of the proposed insurance distribution norms on Piramal Finance? Advt Jairam Sridharan: We have said that, on a gross basis, the impact on us is about 18–24 basis points of ROA.The reason I use the word gross is that our situation is a little unique in the sector. We are also 50 per cent owners of an insurance business, and we distribute 100 per cent of our insurance.A loss of value to me as a distributor is a gain to the insurance company. Half of that gain comes right back to me as the 50 per cent owner. So I have a natural hedge built into our business model because of the ownership of this insurance company.Most other NBFCs do not have that natural hedge. That positions us differently in the context of the IRDAI norms compared with many others.Q. How are your co-lending partnerships faring, and are you looking to increase the book?Jairam Sridharan: We are very happy with the places where we are the co-lending investor. But where we are co-lending originators, we have not made a lot of progress because originating for banks has been hard and some of the new norms have made it a little more complicated. So we have not been doing it a lot.Q. What is the breakup between your role as an originator and an investor, and how large is the co-lending book?Jairam Sridharan: We are not originating much at all. Right now, we are purely investors in co-lending. The co-lending book is about Rs 5,000 crore.Q. Do you intend to increase the book further?Jairam Sridharan: It is currently increasing nicely, at about 20–25 per cent annualised. It is increasing. I do not know how much further it will go, but let's see.Q. What is your assessment of the new co-lending norms, which come into effect from January 1?Jairam Sridharan: The business in which it is a pure origination arrangement continues to work as is.But there are many cases in which originations are happening for the originator to monetise the business right away by doing a direct assignment or co-lending it out to an investor, and by taking the net present value of the cash flows upfront as profit. That model does not seem like it will survive in the new regime.Q. What is the trajectory in terms of AI-led disbursements and collections?Jairam Sridharan: We have had Rs 102 crore of new business disbursed directly and exclusively through AI bots. We have also had Rs 172 crore of collections purely driven by AI, with no humans involved.Q. By when do you expect the legacy negative-margin book to completely run off?Jairam Sridharan: Another two quarters. It will vanish in another two quarters.
