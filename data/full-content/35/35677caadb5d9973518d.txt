(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India set aside the judgments and orders of the Delhi High Court refusing to accept a written statement in a commercial recovery suit merely because the application seeking condonation of delay had been filed beyond the prescribed outer limit of 120 days from the service of summons. The Supreme Court noted that the written statement itself had been filed on the 34th day and held that, in the facts and circumstances of the case, the High Court had adopted an excessively technical approach in interfering with the Commercial Court’s order condoning the delay and taking the written statement on record. Commercial Recovery Suit and Delay in Filing Written Statement OK Play India Pvt. Ltd. instituted a commercial suit against A.P. Distributors and another for recovery of Rs.7,68,000. Summons were served upon the defendants on 23 January 2019, requiring them to file their written statement by 22 February 2019. However, the defendants filed their written statement on 26 February 2019, involving a delay of four days beyond the initial statutory period of 30 days. The written statement was not accompanied by an affidavit of admission and denial of documents or an application seeking condonation of delay. The affidavit of admission and denial was subsequently filed on 20 May 2019, within the outer limit of 120 days from service of summons. The plaintiff objected to the acceptance of the belated written statement and affidavit on the ground that neither had been accompanied by an application for condonation of delay. After hearing the objections, the Commercial Court reserved the matter for orders on 31 October 2019. On the same date, the defendants filed an application under Section 151 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, seeking condonation of delay. The defendants explained that the written statement had been delayed because of the time required to procure original documents. By its order dated 26 November 2019, the Commercial Court accepted the explanation, observed that a hyper-technical approach was unwarranted, and directed that the written statement be taken on record subject to payment of costs of Rs.10,000. Read Delhi HC Judgment in this case: Delayed Written Statement in Commercial Suit Requires Timely Condonation Application: Delhi HC Delhi High Court Insists on Condonation Application Within 120 Days The plaintiff challenged the Commercial Court’s order before the Delhi High Court under Article 227 of the Constitution of India. Relying upon the Supreme Court judgment in SCG Contracts India Private Limited Vs K.S. Chamankar Infrastructure Private Limited and Others, the High Court examined the mandatory timelines introduced in the CPC through Section 16 of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015. It observed that a written statement must ordinarily be filed within 30 days from service of summons, although the Commercial Court could grant an extension for a further period of 90 days upon recording reasons and imposing appropriate costs. Beyond 120 days, the defendant forfeits the right to file the written statement. The High Court further held that a defendant filing a written statement after the initial period of 30 days was required to submit a written application supported by an affidavit explaining the reasons for the delay. According to the High Court, such an application was also required to be filed within the maximum permissible period of 120 days. It considered that the inherent powers under Section 151 CPC could not be exercised to circumvent the mandatory procedural requirements. Consequently, by its judgment dated 17 August 2021, the High Court set aside the Commercial Court’s order, directed that the written statement be removed from the record and required the Commercial Court to consider further proceedings under Order VIII Rule 10 CPC. Defendants Seek Review of High Court Judgment The defendants subsequently filed Review Petition No. 131/2021, contending that the High Court had committed errors of fact and law. They argued that the plaintiff had not initially paid adequate court fees on the interest component of the recovery claim and that the limitation period could not commence until the requisite court fees were paid on 19 August 2019. They further submitted that Order VIII Rule 1 CPC did not expressly require a separate application for condonation of delay and that the High Court had impermissibly introduced an additional procedural requirement. They also questioned the applicability of the Delhi High Court (Original Side) Rules, 2018, to Commercial Courts functioning at the district level. In support of their submissions, the defendants relied upon Red Bull AG Vs Pepsico India Holdings Pvt. Ltd. and distinguished the Supreme Court’s decision in SCG Contracts India Private Limited on the ground that the written statement in that case had itself been filed beyond 120 days, whereas their written statement had been filed well within that period. They also contended that the earlier judgment had curtailed the Commercial Court’s discretion under Order VII Rule 11 and Order VIII Rule 5 CPC. High Court Rejects Review and Upholds Procedural Requirements The Delhi High Court examined the limited scope of review jurisdiction under Order XLVII Rule 1 CPC, relying upon the Supreme Court judgment in Kamlesh Varma Vs Mayawati & Ors. It reiterated that review proceedings could not be converted into appellate proceedings and that repetition of previously rejected arguments or introduction of new pleas would not ordinarily justify review. An error apparent on the face of the record, discovery of previously unavailable material evidence or another legally sufficient reason was necessary for exercising review jurisdiction. The High Court rejected the objection concerning the applicability of the Original Side Rules by referring to the Practice Directions contained in Annexure E, issued under Section 18 of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015. It held that these directions extended to district-level Commercial Courts dealing with commercial disputes of specified value. Relying upon several earlier decisions, including Unilin Beheer B.V. Vs Balaji Action Buildwell, Rajesh Goel Vs Jasbir Singh, Shreeved Consultancy LLP Vs Friends Motels Pvt. Ltd. and Mothers Pride Education Institution Pvt. Ltd. Vs Shukla Sehgal, the High Court reiterated that an extension beyond the initial period of 30 days was discretionary and could not be claimed as a matter of right. The High Court also rejected the argument that payment of additional court fees postponed the institution of the suit, observing that the additional fees had been paid voluntarily following the defendants’ objections and not pursuant to any direction requiring correction of the valuation or payment of deficient court fees. It further clarified that proceedings under Order VIII Rule 10 CPC did not prevent the Commercial Court from exercising its discretion under Order VIII Rule 5 CPC. Finding no ground for review, the High Court dismissed the review petition with further costs of Rs.10,000. Supreme Court Distinguishes Delay in Written Statement From Delay in Condonation Application The Supreme Court, after granting leave and hearing the parties, emphasised the material distinction between the date on which the written statement was filed and the date on which the application seeking condonation of delay was submitted. Although the condonation application had been filed beyond 120 days, the written statement itself had been filed on the 34th day from service of summons. Therefore, the factual position was materially different from a case in which the written statement itself was filed beyond the mandatory outer limit of 120 days. The Supreme Court held that the High Court had taken an excessively technical view in setting aside the Commercial Court’s order accepting the written statement. It accordingly allowed the appeals, quashed and set aside the impugned judgments and orders of the High Court and restored the Commercial Court’s order condoning the delay and taking the written statement on record. No costs were awarded by the Supreme Court. The ruling establishes that, in the circumstances of the present case, the belated filing of the condonation application did not justify rejection of a written statement that had itself been filed within the permissible outer limit. The Supreme Court did not dilute the mandatory 120-day limit applicable to the filing of the written statement itself. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER Leave granted. Having heard learned counsel for the respective parties and in the facts and circumstances of the case and considering the fact that the written statement was filed on the 34th day of the service of notice of summons, however, the application for condonation of delay was filed which was beyond the period of 120 days, the High Court has taken too technical view in setting aside the order passed by the learned Commercial Court directing to accept the written statement filed on behalf of the appellants/defendants. The High Court was not justified in setting aside the order passed by the learned Trial Court directing to take the written statement on record. In view of the above and for the reasons stated hereinabove, the present appeals succeed. The impugned judgment and orders passed by the High Court are hereby quashed and set aside and the order passed by the learned Trail Court directing to take written statement on record after condoning the delay in submitting the written statement is hereby restored. The present Appeals are accordingly allowed. No costs. Leave granted. The appeals are allowed in terms of the signed order. Pending application(s) shall stand disposed of.
