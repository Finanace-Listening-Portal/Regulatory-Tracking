Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Arya Kanya Vidyalaya Samiti Vs CIT (Exemption) (ITAT Jaipur) Wrong Clause in Section 12A Application Is a Curable Defect: ITAT Jaipur Background of the Dispute The assessee challenged the CIT(E)’s order dated 24 September 2025, rejecting its application in Form 10AB under Section 12A(1)(ac)(iii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The rejection rested on three grounds: filing the application under an incorrect clause, absence of registration under the Rajasthan Public Trust Act, and failure to establish the genuineness of activities. The assessee was engaged in the charitable activity of imparting education. It contended that substantial documents and evidence supporting its activities had been submitted but were not properly considered before rejection. Assessee’s Submissions The assessee submitted that only two notices had been issued, dated 2 July 2025 and 15 September 2025, and that it had responded to both. Although the second response was considered incomplete, no further opportunity was provided to address the deficiencies. According to the assessee, the CIT(E) rejected the application for want of bills and vouchers without adequately examining the voluminous material already furnished. Regarding registration under the Rajasthan Public Trust Act, the assessee stated that it had subsequently obtained registration. Evidence showing that it had applied for such registration had already been placed before the CIT(E). The assessee therefore sought another opportunity to establish compliance and demonstrate the genuineness of its educational activities. Why the Application Was Filed Under Clause (iii) The CIT(E) observed that the assessee already held registration under Section 12AA, granted on 3 February 2009. Consequently, according to the CIT(E), it should have applied under Section 12A(1)(ac)(i) instead of clause (iii). However, the assessee explained that the Department had granted it provisional registration twice. The first order, dated 27 May 2021, covered assessment years 2021-22 to 2023-24. The second order, dated 7 April 2023, covered assessment years 2023-24 to 2025-26. As the second provisional registration approached expiry, the assessee filed the application on 28 March 2025 under clause (iii). It maintained that the application had been filed bona fide, considering the provisional registrations previously granted by the Department. Tribunal Finds Inadequate Opportunity of Hearing The Tribunal noted that the assessee had responded to both notices. It therefore could not be regarded as non-cooperative during the proceedings. A paper book comprising four volumes was filed before the Tribunal, demonstrating the documents, details and evidence submitted to the CIT(E). The Tribunal observed that the CIT(E) had treated the genuineness of activities as unestablished for want of bills and vouchers without dealing with the material concerning the assessee’s educational activities. Considering these circumstances, the Tribunal concluded that the assessee deserved another opportunity to address both the public trust registration issue and the genuineness of its activities. Incorrect Clause Does Not Invalidate Application On the objection concerning the incorrect clause, the Tribunal found that the CIT(E) had not properly examined the assessee’s explanation or verified the history of provisional registrations. It observed that there appeared to have been errors or omissions on the part of both the assessee and the Department, concerning the applications and the grant of provisional registration. The CIT(E) was directed to verify the facts, examine where the error or omission occurred, and then determine whether any deficiency existed. Crucially, the Tribunal held that mere mention of a wrong clause does not invalidate an application. It is a curable defect, which should be brought to the assessee’s notice so that an opportunity for correction can be provided. Decision and Significance The Tribunal restored the registration issue to the CIT(E), directing adequate opportunity to furnish evidence regarding Rajasthan Public Trust Act registration, establish genuineness of activities, and address the incorrect-clause objection. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. Registration was not granted outright; the CIT(E) must reconsider the application, including other matters necessary for registration. The ruling underscores the need to examine explanations and supporting records before rejecting an application for a procedural defect. That the appellant prays to add OR alter any ground of appeal at OR before the time of hearing. 3. 1. That Ld. With regards to the denial of registration for the reason that application was filed under the wrong clause, the Ld. CIT(E)’s finding is to the effect that the assessee ought to have applied for registration under clause (i) of Section 12A(1)(ac) of the Act rather than applying for registration under sub-clause (iii) of the Act. As per the Ld. His findings in this regard are contained at page 6 and 7 of his order as under:- …. In view of the above discussions, it is imperative to quote the section 12A of Income Tax Act which is stated as below: “12A. (1) The provisions of section 11 and section 12 shall not apply in relation to the income of any trust or institution unless the following conditions are fulfilled, namely:- (ac) notwithstanding anything contained in clauses (a) to (ab), the person in receipt of the income has made an application in the prescribed form and manner to the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner, for registration of the trust or institution,- (i) where the trust or institution is registered under section 12A (as it stood immediately before its amendment by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 1996 (33) of 1996)] or under section 12AA (as it stood immediately before its amendment by the Taxation and Other Laws (Relaxation and Amendment of Certain Provisions) Act, 2020 (38 of 2020)], within three months from the first day of April, 2021; (iii) where the trust or institution has been provisionally registered under Section 12AB or provisionally approved under sub-clause (iv) or sub-clause (v) or sub-clause (vi) or sub- clause (via) of clause (23C) of section 10, at least six months prior to expiry of period of the provisional registration 87[or provisional approval, as the case may be, or within six months of commencement of its activities, whichever is earlier, (iv) where registration of the trust or institution has become inoperative due to the first proviso to sub-section (7) of section 11, at least six months prior to the commencement of the assessment year from which the said registration is sought to be made operative; In view of the above , we restore the issue of grant of registration u/s 12A(1)(ac) of the Act back to the file of the Ld. CIT(E) to give adequate opportunity of hearing to the assessee to furnish all facts regarding being granted registration under the RPT Act, establishing the genuineness of its activities and also vis-à-vis the issue of application being filed under the wrong or incorrect clause, which is to be considered as per our findings and directions above and to consider all other matters necessary for grant of registration under the Act. 17. In effect, the appeal of the assessee stands allowed for statistical purposes.
