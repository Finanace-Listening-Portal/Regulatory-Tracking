Summary: City Union Bank’s published domestic term-deposit schedule effective 21 October 2026 lists a specific 555-day callable tenure under the CUB Fixed Deposit product. The annual rates shown are 7.15% for the general category, 7.35% for eligible senior citizens and 7.40% for eligible super senior citizens. The bank’s product page allows cumulative and non-cumulative structures, with simple-interest payout choices and quarterly compounding for cumulative deposits. Premature closure is permitted, subject to the bank’s published penalty calculation, and loan or overdraft facilities may be available under bank policy. Interest remains taxable, and resident-bank TDS thresholds for Tax Year 2026-27 are governed by section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025: ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen, subject to the statutory conditions. City Union Bank appears on DICGC’s insured-bank list, but insurance is capped at ₹5 lakh for principal and interest aggregated across branches in the same right and capacity. This article explains the verified product terms without recommending the deposit or predicting future rates. What the CUB Fixed Deposit Product Provides Deposit Period, Amount and Interest Choices The CUB Fixed Deposit page describes deposits from seven days to ten years. It states that simple interest may be paid monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually, while cumulative deposits add interest to principal at the end of every quarter. For individuals, the product page shows a minimum amount of ₹100 in multiples of ₹100. Separate minimums are shown for MSMEs and corporates. Actual channel limits, identity requirements and account-opening conditions should be confirmed with the bank. Cumulative and Non-Cumulative Outcomes A cumulative deposit retains interest in the account and compounds it quarterly under the bank’s product description. A non-cumulative deposit distributes interest at the chosen frequency, so the depositor receives cash flows before maturity and the maturity amount does not include retained quarterly interest in the same way. Monthly-payout amounts may be calculated using the bank’s applicable method and can differ from a simple annual rate divided by twelve. The receipt and maturity advice control the contractual amount. Loan, Overdraft, Nomination and Renewal City Union Bank states that loan or overdraft facilities and auto-renewal options are available for its fixed deposits. Availability, margin, interest rate, documentation and customer eligibility depend on bank policy. A loan against deposit creates a separate borrowing obligation and may reduce net interest benefit. Nomination should be recorded and checked on the deposit receipt. Joint holders should also confirm operating instructions and the right and capacity in which the deposit is held. Premature Closure and the 1% Reduction The bank’s published premature-closure rule states that a 1% penalty is charged on the rate applicable for the period from the deposit date to the pre-closure date or on the original contracted rate, with the lower rate after the penalty being paid. The bank’s separate policy also states that no interest is payable when withdrawal occurs before the minimum required period, currently seven days. The result therefore depends on the actual completed tenure and the rate table that applied on the original booking date. Premature closure should not be modelled by simply subtracting 1% from the 555-day rate. If the deposit closes early, the relevant comparison includes the rate for the period actually completed. The bank may apply the lower of the post-penalty alternatives stated in its schedule. Depositors should request a pre-closure quote showing principal, completed tenure, applicable rate, penalty, accrued interest and net proceeds before authorising closure. TDS is only a collection mechanism. If interest remains below the TDS threshold, the income does not become exempt. Cumulative deposits may create taxable interest accrual before the maturity proceeds are received, depending on the applicable tax treatment. Depositors should reconcile the bank’s interest certificate, Form 26AS, Annual Information Statement and their books or personal records. Section 393 Thresholds and Rate For Tax Year 2026-27, section 393(1), Table serial number 5(ii), sets a ₹1,00,000 threshold for a senior citizen and ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen where interest other than interest on securities is paid by a banking company. The Income Tax Department’s published TDS-rate table shows 10% for this category, subject to the Act, PAN requirements and other applicable provisions. Threshold testing generally concerns aggregate interest rather than each deposit receipt viewed separately. Form 121 for Eligible Nil-Tax Declarations The Income Tax Department explains that the former Form 15G and Form 15H framework corresponds to section 393(6) under the Income-tax Act, 2025 and uses unified Form 121 for Tax Year 2026-27. A declaration is not an automatic facility for every depositor. The person must satisfy the statutory eligibility conditions, including the nil estimated-tax requirement. Submitting a declaration does not make the interest tax-free and does not correct an invalid eligibility assumption. DICGC Cover Is an Aggregate Bank-Level Ceiling DICGC lists City Union Bank Ltd. among insured banks. The insurance ceiling is ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank for principal and interest together, measured in the same right and same capacity. Deposits at different City Union Bank branches are aggregated. Opening several receipts or using several branches does not multiply the cover. Deposits held in different rights or capacities are treated under DICGC rules, and depositors should not assume separation without understanding those rules. DICGC insurance does not guarantee the contracted interest rate, market liquidity or immediate access in every situation. It applies upon the events and process specified by law, including liquidation, cancellation of licence, a qualifying scheme or specified restrictions. Interest accrued up to the relevant date counts within the ₹5 lakh ceiling. A depositor whose combined principal and interest exceed the ceiling bears exposure above the insured amount. Illustrative Reading of the Rate, Not a Return Promise A quoted annual rate cannot be converted into a guaranteed maturity value without the principal, compounding or payout choice, exact day-count method, tax deductions and contractual terms. A non-cumulative depositor receives periodic interest; a cumulative depositor retains interest for quarterly compounding. Premature closure changes the applicable rate basis. For this reason, any calculator result should be checked against the deposit receipt issued by the bank and should not be presented as a guaranteed net return.
