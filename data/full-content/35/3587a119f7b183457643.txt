Gururaj Vs Additional Chief Secretary/Commissioner of Land Administration & Others (Supreme Court of India) Waterbody Patta Cancellation Upheld, but State Directed to Allot Alternative Site to Flat Owners Before Demolition Issue involved Whether a ryotwari patta granted over land historically recorded as a government waterbody could be sustained on the strength of subsequent revenue entries, residential zoning and municipal permissions; and whether innocent flat purchasers should face demolition without protection against the consequences of the State authorities’ own actions. Background of the case The dispute concerned 0.72 acres out of 1.68 acres in Survey No. 572, Thirumullaivoyal Village, Tiruvallur District, Tamil Nadu. The 1911 Resurvey and Resettlement Register classified the land as “Government Poramboke—Kanakkan Kuttai”. Through successive transactions, P. Ramadoss claimed rights over the disputed extent. On an application filed in October 2010, the District Revenue Officer granted him a ryotwari patta on 21 January 2011, reasoning that the land was not a pond and had been wrongly classified in the old records. Ramadoss mortgaged the 0.72 acres to Tamil Nadu Mercantile Bank Ltd. to secure credit facilities of ₹3.40 crore. He subsequently settled 0.22 acres on his wife. Through a developer, 20 residential flats were constructed on that portion pursuant to municipal building approvals and sold to individual purchasers. A third-party objection led the Commissioner of Land Administration to cancel the patta on 26 December 2019. The Commissioner found that the land was a government watercourse, the patta application was entertained beyond the permissible time, and serious irregularities attended the processing of the records. The Madras High Court upheld cancellation and directed eviction, demolition and restoration of the waterbody. A flat purchaser and the Bank appealed to the Supreme Court. Appellants’ contentions The appellants disputed the conclusion that the property was a tank and questioned the basis for its classification. They relied on its inclusion in a Mixed Residential Zone, subsequent revenue entries and permissions granted by municipal authorities. They also contended that the mortgagee Bank and flat purchasers had not been heard before cancellation of the patta. Supreme Court’s observations on land classification The Court held that entitlement to a ryotwari patta depended on the land qualifying as ryoti land. Its character had to be examined with reference to the earliest revenue, survey and settlement records. The appellants had not established that the property was recorded as ryoti land during the relevant settlement proceedings. Relying on Salem Municipality v. P. Kumar, (2019) 13 SCC 307, the Court explained that tank lands fall outside the relevant definition of ryoti land. It emphasised that section 14-A of the Tamil Nadu Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act, 1948 prohibits grant of ryotwari patta even for private tanks or ooranis and provides for cancellation of pattas covered by that statutory prohibition. The grant of patta by the District Revenue Officer could therefore not be sustained. Residential zoning and opportunity of hearing The Court rejected the argument that residential zoning displaced the waterbody classification. Planning classifications do not extinguish the Government’s rights over poramboke lands, forests or waterbodies. Inclusion within a development zone could not override section 14-A. The Court also rejected the hearing objection on the facts. Notice had been issued to Ramadoss, the person in whose favour the patta stood and who continued to claim an interest in the property. Protection for innocent flat purchasers While upholding cancellation, the Court recognised the hardship caused by official conduct. Municipal authorities had sanctioned building plans, and revenue authorities had altered the town survey records. These acts led bona fide purchasers to believe that the property was available for lawful development. The Court observed that the State and its officers could not simply escape the consequences of their facilitation, leaving purchasers exposed to irreparable financial injury and displacement. Accordingly, it directed the Chief Secretary, Tamil Nadu, and the District Collector, Tiruvallur, to allot a vacant, commensurate site to the flat owners represented by their Society, preferably within six months, and thereafter undertake demolition of the constructed portion measuring 0.22 acres. Restoration work on vacant and unoccupied land could proceed expeditiously. Decision The appeals were dismissed subject to the protective direction concerning alternative land. The flat owners and Bank remained free to pursue remedies against the developer, owner or mortgagor before the competent civil court. Disciplinary action against erring officials was also left open. Author’s comments The judgment draws a significant distinction between protecting a waterbody and protecting purchasers misled by official permissions. The Court preserved the statutory prohibition while requiring the State to address the consequences of its own administrative actions. The alternative-site direction does not validate the cancelled patta or regularise the apartments. Demolition remains contemplated, but only after the directed allotment. Equally, the relief is an allotment of a vacant, commensurate site; the judgment does not direct construction of replacement flats or award a quantified monetary compensation. For purchasers and lenders, the decision demonstrates why approved building plans, registered transactions and recent revenue entries may not resolve historical title defects. Here, the earliest classification and the statutory protection of waterbodies prevailed, while the purchasers’ hardship received a separate, concrete remedy. Cases Discussed: 1. Salem Municipality v. P. The Tamil Nadu Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act, 1948 (“Act, 1948”) provides for the repeal of the permanent settlement. It also provides for the acquisition of landholders’ rights in permanently settled and certain other estates in the erstwhile Province of Madras, now the State of Tamil Nadu, and for the introduction of ryotwari settlement in such estates. The Act, 1948, while doing away with the intermediary system in land regulation and administration, contemplated recognising a ryot’s entitlement to protection of possession and the grant of a ryotwari patta for ryoti land. 3. “Ryot land” means1 cultivable land in an estate other than private land, but does not include: a. beds and bunds of tanks and of supply, drainage, surplus or irrigation channels; b. threshing-floor, cattle-stands, village sites and other lands situated in any estate which are set apart for the common use of the villagers; c. lands granted on service tenure either free of rent or on favourable rates of rent if granted before passing of this Act or free or rent if granted after that date, so long as the service tenure subsists. Therefore, the extended definition of Ryoti land means entirable lands in an estate by a Ryot who holds it for agriculture on condition of paying rent to the landholder which is legally due other than private land.2 The consideration relates to the grant of Ryotwari Patta to Respondent No. 2 for Survey No. 572, to an extent of 0.72 acres, for land locally known as “Kanakkan Pillai Kuttai”. 4. The State of Tamil Nadu, through Amendment Act No. 49 of 1974, introduced Section 14-A into the principal Act, which reads as under: “14-A. Ryotwari patta not to be granted in respect of private tank or oorani- (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, no ryotwari patta shall be granted in respect of any private tank or oorani. (2) Any ryotwari patta granted in respect of any private bank or oorani under this Act before the date of publication of the Tamil Nadu Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Amendment Act, 1974, in the Tamil Nadu Government Gazette, shall stand cancelled, and for the purpose of compensation under this Act, the private tank or oorani shall be deemed to be land in respect of which neither the landlord nor any other person is entitled to ryotwari patta under this Act.” 5. The controversy in the Civil Appeals turns on the District Administration’s will and determination to give effect to the letter and spirit of Section 14-A of the Act, 1948. 6. On Jurisdiction and Limitation: The DRO, Tiruvallur granted Ryotwari Patta under G.O. Ms. No. 1300 (Revenue Department) dated 30.04.1971, read with G.O. Ms. No. 370 (Commercial Taxes & Religious Endowments Department) dated 03.10.1974. The statutory time limits for entertaining applications under these Government Orders expired on 30.06.1975 and 31.03.1977, respectively. Under the relevant Inam and Estate Abolition Acts (Tamil Nadu Act 26 of 1963 and Act 26 of 1948), the limitation periods ended on 31.03.1977 and 30.08.1987, respectively. The Respondent P. Ramadoss filed his Application only on 18.10.2010, several decades after the limitation periods had expired. Consequently, the DRO, Tiruvallur, had neither jurisdiction nor authority to entertain the Petition or grant Patta. B. Classification: As per the Resettlement Register (RSR / 1911 Register), S.No. 572 (1.68 acres) is recorded as “Government Poramboke Kanakkan Kuttai”. As per the Town Survey ‘A’ Register, the land in Ward D, Block 84, T.S. No. 4, measuring 6800 sq. meters, is recorded as “Sarkar Poramboke – Kanakkan Pillai Kuttai”. Although a ground inspection found the pond no longer visible due to encroachment, the land remains legally an objectionable watercourse poramboke dedicated to public and cattle use. Granting Patta over watercourse land was strictly illegal and contrary to established law. C. On Handling of Official Files: The then DRO, Tiruvallur, issued the Order dated 21.01.2011 without calling for any reports or records from the jurisdictional Tahsildar. Verification of the 2010 Personal Register of the dealing B1 Assistant confirmed that the current number 25714 was never entered in that seat. The Distribution Register and Tapal Register showed that the current number 25714 had been assigned to the F Section (F3) for land acquisition matters. The actual NOC/Patta Application filed by P. Ramadoss on 18.10.2010 bore file number 35714/2010, which the DRO deliberately misrepresented as 25714/2010 in the proceedings. File No. 35714/2010 was untraceable, contained no entry regarding its closure or disposal, and was never deposited in the Collectorate Record Room, thereby proving that the Order was not passed in a bona fide officiating capacity. D. On Revenue Staff: Subordinate Revenue officials, including the Additional Personal Assistant (Land) to the District Collector, the Ambattur Tahsildar, the Section Head Clerk, the B1 Assistant, and the Taluk Survey staff, failed to object to the grant of Patta over an objectionable watercourse. They also failed to alert higher Authorities, such as the District Collector, the Director of Survey and Settlement, or the CLA, thereby leaving the fraudulent transaction concealed until a Public Welfare Body intervened. S.No. 572/1 (900 sq. m / 0.09.0 Ha): Kanakkankuttai (presently Manikandapuram Main Road). ii. S.No. 572/2 (2915 sq. m / 0.29.0 Ha): Patta No. 6558 in favour of P. S.No. 572/3 (2985 sq. m / 0.30.0 Ha): Kanakkankuttai. The District Administration’s inaction allowed the illegal construction of multi-storey apartments and the subsequent sale of flats to innocent third-party purchasers who were kept unaware of the fraudulent title. Disciplinary and Criminal Proceedings: The District Collector was directed to initiate severe disciplinary action and file criminal cases within 15 days against the then DRO, Tiruvallur, all subordinate officials involved, and any other officials found complicit in facilitating the fraud. 11. Tamil Nadu Mercantile Bank Limited, the Appellant in Civil Appeal Nos. 13630 and 13631 of 2026, filed Writ Petition No. 8661 of 2020 challenging the Order dated 26.12.2019 passed by the CLA. The impugned Order dismissed the Writ Petition. The summary of the conclusions is stated hereunder: – A. The High Court adopted the factual and legal conclusions recorded in paragraph 15 of the Commissioner of Land Administration Order dated 26.12.2019, which characterise the 2011 DRO, Tiruvallur grant of patta as a product of administrative fraud. B. The land was classified as “Kanakkankuttai” before and throughout the settlement proceedings under the Act, 1948. Communal water bodies vest in the State free from all encumbrances and cannot be granted on patta to either ryots or erstwhile landholders. C. Section 14-A of the Act, 1948 expressly bars the grant of patta even in respect of private tanks or ooranis and mandates the cancellation of any patta erroneously or mistakenly issued over such lands. The DRO, Tiruvallur, had no authority to reclassify communal poramboke land into ryoti land. The power of reclassification under Section 20-A of the Tamil Nadu Estates Land Act, 1908, was extinguished upon its repeal by the Act, 1948. D. That G.O.Ms. No. 1300 dated 30.04.1971 applied strictly to ryoti lands in continuous occupation and could never authorise regularisation or grant of patta for water bodies. E. That Tamil Nadu Mercantile Bank is a mere mortgagee claiming under P. Ramadoss. Because P. Ramadoss procured the patta by fraud over Government watercourse land, the mortgage created no valid charge against the State. Since the Bank’s mortgagor had no marketable title, hearing the Bank would have been an empty formality that could not alter the legal character of a public water body. F. The Court directed the District Administration to evict all occupants, demolish all unauthorised residential and commercial structures across the entire 1.68 acres of Survey No. 572, and restore the water body within 16 weeks. G. The Court ordered the District Collector, Tiruvallur, to inquire into the fraudulent issuance of the 2011 Patta, initiate disciplinary and criminal proceedings against all responsible officials, bar them from key posts, and place them under surveillance by the Vigilance and Anti-Corruption Department. H. The Court dismissed the Writ Petition while preserving the Bank’s statutory liberty to proceed against the borrower/mortgagor under SARFAESI and to initiate criminal proceedings, excluding the period of writ pendency from limitation under Section 14 of the SARFAESI Act, 2002. 12. Advocate Jayasree Narasimhan argues that the earliest resurvey and settlement record of 1911 described the subject matter as “Government Poramboke – Kanakkam Kuttai”. The Registered Deed inter se parties does not affect the rights possessed under the Act, 1948, by the Government/Local Bodies. The DRO, Tiruvallur, exercised jurisdiction not available to him. Without prejudice to the first contention, it is argued that the DRO, Tiruvallur, acted beyond the record. The survey and settlement operations, concluded long ago by the grant of ryotwari pattas, were followed by renewed efforts as late as 2010 to secure a ryotwari patta with urbanisation. The Revenue Officer does not have jurisdiction to grant a patta for non-ryoti land, i.e., communal land, Government Poramboke, bank, forest, etc. After examining the record, the CLA rightly set aside the patta dated 21.01.2011. The Assignee/P. Ramadoss did not challenge the CLA Order dated 26.12.2019, but as Mortgagee, Tamil Nadu Mercantile Bank Limited questioned it in Writ Petition No. 8661 of 2020. The impugned Order rejected the challenge for valid and available reasons. To protect non-ryoti land from being granted as ryotwari pattas, the State Legislature enacted Section 14-A of the Act, 1948, and nullified pattas granted prior to the amendment for private land or oorani. Section 14-A further deems that, for private land or oorani, the landholder or any other person is entitled to a ryotwari patta under the Act, 1948. Therefore, the Government’s development work is for the restoration of a water body, and no exception can be made. 16. We have perused the record, including the latest Report dated 09.10.2026, filed by the District Collector, Tiruvallur. The said Report is made part of this Judgment. 17. The Appellant takes exception to the Order dated 26.12.2019 on the ground that no Authority has adjudicated that the subject matter of the Appeal is a tank. In our considered view, this contention appears untenable because the Act, 1948 does not require adjudication to protect communal Government Poramboke, etc. Simply put, the Ryotiwari tenures under the Act, 1948 apply to Ryoti land covered by the said Act. The inquiry in this behalf determines the nature of the land, the type of enjoyment, the period of enjoyment, and the entitlement to grant of patta. 18. In Salem Municipality v. P. Kumar and Others,3 this Court considered the definitions under Sections 3(15), 3(16) and 14-A(1) of the Act, 1948. The background in the Judgment concerns a tank and tank bund. Between 1940 and 1946, Zamindar Ganambal granted multiple seasonal 8-month leases (November–June) for the “Chinneri Tank Bund” dry wasteland to S. Vijayaranga Mudaliar, who held possession before the July monsoon submergence. Following the Act, 1948, the land vested in the State, and the Government took possession on 12.01.1951. Despite Mudaliar’s eligibility for a Ryotwari Patta under Section 11 of the Act, 1948, the plaintiff, P.C. Pachiappan, purchased the 4.91-acre suit property on 29.09.1952. The Plaintiff’s Applications for a Ryotwari Patta were rejected because the land constitutes “Achuvan Eri”, i.e., a Class IV irrigation lake, and must be preserved as a submergible tank for local farmers (ayacutdars). On 23.02.1984, the State Government transferred the entire 24.62-acre area to the Salem Municipality for a new bus stand. In response, the Plaintiff filed three successive Suits for declaration of title and injunctions, culminating in the High Court, where the judgment of the First Appellate Court was set aside. In the above factual background, the following paragraphs are excerpted: “19. It is apparent from the definition of “ryot” as defined under Section 3(15), means a person who holds the land for the purpose of agriculture. It is necessary for such a “ryot” to hold “ryoti land” in an estate. Ryoti land has been defined in Section 3(16) as cultivable land in an estate other than private land but does not include beds and bunds of tanks and of supply, drainage, surplus or irrigation channels. Thus, as the area in question formed part of the tank it was clearly not ryoti land as per the said definition in Section 3(16). As such, the predecessor-in-interest S. Vijayaranga Mudaliar or the plaintiff could not be said to be “ryot” holding “ryoti” land. xxx xxx xxx 28. Section 14-A(1) makes it clear that notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, no ryotwari patta shall be granted in respect of any private tank or ooranies. Even if any patta has been granted the same shall stand cancelled and the land of the private tank or oorani shall be deemed to be land of neither the landholder nor any other person is entitled to ryotwari patta under the Act. 29. It is apparent from the conjoint reading of the provisions contained in Sections 3(15) and 3(16) of the 1908 Act and the provisions contained in Sections 3, 11 and 14-A of the 1948 Act that the land of the tank is not “ryoti land” as such no rights of “ryot” could accrue in the person by holding the land on temporary arrangement of lease granted for 8 months in a year when water was not there in the tanks. Such bodies are protected by virtue of the aforesaid provisions carved out under the Acts of 1908 and 1948. The amendment made in 1974 in Section 14-A makes it clear that even if any ryot patta has been granted to any incumbent even with respect to private tank or ooranies, that shall be inoperative and stand cancelled.” 18.1 Finally, it was held that the Revenue entries show that the entire area of 24.62 acres had been recorded as a tank continuously from 1950 to 1995. Since the land formed part of a tank reserved for common use, no right accrued to the plaintiff to claim Ryotwari Patta, as his predecessor was not a Ryot and the disputed land was not Ryotwari land. The vendor did not hold the land for 12 consecutive years, so no right, title, or interest accrued to the Vendor or the Plaintiff. 19. Therefore, non-Ryoti lands do not fall within the scope of granting a patta under the Act, 1948. Whenever a dispute arises over entitlement to a patta, the nature of the land is determined by appreciating entries in the earliest Revenue Records, including Survey and Settlement Records. The Appellants do not contend that the Revenue Records show the subject matter as Ryoti land during the Survey and Settlement operation or the contemporaneous faslis. Since the Appellants claim a Ryotwari patta, they must establish the ingredients for the land to be termed Ryoti land and that P. Ramadoss is entitled to a Ryotwari Patta. Accordingly, the first objection to the impugned Orders is rejected. 20. It is argued that, under the Tamil Nadu Town and Country Planning Act, 1971 (“Act, 1971”), the urban area development plan for the land has been notified as a “Mixed Residential Zone”. Therefore, the basis of the Commissioner’s Order or the Writ Court’s Order is completely effaced, and consequently the cancellation of the Ryotwari Patta vide Order dated 26.04.2019 is illegal. 21. The Statement of Objects and Scope of the Acts of 1971 and 1948 is noted. Therefore, notifying a block of area in the development zone as commercial, industrial, residential, or mixed residential/mixed-use cannot be construed as taking away the Government’s rights in Poramboke lands, forests, water bodies/tanks, etc. The said argument does not take into account that zoning regulations are not confined to a single survey number but apply when an individual undertakes development activity in the notified area. The development of a plot conforms to the notified zoning requirement. Reference to the subject matter being in a mixed residential zone, either in fact or in law, cannot be treated as effacing the effect of Section 14-A of the Act, 1948. 22. It is further argued that the CLA did not hear the Mortgagee and the flat owners while setting aside the Ryotwari Patta. This argument requires examining whether the CLA heard no one or only the person interested in the subject matter when passing the Order dated 26.12.2019. The Appellants claim that P. Ramadoss, who continues to hold an interest in the balance extent of the subject matter, was heard, and that the flat buyers were not heard during the pendency of the proceedings before the CLA. The objection on the ground of denial of opportunity is not available to the Appellants because the Notice was issued to the person who had the Ryotwari Patta in his favour and claimed an interest in the property. 23. It is argued that the Revenue Records do not record the subject matter as a water body but instead record it in the names of private individuals. This argument is far removed from the Revenue extracts on which the CLA passed the Order dated 26.12.2019. The record filed by the State reinforces that the subject matter has been classified as a tank/Government Poramboke in the Revenue Records from the earliest point in time. This ground is equally untenable and warrants rejection. 24. Section 14-A of the Act, 1948, prohibits officers from considering and granting Ryotwari Patta for non-Ryoti land. The Legislature designed a prohibitory mechanism to prevent changes in land classification. The said Section even renders the Order already issued void. That being the statutory scheme, the DRO, Tiruvallur, without any reason, went beyond the provisions of the said Act and granted Patta. 25. The above consideration records findings on the Appellants’ entitlement to any relief in the Civil Appeals in relation to the subject matter. Other circumstances in the matter include the grant of Permit No. C 3031 on 24.05.2005 by the Avadi Municipality for establishing the timber mart; the revenue department changed the Town Survey Land Register and approved the subdivision of the subject matter; the Avadi Municipality approved the building plans for the construction of apartments; and, as per the Report dated 09.10.2026 of the District Collector, Tiruvallur, the present existence of apartments is to an extent of 0.22 acres. The impugned Judgment directed the demolition of the existing structures and the restoration of the water body. 26. In the present case, the Municipal Administration has approved and sanctioned the building plans. The Revenue, without realising its obligation under Section 14-A of the Act, 1948, is changing the nature in the Town Survey Land Records. These permissions/Orders lead a bona fide purchaser to believe that the subject matter is available and free from any objectionable use. The Court is constrained to observe that a bona fide purchaser for consideration looks at the presumptive value of entries and town survey records while purchasing the property. At the government’s convenience, the nature of the land is changed. The remedies against the developer and the landowner are independent, but the State and its officers ought not to be allowed to pass on without paying the price for such willy-nilly felicitation by the officers of the State. 27. Because of these Orders and Revenue Records, the flat owners have been led to believe that the Government has no interest under Section 14-A of the Act, 1948. Therefore, directing the demolition of flats without examining the hardship of the flat owners and the independent remedies available to them in law against the owner of the land and the developer of the apartments would cause irreparable financial injury and throw the families on the streets. In the circumstances of the case, to ameliorate the possible hardship and, without committing a breach of Section 14-A of the Act, 1948, we direct the Chief Secretary, State of Tamil Nadu, and the District Collector, Tiruvallur, to allot a vacant, commensurate site to the owners of the flats represented by the Society, preferably within 6 months from today, and thereafter to undertake the demolition of the constructed area to an extent of 0.22 acres. 28. Subject to the above direction, we see no reason to interfere with the impugned Judgment. The development work in the vacant and unoccupied area may be completed expeditiously without hindrance. The flat owners and the Appellant Bank are free to avail themselves of the remedies in law against the developer/Owner/Mortgagor, as the case may be, before the Competent Civil Court. The State Government is free to initiate or conclude disciplinary action against the erring Officers for failing to maintain the records and water bodies. 29. For the above consideration and reasons, the Civil Appeals are dismissed. No order as to costs. 30. Pending application(s), if any, stand disposed of accordingly.
