Supreme Court Leaves Section 74(10) Limitation on Erroneous Refund to the Appellate Authority Summary: The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Vyome Therapeutics Limited v. The Commissioner of CGST (East), Delhi & Ors. [SLP (C) No. 34610/2026 dated October 12, 2026] dismissed the Special Leave Petition and declined to interfere with the order of the Hon’ble Delhi High Court [W.P.(C) 9141/2026 dated August 12, 2026], which had refused to examine in writ jurisdiction whether the five-year limitation for passing an order to recover an erroneous refund is to be reckoned under the first or the latter limb of Section 74(10) of the CGST Act, 2017, and had relegated the assessee to the statutory appeal, with a direction that its application under Section 14 of the Limitation Act, 1963 be considered pragmatically by the Appellate Authority. Facts: Vyome Therapeutics Limited (the “assessee”) was served with an adjudication order dated March 28, 2026 for recovery of a refund alleged to have been erroneously granted to it. The assessee filed W.P.(C) 9141/2026 before the Hon’ble Delhi High Court challenging the order as time-barred, and contended that it should not be relegated to the remedy of appeal, which was admittedly available. The Hon’ble Delhi High Court relegated the assessee to the appellate remedy. The assessee then approached the Hon’ble Supreme Court by way of SLP (C) No. 34610/2026. Contentions: Assessee:The order was required to be passed within five years from the date of erroneous refund in terms of the latter limb of Section 74(10) of the CGST Act. Since it was passed beyond that period, the writ petition should be entertained. In rejoinder, it was submitted that the case was not one of audit but of adjudication. Revenue:The assessee was subjected to audit under Section 65 of the CGST Act, during which the erroneous refund came to the notice of the Department. The first limb of Section 74(10) applied, i.e., five years from the due date for furnishing the annual return for FY 2019-20, which stood extended up to February 28, 2021 vide Notification No. 95/2020-Central Tax dated December 30, 2020 and thereafter up to March 31, 2021 vide Notification No. 04/2021-Central Tax dated February 28, 2021. Issues: Whether the Hon’ble Supreme Court should interfere with the order of the Hon’ble Delhi High Court declining, in exercise of writ jurisdiction, to examine the plea that an adjudication order for recovery of an erroneous refund is barred by limitation under Section 74(10) of the CGST Act, 2017? Whether the dispute as to which limb of Section 74(10) applies, i.e., five years from the due date of the annual return or five years from the date of erroneous refund, being dependent on the nature of the demand and the underlying proceedings, is to be agitated before the Appellate Authority and not the writ court? Whether the assessee can seek exclusion of the period of pendency of the writ petition under Section 14 of the Limitation Act, 1963 while pursuing the statutory appeal? Held: The Hon’ble Supreme Court in SLP (C) No. 34610/2026 held as under: Observed that, after hearing the learned senior counsel for the petitioner and going through the materials on record, it was not inclined to interfere with the impugned order passed by the High Court. Held that, the Special Leave Petition is dismissed. Directed that, pending applications, if any, shall stand disposed of. Hence, the order of the Hon’ble Delhi High Court stands undisturbed. That Court had noted that the availability of the statutory appeal was not in dispute, observed that it was debatable which limb of Section 74(10) applies since the provision contemplates different starting points depending on the nature of the demand, held that deciding this would require examination of the nature of the demand, the underlying proceedings and the factual foundation of the adjudication, and directed that any application under Section 14 of the Limitation Act, 1963 for exclusion of the period of pendency of the writ petition shall be considered pragmatically by the Appellate Authority. Our Comments Provision invoked: Section 74(10) of the CGST Act requires the proper officer to issue the order under Section 74(9) within five years from the due date for furnishing the annual return for the financial year to which the tax not paid, short paid or input tax credit wrongly availed or utilised relates, or within five years from the date of erroneous refund. The provision thus carries two limbs with two different starting points. Section 73(10) is the corresponding provision for non-fraud cases with a period of three years, while for FY 2024-25 onwards Section 74A(2) and (7) retain the same two-pronged approach, with notice to be issued within forty-two months from the due date of the annual return or from the date of erroneous refund and the order to follow within twelve months of the notice, extendable by six months. Why the limb matters: On Revenue’s computation, five years from the extended due date of March 31, 2021 expired on March 31, 2026, which would make the order dated March 28, 2026 timely by merely three days. If, however, the refund had been sanctioned before March 28, 2021, the assessee’s reading of the latter limb would render the same order barred. The Delhi High Court order does not record the date or amount of the refund, which is precisely why the limb became the crux of the dispute. Our view: On a plain reading, the latter limb is a specific provision for erroneous refund, and a demand for recovery of a refund wrongly sanctioned should ordinarily be tested on that limb, irrespective of whether the lapse surfaced in an audit under Section 65 or otherwise. Audit is only a mode of detection and does not alter the nature of the demand. Revenue may counter that where the refund is merely a consequence of tax or ITC wrongly availed or utilised in a particular year, the demand “relates to” that financial year. The Delhi High Court found the position debatable and left it open, and the question therefore remains alive. Effect of the dismissal: The Supreme Court order is a short, non-speaking order. Dismissal of a Special Leave Petition in limine without reasons does not result in merger and does not declare law under Article 141 of the Constitution, as held in Kunhayammed v. State of Kerala [(2000) 6 SCC 359] and reiterated in Khoday Distilleries Ltd. v. Sri Mahadeshwara Sahakara Sakkare Karkhane Ltd. [(2019) 4 SCC 376]. Neither the Delhi High Court order, which expressly did not decide the limb, nor this dismissal answers the limitation question. It will now be decided by the Appellate Authority and, in due course, by the higher fora. Writ versus appeal: The approach is consistent with Radha Krishan Industries v. State of Himachal Pradesh [(2021) 6 SCC 771 dated April 20, 2021], where the Hon’ble Supreme Court held that the High Court retains discretion to entertain a writ despite an alternative remedy, but ordinarily should not do so where an efficacious statutory remedy exists, the exceptions being breach of fundamental rights or natural justice, absence of jurisdiction or a challenge to vires, and with Assistant Commissioner of State Tax v. Commercial Steel Ltd. [Civil Appeal No. 5121 of 2021 dated September 03, 2021], where the Hon’ble Supreme Court set aside the High Court’s entertaining of a writ against a GST order for which an appeal lay. In Super Service Point v. Union of India [SLP dated January 07, 2026], the SLP against refusal of a writ challenging a consolidated Section 74 order was withdrawn with liberty to file an appeal under Section 107. The Hon’ble Delhi High Court itself, in Armour Security India Ltd. v. Union of India [W.P.(C) 10738/2026 dated August 03, 2026], relegated the petitioner to appeal despite a plea that the question went to jurisdiction, holding that the Appellate Authority can examine it. Contrary judgment: In Deepak Sharma v. Commissioner, Department of Trade and Taxes, Govt. of NCT of Delhi [W.P.(C) 6449/2026 dated May 12, 2026], the Hon’ble Delhi High Court entertained a writ and quashed a Section 74 order for FY 2017-18 passed on December 30, 2025, after the outer limit of February 05, 2025 (five years from the extended due date of the annual return, February 05, 2020), holding that the limitation under Section 74(10) is mandatory and that the officer becomes functus officio on its expiry. The Revenue itself conceded the delay. The two decisions can be reconciled: in Deepak Sharma the lapse was admitted and apparent on the face of the record, whereas in Vyome Therapeutics the applicable limb itself was disputed and needed factual examination. A writ on limitation is thus realistic only where the time-bar is admitted or ex facie evident. Section 14 of the Limitation Act: An appeal under Section 107 must be filed within three months, with a further condonable period of one month under Section 107(4), and the Appellate Authority cannot condone delay beyond that, as held in Singh Enterprises v. CCE [(2008) 3 SCC 70]. Time spent bona fide in prosecuting an abortive proceeding can, however, be excluded on the principles of Section 14, as recognised in M.P. Steel Corporation v. CCE [(2015) 7 SCC 58]. Here, if limitation is reckoned from the date of the order itself (March 28, 2026), the four-month window had already lapsed by the time the writ was disposed of on August 12, 2026, so the appeal may be saved only through exclusion of the writ period. The direction to consider the application “pragmatically” is not a guarantee of exclusion, which would still depend on bona fides and due diligence being shown. Pari materia: Section 11A of the Central Excise Act, 1944, Section 73 of the Finance Act, 1994 (service tax) and Section 28 of the Customs Act, 1962 reckon limitation from the “relevant date”, which in the case of duty or tax erroneously refunded is the date of refund. The latter limb of Section 74(10) follows the same logic and, in our view, supports the refund date as the starting point for refund recoveries. Key takeaways: On receipt of a notice or order for recovery of an erroneous refund, immediately fix the date of the refund order, the nature of the demand and the limb of Section 74(10) or 73(10) that applies, and plead limitation in the reply and appeal grounds. Do not assume a writ will lie on limitation. It is realistic only where the time-bar is admitted or apparent on the record. File the Section 107 appeal within time. If a writ is already pending or has been disposed of, file the Section 14 application along with the appeal and document the bona fides.
