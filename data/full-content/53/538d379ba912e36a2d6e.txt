In this regard reliance is also placed on the following judicial pronouncements – 1. Hon’ble Supreme Court in case of Land Acquisition Collector vs. MST Katiji & Others 167 ITR 471 (SC) held that “it is on contention of delay that when substantial justice and technical considerations are pitted against each other, the case of substantial justice deserves to be preferred, for the other side cannot claim to have a vested right in injustice being done because of non-deliberate delay.” Keeping in view the discussion made in the preceding paras and following the ratio laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in case of MST Katiji & Others (supra), we are of the considered view that there is “sufficient cause” to condone the delay of 928 days in filing the appeal, hence the same is hereby condoned. So this appeal is ordered to be registered and taken up for disposal on merits. 2. N. Balakrishnan v. M. Krishnamurthy 1998 (7) SCC 123: The Supreme Court held that the expression “sufficient cause” must be considered with a pragmatic approach and that the cause shown for condonation of delay should be examined in the context of facts and circumstances of each case. 3. State of West Bengal v. Administrator, Howrah Municipality (1972 AIR 749) The Supreme Court observed that the words “sufficient cause” should receive a liberal construction, and the courts should lean in favor of condoning the delay if substantial justice is met. 4. Ramlal, Motilal and Chotelal v. Rewa Coalfields Ltd 1962 AIR 361: In this case, the Supreme Court held that the expression “sufficient cause” should be interpreted to advance substantial justice and should not be defeated by a rigid interpretation. 5. National Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Nicolletta Rohtagi & Ors Appeal Civil 4292 of 2002 order dated 17/09/2002: The Hon’ble Supreme Court emphasized that the approach of the court should be dynamic and not static while deciding applications for condonation of delay, taking into consideration the overall interest of justice. We accordingly condone the delay and take up the appeal for adjudication on merits. 3. Brief facts of the case: In this case, a TDS survey/verification u/s 133A(2A) of the Act was conducted on 03.03.2020. The DCIT(TDS), Kanpur passed an order u/s 201(1)/201(1A) of the Act and raised liability for non-deduction of tax u/s. 201(1) and interest u/s.201(1A) for F.Y. 2015-16 and calculated the amount of short deduction and interest as under: Sr no. The DCIT(TDS), Kanpur referred the case for considering penalty u/s 271C of the Act to the Addl. CIT(TDS)Kanpur. Accordingly, an opportunity of being heard was given to the deductor assessee vide show cause notice dated 20.04.2023. In compliance to show cause notice the deductor assessee submitted its reply on 08.05.2023. The Addl CIT(TDS)Kanpur, noted that the reply of assessee was not acceptable for the reason that deductor assessee had admitted its default on account of short deduction of tax and also paid tax of Rs. 1,58,275/- on 07.04.2023 as per the order u/s 201(1)/201(1A) of the Act, of the DCIT (TDS), Kanpur. In view of the above-mentioned facts, the Addl CIT(TDS)Kanpur held that the assessee is liable to penalty and it is a fit case for imposition of penalty u/s 271C of the Act for its failure to deduct the tax deducted at source and deposit it into the account of Govt. of India within the due dates as prescribed in the Act. Accordingly, the AO noted that since, the assessee had failed to deduct the tax deducted at source amounting to Rs.80,753/-, a penalty of Rs.80,753/-, was imposed u/s 271C of the Act, for F.Y. 2015-16 and the assessee was directed to pay the same. 4. Aggrieved with the said order, assessee filed an appeal before the learned CIT(A), who dismissed the appeal of the assesee. The relevant extract of the order of learned CIT(A) is reproduced as under : “5.4 After considering the entire factual matrix and submission of the assessee and case laws relied upon by the appellant, I do not find any force in its submission because first of all, the appellant has itself accepted that it is availing various services from M/s Hilton International LLC like management agreement, license agreement, royalty, franchises etc. It has also been accepted by the appellant that the appellant is using the trademark name of Hilton for its hotel. It has also been accepted by the appellant that it is deducting tax against various payments to M/s Hilton International LLC except this payment of commission. Therefore, I am of the view that Hilton International LLC is having a P.E. in India and its Income which accrues and arise in India is liable to be taxed in India. The appellant cannot have two standard for the same company that some payments it is considering the P.E. of M/s Hilton International LLC and deducting tax on some of its payments and while leave other payments and take shelter of non existent of P.E. Even otherwise, the use of trademark Hilton for its hotels itself proves that assessee / appellant is a P.E. of M/s Hilton International LLC. It is also not disputed that payment is made for commission of bookings in hotel owned by the appellant, therefore, this commission income of M/s Hilton International LLC certainly accrues and arise in India. In light of these facts, it is clear that such commission payments were liable to be taxed in India, thus, liable for TDS u/s 195 of the I.T. Act. As the appellant has failed to deduct that tax on these payments penalty u/s 271C is fully justified. Accordingly, the penalty on these payments is confirmed. 5.5 The second dispute is regarding the penalty u/s 271C of the I.T. Act for non deduction of TDS on professional services received from Hilton, Jaipur (a unit of Fruitful Buildcon Pvt. Ltd.). The appellant in its submission has mainly submitted that Hilton, Jaipur is a taxable entity in India and it has filed its return of income for that year and disclosed the receipts from appellant in its financial accounts, therefore, the appellant is of view that as taxes has been paid by recipients, the appellant cannot be considered assessee in default u/s 201 of the I.T. Act and accordingly the provisions of section 271C are not applicable. In its support, the appellant has relied on some judicial pronouncements. Without prejudice to this, the appellant has also stated that there were frequent changes in the key position in the accounts department of the appellant which has led two inadvertent oversight in TDS compliances without any intention thus, the appellant is of view that there was a reasonable cause for not deducting the tax on the said transaction and thus, prayed that penalty should be deleted. 5.6 From the submission of the appellant, it is apparent that the appellant has accepted there were payments to Hilton, Jaipur which were liable to be Tax Deduction at Source and the appellant has failed in its duty to deduct the due tax thereon. The decision placed by the appellant regarding the 201(1) are of no use as they are regarding only the recovery of the TDS, however, paying of tax by the receiver cannot absolve the appellant from its statutory duty of tax deduction. There are plethora of decision which states that wherever the recipients has disclosed the amount in its return of income and pay due taxes thereon, though, payer cannot be held as assessee in default for principle amount u/s 201(1) of the I.T. Act, however, payer is liable to pay the interest u/s 201(1A) and the penalty u/s 271C on the TDS defaulted. Therefore, the assessee in default for non deduction of TDS and thus, liable for penalty under section 271C of the I.T. Act. The case laws given by the appellant are of no use from the present facts of the case. Now coming to the reasonable cause given by the appellant. The appellant is running a five star hotel named as Hotel Double Tree by Hilton, Agra and is run professionally with the help from even the multinational companies like M/s Hilton International LLC for management etc. In such a big company, professionally managed the plea of leaving one or two personal cannot be sustained for non compliance of its statutory duties. Therefore, the plea of reasonable cause regarding leaving of few employees do not hold water and liable to be rejected. Therefore, the penalty u/s 271C on these payments to Hilton, Jaipur also deserves to be confirmed. 6. In the result, the appeal of the appellant dismissed.” 5. Aggrieved with the said order, the assessee is in appeal before us. The assessee filed the appeal on ground No. 1 to 8. Further during the course of appellate proceedings, the assessee filed a revised grounds of appeal which are reproduced as under : “1. That the Ld. CIT(A)-5, NFAC has erred both on facts and in law in confirming the penalty under section 271C of the Income-tax Act, 1961 amounting to 80,753/- without appreciating that the alleged failure to deduct tax at source was based on a bona fide interpretation of law and was, therefore, covered by the exception of reasonable cause under section 273B of the Act. 2. That the Ld. Consequently, the commission paid for overseas marketing services was not chargeable to tax in India and the provisions of section 195 were not attracted. 3. That the Ld. As no loss of revenue was caused to the Revenue, the levy of penalty under section 271C is unsustainable in law. 4. That the Ld. At the outset, the ld. AR submitted that in respect of payment of Rs.2,12,502/- on account of professional fees paid to Hilton, Jaipur, the assessee submitted that Hilton Jaipur had duly disclosed the entire receipts from the assessee company in the financial statements and it filed its Income Tax Return for A.Y. 2016-17 and had also paid due taxes. In this regard, the learned AR referring to the memorandum of Finance Act, 2012, whereby the first proviso to section 201(1) was introduced vide Finance Act, 2012, submitted that in view of the said provision, since Hilton Jaipur had included the receipts of Rs.2,12,502/- in its income and paid the taxes, the assessee will not be in default for non-deduction of TDS on the said payment and therefore will be not liable for penalty u/s 271C of the Act for its default on the non-deduction of TDS on the payment of Rs. 2,12,502/- to Hilton Jaipur. In this regard, the learned AR relied upon the decision of Hon’ble High Court in the case of CIT vs. Cadburry India Limited, 11 taxmann.com 66 (Delhi), to submit that in this case it was held that when there is no demand u/s. 201(1) of the Act, the provisions of section 271C were not applicable. 6.1 Regarding the commission payment of Rs. 5,77,701/- to Hilton International LLC, the learned CIT(A) confirmed the penalty on the ground that Hilton International LLC is having a Permanent Establishment (PE) in India and its income accrues and arises in India and therefore, liable to be taxed in India. The learned CIT(A) also noted that the assessee accepted deducting tax against various payments to Hilton International LLC except this payment of commission and the ld. CIT(A) noted that the assessee cannot have two standards for the same company. 6.2 In this regard, the learned AR submitted that it disputes the finding of the learned CIT(A) that M/s. Hilton International LLC had a PE in India. Further, the ld. AR submitted that the appellant had a bona fide belief that while deducting TDS in respect of other payments of Hilton International LLC, there was no requirement to deduct TDS in respect of the commission payment made to Hilton International LLC. In this regard, the assessee relied upon the decision of Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of CIT vs. Eli Lilly & Co. (India)(P) Ltd. (2009) 312 ITR 225 (SC), and submitted that in this case, the Hon’ble Apex Court held that no penalty shall be imposed on the person or the assessee for failure to deduct tax at source if the said person or the assessee proves that there was a reasonable cause for the said failure. 6.3 The Ld. It was further submitted that the frequent changes in the key positions in the ‘Accounts Department’ led to disruption in the working which may have led to some inadvertent oversights in TDS compliances, without any intention of TDS default. In this regard, the relevant extract of the said submission is reproduced as under: “4.2 It is reiterated that the assessee company had started its operations during December-2014. In initial periods, employee turnover was high. On the other hand, the LD Sr. DR supported the orders of the authorities below. 8. We have heard both the parties and perused the material on record. In this case, the Ld. AR submitted that in respect of payment of Rs.2,12,502/- on account of professional fee paid to Hilton, Jaipur (a unit of Fruitful Buildcon Private Limited), Hilton Jaipur had duly disclosed the entire receipts from the assessee company in the financial statements and had filed its Income Tax Return for A.Y. 2016-17 and had also paid due taxes. Further, we notice that the conduct of the assessee in deducting tax in respect of other payments made by the assessee to Hilton International LLC, but not deducting TDS on commission payment of Rs.5,77,701/- under a bonafide belief that no such TDS was deductible on such payments, is found to be reasonable. But more importantly in this case we are satisfied by the explanation given by the assessee before the Ld. CIT(A) that due to various factors as mentioned therein led to disruption in the working which may have led to some inadvertent oversights in TDS compliances, without any intention of TDS default. In this regard, we do not agree with the view of the Ld. CIT(A) on this issue and we rely upon the order of the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of CIT, New Delhi vs. Eli Lilly & Co. (India) (P.) Ltd. (supra). The relevant observation of the Apex Court in para no. 35 is reproduced as under: “(iv) On the Scope of section 271C read with section 273B: 35. Section 271C inter alia states that if any person fails to deduct the whole or any part of the tax as required by the provisions of Chapter XVII-B then such person shall be liable to pay, by way of penalty, a sum equal to the amount of tax which such person failed to deduct. In these cases we are concerned with section 271C(1)(a). Thus section 271C(1)(a) makes it clear that the penalty leviable shall be equal to the amount of tax which such person failed to deduct. We cannot hold this provision to be mandatory or compensatory or automatic because under section 273B Parliament has enacted that penalty shall not be imposed in cases falling thereunder. Section 271C falls in the category of such cases. Section 273B states that notwithstanding anything contained in section 271C, no penalty shall be imposed on the person or the assessee for failure to deduct tax at source if such person or the assessee proves that there was a reasonable cause for the said failure. Therefore, the liability to levy of penalty can be fastened only on the person who do not have good and sufficient reason for not deducting tax at source. Only those persons will be liable to penalty who do not have good and sufficient reason for not deducting the tax. The burden, of course, is on the person to prove such good and sufficient reason. In each of the 104 cases before us, we find that non-deduction of tax at source took place on account of controversial addition. The concept of aggregation or consolidation of the entire income chargeable under the head “Salaries” being exigible to deduction of tax at source under section 192 was a nascent issue. It has not be considered by this Court before. Further, in most of these cases, the tax-deductor-assessee has not claimed deduction under section 40(a)(iii) in computation of its business income. This is one more reason for not imposing penalty under section 271C because by not claiming deduction under section 40(a)(iii), in some cases, higher corporate tax has been paid to the extent of Rs. 906.52 lakhs (see Civil Appeal No. 1778/2006 entitled CIT v. Bank of Tokyo-Mitsubishi Ltd. In some of the cases, it is undisputed that each of the expatriate employees have paid directly the taxes due on the foreign salary by way of advance tax/self-assessment tax. The tax-deductor-assessee was under a genuine and bona fide belief that it was not under any obligation to deduct tax at source from the home salary paid by the foreign company/HO and, consequently, we are of the view that in none of the 104 cases penalty was leviable under section 271C as the respondent in each case has discharged its burden of showing reasonable cause for failure to deduct tax at source.” (emphasis supplied by us) 8.1 Relying upon the ratio of the above- mentioned case, for the reasons as stated by the assessee, we are satisfied that the assessee had a reasonable cause for the said failure. Accordingly, we hold that the levy of penalty u/s 271C of the Act imposed in this case is not sustainable and we delete the penalty of Rs 80,753/- in this case. Ground no. 1 of the appeal is allowed. In view of ground no. 1 of the appeal being allowed, the other grounds of appeal become academic and are left open in this case. 9. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed.
