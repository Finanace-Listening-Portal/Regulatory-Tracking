Summary: Indian residents holding qualifying Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) in foreign entities must review their Annual Performance Report (APR) obligations under the Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Regulations, 2022. The general reporting deadline is 31 December 2026, subject to the foreign entity’s accounting year and applicable exceptions. APR compliance may apply to resident individuals, Indian companies, LLPs and registered partnership firms investing in overseas subsidiaries or joint ventures. Certain investments involving holdings below 10% without control, foreign entities under liquidation and investments classified as Overseas Portfolio Investment (OPI) may be exempt. APR is generally based on audited financial statements, with a limited relaxation where the Indian investor lacks control and the host jurisdiction does not mandate an audit. Filing is undertaken through the designated Authorised Dealer Bank, using the prescribed APR format and supporting financial information. Delayed filing generally attracts a Late Submission Fee of ₹7,500 per APR, subject to the applicable regularisation framework. Unresolved defaults may restrict further financial commitments and transfers of overseas investments. Investors should also examine related Form FC, FLA Return and income-tax foreign asset disclosure requirements. Don’t miss your FEMA compliance deadline! If you, your company, LLP or partnership firm hold an Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) in a foreign entity, filing of the Annual Performance Report (APR) with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) through your Authorised Dealer (AD) Bank is an important annual compliance requirement. Due Date: 31 December 2026 Let’s understand who needs to file, applicable exemptions, documents required and the consequences of non-compliance. The principal framework is contained in the Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Rules, 2022, Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Regulations, 2022 and Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Directions, 2022. 1. Who Is Required to File APR? APR compliance generally applies to Indian residents who have made ODI in a foreign entity, including: Resident individuals holding qualifying ODI in foreign entities. Indian companies having foreign subsidiaries or joint ventures. LLPs and registered partnership firms having overseas direct investments. When Does an Investment Qualify as ODI? An investment generally qualifies as ODI where: Investment is made in an unlisted foreign entity, irrespective of the percentage of shareholding. Investment represents 10% or more of the equity capital of a listed foreign entity. The Indian investor has control over a listed foreign entity, even if the shareholding is below 10%. The statutory definition also includes subscription to the memorandum of association of a foreign entity. Classification must be determined in accordance with the applicable FEMA provisions. Important: APR is generally required separately for each foreign entity and continues annually until the investment is fully disinvested or an applicable exemption arises. Where more than one Indian resident has made ODI in the same foreign entity, the investor holding the highest stake generally undertakes APR reporting. Where holdings are equal, joint filing is permitted under the prescribed framework. A dormant foreign subsidiary, an entity incurring losses or an entity having no business turnover is not automatically exempt from APR filing. 2. Who Is Exempt from APR Filing? APR is generally not required in the following circumstances: The Indian resident holds less than 10% equity, without exercising control, and has no other financial commitment except equity capital. The foreign entity is under liquidation. The investment is classified as Overseas Portfolio Investment (OPI), rather than ODI. ODI and OPI: Important Distinction Examples of OPI may include qualifying investments in listed foreign shares, listed employer shares received under ESOP/RSU arrangements and overseas mutual funds. Important distinction: Classification depends on the FEMA provisions and nature of investment, not merely on the platform or route through which the investment was made. The relevant classifications are contained in the FEMA Overseas Investment Rules, 2022. Even where APR is not applicable, foreign assets and relevant foreign income may still require disclosure in Schedule FA and Schedule FSI of the Indian Income Tax Return, as applicable. 3. APR Due Date – 31 December 2026 The reporting deadline depends on the accounting year followed by the foreign entity. Foreign Entity Accounting Year-End APR Due Date 31 March 2026 31 December 2026 30 June 2026 31 December 2026 30 September 2026 31 December 2026 30 November 2026 31 December 2026 31 December 2025 31 December 2026 31 December 2026 31 December 2027 Plan ahead: Obtain the foreign entity’s financial statements and supporting information well before the due date. The reporting requirement is governed by Regulation 10 of the FEMA Overseas Investment Regulations, 2022. 4. Audited or Unaudited Financial Statements? The APR is generally required to be prepared based on the audited financial statements of the foreign entity. An exception is available where: The Indian investor does not have control over the foreign entity; and The law of the foreign jurisdiction does not require a statutory audit. In such cases, unaudited financial statements may be accepted, certified as required under RBI directions by the statutory auditor of the Indian entity or a Chartered Accountant where statutory audit is not applicable. Where the Indian investor exercises control over the foreign entity, audited financial statements are required. The certification framework is discussed in the FEMA Overseas Investment Directions, 2022. 5. Documents Required for APR Filing To ensure timely filing, the following documents and information should be arranged for each foreign entity: 1. UIN (Unique Identification Number) allotted by RBI and details of the AD Bank. 2. Copy of the previous year’s APR and original ODI reporting/Form FC records. 3. Financial statements of the foreign entity – Balance Sheet, Statement of Profit & Loss and Notes to Accounts. 4. Foreign entity’s shareholding pattern and percentage of Indian ownership. 5. Details of dividends, interest, royalty, technical fees and other income received, along with supporting bank documents/FIRC, wherever applicable. 6. Details of acquisition, formation, transfer or winding up of any Step-Down Subsidiary (SDS). 7. Details of loans, guarantees and other financial commitments extended to the foreign entity. 8. Number of employees and brief description of business activities. 9. Investor’s KYC documents, including PAN and address proof. 10. Authorised signatory details in case of companies and LLPs. 6. APR Filing Process The general filing process involves: Step 1: Collect and Verify Documents Collect and verify the financial statements and supporting documents. Step 2: Prepare the APR Prepare the Annual Performance Report in the prescribed format. Step 3: Obtain Certification Obtain the requisite CA certification, wherever applicable. Step 4: Submit Through the AD Bank Complete the declaration and submit the APR through the designated AD Bank. Step 5: Retain Acknowledgement Obtain and retain the bank’s acknowledgement of submission. The reporting process and related requirements are covered in the RBI Master Direction on Overseas Investment. 7. Consequences of Delay or Non-Filing Non-compliance with FEMA reporting requirements can create difficulties for future overseas transactions. Late Submission Fee (LSF) APR reporting delay generally attracts an LSF of ₹7,500 per delayed APR filing. The LSF facility is available within a maximum period of three years from the applicable due date. Beyond the permitted period, the default may require regularisation through the RBI compounding mechanism, as applicable. The prescribed LSF framework is provided in the FEMA Overseas Investment Directions, 2022 and the RBI Master Direction on Overseas Investment. Other Implications Restrictions on further financial commitments towards the foreign entity. Restrictions on transfer or disinvestment of overseas investments until reporting defaults are regularised. Additional regulatory complications and delays in future remittances. These restrictions are addressed in Regulation 12 of the FEMA Overseas Investment Regulations, 2022. 8. Other FEMA Compliances to Remember Apart from APR, Indian entities having overseas investments should also review the following compliances: FLA Return FLA Return: Annual Foreign Liabilities and Assets Return, generally due by 15 July, subject to RBI notifications or extensions. Applicability must be assessed based on the outstanding foreign assets/liabilities and prescribed criteria. Form FC Form FC: Reporting of fresh ODI and additional financial commitments through the designated AD Bank. Schedule FA / FSI Schedule FA / FSI: Disclosure of applicable foreign assets and foreign-source income in the Income Tax Return. Don’t Wait Until the Last Date!
