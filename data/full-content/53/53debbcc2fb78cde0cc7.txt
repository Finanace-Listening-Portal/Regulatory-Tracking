The outstanding amount was Rs1.43 lakh crore in August 2025. Growth was last higher in June 2023, when advances against fixed deposits rose 47% year-on-year. Advt The increase coincided with a large mobilisation of FCNR(B) deposits under the RBI's special forex swap facility. Banks mobilised $132.98 billion through FCNR(B) deposits under the facility by August 31, when the RBI closed the window ahead of its original September 30 deadline. Total inflows through FCNR(B), overseas foreign currency borrowings and external commercial borrowings stood at $143.596 billion by September 18.The RBI had clarified in June that banks could lend against FCNR(B) deposits mobilised under the special facility, leaving the extent of leverage to individual lenders. Some banks offered leverage of several times the underlying deposit, allowing customers to borrow against the deposit and redeploy the funds into FCNR(B) deposits.The RBI's data, however, does not separately identify loans against FCNR(B) deposits. The advances-against-fixed-deposits category also includes loans against NRNR and other deposits. As a result, the precise contribution of leveraged FCNR(B) transactions to the Rs2.04 lakh crore outstanding cannot be established from the published data.The huge scaleThe scale of the FCNR(B) mobilisation nevertheless suggests that such transactions could have become a significant component of the increase. S&P Global Ratings estimates that Indian banks' balance sheets could ultimately expand by $190 billion-$220 billion from the $127 billion of FCNR(B) deposits raised between June 8 and August 31, assuming 50-75% of the deposits are leveraged through loans against pledged FCNR(B) deposits. Advt The leveraged transactions are also changing the economics of the FCNR(B) inflow. Loans against pledged FCNR(B) deposits generally carry very low spreads, while the deposits themselves add to banks' funding costs. S&P estimates that offshore lending against pledged FCNR(B) deposits can earn spreads of around 25-50 basis points. Such lending carries little or no capital charge when fully secured by the borrower's own FCNR(B) deposit, making it capital-efficient even though it can dilute net interest margins.The surge in loans against deposits remains small relative to the overall banking system. The Rs2.04 lakh crore outstanding in August was less than 1% of total bank credit of about Rs224 lakh crore. Loans against fixed deposits are also commonly used for short-term liquidity because borrowers can raise funds without breaking their deposits. Advt The FCNR(B) inflows have simultaneously created a substantial liquidity surplus for banks. The $127 billion raised through FCNR(B) deposits between June 8 and August 31 was equivalent to about 4.5% of the banking system's deposit base as of March 31, according to S&P. The deposits have maturities of three to five years, providing banks with medium-term foreign-currency funding but also creating a concentration of maturities in 2029-31.The immediate effect is therefore not simply an increase in conventional credit demand. A portion of the FCNR(B) money is being converted into additional balance-sheet assets through loans secured by the deposits themselves, creating a capital-efficient but low-spread lending channel alongside the broader liquidity boost from the foreign-currency inflows.
