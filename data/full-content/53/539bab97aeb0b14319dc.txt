Summary : The Income-tax (Eighth Amendment) Rules, 2026, notified on 30 October 2026, insert rule 189A and Form No. 112A under the Income-tax Act, 2025. A registered non-profit organisation can merge into another registered NPO with the same or similar objects without triggering the specified merger consequence only when the detailed statutory conditions are satisfied. Regularly registered and provisionally registered organisations cannot cross-merge; undisputed outstanding demand must be cleared; assets, liabilities and tax obligations move to the successor; related persons must not benefit; and the merged organisation’s objects are locked for five years from the end of the financial year of merger. Both organisations must have their books audited as on the merger date. The merged NPO must file Form 112A electronically, with prescribed annexures, within one month from the end of the month in which the merger takes effect. This practical guide turns the rule into a transaction timetable and evidence file for trustees, finance teams and advisers. It was published on 30 October 2026 and comes into force on the date of publication. The rule operates for sections 352(4), Table serial number 8, and 354A of the Income-tax Act, 2025. The practical benefit is not automatic: the merging and merged organisations must both be registered NPOs with the same or similar objects, and every relevant condition must be planned and evidenced. A transaction may be valid under trust, society or company law yet fail the income-tax route. Conversely, filing Form 112A cannot cure a structural incompatibility, prohibited status combination or benefit to a related person. The board or trustees should therefore approve a tax-condition memorandum before the merger becomes effective, not wait until the form deadline. Registration Status Must Match on Both Sides A registered NPO registered under section 332 cannot merge with an NPO that is only provisionally registered under that section, and a provisionally registered NPO cannot merge with a regularly registered NPO. This two-way prohibition requires current registration orders for both entities. Names, PANs, effective periods and conditions on those orders should be reconciled before documents are signed. The rule also requires the organisations to have the same or similar objects. Similarity should be demonstrated through a clause-by-clause comparison of the operative trust deeds, memoranda or governing instruments and actual activities. A broad statement that both entities are charitable may be inadequate if their beneficiary groups, geography, religious character or methods differ materially. The due-diligence file should obtain the portal demand position, orders, payment challans and reconciliation to the books. An unrecorded order or unmatched challan can undermine the condition even when the amount is small. Where an appeal is pending, the merged NPO must pay the outstanding demand when the appeal attains finality. The successor should record the contingent amount, proceeding, stay status, hearing stage, counsel and funding plan. The merger agreement and governing-body resolution should expressly transfer this obligation rather than leave it as an administrative assumption. Universal Transfer and Continuing Proceedings All assets and liabilities of the merging NPO become those of the merged NPO from the merger date. Existing assessment, reassessment or other proceedings are deemed to continue against the successor; proceedings for the merger tax year up to the merger date and earlier tax years may also be made on the merged NPO. A litigation register, digital records index and authority matrix should move with the transaction so that notices are not missed after the old organisation stops operating. Five-Year Object Lock and Continuing Tax Duties The objects of the merged NPO cannot be amended for five years from the end of the financial year in which the merger occurs. If a merger takes effect during FY 2026-27, the restriction runs from the end of that financial year for the prescribed five-year period. The governance calendar should flag the lock, and any future amendment proposal should first be tested against rule 189A and the underlying statutory consequence. The successor assumes the merging NPO’s obligations. It must give effect to current income application under section 341 and accumulated income under section 342, bear specified-income tax under section 334 where applicable, comply with section 340 conditions, invest funds in the modes specified by section 350, and pay additional income-tax under section 352 if a liability arises. Contribution data of the merging NPO also remains relevant for the threshold calculation identified in Form 112A. Form 112A Annexure A-4 also calls for audited books of both entities as on that date and for the financial year immediately preceding the tax year in which the merger occurs. This is not satisfied merely by attaching the last annual financial statements. A merger-date close should cover cash, investments, restricted funds, grants, receivables, liabilities, related-party balances, property records, donor conditions and contingencies. Inter-entity balances need documented elimination or settlement. The audit file should reconcile the audited closing balances of the merging NPO to the successor’s opening merger ledger and to the asset-and-liability schedule approved by the governing bodies. Form 112A Deadline and Electronic Filing The merged NPO must intimate its jurisdictional Principal Commissioner or Commissioner in Form 112A within one month from the end of the month in which the merger takes effect. A merger effective on 7 November 2026 would therefore require the filing within one month from 30 November 2026; the statutory calculation should be checked on the portal and against later instructions before submission. The form is furnished electronically under digital signature or electronic verification code. The form records the identity and contact details of both organisations and the merger date. The declaration identifies the governing-body resolution and contains undertakings on the five-year object lock, income application, accumulated income, depreciation, specified income, contributions, section 340 conditions, permitted investment modes and additional income-tax. The signatory should read each undertaking against the closing file rather than treating the declaration as standard text. Mandatory Annexures A-1: copies of the trust deeds or governing instruments of both organisations. A-2: copies of the registration grants of both organisations. A-3: the trustees’, governing body’s or management’s merger resolution. A-4: audited books of both organisations as on the merger date and for the immediately preceding financial year specified in the form. A-5: the reasons for undertaking the merger. A separate working file: demand reconciliation, appeal register, object comparison and related-person review. Post-Merger Controls Should Preserve Separate Histories The successor should not collapse the merging NPO’s history into a single undifferentiated balance. Separate cost centres or ledger tags should preserve restricted funds, accumulated income, donor conditions, tax proceedings and asset provenance. Document retention and notice-monitoring responsibilities should be assigned by name. Bank, property, grant and statutory registrations may have their own change processes that are not completed merely by filing Form 112A. A quarterly integration review should test investment modes, utilisation of accumulated income, related-person transactions, pending demands and proceedings, and progress on legal transfers. The five-year object lock should be built into governance templates. A later reorganisation, deed amendment or programme shift should be escalated before approval because it may disturb the conditions relied upon at merger. Frequently Asked Questions When did rule 189A come into force? The Eighth Amendment Rules state that they come into force on the date of publication, 30 October 2026. Can a regularly registered NPO merge with a provisionally registered NPO? No. Rule 189A prohibits cross-mergers between regular and provisional section 332 registrations. How similar must the objects be? The rule requires the same or similar objects. The parties should retain a reasoned comparison of governing clauses and actual activities. What happens to an appealed tax demand? The merged NPO must pay the outstanding demand when the appeal attains finality, subject to the final outcome. Can the merged NPO change its objects soon after merger? The rule imposes a five-year restriction measured from the end of the financial year in which the merger occurs. Who files Form 112A and by when? The merged NPO files it electronically within one month from the end of the month in which the merger takes effect. Are the last annual audited accounts sufficient? No. Both organisations must also have audited books as on the merger date, and Annexure A-4 requests the specified preceding-year audited books. Do pending assessments disappear with the merging NPO? No. The rule carries proceedings and tax responsibility to the merged organisation. Key Takeaways Both entities must have compatible section 332 registration status and same or similar objects. Undisputed demands must be cleared, while pending-appeal obligations transfer to the successor. Assets, liabilities, tax duties and proceedings continue in the merged NPO. Both entities require merger-date audits and a fully reconciled transfer file.
