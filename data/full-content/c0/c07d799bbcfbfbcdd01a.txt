The decline coincided with a sharp increase in bank deposits as the FCNR(B) window closed on August 31, indicating that the inflow of foreign currency deposits reduced banks' immediate reliance on short-term wholesale funding, according to CareEdge Ratings. Advt Bank deposits rose by Rs 9.40 lakh crore during the fortnight to Rs 278.7 lakh crore, taking year-on-year deposit growth to 18.6% from 14.7% at mid-August and above 15.4% at the end of July. The increase was the fastest since December 2016, with FCNR(B) inflows reaching $127.2 billion by August 31 as banks stepped up mobilisation ahead of the deadline.The sharp deposit accretion narrowed the gap between credit and deposit growth to about 149 basis points from 355 basis points at mid-August. The loan-to-deposit ratio fell to 80.3% from 81.7%, marking the sharpest fortnightly decline since March 2026 and taking it to its lowest level since October 2025.Usage still highThe decline in CD funding came even as outstanding CDs remained 28.5% higher year-on-year, indicating that banks continued to use the instrument but had less immediate need for fresh wholesale funding. The banking system had a liquidity surplus of more than Rs 5 lakh crore at the end of August, further reducing the need for market-based short-term borrowing.CD issuance rates also showed a narrower spread during the fortnight. The lower-end rate rose to 6.24% from 5.25%, while the upper-end rate eased to 7.21% from 7.32%, narrowing the spread to 0.97 percentage point from 2.07 percentage points. The low volume of issuance, however, makes these ranges less representative. Advt The deposit increase was concentrated in time deposits, which accounted for about Rs 8.03 lakh crore of the fortnightly accretion. Time deposits grew 18.5% year-on-year to Rs 244.2 lakh crore and accounted for 87.6% of total deposits, while demand deposits grew 19.3% to Rs 34.5 lakh crore.The special FCNR(B) concessional swap window has now closed, although the ECB and overseas foreign currency borrowing windows remain open until December 31, 2026. Deposit growth is expected to moderate from September, making sustained domestic deposit mobilisation a key monitorable.The report expects bank credit growth to moderate from current levels as favourable base effects fade in the second half of FY27, with full-year growth projected at 15-16%.
