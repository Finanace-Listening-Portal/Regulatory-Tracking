Summary: Input Tax Credit (ITC) under the CGST Act, 2017 is governed by statutory eligibility conditions and cannot be claimed merely on the strength of an invoice or its appearance in an auto-drafted GST statement. This article examines the legal distinction between GSTR-2A, a dynamic statement of inward supplies, and GSTR-2B, a period-specific ITC statement used for reconciliation and credit availment. It analyses Sections 16(1), 16(2)(aa), 16(2)(b), 16(2)(c), 16(4) and 17(5), together with Rule 36(4), explaining how supplier reporting, receipt of goods or services, payment of tax and statutory restrictions affect ITC entitlement. The discussion covers delayed supplier reporting, invoice mismatches, duplicate credit, blocked ITC and the importance of reconciling purchase registers with GST portal data. It also examines the Invoice Management System (IMS), introduced in October 2024, and the consequences of accepting, rejecting, keeping pending or taking no action on supplier-reported documents. Judicial decisions involving D.Y. Beathel Enterprises and Suncraft Energy are discussed in the context of supplier defaults and bona fide purchasers. The article concludes with a practical pre-filing checklist covering invoice verification, supplier follow-up, credit-note adjustments, the 180-day payment condition and GSTR-3B reconciliation. Particular attention is given to the distinction between statutory entitlement to ITC and the evidentiary or procedural role of GST portal statements. Table of Contents▸ 1. INTRODUCTION 2. INTRODUCTION The Goods and Services Tax regime in India is fundamentally premised on the seamless flow of Input Tax Credit (ITC). The entire edifice of GST rests upon the principle that tax should be levied only on value addition, and not on tax itself. However, the right to avail ITC is not an absolute or vested right. It is a statutory concession, circumscribed by strict conditions laid down under the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (hereinafter referred to as “the CGST Act”). In this context, two auto-drafted statements – GSTR-2A and GSTR-2B – have assumed paramount significance. While both emanate from the data furnished by the supplier, their legal character, evidentiary value, and consequences are materially different. The confusion between GSTR-2A and GSTR-2B has led to thousands of litigations, show cause notices under Section 73 / 74, and reversal of ITC running into crores. This article seeks to demystify the legal distinction in a structured, practitioner-oriented manner. 2. LEGAL FRAMEWORK GOVERNING ITC The claim of ITC is primarily governed by the following provisions: a) Section 16(1) – Eligibility Every registered person shall be entitled to take credit of input tax charged on any supply of goods or services which are used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of his business, subject to conditions and restrictions. b) Section 16(2)(aa) – The Most Crucial Condition [Inserted w.e.f. 01.01.2022] “The details of the invoice or debit note… has been furnished by the supplier in the statement of outward supplies and such details have been communicated to the recipient of such invoice or debit note in the manner specified under Section 37.” This provision makes the reflection of invoice in GSTR-2B a mandatory pre-condition for availment of ITC. Mere possession of a tax invoice is no longer sufficient. c) Section 16(2)(b) The recipient must have actually received the goods or services or both. d) Section 16(2)(c) The tax charged in respect of such supply has been actually paid to the Government, either in cash or through utilisation of ITC. e) Section 16(4) Time limit for availment – ITC for a financial year must be availed on or before 30th November following the end of such financial year, or the date of furnishing of annual return, whichever is earlier. f) Section 17(5) Blocked Credits – ITC is expressly barred on motor vehicles, food and beverages, personal consumption, works contract for immovable property, etc., even if reflected in GSTR-2B. g) Rule 36(4) – [Omitted w.e.f. 01.01.2022] Earlier, provisional ITC of 5% / 10% / 20% was permitted. Now, with the omission of Rule 36(4), no provisional ITC is permitted. ITC can be availed only to the extent reflected in GSTR-2B. 3. GSTR-2A – REAL-TIME AUTO UPDATING STATEMENT GSTR-2A is an auto-populated, dynamic statement generated on the GST Portal on a real-time basis. Legal Character It is a statement of inward supplies, reflecting details furnished by the supplier in GSTR-1, GSTR-5, GSTR-6, etc. It is governed by Section 37 read with Rule 59. Key Features It keeps on updating continuously as and when the supplier uploads, amends, or deletes an invoice. It is a tool for tracking and reconciliation of purchases. It helps the recipient identify missing invoices and follow up with the supplier. GSTR-2A is not required to be filed. It is only for viewing and reconciliation. GSTR-2A cannot be the sole basis for claiming ITC. The Department cannot demand ITC merely because an invoice appears in GSTR-2A, nor can the assessee claim ITC merely because it appears in GSTR-2A. Illustration-1 : If a supplier uploads an invoice dated 07.11.2026 on 16.12.2026, it will immediately appear in the recipient’s GSTR-2A for November, but it will not form part of November GSTR-2B. Illustration-2 : If a supplier uploads an invoice dated 05.10.2026 on 15.11.2026, it will immediately appear in the recipient’s GSTR-2A for November, but it will not form part of November GSTR-2B. 4. GSTR-2B – A FROZEN STATEMENT GSTR-2B is an auto-drafted, frozen/fixed ITC statement generated for a particular tax period. Legal Character It is generated on the 14th day of the succeeding month, based on the data furnished by the supplier up to the cut-off date. It is the sole statutory document for determining eligible ITC for that period. Key Features Once generated, it does not change, even if the supplier later amends the invoice. The amendment will reflect in the next month’s GSTR-2B. It is meant for ITC Reconciliation and Availment. It bifurcates ITC into “ITC Available” and “ITC Not Available” (e.g., Section 17(5), reverse charge, etc.). GSTR-2B is also not required to be filed, but it is mandatory to be considered before filing GSTR-3B. GSTR-2B is the conclusive basis for claiming ITC under Section 16(2)(aa). If ITC is not reflected in GSTR-2B, the claim is liable to be denied, even if the recipient has a valid tax invoice and has made payment. Practical Illustration of Delayed Supplier Reporting Consider this common factual matrix: Invoice Date: 05th November Supplier Uploads in GSTR-1: 15th December Consequence: Invoice will appear in GSTR-2A immediately, but will NOT appear in November GSTR-2B. It will appear in December GSTR-2B. Many assessees erroneously claim ITC in November on the basis of GSTR-2A, leading to mismatch in GSTR-3B vs GSTR-2B, resulting in issuance of Form GST DRC-01 and demand of tax along with interest under Section 50 and penalty under Section 73/74. Legal Advice: ITC must always be claimed in the month in which it appears in GSTR-2B, and not in the month of invoice date. 5. INVOICE MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (IMS) – THE NEW PARADIGM [w.e.f. October 2024] The introduction of the Invoice Management System (IMS) has added a third layer of compliance. IMS allows the recipient to take action on supplier-reported invoices: Accept: Invoice accepted – ITC will flow to GSTR-2B. Reject: Invoice rejected – ITC will not flow to GSTR-2B, and supplier’s liability will be reduced. Pending: No action taken – Invoice will be deemed accepted and will flow to GSTR-2B. Legal Significance: If the recipient fails to act on IMS, a wrong invoice uploaded by the supplier may be deemed accepted, leading to future litigation. Conversely, if a genuine invoice is wrongly rejected, ITC will be lost. Therefore, IMS action is now as critical as GSTR-3B filing itself. Golden Rule for IMS: Before the 14th of every month, reconcile IMS. Your action / inaction on IMS directly determines your GSTR-2B. 6. COMMON MISTAKES THAT LEAD TO LITIGATION As a practitioner, the following errors are repeatedly observed in audits and scrutiny: 1. Claiming ITC merely because invoice appears in GSTR-2B, without verifying receipt of goods, payment to supplier within 180 days (Second Proviso to Section 16(2)), and Section 17(5) blockage. 2. Ignoring Purchase Register Reconciliation – GSTR-2B is not a substitute for books of accounts. 3. Not following up on Missing Invoices – Where invoice is in books but not in GSTR-2B, the recipient must pursue the supplier to file GSTR-1, rather than claiming ITC provisionally. 4. Claiming Duplicate ITC on both original invoice and amended invoice. 5. Ignoring Credit Notes reflected in GSTR-2B, leading to excess ITC claim. 6. Not reversing ITC where payment to supplier is not made within 180 days. 7. JUDICIAL PRONOUNCEMENTS – EVOLVING JURISPRUDENCE The Hon’ble Courts have taken a nuanced view: In M/s. D.Y. Beathel Enterprises v. State Tax Officer, the Madras High Court held that ITC cannot be denied solely for supplier’s default if the recipient is bona fide and has fulfilled all other conditions. In Suncraft Energy Pvt. Ltd. v. Assistant Commissioner, the Calcutta High Court observed that the Revenue must first proceed against the defaulting supplier before denying ITC to a bona fide purchaser. The settled position as on date: While Courts provide relief in genuine cases, the Department is bound by Section 16(2)(aa). The assessee must first prove – (i) Genuine transaction, (ii) Payment through banking channel, (iii) Receipt of goods, (iv) Supplier’s filing default is not attributable to the recipient. Mere production of invoice is insufficient. 8. BEST PRACTICE – CHECKLIST BEFORE FILING GSTR-3B Before filing GSTR-3B every month, every registered person must follow this mandatory protocol: Step 1: Reconcile Purchase Register + GSTR-2A + GSTR-2B + IMS Step 2: Verify invoice, e-invoice, e-way bill, receipt of goods, and eligibility under Section 17(5) Step 3: Ensure payment to supplier within 180 days Step 4: Take action on IMS – Accept / Reject / Pending Step 5: Claim ITC only as per GSTR-2B (ITC Available Table), after above verification 9. CONCLUSION GSTR-2A is a mirror of supplier’s compliance. GSTR-2B is the legal gateway to ITC. IMS is the lock on that gateway. A taxpayer who claims ITC without reconciling all three is inviting litigation, interest, and penalty. A taxpayer who meticulously reconciles them is litigation-proof. In the post-IMS era, GST compliance is no longer merely about filing returns on time. It is about active management of ITC every month, before the 14th.
