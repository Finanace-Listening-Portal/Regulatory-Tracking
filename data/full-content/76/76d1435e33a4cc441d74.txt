Under the new structure, 40% of the MDR will go to the customer's issuing bank, 30% to the merchant-acquiring bank, 20% to the UPI application and 10% to the bank supporting the UPI application. Payment aggregators will separately negotiate with acquiring banks for a share of the latter's allocation.The 0.4% MDR will apply from October 15 to specified person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, subject to a maximum charge of Rs 300. On a Rs 10,000 eligible transaction, the MDR will be Rs 40. Of this, Rs 16 will go to the issuing bank, Rs12 to the acquiring bank, Rs 8 to the UPI application and Rs 4 to the bank supporting the UPI application. The merchant bears the MDR within the payment ecosystem, while consumers are not charged the fee. Advt The largest prescribed share therefore goes to the bank holding the customer's account. The acquiring bank receives the second-largest share for handling the merchant side of the transaction. This creates two distinct pools of bank revenue, with the value accruing to different banks depending on their role in the transaction.What payment aggregator getThe economics for payment aggregators are more complicated. The NPCI framework does not prescribe a separate share of MDR for payment aggregators. Instead, aggregators are negotiating with acquiring banks for a portion of the 30% allocated to the acquiring side. Current industry discussions have centred on payment aggregators retaining 50-80% of the acquiring bank's allocation. On a Rs40 MDR generated by a Rs10,000 transaction, that would translate into Rs6-Rs9.60 for the aggregator, with the remainder of the Rs12 acquiring-bank allocation staying with the bank.This makes the distinction between UPI applications and payment aggregators important. Third-party application providers such as PhonePe, Google Pay and Paytm participate on the application side of the UPI transaction, while payment aggregators such as Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU and Pine Labs provide merchant-facing acquiring and payment infrastructure. Their economics therefore arise from different parts of the MDR structure, although some companies can operate across multiple roles.The new revenue pool will also be narrower than the overall UPI transaction numbers suggest. The 0.4% MDR applies only to specified merchant transactions above Rs 2,000, while person-to-person payments remain free. Transactions up to Rs2,000 and transactions covered by the zero-MDR framework for small merchants will also remain outside the charge. The government estimates that about 96% of P2M transactions will remain unaffected. Advt There are separate rates for some categories. Transactions above Rs 2,000 in sectors including railways, telecommunications, insurance, fuel and agricultural inputs will attract a flat Rs 5 MDR, while capital-market transactions will carry an MDR of 0.02%, capped at Rs300. Five per cent of total MDR collections will also be contributed to a fund intended to support UPI adoption among small merchants.The size of the revenue pool will consequently depend on the value and composition of eligible P2M transactions rather than overall UPI volumes. NPCI has estimated that the new MDR could generate around Rs 13,000-15,000 crore a year, although this remains an estimate of the potential revenue pool rather than realised earnings for banks or payment companies. Advt The structure also leaves room for further changes in the economics of merchant acquiring. Payment aggregators are exploring ways to increase their share of acquiring revenue, including reported interest in becoming direct participants in the UPI acquiring framework. Such changes, if approved and implemented, could alter how the acquiring-side revenue is distributed.The money trail therefore runs through several layers rather than to a single set of beneficiaries. Issuing banks receive the largest prescribed share, acquiring banks receive the next-largest allocation, UPI applications receive 20% and their supporting banks receive 10%, while payment aggregators must negotiate their economics from the acquiring-bank pool. The eventual earnings impact will depend on transaction volumes, the role played by each participant and the commercial arrangements governing the acquiring side.
