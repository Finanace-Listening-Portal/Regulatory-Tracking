Summary: Applying for an Initial Public Offering (IPO) does not guarantee share allotment or immediate transfer of funds to the issuer. Under the Application Supported by Blocked Amount (ASBA) mechanism, application money remains blocked in the investor’s bank account until allotment or release. Eligible investors can apply through a UPI-based application, but must approve the payment mandate for successful processing. SEBI permits eligible UPI applications up to ₹5 lakh, which should not be confused with the retail investor category threshold. Investors must examine the latest red herring prospectus to verify issue dates, price bands, lot sizes, eligibility conditions and offer structure. Applications may fail because of incorrect details, unsupported UPI IDs, insufficient balances or unapproved mandates. Successful bidding does not ensure allotment, which depends on prescribed allocation rules. Following allotment, the required amount is debited, while unutilised blocked funds must be released. Investors facing delays should approach their bank, intermediary, registrar or applicable grievance-redressal mechanism. Investment decisions should consider financial statements, valuations, promoter holdings, litigation, risk factors and utilisation of proceeds rather than subscription figures or grey-market premiums alone. Investors should use authorised channels, verify mandates, safeguard credentials and retain documentary evidence. Accurate regulatory disclosures, proper verification and timely complaint resolution are essential for protecting investors against avoidable financial losses and misleading IPO-related claims.
