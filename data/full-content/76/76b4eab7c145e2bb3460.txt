Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Rijesh Ravindran Vs Union of India (Karnataka High Court) Summary:The Karnataka High Court considered an application for regular bail filed by Rijesh Ravindran, accused No. 3, under Section 439 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. The case was registered by the Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB), Bengaluru, for alleged offences under Section 8(c) read with Sections 22(c), 27, 27A, 28 and 29 of the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985. According to the prosecution, NCB officers received information on 21 August 2020 concerning the possession of MDMA pills by Mohammed Anoop at a hotel apartment in Kalyannagar, Bengaluru. A search allegedly resulted in the seizure of 60 grams of MDMA pills. During the subsequent enquiry, Mohammed Anoop allegedly disclosed that he had supplied 40 grams of MDMA tablets to the petitioner. Acting on this information, officers searched the petitioner’s residence and allegedly recovered 40 grams of MDMA pills and 180 grams of LSD blots. The petitioner was arrested on the same date and remanded to judicial custody. The petitioner had previously sought bail before the Sessions Court and the Karnataka High Court, but his applications were rejected. While dismissing Criminal Petition No. 1960 of 2023 on 12 June 2023, the High Court directed the trial court to dispose of the case within six months and granted liberty to the petitioner to approach the Court again if the trial remained incomplete. Relying on that direction, the petitioner submitted that he had already undergone approximately three and a half years of judicial custody and that the trial had not commenced even after expiry of the stipulated period. His counsel further contended that he was not a drug peddler and had no criminal antecedents. The petitioner therefore sought release on regular bail on the ground of prolonged incarceration and delay in trial. The High Court examined the circumstances responsible for the delay and noted that accused Nos. 6 and 7 had instituted separate proceedings before the High Court. In those proceedings, a coordinate Bench had stayed further proceedings before the trial court. The Court observed that the trial court could not proceed with the matter because of the subsisting stay orders. Consequently, the failure to complete the trial within six months could not be treated as a default attributable to the trial court. The Court held that the mere expiry of the period specified in its earlier order did not automatically entitle the petitioner to bail. It also noted that connected proceedings involving other accused persons were pending before the coordinate Bench and that their outcome had not yet been determined. On the merits of the bail application, the High Court considered the prosecution’s allegations regarding possession of MDMA pills and LSD, which the Court treated as involving commercial quantity. Although the petitioner asserted that he had no criminal antecedents and was not engaged in drug peddling, the Court declined to grant bail in the circumstances of the case. It also expressed concern regarding the possibility of commission of a similar offence if bail were granted. Accordingly, the High Court dismissed the petition. However, it expressly granted liberty to the petitioner to approach the Court again after disposal of the connected proceedings pending before the coordinate Bench. The decision concerned the petitioner’s entitlement to bail and did not constitute a final determination of criminal liability. Cases Discussed 1. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT 1. This petition is filed by the petitioner – accused No.3 under Section 439 of Cr.P.C., seeking for grant of regular bail in Crime No.NCB.File.No.48/1/14/2020/BZU registered by NCB, Bengaluru for the offence punishable under Section 8 (c) r/w Sections 22(c), 27, 27A, 28 and 29 of the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985 (hereinafter referred as ‘the NDPS Act’ for short). 2. Heard Sri. Praveen M. Thimmaiah, learned counsel for the petitioner and Smt. Shridevi M. Bhosale, learned CGC for the respondent/NCB. 3. The case of the prosecution is that on 21.08.2020 at about 5.50 p.m., the Intelligence Officer of NCB received a credible information that one Mohammed Anoop is staying in Room No.205-206 of Royal Suites Hotel Apartment at Kalyannagar and has purchased 100 grams of Methylenedioxy methamphetamine CMDMA’ for short) pills and immediately the said information has been forwarded to the higher authority and along with panch witnesses they went to the said place and a search has been conducted, where they found 60 grams of MDMA pills and the same has been seized by drawing a mahazar. The said Mohammed Anoop has been apprehended. 4. During the course of enquiry, he disclosed that he has given 40 grams of tablets to the petitioner-accused No.3. On the same day, i.e., on 21.08.2020 at about 11.10 p.m., another information report was prepared on the basis of the statement given by Mohammed Anoop, a search was conducted in the house of the petitioner at Flat No.52401 at Chookanahalli, Jakkur, Yelahanka, Bengaluru and the petitioner-accused No.3 is in possession of 40 grams of MDMA pills and 180 grams of blots of Lysergic acid Diethylamide CLSD’ for short) and were seized under panchanama and the petitioner-accused No.3 has been taken to judicial custody. The present petitioner was arrested on 21.08.2020 and remanded to judicial custody. The petitioner approached the Sessions Court for granting bail, which came to be rejected and later he also approached this Court thrice in CrI.P.No.5009/2020, CrI.P.No.1305/2021 c/w CrI.P.No.4159/2021 and CrI.P.No.1960/2023, which came to be dismissed on merits by order dated 24.11.2020, 28.07.2021 and 12.06.2023 respectively. While dismissing the last criminal petition, this Court directed the Trial Court to dispose of the case within a period of six months from the date of receipt of the copy of the order and if the trial has not been completed within a period of six months, the petitioner is at liberty to approach this Court for seeking bail. Accordingly, the petitioner is before this Court once again to seek grant of bail. Hence, he prays for allowing the petition. 5. Having heard the arguments of learned counsel for the petitioner and learned CGC for the respondent/NCB it reveals that of course, this petitioner has been in judicial custody for nearly 31/2 years and trial has not yet commenced. As per the direction of this Court in CrI.P.No.1960/2023 dated 12.06.2023, six months have already lapsed but the trial has not yet begun. This is the ground for the petitioner to approach this Court once again. On perusal of the order passed by this Court and submission of the learned counsel which reveals that accused No.6 and 7 are in the same case have approached this Court and obtained stay of further proceedings of the Trial Court. 6. Accused No.6 is filed the writ petition No.21006/2021 under Section 227 of the Constitution of India and accused No.7 is filed CrI.P.No.7522/2023 under Section 482 of Cr.P.C., also staying the further proceedings in the Trial Court. For that reason, the Trial Court cannot able to dispose of the matter on merits. In view of the stay granted by the Co-ordinate Bench of this Court, such being the case, it is not fault on the part of the Trial Court for not concluding the trial within six months. Therefore, the contention of the learned counsel for the petitioner cannot be acceptable. The petitioner shall not be released on bail because lapse of six months. However, on the merits of the learned counsel submits that the petitioner is not a drug peddler and there are no criminal antecedents. However, learned counsel for the respondent submitted that some of the accused persons are consumers they moved the Court to quash the proceedings, where the Co-ordinate Bench of this Court reserved the order for passing W.P.No.21006/2001, also the matter was heard in part long back and reserved for orders. Until this order was passed by the Coordinate Bench and without knowing the outcome the result of the Coordinate Bench in the said case, this Court cannot come to any conclusion whether this petitioner is a consumer or some of the accused are consumer or a drug peddler but the fact remains, this petitioner was in possession of 40 grams of MDMA pills and LSD, which is a commercial quantity and punishment prescribed for minimum 20 years. Therefore, it is not a fit case to grant bail, if bail is granted there is every possibility of committing a similar offence are not ruled out. Accordingly, the petition is dismissed. However, liberty is granted to the petitioner to approach the Court after disposing of the petition by the Coordinate Bench.
