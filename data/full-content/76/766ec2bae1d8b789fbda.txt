Advt The new GDP series, with 2022-23 as the base year, incorporates double deflation for manufacturing and agriculture and draws on a wider range of surveys and administrative data to strengthen the measurement of the informal economy.The revised series also uses more recent household consumption expenditure data to build the consumption basket and maps each type of asset under gross fixed capital formation to the sector investing in it, whether public, private corporate or household.This would allow policymakers to assess not just how much is being invested, but also who is investing and in what, Gupta said.Improved GDP estimatesThe revised GDP series also addresses the gap between estimates calculated from the supply and demand sides through the Supply and Use Table framework.In the earlier series, the difference between the two estimates, reported separately as a discrepancy, was large and volatile at times. Under the new series, nominal GDP estimates from the two sides now align closely.A small gap may persist in real terms because the two sides are deflated differently, but it is much narrower than before, Gupta said.She said the improvements would strengthen the ability to analyse, forecast and explain the drivers of headline growth from both the demand and supply sides. Advt The RBI uses early estimates of economic activity, which draw on proxy indicators and provisional information, as an important input into monetary policy decisions. The methodological improvements in the new series are expected to yield estimates that are more stable across successive vintages, with smaller revisions."This in turn supports better-calibrated and more stable policy," Gupta said.Lower food weight in revised CPIOn inflation measurement, Gupta said the revised CPI series, introduced in February this year, reflects changes in household consumption patterns, with the weight of food and beverages declining to 36.8% from 45.9% in the previous series. Advt The weights in the new series are based on the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey of 2023-24, replacing the 2011-12 survey used earlier.With food items historically more volatile than other components, their lower weight in the revised index is expected to make headline inflation less volatile and the monetary policy process less uncertain and easier to explain, she said.The new CPI series also follows the United Nations' Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose (COICOP) 2018, allowing core inflation to be defined more precisely.The revised methodology excludes social transfers, such as free Public Distribution System (PDS) items, so that the index reflects prices actually paid by households. It also includes improved treatment of missing data, particularly for seasonal items, and changes to the compilation of the house rent index. Advt Employer-provided and government accommodation have been excluded from the housing index to address sharp jumps in house rent inflation following pay commission revisions. House rent is also now covered in rural areas.For the first time, prices from 12 online markets are being collected as part of the CPI exercise. Gupta said this would become increasingly important as the share of online consumption grows.The expanded coverage is accompanied by more granular dissemination, with item-level indices being released for rural, urban and combined sectors, and for all states and Union Territories."These revisions should narrow the disconnect between measured and perceived inflation and add value to the conduct of monetary policy under our inflation targeting framework," Gupta said.
