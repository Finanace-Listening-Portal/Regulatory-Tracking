Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Abdul Rahim Vs DCIT (ITAT Chennai) ITAT Chennai deletes penalty under Section 271(1)(c) on income estimated at 5% after deletion of unexplained expenditure addition; penalties relating to estimated income and house property income also deleted by following judicial precedents. Case Summary: The Chennai Income Tax Appellate Tribunal allowed four appeals filed by Mr. Abdul Rahim for AYs 2010-11, 2012-13, 2014-15 and 2015-16 against penalties imposed under Section 271(1)(c) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. In the quantum proceedings, the Tribunal had deleted the addition of ₹56,07,751 under Section 69C and directed the Assessing Officer to compute income by applying an estimated profit rate of 5% to the amount quantified by the Assessing Officer. The penalty was subsequently imposed on the basis of the estimated income. The Tribunal held that penalty under Section 271(1)(c) was not sustainable on an addition made purely on an estimated basis. It also directed deletion of the penalty relating to income from house property by following earlier decisions of coordinate benches, which had relied on the Madras High Court ruling in Babuji Jacob v. ITO. Core Issue: Whether penalty under Section 271(1)(c) can be levied where the original addition under Section 69C has been deleted and income is determined by applying an estimated profit rate of 5%, and whether penalty relating to an addition towards income from house property is sustainable. Fact: The assessee, an individual engaged in the business of trading in Basmati rice through his proprietorship concern, M/s. Excellent Food Products, and also owning M/s. A.R. Rahaman Biryani restaurant, was subjected to search and seizure proceedings on 24 August 2015 in connection with the SS Hyderabad Biryani Private Limited group. In response to notice under Section 153A, the assessee filed his return declaring total income of ₹4,51,958. The assessment involved an addition of ₹56,07,751 under Section 69C towards unexplained expenditure. In the quantum appeal, the coordinate bench of the Tribunal, by order dated 27 September 2024 in ITA Nos. 1062 to 1067/Chny/2024, deleted the addition under Section 69C and directed computation of income by applying a profit rate of 5% on an estimated basis to the figures quantified by the Assessing Officer. Subsequently, the Assessing Officer imposed penalties under Section 271(1)(c), which were upheld by the CIT(A). The assessee challenged the penalties before the Tribunal for the four assessment years. AO / CIT(A) Finding: Following the quantum proceedings, the Assessing Officer imposed penalties under Section 271(1)(c) on the estimated income. The CIT(A), by order dated 9 January 2026, upheld the penalties, leading the assessee to file the present appeals before the Tribunal. ITAT Finding: The Tribunal observed that the addition under Section 69C had already been deleted in the quantum proceedings and income was directed to be computed by applying a profit rate of 5% on an estimated basis. It held that the issue of penalty on estimated income was covered by judicial precedents supporting the assessee. The Revenue did not bring any new facts on record or distinguish the authorities relied upon by the assessee. Following the decisions cited before it, the Tribunal held that penalty under Section 271(1)(c) could not be sustained on the estimated additions. For the appeals concerning penalties relating to income from house property, the Tribunal additionally followed coordinate-bench decisions in Joseph Abraham Peter Joseph, M/s. RR Industries Ltd., Martin Rajadurai and Winners India Land Promoters. These decisions had accepted the legal position in favour of the assessee by relying, inter alia, on the Madras High Court judgment in Babuji Jacob v. ITO. Accordingly, the Assessing Officer was directed to delete the penalties relating to income from house property as well. Cases Relied: CIT v. Reliance Petroproducts (P.) Ltd., (2010) 189 Taxman 322 (SC), concerning the requirement to establish concealment of income or furnishing of inaccurate particulars; CIT v. P. Rojes, [2013] 31 taxmann.com 253 (Madras High Court), holding that penalty based on estimation of income was not warranted in the circumstances of that case; PCIT v. Elcon Pipe and Fittings (P.) Ltd., [2026] 187 taxmann.com 886 (Bombay High Court), concerning penalty on additions sustained on an estimated basis; and Babuji Jacob v. ITO, (2021) 430 ITR 259 (Madras High Court), relied upon in the coordinate-bench decisions concerning penalty relating to house property income. The Tribunal also followed its coordinate-bench decisions in Joseph Abraham Peter Joseph, ITA No. 2722/Chny/2025; M/s. RR Industries Ltd., ITA No. 2741 to 2743/Chny/2025; Winners India Land Promoters, ITA No. 3659/Chny/2025; and Martin Rajadurai, ITA No. 3879/Chny/2025, as identified in the order. Outcome: All four appeals were allowed. The penalties under Section 271(1)(c) imposed on the estimated income were deleted, and the Assessing Officer was specifically directed to delete the penalties relating to income from house property in the relevant appeals. The Tribunal thus granted relief to the assessee for AYs 2010-11, 2012-13, 2014-15 and 2015-16. Cases Discussed / Relied Upon 1. CIT v. Reliance Petroproducts (P.) Ltd. (Supreme Court); (2010) 189 Taxman 322. The Supreme Court held that merely making a claim which is not sustainable in law does not, by itself, amount to furnishing inaccurate particulars of income. The Tribunal relied upon this judgment while considering the penalty imposed on estimated income. 2. CIT v. P. Rojes (Madras High Court); [2013] 31 taxmann.com 253; Tax Case (Appeal) No. 341 of 2010; 05/02/2013. The Madras High Court considered penalty based on income determined through estimation and held that penalty was not warranted in the circumstances before it. The Tribunal relied on this decision in favour of the assessee. 3. PCIT v. Colo Colour Pvt. Ltd. (Bombay High Court); IT Appeal No. 48 of 2022; 16/09/2025. The Bombay High Court held that penalty under Section 271(1)(c) could not be sustained merely on estimated additions in the circumstances considered. This decision was expressly discussed in the extracted judgment of Elcon Pipe and Fittings relied upon before the Tribunal. 4. PCIT v. Elcon Pipe and Fittings (P.) Ltd. (Bombay High Court); [2026] 187 taxmann.com 886. The Bombay High Court upheld deletion of penalty arising from a quantum addition sustained on an estimated basis, following Colo Colour Pvt. Ltd. The Tribunal relied upon this ruling in the present appeals. 5. Babuji Jacob v. ITO (Madras High Court); (2021) 430 ITR 259. This judgment was relied upon in the coordinate-bench decisions followed by the Tribunal concerning penalties relating to income from house property. The decision addressed the invalidity of penalty proceedings arising from a defective notice that failed to specify the applicable charge. 6. Joseph Abraham Peter Joseph (ITAT Chennai); ITA No. 2722/Chny/2025; 05/12/2025. Coordinate-bench decision followed in respect of penalties relating to income from house property. 7. M/s. RR Industries Ltd. (ITAT Chennai); ITA Nos. 2741 to 2743/Chny/2025; 18/02/2026. Coordinate-bench decision followed in respect of penalties connected with house property income. 8. Winners India Land Promoters (ITAT Chennai); ITA No. 3659/Chny/2025; 07/07/2026. Coordinate-bench decision relied upon for deletion of penalty concerning income from house property. 9. Martin Rajadurai (ITAT Chennai); ITA No. 3879/Chny/2025; 09/07/2026. Coordinate-bench decision followed in the appeals involving penalty attributable to income from house property. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee is an individual engaged in the business of trading in Basmati rice through his proprietorship, concern, M/s. Excellent Food Products and owns M/s. A.R. Rahaman Biryani restaurant. The assessee filed his return of income for the A.Y. 2010-11 admitting a total income of Rs. 4, 51, 958/- on 02.01.2011. A search and seizure operation was conducted in the assessee’s residence, and a survey was conducted in his business premises M/s. Excellent Food Products at No.35, Ground Floor, Amman Koil Street, Chicken Market, Chennai-600 001, as part of the search operation in the case of SS Hyderabad Biryani Private Limited group, on 24.08.2015. Consequent to search, a notice u/s. 153A of the Act dated 20.06.2017 was issued and served on 24.06.2017, requiring the assessee to prepare a true and correct return of income and furnish the same within 15 days of receipt of the notice. In response to the notice u/s. 153A of the Act, the assessee filed his return of income on 06.10.2017 admitting a total income of Rs. 4, 51,958/-. Ultimately, the case of the assessee was assessed after making addition u/s. 69C of the Act amounting to Rs. 56, 07, 751/-. The assessee being aggrieved with the same preferred an appeal before the Ld. CIT(A), who in turn dismissed the appeal of the assessee. The assessee being further aggrieved preferred an appeal before the Coordinate Bench of ITAT vide ITA Nos. 1062 to 1067/Chny/2024 dated 27.09.2024. The Coordinate Bench in its orders against the appeals mentioned (supra) deleted the addition made u/s. 69C of the Act and rather directed to apply a profit rate of 5% on estimate basis. 3. After, this order of the Coordinate Bench (wherein the income has been calculated on estimate basis) the AO passed the order of penalty u/s. 271(1)(c) of the Act vide order dated 16.03.2025. The assessee being aggrieved with the same, preferred an appeal before the Ld. CIT(A), who in turn dismissed the appeal of the assessee. The assessee being further aggrieved, preferred the present appeal before us. 4. We have gone through the order of the AO, the order of the Ld. CIT(A) and submissions of the assessee and the grounds taken before us. It is observed that the quantum addition in the case of the assessee has been deleted by the Coordinate Bench as mentioned (supra) and computation of income was directed to be done on estimation basis @ 5% on the figures quantified by the AO u/s. 69C of the Act. Thereafter, on this estimated income the AO levied the penalty u/s. 271(1)(c) of the Act. The issue involved in the present appeal is no more res integra and the proposition of law has already been settled in favour of the assessee i.e. no penalty can be levied on addition made on estimated basis. 5. In his submissions the assessee relied upon the following decisions of the Hon’ble Apex Court, Jurisdictional High Court and other High Courts as under: [2013] 31 taxmann.com 253 (Mad.) CIT, Chennai v. P. Rojes “Though the Assessing Officer invoked penalty under section 271(1)(c) and stated that the assessee failed to furnish complete details from bank statement, on-going through the materials placed before this Court, it is seen that the Assessing Officer has subsequently found that the said deposit was made for the period commencing from 1-4-2004 to 29-3-2005. Therefore, when the Assessing Officer himself had found that deposit was not made on a single day, it could not be said that the assessee had failed to furnish complete particulars. The Tribunal has categorically found that in the return, the assessee had shown the income on estimate basis, and such estimation of income was enhanced by the Assessing Officer and consequently, imposed penalty. Therefore, from the above facts it is clear that levy of penalty was based on the estimation of income. There cannot be any imposition of penalty based on estimation of income. [Para 6] When the revenue itself has not come out with clear case of suppression of turnover and where there was no specific finding with regard to such factual aspect, imposition of penalty under section 271(1)(c) is not warranted. [Para 7]” [2010] 189 Taxman 322 (SC) CIT, Ahmedabad v. Reliance Petroproducts (P.) Ltd. A glance of provision of section 271(1)(c) would suggest that in order to be covered, there has to be concealment of the particulars of the income of the assessee. Secondly, the assessee must have furnished inaccurate particulars of his income. The instant case was not the case of concealment of the income. That was not the case of the revenue either. It was an admitted position in the instant case that no information given in the return was found to be incorrect or inaccurate. It was not as if any statement made or any detail supplied was found to be factually incorrect. Hence, at least, prima facie, the assessee could not be held guilty of furnishing inaccurate particulars. The revenue argued that submitting an incorrect claim in law for the expenditure on interest would amount to giving inaccurate particulars of such income. Such cannot be the interpretation of the words concerned. The words are plain and simple. In order to expose the assessee to the penalty unless the case is strictly covered by the provision, the penalty provision cannot be invoked. By any stretch of imagination, making an incorrect claim in law cannot be tantamount to furnishing inaccurate particulars. [Para 7] Therefore, it must be shown that the conditions under section 271(1)(c) exist before the penalty is imposed. There can be no dispute that everything would depend upon the return filed, because that is the only document where the assessee can furnish the particulars of his income. When such particulars are found to be inaccurate, liability would arise. [Para 8] The word ‘particulars’ must mean the details supplied in the return, which are not accurate, not exact or correct, not according to truth or erroneous. In the instant case, there was no finding that any details supplied by the assessee in its return were found to be incorrect or erroneous or false. Such not being the case, there would be no question of inviting the penalty under section 271(1)(c). A mere making of the claim, which is not sustainable in law by itself, will not amount to furnishing of inaccurate particulars regarding the income of the assessee. Such claim made in the return cannot amount to the inaccurate particulars. [Para 9] The revenue contended that since the assessee had claimed excessive deductions knowing that they were incorrect, it amounted to concealment of income. It was argued that the falsehood in accounts can take either of the two forms: (i) an item of receipt may be suppressed fraudulently; (ii) an item of expenditure may be falsely (or in an exaggerated amount) claimed, and both types attempt to reduce the taxable income and, therefore, both types amount to concealment of particulars of one’s income as well as furnishing of inaccurate particulars of income. Such contention could not be accepted as the assessee had furnished all the details of its expenditure as well as income in its return, which details, in themselves, were not found to be inaccurate nor could be viewed as the concealment of income on its part. It was up to the authorities to accept its claim in return or not. Merely because the assessee had claimed the expenditure, which claim was not accepted or was not acceptable to the revenue, that, by itself, would not attract the penalty under section 271(1)(c). If the contention of the revenue was accepted, then in case of every return where the claim made was not accepted by the Assessing Officer for any reason, the assessee would invite penalty under section 271(1)(c). That is clearly not the intendment of the Legislature. [Para 10] [2026] 187 taxmann.com 886 (Bom.) PCIT v. Elcon Pipe and Fittings (P.) Ltd. “The issue raised is squarely covered by the decision of this Court in the case of Pr. CIT v. Colo Colour Pvt. Ltd. [IT Appeal No. 48 of 2022, dated 16-9-2025]. In this decision, this Court has clearly taken the view that penalty cannot be imposed under Section 271(1)(c) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 when the addition was sustained purely on an estimate basis or when the addition was made which was on pure guesswork. Since the penalty order was set aside by ITAT on the basis that the addition made in the quantum proceedings was on estimation (i.e. 12.5% of the bogus purchases), the ITAT correct in setting aside the penalty order. The decision of the ITAT is in consonance with the decision of this Court rendered in Colo Colour (P.) Ltd. (supra). [Para 10] Considering these facts and circumstances, the impugned Order of the ITAT does not give rise to any substantial question of law. The Appeal is therefore dismissed. However, there shall be no order as to costs. [Para 11]” 6. The decision relied upon by the assessee and the facts of the assessee’s appeal are identical and the Revenue also has not forward with any new fact or able differentiate the decisions relied upon by the assessee. In view of this, respectfully following the decisions of the Hon’ble Apex Court, Jurisdictional High Court and Bombay High Court, without any hesitation, we hold that the action of the AO is sustainable in law as far as penalty on additions made on estimate basis is concerned. Resultantly, grounds taken by the assessee are allowed. 7. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed in above terms. ITA No. 1740/Chny/2026 8.
