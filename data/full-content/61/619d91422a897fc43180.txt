Summary: Bank of India’s Star Sunidhi is a named tax-saving term deposit for individuals and Hindu undivided families with PAN. The bank states a ₹10,000 minimum, ₹1.5 lakh annual maximum, a tenure from five years up to ten years, no ordinary premature withdrawal or loan facility during the first five years, and normal domestic term-deposit rates. For the five-to-less-than-eight-year retail bucket, the official rate card shows 6.00% for the general public, with the bank’s current additional-rate framework producing 6.75% for senior citizens and 6.90% for super senior citizens on eligible deposits below ₹3 crore. This article explains the product terms, the Income-tax Act, 2025 deduction framework, taxable interest, TDS and DICGC aggregation without recommending the deposit. Eligibility is limited to individuals and Hindu undivided families having PAN. The bank publishes a minimum deposit of ₹10,000 and a maximum of ₹1,50,000 per year. Available deposit forms include FDR, monthly-interest, quarterly-interest and double-benefit options, while the selected payout form affects cash-flow timing and compounding rather than the five-year statutory lock-in. The published tenure runs from a minimum of five years to a maximum of ten years. A depositor should record the exact contracted maturity because a ten-year certificate is not the same product outcome as a five-year certificate even though both may satisfy the initial lock-in condition. Interest is governed by the normal domestic term-deposit card applicable when the deposit is opened, plus any eligible senior-citizen addition. Senior and super-senior additions depend on age, first-holder status and amount For eligible retail deposits below ₹3 crore with a tenure of three years or more and up to ten years, the bank states an additional 0.75 percentage point for senior citizens and 0.90 percentage point for super senior citizens. Applied to the 6.00% base, the five-year rates are 6.75% and 6.90% respectively. The bank defines senior citizen as age 60 or above but below 80, and super senior citizen as age 80 or above. Eligible first holder, age 80 or above; 0.90% additional rate for 3–10 years The five-year lock-in changes liquidity and succession planning Ordinary premature withdrawal is not permitted during the first five years. That is a product and tax-saver feature, not merely a penalty choice. The depositor should therefore keep emergency liquidity outside Star Sunidhi and should not assume that paying a lower interest rate will unlock the certificate. The bank also states that an advance facility is unavailable for five years and that the term deposit cannot be pledged as security for another advance. The bank provides a death exception: where the depositor dies before maturity, the nominee or legal heir may receive premature payment even within the lock-in subject to applicable rules, and the premature-payment penalty is exempted. Nomination is available generally, but the product page states that no nomination shall be made for a term deposit applied for and held by or on behalf of a minor. Depositors should obtain the bank’s current operating documents for the precise survivor, nominee and claim process. Tax deduction and interest taxation are separate questions Section 123 and Schedule XV govern the deduction from tax year 2026-27 For deposits made on or after 1 April 2026, the current statutory reference is section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 read with Schedule XV. It allows an individual or HUF a deduction for enumerated eligible payments and deposits within an aggregate ceiling of ₹1,50,000 for the tax year, subject to the Schedule conditions. Star Sunidhi’s bank product page still uses the familiar section 80C label, but current-year tax work should map the deposit to the 2025 Act rather than cite the repealed numbering mechanically. The ₹1,50,000 is an aggregate deduction ceiling, not a separate allowance for this FD. Life-insurance premium, provident-fund contributions and other qualifying items can consume the same limit. Further, deductions under section 123 are not available where the individual’s total income is computed under the concessional regime in section 202, subject to the Act’s terms. A deposit may therefore satisfy product eligibility while producing no incremental deduction for a particular taxpayer. Interest remains taxable and TDS is only a collection mechanism Tax-saving status does not exempt the interest. Interest is included in income under the applicable method and head, and the bank applies TDS rules to the customer’s aggregate interest with the bank rather than assessing each certificate in isolation. For the current 2025 Act framework, the threshold commonly relevant to bank time-deposit interest is ₹50,000 for other depositors and ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens, subject to section 393, current rules, PAN and any valid declaration. No TDS does not mean no tax; the depositor must report taxable interest even where withholding is nil. DICGC cover applies at bank level, not certificate level Bank of India is a bank deposit-taker, so eligible fixed deposits fall within the DICGC framework. The insurance limit is ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in the same right and same capacity, inclusive of principal and accrued interest. All eligible deposits at different branches of Bank of India are aggregated. Opening several Star Sunidhi certificates or using multiple BOI branches does not create a separate ₹5 lakh cover for each certificate. Different ownership capacities can be treated separately only under DICGC’s rules; labels should not be engineered without genuine legal capacity. The insurance limit is not a guarantee of timely liquidity, a return guarantee beyond covered deposits, or a reason to ignore nomination and records. Depositors should preserve deposit receipts, PAN, joint-holder mandate and nomination acknowledgement so the bank can identify the correct depositor capacity. The official card places five years to less than eight years at 6.00% for general retail deposits below ₹3 crore, with eligible additions resulting in 6.75% for senior and 6.90% for super-senior citizens. Does the deposit always produce a ₹1.5 lakh tax deduction? No. The ceiling is aggregate across eligible section 123 items, and the taxpayer’s chosen tax regime and other conditions determine actual deduction. Is Star Sunidhi interest tax-free? No. Interest remains taxable. TDS, where applicable, is withholding and does not determine the final tax liability. Is each BOI tax-saver certificate insured up to ₹5 lakh? No. DICGC aggregates eligible deposits across all BOI branches for the same depositor in the same right and capacity, including principal and interest. Key Takeaways Star Sunidhi is a genuine Bank of India tax-saving term deposit for eligible individuals and HUFs with PAN. The published amount range is ₹10,000 to ₹1,50,000 per year, with a five-to-ten-year tenure. The five-year bucket is 6.00% for general customers, 6.75% for eligible senior citizens and 6.90% for eligible super senior citizens based on the current card framework. No ordinary premature withdrawal, loan or pledge is available during the first five years; death claims follow special rules.
