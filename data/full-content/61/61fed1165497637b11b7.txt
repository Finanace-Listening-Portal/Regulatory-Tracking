Summary: The 57th GST Council Meeting held on 8 October 2026 proposed removing the specific blocked-credit restrictions relating to health insurance and life insurance under Section 17(5)(b)(i) of the CGST Act, 2017. The proposed relaxation may benefit businesses purchasing employer-sponsored group medical insurance, group term life insurance and other qualifying insurance policies, particularly where coverage is voluntarily provided to employees. Under the existing law, such ITC is generally restricted unless a statutory exception applies, including insurance that an employer is legally required to provide. However, the proposed amendment would not automatically permit credit on every insurance arrangement. Individual life and health insurance policies exempt from GST do not generate corresponding ITC, while taxable group policies may qualify subject to the final amendment. The treatment of employees’ dependants, working directors, contract workers, retired employees, premium recoveries, Keyman insurance and business-protection policies will require examination of policy classification, business nexus and continuing statutory restrictions. Businesses making taxable and exempt supplies must also comply with the applicable credit-apportionment provisions, while banks and financial institutions may remain subject to Section 17(4). Organisations operating through multiple GST registrations must examine the mandatory Input Service Distributor framework under Section 20 and Rule 39 for distributing common input-service credit. Compliance with Section 16, invoice requirements and other provisions of Section 17 will remain necessary. As of 20 October 2026, the proposed relaxation had not become operative, and its precise scope and commencement will depend on the final enacted amendment. Table of Contents▸ Introduction 1. The existing law and the proposed amendment therefore need to be examined carefully before determining the actual ITC benefit available in each case. The proposed amendment is particularly significant for companies purchasing group medical insurance and group term life insurance for employees. Difference Between Individual Insurance and Group Insurance The distinction between individual insurance and group insurance is particularly important because the GST treatment of insurance changed with effect from 22 September 2025. Individual life and health insurance policies became exempt from GST, whereas employer-sponsored group insurance policies continue to attract 18% GST. Contract workers and retired employees: The business relationship, actual contractual obligations, recipient of service and policy classification remain relevant. 5. Insurance Provided as an Employment Perquisite – GST Implications Two separate questions arise when an employer provides insurance to employees: Whether the employer is eligible to claim ITC on the GST charged by the insurance company. Whether GST is payable when the employer provides the insurance benefit to employees. These are independent questions. CBIC Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST clarifies that contractual perquisites provided by an employer to employees in accordance with their employment arrangements are generally not separately subject to GST merely because they are provided as part of employment. However, the absence of outward GST on an employment perquisite does not automatically make ITC on the corresponding insurance premium eligible. Under the existing Section 17(5)(b)(i), health and life insurance are generally blocked unless a statutory exception applies. After the proposed amendment, insurance ITC may become available even where there is no separate taxable outward supply from the employer to the employee, provided the remaining statutory conditions are satisfied. Where the employer separately recovers insurance premiums from employees, the nature of the arrangement must be examined independently. The recovery may raise questions concerning whether the employer is making a separate supply or merely recovering part of an employment-related benefit. The proposed relaxation does not automatically determine the GST treatment of these recoveries. 6. Insurance Purchased for Employees Working in Different States Companies operating through multiple GST registrations frequently purchase group insurance centrally for employees working across different States. Where an insurance policy covers employees of multiple establishments, the ITC implications must be considered registration-wise. The principal issues include: Which GST registration is the actual recipient of the insurance service. To which GSTIN the insurance company has issued its invoice. Whether the policy covers employees of one registration or several registrations. Whether the insurance service is attributable to multiple distinct persons under GST. Whether the mandatory Input Service Distributor provisions apply. How eligible ITC must be allocated or distributed between registrations. Mandatory ISD Framework Under the Input Service Distributor framework effective from 1 April 2025, where an office receives third-party input-service invoices for or on behalf of more than one distinct GST registration under the same PAN, the mandatory ISD provisions must be examined and the eligible credit distributed in accordance with Section 20 and Rule 39. Credit attributable exclusively to a particular recipient is to be distributed to that recipient, while common credit is required to be distributed in the prescribed manner. Therefore, even if the proposed amendment removes the insurance-specific credit restriction, it does not mean that the entire ITC can automatically be claimed by the head-office registration merely because the head office pays the premium. The invoice, contractual arrangement, identity of the recipient and applicable credit-distribution provisions must be followed. 7. Insurance ITC for Businesses Making Taxable and Exempt Supplies The proposed amendment relates specifically to the blocked-credit restriction under Section 17(5)(b)(i). It does not remove the restrictions contained in Section 17(1), Section 17(2) or the special provisions of Section 17(4). Businesses Making Both Taxable and Exempt Supplies Where insurance services are used partly for taxable and partly for exempt business activities, the eligible ITC must be determined in accordance with the relevant GST apportionment provisions. A company cannot automatically claim the entire insurance ITC merely because the specific restriction on health or life insurance has been removed. Banks, Financial Institutions and NBFCs Banks, financial institutions and NBFCs engaged in accepting deposits or extending loans or advances may be subject to the special provisions of Section 17(4). Where an eligible entity has opted for the prescribed 50% ITC mechanism, that restriction continues to be relevant even where the underlying insurance service becomes otherwise eligible. The removal of the specific insurance-related restriction under Section 17(5)(b)(i) does not automatically override Section 17(4). Similarly, businesses making exempt supplies must continue to examine the restrictions relating to exempt-supply attribution. Conclusion The proposed relaxation of Section 17(5)(b)(i) is an important development for businesses purchasing group health insurance and group life insurance. Under the existing law, ITC on these services is generally blocked except where a specific statutory exception applies, particularly where the employer is legally obligated to provide the relevant insurance. The proposed amendment may extend eligibility to voluntary group insurance purchased for genuine business purposes. This could benefit manufacturing companies, trading businesses, service-sector organisations and other employers providing insurance benefits to their employees. However, the treatment of insurance covering employees’ dependants, directors, contract workers, retired employees, premium recoveries and business-protection arrangements will depend on the final statutory provisions. Businesses must also continue complying with Section 16, the remaining restrictions under Section 17 and the applicable GST registration and credit-distribution requirements. Particular care is required in relation to Keyman and business-protection policies, because the first issue is whether GST is actually chargeable on the relevant policy. Likewise, businesses operating through multiple GST registrations must separately comply with the mandatory ISD framework wherever applicable. As of 20 October 2026, the proposed relaxation has not been brought into force.
