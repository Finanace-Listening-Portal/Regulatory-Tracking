Summary: The 57th GST Council Meeting held on 8 October 2026 proposed removing the specific blocked-credit restrictions relating to health insurance and life insurance under Section 17(5)(b)(i) of the CGST Act, 2017. The proposed relaxation may benefit businesses purchasing employer-sponsored group medical insurance, group term life insurance and other qualifying insurance policies, particularly where coverage is voluntarily provided to employees. Under the existing law, such ITC is generally restricted unless a statutory exception applies, including insurance that an employer is legally required to provide. However, the proposed amendment would not automatically permit credit on every insurance arrangement. Individual life and health insurance policies exempt from GST do not generate corresponding ITC, while taxable group policies may qualify subject to the final amendment. The treatment of employees’ dependants, working directors, contract workers, retired employees, premium recoveries, Keyman insurance and business-protection policies will require examination of policy classification, business nexus and continuing statutory restrictions. Businesses making taxable and exempt supplies must also comply with the applicable credit-apportionment provisions, while banks and financial institutions may remain subject to Section 17(4). Organisations operating through multiple GST registrations must examine the mandatory Input Service Distributor framework under Section 20 and Rule 39 for distributing common input-service credit. Compliance with Section 16, invoice requirements and other provisions of Section 17 will remain necessary. The proposal is particularly relevant for businesses purchasing group medical insurance, group term life insurance and other employer-sponsored insurance policies for employees, since such credits are generally restricted under the existing law unless a specific statutory exception applies. The existing law and the proposed amendment therefore need to be examined carefully before determining the actual ITC benefit available in each case. The proposed amendment is particularly significant for companies purchasing group medical insurance and group term life insurance for employees. Under the existing law, ITC on health insurance and life insurance is generally blocked, except in certain specified circumstances. The proposed relaxation may allow businesses to claim qualifying ITC even where the employer voluntarily provides insurance as an employee benefit. Important: The recommendation has not yet become an operative amendment. Until the relevant amendment takes effect, the existing restrictions continue to apply. 1. Difference Between Individual Insurance and Group Insurance The distinction between individual insurance and group insurance is particularly important because the GST treatment of insurance changed with effect from 22 September 2025. Individual life and health insurance policies became exempt from GST, whereas employer-sponsored group insurance policies continue to attract 18% GST. Existing Legal Position under Section 17(5)(b)(i) Section 17(5)(b)(i) expressly restricts ITC on life insurance and health insurance. Therefore, merely establishing that an insurance premium has been paid for business purposes does not automatically make the credit eligible. However, there are three statutory exceptions.
