(Bombay High Court) Summary: The Bombay High Court, in Marathon Era Co-operative Housing Society Ltd. v. Competent Authority & District Deputy Registrar, Co-operative Societies & Ors., Writ Petition No. 180 of 2018, decided along with Gulmohar Lokmilan Co-operative Housing Society Ltd. & Anr. v. Competent Authority & Ors., Writ Petition No. 295 of 2015, on 18 April 2024, delivered an important judgment concerning the entitlement of housing societies to obtain unilateral deemed conveyance under Section 11 of the Maharashtra Ownership Flats (Regulation of the Promotion of Construction, Sale, Management and Transfer) Act, 1963 (MOFA). Justice G.S. Patel examined the legal complexities arising when residential buildings are developed in phases on a large undivided parcel of land containing multiple buildings, independent co-operative housing societies, shared amenities and common development rights. The principal questions were whether a society representing flat purchasers in a completed building could demand conveyance before completion of the entire layout, what land and development rights could legitimately form part of that conveyance, how Floor Space Index (FSI) should be treated in multi-building developments, and whether a developer could indefinitely postpone transfer of title by relying on the possibility of future construction or additional development potential. The Court explained that Section 11 of MOFA imposes a statutory obligation upon promoters to convey their right, title and interest in the relevant land and buildings to the organisation representing flat purchasers. Where the promoter fails to comply, the society may approach the competent authority for a certificate of unilateral deemed conveyance. The Court recognised MOFA as legislation intended to protect flat purchasers and emphasised that its conveyance provisions could not be rendered ineffective merely because modern real estate developments involve large layouts, phased construction and multiple societies rather than a single building on an identifiable plot. The dispute involving Marathon Era concerned a residential complex constructed on a larger property measuring approximately 35,000 square metres in Lower Parel, Mumbai. The development included Marathon Era, Marathon Innova, parking facilities, recreational grounds, internal roads, a clubhouse and other common amenities. Under agreements executed with flat purchasers in August 2004, Marathon Era comprised four residential wings occupying an identified portion of approximately 6,787.82 square metres within the larger property. The agreements also contemplated the application of the Maharashtra Apartment Ownership Act, 1970 and the eventual transfer of rights relating to the building and shared facilities. The petitioner society was registered in January 2010 and received an occupation certificate in May 2010. Despite this, disputes arose regarding the developer’s obligation to transfer title while other portions of the layout remained available for development. The society applied for unilateral deemed conveyance under Section 11 of MOFA and relied upon an architect’s certificate suggesting entitlement to approximately 56.19% of the total FSI of the larger property. It also claimed substantial rights over common amenities and other portions of the land. The Competent Authority declined to grant conveyance by an order dated 10 April 2017, principally because the application sought rights extending beyond the conveyance contemplated by the governing agreements and because the overall development remained incomplete. Before the High Court, the society argued that completion of its residential building and issuance of the occupation certificate triggered the statutory obligation to convey the identified land, building and corresponding rights in common areas. It submitted that the developer could not rely upon continued development of the larger layout to withhold title from flat purchasers who had already acquired and occupied their premises. The developer and other contesting respondents maintained that the conveyance claim had to be examined in the context of the entire integrated layout, contractual arrangements, common amenities and remaining development potential. The Court accepted the importance of the society’s statutory entitlement but distinguished that entitlement from the much broader claim originally advanced for a fixed percentage of the entire layout’s FSI and exclusive rights over shared facilities. The High Court explained that deemed conveyance under Section 11 of MOFA must be adapted to the legal and practical realities of multi-building layout developments. Unlike a single-building project, where the building’s development potential can ordinarily be associated with a specific plot, a large phased layout involves FSI calculated with reference to the entire landholding. Such FSI may be distributed unequally among residential towers, commercial buildings, parking structures and common facilities. Consequently, it would be legally and technically incorrect to assume that a completed building must receive a proportionate share of the total land merely because it has consumed a particular percentage of the available FSI. The Court rejected Marathon Era’s claim to 56.19% of the larger property’s development potential, holding that the calculation was based on an unsustainable direct correlation between the building’s constructed area and the FSI available across the entire layout. At the same time, the Court recognised that a completed building could not be deprived of the land and development rights legitimately associated with it under the agreements and statutory framework. A society could be entitled to conveyance of its building, the identifiable land beneath and surrounding it as contemplated in the agreement, the development potential already consumed by that building, and appropriate undivided rights of use and enjoyment in common amenities. The Court distinguished between exclusive ownership of particular land or structures and shared rights over internal roads, open spaces, recreational facilities and other amenities intended for the benefit of the entire layout. Such common facilities could not automatically be appropriated exclusively by one society to the prejudice of other societies and purchasers. The judgment recognised the importance of a federation or apex organisation in managing and holding common areas where the development structure so required. It further emphasised that the existence of separate ownership interests in land and buildings was recognised in law, and that a conveyance could therefore be structured to reflect the actual proprietary rights arising from the agreements and development arrangement. The Court’s interpretation preserved the statutory right of flat purchasers to obtain marketable title without permitting an individual society to claim an unjustified portion of the larger development. The Court also examined the implications of additional FSI becoming available after completion of a building, particularly where changes in development control regulations permit further construction within the same layout. Referring to judicial authorities on the obligations of promoters under MOFA, including Kalpita Enclave Co-operative Housing Society Ltd. v. Kiran Builders Pvt. Ltd., Jayantilal Investments v. Madhuvihar Co-operative Housing Society, Eternia Co-operative Housing Society Ltd. v. Lakeview Developers, Madhuvihar Co-operative Housing Society v. Jayantilal Investments, Malad Kokil Co-operative Housing Society Ltd. v. Modern Construction Co. Ltd. and Dosti Corporation v. Sea Flama Co-operative Housing Society Ltd., the Court discussed the relationship between a developer’s right to undertake additional construction and the statutory protection of existing flat purchasers. It identified two important principles: first, additional FSI could not be utilised in a manner that diminished the common amenities and facilities promised to purchasers; and second, the FSI already consumed in constructing a completed building could not subsequently be reduced or appropriated for another portion of the layout. The Court illustrated that development potential already used in a building must remain protected so that the building could, if necessary, be reconstructed at least to the extent of its existing construction. However, it did not finally determine whether a completed society automatically acquired a proportionate share of additional FSI generated subsequently by changes in planning regulations. The Court further examined the Government Resolution dated 22 June 2018, which had been issued to streamline the deemed conveyance procedure. The resolution recognised the practical difficulties arising in multi-building developments and contemplated conveyance of proportionate land, ground coverage or plinth area, together with undivided rights over common services, facilities and access roads. It also addressed situations where construction in part of a layout remained incomplete or where the developer anticipated additional FSI or Transferable Development Rights (TDR). The High Court held that its interpretation was consistent with the framework contemplated by the Government Resolution. The decision therefore clarified that a developer’s expectation of future development potential could not independently defeat a completed society’s entitlement to an appropriately structured conveyance, while the actual extent of the rights to be conveyed had to be determined with reference to the agreements, approved plans, existing construction, common facilities and applicable law. On the final disposal of the two writ petitions, the High Court adopted different approaches based upon the facts and defects in the respective deemed conveyance proceedings. In Marathon Era Co-operative Housing Society Ltd., the Court held that the society’s original application was legally unsustainable insofar as it demanded 56.19% of the total layout FSI and exclusive rights over common amenities extending beyond its established entitlement. The Court therefore dismissed Writ Petition No. 180 of 2018 but granted the society liberty to submit a fresh application for unilateral deemed conveyance within 60 days of the judgment. The Competent Authority was directed to issue notice, grant the hearing contemplated under Section 11 of MOFA and decide the fresh application independently on its merits in accordance with the principles declared by the Court, uninfluenced by the earlier orders. In the connected Gulmohar Lokmilan Co-operative Housing Society Ltd. matter, the dispute concerned a larger development at Chandivali, Mumbai, comprising several societies occupying buildings constructed during different phases. The Competent Authority had granted unilateral deemed conveyance in favour of three societies through an order dated 30 October 2014, effectively excluding the petitioner Gulmohar society from relevant common property rights. The High Court found that the authority had failed to examine whether Gulmohar formed part of the same layout, how the common amenities were to be shared and whether land already surrendered to the Municipal Corporation could legally form part of the conveyance. It held that a society forming part of a common layout could not be excluded from the conveyance arrangement without proper examination of relevant evidence and findings. Consequently, Writ Petition No. 295 of 2015 was allowed, the impugned deemed conveyance order was quashed and the matter was remanded to the Competent Authority for fresh consideration. Gulmohar was also permitted to make a separate application if it desired. The judgment thus establishes a practical framework for deemed conveyance in phased real estate developments, balancing the statutory rights of completed housing societies with the legitimate rights of other societies, common facility users and developers. It reinforces that conveyance cannot be indefinitely postponed merely because the entire development remains incomplete, but the competent authority must carefully identify the precise land, building, existing FSI and shared rights that can lawfully be transferred.
