FAST-DS 2026: A Practitioner’s Guide to Eligibility, Valuation and Filing Summary: The Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers – Disclosure Scheme, 2026 (FAST-DS), introduced under Chapter IV of the Finance Act, 2026, provides eligible taxpayers an opportunity to regularise undisclosed foreign assets and foreign-source income by 31 December 2026. Under Section 133, Row 1 covers unexplained foreign assets and untaxed foreign income up to ₹1 crore, attracting tax and an additional amount totalling 60%, while Row 2 covers foreign assets acquired from previously taxed income or while non-resident, with an aggregate limit of ₹5 crore and a flat fee of ₹1 lakh for reporting omissions. Taxpayers may need to use both rows where an otherwise explained foreign asset has generated untaxed income. The article explains eligibility, residential-status requirements, valuation rules as on 31 March 2026, and important pitfalls, particularly the valuation of foreign bank accounts based on cumulative deposits rather than closing balances. Through practical examples involving RSUs, returning NRIs, foreign brokerage accounts and overseas bank deposits, it illustrates the financial consequences of choosing the correct disclosure category. It also examines the online declaration procedure, Forms 1 to 4, payment timelines, interest provisions, immunity from tax, penalty and prosecution under the Black Money Act, and limitations relating to false declarations, completed assessments and refunds. Taxpayers should carefully reconcile foreign assets, income, acquisition sources and historical residential status before filing to secure the available statutory benefits. Table of Contents▸ 1. Background The department already gets foreign account data through CRS and FATCA, and many taxpayers have received the automated advisory email on foreign assets, which links to the “Foreign Assets Information” now visible in AIS. Outside the scheme, a Schedule FA omission or untaxed foreign income falls under the Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015 (“BMA”). That means tax at 30%, penalty, and, above the threshold, possible prosecution. FAST-DS is in Chapter IV (sections 130 to 144) of the Finance Act, 2026, read with the FAST-DS Rules, 2026 (CBDT Notification No. 114/2026 dated 14 August 2026) and the CBDT FAQs. The scheme came into force on 16 August 2026. No declaration can be filed after 31 December 2026. 2. The CBDT FAQ example under Row 1: a foreign bank account valued at ₹60 lakh plus undisclosed foreign income of ₹20 lakh gives tax of ₹18 lakh + ₹6 lakh, plus equal additional amounts, for a total payable of ₹48 lakh. 3. Eligibility: Who Can and Cannot Use It Who Can Declare A person who was: resident in India under section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 in the previous year (as defined in section 2(34) of that Act); or non-resident or RNOR in the previous year, but resident in India either (a) in the year to which the undisclosed foreign income relates, or (b) in the year in which the undisclosed foreign asset was acquired. The test is historical: a person who is non-resident or RNOR today still qualifies if they were resident in that earlier year (CBDT FAQ Q7), and Form 1 asks for residential status for the year of acquisition or earning. A declaration may be made “for any previous year” (section 132), and the immunity under section 139 covers the previous year ending 31 March 2026 or any earlier previous year, so omissions spread over several past years can be regularised together. When a Declaration Is Possible (Section 132) The person (a) did not file a return under section 139; (b) filed a return before the scheme began but did not disclose the asset or income in it; or (c) the asset or income has escaped assessment under section 147. Who Is Excluded income or assets that represent proceeds of crime where proceedings under the Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002 have been initiated or are pending; income or assets relating to an assessment year for which BMA assessment proceedings are already completed. A pending BMA assessment is not a bar. Under section 141, the Assessing Officer must take the declaration into account when finalising the pending order. Breach of the Ceilings Ineligibility is all or nothing. If the Row 2 aggregate exceeds ₹5 crore, the declarant is out of Row 2 altogether. Row 1 likewise has a hard ₹1 crore cap on asset value plus income. 4. Row 1 or Row 2: How to Decide The question is not whether the asset was disclosed. It is whether the money that went into it has already borne Indian tax, or was outside the Indian tax net when earned. Work through these questions in order: 1. Was the asset bought from income that was taxed in India (for example, RSUs taxed as a perquisite through Form 16) or from income earned while non-resident? If yes, the omission is only a reporting omission: Row 2, ₹1 lakh. 2. Is any foreign income attached to that asset (dividends, interest, capital gains, rent) that arose in a year you were ROR and was not offered to tax? If yes, that income is Row 1, at 60% of the income amount, even though the asset itself goes in Row 2. 3. Can the source of the asset not be documented (unrecorded cash, an informal transfer abroad, family funds with no paper trail)? Then the asset itself is Row 1, at 60% of its value. 4. Check the ceilings separately: Row 1 items together must not exceed ₹1 crore, and the Row 2 aggregate must not exceed ₹5 crore. The point most often missed is that one taxpayer can need both rows. Form 1 records the Row 1 and Row 2 computations separately, so a single declaration can carry both. The ₹1 lakh Row 2 fee is payable once for all eligible Row 2 assets together, not per asset, as long as their aggregate value stays within ₹5 crore. 5. Worked Examples Illustrative only. Figures are rounded and assume the valuation is supportable. Example 1: RSU Holder, Never Filed Schedule FA Mr A, a resident software engineer in Pune, has held US-listed RSUs since FY 2020-21. The vesting value was taxed through Form 16 each year. Dividends of about ₹1,20,000 (cumulative, converted to INR) were never offered to tax. Value of the shares on 31.03.2026 is ₹45 lakh. Shares: the source was taxed, so Row 2, fee ₹1,00,000. Dividends: untaxed foreign income, so Row 1: 30% of ₹1,20,000 = ₹36,000, plus an equal additional amount = ₹72,000. Total: ₹1,72,000, with BMA immunity for both. Example 2: Returning NRI with a Dormant UK Account Ms B worked in London until 2022 and returned in FY 2022-23. She was RNOR for two years and has been ROR from FY 2024-25. Her UK savings account was funded entirely from UK salary earned while she was non-resident. Interest of ₹80,000 accrued while she was ROR and was not offered to tax. Account: acquired from non-resident earnings, so Row 2, ₹1,00,000. Interest for the ROR years: Row 1, ₹48,000 (60% of ₹80,000). Total: ₹1,48,000. Only her ROR years carry Schedule FA and global-income exposure. Interest from her RNOR years is generally outside the Indian tax net and should not be declared under Row 1. Example 3: Unexplained Foreign Brokerage Account Mr C, a resident, funded a foreign brokerage account over the years through transfers whose source he cannot document. Portfolio value on 31.03.2026, valued at the higher of cost and market price under Rule 3, is ₹40 lakh. Row 1: 30% of ₹40 lakh = ₹12 lakh, plus additional amount ₹12 lakh = ₹24 lakh. Under the regular BMA, the same asset would attract 30% tax plus a penalty equal to three times that tax under section 41 (levied at the Assessing Officer’s discretion), i.e. tax and penalty together of up to 120% of value, besides prosecution exposure. FAST-DS caps the cost at 60% and gives immunity. Example 4: The Bank-Account Valuation Trap Mrs D’s foreign account has a balance of ₹6 lakh on 31.03.2026. Over ten years, however, about ₹70 lakh of deposits have gone through it. Rule 3 values a foreign bank account at the sum of all deposits from the date of opening to the valuation date, not at the closing balance. It excludes deposits made from withdrawals out of the same account, and deposits already declared under Chapter VI of the BMA. If the deposits are unexplained, the Row 1 value is ₹70 lakh, so the payable amount is ₹42 lakh, against a balance of ₹6 lakh. If the deposits came from taxed or non-resident income, the account fits Row 2 at ₹1 lakh. The ₹70 lakh then counts only towards the ₹5 crore ceiling. Lesson: get the account statements from opening and reconcile deposits by source before choosing the row. For a long-running account, the choice of row is the whole case. 6. Valuation as on 31 March 2026 (Rule 3) General rule: fair market value is the higher of cost of acquisition and the open-market price on the valuation date, preferably backed by a valuer’s report. For most asset classes, where this valuation is not carried out, the indexed cost of acquisition is deemed to be the FMV. Quoted shares and bank accounts have their own formulas instead. Quoted shares and securities: higher of cost and the average of the day’s lowest and highest price on 31.03.2026. If there was no trade that day, use the nearest preceding trading date. Unquoted equity shares: higher of cost and the prescribed book-value-based formula. Immovable property abroad: higher of cost and the open-market price per a report from a valuer recognised by the government (or its agency) of the country where the property is located. Foreign bank account: sum of deposits, as in Example 4. Interest in a foreign firm, AOP or LLP: net assets allocated, first in the ratio of capital contributed, and the residue as per the dissolution clause or, failing that, the profit-sharing ratio. Reinvested proceeds: the original asset’s value is reduced by the amount reinvested, and the new asset is valued separately, so the same money is not counted twice. Variance tolerance (Rule 5(2)): for assets other than bank accounts, a variance of up to 20% from the declared FMV does not by itself invalidate the declaration. Exchange Rate For currencies designated by RBI, convert at the RBI reference rate on 31.03.2026. For other currencies, convert first to USD at the rate of the asset country’s central bank (or a regulated bank), then to INR at the RBI reference rate. 7. Procedure and Timelines The declaration is filed entirely online before the Pr. DGIT (Systems) / DGIT (Systems). Keep the acquisition evidence and the valuation report ready. Payment is made through Challan ITNS 289 on the e-filing portal (e-Pay Tax, under the Income-tax Act, 1961). AY 2026-27 and the minor head are filled in automatically (116 for non-corporate PANs, 115 for companies). A payment made under the wrong minor head cannot be corrected or refunded. Consequences and Immunity Benefits of a Valid Declaration A valid declaration gives: exclusion from total income: the declared income, or the investment in the declared asset, is not included in total income under the Income-tax Act, 1961 or the BMA (section 136); immunity from further tax, penalty and prosecution under the BMA for the declared asset or income (section 139); consideration in pending assessments (section 141). What the Scheme Does Not Provide What it does not give: no rectification, revision, set-off or relief on the declared income or asset in any existing proceeding under either Act; no reopening of a completed assessment in the declarant’s favour (section 137); no refund of amounts paid under the scheme, even if the declaration later fails (section 138); no protection for false particulars. Under section 134(3), a declaration is deemed invalid if any material particular in it is found to be false at any stage, or if the declarant breaches any condition of the scheme. The 20% valuation tolerance protects only good-faith valuation differences, not wrong facts. 9. Practical Pitfalls and Grey Areas 9.1 Residential-Status History Residential-status history. Map residence (NR / RNOR / ROR) year by year from the date each asset was acquired. Row 2’s “acquired while non-resident” test and the Row 1 income test both depend on this. 9.2 The ₹20 Lakh BMA Threshold The ₹20 lakh BMA threshold. Under the amended provisos to sections 42 and 43 of the BMA, penalty does not apply where the aggregate value of foreign assets (other than immovable property) does not exceed ₹20 lakh at any time during the year. The Finance Act, 2026 aligned prosecution under sections 49 and 50 with this threshold, retrospectively from 1 October 2024. For years before 1 October 2024, the proviso sheltered only foreign bank accounts with an aggregate balance of up to ₹5 lakh, so older omissions in shares, RSUs or pension accounts had no value-based protection at all. A client whose movable foreign assets stayed within the applicable limit in every affected year may reasonably choose to correct going forward instead of paying ₹1 lakh under Row 2. This threshold does not cover untaxed income, immovable property, or tax under the BMA. 9.3 Joint Holdings and Entities Joint holdings and entities. “Undisclosed foreign asset” includes a financial interest in an entity and assets held as beneficial owner. For joint accounts and holdings through a company or trust, decide who declares what, and avoid double declaration or gaps. 9.4 Partial Disclosure Partial disclosure. Declaring one account while leaving out another that the AIS already shows invites the false-particulars question. Disclose everything that fits, or record clearly why an item is outside the scheme. 9.5 Interaction with the AY 2026-27 ITR Interaction with the AY 2026-27 ITR. If the omission is only in the latest return, a revised return may be enough to correct it, but it gives no BMA immunity for earlier years. For multi-year omissions, FAST-DS is the cleaner route. 9.6 Schedule FA from Now On Schedule FA from now on. FAST-DS covers the past only. Every return from now on must report the asset correctly (on a calendar-year basis, under the correct table), or the problem starts again. 9.7 Documents and Records to Preserve Keep account statements from opening, RSU vesting and Form 16 records, proof of non-resident status (passport stamps, overseas pay slips, foreign tax returns), valuation reports, exchange-rate workings, and Forms 1 to 4 with challans. 10. Conclusion FAST-DS is not a general amnesty. It is a narrow, low-cost route for genuinely small cases. For the large group of salaried employees and returning NRIs whose only fault is a missing Schedule FA, ₹1 lakh for permanent BMA immunity is a fair bargain. For truly unexplained money, 60% is steep, but it is still well below what the regular BMA would cost. ****** Disclaimer: The views expressed are personal and for general information only.
