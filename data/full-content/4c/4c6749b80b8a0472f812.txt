Articles 2 min read Indian cos' overseas investment stock rises 41%; fin services account for 16% of FDI, RBI says The outstanding stock of overseas direct investment by Indian entities rose 40.8% year-on-year to Rs 16.71 lakh crore as of March 2026, outpacing the 8.5% growth in inward FDI stock. Financial and insurance activities accounted for 16.2% of India's outstanding FDI. The faster increase in outward investment moderated the ratio of inward FDI to ODI to 4.2 times at the end of March 2026, from 5.5 times a year earlier. Advt The RBI data showed that outward equity investment increased to Rs 13.30 lakh crore in March 2026 from Rs 10.09 lakh crore a year ago. Outward debt investment, meanwhile, almost doubled to Rs 3.41 lakh crore from Rs 1.77 lakh crore during the period.In comparison, inward FDI equity stock increased to Rs 68.45 lakh crore from Rs 63.04 lakh crore, while the debt component rose to Rs 2.27 lakh crore from Rs 2.13 lakh crore.Financial services account for 16% of FDI stockAmong sectors, financial and insurance activities accounted for Rs 11.45 lakh crore, or 16.2%, of India's total outstanding FDI stock of Rs 70.72 lakh crore as of March 2026.Manufacturing continued to account for the largest share of outstanding FDI, at 50.2%, followed by services at 44.8%. Within services, telecommunications, computer programming, consultancy, computing infrastructure and other information services accounted for 14.8% of total FDI stock.The United States was the largest source of outstanding FDI, accounting for 16.5% of the total, followed by Mauritius at 14.5%, Singapore at 13.7% and the United Kingdom at 10.8%.For outward direct investment, Singapore, the United Arab Emirates and the United States together accounted for more than half of the total outstanding ODI stock. Advt The RBI said changes in outstanding foreign assets and liabilities during a financial year can differ from the flows recorded in India's balance of payments statistics, as the former also include valuation changes arising from price and exchange-rate movements. The increase in the outstanding ODI stock, therefore, should not be read as an equivalent increase in fresh overseas investment flows.
