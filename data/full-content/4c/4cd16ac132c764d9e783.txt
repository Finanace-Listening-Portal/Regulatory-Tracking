Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Sunil Devchand Ghudka Vs ITO (ITAT Rajkot) Reassessment Cannot Stand Where the Section 151 Approval Is Neither Digitally Nor Manually Signed: ITAT Rajkot Unsigned Approval Invalidates Reassessment The Rajkot Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal allowed the assessee’s appeal after finding that the approval under Section 151 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, on which the reassessment was based, bore neither a digital nor a manual signature. The Tribunal followed its coordinate bench decision in M/s Aditya Plastic, which held that reassessment founded on an unsigned approval could not stand. The appeal was thus decided on the validity of the statutory approval, and the Tribunal did not examine the merits of the disputed cash deposit. Reassessment Concerned a ₹1.20 Lakh Cash Deposit The Assessing Officer passed an order on 27 February 2024 under Section 147 read with Section 144B for Assessment Year 2019–20. The officer treated a cash deposit of ₹1,20,000 in the assessee’s bank account as unexplained money under Section 69A and applied the tax rate prescribed by Section 115BBE. After the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre, dismissed the first appeal, the assessee approached the Tribunal. He challenged the dismissal of his first appeal, the jurisdiction to issue the notice under Section 148 and make the reassessment, and the addition on merits. At the hearing, the argument that decided the case was that the document relied on as the specified authority’s approval had never been signed, either manually or digitally. Tribunal Condones the 25-Day Delay The appeal before the Tribunal was filed 25 days late. The assessee supported his request for condonation with an affidavit explaining the delay, while the Revenue opposed the request. On examining the affidavit, the Tribunal found mitigating circumstances sufficient to exercise its discretion in the assessee’s favour. It condoned the delay and proceeded to consider the jurisdictional objection. Section 151 Approval Must Precede the Section 148 Notice The assessee relied on M/s Aditya Plastic, ITA No. 185/Rjt/2024, AY 2013–14. In that case, the Rajkot Bench had examined an approval attributed to the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax that bore no signature and for which no Document Identification Number (DIN) had been generated. It quashed the reassessment on the ground that there was no valid approval under Section 151. The coordinate bench had relied on the Allahabad High Court’s decision in Vikas Gupta v. Union of India, (2022) 142 taxmann.com 253 (Allahabad). There, the digital records showed that the prescribed authority had digitally signed the approval after issuance of the Section 148 notice. The High Court held that the Assessing Officer lacked jurisdiction when the notice was issued because the prescribed authority’s valid satisfaction had yet to be recorded. The later signature did not cure the defect in the notice. The two cases illustrate different defects in the same statutory step. In Vikas Gupta, the signature established when the approval was granted, and that time was too late. In Sunil Devchand Ghudka, the approval placed before the Tribunal lacked both forms of signature. The Tribunal applied Aditya Plastic because the unsigned record could not establish valid sanction for initiating the reassessment. Revenue Produced No Material to Displace the Precedent The Tribunal examined and reproduced the Section 151 approval in its order. It found that the approval bore neither form of signature and that the Revenue had produced no material to distinguish the case from the coordinate bench ruling. It therefore followed Aditya Plastic and allowed the assessee’s appeal. Although the Tribunal reproduced the earlier decision’s reference to a missing DIN, its finding in the present case centred on the absence of authentication by signature. The resulting relief flowed from the jurisdictional defect. The Tribunal made no separate finding on the source of the bank deposit or on the application of Section 115BBE to that deposit. Author’s Comments The decision underscores the importance of examining the approval itself when challenging a reassessment. A reference to sanction in the assessment record does not resolve whether the specified authority authenticated the approval. Where the approval is digital, the record may also show when the approval was authenticated. That timing can be decisive because the approval must exist when the Assessing Officer issues the notice. The practical distinction is between a document purporting to record approval and evidence that the competent authority actually granted it before the notice. In this case, the Tribunal inspected the approval and found no manual or digital signature. In Vikas Gupta, the authenticated record supplied the answer through its timestamp. Read together, the decisions show why both authentication and chronology matter to a jurisdictional challenge under Section 151. Once the Tribunal accepted that challenge, the assessee obtained relief without having to establish the source of the ₹1.20 lakh cash deposit. Cases Discussed M/s Aditya Plastic (ITAT Rajkot), ITA No. 185/Rjt/2024, AY 2013-14: Reassessment proceedings based on an approval under Section 151 lacking the signature of the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax were held invalid. Followed by the Tribunal. Vikas Gupta Vs Union of India (Allahabad High Court), (2022) 142 taxmann.com 253: A notice under Section 148 issued before the prescribed authority digitally authenticated its approval under Section 151 was held to be without jurisdiction. Since, the above approval under section 151 of the Act, is neither signed digitally nor manually, therefore, issue is squarely covered by the Coordinate Bench of ITAT Rajkot, in the case of M/s Aditya Plastic, in ITA No.185/Rjt/2024, for AY 2013-14. The findings of the Coordinate Bench are as follows: “8. We have heard both the parties, perused the material available on record. We note that the approval of the PCIT is without any signature of the Ld.PCIT and without generating any DIN number which is reproduced below: 8.1. Therefore, the Ld.Cousel for the assessee has stated that the approval u/s.151 of the Act dated 30/03/2020 is not signed by the Ld.PCIT and, hence, it is not considered as a valid approval/satisfaction and, thus, the notice u/s.148 of the Act issued on the basis of said approval is bad in law and without jurisdiction. For that, reliance is placed on the judgement of Hon’ble High Court of Allahabad in the case of Vikas Gupta reported at (2022) 142 taxmann.com 253 (Allahabad HC). The relevant part of judgement is reproduced: 13. Thus, as per provision of section 151 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, an assessing officer gets jurisdiction to issue notice to an assessee under section 148 of the Act, 1961 after Principal Chief Commissioner or Chief Commissioner or Principal Commissioner or Commissioner of Income-tax is satisfied on the reason recorded by the assessing officer that it is a fit case for issuing such notice. The date and time of the approval granted digitally under section 151 of the Act and the date and time of the notice under section 148 of the Act, shows that the satisfaction was recorded by the PCTT digitally after the notice under section 148 was digitally signed. 29. In the present set of facts there was no valid satisfaction recorded by the by the Prescribed Authority under section 151 of the Act, 1961 when the Assessing Officer issued notice to the assessee under section 148 of the Act, 1961. At the time when the notice under section 148 of the Act, 1961 was issued by the Assessing Officer to the petitioner there was no valid satisfaction recorded by the Prescribed Authority ie. the Principal Chief Commissioner or Chief Commissioner or Principal Commissioner or Commissioner. Subsequent to issuance of the notice under section 148 of the Act, 1961 by the Assessing Officer, the satisfaction under section 151 was digitally signed by the Prescribed Authority. Therefore, the point of time when the Assessing Officer issued notices under section 148, he was having no jurisdiction to issue the impugned notices under section 148 of the Act, 1961. Consequently the impugned notices issued by the Assessing Officer under section 148 of the Act, 1961 were without jurisdiction. The questions no. (a) and (b) are answered accordingly. 8.2. Therefore, based on these facts and circumstances of the case, the assessment order framed by the AO needs to be quashed as there was no approval u/s.151 of the Act for initiating the re-assessment proceedings by the Ld.PCIT, therefore, on this count the assessment order framed by the AO should be quashed.” 8. As the issue is squarely covered in favour of the assessee by the decision of Coordinate Bench of ITAT Rajkot, in the case of M/s Aditya Plastic, in ITA No. 185/Rjt/2024, for AY 2013-14 (supra), and there is no change in facts and law and the revenue is unable to produce any material to controvert the aforesaid findings of the Division Bench (supra). I find no reason to interfere in the said order of the Division Bench (supra). Therefore, respectfully following the binding precedent of the Coordinate Bench (supra), I allow the appeal of the assessee. 9. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed.
