Articles 3 min read RBI consolidates domestic money transfer rules, specifies transaction limits The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has consolidated its framework for domestic money transfers, prescribing transaction limits for cash-based remittances, mandating additional authentication for senders and requiring banks to strengthen monitoring of unusual fund transfers. Advt Two types of money transfersUnder the framework, RBI has distinguished between cash pay-out and cash pay-in services.Cash pay-out refers to transfers where money is sent from a customer's bank account to a recipient who does not have a bank account. The recipient can collect the amount in cash through an ATM, a Business Correspondent or another authorised payment channel.RBI has prescribed a maximum transfer limit of Rs 10,000 per transaction under this facility, subject to an overall monthly ceiling of Rs 25,000 per beneficiary. The remitting bank must also obtain and maintain the name and address of the beneficiary.Cash pay-in, on the other hand, allows a person to deposit cash through an authorised channel for transfer into a beneficiary's bank account.A walk-in customer at a bank branch can remit up to Rs 50,000 to a beneficiary's bank account through NEFT.For transfers facilitated through Business Correspondents, ATMs and other channels, RBI has prescribed a maximum of Rs 5,000 per transaction, with a monthly ceiling of Rs 25,000 per remitter.Sender verification and authenticationThe central bank has also prescribed identification and authentication requirements for cash pay-in services.Remitting banks and their Business Correspondents must register customers based on a verified mobile phone number and a self-certified Officially Valid Document (OVD), as prescribed under RBI's Know Your Customer Directions, 2025. Advt Additionally, every transaction undertaken by a remitter must be validated through an Additional Factor of Authentication (AFA).The directions also require remitting banks to include the details of the sender in the IMPS or NEFT transaction message, along with a specific identifier to distinguish cash-based remittances from other fund transfers.Banks and BCs must also comply with the applicable provisions of the Income-Tax Act, 2025, and related rules concerning cash deposits.Banks to monitor unusual transaction patternsRBI has asked banks and non-banks, as applicable, to put in place operational safeguards, including velocity checks and alerts to customers regarding credits received into their accounts through these facilities. Advt Any unusual increase in the volume of credits into a particular account or a group of accounts must be investigated immediately. Suspicious transactions must also be reported to the appropriate authorities.The central bank expects transfers under the framework to be completed on a real-time or near-real-time basis. Inter-bank settlement must be carried out only through RBI-approved payment systems.Banks and non-banks can determine reasonable charges for domestic money transfer services, while customer grievances arising from these transactions will fall under the purview of the RBI-Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026. Advt The latest directions subsume and repeal two earlier RBI circulars, the October 5, 2011 circular on Domestic Money Transfer, Relaxations and the July 24, 2024 circular on Domestic Money Transfer, Review of Framework.Card-to-card transfers have been excluded from the scope of the new directions and will continue to be governed by the guidelines and approvals applicable to the respective payment instruments.
