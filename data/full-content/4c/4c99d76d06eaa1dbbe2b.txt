He also cautioned that debt-financed AI investments could become increasingly risky if inflation remains elevated.AI Stock Selloff Raises ConcernsMarket jitters intensified in October as investors pulled money out of technology stocks and moved towards safer assets. More than $2 trillion in market value was wiped off the Nasdaq 100 as concerns grew over whether massive AI investments would generate adequate returns.Reuters reported that credit insurance costs for some technology companies surged, while several proposed data centre projects struggled to secure financing.The selloff triggered a flight towards government bonds. According to Danske Bank estimates, major US technology companies are expected to spend around $1.2 trillion on AI infrastructure next year, following an estimated $900 billion this year. Advt Technology and AI-related stocks account for more than 40% of the S&P 500 and have driven much of its gains over the past five years.Former Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation chair Sheila Bair warned that growing reliance on debt among AI companies and expanding financial-sector lending could create vulnerabilities similar to those seen before the 2008 financial crisis.Iran War and Record Debt Add to RisksThe prolonged Iran war has disrupted oil supplies and pushed crude prices to around $105 per barrel, up nearly 75% this year. Higher energy costs have intensified inflationary pressures and weakened currencies in emerging economies, including Indonesia and the Philippines. Advt Reuters noted that global debt has exceeded $350 trillion, according to the Institute of International Finance, leaving governments with limited room to respond to another major economic crisis.The Bank of England has also warned that geopolitical uncertainty increases the likelihood of major global shocks that could compound one another.Meanwhile, foreign investors' net purchases of US securities reached nearly $280 billion in August, a monthly record, according to Treasury International Capital data.
