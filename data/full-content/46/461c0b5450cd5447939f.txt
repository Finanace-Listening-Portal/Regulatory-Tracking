Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Urban Improvement Trust Vs ACIT (ITAT Jaipur) Urban Improvement Trust Not Immune Under Article 289: ITAT Rejects Exemptions, Remands Income Computation Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Jaipur Bench, in Urban Improvement Trust, Sikar v. ACIT, rejected claims of constitutional immunity and statutory exemptions for the disputed years. However, it directed fresh examination of whether specified receipts represented the trust’s income or amounts held for the State Government and other parties. The common order dated 22 October 2026 covered Assessment Years 2014–15 to 2019–20, in ITA Nos. 649, 651, 652, 654, 657 and 658/JPR/2025. The Bench comprised Shri Kuldip Singh, Judicial Member, and Shri Prakash, Accountant Member. Five appeals were partly allowed for statistical purposes, while the appeal for AY 2019–20 was dismissed. Background and Registration Status The Rajasthan Government established the trust under the Rajasthan Urban Improvement Act, 1959, on 21 January 2013 for improving Sikar’s urban areas. Its activities included land conversion, issuing pattas, auctioning government land and allotting houses and land to economically weaker sections. The trust filed no returns for AYs 2014–15 to 2018–19. Its accounts, subsequently furnished to the Department, disclosed substantial annual surpluses. Registration under Section 12AA was granted on 5 August 2019, effective from AY 2019–20. The effective date of that registration was not challenged. The Assessing Officer reopened the earlier years and assessed the surpluses after denying exemption. Reassessment Challenges Rejected The Tribunal upheld the reassessments for AYs 2014–15 to 2018–19. Notices were issued in March 2021 within the applicable limitation period and after approval under Section 151. The trust had not filed returns despite surpluses exceeding the taxable threshold. Its own accounts supplied the basis for the recorded reasons. The Tribunal also rejected reliance on the protection against reopening only for non-registration under Section 12A(2). The reopening rested on non-filing of returns and the taxability of income, rather than non-registration alone. For AY 2019–20, no specific defect in the procedure followed under Sections 148A and 148 was established. Statutory Exemption Claims Failed The claim under Section 10(20) failed following the Supreme Court’s decision in ITO v. Urban Improvement Trust (2018) 409 ITR 1. The Tribunal held that an Urban Improvement Trust constituted under the Rajasthan legislation was not covered by the relevant definition of local authority. The trust was also not notified under Section 10(46) for the disputed years. Its notification under Section 10(46A) expressly operated from AY 2026–27. The Tribunal held that neither it nor the Assessing Officer could advance the commencement date fixed by the Central Government. Separate Legal Personality Defeated Article 289 Claim The trust argued that it constituted “State” and that its income enjoyed immunity under Article 289(1) of the Constitution. The Tribunal rejected this argument, applying Adityapur Industrial Area Development Authority v. Union of India and Andhra Pradesh State Road Transport Corporation v. ITO. The trust was a separate body corporate, capable of holding property, contracting, and suing or being sued in its own name. Its income therefore did not automatically become State Government income. The Tribunal distinguished status as a State instrumentality under Article 12 from ownership of income protected by Article 289. Public functions and governmental control were insufficient by themselves. Earlier Years Could Not Obtain Section 11 Relief For AYs 2014–15 to 2018–19, the trust relied on the proviso to Section 12A(2) to extend registration benefits backwards. The Tribunal held that the proviso required assessment proceedings to be pending when registration was granted. On 5 August 2019, no returns or assessment proceedings existed for those years. An earlier request for information under Section 133(6) was an inquiry, not an assessment proceeding. Reassessment began only in March 2021. The trust had also furnished no Form 10B or computation of charitable application and accumulation for those years. Its exemption claim therefore failed. Income Computation Required Independent Examination The Tribunal nevertheless accepted that rejection of exemption did not resolve whether every receipt constituted taxable income. The trust claimed that 40% of premium money, 60% of lease money and BSUP fees belonged to the State Government. It also referred to public advances later refunded. Whether these receipts were diverted by overriding title, represented liabilities, or constituted income required examination of statutory instruments, accounts and remittance or refund evidence. The Tribunal restored income computation for AYs 2014–15 to 2018–19 to the Assessing Officer. The exemption and constitutional issues already decided could not be reopened during that exercise. AY 2019–20 and Final Outcome For AY 2019–20, registration was effective. The CIT(A) had already directed verification of the Section 11 claim involving Rs. 7,52,22,813. The Tribunal upheld that direction, refusing to allow an unverified claim outright. It also rejected the additional challenge based on failure to specify a taxing provision or income head, while leaving correct classification for fresh computation where necessary. The first five appeals were partly allowed for statistical purposes. The AY 2019–20 appeal was dismissed, preserving the existing verification directions. We have heard the rival submissions and perused the material on record. The following questions arise for our decision: i) the validity of the reassessments; ii) whether the income of the assessee is exempt under section 10(20), section 10(46) or section 10(46A) of the Act, or is immune from tax under Article 289 of the Constitution; iii) whether the assessee is entitled to exemption under sections 11 and 12 of the Act; iv) the additional ground regarding head of income for taxation; and v) the computation of the income. Validity of the reassessments (Ground No. 1) 15. For assessment years 2014-15 to 2018-19, the notices under section 148 of the Act were issued in March 2021, under the law as it stood before 01.04.2021. The assessee had filed no return for any of these years, although the surplus shown in its own accounts far exceeded the maximum amount not chargeable to tax. Under Explanation 2(a) to section 147 of the Act, such a case is deemed to be one where income has escaped assessment. The notices were issued within the period allowed by section 149 of the Act and after the approval under section 151 of the Act. The certified copies of the reasons and approvals have been placed by the assessee itself in its paper book, and no specific defect in them has been pointed out. 16. We have also considered whether the second proviso to section 12A(2) of the Act bars the reopening. That proviso prohibits action under section 147 of the Act for an earlier year “only for non-registration” of the trust. The reasons recorded in the present case rest on the failure of the assessee to file any return despite having a surplus far above the taxable limit, and on the taxability of its income after the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court on section 10(20) of the Act. The reference to the date of registration is only one of the facts narrated. The reopening was, therefore, not “only for non-registration”. The plea that there could be no reason to believe because the income is constitutionally immune depends on the constitutional plea, which we discuss below. 17. For assessment year 2019-20, the notice under section 148 of the Act was issued on 25.03.2023 after following the procedure under section 148A of the Act. The assessee did not object to the reopening and filed its return in response to the notice. No specific infirmity in the procedure has been pointed out before us. Ground No. 1 is, therefore, dismissed for all the years. Sections 10(20), 10(46) and 10(46A) of the Act and Article 289 of the Constitution (Ground No. 3) 18. The claim under section 10(20) of the Act is no longer open. In ITO v. Urban Improvement Trust (supra), the Hon’ble Supreme Court examined the scheme of the 1959 Act, under which the assessee is constituted, and held in paragraph 33 as under: “33. In above case, this Court, thus, has held that the expression “Municipal Committee and District Board” occurring in Clause (iii) of Explanation referred to those bodies, which are discharging municipal functions and power in any part of country and so far has not been substituted by any other Body by any Act of Legislature. The word “Municipal Committee” occurring in Clause (iii) Explanation, thus, has a definite purpose and object. Purpose and object was to cover those bodies, which are discharging municipal functions but are not covered by the definition of municipalities as was required to be constituted by Article 243Q of the Constitution of India. Urban Improvement Trust constituted under the Rajasthan Urban Improvement Act, 1959, thus, cannot be held to be covered by the definition of Municipal Committee as contained in Clause (iii) of Explanation to Section 10(20) of the I.T. Act. Further, as noticed above, prior to deletion of Section 10(20A), Section 10(20A) was a provision which exempted the income of authority constituted in India by or under any law enacted for the purpose of planning, development or improvement of cities, towns and villages or for both. There cannot be any dispute that Urban Improvement Trust, i.e. the assessee was fully covered by the definition of authorities as contained in Section 10(20A) prior to its deletion. When there is a specific deletion of Section 10(20A), the said deletion was for an object and purpose. The Explanatory Notes in Paragraph Nos. 13.1 to 13.4 as noticed above clearly mentioned that “income of certain Housing Boards etc. to become taxable”. The deletion of authorities, which were enumerated in Section 10(20A) was a clear indicator that such authorities, which were enjoying exemption under Section 10(20A) shall no longer be entitled to enjoy the exemption henceforth. The deletion of Section 10(20A) thus has to be given a purpose and meaning.” 19. The plea that this judgment should operate only prospectively cannot be accepted. A judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court declares the law as it always stood, unless the Court itself limits its operation, and the Hon’ble Supreme Court applied its view to the very assessment years that were before it. The claims under sections 10(46) and 10(46A) of the Act also fail. Both clauses require the body to be notified by the Central Government. The assessee was not notified under section 10(46) of the Act for any of these years. On its own application, it has been notified under section 10(46A) of the Act by Notification No. 25/2026 dated 24.03.2026, which states that it “shall be effective from the assessment year 2026-27”. Neither the Tribunal nor the AO can advance the date fixed by the Central Government in exercise of its statutory power. 20. We now come to the main plea of the assessee, which is based on Article 289 of the Constitution. Article 289 reads as under: “289. Exemption of property and income of a State from Union taxation.— (1) The property and income of a State shall be exempt from Union taxation. (2) Nothing in clause (1) shall prevent the Union from imposing, or authorising the imposition of, any tax to such extent, if any, as Parliament may by law provide in respect of a trade or business of any kind carried on by, or on behalf of, the Government of a State, or any operations connected therewith, or any property used or occupied for the purposes of such trade or business, or any income accruing or arising in connection therewith. (3) Nothing in clause (2) shall apply to any trade or business, or to any class of trade or business, which Parliament may by law declare to be incidental to the ordinary functions of Government.” 21. The immunity under clause (1) is for the “income of a State”. Clauses (2) and (3) come into play only after it is found that the income in question is the income of the State. The question, therefore, is not whether the assessee performs public functions or is under the control of the State Government, but whether its income is the income of the State. This very question, in relation to a development authority constituted under a State enactment, was answered by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Adityapur Industrial Area Development Authority (supra), where it was held in paragraph 9 as under: “9. It is true, as submitted by Sri Venugopal, that Clause (2) of Article 289 empowers the Parliament to make a law, imposing a tax on income earned only from trade or business of any kind carried by or on behalf of the State. It does not authorize the Parliament to impose a tax on the income of a State if such income is not earned in the manner contemplated by Clause (2) of Article 289. This, to our mind, does not answer the question which arises for our consideration in this appeal. Clause (2) of Article 289 presupposes that the income sought to be taxed by the Union is the income of the State, but the question to be answered at the threshold is whether in terms of Clause (1) of Article 289, the income of the appellant/Authority is the income of the State. Having regard to the provisions of the Bihar Industrial Areas Development Authority Act, 1974, particularly section 17 thereof, we have no manner of doubt that the income of the appellant/Authority constituted under the said Act is its own income and that the appellant/Authority manages its own funds. It has its own assets and liabilities. It can sue or be sued in its own name. Even though, it does not carry on any trade or business within the contemplation of Clause (2) of Article 289, it still is an Authority constituted under an Act of the Legislature of the State having a distinct legal personality, being a body corporate, as distinct from the State. Section 17 of the Act further clarifies that only upon its dissolution its assets, funds and liabilities devolve upon the State Government. Necessarily therefore, before its dissolution, its assets, funds and liabilities are its own. It is, therefore, futile to contend that the income of the appellant/Authority is the income of State Government, even though the Authority is constituted under an Act enacted by the State Legislature by issuance of a Notification by the Government thereunder.” 22. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in that judgment followed the Constitution Bench in Andhra Pradesh State Road Transport Corporation (supra) and concluded in paragraph 20 that the authority “could not claim exemption from Union taxation under Article 289(1) of the Constitution of India”. The position of the assessee is no different. Section 8(2) of the 1959 Act provides that every Trust “shall be a body corporate by the aforesaid name having perpetual succession and a common seal with power to acquire, hold and dispose of property both movable and immovable and to contract and shall by the said name sue and be sued”. Under section 105(2)(a) of the 1959 Act, its properties, funds and dues vest in the Municipal Board only on its dissolution. If anything, the case of the assessee is weaker than that of the authority in Adityapur, whose assets were to devolve on the State Government on dissolution. The distinction sought to be drawn by the learned AR, that the authority in Adityapur promoted industries while the assessee develops urban areas, does not touch the ratio of that judgment. The Hon’ble Supreme Court decided the case on the separate legal personality of the authority, and expressly proceeded on the footing that it did not carry on any trade or business. Similarly, the State Government’s order that certain shares of specified receipts are to be remitted to the State may, at best, bear on the quantum of income. It cannot convert the entire income of the assessee into the income of the State. 23. The tests under Article 12 of the Constitution, relied upon by the learned AR, are of no assistance. Article 12 defines “the State” for the purposes of Part III of the Constitution, so that fundamental rights can be enforced against bodies that are instrumentalities of the State. An instrumentality of the State is not, for that reason, the State itself whose income is protected by Article 289. Article 285, which exempts the property of the Union from taxes imposed by a State, has no application to Union income tax on a body constituted by a State. We may also note that when the Hon’ble Rajasthan High Court held Urban Improvement Trusts to be local authorities on the reasoning that their functions are statutory and are carried on for the benefit of the State Government, the Hon’ble Supreme Court in ITO v. Urban Improvement Trust (supra) observed in paragraph 39 that the said reasoning “cannot lead to the conclusion” drawn and set aside the judgments. Statutory or public functions do not, by themselves, take the income of a separate legal entity outside the charge of tax. The conduct of the assessee is also consistent with this position. It sought registration as a charitable institution in 2019 and applied for a conditional notification under section 10(46A) of the Act in 2025. 24. This brings us to the order of the coordinate Bench in the case of Urban Improvement Trust, Kota, on which the assessee has founded its case. The question before us, namely whether the income of a body constituted under a State enactment is the income of the State within Article 289(1) of the Constitution, is directly answered by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Adityapur Industrial Area Development Authority (supra). We also note the submission of the learned DR that the Department has filed appeals against that order before the Hon’ble Rajasthan High Court. Respectfully, we follow the ratio of Adityapur Industrial Area Development Authority (supra) and Andhra Pradesh State Road Transport Corporation (supra). Ground No. 3 is dismissed for all the years. Exemption under sections 11 and 12 of the Act (Ground No. 2) 25. For assessment years 2014-15 to 2018-19, the assessee had no registration. Its registration under section 12AA of the Act was granted on 05.08.2019 with effect from assessment year 2019-20, and that order was not challenged. The claim is, therefore, based only on the first proviso to section 12A(2) of the Act, which, as it stood when the registration was granted, read as under: “Provided that where registration has been granted to the trust or institution under section 12AA, then, the provisions of sections 11 and 12 shall apply in respect of any income derived from property held under trust of any assessment year preceding the aforesaid assessment year, for which assessment proceedings are pending before the Assessing Officer as on the date of such registration and the objects and activities of such trust or institution remain the same for such preceding assessment year: Provided further that no action under section 147 shall be taken by the Assessing Officer in case of such trust or institution for any assessment year preceding the aforesaid assessment year only for non-registration of such trust or institution for the said assessment year:” 26. The benefit of the first proviso is available for an earlier year only if assessment proceedings for that year are pending before the AO “as on the date of such registration”. The CBDT Circular No. 1/2015 dated 21.01.2015, which explains the amendment, says the same thing in paragraph 8.3: “8.3. In order to provide relief to such trusts and remove hardship in genuine cases, section 12A of the Income-tax Act has been amended to provide that in a case where a trust or institution has been granted registration under section 12AA of the Income-tax Act, the benefit of sections 11 and 12 of the said Act shall be available in respect of any income derived from property held under trust in any assessment proceeding for an earlier assessment year which is pending before the Assessing Officer as on the date of such registration, if the objects and activities of such trust or institution in the relevant earlier assessment year are the same as those on the basis of which such registration has been granted.” 27. The Hon’ble Rajasthan High Court in CIT (Exemptions) v. Shree Shyam Mandir Committee (D.B. Income Tax Appeal No. 234/2016, judgment dated 23.10.2017) held that the proviso operates retrospectively and that proceedings pending in appeal are to be treated as pending before the AO. In that case, as in the decisions of the Telangana High Court and the Agra Bench relied upon by the learned AR, the proceedings were already pending when the registration was granted. In the present case, on 05.08.2019 no return had been filed and no assessment proceeding was pending for any of the years from 2014-15 to 2018-19. The only step taken before that date was a call for information under section 133(6) of the Act, which is an enquiry and not an assessment proceeding. The reassessment proceedings began only with the notices issued in March 2021, about nineteen months after the registration. The condition of pendency on the date of registration is, therefore, not satisfied. In Hyderabad Metropolitan Development Authority (supra), registration itself was granted from an earlier year in the registration proceedings, which is not the position here. The plea that the assessee did not apply for registration earlier because it believed itself to be a local authority is a plea of hardship, and it cannot override the plain words of the proviso. 28. There is a further difficulty for the assessee. Exemption under section 11 of the Act depends on the application of income to charitable purposes, which has to be computed and verified. The assessee filed no return, furnished no audit report in Form No. 10B and placed no computation of application or accumulation of income for these years before any authority. Ground No. 2 for assessment years 2014-15 to 2018-19 is, therefore, dismissed. 29. For assessment year 2019-20, the registration under section 12AA of the Act was in force. The AO denied the claim only because the return was not filed under section 139(1) of the Act. The learned CIT(A) has allowed the ground of the assessee in principle in view of the registration and has directed the AO to verify the claim for exemption under section 11 of the Act as per law and to recompute the income. The Revenue is not in appeal against that direction. The application and accumulation of income of Rs 7,52,22,813 claimed in the return have not so far been verified by any authority. We see no reason to direct the AO to allow the claim without such verification. Grounds No. 2 and 4 for assessment year 2019-20 are, therefore, dismissed, and the direction of the learned CIT(A) is upheld. Additional ground 30. The additional ground raises a legal question on facts already on record, and we admit it in the light of National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. (supra). On merits, however, the ground has no force. Income is charged under section 4 of the Act on the total income, and the heads of income in section 14 of the Act are only for the purpose of computation. The assessee knew throughout what was being taxed, namely the surplus of its own income and expenditure account, and it has contested only whether that surplus is exempt. For assessment years 2014-15, 2017-18 and 2018-19, the AO expressly assessed the surplus as “Income from Other Sources”. For assessment year 2019-20, the provision invoked is section 11 of the Act, under which the claim was disallowed. The decisions relied upon by the learned AR concern additions of unexplained credits or deposits where the AO did not identify which of the deeming provisions applied, and they are distinguishable. The failure to state the head of income for assessment years 2015-16 and 2016-17 does not invalidate the assessments, and the head will be determined by the AO in the fresh computation directed below. The additional ground is dismissed. Computation of income (Ground No. 4) 31. The AO assessed the entire surplus of each year as income. Before the learned CIT(A), the assessee claimed that specified portions of the premium money, lease money and BSUP fees were collected on behalf of the State Government and were not its income, and before us it has also claimed that advances received from the public under a scheme, which were later refunded, were credited as income. The learned CIT(A) did not examine these claims on merits and rejected them only because the registration was effective from assessment year 2019-20. That reason has no bearing on whether a particular receipt is the income of the assessee. Whether any part of the receipts was diverted at source by an overriding title, or was a liability rather than income, is a question of fact which has to be decided on the statutory instruments, the accounting treatment and the evidence of remittance or refund. The learned DR has also pointed out discrepancies in the figures claimed. None of these aspects has been examined by the AO. 32. In these circumstances, we set aside the orders of the learned CIT(A) on Ground No. 4 for assessment years 2014-15 to 2018-19 and restore the computation of the income of the assessee for these years to the file of the AO for a fresh determination in accordance with law, after giving the assessee reasonable opportunity of being heard. The issues of chargeability decided above, namely the claims under sections 10(20), 10(46) and 10(46A) of the Act, under Article 289 of the Constitution and under sections 11 and 12 of the Act, shall not be reopened. Subject to this, the AO shall be free to examine all aspects of the computation, including the head of income, on the material that the assessee may produce and the material on record, including the inspection reports of the Accountant General filed before us, which we have not examined on merits. The assessee shall cooperate and furnish the necessary evidence. Ground No. 4 for these years is allowed for statistical purposes. 33. Ground No. 5 relating to interest under sections 234A, 234B and 234C of the Act is consequential and does not require separate adjudication. Ground No. 6 for all the years is general in nature and needs no adjudication. 34. In the result, the appeals filed by the Assessee in ITA Nos. 649, 651, 652, 654 and 657/JPR/2025 are partly allowed for statistical purposes, and the appeal in ITA No. 658/JPR/2025 is dismissed.
