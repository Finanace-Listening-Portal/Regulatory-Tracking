Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); 24 X 7 Total Water Solutions Vs Assistant Commissioner of Commercial Taxes (Karnataka High Court) Summary: The Karnataka High Court, in 24 X 7 Total Water Solutions Vs Assistant Commissioner of Commercial Taxes, partly allowed a writ petition challenging a GST adjudication order dated 13 August 2024 under Section 73(9) of the Karnataka Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. The dispute related to FY 2019-20 and arose from an alleged mismatch between input tax credit reflected in Form GSTR-2A and the credit claimed in Form GSTR-3B. The petitioner, a proprietorship engaged in the repair and sale of water purifiers and presently known as OXSO International, had not participated in the original adjudication proceedings. It contended that it became aware of the adverse order only when its banker received a recovery notice dated 20 February 2026 in Form GST DRC-13. The Court quashed the adjudication order and restored the proceedings, subject to the petitioner filing its response and supporting documents by 30 October 2026 and depositing 10% of the tax proposed in the show cause notice. The petitioner had approached the High Court under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution of India, seeking to set aside the adjudication order issued in Form GST DRC-07 and the consequential demand of tax, interest and penalty. The petition also sought withdrawal of recovery proceedings, release of its bank account and refund of any amount already recovered. Its principal grievance was that input tax credit had been questioned because of a mismatch between GSTR-2A and GSTR-3B without proper consideration of the underlying transactions. The petitioner maintained that it could establish the genuineness of the purchases through purchase invoices, evidence of payments to suppliers and e-way bills. Appearing for the petitioner, counsel requested the Court to restore the proceedings arising from the show cause notice dated 7 May 2024, so that the taxpayer could submit its explanation and supporting evidence. The petitioner explained that it had not become aware of the adjudication proceedings because the relevant notices and orders were uploaded on the GST portal without being brought to its attention. According to the petitioner, the recovery notice served on its banker was the first occasion on which it learned of the adjudication order. The requested relief was therefore directed towards securing an opportunity to contest the demand on evidence rather than obtaining a final determination of ITC eligibility in the writ proceedings. The State, represented by the High Court Government Pleader, pointed out that the petitioner had failed to respond both to the observations communicated in Form GST ASMT-10 dated 15 February 2024 and to the show cause notice dated 7 May 2024. The State maintained that these communications had been uploaded on the GST portal and sought permission to place supporting portal printouts on record. The Court considered these submissions while examining whether the petitioner should nevertheless receive another opportunity to participate in adjudication. The High Court observed that where a mismatch for FY 2019-20 resulted from the supplier’s failure to update relevant information, thereby affecting the automatic population of GSTR-2A, the registered taxpayer could rely upon CBIC Circular No. 193/05/2023-GST and produce documents demonstrating the genuineness of the transactions. The Court noted that, in comparable circumstances, adjudication orders had been set aside and proceedings restored where taxpayers had provided bona fide explanations for their earlier non-participation. This observation recognised the need to consider documentary evidence rather than treating the return mismatch as the final determination of eligibility. On the facts, the Court accepted the petitioner’s explanation that it had not become aware of the proceedings because the notices and orders were only uploaded on the portal and the persons concerned had not informed it. The Court considered the explanation bona fide and concluded that the petitioner should receive a fresh opportunity to respond to the show cause notice. However, the Court did not grant unconditional relief. It directed the petitioner to submit its response, together with documents establishing the genuineness of the transactions underlying the GSTR-3B declarations, by 30 October 2026 and to deposit 10% of the tax proposed in the show cause notice. Accordingly, the High Court quashed the adjudication order dated 13 August 2024 and restored the proceedings under the show cause notice dated 7 May 2024. The writ petition was allowed in part. The order did not finally decide whether the disputed ITC was admissible under Section 16, nor did it expressly grant every consequential relief concerning the bank account, recovery or refund sought in the petition. The decision illustrates that, in appropriate circumstances, a taxpayer facing a historical GSTR-2A/GSTR-3B mismatch may be permitted to establish the genuineness of purchases through supporting documents, subject to the conditions imposed by the Court. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT The petitioner has impugned the Adjudication Order dated 13.08.2024 [Annexure – B] in FORM GST DRC-07 under Section 73(9) of the Karnataka Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 [for short, ‘the KGST Act’]. The petitioner, who has not participated in the adjudication proceedings, asserts that it has come to know about the Adjudication Order only with its Banker being served with the Recovery Notice dated 20.02.2026 in Form GST DRC-13 [Annexure – C]. 2. Ms. Pratibha Vasantrao Rajeshwarkar, the learned counsel for the petitioner, submits that this Court could consider interfering with the Adjudication Order and restoring the proceedings with opportunity to the petitioner to file response to the Show Cause Notice dated 07.05.2024; and in elaboration, the learned counsel submits that the Adjudication Order relates to the financial year 2019-20 and it is because of an alleged mismatch between Form GSTR-2A and GSTR-3B and that the petitioner can produce documents such as purchase invoices, details of the payments to the supplier and E-way bills to justify the genuineness of the transaction. 3. Ms. Malavika Prasad, a learned High Court Government Pleader, is heard for the disposal of the petition to restore the proceedings for reconsideration with opportunity as now asked for. The learned High Court Government Pleader, relying upon the contents of the Adjudication Order, submits that the petitioner has neither responded to the audit observations in Form GST ASMT-10 dated 15.02.2024 nor to the Show Cause Notice dated 07.05.2024 and the petitioner cannot dispute that these have been uploaded on the Portal. In fact, the learned High Court Government Pleader seeks leave to place on record copy of the printout in these regards. 4. However, what is not in contest is that if the reason for the mismatch during the year 2019-20 is because of the supplier’s failure to update the data and the consequential auto population in GSTR-2A, it would be open to a Registered Taxable Person [RTP], given the terms of the Circular in No.193/05/2023, to produce the documents to justify the genuineness of the transactions. It is also not in dispute that in similar circumstances this Court, based on the bonafides assigned not to participate in the adjudication proceedings, has quashed the Adjudication Orders and the concerned RTP is extended an opportunity to respond to the Show Cause Notice on terms. 5. In this case as well, this Court is persuaded to opine that the petitioner is bonafide in contending that it could not know of the proceedings because the notices and the orders are only uploaded on the Portal and the concerned did not inform the petitioner about these. There must be restoration of the proceedings quashing the Adjudication Order with the petitioner being put on terms. Hence, the following. ORDER [A] The petition is allowed in-part. [B] The Adjudication Order dated 13.08.2024 [Annexure – B] is quashed restoring the proceedings under the Show Cause Notice dated 07.05.2024 with opportunity to the petitioner without further notice to file a response with the respondent by 30.10.2026. [C] The petitioner shall file on this day its response along with the documents to show the genuineness of the transactions based upon which the declaration is made in Form GSTR-3B by 30.10.2026 and deposit 10% of the tax that is proposed in the Show Cause Notice.
