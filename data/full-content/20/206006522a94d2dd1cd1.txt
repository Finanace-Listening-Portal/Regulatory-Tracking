Articles 1 min read Cross a National Highway toll plaza without valid insurance and get an SMS within minutes, NHAI begins Delhi pilot to alert vehicle owners of expired policies and warn them of penalties under Motor Vehicles Act NHAI has initiated a pilot program at Mundka toll plaza in Delhi for uninsured vehicle alerts. After passing through the toll, vehicle details will be checked against the Vahan database for insurance validity. If the insurance is expired, owners will receive an SMS with relevant details and renewal reminders. This system aims to prompt timely insurance renewals and reduce uninsured driving incidents. Dipak K Dash TNN Published On Nov 18, 2026 at 08:17 AM IST var news_msid = 135946447; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook NEW DELHI: If your vehicle does not have valid third-party insurance, which is mandatory, you will receive an SMS alert within minutes of crossing a toll plaza on a National Highway.The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) has launched a pilot at Mundka toll plaza on UER-II in Delhi and it will be extended across the country soon.TOI has learnt that the trial will be carried out for stabilisation of the system that aims at nudging vehicle owners to renew their insurance in time. Once a vehicle passes through a toll plaza, the details are shared with the Vahan database hosted by NIC, where an automated backend check verifies the validity of insurance. Advt “If the policy has expired or the vehicle is uninsured, an SMS alert is sent to the owner with details of the registration number, the time when it crossed and that the insurance has expired. The SMS mentions the expiry date and advises such vehicle owners to renew them so as to avoid penalty under Motor Vehicles Act,” said an official.The move is aimed at nudging vehicle owners to renew their insurance on time, given that uninsured vehicles remain a major concern, particularly when they are involved in road accidents, leaving victims with limited avenues for compensation.Under the current law, uninsured vehicle owners face the risk of paying three times the vehicle’s base premium, or Rs 5,000, whichever is higher for first offence.
