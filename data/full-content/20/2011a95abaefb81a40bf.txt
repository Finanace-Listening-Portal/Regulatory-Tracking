Articles 4 min read Bandhan Bank sees Rs 200 crore annual hit from IRDAI distribution draft; seeks cost linked commission Bandhan Bank expects the proposed IRDAI distribution norms to impact its third party income by around Rs 200 crore annually, MD & CEO Partha Pratim Sengupta told ETBFSI and that the bank has represented directly to the regulator seeking recognition of the costs, technology investments and effort involved in insurance distribution. He said the bank generated Rs 150 crore in third-party income in Q2 FY27 and around Rs 120 crore in Q1, with the potential hit estimated at about Rs 200 crore on an annual basis if the proposed framework is implemented in a manner that affects its current distribution economics. Advt The bank's investor presentation shows third-party income at Rs 155.5 crore in Q2 FY27, up 25.9 per cent sequentially and 26 per cent year-on-year. For H1 FY27, third-party income stood at Rs 279 crore, up 34.6 per cent year-on-year. Third-party income was the largest component of Bandhan's non-interest income after processing fees during the quarter.Bandhan asks IRDAI to factor in distribution effort, technology costsSengupta said Bandhan has made its own representation directly to IRDAI. Per Sengupta, their representation has suggested that the commission cannot be determined only as a percentage of the premium or product value, but should also compensate institutions for the effort involved in distributing insurance products.“The commission cannot be only distinct from the percentage, but it also needs to compensate the efforts that are being put in,” Sengupta said and spoke of the technology investments required as part of the distribution process, particularly to guard against mis-selling.“It requires a lot of technological changes also, technological investments also, specially to guard the mis-selling and other things,” he said, adding that these factors also need to be taken into account while determining the commission structure.This is particularly relevant for Bandhan given its distribution model. Sengupta said the bank's insurance business is predominantly retail and has a greater presence in semi-urban and rural areas. The bank, he added, does not undertake credit-linked insurance. Advt Bank to shift mix, deepen MF and wholesale fee incomePer the CEO, Bandhan is already making changes to its business model and expects to recover a substantial portion of the potential income loss through other sources.The bank is looking to shift its insurance strategy towards Tier-2, Tier-3 and Tier-4 cities, where it believes commission rates are higher and where Bandhan has a relatively larger presence. It is also increasing its focus on mutual fund distribution and is in discussions with insurance companies on product design, both at the group and individual level.“We are already making some changes in the business model and we are well-positioned to mitigate these changes,” Sengupta said. He pointed to continued business growth, product optimisation, deeper customer engagement and diversification of fee-income streams as the key levers. Advt The bank is also looking at wholesale banking as an alternative source of fee income. Sengupta said wholesale banking has already started generating profits and that the focus is shifting from vanilla lending income towards other income, particularly through trade finance and forex.“You will find that we are introducing new products in our wholesale banking, which has started giving the results, and in the future, it will give much, much more other income,” he said, pointing to mutual funds, forex and other wholesale banking activities as areas where Bandhan sees scope to scale non-insurance income. Advt How did Bandhan Bank fare in Q2 FY27?Bandhan Bank’s net profit rose 23.9 per cent sequentially to Rs 622 crore in Q2 FY27. MD & CEO Partha Pratim Sengupta described the quarter as the bank's best in the last eight quarters, pointing to an improvement across operating and profitability parameters.Gross advances rose 13.1 per cent year-on-year to Rs 1.58 lakh crore, while deposits grew 9.2 per cent to Rs 1.73 lakh crore. Net interest income rose 14.4 per cent year-on-year to Rs 2,960 crore, while total revenue increased 16.9 per cent to Rs 3,670 crore. Operating profit rose 18.8 per cent year-on-year to Rs 1,560 crore.Asset quality also improved, with GNPA at 3.1 per cent and NNPA at 0.9 per cent, while credit cost stood at 1.8 per cent. NIM was 6.1 per cent, ROA improved to 1.2 per cent and ROE to 9.3 per cent.
