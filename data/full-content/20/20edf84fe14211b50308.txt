Summary: When the supplier and recipient are both in India, restaurant and catering services generally follow the place of actual performance; training changes according to whether the recipient is GST-registered; admission follows the place where the event is held; and event organisation or sponsorship follows the registered recipient’s location for B2B supplies but usually the event location for unregistered recipients. This article separates these easily confused rules, then shows how the place of supply and the supplier’s location determine whether IGST or CGST plus SGST or UTGST is charged. That boundary must be tested before choosing a sub-section. If either party is located outside India, Section 13 may govern and the matrix in this article cannot be copied across. For a domestic transaction, the place of supply is then identified under the most specific rule that fits the service actually supplied. The invoice label, accounting code or commercial description is relevant evidence, but it cannot override the substance of the contract. After determining the place of supply, compare it with the supplier’s location. In the ordinary domestic case, different States or Union territories point toward an inter-State supply and IGST, while the same State or Union territory points toward an intra-State supply and CGST plus the relevant SGST or UTGST. This second step is essential: the place-of-supply rule does not, by itself, name the tax to charge. Special provisions, particularly supplies to an SEZ developer or SEZ unit, must also be checked before finalising the invoice. The recipient’s GST registration does not switch this rule. A company registered in Maharashtra may book a business lunch at a restaurant in Karnataka; the restaurant service is performed in Karnataka. The supplier must then compare that Karnataka place of supply with its own location to determine the nature of supply and the tax components. The practical difficulty is identifying the supply. A caterer preparing and serving food at a client’s venue is not necessarily in the same factual position as a restaurant delivering packaged food, a hotel providing accommodation with meals, or an event manager procuring catering as one component of a larger contract. Contracts and invoices should describe the principal supply and the actual performance location. Composite-supply analysis may be required; teams should not split or bundle charges merely to obtain a preferred place of supply. Example restaurant bill for an out of State registered customer Assume a Delhi-registered company’s employee dines at a restaurant located and registered in Jaipur. The restaurant service is actually performed in Rajasthan, so the place of supply is Rajasthan under Section 12(4). Because the restaurant supplier is also located in Rajasthan, the ordinary result is an intra-State supply, even though the customer provides a Delhi GSTIN. The recipient’s ability to claim input tax credit is a separate question and must not be used to alter the place shown on the tax invoice. Training changes when the recipient is registered Section 12(5) creates a deliberate B2B/B2C split for training and performance-appraisal services. When supplied to a registered person, the place of supply is the location of that person. When supplied to a person other than a registered person, it is the place where the services are actually performed. A training provider must therefore verify registration status for the relevant recipient and identify which establishment contracts for and receives the programme. Merely collecting any GSTIN from a corporate group is not a sound control. For an in-person course held in Chennai by a Tamil Nadu supplier for a Karnataka-registered company, the B2B place of supply is ordinarily Karnataka, not the classroom venue. If the same course is sold directly to unregistered individuals and performed in Chennai, the place of supply is Tamil Nadu. Online or hybrid programmes require closer factual analysis of where the service is actually performed for an unregistered recipient and whether the service is genuinely training under Section 12(5) rather than an online information service or another category. The statute should be applied to the supply, not to a marketing label. The place of supply is where the event is actually held or where the park or other place is located. This rule applies whether the ticket buyer is registered or unregistered. A corporate GSTIN therefore does not move the place of supply for admission to a conference, exhibition or match. By contrast, Section 12(7) addresses organisation of events and services ancillary to organisation, as well as assigning sponsorship of such events. For a registered recipient, the place of supply is the recipient’s location. For an unregistered recipient, it is the place where the event is actually held; if the event is held outside India, the recipient’s location applies. A conference organiser may consequently issue different invoices under different rules: one for organising the conference for a corporate client and another for admission sold to delegates. The team should map each contract and consideration rather than applying one venue rule to every revenue line. Example corporate conference with delegate tickets A Mumbai event company organises a conference in Hyderabad for a Bengaluru GST-registered client. The organisation service ordinarily takes the registered recipient’s Karnataka location under Section 12(7), producing an inter-State result against the Maharashtra supplier location. If the organiser separately sells admission tickets to delegates for the Hyderabad event, Section 12(6) points to Telangana for those admission supplies. The same physical event can therefore generate different places of supply because the legal supplies are different. Multi State events require an allocation basis Where an event covered by Section 12(7) is held in more than one State or Union territory and a consolidated amount is charged for services supplied to an unregistered person, the place of supply is treated as being in each relevant State or Union territory in proportion to the value for services separately collected or determined under the contract or agreement. In the absence of such a basis, the prescribed method applies. The allocation should be designed from commercial evidence such as venue days, separately priced modules, capacity, deliverables or another supportable value driver, rather than invented after the invoice is raised. A tour, roadshow or training series may look like one campaign but still contain several supplies. The Section 12(7) multi-State event rule does not automatically govern training; Section 12(5) has its own rule. Likewise, separately sold admissions remain within Section 12(6). The contract should identify deliverables, recipient status, locations and consideration for each supply. The tax team should reconcile that map with e-invoice data, books, returns and customer documentation so that the declared place of supply is consistent across systems. SEZ recipients override the ordinary same State conclusion Section 7(5)(b) of the IGST Act treats supplies to an SEZ developer or SEZ unit as inter-State supplies. CBIC Circular No. 48/22/2018-GST specifically explains that short-term accommodation, conferencing and banqueting supplied to an SEZ developer or unit are treated as inter-State even where Section 12(3) otherwise points to the immovable-property location. The circular also explains that zero-rating for services supplied to an SEZ depends on receipt for authorised operations and the required endorsement and conditions. Businesses should therefore identify SEZ status and authorised-operation evidence separately from the general restaurant, training or event matrix. Invoice controls that prevent place of supply errors Classify the actual service: restaurant or catering, training, admission, organisation, sponsorship, accommodation or another supply. Confirm that both supplier and recipient are in India before using Section 12. Validate the recipient GSTIN and the relevant establishment; do not rely on a group company’s convenient registration. Record the actual restaurant, performance, training or event venue whenever the applicable sub-section uses physical performance. Separate admission consideration from event-organisation or sponsorship consideration in contracts and invoicing records. For multi-State events, document a defensible contractual allocation and reconcile it with invoices and GST returns. Check SEZ status, authorised operations and endorsement requirements before deciding inter-State treatment or zero-rating. Frequently Asked Questions on Restaurant Training and Event Place of Supply 1 Does a customer GSTIN always determine the place of supply No. It determines the place for training supplied to a registered person and for event organisation or sponsorship supplied to a registered person, but restaurant services follow actual performance and event admission follows the event location. The specific Section 12 rule controls. 2 Is a corporate conference ticket treated as event organisation Not merely because a company buys it. A right of admission is governed by Section 12(6) and follows the event location. A service of organising the conference for a client is governed by Section 12(7). Contracts and deliverables must distinguish the two. 3 Where is B2B training supplied when the class is held in another State For training supplied to a registered person, Section 12(5)(a) points to the location of that registered person. The physical classroom State does not replace that rule, although the correct recipient establishment and GSTIN must be verified. 4 Where is training supplied to an unregistered participant Section 12(5)(b) points to the location where the training or performance-appraisal service is actually performed. Online and hybrid facts should be reviewed carefully rather than assuming the billing address or server location controls. 5 Can one event produce both IGST and CGST plus SGST invoices Yes. Different supplies or recipients can produce different places of supply. Admission, organisation, sponsorship and restaurant services may fall under different sub-sections. Each invoice line must follow the applicable rule and supplier-location comparison. 6 Are services to an SEZ always zero rated A supply to an SEZ developer or unit is treated as inter-State, but zero-rating and refund consequences require satisfaction of statutory conditions, including the authorised-operations requirement and prescribed evidence. SEZ status alone should not be used as a shortcut. Key Takeaways Restaurant and catering services follow where they are actually performed, regardless of customer registration. Training follows the registered recipient’s location for B2B and actual performance for an unregistered recipient. Admission follows the venue, while event organisation and sponsorship use a separate B2B/B2C rule. Determine the place of supply first, then compare it with the supplier’s location to select IGST or CGST plus SGST or UTGST. Multi-State events and SEZ supplies require additional allocation and evidence controls.
