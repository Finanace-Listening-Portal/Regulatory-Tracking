Axis Bank's corporate advances rose 34% year on year in the September quarter of FY27, against 25% growth in small and medium enterprise (SME) loans and 16% in retail advances. ICICI Bank's domestic corporate portfolio grew 30.6%, while HDFC Bank's corporate and other wholesale loans expanded 16.4%. Retail loan growth was slower at both lenders, at 14.8% for ICICI Bank's retail and rural portfolio and 11.7% for HDFC Bank. Advt At Axis Bank, corporate advances stood at Rs 4.69 lakh crore at the end of September, compared with retail loans of Rs 7.4 lakh crore and SME loans of Rs 1.65 lakh crore. ICICI Bank's business banking portfolio grew 33.8%, while HDFC Bank's small and mid-market enterprise loans expanded 23.5%. The figures indicate that the acceleration extends beyond large corporate borrowers to smaller businesses, although growth rates differ by lender and portfolio definition.Overall growthThe banks' overall loan growth also reflects differences in business mix and reporting. ICICI Bank's total loan portfolio increased 30% year on year to Rs 18.32 lakh crore, but growth was 24.1% excluding loans from international branches against FCNR(B) deposits. HDFC Bank's gross advances rose 16.3% to Rs 32.2 lakh crore, while Axis Bank's net advances increased 23% to Rs 13.74 lakh crore. Gross and net advances are different measures and should not be treated as directly interchangeable.Corporate lending can offer scale and lower servicing costs relative to loan size, particularly when banks lend to established companies with strong credit profiles. But competition for large, highly rated borrowers can limit pricing power. Retail lending has a more varied return profile: unsecured personal loans and credit cards can carry higher yields but also higher acquisition costs and credit risks, while secured products such as housing loans typically operate with different margins and risk characteristics.The September-quarter results do not provide a directly comparable measure of profitability for corporate and retail lending. Overall net interest margins reflect each bank's funding costs, asset mix and balance sheet, rather than the return from a single loan category. ICICI Bank's net interest margin was 4.18%, Axis Bank's was 3.36% and HDFC Bank reported 3.16% on total assets and 3.32% on interest-earning assets. These measures should not be used to rank the profitability of corporate versus retail lending. Advt The more meaningful comparison is the return after funding costs, operating expenses, credit losses and the capital required to support each loan. Corporate lending may deliver scale and lower servicing costs, while retail credit can offer higher yields and a more diversified borrower base.
