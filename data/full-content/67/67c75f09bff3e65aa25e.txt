(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India considered Civil Appeal Nos. 6959–6960 of 2025 and Civil Appeal No. 6613 of 2025 arising from a common order of the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, Chennai. After hearing counsel and examining the record, the Bench comprising Justice J.B. Pardiwala and Justice R. Mahadevan found no good reason to interfere with the impugned NCLAT order. It accordingly dismissed all three civil appeals and disposed of pending applications. The Court separately considered Special Leave Petition (Civil) Nos. 19647–19648 of 2025, arising from Diary No. 26887 of 2025. It granted permission to file the special leave petitions, but after hearing counsel and examining the material, found no good reason to interfere with the common order of the National Company Law Tribunal, Bengaluru. The special leave petitions were dismissed, with pending applications disposed of. The signed order is brief and does not independently discuss the merits of the insolvency dispute or set out a detailed interpretation of Section 12A of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code. Read NCLAT Chennai Order in this case: Byju’s Insolvency: NCLAT Chennai Rejects BCCI’s Pre-CoC Settlement Plea The Supreme Court of India, in Board of Control for Cricket in India Vs Pankaj Srivastava & Ors., considered Civil Appeal Nos. 6959–6960 of 2025 and Civil Appeal No. 6613 of 2025 arising from a common order passed by the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT), Chennai. The appeals were heard by a Bench comprising Justice J.B. Pardiwala and Justice R. Mahadevan. After hearing learned counsel appearing for the parties and examining the materials available on record, the Supreme Court found no good reason to interfere with the common impugned order of the NCLAT. Accordingly, the Court dismissed all three civil appeals and directed that pending applications, if any, would also stand disposed of. The signed order contains a brief determination and does not elaborate upon the factual background, competing submissions or detailed legal reasoning underlying the NCLAT’s decision. The Supreme Court also considered Special Leave Petition (Civil) Nos. 19647–19648 of 2025, arising from Diary No. 26887 of 2025, challenging a common order passed by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Bengaluru. The Court first granted the application seeking permission to file the special leave petitions. Thereafter, upon hearing learned counsel for the petitioner and examining the materials on record, the Bench concluded that no sufficient reason existed to interfere with the impugned NCLT order. Consequently, the special leave petitions were dismissed, and all pending applications were disposed of. The Court’s determination was confined to declining interference with the orders under challenge. It did not separately examine the merits of the underlying insolvency proceedings or provide an independent assessment of the legal questions that may have arisen before the NCLT or NCLAT.
