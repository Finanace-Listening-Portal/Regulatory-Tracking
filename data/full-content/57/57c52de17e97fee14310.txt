Summary: Punjab & Sind Bank’s PSB Fixed Deposit Tax Saver Scheme is a five-year bank term deposit for individuals and Hindu undivided families. The bank’s rate table checked on 20 November 2026 states that the five-year rate for domestic deposits below ₹3 crore, including the tax-saver scheme, is 5.95% per annum with effect from 14 November 2026. The published senior-citizen addition of 0.50 percentage point for eligible domestic term deposits of 180 days and above implies 6.45% per annum for an eligible resident senior citizen, subject to the bank’s conditions. The deposit cannot ordinarily be encashed during the five-year lock-in or pledged as security. Tax Year 2026-27 claims must refer to section 123 and Schedule XV of the Income-tax Act, 2025; the ₹1.5 lakh amount is an aggregate deduction limit and is unavailable under the concessional regime in section 202. Who can open the deposit and who can claim the deduction Single, joint, adult-minor and HUF holdings The bank states that an individual or HUF may invest. The account may be opened singly or jointly. A joint receipt may be issued to two adults or to an adult jointly with a minor and may be payable to either holder or the survivor. However, where the deposit is joint, the tax benefit is available only to the first holder. A joint holder should not claim a second deduction for the same deposit merely because both names appear on the receipt. The scheme’s minimum is ₹100 or multiples of ₹100. The bank’s page describes the ₹1.5 lakh ceiling in the context of the deduction together with other qualifying savings. For a deposit placed in Tax Year 2026-27, section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 allows an individual or HUF a deduction for qualifying Schedule XV sums, in aggregate, up to ₹1.5 lakh, subject to the Schedule’s conditions. This is not a separate ₹1.5 lakh allowance for every tax-saving product. The tax regime decision comes before the deduction calculation The Income Tax Department’s transition guidance confirms that section 123 and Schedule XV replace the old section 80C framework for Tax Year 2026-27 and retain the ₹1.5 lakh aggregate limit. The Department also states that section 123 is not available under the new concessional regime in section 202. A depositor should therefore compare the applicable regimes using the full income and deduction profile; opening a tax-saver FD does not by itself create a usable deduction when section 202 applies. Only the first holder, subject to eligibility and applicable regime Five-year lock-in changes the liquidity analysis No ordinary premature encashment or pledge The deposit cannot be encashed before expiry of five years and cannot be pledged to another agency or offered as security. That removes the ordinary premature-closure option associated with many bank term deposits and means that a depositor should not assume access through a loan or overdraft against the receipt. The investment decision should account for emergency liquidity separately; tax treatment does not change the contractual lock-in. Death of the holder is a specific exception Where a valid nomination exists and the holder dies, the nominee may encash the deposit before or after maturity. For premature payment in that circumstance, the bank states that interest is paid for the period actually run, without penalty, at the rates applicable under the normal fixed-deposit scheme. The published exception should not be generalised to voluntary early withdrawal by a living holder. The scheme permits transfer of the deposit from one Punjab & Sind Bank branch to another at the holder’s request. A lost, stolen, destroyed, mutilated or defaced receipt may be replaced through the bank’s prescribed duplicate-receipt procedure, including an indemnity and, where required, surety or bank guarantee. Branch transfer or duplicate issue does not remove the five-year lock-in. Interest is taxable even when the principal qualifies for deduction Accrual or receipt depends on the taxpayer’s accounting method The bank expressly states that interest is taxable on the accrual or receipt basis depending on the investor’s method of accounting. The principal deposit’s qualification under section 123 does not exempt the interest. A cumulative deposit can therefore create taxable interest before cash is received at maturity. Depositors should reconcile the bank’s interest certificate, Annual Information Statement or Form No. 168 for the relevant tax year, tax deducted at source and the amount offered in the return. TDS is a collection mechanism, not the final tax For interest credited or paid from 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 consolidates resident withholding under section 393. Official tax tables retain a 10% rate for interest other than interest on securities. The official threshold guidance states that a banking company generally does not deduct when aggregate time-deposit interest stays within ₹50,000 for other resident depositors or ₹1,00,000 for resident senior citizens; core-banking aggregation is not branch-wise. Actual tax depends on total income, regime, slab, declarations, PAN compliance and other facts. No TDS does not mean no taxable income, and TDS can exceed or fall short of the final liability. Senior-citizen interest deduction is separate from the principal deduction Section 153 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 allows an eligible senior citizen a deduction up to ₹50,000 for interest on deposits, including time deposits, subject to the statutory conditions. For a non-senior individual or HUF, section 153’s ₹10,000 interest deduction is confined to savings-account interest and excludes time deposits. This interest deduction should not be confused with the section 123 deduction for the amount invested in the tax-saver deposit. Rate interpretation and maturity estimates The stated 5.95% and 6.45% figures are annual rates, not guaranteed post-tax returns. The actual maturity amount depends on the deposit option, interest-payment frequency, compounding convention, deposit date, tax withholding and the bank’s account terms. A depositor should obtain the bank-generated maturity value before opening the account. Any illustration that simply compounds annually may differ from the bank’s contracted calculation and should not be treated as the receipt value. Super-senior special-tenure addition Not applicable to five-year bucket Not applicable to five-year bucket DICGC insurance: one bank-wide aggregate, not one cover per FD Punjab & Sind Bank states that it is registered with the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation. DICGC insurance covers eligible bank deposits, including fixed deposits, up to ₹5 lakh for each depositor in each insured bank in the same right and same capacity. The ceiling combines principal and accrued interest. Savings, current, recurring and fixed deposits held across all branches of the same bank in the same right and capacity are aggregated; opening several receipts or using several branches does not multiply the cover. Different banks are insured separately, and accounts held in genuinely different rights or capacities may receive separate treatment under DICGC rules. The existence of insurance does not make the tax-saver FD freely withdrawable, does not guarantee payment above the statutory ceiling and does not replace assessment of liquidity, concentration and documentation. Deposit insurance becomes payable only in the circumstances specified by the DICGC framework, such as liquidation, licence cancellation, specified reconstruction or amalgamation outcomes, or qualifying withdrawal restrictions. Frequently Asked Questions on the PSB Tax Saver FD What is the verified five-year rate on 20 November 2026? Punjab & Sind Bank’s official table effective 14 November 2026 lists 5.95% per annum for the five-year bucket. The published 0.50 percentage-point senior addition implies 6.45% for an eligible senior citizen, subject to bank conditions. Can the deposit be closed early for a personal emergency? The scheme states that the deposit cannot be encashed before five years. A specific death-related exception permits nominee encashment under the published terms. Can the receipt be pledged for a loan? No. The bank states that the tax-saver deposit cannot be pledged or offered as security. Can both joint holders claim the tax deduction? No. The bank’s published terms state that only the first holder can claim the tax benefit, subject to the tax law and applicable regime. Is the ₹1.5 lakh deduction available under the section 202 concessional regime? No. Official Income Tax Department guidance says the section 123 deduction is not available under section 202. Is interest on the tax-saver FD exempt? No. Interest remains taxable; the bank states that it is considered on accrual or receipt depending on the investor’s accounting method. Does every PSB fixed deposit receive separate ₹5 lakh insurance? No. Eligible deposits across all Punjab & Sind Bank branches are aggregated for the same depositor in the same right and capacity, and principal plus interest share the ₹5 lakh ceiling.
