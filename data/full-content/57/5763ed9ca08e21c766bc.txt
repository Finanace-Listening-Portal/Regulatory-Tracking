Articles 6 min read Motor discounts at 80%, may touch 95%; persistency remains at 40%: Shriram General CEO Anil Kumar Aggarwal says motor insurance discounts could rise to 90-95%, while the insurer is targeting an improvement in persistency to over 60% in the next two to three years. The company also aims to reduce motor's share of its portfolio from over 90% currently to 85% by 2030. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Nov 26, 2026 at 01:26 PM IST var news_msid = 136183484; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Steep discounting in motor insurance and weak policy persistency remain key challenges for the general insurance business, with discounts in the motor segment already estimated at nearly 80% and potentially rising to 90-95%, said Anil Kumar Aggarwal, CEO, Shriram General Insurance, in an interview with ETBFSI.Anil Kumar Aggarwal, MD & CEO, Shriram General InsuranceThe insurer, which derives more than 90% of its premium from motor insurance, currently has a persistency ratio of around 40%. Aggarwal said the company is targeting an improvement to over 60% over the next two to three years, with premium persistency targeted at around 70%. Advt The company is also looking to reduce its dependence on motor insurance, targeting a portfolio mix of 85% motor and 15% non-motor by 2030. It has entered the health insurance segment and expects to write more than ₹100 crore in health premiums over the next two to three years.On the business front, Shriram General Insurance reported growth of around 21% in the first half, while its market share increased to 1.38% from 1.24% in the year-ago period. The insurer has also expanded its point-of-sale financial advisor network to 1.13 lakh and is targeting two lakh advisors by 2030, subject to regulatory guidelines.Here are the edited excerpts from the interview:Q. You have reported strong growth in the first half. Could you take us through the company's performance and key numbers?Anil: We have delivered a strong performance this half year, growing by around 21%. Over the last four-and-a-half years, we have consistently grown at more than twice the industry rate.Our market share has also increased to 1.38% from 1.24% last year. In motor insurance, we are ranked eighth among private players and 11th in the overall industry, compared with 12th previously.Our point-of-sale financial advisor network has increased to 1,13,770 from 1,06,620. We have recruited 9,802 financial advisors during the first half, compared with 9,482 in the corresponding period last year. Our target is to reach two lakh financial advisors by 2030, subject to the new regulatory guidelines. Advt We have also increased our branch network to 491, adding 10 branches during the period. Our focus remains on recruiting financial advisors, opening branches and strengthening our manpower.Q. Motor accounts for more than 90% of your premium. Isn't this an uncomfortable level of dependence on a single business segment? How are you addressing it?Anil: We have taken a conscious call on this. During the first half, the non-motor premium base was still relatively small. We do not want to aggressively increase that business because even one small claim can disturb the non-motor portfolio.Our target for 2030 is to have 85% of our business from motor and 15% from non-motor. We have already taken several steps towards this and entered the health insurance segment. Over the next two to three years, we expect to write more than ₹100 crore in health premiums. Advt I believe we will be able to achieve our target of bringing motor's share down to around 85% by 2030.Q. Given your motor-heavy portfolio, what impact do you expect IRDAI's proposed four-layer motor insurance framework to have on the company? Could it increase compliance and distribution costs or change premiums?Anil: I think it will have a positive impact. Earlier also, the courts were giving judgments in favour of insurance. Now there are options to charge premiums for additional improvements, which will give more choices to policyholders.It will also allow insured customers to choose the right coverage. I believe it will have a positive impact on the industry as well as on us. There may be a significant increase in premiums, but different categories of customers will also get the required coverage. Advt Q. Do you expect the new framework to trigger a price war in motor insurance, given that premiums are already under pressure?Anil: If the new guidelines are implemented, I do not see a price war. The reason is that third-party premiums are regulated. You cannot offer discounts on the TP premium.Already, discounts of almost 80% are being offered. These could increase to 90% or even 95%. The product may become cheaper for the insured, but I do not think there will be a major price war.Q. Your investment portfolio has grown relatively modestly despite strong premium growth. What explains this?Anil: One reason is that we are among the highest dividend-paying companies. I think we are number two in the industry. We have already paid around ₹3,500 crore in dividends to our promoters and investors, and we are comfortable with our solvency position.Another focus area is settling motor accident claims. Given that more than 90% of our portfolio is motor, our outstanding motor accident claims are not more than 45,000.Around 60% of cases are settled through negotiated settlements, while around 20% are withdrawn. Litigation is involved in only a limited proportion of cases. Our focus is on settling these claims, and that is one reason our investment portfolio has not grown at the same pace.Q. How much of your premium growth is coming from renewals versus new business? And how has persistency been?Anil: Around 15% is new business and 85% is existing business. Renewals are definitely a concern. The renewal issue is still there, although we expect it to improve over the next two to three years.One of the challenges is getting the contact numbers of policyholders. We are working on solutions for this. We also need to give financial advisors the confidence that we will not approach their customers directly.These are some of the issues we are addressing. I believe they will be resolved over the next couple of years.Q. Persistency has been a sticky issue in the Industry. What is your current persistency ratio, and what target have you set for improvement?Anil: Our current persistency ratio is around 40%. We want to take it above 60% over the next two to three years. In terms of premium, our target is around 70%.Q. What is your current combined claims ratio? Is the core underwriting business profitable without investment income?Anil: Our combined claims ratio is currently 103%. I would say that our business is showing an underwriting profit. We take claims and investment income into consideration while assessing overall profitability. We consider the business profitable.Q. What is the current effective stake of the Sanlam Group in Shriram General Insurance? With 100% FDI now permitted, are there plans to increase the stake or change the ownership structure?Anil: That question will have to be answered by our group company, whatever plans they have, I cannot comment on them. The current effective holding, including direct and indirect ownership, is around 51%.
