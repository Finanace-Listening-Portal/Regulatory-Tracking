Summary: BIS registration is an important regulatory requirement for manufacturers and importers dealing in products subject to compulsory Indian Standards certification. The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), established under the BIS Act, 2016, administers different conformity assessment schemes, including Scheme I for ISI Mark certification, Scheme II for the Compulsory Registration Scheme (CRS), Scheme IV for Certificates of Conformity, Scheme X for specified products, and the Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme (FMCS). Although BIS certification is generally voluntary, it becomes mandatory for products covered by applicable Quality Control Orders (QCOs) and other compulsory certification requirements. Businesses must identify the correct product classification, applicable Indian Standard, certification scheme, implementation date and available exemptions before manufacturing, importing or selling covered products. The registration procedure may involve manufacturer and factory documents, product specifications, laboratory test reports, quality control records, authorised representative documents, application scrutiny and factory assessment where applicable. The article explains the registration process, product testing requirements, expected expenses, approval timelines, marking obligations and continuing compliance after certification. It also addresses requirements for foreign manufacturers, importers, private-label businesses and online sellers. Businesses must ensure that approvals cover the correct factory, product varieties, brands and models, and must review design changes, surveillance requirements and renewals. Early planning and product-specific regulatory assessment help reduce the risks of non-compliance, shipment delays, penalties, interrupted sales and misuse of the BIS Standard Mark. Products such as mobile phones, power banks, toys, electrical accessories and various industrial goods may require BIS approval before they can legally enter the Indian market. Understanding these requirements early helps businesses plan production, testing, imports and sales without avoidable delays. However, the BIS approval process is different for different products. The applicable Indian Standard, certification scheme and government order determine the correct procedure. Manufacturers and importers must assess their specific products rather than assume that one certificate covers their entire product range. What Is BIS Registration? BIS stands for the Bureau of Indian Standards, India’s national standards body. It develops Indian Standards and operates certification schemes to assess whether products meet specified requirements relating to safety, quality, performance and other characteristics. The term “BIS registration” is commonly used for different BIS product approvals. However, registration under the Compulsory Registration Scheme, an ISI mark licence and a Certificate of Conformity are separate approval routes. The main legal provisions include the BIS Act, 2016, and the BIS (Conformity Assessment) Regulations, 2018. For businesses, the first step is identifying the product correctly. Its technical specifications, intended use, construction and manufacturing details help determine the applicable standard and certification route. A general commercial description may not provide enough information to make this decision. Is BIS Registration Mandatory in India? BIS certification is generally voluntary. It becomes compulsory when the Central Government brings a product under a mandatory certification requirement through a Quality Control Order, commonly called a QCO, or another applicable compulsory certification order. The relevant order identifies the covered products, applicable standards, certification scheme and implementation date. It may also contain exemptions or separate commencement dates for different categories of manufacturers. Businesses should read the order together with subsequent amendments before deciding whether certification is required. The existence of an Indian Standard does not automatically make BIS certification mandatory. Similarly, products that previously did not require compulsory certification may become subject to it when a new government order takes effect. Types of BIS Certification in India Scheme I: ISI Mark Certification Scheme I is the product certification route commonly associated with the ISI mark. Under this scheme, BIS assesses the manufacturer’s production arrangements and the product’s conformity with the relevant Indian Standard. The assessment generally considers manufacturing infrastructure, process controls, testing facilities and quality control arrangements. Product conformity is established through the applicable testing procedure, which may involve independent laboratory testing, testing at the manufacturing premises or both. Manufacturers should review the relevant product manual before applying. It helps explain important requirements relating to sampling, testing, product grouping and the intended scope of the licence. Scheme II: Compulsory Registration Scheme The Compulsory Registration Scheme, or CRS, operates under Scheme II. It applies to products covered by the relevant compulsory registration orders and includes prescribed testing and conformity declaration requirements. CRS registration is linked to the manufacturer and the approved product scope. The manufacturer must establish the correct factory, product, brand and model details before testing and application submission. Registration should not be treated as a general approval for every product sold by the business. Scheme IV: Certificate of Conformity Scheme IV provides a Certificate of Conformity for products covered by this particular route. Businesses should follow it where the applicable order specifies certification under Scheme IV. The assessment and continuing obligations depend on the relevant product and scheme guidelines. Applicants should confirm these requirements before preparing documents or arranging testing. Scheme X: Certification Scheme X is another BIS certification route used for products brought within its scope. The applicable order determines the products covered and the requirements that manufacturers must follow. Manufacturers may need to demonstrate suitable production infrastructure, quality controls and testing capabilities. The exact procedure must be checked for the product concerned rather than assumed from another BIS scheme. Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme The Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme, commonly called FMCS, enables overseas manufacturers to obtain BIS licences for applicable products supplied to India. The approval concerns the foreign manufacturer and manufacturing facility. Foreign manufacturers must follow the relevant requirements, including those relating to an Authorised Indian Representative. Products covered by CRS follow the applicable CRS registration procedure rather than automatically following the FMCS route. Products Covered Under BIS Requirements Electronic and IT Products The CRS product list includes categories such as laptops, notebooks, tablets, mobile phones, power banks, printers, scanners, specified power adaptors, wireless headphones and certain lighting products. Each category must be examined carefully because coverage can depend on technical descriptions, ratings or other limits. Businesses should compare the actual product specifications with the relevant notified category before deciding which approval is needed. A finished product and its components may also require separate consideration. An approval relating to a component should not automatically be treated as approval for the complete product. Consumer Products Various consumer products, including specified toys, footwear and household goods, are covered by mandatory BIS requirements under applicable orders. The exact product description remains important. Goods within the same broad commercial category may have different standards, implementation dates or exclusions. Businesses should therefore assess the specific product rather than rely only on its retail category. Construction and Industrial Products Mandatory BIS requirements also cover various construction materials, metal products, chemicals, electrical goods and industrial products. For these goods, technical characteristics such as grade, composition, dimensions and intended use can affect the applicable standard. Manufacturers should clearly identify the varieties they intend to produce and include them within the appropriate approval scope. Checking Product Applicability A proper applicability review compares the product’s specifications with the relevant government order and Indian Standard. It also considers the effective date, later amendments and any available exclusions or exemptions. An HS code may support initial research, but it should not replace the technical and legal assessment. Keeping a written record of the applicability decision helps purchasing, production and sales teams follow a consistent approach. Who Can Apply for BIS Registration? Under CRS, the manufacturer or factory owner is the eligible applicant. Manufacturers located in India and overseas can apply for covered products under the relevant requirements. Importers, distributors and private-label businesses should identify the actual manufacturing facility and coordinate with the manufacturer. Their role in selling the product does not automatically make them the manufacturer for certification purposes. For private-label arrangements, responsibilities should be agreed before commercial production. The parties should decide who will provide samples, technical documents, brand information and responses to regulatory queries. Documents Required for BIS Registration Manufacturer Identity and Factory Documents Applicants must provide documents establishing the manufacturer’s identity and manufacturing location. Under CRS, the relevant government-issued evidence should establish the factory’s name, address and manufacturing activity. The manufacturer’s details should remain consistent across the application, supporting records and test reports. Any differences should be corrected or explained with appropriate evidence before submission. Product Specifications and Technical Documents Product documents may include technical specifications, model details, photographs and proposed labels. Depending on the product and certification scheme, additional information such as component details, drawings or construction records may be required. These documents should describe the product being submitted for approval accurately. Records relating to an older design or a different model can create inconsistencies during scrutiny. Product Test Reports Test reports provide evidence of the product’s conformity with the applicable standard. Testing must follow the requirements of the relevant scheme, including laboratory recognition and report submission conditions. Applicants should ensure that product names, model details and manufacturer information are recorded correctly. They should also check the permitted age of the test report before submitting the application. Manufacturing and Quality Records Where factory assessment applies, manufacturers should prepare records explaining their production and quality control arrangements. These may include manufacturing flow charts, machinery details, testing facilities and calibration records. Well-organised records help demonstrate how the factory controls production and checks finished goods. They also help identify gaps before formal assessment begins. Authorisations and Representative Documents The person handling the application must have the required authority to act for the manufacturer. Foreign applicants must also examine the applicable requirements for appointing an Authorised Indian Representative. The final document checklist should be specific to the product and scheme. A general checklist copied from another application may leave out important requirements. BIS Registration Process in India Step 1: Identify the Applicable Standard and Scheme The process begins by identifying the Indian Standard and certification scheme applicable to the product. This requires examining its technical characteristics, intended use and the wording of the relevant government order. The manufacturer should also decide the intended approval scope. Factory details, product varieties, brands and models should be established before testing starts. Step 2: Assess Product and Factory Readiness The manufacturer should review whether the product can meet the standard and whether the factory has suitable production and quality controls. Where inspection applies, the review should cover manufacturing arrangements, testing facilities and supporting records. Correcting missing equipment, incomplete records or design problems early can reduce delays during assessment. Step 3: Arrange Product Testing Testing should be conducted through a laboratory accepted under the relevant scheme and having the appropriate testing scope. Under the CRS workflow, the manufacturer generates a test request, obtains testing from a BIS-recognised laboratory, verifies the report and applies using that report within 90 days of its issue. Document preparation should be coordinated with testing so that the report remains valid for submission. Step 4: Submit the Application The application must be submitted through the portal prescribed for the scheme. Scheme I applications are handled through Manakonline, while CRS uses its dedicated registration portal. Before submission, the applicant should compare every entry with the supporting records. Manufacturer names, factory addresses, brands and model details should match throughout the application. Step 5: Respond to Queries BIS may request clarification or additional documents during scrutiny. Applicants should monitor the relevant portal and provide complete responses within the applicable requirements. A response should resolve the actual issue. For example, an address mismatch requires an explanation and supporting evidence rather than repeated submission of the same inconsistent documents. Step 6: Complete Assessment Where Required Under Scheme I, the applicable assessment examines manufacturing infrastructure, process controls, quality arrangements and testing capabilities. Product conformity must also be established. Different schemes follow different assessment procedures. Businesses should prepare according to their own certification route rather than assume that every BIS application involves the same inspection process. Step 7: Review the Approval Once approval is granted, the manufacturer should check the licence or registration carefully. The manufacturing location and approved product scope should match the intended commercial supply. The approval details should be shared with production, procurement and sales teams. This helps prevent unapproved models or varieties from being supplied as certified goods. Step 8: Apply the Required Marking Products and packaging must follow the applicable marking and labelling instructions. Artwork should be checked against the final approval before commercial stock is released. The required Standard Mark and licence or registration details must be used correctly. Businesses should not print an assumed approval number or treat a pending application as permission to use the mark.
