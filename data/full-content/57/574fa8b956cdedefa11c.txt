Vs ACIT (ITAT Delhi) Section 68 Addition Cannot Rest on General Allegations of Accommodation Entries When Loan Evidence Remains Unrebutted Third-Party Search Led to Reassessment MMR Townships P. Ltd. was incorporated on 28 February 2019 and did not file its return for AY 2019–20. On 17 November 2021, the Department conducted a search under section 132 in the cases of the Galaxy Group and alleged entry operators Deepak Agarwal and Himanshu Verma. According to the Department, the seized digital material included bank account details, unsigned board resolutions, WhatsApp communications concerning fund transfers and beneficiary lists. The Department treated the assessee as a beneficiary of an alleged accommodation entry of ₹3.99 crore from RCM Finance and Leasing Pvt. Ltd. The Assessing Officer issued a notice under section 148 and ultimately completed the reassessment under sections 147 read with 144 on 26 March 2025. In response to the show-cause notice, the assessee furnished a loan confirmation, the lender’s bank statement and its income-tax return acknowledgement. The Assessing Officer nevertheless added ₹3.99 crore under section 68 and ₹11.97 lakh under section 69C, estimating commission at 3% of the alleged accommodation entry. The CIT(A) sustained the additions. Lender’s Confirmation Addressed the Transaction in Dispute The assessee argued that the loan was supported by documents showing the receipt and repayment of funds. The Tribunal recorded that the lender had responded to the notice under section 133(6) issued by the CIT(A) on 28 November 2025. The lender’s confirmation dated 5 December 2025 was therefore part of the appellate record. The Tribunal found that the CIT(A), in referring to the absence of particulars concerning the lender’s own creditors, had overlooked this confirmation. The distinction mattered because the confirmation and banking record addressed the specific transaction under assessment. The Department’s investigation described an alleged network and its method of operation. To sustain the addition after the borrower produced evidence of receipt and repayment and the lender confirmed the account, the Revenue needed material that discredited the documents or connected the particular advance to the alleged entry operation. Source-of-Source Amendment Applies from AY 2023–24 The Tribunal held that the CIT(A) had applied the source-of-source requirement for loans to the wrong assessment year. The amendment to section 68 requiring the person in whose name a loan or borrowing is credited to explain its nature and source took effect on 1 April 2023 and applies from AY 2023–24 onward. The year before the Tribunal was AY 2019–20. For that year, the ordinary section 68 inquiry concerned the identity of the lender, its creditworthiness and the genuineness of the transaction. The Assessing Officer could examine whether the lender had the financial capacity to make the advance and whether the banking trail supported the stated transaction. The CIT(A), however, could not sustain the addition by imposing the later statutory requirement to explain the lender’s own source of funds. Coordinate Bench Decisions on the Same Lender The Tribunal followed Trishul Realcon Pvt. Ltd. v. ACIT, ITA No. 554/Del/2025, which also concerned a loan from RCM Finance and Leasing Pvt. Ltd. for AY 2019–20. In that case, the Delhi Bench deleted the addition after examining the documentary evidence and repayment. The Assessing Officer had relied on the alleged entry operators’ modus operandi without bringing contrary material to show that the particular loan was an accommodation entry. The Tribunal also drew on Mukul Agencies Pvt. Ltd., ITA Nos. 2419 and 2420/Del/2026. There, the coordinate Bench upheld the deletion of additions involving several lenders, including RCM Finance and Leasing Pvt. Ltd. The evidence included audited financial statements, confirmations and banking records. The decision emphasized the need for independent verification and a specific link between third-party investigation material and the borrowing sought to be taxed. Loan and Consequential Commission Additions Deleted On the facts of MMR Townships, the Tribunal relied on the disclosed receipt and repayment of the loan and the lender’s confirmation to set aside the section 68 addition. It also allowed the ground against the ₹11.97 lakh commission addition under section 69C, which depended on the allegation that the loan was an accommodation entry. The appeal was allowed on merits. The grounds challenging reassessment jurisdiction and approvals became academic and were left open. Author’s Comments The most significant fact was that the lender directly answered the appellate authority’s statutory inquiry. That response supported the borrower’s own records and gave the Revenue a specific transaction to verify. An investigation report can justify scrutiny of a loan. Once the account stands confirmed and the banking records show both receipt and repayment, a sustainable addition requires the Revenue to identify what is unreliable in those records or what other evidence links the advance to an accommodation entry. The ruling also shows why the assessment year matters when evaluating an objection about the lender’s own source of funds. Cases Discussed Trishul Realcon Pvt. Ltd. It is an undisputed fact that lender companies are NBFC companies and loans were repaid in subsequent period and such repayment was prior to issuance of notice u/s 148 of the Act. 10. With regard to the addition of Rs.10,81,00,000/- u/s 68 in respect of loans from M/s. Highrise Securities & Trading P. Ltd. (Rs. 5,10,00,000/-), M/s. RCM Finance and Leasing P. Ltd. (Rs. 4,11,00,000/-) and M/s. Sarvottam Securities P. ltd. (Rs. 1,60,00,000/-), the Assessing Officer proceeded to make the impugned additions primarily on the basis of information received from the Investigation Wing alleging that certain concerns controlled by alleged entry operators had advanced accommodation loans to the assessee. The assessment order further proceeds on general observations regarding the activities of the alleged entry operators and statements recorded during search proceedings in cases of third parties. However, it is noted that the AO has not brought on record any specific material found during the third party search on 17.11.2021 and the reliance has been placed on stale information arising from search action carried out in the very same group in 2012 and 2017 which has no relevance to the transactions taken place in year under reference i.e. AY 2019-20. Further, except placing reliance upon such investigation material, no independent enquiry whatsoever was conducted by the ITA Nos.2419 & 2420/Del/2026 Assessing Officer to establish that the loans received by the assessee represented its own unaccounted money introduced in the guise of unsecured loans. 11. Ld. CIT(A), after extensively examining the documentary evidence furnished by the assessee, has recorded categorical findings that the assessee had discharged the initial onus cast upon it u/s 68 by furnishing complete documentary evidence establishing the identity of the lender companies, their corporate existence, PAN, audited financial statements, confirmations, bank statements, income-tax returns and complete details of the loan transactions carried through regular banking channels. The findings recorded by the ld. CIT(A) in paragraph 5.13 further reinforce the creditworthiness of the lender companies. The ld. CIT(A), instead of merely accepting the documentary evidence at face value, independently analysed the audited financial statements of each of the three lender companies, namely M/s Highrise Securities & Trading Pvt. Ltd., M/s Sarvottam Securities Pvt. Ltd. and M/s RCM Finance & Leasing Pvt. Ltd., all of which are admittedly RBI-registered NBFCs, and examined their financial position with reference to their share capital, reserves and surplus, loans and advances, revenue from operations and profitability for the relevant as well as the preceding financial year. On such analysis, the ld. CIT(A) found that these companies possessed substantial net worth running into several crores of rupees, had sizeable loan portfolios as part of their regular financing business and were generating significant operational revenue, thereby clearly demonstrating their financial capacity to advance the loans in question. The ld. CIT(A) also observed that the allegations of the Investigation Wing regarding meagre taxable income or cross-holding of shares could not override the undisputed financial strength reflected in the audited balance sheets of the lender companies. We find ourselves in agreement with the aforesaid factual analysis. The financial parameters examined by the ld. CIT(A) unmistakably establish that the lenders possessed adequate financial resources to extend the impugned loans, thereby fully satisfying the requirement of creditworthiness u/s 68 of the Act. In the absence of any material brought on record by the Assessing Officer to discredit these audited financial statements or to demonstrate that the funds advanced to the assessee had actually emanated from the assessee itself, the findings recorded by the ld. CIT(A) deserve to be affirmed. 12. We also find considerable force in the contention of the ld. AR that the lender companies in question are Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) duly registered with the Reserve Bank of India and are subject to a stringent regulatory framework governing their lending activities. The Assessing Officer has neither disputed their legal existence nor brought any material on record to demonstrate that their RBI registration had been cancelled or that they were prohibited from carrying on ITA Nos.2419 & 2420/Del/2026 financing activities during the relevant period. Merely because certain allegations were made against some of the entities in the report of the Investigation Wing could not, by itself, justify the conclusion that every loan advanced by such entities was a sham transaction, particularly in the absence of any independent enquiry or tangible material establishing that the funds received by the assessee represented its own unaccounted money. Significantly, the documentary evidence placed on record demonstrates that the impugned loans were subsequently repaid in their entirety through normal banking channels, along with payment of contractual interest after deduction of tax at source, much prior to the issuance of notice under section 148 of the Act and the AO has not disputed the factual position to this effect. It is worth noting that had the transactions been mere accommodation entries or colourable devices, there would have been little commercial rationale for the assessee to discharge the entire liability by repaying the loans with interest from disclosed banking channels long before any reassessment proceedings were even contemplated. The subsequent repayment of the loans, effected in the ordinary course of business without any intervention from the Department, constitutes a significant contemporaneous circumstance lending credence to the assessee’s claim regarding the genuineness of the borrowing transactions and completely demolishes the allegation that the loans were fictitious or represented the assessee’s undisclosed income. 13. We also find that the additions have substantially been made merely because the Investigation Wing had allegedly unearthed accommodation entry operations in respect of certain entities. However, it is now well settled that information received from another wing of the Department, by itself, cannot constitute evidence against an assessee unless the Assessing Officer independently verifies the material and establishes a live nexus between such information and the assessee’s transactions. Mere reliance upon third-party statements, without affording opportunity of cross-examination and without conducting any independent enquiry, cannot form the sole basis for making additions u/s 68. In the present case, the assessment order does not demonstrate any investigation into the funds actually transferred to the assessee or establish that the monies received originated from the coffers of the assessee itself and no enquiry u/ss 131 or 133(6) was conducted with the lender companies after the assessee had furnished complete documentary evidence. No material has been brought on record to rebut the evidences filed by the assessee. Ld. DR has not been able to place any supporting documents to corroborate the findings of the AO or dispute the genuineness of the documents furnished by the assessee before AO and CIT(A). Further, no opportunity of cross examination was afforded to the assessee even though specifically requested. 14. It is worth appreciating that ld. CIT(A) has dealt with the concept of accommodation entry and shell companies in comprehensive manner and we are in agreement with ld. CIT(A) that the allegation of the AO is unsubstantiated and uncalled for. 15. We also note that the Assessing Officer has neither questioned the repayment nor established that the monies so repaid ultimately reverted to the assessee in any manner. In these circumstances, we find no infirmity in the conclusion reached by the ld. CIT(A) that the assessee had duly discharged the onus cast upon it under section 68 of the Act and that the impugned additions were made merely on suspicion and generalized allegations, unsupported by any cogent evidence. 16. We further find that the decisions of the Coordinate bench relied upon by the ld. AR are squarely relevant and supports the conclusion arrived by us: i. KTM Impex Private Limited vs. ITO [ITA No. 2377/Del/2025] (ITAT, Delhi) (09/01/2026) ii. Real Innerspring Technologies Private Limited vs ACIT [ITA No. 647/DEL/2023] (ITAT Delhi) iii. Agarwal Earth Movers Private Limited vs. ITO [ITA No. 6084/Del/2025] (ITAT Delhi) iv. Rise Projects (P.) Ltd. vs. ACIT [ITA Nos. 5750, 5751 & 5752/Del/2025] (ITAT Delhi) 17. In the view of the aforesaid discussion, the factual finding recorded by the CIT(A) does not warrant any interference on our part and accordingly, ITA Nos.2419 & 2420/Del/2026 the deletion of addition u/s 68 amounting to Rs.10,81,00,000/- Rs.10,81,00,000/ and the consequential addition of Rs.32,43,000/ Rs.32,43,000/- u/s 69C by CIT(A) is upheld. 18. With regard to the addition of Rs.6,15,00,000/ Rs.6,15,00,000/- u/s 68 in respect of loans from 5 other parties, the details of which as referred at Page 554 of the impugned order is as under: 19. We have already deleted the addition in respect of the parties which were subject matter of reopening u/s 147 and as such the addition in respect of other parties do not survive in the light of principle laid dow down by Hon’ble Delhi High Court in the case of Ranbaxy Laboratories Ltd. v. CIT [2011] 336 ITR 136 (Delhi). 20. Even otherwise, it is noted that the assessee has placed on record following documents in support of loan: i. Copy of audited financial statement of the lender companies ii. Signed Affidavits ITA Nos.2419 & 2420/Del/2026 iii. Confirmed ledger account iv. Bank statement highlighting receipt and repayment of loans v. TDS deduction on interest payment. 21. We find that ld. CIT(A) has rightly noticed that the allegation in respect of these loans was not even part of the reasons recorded for reopening u/s 147 and was introduced only at the fag end of the assessment proceedings without any fresh incriminating material. Even otherwise, on merits also, the assessee had produced complete documentary evidence in support of these loans, which has not been effectively rebutted by the Assessing Officer through any independent enquiry. We further find that the assessing officer has not brought on record any adverse material or information disputing the genuineness of these loans and the assessee having discharged the primary onus u/s, the action of CIT(A) in deleting the addition cannot be faulted. 22. Once the additions u/s 68 fail on merits, the consequential additions u/s 69C towards alleged commission expenditure and disallowance of interest expenditure automatically lose their very foundation. Since the principal additions do not survive, there remains no legal basis for sustaining the consequential additions made u/s 69C of the Act. 23. Thus, we find ourselves in complete agreement with the detailed appreciation of facts and evidence recorded by the ld. CIT(A). The Revenue has not been able to point out any factual infirmity or perversity ITA Nos.2419 & 2420/Del/2026 in the findings recorded by the first appellate authority. We therefore see no reason to interfere with the well-reasoned order of the ld. CIT(A). Accordingly, the order of the ld. CIT(A) deleting the addition Rs.6,15,00,000/- u/s 68 and the consequential addition of Rs.14,96,565/- u/s 69C is upheld. As a result, the grounds raised by the Revenue are dismissed.” 7.5. In view of above material facts of receiving unsecured loan of Rs. 3,99,00,000/- and repayment with complete disclosure and confirmation by the lendor, by respectfully following the judicial precedents the addition of Rs. 3,99,00,000/- being illegal is set aside. Ground of Appeal No. 4 to 4.7, 5 & 6 are allowed. 8. Ground of Appeal No. 1 to 3 and 7 having become academic are left open. 9. In the result, appeal filed by the Assessee is allowed.
