Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Devoir Trading Limited (CAAR Mumbai) The Customs Authority for Advance Rulings (CAAR), Mumbai, in In re Devoir Trading Limited, ruled that the import of Inshell Walnuts under valid transferable Duty Free Import Authorisations (DFIAs) could qualify for exemption from Basic Customs Duty (BCD) under Notification No. 25/2023-Customs dated 1 April 2023, subject to fulfilment of the applicable statutory and authorisation conditions. The applicant proposed importing inshell walnuts for trading against transferable DFIAs issued following exports of assorted confectionery products, biscuits, vegetable pickles and namkeens, mixtures or savouries under SION E-1, E-5, E-126 and E-132. The applicant sought clarification on whether percentage-based CIF value restrictions and actual-user conditions introduced through DGFT Public Notice No. 41/2015-2020 dated 2 November 2016 could restrict the exemption. It argued that these restrictions affected substantive benefits under the DFIA Scheme and could not be enforced merely through a public notice without the legally prescribed notification process. The Authority examined the relevant provisions of the Foreign Trade Policy, 2023, particularly Paragraphs 4.12, 4.24, 4.26, 4.28 and 4.29, along with the conditions of Notification No. 25/2023-Customs and CBIC Circular No. 20/2025-Customs dated 24 July 2025. It observed that inshell walnuts could fall within generic input descriptions such as fruit, flavour and dietary fibre under the relevant SIONs, as recognised in earlier decisions involving VKC Nuts Pvt. Ltd. However, the imported goods must correspond to the input description endorsed in the particular DFIA, and the prescribed quantity, value, validity and other authorisation requirements must be satisfied. The Authority also relied on Nrapen Shankar Acharya v. Union of India (Rajasthan High Court), Frunuts Exim LLP v. Commissioner of Customs (CESTAT Mumbai) and Global Exim v. Commissioner of Customs (CESTAT Kolkata). These decisions established that an actual-user condition or percentage-based CIF value cap affecting DFIA entitlements could not acquire independent legal enforceability merely through a public notice where the applicable legal framework required a notification issued through the prescribed statutory process. Accordingly, the CAAR held that percentage-based CIF value restrictions and actual-user conditions introduced solely through the disputed DGFT public notice could not independently justify denial of the exemption under Notification No. 25/2023-Customs. The Authority clarified that its finding did not remove the obligation to comply with the express conditions of the exemption notification or the description, quantity, value and validity limits specified in the individual DFIA. Therefore, Devoir Trading Limited was entitled to claim the Basic Customs Duty exemption on eligible imports of inshell walnuts under transferable DFIAs issued against the relevant SIONs, provided all applicable conditions were fulfilled. The ruling distinguished restrictions imposed solely through an unenforceable public notice from binding requirements contained in the governing notification, Foreign Trade Policy and authorisation. The notification requires, inter alia, that the imported material to be covered by the valid DFIA and that the description, value and quantity of the imported material be within the limits specified in the authorisation, subject to the conditions expressly prescribed therein. 7.26 Therefore, where a valid transferable DFIA has been issued by the competent Regional Authority and the imported goods correspond to the input description endorsed therein, a percentage-based value restriction introduced through the impugned Public Notice cannot, in view of the judicial pronouncements discussed above, be independently invoked by Customs for denying the exemption, insofar as such restriction is sought to be enforced solely on the strength of Public Notice. 7.27 I, however, find it necessary to clarify that the above finding does not dispense with the requirement that the imported goods must correspond to the description endorsed in the I)FIA, nor does it dispense with compliance with the quantity, validity and other particulars specified in the authorization or the express conditions of Notification No. 25/2023-Cus dated 01.04.2023. The finding is confined to the enforceability of the percentage-based CIF value restrictions and the “Actual User” condition insofar as the same are sought to be imposed merely on the basis of Public Notice No. 41/2015-2020 dated 02.11.2016.
