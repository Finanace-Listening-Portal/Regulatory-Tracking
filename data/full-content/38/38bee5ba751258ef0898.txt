Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Bikram Exports Vs ACIT (ITAT Delhi) Summary : The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Delhi Bench C, in Bikram Exports Vs Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax, allowed the assessee’s appeals for Assessment Years 2015-16 and 2016-17, holding that the deduction claimed under Section 10AA of the Income-tax Act, 1961, could not be denied merely because the jewellery manufacturing unit had limited fixed assets and engaged artisans or contract workers. The assessee was engaged in manufacturing and exporting jewellery from its unit situated in the Noida Special Economic Zone. The Assessing Officer questioned the genuineness of its manufacturing activities because the assessee reported turnover of approximately ₹53 crore while its fixed assets amounted to only ₹18,54,014, including machinery valued at ₹21,927. The Assessing Officer consequently disallowed the Section 10AA claim, resulting in an addition of ₹71,18,075 in the lead appeal. The Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) upheld the disallowance, prompting the assessee to approach the Tribunal. The principal controversy concerned whether the assessee had actually undertaken manufacturing activities within its SEZ premises or had merely outsourced production to outside parties. The Assessing Officer questioned the limited machinery, details of employees and karigars, production processes, jewellery designs, manufacturing arrangements and payments to workers. The assessee explained that jewellery manufacturing was predominantly a labour-intensive activity, involving skilled artisans rather than substantial machinery. It furnished details of its manufacturing process, including distribution of gold to workers, melting, drawing, setting, soldering, polishing, checking and packaging. The assessee also maintained that manufacturing was carried out within its SEZ premises, even where contract labour was engaged, and that outsourcing the workforce did not mean outsourcing manufacturing outside the eligible unit. The Tribunal examined the material and observed that the assessee had furnished complete details of jewellery manufacturing, employee and artisan particulars, payments, purchases of gold and related records. The Department had not established that the manufacturing activities were undertaken outside the SEZ premises. The Tribunal specifically observed that Section 10AA did not impose a condition requiring the assessee to employ its own workers or own substantial machinery. The relevant question was whether the eligible undertaking carried out manufacturing or production activities within the SEZ. The Tribunal found that the Assessing Officer had misunderstood the assessee’s business model and that the available evidence supported the manufacturing operations conducted at the eligible unit. The Tribunal also considered the objections concerning jewellery designs, customer orders, production wastage and export turnover. The assessee explained that foreign customers supplied proprietary designs and templates, which could not be disclosed indiscriminately because of commercial confidentiality. Orders were frequently received through telephone communications because of longstanding business relationships. Regarding wastage, the assessee explained that it remained within the prescribed customs standards and was borne by foreign customers. The Tribunal further noticed that the Assessing Officer had incorrectly referred to exports of ₹253 crore at one point, whereas the actual turnover was approximately ₹53 crore. Having considered these circumstances, the Tribunal held that the objections raised by the Department did not justify denying the exemption under Section 10AA and allowed the assessee’s claim. A separate issue arose for Assessment Year 2016-17 concerning the disallowance of bonus expenditure of ₹16,13,250. The assessee had submitted employee particulars, payment details and acquittance rolls acknowledging receipt of bonus. The Assessing Officer and CIT(A) had nevertheless sustained the disallowance, partly in view of a discrepancy between the bonus expenditure of ₹16,11,450 reflected in the audit report and ₹16,13,250 appearing in other supporting documents. The Tribunal held that the difference of ₹2,000 appeared to be a clerical mistake or book adjustment. Since the Department had not identified any substantive defect in the supporting evidence, the Tribunal deleted the disallowance and allowed the bonus claim. Accordingly, the Tribunal allowed both appeals. The decision establishes that the eligibility of an SEZ undertaking for deduction under Section 10AA must be determined from its actual manufacturing activities and compliance with statutory conditions, rather than assumptions based solely on the value of machinery, use of contract labour or the absence of written customer agreements. The ruling also demonstrates that a minor accounting discrepancy cannot, by itself, justify disallowing an otherwise substantiated business expenditure.
