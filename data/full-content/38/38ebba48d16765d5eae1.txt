Articles 4 min read How AI agents under UPI could work The framework, called the Unified Agent Protocol (UAP), is expected to support low-value, frequent purchases such as groceries initially, with more sophisticated use cases potentially emerging later ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Sep 8, 2026 at 07:00 AM IST var news_msid = 133894286; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook AI agents could eventually make small UPI payments on behalf of users without requiring approval for every transaction, under a proposed framework being developed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI). The proposed model would allow users to establish rules and spending limits within which an AI agent could potentially transact, rather than giving the software unrestricted access to a bank account.The framework, called the Unified Agent Protocol (UAP), is expected to support low-value, frequent purchases such as groceries initially, with more sophisticated use cases potentially emerging later. NPCI is working on the framework and that it could be unveiled at the Global Fintech Fest in Mumbai. The exact technical architecture, however, has not been publicly detailed by NPCI. Advt The basic idea is to move UPI from a model in which a user has to actively approve each payment towards one where an AI agent can act within a clearly defined boundary. A user could, for example, instruct an agent to buy a particular item when its price falls below a specified level, or make routine purchases subject to a daily or transaction limit. The agent would then be expected to initiate the payment only when the transaction meets those conditions.Delegating payment authorityNPCI already has mechanisms that could form part of such an architecture. UPI Circle allows a primary user to delegate payment authority to another user, while UPI Reserve Pay allows funds to be blocked for multiple subsequent debits. Reuters has reported that the proposed UAP is expected to draw on these mechanisms.Importantly, these existing features should not be confused with fully autonomous AI payments. Under the current Reserve Pay framework, funds are blocked by the customer for multiple future payments, while subsequent debits are initiated through the merchant platform. NPCI's guidelines also require a user journey for setting up and managing the reserve.Similarly, existing UPI Circle functionality does not mean an AI agent can currently spend freely from a customer's account. Any future agentic layer would therefore require additional controls and authorisation mechanisms.How the proposed system could work Advt The likely process can be understood in five stages, although this is a description of the proposed model rather than a published NPCI transaction flow.First, the user sets the boundaries. The customer could specify what the AI agent is allowed to buy, the maximum amount it can spend, how frequently it can transact and potentially other conditions such as price thresholds.Second, the agent receives limited authority. Instead of handing over unrestricted access to the customer's bank account, the proposed system could link the agent to a delegated payment mechanism, with its authority constrained by the rules set by the user. Advt Third, a payment facility could be established. Existing mechanisms such as Reserve Pay could potentially be used to set aside funds or otherwise control the amount available for agent-led transactions. The current Reserve Pay limit is Rs 10,000 for up to 90 days, although Reuters reported that these parameters could be revisited for agentic payments.Fourth, the agent identifies an eligible transaction. If the purchase satisfies the user's instructions, such as being below a specified price and within the permitted spending limit, the agent could initiate the payment without asking the user to approve that individual transaction. Advt Finally, the transaction would pass through the UPI ecosystem. The proposed framework is expected to incorporate controls such as spending limits, identity checks and audit trails. Reuters also reported that NPCI plans to develop a liability framework, although details have not yet been disclosed.Why this is different from AutoPayAgentic payments would also be different from UPI AutoPay.AutoPay allows a customer to establish a mandate for recurring payments, such as subscriptions or scheduled bills. The basic instruction has already been defined in advance.An AI agent, by contrast, could potentially assess a situation and decide whether a transaction falls within the conditions specified by the user. For example, an agent could be instructed to purchase a product whenever its price falls below Rs 1,000, rather than being told to pay a fixed amount on a particular date.That distinction is important because the agent is not merely executing a recurring instruction; it is potentially making a transaction decision within a predefined set of constraints.The security challengeThe move could make digital payments significantly more convenient, but it also changes the risk equation. Once software is allowed to initiate transactions, the system needs to ensure that the agent's authority cannot be expanded or manipulated beyond what the customer intended.That makes spending caps, identity verification, transaction records and clear liability rules particularly important. UPI itself currently relies on user authentication for bank transactions, with NPCI stating that the UPI PIN is used to authorise bank transactions.The proposed agentic framework therefore represents a shift in how authorisation could work: rather than asking the customer to authenticate every individual payment, the system would need to authenticate the agent's authority and ensure that each transaction remains within the authority granted by the customer.For now, the precise mechanism remains under development. What is clear is that NPCI is looking to build an additional layer around UPI that could allow AI agents to transact within controlled limits, rather than turning UPI itself into an unrestricted autonomous payment system.
