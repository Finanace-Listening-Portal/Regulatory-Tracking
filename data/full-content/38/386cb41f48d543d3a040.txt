Summary: Registration of a Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) with the Reserve Bank of India requires careful assessment of the proposed financial activities, applicable regulatory category, minimum Net Owned Fund (NOF), promoter suitability, ownership structure and operational readiness. Section 45-IA of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 generally requires eligible companies undertaking regulated non-banking financial business to obtain a Certificate of Registration before commencing operations, subject to applicable exemptions. The principal business assessment commonly involves the 50–50 test relating to financial assets and financial income. New NBFC–Investment and Credit Companies, NBFC–Micro Finance Institutions and NBFC–Factors generally require minimum NOF of ₹10 crore, while different capital thresholds apply to other specialised categories. Applicants must prepare incorporation records, financial statements, capital certificates, promoter and director disclosures, beneficial ownership information, board resolutions, business plans and operational policies. The registration application is submitted through RBI’s PRAVAAH portal and may undergo further regulatory scrutiny. A specific exemption effective from 1 July 2026 is relevant to qualifying NBFCs with assets below ₹1,000 crore that neither access public funds nor have customer interface, subject to prescribed conditions. Obtaining registration does not automatically permit public deposit acceptance. After approval, NBFCs must comply with applicable customer identification, anti-money laundering, fair lending, capital adequacy, reporting, governance and regulatory requirements. They help meet the financial needs of individuals and businesses across different sectors. Entrepreneurs planning to establish a finance company in India must understand the requirements for NBFC registration with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). Incorporating a company is only the beginning. Before commencing regulated financial activities, the company must assess its registration obligations, arrange the required capital, and establish suitable management and compliance systems. The registration process involves examining the proposed business model, selecting the appropriate NBFC category, preparing supporting documents, and submitting an application for RBI’s consideration. What Is an NBFC? An NBFC is a company incorporated under the Companies Act that carries on specified financial activities as its principal business. These activities may include providing loans and advances, financing assets, acquiring securities, leasing, and hire-purchase. Specialised NBFC categories cover activities such as microfinance, factoring, peer-to-peer lending facilitation, and financial information aggregation. For example, a company established primarily to provide loans to small businesses may require registration as an NBFC–Investment and Credit Company. However, a manufacturing company does not automatically become an NBFC merely because it occasionally lends money or earns interest on investments. Its principal business, asset composition, and income pattern must be examined together. Companies principally engaged in agriculture, industrial activities, trading in goods other than securities, or specified non-financial activities generally fall outside the ordinary NBFC definition. Legal Structure for NBFC Registration The principal provision governing ordinary NBFC registration is Section 45-IA of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. Subject to applicable exemptions, a company must obtain a Certificate of Registration and maintain the prescribed Net Owned Fund before commencing or carrying on the business of a non-banking financial institution. The Companies Act governs the incorporation and corporate administration of the applicant company, while RBI’s regulatory framework governs its permitted financial activities. Depending on the business, additional requirements concerning anti-money laundering, foreign investment, customer protection, information technology, and financial reporting may apply. Specialised entities, including Housing Finance Companies, Account Aggregators, and Peer-to-Peer Lending Platforms, must also satisfy their category-specific requirements. Therefore, applicants should identify the relevant framework before preparing their registration documents. When Is NBFC Registration Required? The requirement for NBFC registration depends on the company’s financial activities, principal business, and eligibility for any specific exemption. A company should assess the substance of its operations rather than rely only on its name or the objects mentioned in its Memorandum of Association. Understanding the 50–50 Principal Business Test The principal business assessment is commonly called the 50–50 test. Under this assessment, financial assets must exceed 50% of the company’s total assets after the prescribed adjustment for intangible assets. Income from financial assets must also exceed 50% of its gross income. Both conditions must be satisfied. For example, suppose a company has adjusted total assets of ₹20 crore, including ₹14 crore of relevant financial assets. It earns gross income of ₹3 crore, of which ₹2 crore comes from those financial assets. Its financial assets represent 70% of adjusted total assets, while its financial income represents approximately 66.67% of gross income. On these simplified figures, both conditions are satisfied. A proposed finance company should assess its registration obligations before launching regulated operations. It should not assume that it can first build a lending business and seek RBI approval later. Eligibility Standards for NBFC Registration with RBI Incorporation Under the Companies Act The applicant must be a company incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013, or an existing company incorporated under the earlier Companies Act. A private limited company or public limited company may apply, depending on the proposed structure and category requirements. An LLP, partnership firm, or sole proprietorship cannot apply through the ordinary company-based NBFC registration route. The company’s Memorandum of Association should appropriately cover the proposed financial activities, and its constitutional documents should be consistent with the business described in the application. Selection of the Appropriate NBFC Category The applicant must select a category that matches its actual business model. A company that lends directly from its balance sheet operates differently from a platform that facilitates lending between participants. Similarly, an Account Aggregator facilitates consent-based financial information sharing and does not operate as an ordinary lending company. NBFC–Investment and Credit Companies generally undertake lending, asset finance, and investment activities. NBFC–Micro Finance Institutions focus on microfinance under the applicable framework, while NBFC–Factors undertake factoring and receivables financing. Infrastructure Finance Companies, Core Investment Companies, and Housing Finance Companies have separate defining conditions. Selecting the correct category at the beginning helps determine the applicable capital requirement, documentation, operating restrictions, and continuing compliance obligations. Minimum Net Owned Fund Requirement The company must maintain the minimum Net Owned Fund, commonly called NOF, prescribed for its category. For a new NBFC–Investment and Credit Company, NBFC–Micro Finance Institution, or NBFC–Factor, the prescribed minimum NOF is ₹10 crore. The minimum NOF for NBFC–Peer-to-Peer Lending Platforms and NBFC–Account Aggregators is ₹2 crore. NBFCs holding registration as Type I also have a ₹2 crore requirement. Infrastructure Finance Companies and Infrastructure Debt Fund–NBFCs have a prescribed NOF requirement of ₹300 crore. Other specialised categories require separate examination. Certain existing NBFC–ICCs, NBFC–MFIs, and NBFC–Factors have a transition schedule to reach ₹10 crore by 31 March 2027. This transition should not be interpreted as a general entitlement for a new applicant to seek registration with lower capital. Correct Calculation of Net Owned Fund NOF is a regulatory calculation and should not be confused with authorised share capital or the amount appearing in a bank account. Broadly, the calculation considers eligible paid-up equity capital and free reserves, reduces prescribed losses and intangible items, and applies relevant deductions for specified investments and exposures. For example, a company with authorised capital of ₹10 crore does not automatically satisfy a ₹10 crore NOF requirement. The capital must actually be contributed, and the prescribed calculation must establish sufficient qualifying funds. Accumulated losses, expenses, or other deductions may reduce the available NOF below the required amount. An appropriate auditor’s certificate should support the calculation, and the figures should reconcile with the company’s financial statements and supporting records. Suitable Promoters and Directors RBI examines the background and suitability of the persons responsible for owning and managing the proposed NBFC. Their professional experience, financial standing, credit history, regulatory record, and associations with other businesses may be relevant to the assessment. Promoters and directors should disclose material litigation, defaults, regulatory proceedings, and other relevant matters accurately. An issue requiring explanation should be supported by appropriate records. Incomplete or misleading disclosures can create concerns about the reliability of the application and the proposed management. The management team should also demonstrate that it understands the responsibilities involved in operating a regulated financial institution. Transparent Ownership and Source of Capital The applicant should be able to explain who ultimately owns and controls the company and how its capital has been generated. Capital contributions should be supported by a clear banking trail and relevant financial records. Where a shareholder is another company, the ownership structure may need to be traced through several entities to identify the ultimate beneficial owners. Complex arrangements should have a clear commercial explanation and complete supporting documents. Foreign investment requires an additional examination of applicable investment conditions, reporting obligations, beneficial ownership, and regulatory restrictions. Viable Business Plan and Operational Readiness A proposed NBFC should demonstrate that its business model is commercially realistic and operationally manageable. Its business plan should explain the proposed products, target customers, funding sources, lending approach, geographical coverage, staffing, technology, and risk controls. For example, a company projecting substantial loan growth should explain how it will fund disbursements, assess borrowers, monitor repayments, handle collections, and absorb credit losses. Financial projections should reflect realistic assumptions about income, expenses, defaults, and capital needs. The company should also identify the people responsible for lending, finance, compliance, customer complaints, and risk management. Documents Required for NBFC Registration Company Incorporation Documents The applicant should prepare its Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum of Association, Articles of Association, PAN, registered office information, and details of its directors and authorised signatories. These documents establish the company’s legal identity and proposed scope of business. An existing company should also organise its statutory records, previous filings, and information about its historical activities. Any inconsistency between the company’s records and the proposed financial business should be examined before submission. Financial and Capital Documents Financial documents may include audited financial statements, the latest financial position, company bank statements, capital contribution records, and an auditor-certified NOF calculation. Supporting information concerning borrowings, investments, and related-party exposures may also be required. The source of capital should be supported by appropriate records, which may include promoter financial statements, tax records, bank statements, and investment documents. Financial figures must remain consistent across the application and its supporting certificates. Promoter and Director Documents Promoters and directors should prepare identity and address documents, professional profiles, information about other directorships, and details of their business interests. Relevant declarations concerning financial standing, litigation, defaults, and regulatory matters may also be required. Depending on the applicable checklist and RBI’s scrutiny, banker reports and additional background documents may be requested. These records help establish the identity, experience, and suitability of the persons involved. Shareholding and Beneficial Ownership Documents The company should provide an accurate shareholding pattern showing the ownership held by each shareholder. Where corporate shareholders or layered ownership structures are involved, ownership charts and supporting records may be necessary. Beneficial ownership information should clearly identify the persons who ultimately own or control the company. Any changes during the application process should be appropriately recorded and communicated where required. Board Resolutions and Authorisations The board should approve the application for NBFC registration and authorise the person responsible for signing and submitting it. Relevant resolutions may also address the business plan, proposed activities, applicable public deposit declarations, and adoption of policies. The wording of these resolutions should match the company’s actual intentions and the requirements of the applicable application checklist. Business Plan and Policy Documents The application should be supported by a business plan and relevant policies reflecting the company’s operating model. Depending on the proposed activities, these may cover lending, customer identification, fair practices, risk management, recovery, grievance redressal, outsourcing, and information security. Policies should explain how the company will operate in practice. Documents copied from unrelated businesses may create inconsistencies if they refer to products, systems, or personnel that the applicant does not have. Step-by-Step Procedure for NBFC Registration with RBI Step 1: Assess the Proposed Business Activities The first step is to define the company’s proposed financial activities clearly. The promoters should determine whether the company will lend directly, finance assets, undertake microfinance, acquire receivables, facilitate lending, or primarily invest in group entities. This assessment determines the appropriate category and whether a specific exemption applies. It also establishes the applicable capital requirement and regulatory framework. Step 2: Incorporate or Review the Applicant Company For a new venture, the company should be incorporated with appropriate objects and a suitable ownership structure. The promoters should ensure that its constitutional documents support the proposed financial business. For an existing company, the review should cover statutory filings, financial statements, historical transactions, ownership records, and previous business activities. Any gaps should be addressed before the registration application is prepared. Step 3: Arrange the Required Capital The company should bring in qualifying capital through properly documented banking channels and complete the associated corporate formalities. The amount contributed should be sufficient after considering the deductions applicable to NOF. Promoters should also budget for operating expenses beyond the minimum capital requirement. Salaries, premises, technology, professional support, and early business losses can affect the company’s financial position. Step 4: Obtain Financial Review and Certification The company’s financial position and NOF calculation should be reviewed before filing. This review should identify whether losses, expenses, investments, or related-party exposures affect its eligibility. The relevant auditor’s certificate should accurately reflect the financial records as of the specified date. Supporting schedules should be available to explain the calculation if RBI seeks clarification. Step 5: Prepare the Business Plan and Operating Framework The applicant should prepare a detailed business plan explaining its products, customers, distribution channels, funding sources, credit assessment methods, and financial projections. It should also establish responsibility for loan approvals, collections, compliance monitoring, financial reporting, and customer complaints. The operating framework should be proportionate to the proposed business and capable of being implemented. Step 6: Compile and Review the Application The company should complete the prescribed application and assemble the documents required under the current checklist. Before submission, it should reconcile the company name, capital, ownership, director details, financial figures, and proposed activities across every document. A clear index and organised supporting records make the application easier to review. Any material inconsistency should be resolved before filing. Step 7: Submit Through RBI’s PRAVAAH Portal The applicant should use the relevant current application facility on RBI’s PRAVAAH portal. It must provide the prescribed information, upload supporting documents, and follow the instructions applicable to its category. Any additional submission method or physical document requirement should be followed where specifically prescribed. The company should retain the acknowledgement and application reference for tracking and correspondence. Step 8: Respond to RBI Queries During scrutiny, RBI may request additional information concerning capital, ownership, promoter background, financial projections, previous activities, or operational readiness. Responses should address each query directly and include supporting evidence. If an earlier figure or statement requires correction, the company should explain the reason and submit the revised document. Communications should be monitored closely so that responses are provided within the specified timeline. Step 9: Obtain and Review the Certificate of Registration If RBI is satisfied that the applicable conditions have been met, it may grant the Certificate of Registration. The company should review the approved category, permitted activities, restrictions, and any conditions attached to the approval. Before commencing business, the board should ensure that the necessary operating arrangements are in place. The certificate should not be presented as RBI guaranteeing the company’s performance or endorsing its financial products. Registration Exemption Effective from July 2026 From 1 July 2026, a specific exemption applies to eligible NBFCs with assets below ₹1,000 crore that neither access public funds nor have customer interface, subject to the prescribed conditions. This exemption requires careful examination. A company lending to customers cannot assume that it qualifies merely because its funds come from shareholders. The regulatory meaning of public funds is also broader than public deposits and can include bank finance and specified external funding. Eligible existing entities may apply for deregistration through the prescribed process. Group aggregation requirements and other conditions must also be considered. The exemption does not remove every applicable provision of the RBI Act or permit unrestricted financial activities. Timeline for NBFC Registration The registration timeline depends on the NBFC category, application completeness, ownership complexity, capital verification, and regulatory queries. Preparation may involve company incorporation or review, capital contribution, financial certification, business planning, and policy development. RBI examination is a separate stage and may involve clarification rounds. Promoters should therefore avoid assuming a fixed approval date or committing to a business launch that depends on immediate registration. A well-prepared application can reduce avoidable clarification, but approval remains subject to regulatory assessment. Cost of NBFC Registration The overall financial commitment includes regulatory capital, incorporation expenses, professional preparation, operating infrastructure, and continuing compliance expenditure. The minimum NOF is capital maintained by the company; it is not a registration fee paid to RBI. Other expenses may include applicable statutory charges, accounting and certification, legal documentation, application assistance, technology, premises, and staffing. A proper cost estimate should distinguish capital from fees and operating expenses. This helps promoters assess whether the proposed business has sufficient resources to launch and remain compliant. Can an NBFC Accept Public Deposits? Ordinary NBFC registration does not automatically authorise a company to accept public deposits. Deposit acceptance is subject to a separate framework and specific authorisation requirements. RBI has historically followed a policy of not registering new public deposit-taking NBFCs. Promoters should therefore avoid preparing a business model that assumes they can collect deposits from the public after obtaining an ordinary NBFC registration. Each proposed funding arrangement should be examined independently for compliance. Compliance After NBFC Registration Customer Identification and Anti-Money Laundering Controls The company must implement the customer identification and anti-money laundering requirements applicable to its business. These may involve verifying customers, identifying beneficial owners, maintaining records, monitoring transactions, and making prescribed reports. Responsibilities should be assigned clearly, and employees should understand how to identify and escalate unusual activity. Fair Lending and Customer Communication Borrowers should receive clear information about interest, charges, repayment obligations, and other material terms. Loan documents and communications should accurately describe the product and the customer’s responsibilities. Collection practices and grievance handling should follow the applicable requirements. Customer complaints should be recorded, reviewed, and addressed through an established process. Financial and Regulatory Reporting The NBFC should maintain accurate books, complete applicable audits, and submit the returns relevant to its category. Financial statements and regulatory reports should remain consistent with the underlying records. A compliance calendar can help assign responsibility and track reporting dates, board reviews, certifications, and other recurring obligations. Capital and Loan Portfolio Monitoring Maintaining the prescribed capital is a continuing responsibility. The company should regularly review its financial position and the effect of losses, expenses, and exposures on regulatory requirements. Loan performance, overdue accounts, provisioning, liquidity, and concentration risks should also be monitored under the applicable framework. Changes in Ownership or Management Certain acquisitions, changes in control, shareholding transfers, or management changes may require prior regulatory approval. The company should assess the applicable requirements before completing a transaction. Corporate approvals alone may not satisfy the regulatory conditions governing an NBFC. Common Mistakes Applicants Should Avoid Applicants often face avoidable difficulties when they choose an unsuitable category, confuse authorised capital with NOF, provide an incomplete source-of-funds trail, or submit inconsistent financial information. Other problems arise from incomplete director disclosures, unrealistic projections, outdated filing instructions, and policies that cannot be implemented in practice. Assuming that registration permits public deposit acceptance or commencing regulated activities before approval can create further complications. A thorough review should confirm that every material statement in the application is supported by records, financial calculations, or a credible operating arrangement. Conclusion NBFC registration with RBI is an important step towards establishing a regulated finance business in India. Choosing the appropriate category, meeting the applicable Net Owned Fund requirement, preparing accurate documents, and developing a realistic business plan form the foundation of a strong application. After registration, the company must continue to meet its applicable regulatory and compliance obligations. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ’s) Q1. What is NBFC registration with RBI? Ans. NBFC registration is the process of obtaining a Certificate of Registration from RBI to undertake the relevant non-banking financial business. The applicant must satisfy the eligibility, capital, management, and other requirements applicable to its category. Q2. Who can apply for NBFC registration in India? Ans. A company incorporated under the Companies Act can apply, subject to the applicable requirements. An LLP, partnership firm, or sole proprietorship cannot apply through the ordinary company-based NBFC registration route. Q3. What is the minimum capital required for NBFC registration? Ans. For a new NBFC–Investment and Credit Company, NBFC–Micro Finance Institution, or NBFC–Factor, the prescribed minimum Net Owned Fund is ₹10 crore. Other categories have different requirements, so the proposed business model must be assessed first. Q4. Is Net Owned Fund the same as authorised share capital? Ans. No. Authorised share capital is the maximum share capital permitted under the company’s constitutional documents. NOF is calculated using eligible capital and reserves after prescribed deductions. Merely having authorised capital of ₹10 crore does not satisfy a ₹10 crore NOF requirement. Q5. What documents are required for NBFC registration? Ans. The documents generally include incorporation records, constitutional documents, financial statements, an NOF certificate, capital and source-of-funds records, promoter and director information, ownership details, board resolutions, and a business plan. The exact checklist depends on the category and current application requirements. Q6. How can a company apply for NBFC registration? Ans. The company should identify its category, meet the applicable eligibility requirements, prepare the prescribed documents, and submit its application through the relevant facility on RBI’s PRAVAAH portal. RBI may seek additional information before deciding the application. Q7. How long does NBFC registration take? Ans. The timeline varies according to the category, application completeness, ownership structure, capital verification, and regulatory queries. Applicants should allow time for preparation and RBI scrutiny rather than assume a fixed approval date. Q8. Can an NBFC start lending before obtaining RBI registration? Ans. A company that requires NBFC registration must obtain the relevant Certificate of Registration before commencing the regulated business. Incorporation alone does not authorise it to begin such activities. Any claimed exemption should be examined carefully. Q9. Does NBFC registration permit public deposit acceptance? Ans. No. Ordinary NBFC registration does not automatically permit public deposit acceptance. Deposit-taking activities are subject to separate regulatory conditions and specific authorisation requirements. Q10. Does an NBFC need compliance support after registration? Ans. Yes. Depending on its category, an NBFC must meet continuing requirements concerning customer identification, fair practices, financial reporting, audits, capital, regulatory returns, and other obligations.
