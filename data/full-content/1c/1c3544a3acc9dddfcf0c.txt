Transactions rose 28.1% to 63.2 crore, while the average transaction value fell 17.4% to Rs 3,201.The credit card base increased 10.5% year-on-year to 12.41 crore in August, with 11.96 lakh cards added during the month. The pace of card additions has exceeded spending growth for the past two months, with the gap widening to 450 basis points in August from 270 basis points in July. Spending per card fell 4.1% year-on-year to Rs 16,351. Advt The increase in transaction volumes was accompanied by more frequent but smaller payments. The average card was used nearly five times during August, compared with just over four times a year earlier. The growing use of RuPay credit cards on UPI is also taking cards into lower-value payments at small merchants.The shift in transaction mix was more visible online. The average online transaction value fell about 21% year-on-year, compared with a 12% decline at point-of-sale terminals. Online spending rose 3.9% year-on-year to Rs 1.23 lakh crore, while point-of-sale spending increased 9.1% to Rs 79,355 crore. Point-of-sale spending has grown faster than online spending every month since January 2026.Private banks gain sharePrivate sector banks regained market share in August, with their share of spending rising 121 basis points month-on-month to 73.34%. Public sector banks' share fell 145 basis points to 20.75%. The June increase in the public sector share had been driven mainly by a one-off jump in corporate spending at SBI Group, which subsequently reversed.The changing spending pattern was also reflected in individual issuer strategies. One large private sector bank reduced spending after repricing wallet loading, online gaming and high-value transport transactions and linking benefits to quarterly spending thresholds, while a mid-sized private bank continued to shrink its unsecured book and focus on secured lending. Advt Credit card spending declined 2.7% month-on-month in August, compared with a 1.4% decline in August 2025 and a 2.6% decline in August 2024. The timing of festivals that fell in August last year but move to September this year also weighed on the monthly comparison.Spending is expected to rise to Rs 2.09-2.16 lakh crore in September, although the year-on-year comparison could remain subdued because September 2025 had strong festive online sales and followed GST cuts. Spending is expected to increase further in October and November with festive sales and Diwali.
