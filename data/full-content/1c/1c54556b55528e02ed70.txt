Summary: ICICI Prudential Balanced Advantage Fund is an open-ended dynamic asset-allocation mutual-fund scheme that may invest in equity and equity-related instruments, derivatives, debt and money-market instruments. Its official Scheme Information Document (SID), available through AMFI and reflecting information as at September 2025, identifies the CRISIL Hybrid 50+50 – Moderate Index as its Tier-I benchmark and an investment objective of capital appreciation/income without any assurance of achievement. The AMC’s official March 2026 scheme factsheet reported closing AUM of ₹66,397.80 crore, Regular Plan TER of 1.45% and Direct Plan TER of 0.88%; those are explicitly historical figures, not verified October 2026 live values. The AMC’s online factsheet index is dated 31 July 2026, so the latest current TER, portfolio, AUM and riskometer must be checked before any transaction. The documented exit-load structure provides a 30% free-unit limit within one year and 1% load on the excess units redeemed within one year, with nil load after one year, subject to the operative terms. The scheme’s equity-oriented tax status, if maintained under statutory tests, can attract 20% qualifying short-term and 12.5% qualifying long-term capital-gains rates above the ₹1.25 lakh annual threshold under the Income-tax Act, 2025 framework. This article distinguishes gross equity exposure, derivatives hedges, debt holdings, risk, expenses, taxation, NRI issues and source-date limitations without ranking or recommending the scheme. It is open ended and offers subscriptions, redemptions and switches on business days at applicable NAV, subject to the relevant terms. The objective in the official SID is to provide capital appreciation/income by investing in equity and equity-related instruments, including derivatives, and debt and money-market instruments. The AMC expressly disclaims any assurance that this objective will be achieved. The Tier-I benchmark is the CRISIL Hybrid 50+50 – Moderate Index. This is not an instruction that the fund must permanently hold exactly half its assets in unhedged equities and half in debt. Its dynamic strategy may use derivatives to reduce effective equity exposure while retaining a higher gross equity holding. Benchmark-relative return therefore needs to be read alongside the fund’s net equity exposure, debt duration and market conditions. Gross equity is the market value of equity securities held. Effective or net equity exposure can be materially lower when index futures or other permitted derivatives offset market sensitivity. Arbitrage or hedged-equity positions may serve a different risk and tax function from unhedged directional holdings. A headline equity percentage alone cannot describe the scheme’s true market risk. The official March 2026 portfolio disclosure showed equity holdings, index-futures/options positions, debt instruments, certificates of deposit, government securities, corporate securities, REIT units, money-market positions and cash/current assets. For example, the January 2026 factsheet disclosed index futures/options with negative exposure alongside physical equity and a separate debt allocation. These disclosures demonstrate why a reader should not infer a stable 50:50 allocation from the benchmark name. Debt sleeve and interest-rate exposure The debt allocation may include government securities, treasury bills, certificates of deposit, corporate securities, securitised instruments and short-term cash equivalents. Debt securities introduce duration and credit risk; the value of longer-duration bonds may fall when yields rise. A sovereign rating does not eliminate interest-rate volatility. Lower-rated or less liquid credit holdings, if any, introduce separate spread and liquidity risks. Derivatives can also create margin and basis risks. REIT, InvIT and other permitted assets The AMC factsheet disclosed REIT/InvIT positions and other permitted assets in its portfolio at the stated historical date. Such holdings do not behave exactly like large-cap equities or short-term bank deposits. Real-estate cash flows, infrastructure regulations, interest rates and valuation changes can influence their prices. Portfolio composition is actively managed and must not be treated as fixed until October 2026. What the Available AMC Figures Do—and Do Not—Show The most detailed independently retrievable AMC PDF used for numeric values in this article is the March 31, 2026 scheme factsheet. Its AUM, TER, NAV and portfolio data are therefore identified by that exact date. The AMC’s online digital factsheet landing page carries a July 31, 2026 date, but the rendered page did not expose a complete independently verifiable October TER, portfolio and riskometer table. It would be inaccurate to label the March values “current October 2026.” Investors and publishers should use the latest downloadable AMC portfolio, TER disclosure and KIM for a live transaction. The SID’s historical performance table is dated September 30, 2025 and reports Regular Plan returns of 4.62% for one year, 13.25% annualised for three years and 14.59% annualised for five years; Direct Plan figures were 5.22%, 13.94% and 15.30% respectively for those periods. These figures are included only to illustrate how plan expenses and observation dates affect returns. They are not 2026 current returns, are not annualised for the one-year comparison in a predictive sense and must not be extrapolated. No future-return estimate is made. The AMC’s March 2026 disclosure reported 1.45% per annum for the Regular Plan and 0.88% for the Direct Plan. The difference was 0.57 percentage point at that date, not a guarantee of a constant future gap. TER can change subject to regulatory caps and disclosure requirements; investors should use the current daily TER rather than assuming the March rate remains operative. Direct and Regular Plans hold the same underlying scheme portfolio but have different expense structures. The choice of plan affects the investor’s NAV path and service/distribution arrangement; it does not transform a high-risk market asset into a capital-guaranteed deposit. The published expense ratio is not the same as a brokerage bill separately charged to the investor. Transaction charges, stamp duty and taxes must be considered according to their separate rules. Exit Load, SIP and Redemption Mechanics The AMFI-hosted SID provides that up to 30% of units purchased or switched in may be redeemed or switched out within one year without exit load. For units above that free limit redeemed or switched out within one year, the stated load is 1% of applicable NAV. After one year, the stated load is nil. The limit is tied to the units and applicable lot conditions; it should not be interpreted as permission to redeem 30% of the entire portfolio without considering purchase dates and scheme rules. Each SIP instalment is a separate allotment for holding-period, exit-load and capital-gains calculations. A switch to another scheme generally triggers a redemption from the source scheme and can create a taxable event even if the investor receives no cash in a bank account. Applicable NAV is subject to SEBI cut-off and funds-realisation rules. Investors should preserve allotment statements, NAV, redemption confirmations, capital-gain reports and bank records. Riskometer and Principal Risks The SID displays a scheme Riskometer and benchmark Riskometer, but expressly states that its image reflects the September 30, 2025 portfolio and directs readers to the latest monthly portfolio for an updated rating. Accordingly, this article does not assert an unverified October 2026 Riskometer level. Riskometer is a regulated, portfolio-based disclosure that can change with asset mix and volatility; it is not a capital-protection promise or a substitute for reading the SID. Material risks include equity drawdowns, derivatives basis and counterparty exposure, debt duration, issuer credit quality, liquidity, valuation-model failure, REIT/InvIT concentration and operational or redemption delays under exceptional circumstances. Dynamic allocation may cushion some market environments but may also underperform rising equity markets or respond slowly to rapid reversals. These are risk characteristics, not a forecast of the scheme’s direction. Taxation for Tax Year 2026-27 Equity-oriented status is a legal test The AMC’s historical SID tax annexure described the scheme as an equity-oriented mutual fund under the then applicable Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, and the current tax result depends on the statutory definition and the scheme satisfying the relevant equity-oriented fund conditions for the applicable period. Derivatives, gross equity and hedged equity should not be casually equated for every statutory test. The fund’s actual qualification and transaction date must be checked before applying the special rates. Short-term and long-term gains For qualifying equity-oriented mutual-fund units where the statutory conditions including STT are met, short-term capital gains are generally taxed at 20% plus applicable surcharge and cess. Qualifying long-term capital gains are generally taxed at 12.5% on the aggregate amount exceeding ₹1,25,000 for the tax year, subject to the prescribed holding period and conditions. The ₹1.25 lakh amount is an aggregate annual threshold across qualifying gains, not a separate exemption for each mutual fund or each redemption. Gains that do not meet the special provisions must be analysed under the applicable general rules. See also TaxGuru: TaxGuru capital gains for tax year 2026-27. Losses, IDCW and NRI taxation A short-term capital loss may generally be adjusted against eligible short- or long-term capital gains, while a long-term capital loss can generally be adjusted only against long-term capital gains, subject to statutory restrictions. Eligible unabsorbed capital losses may be carried forward if return-filing conditions are satisfied. IDCW distributions are generally taxable in the hands of investors under the applicable provisions; an IDCW payout is not a guaranteed income or tax-free dividend. Withholding on IDCW or redemptions is separate from final tax liability. For NRIs, FEMA account eligibility, repatriation route, FATCA/CRS information, treaty claims and non-resident TDS rules require separate analysis. A foreign investor’s redemption withholding may be higher than the final tax after treaty or domestic-law computation. A resident’s TDS assumptions should not be copied to an NRI folio. Tax residency certificates and other treaty documentation may be necessary for relief.
