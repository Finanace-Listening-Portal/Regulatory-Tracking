Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 24, 2026 at 10:06 PM IST var news_msid = 134466820; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India’s final tally of FCNR(B) mobilisation stands at USD 133 billion between June 8 and August 31, Deputy Governor Rohit Jain expects banks to deploy the liquidity over the coming months, while leaving individual lenders to decide how they use the funds based on their credit pipeline and asset-liability position.“So over the next few months, I think the banks will be able to deploy this liquidity. And we have had a meeting with all the banks discussed their position. And we shouldn't have any problem,” Jain said on the sidelines of the SBI Banking & Economic Conclave in Mumbai on Thursday. Advt Asked whether the RBI had suggested any framework for deployment of the FCNR(B) liquidity, Jain said banks would take their own decisions based on their pipeline and liquidity outlook.“No banks will take their own decisions on the credit and the deployment, depending on their pipeline, the proposals that they have, and, you know, the outlook that they have for their liquidity position,” he said.“So their asset liability position will determine how they deploy that liquidity. It's basically the bank's call on how they use this,” Jain added.Jain said the RBI was not asking banks to be watchful of any particular sector while deploying the funds, pointing to broad-based credit demand.“In general if you see the credit demand has been quite broad based, all the sectors and segments have been growing reasonably well. So, we do not find any concern on any particular sector of front, which we need to own the banks,” he said.He added that banks have remained cautious on credit and underwriting standards.DG Jain also said India's forex reserves stood at USD 781 billion as of September 11, the highest level the country has recorded, making India the fifth-highest holder of forex reserves globally.He said the RBI's approach to managing forex reserves is guided by three considerations, “Safety, liquidity and return”, in that order. Since the reserves are borrowed funds, return should not be the sole consideration in their deployment, he said. Advt The objective, Jain said, is to ensure that India's forex reserves provide “very strong external resilience” and give foreign investors and counterparties confidence that the country has sufficient forex reserves to meet its needs.
