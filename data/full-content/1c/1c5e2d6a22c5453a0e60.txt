The NBFC loan book expanded by about Rs 7.8 lakh crore over the 12 months, while credit grew about 1% month-on-month in July.Retail credit grew 21.4% year-on-year to around Rs 26.1 lakh crore, raising its share of the overall NBFC book to 43.5% from 41.2% a year earlier, while industry credit grew only 7.4% to Rs 22.1 lakh crore. The shift reflects stronger growth in consumer finance, gold loans and other asset-backed retail products, while large-ticket industrial and infrastructure lending faced greater competition from banks, according to CareEdge Ratings. Advt Gold loans recorded the fastest growth among major NBFC lending segments, rising 68.5% year-on-year to Rs 3.5 lakh crore. Higher gold prices increased collateral values and supported demand for quick, low-documentation credit, while banks also increased their participation in gold-loan financing.Overall riseConsumer durables financing grew 51.5%, sharply accelerating from 18.8% a year earlier, while vehicle finance and housing credit grew 15.1% and 11.9%, respectively. Other retail loans increased 20.4%. Housing remained the largest retail category, although its share of the overall NBFC book declined to 14.2% from 14.6%.NBFC infrastructure credit grew 6.2% to Rs 20.2 lakh crore, slower than the 10.2% growth in bank infrastructure credit to Rs 15.1 lakh crore. Bank power-sector credit grew 21.6%, contributing to stronger bank infrastructure lending as banks increased their participation in large, long-tenor projects.Services-sector NBFC credit grew 15.2% to Rs 7.7 lakh crore, compared with 24.5% a year earlier. Commercial real estate credit grew 22.3%, although outstanding CRE credit fell 7.1% month-on-month in July. Trade credit and lending to transport operators grew 14.7% and 10.7%, respectively.Agriculture credit grew 18% year-on-year, sharply up from 5.4% a year earlier, although it accounted for only 1.3% of the NBFC loan book. The other-loans category grew 19.7%, reversing a 20.5% decline a year earlier. Advt Retail and asset-backed lending are expected to remain important growth drivers for NBFCs, but the sector faces risks from underwriting standards, funding conditions and asset quality. CareEdge has also flagged the potential impact of El Niño on rabi output and rural cash flows, while elevated crude prices and a prolonged West Asia crisis could contribute to a steeper interest-rate trajectory.
