Articles 2 min read Credit offtake stays ahead of banks' deposit growth In the second quarter of FY27, advances at several banks grew significantly faster than deposit growth. Smaller banks, such as Tamilnad Mercantile Bank and Karnataka Bank, recorded impressive credit expansions. Larger public sector banks like Bank of India and Canara Bank also saw substantial growth in advances. However, deposit growth varied, with some banks struggling to keep pace with advances. Tamilnad Mercantile Bank led the pack with 29.4% growth in advances, followed by Karnataka Bank at 24.9%, UCO Bank at 24.8% and J&K Bank at 23.7%. Advt Among larger public sector banks, Bank of India reported the fastest credit growth at 20.4%, followed by Canara Bank at 19.3%, Punjab & Sind Bank at 19.4%, Union Bank of India at 18.5% and Bank of Baroda at 18.3%. Punjab National Bank's advances grew 14.8%, while Indian Bank reported 16.6% growth.Deposit growth, however, was more uneven. Bank of India's deposits rose 21.6%, ahead of its 20.4% credit growth, while Bank of Baroda's deposits grew 16.7%, broadly keeping pace with its 18.3% advance growth. Canara Bank's deposits rose 13.1% against 19.3% credit growth, while Union Bank's deposits grew 6.9% against 18.5% loan growth. At PNB, deposits grew 9.9%, compared with 14.8% growth in advances.The divergence was more pronounced among some mid-sized lenders. UCO Bank's advances grew 24.8%, compared with 14.2% deposit growth, while J&K Bank reported 23.7% credit growth against 16.3% deposit growth. Karnataka Bank's advances rose 24.9%, more than twice the 12% growth in deposits.Some lenders, however, managed to expand deposits faster than advances. Tamilnad Mercantile Bank's deposits grew 25.5%, compared with 29.4% growth in advances, while Dhanlaxmi Bank reported deposit growth of 20.2% against 22.3% credit growth. At South Indian Bank, both advances and deposits grew 18.7%.The numbers point to continued pressure on banks to mobilise deposits even as credit demand remains firm.
