Summary : ICICI Prudential Value Fund is the current name of ICICI Prudential Value Discovery Fund. The AMC changed the name with effect from 16 June 2025, so the rename did not create a separate new scheme or erase the earlier track record. It is an open-ended equity scheme following a value-investment strategy. The scheme searches for companies whose market price appears below the manager’s assessment of intrinsic value, but that assessment can remain unrecognised for long periods or prove wrong. The official factsheet identifies the NIFTY 500 Total Return Index as benchmark and shows an inception date of 16 August 2004. Current scheme information should be read with the SID, KIM, addenda, portfolio and daily expense disclosures. The published exit-load structure is one per cent of applicable NAV for redemption or switch-out within twelve months of allotment and nil thereafter. Direct and Regular Plans hold the same portfolio but have different expense structures; Growth and IDCW options have different cash-flow and tax consequences. This article explains the rename, mandate, portfolio-review method, costs, liquidity, riskometer and Indian equity-fund tax without ranking the scheme or predicting returns. A low price-to-earnings or price-to-book ratio alone is not proof of value. Cyclical profits can make a company appear cheap near a peak, while governance, leverage or business disruption can make a discount persistent. The scheme manager may also hold cash, large companies or positions that differ from a reader’s narrow idea of a value portfolio. Mandate Benchmark and Portfolio Review The scheme’s investment objective is to seek long-term capital appreciation through a diversified portfolio of value-style equity and equity-related instruments. An objective is not a guarantee. The NIFTY 500 TRI gives a broad reference set, but the scheme can differ materially by sector, market capitalisation, cash level and security weights. Tracking difference is therefore expected and is not automatically evidence of an error. The official digital factsheet available during this review presented the scheme as ICICI Prudential Value Fund and displayed a dated portfolio snapshot. Portfolio data should always be tied to its disclosure date. Security weights can change after month-end through market movement or transactions. A useful review focuses on sector concentration, top holdings, cash, market-cap mix, turnover and whether the disclosed portfolio still reflects a genuine value style rather than judging the scheme from one month’s return. Direct and Regular Plans Growth and IDCW Options Direct and Regular Plans invest in the same portfolio. The difference is distribution expense, so the Direct Plan normally has a lower expense ratio and a different NAV. Direct is not automatically appropriate for every investor because suitability depends on the ability to select, monitor and transact without distributor assistance. Regular Plan commission is reflected in expenses rather than billed separately to the investor. Growth leaves distributable surplus in the scheme NAV. IDCW permits distributions when declared from distributable surplus; the amount and timing are not guaranteed, and NAV falls to the extent of distribution and applicable levies. IDCW is not interest. The option selected can affect cash flow and tax reporting, so the folio confirmation should be checked after every transaction. Expense Ratio Exit Load and Transaction Cut Offs The total expense ratio is deducted from scheme assets before NAV is reported and can change within regulatory limits. A historical factsheet figure is not a permanent tariff. Investors should check the AMC’s current daily TER disclosure for the relevant plan on the transaction date. Even a modest annual percentage difference compounds over a long holding period, but TER is only one element of suitability and should not be used as a return forecast. The disclosed exit load is one per cent for redemption or switch-out within twelve months from allotment and nil after twelve months. The rule applies to the units being redeemed, commonly on a first-in-first-out basis within a folio. Exit load is not a statutory lock-in: units may generally be redeemed earlier, but the load reduces proceeds. A switch from Regular to Direct or Growth to IDCW is normally treated as a redemption and fresh purchase, so exit load and tax can apply. NAV Date Depends on Valid Application and Funds The applicable NAV depends on the transaction type, cut-off, receipt of a valid request and realisation of funds under current SEBI and AMC rules. A timestamp alone may not secure the desired NAV if funds are not available for utilisation. Online platform status should be reconciled with the AMC or registrar confirmation, especially near holidays or market cut-offs. Indian Tax Treatment for Equity Oriented Fund Units When the scheme satisfies the statutory equity-oriented-fund conditions, a transfer of units held for not more than twelve months is generally short term. For transfers on or after 23 July 2024, qualifying short-term capital gains under section 111A are generally taxed at twenty per cent, while qualifying long-term gains under section 112A are generally taxed at twelve and one-half per cent on aggregate gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh in the financial year. Surcharge, cess, residence, status and transaction conditions can change the final liability. IDCW is generally taxable in the investor’s hands at the applicable rate, with TDS provisions potentially applying when statutory conditions and thresholds are met. Capital-gain computation requires purchase-wise cost, date, units, exit load and consideration. A switch is taxable even when no cash reaches the bank.
