Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Bhavesh Chandulal Dasadiya Vs DCIT (ITAT Rajkot) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Rajkot Bench, examined an appeal for AY 2020-21 against an addition of ₹20 lakh under Section 69A of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The assessee, an individual deriving remuneration and interest from partnership firms, had filed his return declaring a total income of ₹28,85,890. Following a search conducted on the R.K. Group on 24 August 2021, certain digital Miracle files were recovered from the possession of the group’s accountant. The Assessing Officer inferred that the ledger entries had been backdated by ten years and the amounts manipulated by eliminating two zeros. Based on these assumptions, the AO alleged that the assessee had paid ₹20 lakh as on-money for purchasing office Nos. 1001 and 1002 in R.K. World Tower, Rajkot, and accordingly made the addition, which was subsequently upheld by the CIT(A). The assessee categorically denied purchasing the properties or making any on-money payment. He furnished registered sale deeds establishing that the properties were purchased by two other individuals. The assessee contended that the third-party digital ledger neither bore his signature nor contained his approval. He further disputed the alleged bank entries, manipulation of dates and figures, and the reliance placed on untested third-party statements. Despite specifically requesting an opportunity for cross-examination, the same was not granted. Upon examining the property documents, the Tribunal found that the assessee had never purchased the properties and that the Revenue had failed to establish any nexus between the assessee and the alleged cash payments. The ITAT held that an addition cannot be sustained merely on the basis of an unverified digital ledger recovered from a third party without independent corroborative evidence establishing actual payment or investment by the assessee. Relying upon the Supreme Court’s decision in ITO Vs Daulat Ram Rawatmull (1973) 87 ITR 349 (SC), the Tribunal reiterated that suspicion, however strong, cannot substitute for evidence. Neither the agreement to sale nor the registered conveyance deeds identified the assessee as the purchaser. The Tribunal also did not accept the Revenue’s request for an estimated addition. Accordingly, in the absence of cogent evidence establishing ownership or payment of on-money, the ITAT directed deletion of the entire ₹20 lakh addition under Section 69A and allowed the assessee’s appeal. The grounds challenging reopening of the assessment were not separately adjudicated. Cases Discussed ITO Vs Daulat Ram Rawatmull (Supreme Court); (1973) 87 ITR 349 (SC). – Relied upon by the Tribunal for the requirement of evidence rather than suspicion. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT RAJKOT Captioned appeal filed by the assessee, pertaining to Assessment Year 2020-21, is directed against the order under section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as “the Act”) by Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC) Delhi, dated 08.09.2025, which in turn arises out of an assessment order passed by the Assessing Officer u/s 147 of the Act, on 23.03.2025. 2. The grounds of appeal raised by the assessee are as follows: “1. The assessment order u/s. 147 of the Act is bad in law. 2. The reopening of the assessment is bad in law. ITA No. 839/Rjt/2025 Bhavesh Chandulal Dasadiya 3. The learned Assessing Officer has erred in law as well as on facts in making an addition of Rs.20,00,000/- u/s 69A of the Act on the ground of alleged unexplained money.” 3. Brief facts of the case are that the present appeal arises from the assessment order u/s. 147 dated 23.03.2025. The assessee is an individual who primarily derived remuneration and interest from his partnership firms, namely, M/s Icon Export and m/s Ncraze Ceramic LLP during the year under appeal. The assessee filed his income tax return on 14.12.2020, declaring a total income of Rs. 28,85,890/-. There was a search action carried out on various premises of R.K. Group on 24.08.2021. As a part of the same, the residential premises of Shri Girish Vanjani were also covered, who is accountant of the R.K. Group. From his possession, certain Miracle files were discovered on which he stated that the data was maintained as per the instructions of Shri Sarvanand Sadhuram Sonwani who is the key person of the R.K. Group and the statement of Shri Girish Vanjani and Shri Sarvanand Sadhuram Sonwani were recorded. In the seized miracles files, ledgers named ‘WT 1001 1002’, ‘WT 1001 Bhaveshbhai Morbi’, and ‘WT 1002’ were found for the period of 01.04.2009 to 31.03.2099. The data in this ledger was inferred to have been backdated by 10 years, with figures manipulated by eliminating two zeros. Based on this, it was inferred that an “on-money” amounting to Rs.20,00,000/- was paid during the year under assessment for the purchase of commercial property bearing office nos. 1001 and 1002 of the project ‘R.K. World Tower’ in Rajkot, developed by M/s Vivan Infraspace LLP. Consequently, the ld. AO proposed the addition of Rs.20,00,000/- u/s. 69A of the Income Tax Act. 4. In response to the proposed addition, the assessee clearly denied the allegation of “on-money” payment. The assessee explained that he had not purchased any property and hence, the question of on-money does not arise. Since the property was ITA No. 839/Rjt/2025 Bhavesh Chandulal Dasadiya not purchased by the assessee, therefore no addition should be made in the hands of the assessee. The commercial property bearing office nos. 1001 and 1002 of the project ‘R.K. World Tower’ in Rajkot, developed by M/s Vivan Infraspace LLP were purchased by Mr. Nirav Dilipbhai Bhatt and Mr. Chetan Jayantilal Patel, respectively. The registered sale deed is always overwhelming evidence compared to loose ledgers unilaterally created by one party. The Id. AO made an addition on the basis of seized material, stating that there were transactions indicating the purchase of immovable property by the assessee involving both cash and bank entries. Further, the Id. AO also stated that such bank entries are also appearing on the regular book/respective conveyance deed. However, upon perusal of the seized material relied upon during the assessment, it was noted that the seized ledgers only contained cash transactions, and there was no mention or indication of any bank-related entries. Further, the seized ledger has neither been found in the assessee’s possession nor does it contain any signatory approval of the assessee. The seized ledger was found in the possession of a third party and does not contain the assessee’s name. Moreover, the data found in digital form is always amenable to changes at the discretion of the possessor. The data related to dates and figures in the ledger are mentioned in an ambiguous manner, and assumptions are made to correlate them with the dates of the assessment year under appeal. The Ld. A.O. failed to appreciate that the presumption regarding payment of “On- Money” has been drawn merely on the basis of some uncorroborated digital ledger found from the premises of and untested deposition made by the third-party. There was no any other corroborating real adverse evidence found which could establish that the assessee actually made a payment of “on-money” in cash for the purchase of commercial property. The Id. A.O. failed to appreciate that it is absolutely improper to accept the material found from the premises of third party as a gospel truth without testing its correctness. Though the assessee opposed the payment of any amount in cash, the A.O. did not ITA No. 839/Rjt/2025 Bhavesh Chandulal Dasadiya bother to confirm this fact or allow the assessee an opportunity of cross-examination in this connection, although specifically requested, and failed to bring on record any evidence in support of the contention of the payment in cash. 5. However, the Id. AO, without appreciating the submission of the assessee in true perspective, made an addition of Rs.20,00,000/- u/s. 69A of the Act. 6. Being aggrieved by the said order of the Assessing Officer, the assessee filed an appeal before the Ld. CIT(A), but remained unsuccessful. Therefore, assessee is left with no other alternative but to knock on the doors of the Tribunal with this appeal praying for justice. 7. I have heard, both the parties and carefully gone through the submission put forth on behalf of the assessee along with the documents furnished and the case laws relied upon, and perused the fact of the case including the findings of the Ld. CIT(A) and other materials brought on record. Learned Counsel for the assessee submitted that during the assessment proceedings, the assessee submitted all the possible documents and evidences to prove his claim. The Learned Counsel for the assessee, also argued that the assessee clearly denied the allegation of “on-money” payment. The assessee explained that he had not purchased any property and hence, the question of on-money does not arise. Since the property was not purchased by the assessee, therefore no addition should be made in the hands of the assessee. 8. On the other hand, the Ld. DR for the Revenue has stated that considering the smallness of the amount and to protect the interest of the revenue an estimated addition may be made in the hands of the assessee. 9. I have examined the property documents and noted that assessee never purchased the property, therefore no addition should be made in the hands of the ITA No. 839/Rjt/2025 Bhavesh Chandulal Dasadiya assessee. I note that the addition under consideration has been made primarily on the basis of a digital ledger allegedly seized from the premises of a third party. The assessee has categorically denied having purchased the commercial property bearing office Nos. 1001 and 1002 in the project R. K. World Tower, Rajkot. It is the contention of the assessee that the registered sale deeds identify Mr. Nirav Dilipbhai Bhatt and Mr. Chetan Jayantilal Patel as the respective purchasers of the aforesaid properties and that the assessee is neither a party to the conveyance deeds nor has made any payment towards the purchase of the said properties. I also find that the alleged seized ledger was not found in the possession of the assessee and does not bear his signature or approval. Further, the material relied upon by the Assessing Officer does not, by itself, establish that the assessee was the purchaser of the properties or that he had actually made the alleged cash payment ( on-money). The Assessing Officer has also referred to bank-related entries, whereas, upon examination of the seized material, the assessee contends that no such bank entries are appearing therein. The alleged correlation between the dates and figures appearing in the seized ledger and the transactions relating to the properties is also disputed by the assessee. In the absence of any independent corroborative evidence establishing the identity of the assessee as the purchaser or demonstrating actual payment of cash by the assessee, the addition cannot be sustained merely on the basis of an unverified digital ledger found from the premises of a third party. 10. I note that Ld. Counsel for the assessee has contended that the subject property was never purchased or acquired by assessee and, therefore, no addition on account of alleged unexplained investment or payment of “on-money” could be made in his hands. I find that the Revenue has failed to bring any cogent material on record establishing that the assessee was a party to the transaction relating to the subject property or that any amount, either by way of recorded consideration or alleged on-ITA No. 839/Rjt/2025 Bhavesh Chandulal Dasadiya money, was actually paid by the assessee. Neither the agreement to sale nor the registered conveyance deed identifies the assessee as the purchaser of the property. Further, no corroborative evidence has been brought on record to establish that the assessee had made any investment in the subject property or had paid any amount over and above the recorded consideration. In the absence of any evidence establishing the nexus between the assessee and the alleged investment, the addition made merely on the basis of suspicion or presumption cannot be sustained. The principle laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in ITO v. Daulat Ram Rawatmull (1973) 87 ITR 349 (SC), that suspicion, however strong, cannot take the place of evidence, squarely applies to the facts of the present case. Accordingly, in the absence of evidence establishing that the assessee had purchased the subject property or had made the alleged unexplained investment therein, the addition made in the hands of the assessee is not sustainable and is directed to be deleted. Accordingly, the addition is deleted. 11. In the result, appeal of the assessee is allowed. Order is pronounced in the open Court on 20/11/2026.
