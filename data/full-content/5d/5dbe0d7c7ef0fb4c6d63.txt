(File Photo)follow usfollow usThe government has defended the bidding process for IDBI Bank's proposed privatisation, clarifying that the reserve price remained unchanged during the second round of bidding. It also said the revised procedure resulted in a Rs 3,917 crore increase in the discovered bid price. In a statement, the Finance Ministry rejected suggestions that the reserve price was either lowered or raised to facilitate the transaction. It maintained that the initial bidding exercise had ceased to remain operational after the first set of offers failed to secure approval.“The reserve price was neither decreased nor increased, and the first round itself became functus officio after the initial bids were not approved,” the ministry said.The clarification comes amid questions about the government's proposed strategic restructuring of IDBI Bank, particularly the valuation process and the transaction terms.The Finance Ministry explained that the initial bidding exercise took place in 2025-26, following the Department of Investment and Public Asset Management's (DIPAM) indicative guidelines. These guidelines allow flexibility in handling complex strategic transactions.According to the ministry, the bidding exercise did not involve a fixed benchmark price. Instead, interested parties were required to independently evaluate the bank and submit confidential offers.To ensure that the offers were assessed appropriately, independent agencies were engaged to undertake valuations. These assessments subsequently served as reference points for determining whether the bids were reasonable.After the initial offers failed to receive approval, the first bidding round was considered closed. The Core Group of Secretaries on Disinvestment (CGD) subsequently finalised the framework for the second round, also referred to as the best-offer process.The CGD had been authorised by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) to resolve procedural matters and introduce necessary changes to ensure the smooth execution of strategic transactions.How Was IDBI Bank's Valuation Calculated?The ministry also provided details of the valuation exercise undertaken before fresh offers were invited. It said the CGD examined the complete valuation methodology in April 2026.Two independent advisers were appointed to assess IDBI Bank's value through different approaches.For the business valuation, the market multiple method was adopted, with listed public sector banks considered as comparable entities. The asset valuation, meanwhile, took into account several parameters, including disputed tax demands.The government said the independent valuation exercises were intended to provide an objective basis for evaluating the offers received from prospective buyers.Govt Defends Rs 627 Crore Tax Indemnity ArrangementAddressing concerns over the tax-related indemnity provision, the Finance Ministry said the potential liability involved was limited to Rs 627 crore.It pointed out that this amount was significantly lower than the Rs 3,917 crore increase recorded in the discovered bid price following the revised bidding process.The ministry characterised the likelihood of the potential tax liability materialising as low. It also maintained that the arrangement would not result in an additional financial burden on the government under normal circumstances.This is because any reimbursement would become applicable only if the additional tax demand was actually paid, making the provision revenue-neutral for the government, according to the release.IDBI Bank Privatisation: What Is The Latest Deal Structure?The government's clarification comes as the proposed sale of IDBI Bank continues to attract attention, with bank employees expressing reservations about the transaction.Their concerns include the continuation of reservation provisions, protection of existing service conditions and safeguards relating to pension benefits.On October 14, the Union Cabinet approved the proposed sale of a 60.72 per cent stake in IDBI Bank for Rs 52,878 crore to a subsidiary of Canada's Fairfax Financial Holdings.Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has described the transaction as a "strategic restructuring" rather than strategic disinvestment.Under the proposed ownership structure, the government and the Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) will continue to hold stakes of 15 per cent and 19 per cent, respectively, after the transaction.Latest News entertainment newsAadarsha Kutumbam House No: 47 – AK47 X Review: Venkatesh Film Hailed As 'Refreshing' Family Entertainer, BUT...india'Hijacked' By Ministry?
