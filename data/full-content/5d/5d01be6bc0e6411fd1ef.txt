Articles 3 min read UPI must move beyond India, connect more countries like Singapore: PM Modi at GFF 2026 With UPI completing a decade, Prime Minister Narendra Modi has set out its next ambition: linking India’s payment system with more countries, reducing transaction costs for Indians overseas and expanding fintech beyond its payments-heavy model. Addressing the Global FinTech Fest 2026, Modi said UPI’s success should not become the final destination, calling for deeper international payment integration similar to India’s linkage with Singapore. Anushka Sengupta & Amol Dethe ETBFSI Updated On Sep 8, 2026 at 07:49 PM IST var news_msid = 133928044; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook PM Modi inaugurates GFF 2026With UPI completing 10 years last month and processing more than 2,400 crore transactions in August alone, Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Tuesday said India must now push the next phase of its digital payments revolution by connecting its payment system with more countries around the world.Addressing the Global FinTech Fest 2026, Modi said UPI's rapid adoption has transformed India's financial landscape, but the country should not view its success in domestic payments as the final destination.“Today, our UPI has reached many countries. But this is only the first step. Now connecting with the payment systems of those countries should be our next goal,” Modi said. Advt India has already established such a linkage with Singapore, enabling faster and more seamless cross-border payments. Modi said similar connections should now be developed across other markets, particularly countries with large Indian populations or strong trade links with India.“Wherever Indians live in large numbers, wherever India trades on a large scale, and whichever country is ready to work with us, we will have to move forward to connect with them,” he said.UPI could cut costs for Indians sending money homeThe Prime Minister said deeper international integration of UPI could also help address one of the major challenges faced by Indians working overseas—the cost of sending money back home.India receives one of the largest inflows of remittances globally, but a portion of this money is lost to transaction charges, Modi said.“Most Indians working abroad send money back to their families. A part of that money is spent on transaction costs. UPI can help save Indians from these costs and ensure money reaches their families faster,” Modi said.The comments signal a stronger policy push towards taking India's digital public infrastructure and homegrown payments architecture beyond domestic borders.Modi said UPI's journey over the past decade demonstrates how quickly an ambitious digital vision can become part of everyday life. Advt UPI completed 10 years on August 25. “Ten years ago, when I used to say that your bank will be at your fingertips, even well-informed people thought it was impossible,” Modi said.Today, those who had remained outside the formal banking system for generations have become major drivers of India's fintech growth, he added.Modi highlighted that UPI processed more than 2,400 crore transactions in August, translating into over 50 lakh transactions every 10 minutes.Success in payments cannot be the final goalWhile UPI has made India one of the world's largest digital payments markets, Modi said the fintech sector must now look beyond payments and expand its role across the broader financial ecosystem. Advt “Today, a large part of the fintech market is only payments. But now we have to take it to the next level—and we have to move fast,” he said.The Prime Minister called for a greater share of fintech activity in credit, insurance, savings, investments and pensions.“Along with payments, the share of credit transactions should increase. The share of insurance should increase. The share of savings, investments and pensions should also increase,” Modi said.UPI, he said, has demonstrated how technology can reach citizens and businesses where traditional financial infrastructure could not.“The unique feature of fintech should be to reach where the traditional model could not reach—where banks and bank branches could not reach. UPI has brought the banking system to that place and to that person,” Modi said. Advt The Prime Minister's remarks underline a broader ambition for India's fintech ecosystem, leveraging the success of UPI not only to build global payment linkages but also to use digital rails to deepen access to credit, insurance, investments and other financial services.
