The initiative, managed by the General Insurance Council, allows hospitals to apply to multiple general and standalone health insurers through a single digital platform instead of following separate empanelment procedures with individual insurers.Niva Bupa management said on November 4 that more than 5,000 hospitals had signed MOUs, with many thousands more in the queue or under process. Advt The initiative is part of a broader industry effort to standardise hospital interactions, treatment protocols and claims processes. Eighteen standard treatment workflows are set to go live, in addition to admission criteria already introduced for eight infectious diseases and position statements covering robotic and other modern treatments. The objective is to bring greater clarity to treatment decisions and address costs arising from variations in clinical pathways, according to a Nomura report on Niva Bupa.The General Insurance Council's platform provides a single-window application process for hospitals seeking empanelment with more than 30 participating insurers. The process is free, and participation is voluntary. The initiative is particularly intended to bring small and medium-sized hospitals, including those in peripheral and suburban areas, into insurance networks.The processThe initiative could reduce the administrative burden on hospitals that otherwise have to deal with multiple insurers through separate onboarding procedures. A common process could also improve the availability of cashless treatment options, particularly in smaller cities and peripheral areas where hospital networks may be less extensive. However, signing an MOU does not necessarily mean that a hospital is operational across all insurer networks or that cashless treatment is available under every policy.Insurers are also working towards standardising treatment pathways because the procedures followed, admission decisions and duration of hospital stays can materially influence claims costs. The industry has introduced admission criteria for eight infectious diseases, while the additional standard treatment workflows are expected to provide greater clarity on appropriate treatment. Management has said a significant part of claims costs is determined by the treatment pathway, making clinical standardisation an important part of efforts to manage healthcare expenses. Advt The push comes amid rising medical costs. Industry estimates put medical inflation at around 12-14% annually, increasing pressure on insurers to manage claims while keeping health insurance affordable. Standard treatment protocols and more consistent processes could help reduce ambiguity and disputes, although the impact on claims costs will depend on adoption by hospitals and insurers.The initiative has also faced concerns over the relationship between hospitals and insurers. Hospital representatives have previously objected to the possibility of collective tariff-setting, arguing that common arrangements could weaken individual hospitals' ability to negotiate reimbursement rates. The General Insurance Council is working on a dispute-resolution forum to address disagreements between hospitals and insurers over payments, empanelment, delays and clinical decisions. Advt The success of common empanelment will depend on more than the number of MOUs signed. The extent to which hospitals become active across insurer networks, the geographical spread of the network and the resolution of payment and clinical disputes will determine whether the initiative improves cashless access and creates a more efficient health insurance ecosystem.
