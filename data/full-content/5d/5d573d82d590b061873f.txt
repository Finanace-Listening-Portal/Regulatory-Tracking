Summary: Ind AS 32 requires incremental costs directly attributable to an equity transaction to be deducted from equity, net of related income-tax benefit. Costs that would have been incurred anyway are expenses, while costs of an abandoned equity transaction are recognised in profit or loss. This guide applies those principles to IPO and private-placement projects, concurrent listings, mixed debt-and-equity offerings and compound instruments. It provides an allocation method, a worked fact pattern and a closing evidence file that distinguishes direct transaction costs from corporate overhead, listing-status costs and post-issue expenditure. Key Takeaways Show all 21 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); The accounting answer follows the nature and outcome of the transaction Ind AS 32 treats the transaction costs of an equity transaction as a deduction from equity, net of any related income-tax benefit, but only to the extent they are incremental and directly attributable to the equity transaction. The avoidability test is central: would the cost have been incurred if the equity issue had not taken place? A fee does not qualify merely because it appears in the project budget or was paid during an IPO process. The standard reaches a different result when the equity transaction is abandoned. Costs of an abandoned equity transaction are recognised as an expense. This creates a continuing reassessment requirement. An amount initially accumulated as a transaction cost cannot stay parked in an asset or equity suspense account after management concludes that the transaction will not proceed. In contrast, a broad corporate restructuring review, readiness assessment, finance-team training or recurring audit work may benefit the company independently of the issue. The engagement letter, scope, milestone and invoice narrative should establish which service was performed and whether the fee would have arisen without the proposed transaction. A single adviser invoice can contain both qualifying and non-qualifying work. Management should not classify the whole invoice by its dominant label. It should obtain a work breakdown or use a supportable allocation based on hours, deliverables or another rational driver. Allocation should be documented contemporaneously because a percentage selected after the reporting result is known can look outcome-driven. Listing status and equity issuance are not always the same transaction A company may incur costs to issue new shares and costs to obtain or maintain a stock-exchange listing. If the listing work provides access or status beyond the equity proceeds, it may not be a transaction cost of the equity issuance. Concurrent secondary sales by existing shareholders also require separation: costs attributable to the selling shareholders are not automatically a reduction of the company’s equity. Contracts should state who receives the service and who bears the obligation. Allocate costs when funding contains debt, equity or more than one offering Compound financial instruments require proportional allocation Ind AS 32 requires the issuer of a compound instrument to recognise the liability and equity components separately. Transaction costs relating to the issue are allocated to those components in proportion to the allocation of proceeds. The portion assigned to the liability enters its initial measurement and affects the effective interest calculation under Ind AS 109; the portion assigned to equity is deducted from equity. Assigning every rupee to equity because the instrument is convertible would bypass the liability component. Concurrent transactions need a rational and repeatable basis Where one mandate covers a share issue and a stock-exchange listing, or a debt facility and equity placement, Ind AS 32 calls for allocation on a basis that is rational and consistent with similar transactions. Direct tracing should be used first. Residual common costs may be allocated using adviser hours, relative independently priced services, transaction values or another driver that faithfully represents consumption. The policy and why it was selected should be retained for future offerings. Determining whether a tax benefit exists and when it is recognised requires Ind AS 12 and the tax law applicable to the expenditure. Accounting teams should not automatically multiply every issue cost by the headline tax rate. Deductibility, timing, jurisdiction, probability of recovery and the entity’s tax position must be assessed. Where the underlying transaction cost is recognised directly in equity and the related tax effect qualifies for recognition, the destination principle generally places that tax effect consistently with the underlying item. Costs expensed because an offering is abandoned may have a different current or deferred tax treatment. The tax memorandum should connect each cost category to its accounting presentation and statutory tax basis rather than calculate one blended tax adjustment for the entire project. Abandonment, postponement and uncertainty require distinct judgments A delay is not automatically an abandonment A market-window delay may leave a transaction active if the board mandate, advisers, regulatory work and funding plan continue. Formal cancellation, expiry without renewal, termination of advisers or a strategic decision to use a different funding route may support abandonment. Management should record the decision date and evidence because that date controls when accumulated equity-issue costs cease to qualify for equity treatment and move to profit or loss. If a project is only uncertain at year-end, the entity should evaluate whether the amounts meet any asset-recognition basis under another standard or should be expensed; Ind AS 32 does not create a free-standing asset called deferred issue expenses. Costs cannot remain indefinitely in other assets pending a favourable market. Subsequent events and authorisation-date evidence should be considered under Ind AS 10 where relevant. Reused work may require a fresh allocation An abandoned IPO may produce legal, valuation or systems work later used in a private placement or debt issue. Reuse does not retroactively make the original cost a qualifying cost of the later transaction. The company should determine whether a separately identifiable service was transferred to the new transaction and whether recognition criteria are met at that time. A broad assertion that the work remains useful is not enough. The year-end file should make the conclusion reproducible A robust file contains board approvals, transaction structure, adviser contracts, fee schedules, invoices, work breakdowns, issue and closing documents, allocation calculations, abandonment or postponement evidence, tax analysis and journal entries. It should identify the financial-instrument classification because a cost cannot be allocated correctly until the proceeds are classified as liability, equity or a compound of both. The closing review should also test cut-off, accrual completeness and presentation. Confirm that success fees are accrued only when their contractual trigger is met, that paid retainers are matched to services received, and that equity deductions reconcile to the statement of changes in equity. Material judgments and estimation uncertainty should be considered for disclosure under the applicable presentation standards. Frequently Asked Questions on equity transaction costs Are all IPO-related professional fees deducted from equity? No. Only incremental costs directly attributable to the completed equity transaction qualify. General corporate or listing-status work may be expensed. What happens to costs when the equity issue is cancelled? Costs of an abandoned equity transaction are recognised in profit or loss when abandonment is established. How are costs of a convertible debenture allocated? For a compound instrument, issue costs are allocated to liability and equity components in proportion to the allocation of proceeds. Can common legal fees be split between a share issue and a debt facility? Yes, if the allocation uses a rational, consistently applied basis supported by the adviser’s work and transaction facts. Is a postponed offering treated as abandoned immediately? Not necessarily. Evidence of continued board authority, active advisers and a viable execution plan may support postponement rather than abandonment. Should every equity issue cost receive a tax-effect adjustment? No. Recognition and measurement of any tax benefit depend on Ind AS 12 and applicable tax law, not an automatic rate multiplication. Can issue costs remain in other assets until markets improve? Ind AS 32 does not create a general deferred issue-cost asset. Recognition must be justified under the applicable standards and reassessed as facts change. Key Takeaways Use the incremental-and-directly-attributable test for each service, not the vendor’s label. Deduct qualifying completed equity-issue costs from equity net of recognised tax benefit. Expense abandoned equity transaction costs when abandonment is established. Allocate mixed and compound-instrument costs rationally between equity, liability and expense.
