Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Anil Valiaparampil Abraham Vs Directorate of Enforcement (Supreme Court of India) PMLA: Supreme Court Grants Bail After 15 Months in Custody as Trial Remains at Nascent Stage Introduction The Supreme Court of India granted bail to Anil Valiaparampil Abraham in a prosecution under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA), considering his approximately one year and three months of incarceration and the preliminary stage of the proceedings. The Court noted that cognizance had not yet been taken against the appellant, although the prosecution complaint had been filed. It also took account of the position of other accused persons and the substantial time likely to be required to complete the trial. Setting aside the Calcutta High Court’s refusal of bail, the Supreme Court directed the Trial Court to frame charges within four months from receipt of its order. Background of the Case The appellant was implicated in M.L. Case No. 11 of 2025, arising from ECIR No. KLZO-I/21/2023, registered by the Enforcement Directorate, Kolkata Zone-1. The prosecution concerned alleged offences under sections 3 and 4 read with section 70 of the PMLA. The appellant initially sought regular bail before the Special Court. That application was rejected through an order dated 22 December 2025. He thereafter approached the Calcutta High Court, which dismissed his regular bail application on 24 July 2026 in CRM (R) No. 16 of 2026. Challenging the High Court’s decision, the appellant approached the Supreme Court through a special leave petition. The Supreme Court granted leave and considered his request for release on bail. Appellant’s Submissions The appellant’s senior counsel submitted that the prosecution complaint had already been filed, but the court concerned had not yet taken cognizance against him. Counsel also highlighted that the appellant had remained incarcerated for approximately 15 months. These submissions placed the duration of custody alongside the limited progress of the proceedings. The appellant’s case for bail therefore rested substantially on the time already spent in custody and the fact that the prosecution had not progressed beyond a nascent stage against him. The Supreme Court recorded these circumstances while examining whether his continued detention was required pending trial. Enforcement Directorate’s Opposition The Additional Solicitor General, appearing for the Enforcement Directorate, opposed the request. The ED submitted that the appellant had misappropriated huge amounts of funds, notwithstanding the fact that the transactions were being monitored by the Supreme Court. This submission emphasised the seriousness of the allegations. However, the order does not record any final determination concerning the alleged misappropriation or the appellant’s criminal liability. The Supreme Court proceeded to assess the request for bail in light of the custody period, procedural progress and circumstances concerning the other accused. Position of Other Accused Persons The Court was informed that other accused persons who had been arrested had already been granted bail. It was also informed that many other accused persons had not been arrested. The Supreme Court recorded both circumstances before considering the likely duration of the trial. They formed part of the factual setting in which the appellant’s continued incarceration was examined. The order did not identify the other accused individually or undertake a detailed comparison of their alleged roles. Its reasoning focused on the overall circumstances relevant to the appellant’s request for release. Supreme Court’s Findings The Supreme Court observed that completion of the trial would certainly require a substantial amount of time. In that context, it held that the appellant’s continued incarceration was not required for that purpose. Two considerations were expressly identified while granting relief: the period of incarceration already undergone and the fact that the trial remained at a nascent stage, with cognizance yet to be taken against the appellant. The Court accordingly concluded that the High Court’s order refusing bail should be set aside. The decision grants relief on the circumstances recorded in this case. It does not decide the prosecution allegations or express a finding that the appellant was innocent of the alleged offences. Bail Conditions and Direction to Frame Charges The Supreme Court granted bail on such terms and conditions as the concerned Trial Court may impose. It did not itself prescribe a bond amount, surety requirement or other specific release conditions in the signed order. Separately, taking account of the facts, the Court directed the Trial Court to frame charges within four months from the date it receives a copy of the order. This direction concerns the framing of charges. The Court did not direct completion of the entire trial within that four-month period. Final Outcome The Supreme Court allowed the appeal and set aside the Calcutta High Court’s order dated 24 July 2026. Pending applications, if any, were also disposed of. The ruling highlights the relevance of prolonged custody, the stage of proceedings and the anticipated duration of trial when considering bail. The prosecution remains pending, subject to the Trial Court’s further proceedings and the bail conditions it imposes. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF SUPREME COURT OF INDIA 1) Leave granted. 2) The appellant has been implicated as an accused in connection with M.L. Case No. 11 of 2025 arising out of ECIR No. KLZO-I/21/2023, registered by the respondent-Enforcement Directorate for the offences under Sections 3 and 4 read with Section 70 of the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (for short, ‘the PMLA’). Upon rejection of the regular bail application by the Special Court vide order dated 22.12.2025, the appellant approached the High Court of Calcutta. The High Court vide impugned order dated 24.07.2026 passed in CRM (R) No.16 of 2026, dismissed the regular bail application of the appellant. Aggrieved, the appellant is before us. 3) We have heard the learned senior counsel appearing for the appellant and the learned Additional Solicitor General appearing for the respondent-Enforcement Directorate and have also perused the materials placed on record. 4) The learned senior counsel for the appellant submits that though the prosecution complaint has been filed as against the appellant, the cognizance is yet to be taken qua the appellant. He further submits that the appellant has already undergone incarceration for a period of about one year and three months. 5) The learned A.S.G. submits that, notwithstanding the fact that the transactions were being monitored by this Court, the appellant has misappropriated huge amounts of funds. 6) Be that as it may, we have been informed that the other accused person(s) who were arrested have been granted bail, and also many of the other accused persons have not been arrested so far. 7) Suffice it to state that, certainly, substantial amount of time will be required for the trial to be completed and, hence, continued incarceration of the appellant is not required for that purpose. 8) In such view of the matter, we are inclined to set aside the impugned order and grant bail considering the period of incarceration undergone by the appellant, as well as on the premise that the trial is at a nascent stage as cognizance is yet to be taken qua the appellant. 9) Accordingly, the impugned order dated 24.07.2026 passed in CRM (R) No.16 of 2026 by the High Court stands set aside, and the appellant is granted bail on such terms and conditions as may be imposed by the concerned Trial Court. 10) Taking into consideration the facts of the case, we direct the concerned Trial Court to frame charges within a period of four months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order. 11) The appeal stands allowed in the above terms. 12) Pending application(s), if any, shall stand disposed of. 1. Leave granted. 2. The appeal stands allowed in terms of the signed order. 3. Pending application(s), if any, shall stand disposed of.
