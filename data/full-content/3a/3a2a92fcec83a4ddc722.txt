The report highlights challenges in consolidation efforts due to rising expenditure and subsidy bills. Economic growth remains steady, with GDP forecasts at 7.3 percent for FY27 and 7.2 percent for FY28. Retail inflation surpassed the RBI's upper threshold driven primarily by higher food prices. While the central government is on track to meet its fiscal deficit target of 4.3 per cent of GDP in FY27, the path forward presents steeper hurdles."The central government will most likely meet its FY27 fiscal deficit target of 4.3% of GDP; however, FY28 consolidation looks considerably more challenging. Considering the various risks, we forecast the FY28 fiscal deficit to remain unchanged at 4.3% of GDP," the report stated. Advt Read more: India's GDP growth likely slowed to 7.6% in Q2FY27, India Ratings projectsSeveral cushions support the government's fiscal math in the current fiscal year, including a substantial Reserve Bank of India dividend of Rs 2.9 trillion, an Rs 1 trillion Economic Stabilisation Fund provision, potential capital expenditure compression, and higher-than-expected disinvestment receipts. However, the subsequent fiscal year faces headwinds from persistently elevated oil prices, larger subsidy bills, a potentially lower central bank dividend, and the rollout of the 8th Pay Commission."Implementation of the 8th Pay Commission, which could add at least Rs 3 trn to expenditure. Delaying the pay award to 2028 could provide near-term relief but would merely shift the burden forward through a larger accumulation of arrears," the report noted.Despite fiscal and external challenges, the report maintains a steady view on economic expansion. Real GDP growth is projected at 7.3 per cent year-on-year in FY27, 7.2 per cent in FY28, and 7.3 per cent in FY29.Read more: 'India is growing at 7%': Trump takes aim at US Fed over high interest rates, pushes for rate cuts'Although a sub-normal monsoon, higher oil prices, and unfavourable base effects from last year's GST rate cuts are expected to moderate growth in the second half of FY27, the implementation of pay commission awards is anticipated to lift domestic demand next year."We remain broadly constructive on India's growth trajectory and general resilience of the economy despite the ongoing West Asia crisis and a sub-normal monsoon this year," the report added. Advt On the price front, headline retail inflation breached the RBI's 6 per cent upper tolerance threshold in October to reach 6.09 per cent, driven by rising cereal and pulse prices.Deutsche Bank maintains its CPI inflation forecasts at 5.2 per cent for FY27 and 5.0 per cent for FY28, though it noted upside risks from broadening core price pressures and wholesale inflation remaining above 11 per cent."We continue to expect 100bps of repo rate hikes in this cycle, taking the policy rate to 6.25% by mid-2027 or earlier. With CPI now above the tolerance band, underlying inflation broadening and real GDP growth likely to remain above 7% YoY, despite the ongoing West Asia crisis, we expect the RBI to continue tightening in December 2026 with a 25bp hike," the report said. Advt Meanwhile, reduced capital inflows and a nearly USD 70 billion drop in foreign exchange reserves from their early-September peak of USD 786 billion continue to weigh on the currency.While recent central bank measures offered temporary relief to the rupee, the report forecasts the currency to gradually depreciate to 98.50 against the US dollar by the end of December 2027 as the RBI moves to rebuild its reserve buffer.
