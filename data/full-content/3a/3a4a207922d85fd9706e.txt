The bank has not disclosed a merchant-wise breakdown of the offers in its announcement. Customers should therefore not assume that every participating brand offers a 20 per cent discount or that the same terms apply to all transactions. The campaign, titled 'Dil Se Open Celebrations 2026', is being promoted through digital platforms, social media, outdoor advertising, direct customer communication, ATMs and the bank's branch network. Should customers use credit cards to maximise festive savings?Festive discounts can help reduce the cost of planned purchases, particularly when consumers are buying expensive electronics, replacing mobile phones or booking travel. But the advertised maximum discount does not necessarily translate into the same level of savings on every transaction. Customers should check the following before using their cards. Maximum discount: A 20 per cent discount may be subject to a cap. Check the maximum amount that can be saved on a transaction. Minimum spending requirement: Some offers may require customers to spend a specified amount to qualify. Eligible transactions: Verify whether the benefit applies to full payment, EMI purchases or both, and whether particular card variants are excluded. EMI costs: Check the interest rate, processing fee and total repayment amount. An EMI option should not automatically be treated as interest-free. Other discounts: Confirm whether the bank offer can be combined with merchant discounts, coupons or other promotions. For example, a 20 per cent discount on a purchase worth Rs 20,000 would amount to Rs 4,000 if the full discount applies without any cap or exclusion. If the offer has a lower maximum discount, the actual saving would be smaller. Customers should also distinguish between an upfront discount and an EMI offer. An EMI facility spreads payments over a period, but it does not necessarily reduce the overall cost of a purchase unless the applicable terms provide a genuine financial benefit. What does the partnership mean for cardholders? The campaign comes as banks and payment networks compete to make credit cards more useful for everyday spending and larger festive purchases. Merchant-linked offers can encourage customers to route transactions through a particular card, while EMI options can make high-value purchases easier to spread across monthly payments. Arnika Dixit, group head – cards, payments and wealth management at Axis Bank, said customers increasingly expect their cards to offer relevant benefits across different spending categories. The bank aims to make festive and planned purchases more rewarding through its partnership with Visa. For customers, however, the value of such offers depends on their existing spending plans and the actual terms available at checkout. Buying an unnecessary product to qualify for a discount can outweigh the benefit, while carrying an unpaid credit card balance can lead to interest charges that erode the savings. The more prudent approach is to compare the final price across sellers, check the applicable card offer and calculate the total cost before choosing between an upfront payment and an EMI.
