CS Rashmi H Summary: The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT), Chennai Bench, in Mr. Tajinder Singh Bhathal v. MRF Limited, held that procedural technicalities should not defeat a litigant’s right to access justice under Article 21 of the Constitution of India. The dispute concerned 100 equity shares of MRF Limited, originally purchased in 1987, over which rival ownership claims arose. The appellant filed a civil suit seeking declaration of ownership and subsequently approached the NCLT under Section 59 of the Companies Act, 2013, seeking rectification of the register of members. Although he undertook to withdraw the civil suit and filed an application for withdrawal, the civil court had not passed the withdrawal order when the NCLT dismissed his company petition on 10 August 2022 for pursuing parallel remedies. The civil court subsequently permitted withdrawal on 13 August 2022. The NCLAT observed that the appellant had acted bona fide and that the delay in recording the withdrawal was beyond his control. Upholding the dismissal would have deprived him of both remedies, contrary to the principle of access to justice protected under Article 21. Accordingly, the NCLAT set aside the NCLT’s order and directed adjudication of the company petition on merits, subject to payment of Rs. 5,000 as costs to the respondents. The Supreme Court has read this right widely. It covers the right to live with dignity, to livelihood and to a healthy environment. This right includes the right to access to justice, meaning that a person should not lose the right to approach a forum for relief merely because of a technical lapse. The Hon’ble NCLAT, Chennai Bench, applied this principle in Mr. Tajinder Singh Bhathal v. MRF Limited. Case Details Forum: National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT), Chennai Bench Appeal No.: Company Appeal (AT) (CH) No. 94/2023 (IA No. 1191/2023) Bench: Justice Sharad Kumar Sharma, Member (Judicial) Appellant: Mr. Tajinder Singh Bhathal, Pune Respondents: MRF Limited (R1); Mr. K. V. Munivenkata Reddy (R2); Mr. Kumar V. Jahgirdar (R3) Facts of the Case 1. Purchase of shares: On 27.02.1987, Late Shri Iqbal Singh Bhathal bought 100 equity shares of MRF Limited (Folio No. B02990) in the joint names of his son, Mr. Tajinder Singh Bhathal (the Appellant), and his wife, Late Smt. Satwant Kaur Bhathal. Mr. Tajinder Singh Bhathal came to know of the shares only on 16.10.2018, when he was asked to verify his shareholding as the shares had been transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF). 2. Request for duplicate certificates: Mr. Tajinder Singh Bhathal asked MRF Limited to issue duplicate share certificates, saying the originals (Certificate Nos. 168061 and 168062) were lost. MRF Limited replied that the originals were not lost but were held by a third party, M/s. Fair Growth Investments Limited, Mumbai. On 06.12.2018, Mr. Tajinder Singh Bhathal lodged an FIR in Pune about the loss or theft of the certificates. 3. Rival claim: MRF Limited disclosed that Mr. K. V. Munivenkata Reddy had lodged transfer documents claiming ownership of the same shares. Mr. K. V. Munivenkata Reddy had also filed a civil suit (O.S. No. 7586/2019) in Bengaluru claiming title to the shares. 4. Parallel proceedings: On 24.01.2020, Mr. Tajinder Singh Bhathal filed his own civil suit (R.C.S. No. 123/2020) in Pune claiming title to the 100 shares. While this civil suit was pending, he filed Company Petition No. 106(CHE)/2021 before the NCLT, Chennai on 28.07.2021 under Section 59 of the Companies Act, 2013. This section allows a person to seek rectification of the register of members. Proceedings before the NCLT In the company petition (Paras 42 and 43), the Appellant gave an undertaking, a formal promise to the Tribunal, that he would withdraw the civil suit. He had filed a withdrawal application, but the civil court had not yet passed an order on this matter. On 10.08.2022, the NCLT dismissed the petition. NCLT held that the Appellant had not honoured his undertaking and he could not pursue two remedies on the same cause of action, that is, the same set of facts giving rise to the right to sue. Three days later, on 13.08.2022, the civil court allowed the withdrawal. Issue before the NCLAT Could the company petition be dismissed because the civil suit was still pending on the date of the order by the NCLT, when the delay in recording the withdrawal was not the Appellant’s fault? Findings of the NCLAT 1. No lasting parallel proceedings: The concern behind the rule against parallel proceedings is to prevent multiplicity of litigation, i.e. pursuing the same dispute in two forums at once between the same parties. 2. Bona fide conduct: The Appellant had acted in good faith (bona fide) by filing the withdrawal application. Passing of order for withdrawal of suit in the civil court was beyond his control. 3. Article 21 and access to justice: The right to seek a judicial remedy is protected under Article 21, and a person cannot be denied of this right due to a minor procedural technicality. Had the dismissal of the Company petition in the NCLT confirmed, the Appellant would have lost both remedies, the Civil suit having been withdrawn and the petition having been dismissed. 4. Balancing the equities: To balance the equities between the parties, the NCLAT set aside the impugned order of the NCLT and held that the case has to be decided on merits. Costs The Appellant was directed to pay Rs. 5,000 to the Respondents for the inconvenience caused by his procedural lapse. Takeaway Procedural rules exist to ensure fair adjudication and not to defeat genuine claims. Where a litigant has acted in good faith and the defect has been cured, the appellate forum choos substance over form. Cases Discussed 1. Mr. Tajinder Singh Bhathal v.
