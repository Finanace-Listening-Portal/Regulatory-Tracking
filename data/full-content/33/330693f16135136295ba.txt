Articles 4 min read Healthcare needs stronger partnerships between hospitals and insurers Aditya Birla Health Insurance CEO and Manipal Hospitals MD-CEO argue that India's healthcare system must move beyond transactional relationships between hospitals and insurers towards patient-centric partnerships. They call for greater clinical coordination, digital integration, transparency and preventive care, with success measured by better health outcomes rather than individual claims or procedures. Their roles are distinct, but their interests converge on a common objective: better outcomes for patients through care that is high-quality, accessible and financially sustainable.Historically, the relationship between hospitals and insurers has been transactional, with one delivering care and the other paying for it. Given the growing complexity of healthcare and rising patient expectations, this model is no longer adequate. The opportunity before us is to evolve the payer-provider relationship into a genuine partnership. Advt Payers and providers share an interest in clinical outcomes. Hospitals are committed to delivering evidence-based care that achieves the best possible results, and insurers are committed to ensuring access to care while providing financial protection. When the two work together, the conversation can move beyond cost to the patient's health outcome.Such collaboration supports early detection, appropriate treatment, better management of chronic conditions and stronger post-discharge care. It also helps reduce avoidable complications, unnecessary procedures and readmissions. Patients receive well-timed care, providers can deliver better care, insurers serve a healthier population, and healthcare expenditure becomes more sustainable over the long term.Appropriate care is central to any well-functioning healthcare system. The objective should be to ensure that each patient receives the care that clinical evidence supports, when they need it, rather than to maximise treatment or simply to minimise cost.Greater sharing of clinical guidelines and evidence-based treatment pathways will strengthen confidence between providers and insurers. In due course, this should shift discussions from individual approvals and claims towards agreed standards of care and outcomes. Such an approach respects clinical judgement while offering patients and payers greater predictability. Advt Trust is essential in healthcare and it rests on transparency. Patients should understand the treatment they are receiving, the reason for it, its cost and the extent of their insurance coverage. Any lack of clarity adds to anxiety of patients and families who are under considerable stress.Providers and insurers can address this together. Digital integration between hospitals and insurers can simplify pre-authorisations, documentation and claims, reducing the administrative burden for all parties. The principle should be clear: patients should not have to act as intermediaries between their hospital and their insurer. Advances in technology and process integration now make it possible for both to coordinate in the background, allowing patients to concentrate on their recovery. Advt Healthcare is increasingly extending beyond the hospital. Patients require support across prevention, screening, diagnosis, inpatient treatment, rehabilitation, home care, medication, remote monitoring and the long-term management of chronic conditions.Collaboration between payers and providers is critical here. Together, they can develop programmes that encourage healthier lifestyles and earlier detection of disease, so that insurance plays a role well before hospitalisation.Preventive health checks, disease management programmes, teleconsultations, home care, rehabilitation and remote monitoring can increasingly form part of an integrated continuum of care. For chronic conditions such as diabetes, heart disease and cancer, success is determined by how effectively a patient's health is managed over months and years, rather than by a single hospital stay. Advt Providers have visibility into the clinical journey, while payers have insight into long-term health patterns and claims. When these datasets are combined responsibly, the industry's focus can move from the volume of procedures to clinical outcomes, early intervention and long-term health.At the same time, as healthcare becomes more digital, it must remain human. Hospitals must provide compassionate care, and insurers must ensure that approvals are simple and predictable, and that coverage is easy to understand.The future of healthcare will require a fundamental shift from treating individual episodes of illness to managing health over a person's lifetime. No single stakeholder can achieve this.The next phase of healthcare should be defined by collaboration around the patient rather than negotiation between payer and provider. Progress will be evident when providers and payers share information more effectively, coordinate around evidence-based care, improve transparency, simplify the patient journey, and measure success by health outcomes rather than transactions.Ultimately, healthcare needs partnerships that ensure every patient receives appropriate care, achieves better outcomes and can access that care with confidence.Bathwal is CEO, Aditya Birla Health Insurance.
