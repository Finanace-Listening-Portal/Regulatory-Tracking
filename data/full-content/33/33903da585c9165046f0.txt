The dispute stems from Yes Bank's rescue in 2020, when authorities wrote off ₹84.15 billion worth of additional Tier-1 (AT-1) bonds, a type of security designed to absorb losses when banks run into trouble. Last month, the Supreme Court ruled the write-off illegal over procedural lapses and ordered the bonds restored. However, review petitions by the bank and the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) have delayed relief for investors. Bondholders, in their letter dated November 18, said the RBI had remained "absent" in its capacity as the regulator "responsible for protecting the interests of the investing public and the integrity of the banking system".
