Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Eastern India Healthcare Foundation Vs CIT (Exemption) (ITAT Kolkata) Summary: The Kolkata Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal allowed Eastern India Healthcare Foundation’s appeal for AY 2022-23 and quashed the revision proceedings and order dated 17 March 2026 under section 263. After condoning a 59-day delay, the Tribunal examined the Commissioner’s allegation that the AO had inadequately verified a payment of ₹5,65,56,841 to Medica Hospital Private Limited, allegedly involving a related-party benefit and non-compliance with FCRA. The trust had returned nil income, accepted in the assessment dated 23 February 2024. The assessment record contained specific enquiries through notices under section 142(1), detailed replies, audited accounts, the agreement with DEG, Germany, and a chartered accountant-certified cost and funding statement. The COVID-19 relief activities involved actual expenditure of approximately ₹37.75 crore, funded by DEG with ₹14.40 crore, other corporates with ₹10 crore and Medica with ₹13.35 crore. The Tribunal held that the AO had examined the foreign contribution and its utilisation and adopted a possible view; absence of elaborate discussion in the assessment order did not establish absence of enquiry. It also considered Explanation 2 to section 263 and found no basis to treat the assessment as erroneous. On the alleged violation of section 13(1)(c)(ii) read with section 13(3), the Tribunal found that the Commissioner had established neither Medica’s status as a specified concern nor any benefit transferred to a specified person. Medica Hospitals was wholly owned by Medica Synergie Private Limited, whose shareholding comprised 87.11% held by FII, 8.1% by public shareholders other than promoters and only 4.79% by Indian promoters. On that record, the Tribunal found no substantial interest meeting the statutory 20% voting-power threshold. It also noted Medica’s own ₹13.35 crore contribution to the relief activities. The trust had obtained FCRA registration on 9 July 2021 and filed Form FC-4; the FCRA authorities had raised no objection to the utilisation recorded in the order. Relying on the cited precedents concerning permissible views, enquiries and the twin requirements of section 263, the Tribunal concluded that the Commissioner had failed to establish either error or prejudice to the Revenue and had invalidly assumed revisional jurisdiction. It is absolutely clear from the above that the AO has taken a possible view on the issue and passed the order. The ld. PCIT has invoked the jurisdiction on the ground that the AO has failed to enquire into the issue which has rendered the assessment to be erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of the revenue. In our considered view the jurisdiction u/s 263 is not available to the PCIT where the AO has taken a possible view. The case of the assessee is squarely covered by the decision in CIT vs. Max India Ltd. (2007) 295 ITR 0282 (SC) wherein it has been held as under: “The phrase “prejudicial to the interests of the Revenue has to be read in conjunction with an erroneous order passed by the Assessing Officer. Every loss of revenue as a consequence of an order of the Assessing Officer, cannot be treated as prejudicial to the interests of the Revenue, for example, when an Income-tax Officer adopted one of the courses permissible in law and it has resulted in loss of revenue, or where two views are possible and the Income-tax Officer has taken one view with which the Commissioner does not agree, it cannot be treated as an erroneous order prejudicial to the interests of the Revenue unless the view taken by the Income-tax Officer is unsustainable in law.” 7. We have perused the new explanation “Explanation – 2” to section 263 vide Finance Act, 2015 w.e.f. 01-06-2015 of the Act which reads as follows: “Explanation 2.- For the purposes of this section, it is hereby declared that an order passed by the AO shall be deemed to be erroneous in so far as it is prejudicial to the interests of the revenue, if, in the opinion of the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner.- (a) the order is passed without making inquiries or verification which should have been made: (b) the order is passed allowing any relief without inquiring into the claim; (c) the order has not been made in accordance with any order, direction or instruction issued by the Board under section 119; or (d) the order has not been passed in accordance with any decision which is prejudicial to the assessee, rendered by the jurisdictional High Court or Supreme Court in the case of the assessee or any other person.” 8. It is apparent from the perusal of the above explanation that the following orders passed by assessing officer shall now be considered as erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of revenue w.e.f. 01.06.2015, where AO passed order in the following manners, (i) without making any inquiries/verification which he/she is required to be made, (ii) without making inquiry into a claim which is claimed by assessee and allowed such claim, (iii) which is not in accordance with any order/direction/instruction (i.e. circulars) issued by CBDT, (iv) which is not in accordance with any decision of jurisdictional High Court or Supreme Court which is prejudicial to the assessee or any other person. In other words, where jurisdictional High Court or Supreme Court’s decision is against the assessee or any other person and AO passed the order without considering such judgment then such order shall be considered as erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of revenue. 9. In our opinion the AO is not supposed to give detailed reasoning or discussion on every issue in the assessment order. It would be sufficient if the has made an enquiry into the issue. The CIT(E) cannot invoke the jurisdiction for the reason that he is not agreeing with the view taken by the AO and the AO should have taken other view. The case of the assessee find support the judgment of the Bombay High Court in the case of Commissioner of Income-tax v. Sunbeam Auto Ltd (2011) 332 ITR 0167-Del wherein it was held as under: “The Assessing Officer in the assessment order is not required to give a detailed reason in respect of each and every item of deduction, etc. Whether there was application of mind before allowing the expenditure in question has to be seen. If there was any inquiry, even inadequate that would not by itself give occasion to the Commissioner to pass orders under section 263 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, merely because he has a different opinion in the matter. It is only in cases of lack of inquiry that such a course of action would be open.” 15. Further section 13(1)(c)(ii) of the Act states that a charitable or religious trust or institution loses its tax exemption on any part of its income if its income or property is used or applied directly or indirectly for the private benefit of any person defined under Section 13(3) of the Act. The provision of section 13(3) of the Act is reproduced as under: “(3) The persons referred to in clause (c) of sub-section (1) and sub-section (2) are the following, namely:- (a) the author of the trust or the founder of the institution: (b) any person who has made a substantial contribution to the trust or institution, that is to say, any person whose total contribution up to the end of the relevant previous year exceeds fifty thousand rupees; (c) where such author, founder or person is a Hindu undivided family, a member of the family: (cc) any trustee of the trust or manager (by whatever name called) of the institution; (d) any relative of any such author, founder, person, member, trustee or manager as aforesaid; (e) any concern in which any of the persons referred to in claves (a), (b), (c), (cc) and (d) has a substantial interest. Explanation 3. For the purposes of this section, a person shall be deemed to have a substantial interest in a concern- (i) in a case where the concern is a company, if its shares (not being shares entitled to a fixed rate of dividend whether with or without a further right to participate in profits) carrying not less than twenty per cent of the voting power are, at any time during the previous year, owned beneficially by such person or partly by such person and partly by one or more of the other persons referred to in sub-section (3) (ii) in the case of any other concern, if such person is entitled, or such person and one more of the other persons referred to in sub-section (3) are entitled in the aggregate, at any time during the previous year, to not less than twenty per cent of the profits of such concern.” 19. A clear reading of provision of section 13(1)(c)(ii) read with section 13(3) provides that any part of income or property of the trust shall not be utilized for the benefit of author of the trust or the founder of institution, any person who has made contribution exceeding Rs.50,000/- where such author, founder or person is a HUF, a member of the family, trustee or manager of the trust/institution, any of their relative or any concern in which such persons has substantial interest. Here in the case of the assessee, the ld.CIT(E) has failed to show what benefit has been given to the person referred to in section 13(3) of the act on account of fund received from DEG. Further, it is submitted that, amount was paid to Medica Hospital Pvt Ltd and therefore as per clause (e) of section 13(3), a company can be said to be related party only if the persons referred to in clauses (a), (b), (c), (cc) and (d) has a substantial interest in the company. As per Explanation 3 of section 13, a person shall be deemed to have substantial interest in company, if its shares (not being shares entitled to a fixed rate of dividend whether with or without a further right to participate in profits) carrying not less than twenty per cent of the voting power are, at any time during the previous year, owned beneficially by such person or partly by such person and partly by one or more of the other persons referred to in sub-section (3). Here, in order to show that neither the assessee trust nor any of the person referred to in clauses (a), (b), (c), (cc) and (d) of section 13(3) has substantial interest in Media Hospital Pvt Ltd, the shareholding pattern of Media Hospital Pvt Ltd is enclosed at page 184 of the Paper book. On perusal of the same it shall be evident that 100% of shares of Medica Hospitals is held by Media Synergie Private Limited. 20. Further, shareholding pattern of Medica Synergie Pvt Ltd is enclosed at page 200 of the paper book and MGT-7 is enclosed at page 185-199 of paper book. On perusal of the same, it shall be evident that 87.11% of shares were held by FII and 8.1% of shares were held by public shareholders- other than promoters. Only 4.79% of shares were held by Indian Promoters. Hence in no case, any of the trustee/author/founder of trust whose name is appearing in the list of shareholders of Medica Synergie has substantial interest i.e. 20% of voting power in Media Hospitals Pvt Ltd. Hence the said transactions do not fall under section 13(1)(c)(ii) of the Act. We further note that the said fund was never used for the benefit of persons referred to in section 13(3) of the Act. In this regard, reference is made to the Funds Flow enclosed at page 120-122 of the paper book which shows as under: Estimated cost : Euro 48,77,761.00 Actual Cost incurred : EURO 43.92.227.00 Actual Cost IN INR : Rs. 37.75 crore Funded by DEG : Rs. 14.40 cr. Funded by Other corporate : Rs. 10 cr. Funded by Medica : Rs. 13.35 cr 21. Since, Medica itself contributed Rs.13.35 crore, we are not able to understand as to how an amount paid to Medica by the assessee company may lead to any benefit to the persons referred to in section 13(3) of the Act. In view of the above, it stands establish that the ld.CIT(E) has initiated this present proceeding without application of mind to the case of the assessee. The ld.CIT(E) failed to establish how Medica Hospitals Pvt Ltd fall under persons referred to in section 13(3) of the Act. The ld.CIT(E) also failed to establish what benefit has been transferred to the Persons referred to Section 13(3) of the Act. Considering these facts and the ratio laid in the above decisions, we are of the view that the jurisdiction u/s 263 has been invalidly invoked by the ld. CIT(E) and consequently the proceedings u/s 263 and order u/s 263 are hereby quashed. 22. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed. The order is pronounced on 26/10/2026.
