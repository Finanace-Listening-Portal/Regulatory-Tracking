(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court dismissed the Union of India’s Special Leave Petition challenging the Delhi High Court’s judgment concerning the mandatory deposit of building and construction workers’ welfare cess and penalty before pursuing a statutory appeal. After condoning delay and hearing the Additional Solicitor General, the Supreme Court stated that it was not inclined to interfere with the High Court’s judgment and order. It dismissed the SLP and disposed of pending applications. The supplied Supreme Court order contains no independent discussion of the statutory provisions or reasons for refusing interference. The detailed legal reasoning belongs to the underlying Delhi High Court judgment reproduced with the order. The underlying dispute arose when the assessing authority imposed a demand of ₹9,13,77,520 on M.T.N.L. by order dated 7 March 2012, comprising cess of ₹3,87,19,288, interest of ₹1,39,38,944 and penalty of ₹3,87,19,288. M.T.N.L.’s statutory appeal was dismissed because it had not deposited the disputed amounts as required by Rule 14(2) of the Building and Other Construction Workers Welfare Cess Rules, 1998. M.T.N.L. challenged both the appellate order dated 5 August 2013 and the validity of the pre-deposit requirement. Before the Delhi High Court, M.T.N.L. argued that Section 11(2) of the Building and Other Construction Workers Welfare Cess Act, 1996 authorised only a prescribed appeal fee, not compulsory deposit of the disputed cess and penalty. The Union of India argued that the statutory expression “fees”, read with the rule-making powers under Section 14, was wide enough to support the requirement. The High Court rejected that interpretation, distinguishing the one-per-cent appeal fee prescribed by Rule 14(2)(c) from the deposit certificate required under Rule 14(2)(b). It also observed that the Rules contained no provision for waiver or reduction of the disputed cess or penalty. Relying on Additional District Magistrate (Rev.), Delhi Administration v. Siri Ram, (2000) 5 SCC 451, the High Court held that delegated legislation could not impose a requirement exceeding the authority conferred by its parent statute. It accordingly struck down Rule 14(2)(b) as ultra vires Section 11 of the 1996 Act, set aside the appellate order and directed that M.T.N.L.’s appeal be decided on merits, subject to payment of the prescribed fee under Rule 14(2)(c), if outstanding, within two weeks of receiving the judgment. The High Court did not decide the substantive cess liability. The Supreme Court’s subsequent dismissal of the SLP left that judgment undisturbed without supplying additional reasoning. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER Delay condoned. Heard the learned Additional Solicitor General. We are not inclined to interfere with the impugned judgment and order passed by the High Court. Hence, the Special Leave Petition is dismissed. Pending application(s), if any, shall stand disposed of.
