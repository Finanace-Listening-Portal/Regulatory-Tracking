Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Dinesh Singh Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Substantial Justice Must Prevail Where Delay Is Explained: Delhi ITAT Condones 1,356-Day Delay Summary: The Delhi Income Tax Appellate Tribunal has condoned a delay of 1,356 days in filing an appeal before the CIT(A)/NFAC, after noting that the assessee had explained the delay by reference to circumstances beyond his control. Relying on Collector, Land Acquisition v. Mst. Katiji & Others, the Tribunal held that technical considerations must give way to substantial justice in the circumstances before it. The matter was restored to the CIT(A)/NFAC for fresh adjudication with three effective opportunities, subject to the assessee’s responsibility to plead and prove his case. The Tribunal separately condoned an approximately 884-day delay in filing the appeal before it. Facts of the Case The dispute arose from an assessment framed on 6 December 2019 under Section 147 read with Section 144 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, for Assessment Year 2012–13. The assessee filed an appeal before the CIT(A)/NFAC on 13 October 2023. The first appellate authority recorded a delay of 1,356 days and refused to condone it, holding that the delay had not been explained through justifiable reasons. Aggrieved by the CIT(A)/NFAC’s order dated 29 December 2023, the assessee approached the Tribunal. That appeal was itself delayed by approximately 884 days. For a related discussion of procedural rejection of appeals, see Appeal Cannot Be Dismissed Without Deciding Delay Condonation. Issue Before the Tribunal The principal question was whether the assessee should be denied consideration of his assessment appeal because of the delay, despite having filed a condonation petition explaining circumstances beyond his control. The Tribunal therefore examined the procedural rejection of the appeal, rather than the merits of the underlying assessment. Delay Before the Tribunal Condoned At the outset, the Tribunal accepted the reasons stated in the assessee’s condonation averments and condoned the approximately 884-day delay in filing the appeal before it. For this conclusion, it relied on the Supreme Court’s decision in Collector, Land Acquisition v. Mst. Katiji & Others, (1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC). This enabled the Tribunal to examine the CIT(A)/NFAC’s refusal to condone the separate delay in the first appeal. Other decisions involving condonation of substantial delays are discussed in ITAT Condones 308-Day Delay, Revives CPC Adjustment Appeal and Mumbai ITAT Condones 690-Day Delay, Deletes Wife’s Credit Card Expense Disallowance. Assessee Had Explained Circumstances Beyond His Control The Tribunal recorded that the departmental representative could hardly dispute that the assessee had filed a condonation petition before the CIT(A)/NFAC explaining the reasons for delay on account of circumstances beyond his control. On that factual basis, it considered refusal to admit the appeal inappropriate. The short order does not reproduce the specific circumstances or supporting documents relied upon by the assessee. Accordingly, the decision should be understood as an acceptance of the explanation on the record in this particular case. A related instance of appellate restoration following condonation of a substantial delay is discussed in ITAT Surat Condoned 1,279-Day Delay, Restores Assessment and Penalty. Substantial Justice Preferred over Technical Rejection Applying Mst. Katiji, the Tribunal held that technical aspects must make way for the cause of substantial justice. The Supreme Court’s approach in that decision favours a meaningful examination of sufficient cause so that a matter is not defeated at the threshold merely by a technical approach to a non-deliberate delay. That approach has also been reiterated in subsequent Supreme Court judgments. In the present case, the Tribunal found the explanation sufficient to condone the 1,356-day delay before the CIT(A)/NFAC. For further reading on restoration of appeals dismissed on limitation grounds, see ITAT Agra Restores Appeal Dismissed Over 106-Day Filing Delay. Matter Restored with Three Effective Opportunities After condoning the delay, the Tribunal restored the matter to the CIT(A)/NFAC for fresh adjudication. It directed that the assessee be afforded three effective opportunities, while expressly placing responsibility on the taxpayer to plead and prove the case at his own risk and responsibility in the consequential proceedings. Thus, the restoration gave the assessee an opportunity to contest the assessment; it did not dispense with the need to furnish evidence and pursue the appeal diligently. The distinction between procedural dismissal and adjudication on merits is also addressed in Technical Dismissal by CIT(A) Set Aside – Appeal to Be Decided on Merits, Not Procedural Lapses. Outcome of the Appeal The Tribunal: Condoned approximately 884 days’ delay in the appeal before it. Condoned 1,356 days’ delay in the appeal before the CIT(A)/NFAC. Restored the matter for fresh adjudication with three effective opportunities. Allowed the appeal for statistical purposes. The underlying assessment was not annulled, and no addition was deleted on merits through this order. Practical Takeaway The decision illustrates that even a substantial delay may be condoned where the explanation establishes circumstances beyond the taxpayer’s control and warrants consideration in the interests of justice. It does not create an automatic entitlement to condonation. A taxpayer must still provide a credible explanation for the delay and, after restoration, actively substantiate the assessment appeal. Here, the relief was an opportunity for adjudication, rather than a decision in the assessee’s favour on the tax dispute. Cases Discussed Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag Vs Mst. Katiji & Others (Supreme Court); Civil Appeal No. 460 of 1987; 19/02/1987; (1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC); AY: Not Applicable. The Supreme Court held that the expression “sufficient cause” must be applied in a justice-oriented manner. The Court preferred substantial justice over an unduly technical approach to limitation and recognised that condonation permits disputes to be decided on merits. The Delhi ITAT expressly relied on this judgment while condoning both the approximately 884-day delay before it and the 1,356-day delay before the CIT(A)/NFAC. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI This assessee’s appeal for assessment year 2012-13, arises against the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)/National Faceless Appeal Centre [in short, the “CIT(A)/NFAC”], Delhi’s DIN and order no. ITBA/NFAC/S/250/2023-24/1059204144(1), dated 29.12.2023 involving proceedings under section 147 r.w.s. 144 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’). Heard both the parties. Case file perused. 2 For the reasons stated in the assessee’s condonation averments, delay of 884 (approx.) days in filing of the instant appeal is condoned in light of Collector, Land & Acquisition vs. Mst. Katiji & Others (1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC). 3. It emerges during the course of hearing with the able assistance coming from both the parties that the learned CIT(A)/NFAC has refused to condone the delay of 1356 days in filing of the assessee’s lower appeal instituted on 13.10.2023 against the Assessing Officer’s assessment framed on 06.12.2019, thereby holding that the same had not been explained in light of the justifiable reasons. 4. Faced with the situation, learned departmental representative could hardly dispute that the assessee had indeed filed his condonation petition before the CIT(A)/NFAC explaining all the reasons on account of circumstances beyond his control. 5. That being the case, I hereby quote Collector, Land & Acquisition vs. Mst. Katiji & Others (1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC), settling the issue long back that all such technical aspects must make way for the cause of substantial justice. Accordingly, the delay caused in filing the appeal before the CIT(A)/NFAC is condoned and the assessee’s instant appeal is restored back to the CIT(A)/NFAC for its afresh appropriate adjudication within three effective opportunities subject to a rider that the taxpayer shall plead and prove the case at his own risk and responsibility, in consequential proceedings. Ordered accordingly. 6. This assessee’s appeal is allowed for statistical purposes.
