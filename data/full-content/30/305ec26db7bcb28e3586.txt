Summary: Errors in Form 3CD often arise when financial statements, ledgers, statutory returns and income-tax computations are prepared using inconsistent classifications or figures. Before uploading the tax audit report, confirm whether Form 3CA or Form 3CB applies and whether the attached Form 3CD relates to the correct assessment year. Review the method of accounting, depreciation schedules, payments to specified persons, statutory dues, disallowances, tax deduction and collection at source, cash receipts and payments, loans, deposits and specified advances. Reconcile turnover and indirect-tax data with the books and explain genuine differences rather than using unsupported balancing entries. Verify PAN and other identification details, actual dates of statutory payments, and supporting evidence for each reportable item. Distinguish the auditor’s reporting obligation from the assessee’s ultimate income-tax treatment. The filing deadline must be determined for the relevant taxpayer and assessment year after considering any applicable CBDT extension. This article sets out a practical, evidence-based checklist for identifying and resolving common reporting errors before the tax audit report is finalised. Incorrect or incomplete disclosures can lead to queries, mismatches with the return of income and avoidable disputes. The auditor should report based on appropriate evidence, and unresolved qualifications or limitations should not be hidden through unsupported adjustments. Choose the Correct Tax Audit Report and Assessment Year When Form 3CA Applies Form 3CA generally accompanies Form 3CD where the accounts of the business or profession are required to be audited under another law. Verify the statutory audit requirement and the relevant audited financial statements. When Form 3CB Applies Form 3CB generally accompanies Form 3CD where the accounts are not required to be audited under another law. Ensure the reporting basis, financial statements and supporting schedules are consistent. Confirm the Applicable Period and Due Date Match the financial year, assessment year, taxpayer category and relevant audit-report deadline. Verify any CBDT extension from the actual notification or order; do not assume that a deadline quoted in an earlier article remains applicable. Review Accounting Policies, Turnover and Financial Statements Method of Accounting and Changes Review the disclosures under clause 13 and reconcile the accounting method, material changes and tax computation adjustments with the financial statements. Distinguish accounting-policy disclosures from income-computation requirements. Turnover and GST Reconciliation Compare sales ledgers, financial statements, GST returns, credit notes, debit notes, advances and year-end adjustments. Document differences arising from timing, exempt or non-GST supplies, valuation and other legitimate causes. Do not introduce a balancing figure merely to make totals agree. Check Depreciation, Deductions and Disallowances Depreciation and Asset Records For clause 18, compare the income-tax depreciation schedule with the fixed-asset register, additions, disposals, put-to-use dates, applicable rates and opening written-down values. Consider whether the tax classification differs from depreciation charged in the books. Specified Persons and Related-Party Payments For clause 23, identify payments to persons covered by section 40A(2)(b) and reconcile the list with ownership records, related-party schedules, ledgers and supporting invoices. Reporting a payment does not automatically establish that it is excessive or disallowable. Statutory Dues and Other Disallowances Review the applicable reporting under clauses 21 and 26 and the relevant provisions, including sections 40(a), 40A and 43B where applicable. Match amounts with challans, bank debits, ledgers and statutory returns. Examine the nature of the payment and the actual statutory conditions before reaching a tax conclusion. Verify TDS and TCS Reporting Under Clause 34 Reconcile expense ledgers, payments, tax deduction or collection registers, challans and filed TDS/TCS statements. Check whether tax was required to be deducted or collected, the relevant rate, amount, date and payment status. Investigate differences between books and statements, including reversals, credit notes and year-end provisions. Avoid treating every accounting entry as subject to identical withholding requirements. Examine Cash Transactions, Loans and Deposits Review reporting related to sections 269SS, 269ST and 269T and the applicable Form 3CD clauses. Reconcile cash books, bank statements, loan confirmations, receipts, repayments and journal entries. Assess the precise transaction type and statutory exceptions before concluding that a contravention occurred. Identify loan movements separately from revenue receipts and routine book transfers. Validate Identification Details and Other Disclosures Check the taxpayer’s name, PAN, address, constitution, relevant registrations and particulars required by the applicable Form 3CD. Compare the tax audit report with the financial statements, income-tax return workings and statutory records. Review all applicable clauses, not only the clauses highlighted in this checklist, and document why any clause is marked inapplicable. Practical Example: Resolving a GST Turnover Difference Suppose the turnover in the books differs from the aggregate turnover reported in GST returns. Rather than entering an unexplained balancing amount, prepare a reconciliation showing invoice-date differences, credit notes, advances, exempt supplies and any other supported items. Match each material difference to source documents, identify errors requiring correction and retain the signed reconciliation in the audit file. This is an illustrative procedure, not a prescribed reconciliation format. Documents and Working Papers to Retain Audited financial statements, trial balance, ledgers and tax computation. Fixed-asset register and income-tax depreciation workings. GST returns, sales reconciliation and supporting invoices. TDS/TCS returns, challans, registers and expense reconciliations. Statutory payment challans, related-party records and loan confirmations. Cash transaction reviews, management explanations and unresolved-exception records. Final clause-wise review, applicable due-date evidence and filing acknowledgement. Common Form 3CD Mistakes to Avoid Using the wrong report form, financial year or assessment year. Copying previous-year disclosures without fresh verification. Reporting tax depreciation using book depreciation without reconciliation. Leaving GST and accounting turnover differences unexplained. Omitting specified-person payments, cash transactions or loan movements. Reporting statutory dues or TDS/TCS figures without checking actual payment records. Assuming that a reporting disclosure necessarily determines the final disallowance. Relying on an unverified deadline instead of the applicable statutory provision and CBDT orders. Frequently Asked Questions on Form 3CD Is Form 3CD Filed With Form 3CA or Form 3CB? Yes. Form 3CD contains the prescribed particulars and accompanies the applicable audit report in Form 3CA or Form 3CB, depending on whether the accounts are audited under another law. Does a GST Turnover Difference Always Mean the Books Are Wrong? No. Differences can arise from timing and classification. Each difference should be reconciled with underlying records, and any actual error should be corrected. Does Disclosure Under Clause 23 Automatically Result in Disallowance? No. Reporting specified-person payments and determining whether any amount is excessive or unreasonable are distinct questions. What Is the Main Control for Clause 34? Reconcile the underlying transactions with TDS/TCS registers, statutory statements and challans, and separately investigate exceptions. Can the Previous Year’s Tax Audit Due Date Be Used? No. Verify the relevant assessment year’s due date, taxpayer-specific applicability and any valid CBDT extension.
