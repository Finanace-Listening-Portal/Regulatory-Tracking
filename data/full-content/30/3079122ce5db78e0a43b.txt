Articles 2 min read Can NPCI replicate the UPI success in AI and blockchain? NPCI's expansion into artificial intelligence (AI), blockchain and digital financial infrastructure raises the question of whether the organisation that built India's largest retail payments network can replicate its success in other technology domains. NPCI has appointed its chief technology officer Vishal Kanvaty as the subsidiary's managing director and chief executive. Advt The move gives NPCI a separate organisational platform to pursue technology development beyond its existing payment operations. While its other subsidiaries are largely organised around specific payment businesses, NTSL has a broader technology mandate covering multiple domains.NTSL mandateNPCI had announced the NTSL launch in 2025 as a platform for experimentation and fintech innovation. Its expanded mandate now gives it a wider technology focus.AI is one area where NPCI has already developed capabilities. It has developed FiMI (Financial Model for India), a domain-specific language model for the country's payments ecosystem, and introduced an AI-powered UPI Help Assistant to support transaction-related queries, grievance redressal and mandate management. It has also partnered with Nvidia to develop AI capabilities for the payments ecosystem, with potential applications for banks and fintech companies.The scale of NPCI's existing operations provides a substantial base for this technology push. UPI processed 25.47 billion transactions worth Rs 31.53 lakh crore in October 2026, across 758 banks. Its experience in building and operating interoperable systems at this scale could be relevant to other digital financial infrastructure projects.Blockchain and GCC services represent newer areas of focus. However, NPCI has not specified the projects it intends to develop in these areas or disclosed whether NTSL will offer technology services commercially to institutions outside its existing ecosystem. Advt The larger question is whether NPCI can translate its experience in building public digital infrastructure into a broader technology business. Its established relationships with banks, financial institutions and public-sector institutions could provide an entry point, but success in AI and blockchain will require capabilities and applications beyond those developed for payment processing.For now, the expansion signals an effort to broaden NPCI's technology role. Whether it can establish itself as a significant technology provider beyond payments remains to be seen.
