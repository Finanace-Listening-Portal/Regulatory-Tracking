} } Listen to This ArticleState-owned Bank of India (BoI) and Suryoday Small Finance Bank have raised interest rates on fixed deposits (FDs), offering higher returns across select maturities, following the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) shifting towards monetary tightening after its first repo rate increase in nearly four years. BoI has raised interest rates on deposits of less than ₹3 crore and those between ₹3 crore and ₹10 crore across select maturities of one to less than three years, effective November 2. It has also introduced a special 500-day deposit scheme, offering an interest rate of up to 6.85 per cent for general customers, 7.35 per cent for senior citizens and 7.5 per cent for super senior citizens. For deposits below ₹3 crore, the bank has raised the rate on one-year deposits to 6.6 per cent and on deposits with maturities of more than one year to less than three years to 6.7 per cent. Senior citizens will get an additional 50 basis points (bps), while super senior citizens will receive an additional 65 bps on deposits with maturities of 180 days to less than three years. For deposits of ₹3 crore to less than ₹10 crore, the revised rates stand at 6.55 per cent for one-year deposits and 6.65 per cent for maturities of more than one year to less than three years. The bank is also offering an additional 15 bps on non-callable deposits of above ₹1 crore for tenures of one to three years. Separately, Suryoday Small Finance Bank has introduced special festive-period FD rates, offering up to 8.25 per cent to general customers and 8.6 per cent to senior citizens on five-year deposits of less than ₹3 crore. The rates translate into annualised yields of 8.51 per cent and 8.88 per cent, respectively. The lender is also offering 8.1 per cent to general customers and 8.25 per cent to senior citizens on 30-month deposits. For 18-month deposits, the rates are 8.01 per cent and 8.16 per cent, respectively. The special rates are applicable from October 30 to November 16. The increase in deposit rates comes against the backdrop of a shift in the RBI's monetary policy stance. In its October policy review, the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) raised the benchmark repo rate by 25 bps to 5.5 per cent, the first increase in nearly four years, and shifted its stance from neutral to calibrated tightening. The change in stance signals that rate cuts are off the table in the near term, with the possibility of further increases or a pause depending on the inflation and growth outlook. RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra has said the extent and duration of the rate-hike cycle would depend on the trajectory of underlying inflation, the broadening of price pressures and the extent of second-round effects from supply-side shocks.
