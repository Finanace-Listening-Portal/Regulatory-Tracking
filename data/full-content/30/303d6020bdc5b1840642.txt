Justice Girish Kathpalia, by an oral judgment dated 17 November 2026, held that the gravity of the allegations, the applicant’s dominant official position, the alleged involvement of his immediate subordinate in accepting a bribe and the recovery of substantial unexplained cash did not justify granting bail at that stage. The application arose from RC No. 0032026A0026, registered by the Anti Corruption Branch of the CBI, New Delhi, for offences under Section 61(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, and Section 7 of the Prevention of Corruption Act. The prosecution case originated from a written complaint dated 21 April 2026 alleging that five officials of the Anti Narcotics Cell, Dwarka, entered the complainant’s residence and conducted an unauthorised search. According to the complaint, the officials brought a bag from their vehicle, placed it inside the house, claimed that it contained ganja and threatened to falsely implicate the complainant in a case under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act. Head Constable Ajay Kumar allegedly demanded ₹25 lakh to avoid registration of a criminal case. When the complainant expressed her inability to pay, one official allegedly contacted a person named Subhash, identified by the prosecution as the applicant, following which the demand was reduced to ₹15 lakh, with ₹5 lakh payable immediately. For verification, the complainant made a WhatsApp call to Head Constable Ajay Kumar in the presence of a CBI verifying officer and two independent witnesses. During the recorded conversation, she stated that she could arrange only ₹2 lakh towards the demanded amount. Ajay Kumar allegedly instructed her to deliver that sum the same evening. A trap was subsequently organised at the Anti Narcotics Cell office in Dwarka on 21 April 2026 at approximately 7:07 p.m. According to the prosecution, Ajay Kumar demanded and accepted ₹2 lakh and was caught red-handed. The recovered currency matched the trap money, and the phenolphthalein hand-wash test returned positive results, subsequently confirmed by the Central Forensic Science Laboratory. The prosecution further relied upon the recorded trap proceedings in which Ajay Kumar referred to having spoken with Subhash Sahab, while the complainant expressed fear of Subhash. During the search of the Anti Narcotics Cell premises, investigators recovered ₹48,87,600 in unaccounted cash from an area adjoining and immediately below the window of the applicant’s restroom. A currency-counting machine was also found in his official room. The CBI maintained that the applicant had not satisfactorily explained either the cash recovery or the presence of the machine. The applicant disputed the allegations and contended that he had been falsely implicated to discourage officers from taking action against persons allegedly involved in narcotics and excise offences. His counsel argued that criminal law does not recognise vicarious liability merely because a subordinate demanded or accepted a bribe. It was further submitted that the cash had been recovered from a publicly accessible place rather than from the applicant personally. The defence questioned the investigation because the recovery had not been videographed and no seizure memorandum had been prepared concerning the currency-counting machine. Opposing bail, the CBI submitted that the charges had not yet been framed and that the seriousness of the alleged corruption warranted continued custody. The prosecution asserted that the cash was recovered from the window area of a restroom attached to the applicant’s official room, which was not accessible to the general public. It also alleged that investigators discovered a dummy CCTV camera in the applicant’s office that had been used for extortion. The CBI further claimed that the applicant had destroyed his Samsung mobile phone and misled investigators by saying that he had thrown it into a drain. The defence responded that the applicant had already handed over his Apple phone and alleged that investigators had taken the Samsung phone without issuing any receipt. Examining the material, the High Court emphasised that the applicant was heading the Anti Narcotics Cell when his immediate subordinate was caught accepting a bribe allegedly demanded on the applicant’s instructions. The Court considered the repeated references to the applicant during the trap proceedings significant while assessing the gravity of the offence. It also rejected, for bail purposes, the argument that the ₹48,87,600 had been recovered from a publicly accessible location. The chargesheet material, including the Duty Officer’s statement, indicated that the restroom was attached to the applicant’s official room and was intended for his exclusive use. The Court additionally considered that the CBI had registered a separate disproportionate-assets case against the applicant following further investigation. It also noted the CBI’s discovery of an alleged earlier demand of ₹20 lakh on 18 March 2026 concerning the detention of the complainant’s daughter under the NDPS Act. Regarding the missing Samsung mobile phone, the Court found the applicant’s explanation unconvincing, observing that it was difficult to accept that a senior police officer would surrender his phone to an investigating officer without obtaining any acknowledgment or receipt. The Court also recorded that co-accused Head Constable Ajay Kumar had not been granted bail. Considering these circumstances collectively, particularly the applicant’s influential position as head of the Anti Narcotics Cell and the alleged extortion committed through a subordinate, the Delhi High Court held that the case was not fit for grant of regular bail. The application was accordingly dismissed, with a direction to transmit the order immediately to the concerned Jail Superintendent for informing the applicant. Importantly, the Court expressly clarified that its observations were confined to deciding the bail application and that the trial court must independently evaluate the evidence. The judgment therefore represents a refusal of bail based on the material available at that stage, rather than a final determination of criminal guilt. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF DELHI HIGH COURT 1. The accused/applicant seeks regular bail in case RC No. 0032026A0026, registered by Anti Corruption Branch of CBI, New Delhi for offence under Section 61(2), BNS and Section 7, Prevention of Corruption Act. 2. Broadly speaking, the accused/applicant, being head of the Narcotics Cell of the Delhi Police was allegedly involved in extortion racket with the help of his subordinates. 2. 1 The relevant factual matrix as per prosecution case is as follows. On the basis of a written complaint dated 21.04.2026 lodged by the complainant de facto, the CBI swung into action. In the said complaint, it was alleged that five officials of Anti Narcotics Cell, Dwarka came to the residence of the complainant de facto and despite her protests began search of the house without any authority; that they took out a bag from their own vehicle and placed the same inside her house claiming it to contain ganja and threatened to falsely implicate her in a case under NDPS Act; that one of the those police officials namely, HC Ajay Kumar told her that if she paid Rs. 25,00,000/-, no case would be registered against her and when she expressed inability, one of those officials made a telephone call to a person named Subhash (allegedly the present accused/applicant) and thereafter, they reduced the demand to Rs.15,00,000/-, out of which Rs.5,00,000/- to be paid on 21.04.2026. 2.2 For verification of the complaint, a WhatsApp call on speaker mode was made by the complainant de facto to HC Ajay Kumar in presence of the verifying officer and two independent witnesses. During that phone call, the complainant de facto told HC Ajay Kumar that she had been able to arrange only Rs.2,00,000/- towards part payment of bribe amount, which he instructed her to deliver on the same evening. The said telephone call was recorded and after verification of the demand, a trap was laid. 2.3 On 21.04.2026 at about 07:07pm at the office of Anti Narcotics Cell Dwarka, the trap laying officer Inspector Satyavir in presence of two independent witnesses, the complainant de facto and witness of the verification proceedings, laid the trap. 2.4 During the trap, HC Ajay Kumar demanded and accepted the bribe of Rs.2,00,000/- from the complainant de facto and was caught red handed. The currency recovered from HC Ajay Kumar tallied with the currency that had been used for trap. Both hands of the HC Ajay Kumar on being washed turned pink, thereby confirming his having handled the phenolphthalein treated notes and the said report returned positive from CFSL. Contemporaneous to the transaction, the trap proceedings were captured and recorded, during which HC Ajay Kumar, at the time of accepting the bribe stated: “abhi Subhash Sahab se baat hui hai” and the complainant de facto stated: “mereko darr lagta hai…. Subhash sir se darr lagta hai”. 2.5 During the search of the premises of Anti Narcotics Cell in presence of both independent witnesses, an unaccounted cash amount of Rs.48,87,600/- was recovered from the area adjacent to the an immediately below window of the restroom of the accused/applicant regarding which no explanation came from the accused/applicant. Further, in the official room of the accused/applicant, currency counting machine was found, but for that also no explanation was advanced. 3. Against the above backdrop, it is contended by learned counsel for accused/applicant that he has been falsely implicated in order to discourage the other officials of Narcotics Cell, as the complainant de facto and her family members are involved in a number of cases under the NDPS Act and under the Excise Act. It is contended by learned counsel that the criminal law does not recognize vicarious liability, so for the bribe demanded and accepted by HC Ajay Kumar, no liability can be foisted on the present accused/applicant. Learned counsel for accused/applicant contends that the amount of Rs. 48,87,600/- was recovered from a public place and not from person of the accused/applicant, so that recovery is of no consequence. It is also contended that since no videography of the alleged recovery of money was carried out, the investigation was faulty. Learned counsel for accused/applicant also contends that since no seizure memo of the currency counting machine was prepared, that fact raises doubts about truthfulness of prosecution case. 4. Per contra, learned SPP for CBI strongly opposes the bail application on the ground that the framing of charges is yet to be considered by the learned trial court, so this is not an appropriate stage, looking into the gravity of the offence. It is contended that the recovery of Rs. 48,87,600/- was not from a public place but from window of the restroom attached with the official room of the accused/applicant and the place of the alleged recovery is not accessible to anyone except the accused/applicant. It is also contended that during the trap proceedings, the investigating officer also found a dummy CCTV Camera in the official room of the accused/applicant, which was being used to extort money from the victims. Further, it is submitted that the accused/applicant even destroyed his mobile phone and tried to mislead the investigating officer by stating that he had thrown the mobile phone in a drain, so the same could not be recovered. As regards the seizure memo pertaining to the currency counting machine, it is submitted by learned SPP that since the same was not seized, there was no purpose to prepare any seizure memo. 5. In rebuttal, learned counsel for accused/applicant submits that the accused/applicant had handed over his Apple phone to the Investigating Officer, so there was no reason for him not to hand over his Samsung phone as well. It is also contended that even the Samsung phone of the accused/applicant had been taken away by the Investigating Officer during the trap proceedings itself, though without issuing any memo or receipt. 6. To begin with, it has to be kept in mind that at the relevant time, the accused/applicant was heading the Anti Narcotics Cell, Dwarka and his immediate subordinate was caught red handed while accepting bribe that had been demanded by him through HC Ajay Kumar. As mentioned above, the investigation also reveals that in the course of trap proceedings, there was repeated reference to the accused/applicant by name. That being so, the gravity of offence becomes significantly vital in the present case. 7. Then comes the recovery of Rs.48,87,600/- attributed to the accused/applicant, which money has not been explained by the accused/applicant. The argument advanced on behalf of the accused/applicant that the said amount was recovered from a place accessible to public does not convince in view of the material on record and forming part of the chargesheet that the said place was accessible only to the accused/applicant. According to the same, the restroom from window whereof the money was recovered was attached to the office room of the accused/applicant, key whereof remained with the Duty Officer for being used by the accused/applicant only, according to statement of the Duty Officer. 8. It would also be significant to note that consequent upon further detailed investigation by the CBI, a case for disproportionate assets also stands registered against the accused/applicant, which also significantly adds to the gravity of the offence alleged against the accused/applicant in view of his official position. 9. Further, the CBI also found an antecedent demand of Rs.20,00,000/- made by the accused/applicant on 18.03.2026 in connection with the detention of daughter of the complainant de facto under the NDPS Act. 10. So far as the alleged destruction of Samsung mobile phone is concerned, I do not find any convincing explanation from the side of the accused/applicant. I find it unbelievable that the accused/applicant despite being such a senior police officer would be so gullible that he would handover his mobile phone to the Investigating Officer without any receipt or memo issued by the Investigating Officer. 11. The co-accused HC Ajay Kumar also has not been granted bail till date and he, as mentioned above, being subordinate of the present accused/applicant was acting on his instructions according to the CBI. 12. At this stage, I must also add a cautious rider that the above observations have been recorded for the limited purposes of analyzing the rival arguments for consideration of bail. The learned trial court shall take an independent view on the basis of material on record. 13. Considering the overall circumstances described above, especially the dominant and powerful position held by the accused/applicant as head of the Anti Narcotics Cell and the extortion allegedly committed at his behest by his subordinate Head Constable, I do not find it a fit case to grant bail to the accused/applicant at this stage. 14. Therefore, the bail application is dismissed. 15. A copy of this order be immediately transmitted to the concerned Jail Superintendent for informing the accused/applicant.
