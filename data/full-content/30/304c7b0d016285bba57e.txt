The acceleration was particularly sharp compared with the 3.7% growth recorded in bank lending to NBFCs in the corresponding period last year. NBFCs accounted for nearly 35% of outstanding bank credit to the services sector, which grew 28.3% year on year to Rs 64.9 lakh crore.Lending to public financial institutions increased 79.7%, while credit to housing finance companies grew 19.7%. Overall bank credit, including food and non-food credit, rose 19.4% year on year to Rs 229.95 lakh crore as of September 30. Advt The sharp increase in bank funding to NBFCs highlights the growing importance of non-bank lenders in the credit transmission mechanism. NBFCs borrow from banks and capital markets to finance their lending operations, extending credit to retail borrowers, small businesses and other underserved segments. This allows banks to gain exposure to customer segments through intermediaries, while NBFCs use their distribution networks and specialised lending capabilities to expand credit access.Interdependence under cloudHowever, the growing interdependence between banks and NBFCs also raises questions about the concentration of credit risk within the financial system. Any deterioration in the asset quality of NBFCs could affect their repayment capacity and transmit stress to banks that finance them. The sharp increase in bank lending also raises the question of how much of the funding is supporting incremental lending and how much is being used to refinance existing liabilities or replace market borrowings.The acceleration in NBFC lending comes alongside strong growth in other services segments. Bank credit to computer software companies increased 45.8% year on year, while lending to aviation grew 42.7%. Trade credit expanded 23.9% and commercial real estate lending rose 23.3%. Services sector credit growth accelerated from 9.9% in September last year to 28.3% this September.Overall non-food bank credit grew 19.1% in September, compared with 11.2% in the corresponding period last year. Industrial credit increased 19.5% to Rs 49.77 lakh crore, led by growth in medium enterprises, while personal loans expanded 17.3% to Rs 74.27 lakh crore. Credit to agriculture and allied activities also grew 19.5% to Rs 28.19 lakh crore.
