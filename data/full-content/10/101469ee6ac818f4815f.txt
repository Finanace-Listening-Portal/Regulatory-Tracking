ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Oct 1, 2026 at 08:08 AM IST var news_msid = 134606580; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Shriram Finance is targeting a 20-25% share of new-vehicle financing in its disbursements over the next two to three years, as the company broadens its growth beyond its traditional commercial vehicle (CV) franchise. New-vehicle financing currently accounts for around 16-17% of disbursements, up from about 10% earlier.Management is also targeting a new-vehicle financing mix of around 30% over the longer term, while expanding its MSME and gold loan businesses, according to Motilal Oswal Financial Services. The brokerage expects Shriram Finance's AUM to grow at a compound annual rate of around 18% over FY26-28, supported by multiple growth engines. Advt CV financing remains the company's core growth engine, but MSME and gold are expected to become increasingly important contributors. Shriram Finance is targeting an increase in MSME's share of AUM to around 20% from about 13-14% currently, while gold loans are expected to account for around 5% of AUM over the next three years.The expansion in new-vehicle financing is also expected to help the company deepen relationships with its existing customer base. Borrowers can graduate from used CV financing to new-vehicle financing within the Shriram ecosystem, while the addition of MSME and gold products could increase customer retention and wallet share.Lower funding costs are expected to support the expansion. Historically, relatively high funding costs had constrained Shriram Finance's ability to compete aggressively in new-vehicle financing. The company's partnership with MUFG, which holds a roughly 20% strategic stake, is expected to improve its capital position and funding economics.Shriram Finance expects borrowing costs to decline by around 1 percentage point over the next two to three years, supported by potential credit-rating upgrades, liability repricing, lower deposit rates and improved access to debt capital markets. Management has indicated that the existing capital base can support growth for the next four to five years, reducing the near-term need for another large primary capital raise. Advt MUFG dealThe MUFG relationship could also open opportunities with Japanese corporates including Suzuki, Toyota, Honda, Yamaha, Denso and Hitachi. Potential areas include supply-chain finance, equipment financing, manufacturing and rural and semi-urban ecosystems, although these opportunities are still evolving.Shriram Finance continues to target around 18% AUM growth in FY27, although second-quarter growth could remain at around 15-16% year-on-year amid macroeconomic uncertainties. The company expects growth to increasingly come from multiple businesses rather than being dependent on a single product category. Advt Asset quality remains a key monitorable. Stage 2 and Stage 3 trends have remained broadly stable despite seasonal weakness, but weak monsoons, elevated fuel prices and potential El Niño conditions could put pressure on rural and transporter cash flows and, in turn, repayment behaviour.Lower funding costs and operating leverage are expected to support profitability. The report estimates net interest margin at around 9.2% in FY27 and 9.1% in FY28, while the cost-to-income ratio is expected to decline from around 30% in FY26 to about 26% by FY28. Profit after tax is estimated to grow at around 29% annually over FY26-28.
