Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Vivek Jajodia Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Section 148 Notice Issued by an ITO Is Invalid Where Returned Income Exceeds the Applicable ₹20 Lakh Jurisdictional Limit Returned Income Placed the Case Within Circle Jurisdiction The Delhi ITAT quashed a reassessment because the ITO who issued the notice under section 148 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 lacked pecuniary jurisdiction. The assessee had declared income of ₹20,77,420, above the ₹20 lakh limit assigned to ITOs for non-corporate returns in metro charges under CBDT Instruction No. 1/2011. The Tribunal held that jurisdiction lay with the Circle officer, and that the defect in initiating reassessment was incurable. It allowed the appeal on the legal grounds and left the grounds on merits open. Reassessment Concerned an Alleged ₹30 Lakh Accommodation Entry The assessee filed his original return for AY 2018–19 on 26 September 2018. Reassessment proceedings followed information flagging a transaction of ₹30 lakh with Kasera Agency Pvt. Ltd. as a high-risk accommodation entry. The ITO, Ward 35(1), New Delhi, passed the order under section 148A(d) and issued the notice under section 148 on 2 April 2022. The reassessment resulted in an addition of ₹30 lakh under section 68 read with section 115BBE, along with disallowance of ₹2,17,479 in interest on the unsecured loan. The assessee challenged those adjustments and the validity of reassessment before the Tribunal after the CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, passed its order on 8 April 2026. At the hearing, the assessee pressed only the legal grounds. CBDT Instruction Fixed the Monetary Limit for Delhi The Tribunal reproduced CBDT Instruction No. 1/2011 dated 31 January 2011, applicable from 1 April 2011. For non-corporate returns in metro charges, the instruction allocated cases with declared income up to ₹20 lakh to ITOs and those above ₹20 lakh to Assistant or Deputy Commissioners. Delhi was expressly included among the metro charges. The returned income of ₹20,77,420 was evident from the original return and was also recorded in the section 148A(d) order and the reassessment order. The Tribunal accordingly held that the case belonged to the Circle. The figure relevant to that allocation was the income declared in the return. The alleged ₹30 lakh accommodation entry was the subject of the proposed reassessment and served a different purpose. Earlier Decisions Treated the Defect as Incurable The Tribunal followed Om Prakash Bansal (HUF) v. ACIT, ITA No. 2984/Del/2024, where a coordinate Bench had examined the validity of a scrutiny notice issued by an ITO despite the returned income placing the case within Circle jurisdiction. That decision had relied on YKM Holdings Pvt. Ltd. v. ACIT, ITA No. 1020/Del/2019. The reasoning reproduced in the present order treated a foundational notice issued beyond the officer’s monetary authority as an inherent jurisdictional defect. It also distinguished an objection to pecuniary competence from the objection to territorial jurisdiction discussed under section 124(3). The Tribunal also drew on Ashok Devichand Jain v. Union of India (2023) 452 ITR 43 (Bom.). The Bombay High Court had set aside a section 148 notice issued by an ITO where the relevant monetary allocation placed the case with an Assistant or Deputy Commissioner. The fact that the assessee’s PAN remained with the ITO’s ward did not cure the lack of authority to issue the notice. The Delhi ITAT applied the same principle to the initiation of reassessment in Jajodia’s case. Reassessment Quashed and Loan Dispute Left Open The assessee had also challenged the approval under section 151 and argued that the information underlying the notice misstated the amount received during the year. According to him, only ₹20 lakh was received during the relevant year, while ₹10 lakh represented an opening balance. The Tribunal’s operative reasoning rested on the ITO’s lack of pecuniary jurisdiction, which was sufficient to quash the reassessment. The Tribunal allowed the appeal and left the grounds on merits open. The genuineness of the loan, the effect of its claimed repayment and the deductibility of the related interest were therefore left undecided. Author’s Comments The documents that resolved the jurisdictional issue were the original return, the CBDT instruction applicable to the assessee’s category and charge, and the notice identifying the issuing officer. Here, the section 148A(d) order itself recorded the returned income, making the factual comparison straightforward. The ruling is particularly relevant where the returned income only marginally exceeds the assigned limit. In this case, an excess of ₹77,420 placed the assessee within the Circle’s jurisdiction and determined whether the Ward officer could validly initiate reassessment. On facts and circumstances of the case, the authorities below have erred in upholding the reassessment proceedings ignoring the fact that the same has been initiated after taking approval from incorrect authority u/s 151 of IT Act and therefore, the reassessment proceedings need to be deleted. 3. On facts and circumstances of the case, the authorities below have erred in upholding the reassessment proceedings ignoring the fact that impugned assessment is invalid and without jurisdiction as the said assessment is completed without complying with legal requirements of the provisions of section 147/l48/151/148A/149 of the Income Tax Act therefore such assessment is void ab initio and liable to Be quashed. 4. At the time of hearing, Ld. AR contested only the legal issues raised in the Grounds No.1 to 3. The submissions of the assessee are reproduced below:- “Ground No.1 Initiation of proceedings u/s 148 without jurisdiction and completion of assessment by the authority having no jurisdiction. In the present case, the proceedings u/s 148 dated 02.04.2022(PB 29) were initiated by ITO, Ward 35(1), New Delhi, i.e. AO, who did not have jurisdiction over the appellant. As per the ITR available for AY 2018-19 filed on 26.09.2018 (PB 1), the returned income of the appellant is Rs.20,77,420/- and this fact is mentioned in para 2 of the order u/s 148A(d) dated 02.04.2022 (PB 26-28) and also in the assessment order in para 1 (PB 190). Therefore, the jurisdiction over the appellant was with the Circle and not with the above Ward officer. The above limit of income for non-corporate charge is fixed as per CBDT Instruction No.1/2011 F.No.187/12/2010-IT(A-I) dt: 03.01.2011 wherein it was specified that if the returned income of the non-corporate assessee exceeds Rs.20 lakh then the jurisdiction over that case will be with the Circle only and not with ITO. Hence, the reassessment proceedings in the present case are to be treated as being initiated by a non-jurisdictional assessing officer and consequently, the reassessment proceedings and reassessment order both need to be quashed. The co-ordinate Bench of Hon’ble ITAT in the case of YKM Holdings P Ltd vs ACIT ITA No.1020/Del/2019 dated 29.04.2024, Sh Vipul Mittal vs DCIT ITA No.2850/Del/2019 dated 15.01.2025, Vertex Consultants P Ltd vs DCIT ITA No.2617/Del/2026, Om Parkash Bansal HUF vs ACIT ITA No.2984/Del/2024, Balraj Sharma vs ITO ITA No.4370/Del/2025 dated 03.03.2026, New Star System Solution P Ltd vs DCIT ITA No.3585/Del/2025 dated 21.01.2026, Ketan Tokershi Shah vs DCIT ITA No.1107/Mum/2023, ITO vs M/s Watermark Systems (I) P Ltd 2023 (2) TMI 1348-ITAT Mum and Monarch & Qureshi Builders vs ACIT 110 ITR (Trib) 76 (ITAT Mum) has quashed the assessment proceedings on identical grounds relying on decision of Bombay High Court in the case of Ashok Devichand Jain vs UOI 452 ITR 43 (Bom) & Hon’ble Calcutta High Court in the PCIT vs M/s Shree Stoppers ltd 468 ITR 18 (Cal) and has also rejected the revenue objection of applicability of sec 124(3)/292BB of IT Act. It is a settled law that the assessment proceedings should be initiated by the authority having jurisdiction over an assessee. The Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of CIT vs. Lalitkumar Bardia (2017) 84 taxmann.com 213 (Bombay)) has held that mere participation in proceedings or acquiescence would not confer jurisdiction upon the Assessing Officer who otherwise was not the Assessing Officer of the assessee. The Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Kanwar Singh Saini vs Delhi High Court (2012) 4 SCC 307 (SC) in Para 13 has held that there can be no dispute regarding the settled legal proposition that conferment of jurisdiction is a legislative function and it can neither be conferred with the consent of the party nor by a superior court. Ground Nos.2 and 3: Assumption of reassessment proceedings in violation of provisions of sec 147/148/148A/151/149 of IT Act. A. Non-Application of mind The foundational reason for initiation of proceedings u/s 148A was that the appellant had been a beneficiary of a so-called accommodation entry of Rs.30,00,000/- from M/s Kasera Agency P Ltd (“Kasera”) which was highlighted as a high-risk transaction in the CRIU/VRU category in the line of the high-risk management strategy formulated by CBDT. The above entry has been identified as related to the bank account of the above-named Kasera maintained with Union Bank of India vide Account No.552401010050232. These details are mentioned in the notice u/s 148A(b) (PB 19-21)and also in the order u/s 148A(d) dt: 02.04.2022(PB 26-28).The above information based on the presumption that the transaction of Rs.30,00,000/- has taken place during the assessment year 2018-19 which is factually incorrect. The appellant, vide e-submission dated 24.03.2022(PB 24-25) had explained that during the year under consideration only Rs.20,00,000/- has been received and the balance Rs.10,00,000/- represents opening balance due as on the opening date of the financial year. The Ld AO takes the above explanation in para 4 of the order u/s 148A(d) on record along with the information that the loan has been repaid during FY 2019-20 along with interest. Despite the above explanation brought on record, the Ld AO, without contradicting the above factual submission, opted to reopen the case based on the transaction of Rs.30,00,000/- as if it pertains to the AY under consideration, without bringing any material on record to dispute the factual position highlighted in the submission made by the appellant. It is brought to our notice that proceedings u/s 148 dated 02.04.2022 were initiated by ITO, Ward 35 (1), New Delhi, who did not have jurisdiction over the assessee in the case considering the fact that the return of income declared by the assessee is over and above Rs.20 lakhs. Therefore, the jurisdiction over the assessee was with the Circle and not with the above Ward officer. We observed that the above limit of income for non-corporate charge is fixed as per CBDT Instruction No.1/2011 F.No.187/12/2010-IT(A- I) dt: 03.01.2011 wherein it was specified that if the returned income of the non-corporate assessee exceeds Rs.20 lakh then the jurisdiction over that case will be with the Circle only and not with ITO. Hence, we observed that the reassessment proceedings in the present case are to be treated as being initiated by a non-jurisdictional assessing officer and consequently, the reassessment proceedings and reassessment order are to be quashed. 9. In the instant case, the notice under section 143(2) of the Act stood issued to the assessee on 12.04.2016 by ITO Ward 27(4), Delhi. Hence, it was argued that the notice under section 143(2) of the Act dated 12.04.2016 issued by the ITO selecting the return of assessee for A.Y. 2015-16 for scrutiny is without jurisdiction and consequently, the assessment framed under section 143(3) of the Act dated 14.12.2017 required to be quashed as void ab initio. When this was confronted to learned DR, he pointed out to the provisions of section 124(3) of the Act wherein it was mentioned that assessee should challenge within one month about the jurisdiction of the AO on receipt of the notice. In the instant case, nowhere up to learned CIT(A), the assessee has challenged the jurisdiction of the learned AO. In our considered opinion, this argument of the learned DR is wrong in as much as section 124(3) of the Act talks only about territorial jurisdiction, whereas the issue involved here is pecuniary jurisdiction. Further, the provisions of section 124(3) of the Act could be taken shelter by the Revenue only when legal valid notice under section 143(2) of the Act has been issued by the Revenue. In the instant case, notice issued under section 143(2) of the Act on 12.04.2016 by ITO is not legal as he did not possess jurisdiction over the assessee for A.Y. 2015-16 in as much as the returned income for A.Y. 2015- 16 had exceeded Rs. 30,00,000/-. We find that the issue in dispute is no longer res integra by the decision of Hon’ble Delhi High Court in the case of Ashok Devichand Jain vs. UOI reported in 452 ITR 43 (Bom). In this case, very same issue was addressed in the light of CBDT Instruction No.1/2011[F. No.187/12/2010-IT(A-I)] Dated 31.01.2011. For the sake of convenience, the entire order is reproduced hereunder: “1. Petitioner is impugning a notice dated 30th March, 2019 issued under section 148 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (the Act) for A.Y. 2012-13 and order passed on 18th November, 2019 rejecting Petitioner’s objection to reopening on various grounds. 2. The primary ground that has been raised is that the Income Tax Officer who issued the notice under section 148 of the Act, had no jurisdiction to issue such notice. Said Mr. Kamble, ITO, Ward 12(3)(1), Mumbai admits that such a defective notice has been issued but according to him, PAN of Petitioner was lying with ITO Ward (12)(3)(1), Mumbai and it was not feasible to migrate the PAN having returned of income exceeding Rs. 30 lakhs to the charge of DCIT, Circle 12(3)(1), Mumbai, as the time available with the ITO 12(3)(1) was too short to migrate the PAN after obtaining administrative approval from the higher authorities by 31st March, 2019. 5. The notice under section 148 of the Act is jurisdictional notice and any inherent defect therein is not curable. In view of the aforesaid observations and respectfully following the judicial precedent relied upon hereinabove, we have no hesitation to hold that the assessment framed under section 143(3) of the Act deserves to be quashed in the instant case as the initial scrutiny notice issued under section 143(3) of the Act dated 12.04.2016 by ITO was without jurisdiction as he did not possess jurisdiction over the assessee for the A.Y. 2015-16. Consequently, assessment framed under section 143(3) of the Act is hereby quashed as void ab initio. The additional ground no.2 is hereby allowed.” 13. Since the issue of notice u/s 143(2) is the basis of initiation of the assessment u/s 143(3) and the jurisdictional officer should have issued the notice and also completed the assessment. The present Assessing Officer has completed the assessment without following the due process of law and we, respectfully following the decisions of the coordinate Bench and ITAT Mumbai, are inclined to hold that the jurisdictional notice u/s 143(2) was not issued by the DCIT before completing the assessment u/s 143(3) of the Act and that there is an unwarranted defect in this case which is not curable. Accordingly, the assessment passed in the given case is quashed and accordingly, the additional grounds raised by the assessee are allowed.” 15. In view of our above findings and respectfully following the aforesaid decision, we hold that the present Assessing Officer has completed the assessment without following the due process of law and there is an unwarranted defect in this case which is not curable. Accordingly, the assessment passed in the given case is quashed and accordingly, the legal grounds raised by the assessee are allowed. 16. The assessee has raised several grounds on merits as well. Since we have already decided the legal grounds in favour of the assessee, we are inclined to keep the other grounds of appeal open at this stage. 17. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed as above.
