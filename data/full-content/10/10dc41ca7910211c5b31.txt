September 30, 2022: RBI raises repo rate to 5.9% The RBI raised the repo rate by another 50 bps to 5.9 per cent on September 30, 2022. This came after the central bank had already raised rates by 90 bps in May and June and another 50 bps in August. The RBI’s concern at the time was that inflationary pressures were proving more persistent than expected. The central bank was also dealing with a sharp tightening of global financial conditions, a strong US dollar and capital outflows from emerging markets. The RBI said domestic economic activity was showing resilience, but inflation remained the bigger concern. It maintained that monetary policy had to continue moving towards withdrawal of accommodation to bring inflation back within the target while supporting growth. August 5, 2022: RBI says inflation remains uncomfortably high On August 5, 2022, the MPC raised the repo rate by 50 bps to 5.4 per cent. It also retained its focus on withdrawing accommodation to ensure inflation returned towards the target while supporting growth. The RBI said consumer price inflation had eased from its April peak but remained “uncomfortably high” and above the upper threshold of its target. It noted that inflationary pressures had become broad-based and core inflation remained elevated. The central bank also warned that inflation was expected to remain above the 6 per cent upper tolerance level through the first three quarters of 2022-23. Sustained high inflation, it said, could destabilise inflation expectations and trigger second-round effects. Against this backdrop, the MPC said further calibrated withdrawal of monetary accommodation was needed to contain inflationary pressures, bring headline inflation back within the tolerance band and keep inflation expectations anchored. At the same time, the RBI said domestic economic activity remained resilient, with improving urban demand and signs of a recovery in rural demand. June 8, 2022: RBI steps up pace of tightening The June 2022 meeting marked another 50-bps rate hike, taking the repo rate to 4.9 per cent. The MPC unanimously decided to remain focused on withdrawing accommodation to ensure inflation stayed within the target while supporting growth. The RBI said the Russia-Ukraine war and the resulting sanctions had kept global commodity prices elevated, putting sustained pressure on consumer inflation. It also warned that faster monetary tightening by major advanced economies was creating volatility in global financial markets, putting pressure on emerging-market currencies and increasing capital outflows. The central bank said the “upside risks to inflation” identified in its earlier policies had materialised earlier than anticipated and with greater intensity. It noted that inflationary pressures had become broad-based and that there were growing signs of higher input costs being passed on to consumers. The RBI expected inflation to remain above the upper tolerance limit of 6 per cent through the first three quarters of 2022-23. The MPC therefore concluded that further monetary policy action was necessary to anchor inflation expectations and prevent second-round effects. It raised the repo rate by 50 bps to 4.9 per cent.
