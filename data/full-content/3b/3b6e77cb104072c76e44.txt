The central bank has issued four separate amendment notifications covering the respective categories of regulated entities, with the substantive changes remaining identical across all four. Advt Under the amended provisions, securitisation notes can only be issued, held and subsequently transferred in dematerialised form. RBI has also clarified that the minimum ticket size of Rs 1 crore will apply at the time of issuance as well as at any subsequent transfer of the notes.Ticket size, under the directions, refers to the size of investment by a single investor.The amendment also places responsibility on the originator to ensure that its agreement with the Special Purpose Entity (SPE) requires the latter to put in place necessary arrangements with entities such as registrars, depositories and stock exchanges, wherever applicable, to ensure compliance with the minimum ticket-size requirement.Exception for amortising securitisation notesRBI has provided an exception for securitisation notes with amortisation structures, allowing them to be traded at their amortised value even if the ticket size falls below Rs 1 crore.Amortisation structures involve the gradual repayment of principal over the life of the instrument, which can reduce the outstanding value of the investment over time.The exception allows such notes to be traded at their outstanding amortised value even when the remaining investment falls below the prescribed minimum threshold.Public offers to comply with SEBI regulations Advt Separately, RBI has replaced the existing provision on issuance and listing of securitisation notes with a requirement that public offers comply with Regulation 21 of the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Issue and Listing of Securitised Debt Instruments and Security Receipts) Regulations, 2008, as amended from time to time.The revised provision applies to public offers of securitisation notes and brings the issuance process within the applicable SEBI regulatory framework.The November 2025 directions had recommended listing of securitisation notes, particularly for certain product classes such as residential mortgage-backed securities (RMBS), and for issuances above specified thresholds. They also required listing where notes were offered to 50 or more persons in an issuance. Advt Existing issuances also coveredIn another significant provision, RBI has clarified that transactions in securitisation notes issued before April 1, 2027, will also have to comply with the revised requirements on dematerialisation, minimum ticket size and public offers.This means the changes will not be restricted to fresh issuances made after the effective date. Subsequent transactions involving notes issued before April 2027 will also fall within the scope of the amended provisions.The amendments have been issued separately for commercial banks, small finance banks, NBFCs and AIFIs, modifying their respective 2025 securitisation transaction directions.
