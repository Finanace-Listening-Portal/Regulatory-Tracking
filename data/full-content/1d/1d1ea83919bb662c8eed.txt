Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Dattatreya Ghosh Vs ITO (ITAT Bangalore) Flat Received Earlier Cannot Be Taxed Under Section 56(2)(x) in Year of Registration: Bangalore ITAT Introduction Can registration of a conveyance deed trigger taxation under section 56(2)(x) when the assessee had already received possession of the flat in an earlier financial year? The Bangalore Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal answered this question in favour of the assessee in Dattatreya Ghosh v. ITO, ITA No. 1806/Bang/2026, Assessment Year 2020-21, decided on 5 October 2026. The Tribunal deleted an addition of ₹37,60,121, holding that the flat had already been received in financial year 2017-18. Mere registration of the conveyance deed during financial year 2019-20 did not justify taxing the difference between stamp duty value and consideration in that later year. Facts of the Case The assessee became a member of QUEST Cooperative Housing Society, Kolkata, in 2012. The society constructed eight flats on land acquired from the West Bengal Housing Board for allotment to its eight members. By an allotment letter dated 12 September 2012, the society allotted the assessee a first-floor flat together with a car parking space. Between 5 November 2012 and 6 February 2018, the assessee paid an aggregate amount of ₹22,00,879 in instalments towards construction of the flat. The New Town Kolkata Development Authority issued an occupancy certificate on 8 December 2017, certifying that the building was fit for occupation. Thereafter, the society issued a possession letter dated 15 December 2017 and handed over the flat and parking space to the assessee. However, the deed of assignment/conveyance was executed subsequently, on 17 September 2019. Reassessment and Addition by the Assessing Officer Based on information received under the e-Verification Scheme, reassessment proceedings were initiated concerning the property transaction. The assessee explained that the expenditure represented contributions towards construction of the allotted flat. He furnished the allotment letter, conveyance deed, share certificate, construction cost details and information concerning the source of payments. The Assessing Officer nevertheless observed that the property’s value reflected in the conveyance deed was ₹59,61,000, whereas the assessee had paid ₹22,00,879. Consequently, the difference of ₹37,60,121 was brought to tax under section 56(2)(x)(b) for Assessment Year 2020-21. Findings of the Commissioner (Appeals) The Commissioner (Appeals) sustained the addition, treating the difference between the stamp duty value and the consideration as taxable under the provision. The appellate authority also made a general observation that property transactions sometimes involve understatement of consideration, with part of the price being paid in cash. The assessee challenged the addition before the Tribunal, maintaining that the property had already been received in an earlier year and that registration in September 2019 merely completed the documentation. Assessee’s Principal Contention The assessee argued that receipt of the property, rather than subsequent registration alone, was material for identifying the relevant year under section 56(2)(x). Possession had been handed over on 15 December 2017, and the entire construction contribution had been paid by 6 February 2018. Accordingly, no immovable property was received during financial year 2019-20. The assessee also raised arguments concerning the cooperative society arrangement, mutuality and the nature of allotment under the applicable State cooperative legislation. However, the Tribunal’s operative reasoning centred on the year in which the property was received. Tribunal’s Analysis and Decision The Tribunal examined the statutory language, which applies where a person receives immovable property in a previous year, subject to the prescribed conditions. It found that the documentary record established a clear sequence: allotment in 2012, construction payments extending up to February 2018, the occupancy certificate in December 2017, and actual handing over of possession on 15 December 2017. Significantly, no material contrary to these documents had been brought on record. The Tribunal accepted that the assessee had already received the flat and parking space in 2017. It therefore held that, if at all the provision could apply, the relevant financial year was 2017-18, rather than financial year 2019-20. Since no immovable property was received in the year under consideration and only registration occurred in that year, the addition was deleted and the appeal was allowed. Author’s Comments The decision highlights that the correct year of taxation is an essential part of a valid addition. A difference between stamp duty value and consideration does not, by itself, establish that the difference is taxable in whichever year the conveyance happens to be registered. The ruling is particularly relevant to cooperative housing arrangements where allotment, construction contributions, possession and registration occur at different stages. Allotment letters, occupancy certificates, possession letters and bank statements can therefore assume decisive evidentiary importance. However, the decision must be applied within its factual limits. The Tribunal did not grant a blanket exemption to allotments by cooperative housing societies, nor did it conclusively determine whether any addition was otherwise sustainable for Assessment Year 2018-19. Its finding was that the impugned addition could not be sustained for Assessment Year 2020-21. The practical lesson is clear: before invoking section 56(2)(x), the authority must establish when the property was actually received, after examining the complete documentary record. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT BANGALORE The assessee has filed the present appeal against the impugned order dated 12/03/2026, passed under section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (“the Act”) by the learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi, [“learned CIT(A)”], for the assessment year 2020-21. 2. In this appeal, the assessee has raised the following grounds: – 1. Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax Appeals erred in law and on facts in confirming the assessment order dated 25.11.2024 passed in the Appellant s case for Assessment Year 2020 21 us 147 r.w.s 144B of the Income Tax Act,1961. 2. The Ld. CIT A has erred in law and on facts in ignoring the facts submitted by the Appellant that there was no transfer of immovable property between the QUEST Cooperative Housing Society Limited herein after referred to as QUEST Assignor Housing Society and the Appellant. 3. The Ld. CIT A has erred in law and on facts in understanding the facts of the case and considering the deed of assignment as transfer within the definition of section 45 and thereby confirming the Assessing officer action of invoking section 56 2 x b of Income Tax Act. 4. The Ld. CIT A has failed to understand the facts that the Appellant being a lawful member of the Housing Society, which was primarily established on Co operative basis, has only received the allotment of a house by a co operative housing society to the Appellant in compliance with its by laws. 5. The Ld. CIT A has erred in understanding the fact that the Assignor i.e., Housing Society is created under the principle of mutuality and the Appellant has contributed towards his share of cost of plot and construction cost of the residential flat bearing Flat No. A1 on the 1st Floor of the building at premises no. 04 0202 Plot No. CE 1 C 61, New Town, Kolkata hereinafter referred to as residential flat allotted to him and there is no transfer of residential flat took place by way of executing the Deed of Conveyance Assignment on 17.09.2019. 6. The Ld. CIT A was completely wrong in ignoring the submissions and supporting documents provided during the proceedings and coming to conclusion that the Appellant has paid consideration towards purchase of property whereas the Appellant being Bonafide member in the Housing Society and having contributed to the construction of residential flat as per the bye laws of the Society has only executed the Deed of conveyance Assignment for the purpose of revenue records and to confirm the right to occupy and to hold the residential flat allotted to him. 7. The Ld. CIT A was wrong in not appreciating the fact that as per Section 92 of the West Bengal Co operative Societies Act, 2006, the allotment of a house OR plot by a co operative housing society to its member in compliance with its bye laws is not a transfer of property but a confirmation of right to occupy and hold the property by the member. 8. The Ld. CIT A was failed to understand that the Appellant was already holding an interest in the residential flat through his membership in the Society and the Deed of conveyance Assignment is executed for the purpose of revenue records only. 9. The Ld. CIT A has erred in presuming that the difference between the market price of the residential flat and the amount contributed of Rs. 37,60,121 could have been paid by cash to the seller to cover up the undisclosed income of the purchaser and thus without applying his mind and has confirmed invoking provisions us 56 2 x b of the Act. 10. The Ld. CIT A erred in law and on facts in passing order without dealing with the issues raised in the appeal thus violating the principles of natural justice. 11. Without prejudice to the above grounds, the said residential flat has been handed over to the Appellant on 15.12.2017, i.e., relevant to AY 2018 19 and issued possession letter of the property by the Housing Society and therefore treating the difference between the market price of the residential flat and the amount contributed of Rs. 37,60,121 for the AY 2020 21 is incorrect. Any other ground to be raised with the leave of the Honorable Tribunal. 3. The solitary grievance of the assessee is against the addition made under section 56(2)(x) of the Act in respect of residential flat purchased by the assessee. 4. The brief facts of the case pertaining to this issue, as emanating from the record, are: The assessee is an individual and, for the year under consideration, filed his return of income on 08/09/2020, declaring a total income of ₹ 5,21,920. Based on information received under the e-Verification Scheme, 2021, that the assessee purchased immovable property for ₹ 22,00,879, proceedings under section 148A of the Act were initiated. After considering the submissions of the assessee, notice under section 148 of the Act was issued on 29/03/2024. In response to the said notice, the assessee filed his return of income on 01/05/2024, admitting total income of ₹ 5,21,920. In response to the statutory notices, the assessee submitted that he had invested a total sum of ₹ 22,00,879 towards construction of a flat purchased from QUEST Cooperative Housing Society, Kolkata, and there was no additional investment of ₹ 37,60,121 as stated in the statutory notice. It was further submitted that no undisclosed investment or income has escaped assessment in the financial year 2019-20. The assessee submitted that the aforementioned Housing Society allotted him one flat on the 1st floor, as per the allotment letter received from the West Bengal Housing Board. In support of its contention, the assessee submitted the copy of the allotment letter, conveyance deed and the share certificate of the flat, evidencing that the plot value of the flat was ₹ 1,08,000. The assessee also furnished the cost of construction breakup details and its source. 5. The Assessing Officer (“AO”), vide order dated 25/11/2024 passed under section 147 read with section 144B of the Act, disagreed with the submissions of the assessee and held that as per the conveyance deed, the market value of the flat is ₹ 59,61,000 and since the assessee has only paid an amount of ₹ 22,00,879, the differential amount of ₹ 37,60,121 is taxable in the hands of the assessee under section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act. 6. The learned CIT(A), vide impugned order, upheld the initiation of reassessment proceedings under section 148 of the Act. Further, the learned CIT(A) held that as per the provisions of section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act, there is a difference of ₹ 37,60,121 in the amount of stamp duty value of the property purchased by the assessee and the purchase consideration paid by the assessee as per the agreement, which is not acceptable. The learned CIT(A) held that many times, the purchase price is shown at a lesser value than the stamp duty value, as part of the price is paid in cash to the seller to cover up the undisclosed income of the purchaser, and therefore, in order to avoid the loss of revenue in such activities, section 56(2)(x) was inserted in the statute. Accordingly, the addition made by the AO regarding the difference in consideration between the amount paid for the purchase of property and the stamp duty value amounting to ₹ 37,60,121 under section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act was upheld by the learned CIT(A). Being aggrieved, the assessee is in appeal before us. 7. During the hearing, the learned Authorised Representative (“learned AR”), reiterating the submissions made before the Revenue authorities, submitted that the assessee was allotted one flat on the 1st floor by QUEST Cooperative Housing Society, which is registered by the Office of the Joint Registrar of Co-Operative Societies, Government of West Bengal. The learned AR submitted that, as per the bye-laws of the Housing Society, every member shall contribute such sum as may be deemed necessary by the Board for the purchase of the land and allotment of a plot and/or for the construction of a house for allotment to the members by the Housing Society. The learned AR submitted that the Housing Society had 8 members and on the plot of land allotted by the West Bengal Housing Board, it constructed 8 flats, out of which one flat on the 1st floor was allotted to the assessee vide allotment letter dated 12/09/2012. It was further submitted that after completion of the construction, the possession of the flat was handed over to the assessee on 15/12/2017. Thus, the learned AR submitted that the assessee received the property in the financial year 2017-18 and that only the deed of conveyance was registered in the year under consideration. It was also submitted that the assessee paid the entire consideration for the flat by February 2018. Therefore, the learned AR submitted that the provisions of section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act have no applicability in the year under consideration. 8. On the other hand, the learned Departmental Representative (“learned DR”) vehemently relied upon the order passed by the Revenue authorities. 9. We have considered the submissions of both sides and perused the material available on record. 10. Before proceeding further, it is relevant to analyse the provisions of section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act, which are relevant for the adjudication of the issue at hand. Section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act reads as follows: – “(2) In particular, and without prejudice to the generality of the provisions of sub-section (1), the following incomes, shall be chargeable to income-tax under the head “Income from other sources”, namely:— …….. (x) where any person receives, in any previous year, from any person or persons on or after the 1st day of April, 2017,— (b) any immovable property,— (A) without consideration, the stamp duty value of which exceeds fifty thousand rupees, the stamp duty value of such property; (B) for a consideration, the stamp duty value of such property as exceeds such consideration, if the amount of such excess is more than the higher of the following amounts, namely:— (i) the amount of fifty thousand rupees; and (ii) the amount equal to five per cent of the consideration:” 11. Therefore, as per the provisions of section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act, where any person receives any immovable property from any person or persons on or after 01.04.2017 either without consideration or for consideration, the stamp duty value of such property exceeding such consideration shall be considered as its income from other sources, if the amount of such excess is more than the amount mentioned in the section. 12. The primary contention of the assessee is that no addition can be made under section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act in the year under consideration, as he received the immovable property/flat in the financial year 2017-18 and only the deed of conveyance was registered in the year under consideration. On the other hand, as per the Revenue, since the deed of conveyance was registered in the year under consideration and the stamp duty value of the property was more than the consideration paid by the assessee, the differential amount is taxable under section 56(2)(x)(b) of the Act. 13. From the documents placed on record, we find that the assessee became a member of QUEST Cooperative Housing Society, Kolkata in the year 2012. On 12/09/2012, the Housing Society issued a letter allotting Flat No. A1 on the 1st floor and car parking on the ground floor in the building proposed to be constructed by the Housing Society on the land sold by the West Bengal Housing Board to the Housing Society. We find that from 05/11/2012 to 06/02/2018, the assessee paid ₹ 22,00,879 in various instalments towards construction of the flat. The assessee has placed on record its bank statement in support of this contention. We find that on 08/12/2017 the New Town Kolkata Development Authority granted an occupancy certificate to the Housing Society after finding that the building is fit for occupation. Thereafter, on 15/12/2017, the Housing Society issued a possession letter in respect of Flat No.A1 on the 1st floor and a car parking space on the ground floor to the assessee. On perusal of the possession letter dated 15/12/2017, placed at page 73 of the paper book, we find that the Housing Society intimated the assessee that the property is ready for possession and, accordingly, handed it over to the assessee on the same date. As per the assessee, the Statutory Auditor, Directorate of Co-Operative Audit, Government of West Bengal, conducted a statutory audit of the Housing Society to determine the total cost of constructing 8 apartments, including common areas and facilities. It is submitted that thereafter, the deed of assignment/conveyance was executed on 17/09/2019 between the Housing Society and the assessee only to confirm and maintain proper records regarding the property, while the transfer of the flat had already taken place in 2017. It is pertinent to note that no material contrary to the documents furnished by the assessee has been brought on record. 14. Therefore, from the material placed on record, we find merit in the submissions of the assessee that the immovable property/flat along with parking space was already received by the assessee in the year 2017 vide possession letter dated 15/12/2017, whereby the possession of the flat along with the parking space was handed over to the assessee. Thus, we agree with the submissions of the assessee that, if at all, only in the financial year 2017-18 can the provisions of section 56(2)(x) of the Act be applied in the present case, and the same has no applicability in the year under consideration, as no immovable property was received by the assessee in the year under consideration and only the same was registered in this year. This fact is further substantiated by the payments made by the assessee towards construction of the flat, which were completed by 06/02/2018. Therefore, we are of the considered view that the provisions of section 56(2)(x) of the Act have no applicability for making the addition in the year under consideration in the peculiar facts of the present case. Thus, the impugned addition made under section 56(2)(x) of the Act is deleted. 15. In the result, the appeal by the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 05-Oct-2026.
