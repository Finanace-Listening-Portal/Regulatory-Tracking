Its 1-year returns are -2.54% and 3-year returns are 16.78%. The risk rating is “Very High”. Another fund, the Canara Robeco Mid Cap Fund, has a 1-year returns of 1.24% and 3-year returns of 15.5%. It also has a “Very High” risk rating. What should one make of the numbers? Well, a 3-year SIP in the infrastructure fund would yield 16.78% returns. But the 1-year number tells us that the returns in the past year have been negative. Had one invested in a lump sum a year back, they would have seen negative returns. Now that is the beauty of a SIP, it helps one ride through the volatility. But one must assess if they can withstand a “Very High” risk level. The small print: Expense ratio Looking at the expense ratio in the image above, the infrastructure fund has an expense ratio of 1.16% and the mid-cap fund has an expense ratio of 0.86%. The funds mentioned here are direct plans, which usually have a lower expense ratio than regular plans. These numbers might seem inconsequential, but they add up over the years. A difference of 0.30% in the expense ratio can cost lakhs of rupees over a 20-year SIP. Thus, the mid-cap fund, with its 0.86% expense ratio is a better choice than the infrastructure fund, which has an expense ratio of 1.16%.
