As financial awareness grows, many investors are exploring alternative avenues that can offer diversification, income potential, or access to asset classes that may otherwise require significant capital. From owning a fraction of a high-value asset to investing in corporate debt, alternative investment strategies are becoming increasingly relevant. Fractional investing, NCDs, AIFs, and property flipping are four such avenues that attract investors for different reasons. However, each option comes with its own structure, risk profile, investment horizon, and return potential. Understanding how they work can help investors make more informed decisions. What Makes Alternative Investments Relevant Today? Traditional investment products remain an important part of a diversified portfolio. However, investors may also want exposure to assets beyond listed equities and conventional savings products. Alternative investments can provide access to areas such as real estate, private companies, structured credit, and other specialized opportunities. They can also enable investors to pursue wealth creation through diverse strategies rather than relying on a single asset class. The right choice depends on factors such as financial goals, risk tolerance, liquidity requirements, investment horizon, and the level of involvement an investor wants. Fractional Investing: Having Access To High-Value Assets With Less Capital Fractional investing allows multiple investors to own a portion of an asset instead of purchasing the entire asset themselves. This model has become particularly relevant in real estate, where individual properties can require substantial capital. Through fractional ownership structures, investors can gain exposure to a property by investing a smaller amount alongside other investors. Accessibility is one of the main features. Opportunities that would otherwise be hard to access may be available to investors who might not have the funds to buy a property outright. Fractional investing can also help with diversification. Instead of allocating a large portion of their portfolio to one property, investors may be able to spread capital across different assets, depending on the structure and minimum investment requirements. However, investors should understand the ownership structure, fees, exit mechanism, underlying asset quality, and potential liquidity before investing. NCDs: Exploring Opportunities for Corporate Debt Non-Convertible Debentures, commonly known as NCDs, are debt instruments issued by companies to raise capital. Investors provide funds to the issuer and receive interest according to the terms of the instrument. NCDs can be considered as part of a diversified portfolio for investors who are looking for income-generating opportunities. Depending on the issue, NCDs can have different interest rates, tenures, and repayment structures. But more potential for interest also means more risk of credit. Prior to investing, consider the issuer’s financial health and credit quality. Investors should also know whether the NCD is secured or unsecured and understand the maturity period and the ease of exiting before maturity if funds are needed. NCDs, therefore, should not be evaluated purely on the basis of the interest rate. The underlying credit quality and terms of the issue are equally important. AIFs: Access to Specialized Investment Strategies Alternative Investment Funds, or AIFs, are investment vehicles designed to invest in strategies and asset classes that may differ from traditional mutual funds. Depending on their category and investment mandate, AIFs can provide exposure to areas such as private equity, venture capital, private credit, real estate, and other specialized strategies. One of the biggest advantages of AIFs is access. They can provide investors with opportunities that may not be readily available through conventional investment products. At the same time, AIFs can involve higher minimum investments, longer investment horizons, complex structures, and varying levels of liquidity. They may therefore be more suitable for investors who understand alternative assets and can tolerate the associated risks. Before investing in an AIF, investors should carefully evaluate the fund strategy, portfolio, fees, manager experience, investment horizon, and exit mechanisms. Property Flipping: A Strategy Based on Real Estate Value Creation Property flipping involves purchasing a property with the intention of selling it later at a higher value. The strategy can involve identifying undervalued properties, improving them, and selling them when market conditions are favorable. Unlike simply holding property for rental income or long-term appreciation, property flipping typically has a shorter investment horizon and requires more active involvement. Success can depend on several factors, including the purchase price, renovation costs, location, demand, transaction expenses, and selling price. Market conditions can also significantly influence the outcome. Property flipping may appear straightforward, but costs can quickly affect profitability. Investors need to account for acquisition expenses, taxes, renovation costs, financing costs, maintenance, and selling expenses when evaluating potential returns. How Should Investors Compare These Options? The choice should ultimately align with the investor’s broader financial plan rather than being driven solely by potential returns. Professional advice may also be useful when evaluating complex investment structures. Final Thoughts The growing interest in fractional investing, NCDs, AIFs, and property flipping reflects the expanding range of opportunities available to modern investors. Each offers a different approach to investing and can serve a distinct role within a broader portfolio. Rather than asking which option offers the highest return, investors should ask which opportunity aligns with their financial objectives, risk appetite, liquidity needs, and investment horizon. A well-considered approach focuses not just on potential returns but also on understanding the underlying asset, structure, risks, costs, and exit strategy. FAQs What is fractional investing? Fractional investing allows multiple investors to participate in the ownership or economic benefits of an asset by investing a portion of the required capital rather than purchasing the entire asset. Are NCDs a safe investment? NCDs carry investment risk, particularly credit risk. The safety of an NCD depends on factors such as the issuer’s financial strength, credit rating, security, and terms of the issue. Investors should evaluate these factors before investing. Who can invest in AIFs? AIFs are generally designed for sophisticated investors who meet the applicable eligibility and minimum investment requirements. The specific requirements can vary depending on the fund and applicable regulations. Is property flipping a good investment strategy? Property flipping can generate returns when a property is purchased at an attractive price and sold profitably.
