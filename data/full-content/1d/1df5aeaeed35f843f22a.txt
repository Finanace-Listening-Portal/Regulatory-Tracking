ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Nov 16, 2026 at 05:42 AM IST var news_msid = 135868760; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook RBI is considering restricting private equity firms from controlling more than one NBFC to limit concentration risks across lenders.Holdings of 15% or less could be treated as financial investments and attract lighter regulatory scrutiny.The proposed curbs could affect foreign investment structures, including the use of multiple funds and investment vehicles to hold stakes in NBFCs.The rules could reshape future acquisitions, with the RBI needing to clarify how it treats common ownership, affiliates and existing investments.The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is considering rules that could prevent private equity (PE) firms from controlling more than one non-banking financial company (NBFC), a move that could reshape investment structures in India's financial sector and influence future foreign investments in lenders. The proposed framework is aimed at limiting the risk of financial stress at a common investor or its affiliates spreading across multiple lenders in which it exercises control, while allowing smaller, non-controlling investments to continue with less regulatory scrutiny. Advt The proposal is still under consideration and has not been issued as a draft or notified as a regulation. The framework is expected to undergo public consultation before being finalised and could be formally issued early next year. Under the approach being considered, holdings of 15% or less could be treated as financial investments and attract lighter scrutiny.The impactThe proposed restrictions could affect how global investment firms structure their holdings in Indian financial services companies. Control could be established through a substantial shareholding or through governance rights, including the ability to appoint a majority of directors or influence management and policy decisions. The reported 25% threshold for listed companies should not be treated as a universal test, since the RBI's existing acquisition rules also recognise control through such rights.The distinction between control and financial investment is significant for PE firms that operate through multiple funds and investment vehicles. Treating different funds within the same investment group as a single controlling entity could constrain their ability to acquire significant stakes in several lenders. The final framework will need to clarify how common ownership, related funds and affiliates are assessed.The proposed changes come amid strong foreign investor interest in India's financial sector. Private equity investments in the sector stood at $5.4 billion in 2025, up 6% from 2024 and 17% from 2023. A further $3.6 billion flowed into the sector in the first nine months of 2026. Advt Recent transactions underline the scale of this interest. Blackstone has agreed to invest Rs 5,000 crore for an approximately 16% fully diluted stake in IIFL Finance as part of a Rs 6,414 crore primary capital raise. The investment will be made through compulsorily convertible preference shares, which are to convert into equity within 18 months of allotment. The transaction is subject to regulatory and shareholder approvals.Separately, Temasek will invest Rs 2,500 crore in Godrej Capital at a valuation of up to Rs 28,000 crore. The investment, which is also subject to regulatory approvals, will leave Godrej Industries Group with approximately 80% ownership of the financial services business. Advt The RBI has previously scrutinised common ownership of multiple NBFCs. Bain Capital's proposed acquisition of a 41.7% stake in Manappuram Finance was approved subject to the condition that it divest its 93% holding in Tyger Capital, formerly Adani Capital. The principle has also been applied to non-PE investors. Mizuho Financial Group, which has majority stakes in two Indian non-bank lenders, has reportedly been asked to submit a plan to integrate or consolidate its existing NBFC businesses.The Indian Venture and Alternate Capital Association has sought greater flexibility, including permission for different entities within an investment group to acquire stakes exceeding 20% in multiple NBFCs. Investment firms often operate several funds with different investors and mandates. Applying a common ownership threshold across these vehicles could affect their ability to invest across financial services businesses. Advt The final rules could therefore have implications beyond PE firms' acquisition strategies, influencing the governance structures of NBFCs and the flow of foreign capital into the sector. The extent of the impact will depend on how the RBI defines control, treats investments made through related funds and provides for existing holdings.
