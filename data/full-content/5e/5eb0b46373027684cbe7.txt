Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Bala Sreenivas Rao Somisetty Vs ITO (Andhra Pradesh High Court) Section 147A Struck Down: Retrospective Validation Cannot Override Faceless Reassessment Scheme Without Removing Judicial Basis The batch of writ petitions concerned the authority of Jurisdictional Assessing Officers (JAOs) to initiate reassessment proceedings under sections 148A and 148, notwithstanding the faceless scheme notified under section 151A. By Notification No. 18/2022 dated 29 March 2022, the Central Government introduced the e-Assessment of Income Escaping Assessment Scheme, 2022. Its scope expressly included reassessment under section 147 and issuance of notices under section 148 through automated allocation. In the earlier round, the Andhra Pradesh High Court had set aside the impugned notices and orders, holding that proceedings initiated by JAOs violated section 151A read with the notified scheme. While the Revenue’s appeals were pending before the Supreme Court, Parliament inserted section 147A through the Finance Act, 2026, with retrospective effect from 1 April 2021. What Section 147A Sought to Achieve Section 147A provided, notwithstanding judgments, section 151A or schemes framed under it, that the Assessing Officer for sections 148 and 148A would mean an officer other than the National Faceless Assessment Centre or an assessment unit under section 144B. The Revenue presented the provision as a clarification separating the reopening stage from the subsequent assessment. According to it, the JAO could undertake the section 148A procedure and issue the section 148 notice, after which the reassessment would proceed through the faceless mechanism. Following the amendment, the Supreme Court, by its order dated 10 April 2026, set aside the earlier High Court judgments on the limited ground that their statutory foundation required reconsideration. It remitted the matters and permitted challenges to section 147A, expressly leaving its validity, retrospectivity and applicability undecided. Faceless Scheme Could Not Be Ignored The High Court held that the notified scheme expressly included issuance of notices under section 148. The Revenue could not confine faceless operation to the assessment stage while disregarding that inclusion. The Court also recorded the Revenue’s admission concerning the absence of an automated arrangement for identifying cases for initiation under sections 148A and 148. It found that the cases had instead been identified by the respective JAOs, contrary to the scheme. The Court rejected the argument that section 148A remained outside the faceless requirement merely because it was not expressly mentioned in clause 3 of the notification. It viewed the section 148A enquiry as the connected preliminary process leading to the section 148 notice. It also rejected the Revenue’s reliance on concurrent jurisdiction and disagreed with the Gujarat High Court’s view in Snehdham Trust. Legislature Must Remove the Basis of the Judgment The central constitutional finding concerned the limits of retrospective validating legislation. The Court acknowledged that Parliament possesses power to legislate retrospectively and remove the foundation of an earlier judicial decision. However, it cannot merely nullify a judgment without fundamentally altering the legal basis on which that judgment rests. Relying on Shri Prithvi Cotton Mills Ltd. v. Broach Borough Municipality, (1969) 2 SCC 283, and other Supreme Court authorities, the Court distinguished permissible legislative correction from an impermissible legislative override. It found that section 151A and the scheme remained unamended, including the scheme’s express coverage of section 148. In the Court’s view, insertion of section 147A without addressing that foundation attempted to override judicial decisions and offended the separation of powers. Additional Finding on Amendment of the Repealed Act The Court also examined the commencement of the Finance Act, 2026 alongside the repeal of the Income-tax Act, 1961. It held that although Presidential assent had been received on 30 March 2026, the relevant amendment commenced on 1 April 2026, when the 1961 Act stood repealed. After considering section 536 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 and section 5 of the General Clauses Act, the Court concluded that the insertion into the repealed Act was unsustainable. This was an additional ground supporting its decision. Final Decision The High Court declared section 147A unconstitutional and struck it down. It consequently held that the impugned proceedings initiated by the JAOs under sections 148A and 148 lacked jurisdiction and set aside the challenged proceedings and orders. The writ petitions were allowed without costs. Author’s Comments Calling an amendment a “clarification” does not conclude the constitutional enquiry. The Court examined whether the legislation actually removed the foundation of the earlier judgments or simply directed a different outcome while leaving the existing scheme intact. The decision also emphasises that a notified statutory scheme cannot be treated as optional administrative guidance. Its express inclusion of section 148 was central to the Court’s reasoning. The judgment records that a similar Punjab and Haryana High Court decision dated 10 September 2026 had been stayed by the Supreme Court on 18 September 2026. The Andhra Pradesh High Court nevertheless reached its conclusions independently. That recorded stay concerns the other judgment; it should not be confused with a stay of this decision. Finally, the ruling addresses reopening jurisdiction and the validity of section 147A. It does not decide the merits of the alleged escaped income or every separate question concerning valuation enquiries and assessment-stage notices. Its strongest principle is that retrospective validation must cure the judicially identified legal foundation, rather than merely command that earlier judgments be disregarded. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF ANDHRA PRADESH HIGH COURT 1. As the issues involved in these writ petitions are similar, they are being disposed of by this common order. 2. All these writ petitions were filed challenging the constitutional validity of Section 147A of the Income Tax Act, 1961, introduced vide Finance Act, 2026 (Act No.4 of 2026) giving retrospective effect from 01.04.2021 as ultra vires, apart from being violative of the basic structure of the Constitution of India, including the Rule of Law and separation of powers, inter alia the petitioners also challenged orders passed by Jurisdictional Assessing Officers (JAO) under Section 147 r/w. Section 144 r/w. Section 144B of the Income Tax Act, 1961 as illegal, arbitrary, void abinitio apart from violative of Articles 14, 19(1)(g) and Article 265 of the Constitution of India and contrary to Section 151A of the Income Tax Act,1961. 3. Before adverting to various grounds raised in these writ petitions, it is appropriate to discuss the factual matrix of the case for deciding the issues on hand. 4. Through Finance Act, 2018, a scheme namely e-Assessment Scheme, 2019 was formulated describing a detailed procedure to be followed for the purpose of making assessment, which was notified on 12.01.2019. Initially, the said scheme was made applicable for passing assessment orders under Section 143(3) of the Act. Subsequently, proceedings under Section 144 were also included in the said scheme. Later, the said scheme was modified into “Faceless Assessment Scheme, 2019” by notifying in the Gazette. 5. The Finance Act, 2021 introduced Section 144B to the Income Tax Act, with effect from 01.04.2021 incorporating the Faceless Assessment Scheme directly into the Act with certain modifications. Later, the said provision was amended by Finance Act, 2022 by including the proceedings under Section 147 of the Act under Faceless Assessment Scheme. In other words, Faceless Assessment Scheme, 2019 made inapplicable with effect from 01.04.2021 after introduction of the said scheme as per Section 144-B of the Act. 6. Be that as it may, Section 151A was introduced to the Income Tax Act, 1961 by Act 38 of 2020 with effect from 01.11.2020 for Faceless Assessment of Income Escaping Assessment. Through which, the Central Government may make a scheme, by notification in the Official Gazette for the purposes of Assessment, Reassessment or Re-computation under Section 147 or issuance of notice under Section 148 or conducting of enquiries or issuance of show cause notice or passing of order under Section 148-A or sanction for issue of such notice under Section 151, so as to impart greater efficiency, transparency and accountability. In furtherance thereto, Union of India issued Notification dt.29.03.2022 through the Central Board of Direct Taxes formulating the scheme for e-Assessment of Income Escaping Assessment Scheme, 2022, which is reproduced hereunder. “MINISTRY OF FINANCE (Department of Revenue) (CENTRAL BOARD OF DIRECT TAXES) NOTIFICATION New Delhi, the 29th March, 2022 S.O.1466(E)-In exercise of the powers conferred by sub-section (1) and (2) of section 151 A of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (43 of 1961), the Central Government hereby makes the following scheme. 1. Short title and commencement.-(1) This Scheme may be called the e-Assessment of Income Escaping Assessment Scheme, 2022. (2) It shall come into force with effect from the date of its publication in the Official Gazette. 2. -(1) In this Scheme, unless the context otherwise requires,- (a) “Act” means the Income Tax Act, 1961 (43 of 1961); (b) “automated allocation” means an algorithm for randomized allocation of cases, by using suitable technological tools, including artificial intelligence and machine learning, with a view to optimize the use of resources. (2) Words and expressions used herein and not defined, but defined in the Act, shall have the meaning respectively assigned to them in the Act. 3. Scope of the Scheme.-For the purpose of this Scheme,- (a) assessment, reassessment or re-computation under section 147 of the Act, (b) issuance of notice under section 148 of the Act, Shall be through automated allocation, in accordance with risk management strategy formulated by the Board as referred to in section 148 of the Act for issuance of notice, and in a faceless manner, to the extent provided in section 144B of the Act with reference to making assessment or reassessment of total income or loss of assessee. [Notification No.18/2022/F.No.370142/16/2022-TPL (Part1)] SHEFALI SINGH, Under Secy” 7. By virtue of the introduction of the above scheme, the procedure to be followed had been laid under Section 144B of the Income Tax Act, 1961. For brevity the procedure is extracted as under:- ” (i) the National Faceless Assessment Centre shall assign the case selected for the purposes of faceless assessment under this section to a specific assessment unit through an automated allocation system. (ii) the National Faceless Assessment Centre shall intimate the assessee that assessment in his case shall be completed in accordance with the procedure laid down under this section. (iii) a notice shall be served on the assessee, through the National Faceless Assessment Centre, under sub-section (2) of section 143 or under sub-section (1) of section 142 and the assessee may file his response to such notice within the date specified therein, to the National Faceless Assessment Centre which shall forward the same to the assessment unit; (iv) where a case is assigned to the assessment unit, under clause (i) it may make a request through the National Faceless Assessment Centre for (a) obtaining such further information, documents or evidence from the assessee or any other person, as it may specify; (b) conducting of enquiry or verification by verification unit; (c) seeking technical assistance in respect of determination of arm‘s length price, valuation of property, withdrawal of registration, approval, exemption or any other technical matter by referring to the technical unit. (v) where a request under sub-clause (a) of clause (iv) has been initiated by the assessment unit, the National Faceless Assessment Centre shall serve appropriate notice or requisition on the assessee or any other person for obtaining the information, documents or evidence requisitioned by the assessment unit and the assessee or any other person, as the case may be, shall file his response to such notice within the time specified therein or such time as may be extended on the basis of an application in this regard, to the National Faceless Assessment Centre which shall forward the reply to the assessment unit;‖ 8. While things stood thus, the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer (JAO) issued notices under Sections 148A of the Income Tax Act, 1961 to the petitioners requiring them to furnish return of income for the relevant assessment year on the premise that certain transactions chargeable to tax has escaped assessment. Questioning the said notices, the petitioners filed writ petitions on the ground that notices/orders have been issued by the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer deviating the procedure prescribed under e-Assessment of Income Escaping Assessment Scheme, 2022 which contemplates that any procedure to be initiated under Section 147 and 148 of the Act shall be through Faceless manner, which came into force from 29.03.2022, as the impugned notices/orders were issued by the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer, the same is without jurisdiction. 9. The Revenue filed counter affidavit contending that notification dt.29.03.2022 does not state whether the notice is to be issued by National Faceless Assessment Centre (NFAC) or the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer (JAO). It was further contended that the said notification deals with the procedure to be followed, while exercising the powers under Section 148 of the Act. Further, for the purpose of issuance of notice under Section 148 of the Act, the same is done in accordance with the risk management strategy and the assessment shall be in a faceless manner as prescribed under Section 144B of the Act. 10. A co-ordinate bench of this Court having considered the respective submissions made by the counsel for the petitioners and the Revenue, by order dt.28.10.2025, allowed the writ petitions by setting aside the impugned notices/orders issued under sections 148A(b), 148A(d) and 148 of Income Tax Act, 1961, holding that the same were violative of provisions of Section 151A of the Act r/w. Notification No.18/2022, dt.29.03.2022. 11. Aggrieved by the said order, the Revenue preferred Special Leave Petitions before the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India. While pending the SLPs, the Parliament through Act No.4 of 2026 namely the Finance Act, 2026, which came into force from 01.04.2026 introduced new provisions into the Income Tax Act, 1961 with a view to ensure greater procedural clarity and certainty. Through the said Act, Section 147A was introduced into the Income Tax Act, 1961 whereunder the term “Assessing Officer” has been expressly clarified to mean officer other than the National Faceless Assessment Centre (NFAC) or any assessment unit and the same is deemed to have come into effect retrospectively from 01.04.2021. The said provision reads as follows:- “9. After section 147 of the Income Tax Act, the following section shall be inserted and shall be deemed to have been inserted with effect from the 1st day of April, 2021, namely:- 147A. Notwithstanding anything contained in any judgment, order or decree of any Court on in Section 151A or in any scheme framed thereunder, for the removal of doubts, it is hereby clarified that the Assessing Officer for the purposes of sections 148 and 148A shall mean and shall always be deemed to have meant to be an Assessing Officer other than the National Faceless Assessment Centre or any assessment unit referred to in sub-section (3) of Section 144B.‖ 12. In view of the insertion of Section 147A into the Income Tax Act, 1961, the Hon’ble Apex Court disposed of the SLPs vide order dated 10.04.2026, the relevant portion of which reads as follows: “20. The Revenue filed counter affidavit inter alia contending that challenge to the constitutional validity of Section 147A of the Income Tax Act, 1961, as inserted by the Finance Act, 2026 is wholly misconceived, devoid of merits and not maintainable either in law or on facts and writ petitions are liable to be dismissed. 16. It was further contended that a statutory provision enacted by Parliament carries a strong presumption of constitutionality and the burden lies heavily upon the persons assailing its validity to establish a clear manifest violation of constitutional limitation. It was further averred in the counter affidavit that the said presumption operates with greater force in matters relating to taxation and fiscal policy, whereunder the legislature is entitled to a wider degree of latitude in classification, prescription of procedures and formulation of measures for assessment and collection of revenue. Therefore, the petitioners cannot seek invalidation of a fiscal enactment merely on the basis of hardship, inconvenience, perceived inequality or prefer alternative legislative scheme, especially in the absence of any demonstrable legislative in-competence. 17. It was stated in the counter affidavit that the TOLA, 2020 [The Taxation and Other Laws (relaxation and amendment of certain provisions) Act, 2020] inserted Section 144B and 151A into the Income Tax Act, 1961 with effect from 01.04.2021, providing the statutory frame work for carrying out assessments in a faceless manner. Section 151A empowers the Central Government to make a scheme, by notification in the official gazette for the purposes of assessment, reassessment or re-computation under the Act. It was further stated that under the said scheme there are two distinct stages of reassessment which contained in chapter-XIV of the Act. Section 147 empowers the Assessing Officer to reassess, re-compute income chargeable to tax, which escaped assessment for any assessment year. Section 148 provides for issuance of notice by the Assessing Officer requiring the assessee to furnish a return of income where income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment. Further that Section 148A inserted vide Finance Act, 2021 with effect from 01.04.2021, prescribes a mandatory pre-assessment procedure before issuance of a notice under Section 148. Under Section 148A, the Assessing Officer is required to conduct such inquiry, if necessary, with prior approval of the specified authority, provide an opportunity of being heard by issuing a show cause notice along with the information suggesting escapement of income, consider the reply furnished by the assessee and thereafter pass an order under Section 148A (d). Further, such order is required to be passed before issuance of notice under Section 148 and both the order under Section 148A (d) and notice under Section 148 are issued with the prior approval of the specified authority. 18. It was further stated that the re-assessment proceedings under the Act comprise two distinct stages. The first stage is the pre-assessment stage governed by Sections 148A and 148, whereunder the Assessing Officer examines whether the income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment and whether a notice under Section 148 is required to be issued. The second stage commences after issuance of notice under Section 148 and relates to conduct of reassessment proceedings under Section 147. 19. The counter affidavit further reads that subsequent to the introduction of faceless assessment provisions and the scheme notified under Section 151A, divergent views emerged regarding the authority competent to undertake proceedings under Section 148A and 148. While certain judicial pronouncements took the view that such proceedings were required to be undertaken through the faceless mechanism, whereas the other decisions held that the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer continued to be the competent authority for the purposes of Section 148A and Section 148. Divergence of opinions, led to uncertainty regarding the legislative intent underlying the reassessment frame work. 20. It is the consistent stand of the Revenue throughout the proceedings that the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer is empowered to issue notice under Section 148 and 148A. It was further averred in the counter affidavit that in view of conflicting decisions of various High Courts in this regard multiple writ petitions are being filed before the Hon’ble High Courts which opened the flood gates for huge pendency. It was further stated that at the assessment level, this issue has single handedly contributed to increasing the work load of the officers which caused hindrance to the Assessing Officers from carrying out reassessments for the last four (04) years. In order to reinforce the legislative intent and to put an end to this issue, the Parliament in its wisdom, has chosen to enact a retrospective amendment to the Income Tax Act, 1961 with effect from 01.04.2021 vide Section 9 of Finance Act, 2026 and introduced Section 147A, which reads as follows:- “147A. Notwithstanding anything contained in any judgment, order or decree of any Court or in Section 151A or in any scheme framed thereunder, for the removal of doubts, it is hereby clarified that the Assessing Officer for the purposes of Sections 148 and 148A shall mean and shall always be deemed to have meant to be an Assessing Officer other than the National Faceless Assessment Centre or any assessment unit referred to in sub-section (3) of Section 144B.” 21. By introducing Section 147A, the Parliament has legislatively clarified that who is the proper statutory authority having the jurisdiction to initiate action under Section 148A and 148. It was further stated that the expression “Assessing Officer” shall be considered in the context of Section 148 and 148A as the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer. Thus, by introducing Section 147A the legislative intent was to clarify that initiation of pre-assessment proceedings in issuance of notice under Section 148 falls outside the faceless assessment procedure contemplated under Section 144B of the Act and it was clarified in the memorandum explaining the provisions in the Financial Bill, 2026, which is as follows: “Clarification regarding Jurisdiction to issue notice u/s 148 where income has escaped assessment and for carrying out pre-assessment procedure under Section 148A.” 22. It was further stated that the clarificatory amendment rooted in statutory provisions and the foundation was from statutory scheme itself inasmuch as the e-assessment of Income Escaping Assessment (re-assessment) Scheme, 2022 divides the re-assessment process into two stages namely, the pre-assessment enquiry stage and the assessment/reassessment stage. The first stage requires the JAO to follow the procedure prescribed under Section 148A and determine whether a case is fit for reassessment and upon such satisfaction, issue notice under Section 148. The second stage relates to the conduct of assessment/reassessment proceedings through National Faceless Assessment Centre. Consequently, once JAO reaches the satisfaction contemplated under Section 148A, the issuance of notice under Section 148 necessarily follows from such satisfaction and forms part of the same statutory process. 23. It was further averred in the counter affidavit that the notification No.18 of 2022, dt.29.03.2022 does not provide that notice under Section 148 is required to be issued in a faceless manner and the same was never brought within the ambit of faceless procedure. Introduction of Section 147A into the Income Tax Act, 1961, with retrospective effect is to remove the basis on which the various Hon’ble High Courts have quashed the respective notices issued by Jurisdictional Assessing Officers. 24. It was further stated that through the amendment, the Revenue has validated the Act by removing the defect in the statute retrospectively. The twin test for Validating Acts is 1) Remove the cause of invalidity by curing the defect or lack of authority pointed out by a Court and 2) such removal of defect must be done through a subsequent enactment passed with retrospective effect. The counsel for the petitioners would strenuously contend that the amendment is unconstitutional inasmuch as the intent of the legislature in bringing out the said provision into the Act is to see that the judgments rendered by various High Courts in this regard are nullified. It is contended that in order to validate the acts done by the authorities though the same was without jurisdiction, the present amendment was brought in. They would further contend that in order to validate any enactment, there should be ambiguity in the provision, that there was no such ambiguity either in Section 151A or in the scheme made thereunder. In such circumstances, there is no occasion for the Central Government to bring the amendment to the Act. It is further contended that if the legislature is allowed to make such amendments with retrospective effect, the same would be in violation of the Doctrine of separation of powers which is the basic structure of the Constitution. Reliance is placed on judgment of Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of “Shri Prithvi Cotton Mills Ltd. and another Vs. Broach Borough Municipality and another1” to contend that such a validation cannot be permitted especially so as to nullify the judgment rendered by various High Courts. It is further contended that the legislature has no competence to make such law with retrospective effect more particularly when the rights of the assessees are at stake. In the said decision, it was inter alia held as follows:- ―4. When a Legislature sets out to validate a tax declared by a Court to be illegally collected under an ineffective or an invalid law, the cause for ineffectiveness or invalidity must be removed before validation can be said to take place effectively. The most important condition, of course, is that the Legislature must possess the power to impose the tax, for, if it does not, the action must ever remain ineffective and illegal. Granted legislative competence, it is not sufficient to declare merely that the decision of the Court shall not bind for that is tantamount to reversing the decision in exercise of judicial power which the Legislature does not possess or exercise. A court‘s decision must always bind unless the condition on which it is based are so fundamentally altered that the decision could not have been given in the altered circumstances. Ordinarily, a Court holds a tax to be invalidly imposed because the power to tax is wanting or the statue or the rules or both are invalid or do not sufficiently create the jurisdiction. Validation of a tax do declared illegal may be done only if the grounds of illegality or invalidity are capable of being removed and are in fact removed and the tax thus made legal. Sometimes this is done by providing for jurisdiction where jurisdiction had not been properly invested before. Sometimes this is done by re­enacting retrospectively a valid and legal taxing provision and then by fiction making the tax already collected to stand under the re-enacted law. Sometimes the Legislature gives its own meaning and interpretation of the law under which tax was collected and by legislative fiat makes the new meaning binding upon courts. The Legislature may follow any one method or all of them and while it does to it may neutralize the effect of the earlier decision of the court which becomes ineffective after the change of the law. Whichever method is adopted it must be within the competence of the legislature and legal and adequate to attain the object of validation. If the Legislature has the power over the subject matter and competence to make a valid law, it can at any time make such a valid law and make it retrospectively so as to bind even past transactions. These are not judicial functions but the exclusive province of the Legislature. Therefore, they are not the encroachment on judicial power. (8) In exercising legislative power, the Legislature by mere declaration, without anything more, cannot directly overrule, revise or override a judicial decision. It can render judicial decision ineffective by enacting valid law on the topic within its legislative field fundamentally altering or changing its character retrospectively. The changed or altered conditions are such that the previous decision would not have been rendered by the Court, if those conditions had existed at the time of declaring the law as invalid. It is also empowered to give effect to retrospective legislation with a deeming date or with effect from a particular date. (9) The consistent thread that runs through all the decisions of this Court is that the legislature cannot directly overrule the decision or make a direction as not binding on it but has power to make the decision ineffective by removing the base on which the decision was rendered, consistent with the law of the Constitution and the Legislature must have competence to do the same.‖ In the aforesaid case, the issue that arose for consideration was as to the vires of Section 11 of the Kerala Electricity Surcharge (Levy and Collection) Act, 1989. From the aforesaid authorities, it is settled that there is a demarcation between legislative and judicial functions predicated on the theory of separation of powers. The legislature has the power to enact laws including the power to retrospectively amend laws and thereby remove causes of ineffectiveness or invalidity. When a law is enacted with retrospective effect, it is not considered as an encroachment upon judicial power when the legislature does not directly overrule or reverse a judicial dictum. The legislature cannot, by way of an enactment, declare a decision of the court as erroneous or a nullity, but can amend the statute or the provision so as to make it applicable to the past. The legislature has the power to rectify, through an amendment, a defect in law noticed in the enactment and even highlighted in the decision of the court. This plenary power to bring the statute in conformity with the legislative intent and correct the flaw pointed out by the court can have a curative and neutralizing effect. When such a correction is made, the purpose behind the same is not to overrule the decision of the court or encroach upon the judicial turf, but simply enact a fresh law with retrospective effect to alter the foundation and meaning of the legislation and to remove the base on which the judgment is founded. If the notice is issued by the Faceless Assessing Officer under Section 148 of the Act on the basis of the order which has been passed by the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer under Section 148A (d) of the Act, the same would result into absurdity. 31. (emphasis supplied) 74. In this connection, it is pertinent to extract clause 3 of the scheme dated 29.03.2022. “3. On a conjoint reading of the Memorandum and Section 147A of the Act makes it clear that, the amendment was brought in to achieve certainty, clarity and to avoid litigation. However, a plain reading of the scheme made under Section 151A, it is apparently clear that Section 148 is brought under the said scheme, so as to initiate proceedings in faceless manner as aforesaid. By introducing Section 147A, admittedly the scheme made under Section 151A remained un-amended. Without amending the said scheme, any clarification or validation through amendment cannot be permitted especially when the basis is not removed. Therefore, by introducing Section 147A of the Act, the Legislature is trying to over reach the judgments rendered by various High Courts in this regard, in direct conflict with Doctrine of separation of powers, which is the basic structure of the Constitution. 93. The Hon’ble Supreme Court of India in the case of “Shri Prithvi Cotton Mills Ltd., Vs. Boroach Borough Municipality & others41” had held that a Court’s decision must always bind, unless the conditions on which it is based are so fundamentally altered that the decision could not have been given in the altered circumstances. If the Legislature has the power over the subject matter and competence to make a valid law, it can at any time make such valid law and make it retrospectively, so as to bind even the past transactions. While holding so, the Hon’ble Supreme Court also observed that the validity of a validating law, therefore, depends upon whether the Legislature possesses the competence which it claims over the subject matter and whether in making the validation, it removes the defect which the Courts had found in the existing law and makes adequate provisions in the validating law for a valid imposition of tax. In the cases on hand, no doubt the Legislature has got plenary power to validate any law, but the same is subject to certain limitations, viz., while validating the law it shall remove the defect which the Courts had found in the existing law. Admittedly, in all these cases, no defect whatsoever was expressed by various High Courts either in the scheme or in the Act in the earlier round of litigation. All that the Courts have held that JAOs have no jurisdiction to exercise powers under Section 148 of the Act. In such circumstances, without amending the scheme made under Section 151A of the Act, insertion of Section 147A into Income Tax Act, 1961 in the name of clarification or validation cannot be permitted. 94. Further, the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of “State of Tamilnadu and another vs. M.Rayappaiah Gounder and another42” while referring to the case of “The Municipal Corporation of the City of Ahmedabad vs. The New Shrock Spg. and Wvg. Co. Ltd.,43” had held that the Legislature under our constitution have within the prescribed limits, powers to make laws prospectively as well as retrospectively. By exercise of those powers the legislature can remove the basis of a decision rendered by a competent Court, thereby rendering that decision ineffective. But, no Legislature in this Country has power to ask the instrumentalities of the State to disobey or disregard the decision given by Courts. 95. From the above judgments it is clear that, if any law enacted by Legislature, if it is found beyond the Legislative competence, the Courts have got powers to declare the same to be null and void and it can strike down the same, especially if the Legislature tries to over reach any decision rendered by the Court. In other words, the Legislature cannot nullify the judgment rendered by any Court by enacting law without removing the basis. 96. Further, in the earlier round of litigation either this Court or any other High Court found fault with the scheme made under Section 151A. It was held that having included Section 148 into the scheme, the JAOs have no jurisdiction to issue notices under Section 148. Admittedly, even as on today, Section 148 remains in the scheme and without making amendment to the scheme, incorporation of any provision to nullify the judgments rendered by various High Courts, is beyond Legislative competence and legally impermissible. Such acts of Legislature over reach which unsettle the decisions of Courts and lead to chaos cannot be allowed. 97. Further, Section 147A starts with non-obstante clause, for the removal of doubts, it was clarified that the assessing officer for the purposes of Sections 148 and 148A shall mean and shall always be deemed to have meant to be an assessing officer other than faceless assessment center or any assessment unit referred to in sub-section (3) of section 144B. As already observed supra, the amendment was brought in so as to nullify the judgments rendered by various Hon’ble High Courts. Further, through the aforesaid judgments, the respondents were directed to follow their own scheme framed under section 151A, while holding that JAOs have no jurisdiction to initiate proceedings under Section 148A or 148. In the aforesaid situation, without amending Section 151A or the scheme made there under, the impugned amendment inserted through Section 147A does not stand for judicial scrutiny. 98. In this connection, it is relevant to refer section 5 of General Clauses Act, as it assumes importance, which reads thus: “5. Coming into operation of enactments. –(1) Where any Central Act is not expressed to come into operation on a particular day, then it shall come into operation on the day on which it receives the assent,- (a) in the case of a Central Act made before the commencement of the Constitution, of the Governor General, and (b) in the case of an Act of Parliament, of the President. (2) omitted (3) Unless the contrary is expressed, a Central Act or regulation shall be construed as coming into operation immediately on the expiration of the day preceding its commencement.‖ 105. On a reading of section 5 of General Clauses Act, it is clear that, if any Act does not express the date of which it come into operation, then it will come into force from the date on which it received assent of the President. In the case on hand, Finance Act, 2026 (No.4 of 2026), though received the assent of the President on 30.03.2026, as per Section 2 (a) of the Act, it shall come into force from 01.04.2026. Since, Finance Act, 2026 has expressly provided 1st day of April, 2026, it cannot be said that the same has come into force on 30.03.2026, the date on which it received the assent of the President. Further, our view is fortified by judgment of Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of “Common Cause Vs. Union of India and others44”, wherein Hon’ble Apex Court at para 28 held as under:- “28. The submission that by virtue of Section 5 of General Clauses Act, the Act has come into force is misconceived. Section 5 of the General Clauses Act has no application. Section 5 is applicable only when the Act does not express any date with effect from which the Act would come into force. It will apply to such cases where there is no provision like Section 1(3) of the Act or Section 1(2) of the 44th Constitutional Amendment. When the Legislature itself provides that the date of coming into force of the Act would be a date to be notified by the Central Government, Section 5 of the General Clauses Act will have no application. It is plain and evident from the language of the provision. Section 5(1) provides that ‘where any Central Act is not expressed to come into operation on particular day, then it shall come into operation on the day on which it receives the assent’. Sub-clause (3) provides that ‘unless the contrary is expressed, a Central Act or Regulation shall be construed as coming into operation immediately on the expiration of the day preceding its commencement’.’ In simple words it would mean that unless otherwise provided a Central Act would come into operation on the date it receives Presidential assent and is construed as coming into operation immediately on the date preceding its commencement. Thus, if a Central Act is assented by the President on 23.8.1995 then it would be construed to have come into operation on the mid­night between 22nd and 23rd August, 1995. Sub-section (3) has to be read as a corollary to sub-section (1). Sub­section (1) provides that the Act would come into operation on the date it receives the assent of the President where a particular day w.e.f. which the Act would come into force is not prescribed whereas sub- section (3) provides the exact time of the day/night when the Act would come into force. It would not apply to cases where the legislature has delegated the power to the executive to bring into force the Act from a date to be notified by publication in the Official Gazette.” (emphasis supplied) 106. From the above judgment, it can be culled out that any enactment shall come into force from the date on which, it is expressly provided under that enactment. If the enactment does not express a particular day as to coming into its operation, in such case, the date on which the said Act received assent from the President would be the date on which the said Act would come into force. In that view of the matter, any amendment/insertion made to the Repealed Act is not sustainable under law and it has no legal sanctity, accordingly the same is liable to be struck down from the date of its inception or nativity. 107. For the foregoing reasons, this Court is of the considered opinion that Section 147A of the Income Tax Act, 1961 is unconstitutional and the same is accordingly struck down. Further, as we held that Section 147A as unconstitutional, all the proceedings initiated by the respective Jurisdictional Assessing Officers (JAOs) under Sections 148A and 148 are without jurisdiction and are liable to be set aside, inasmuch as the same have to be undertaken in faceless manner. Accordingly the writ petitions are allowed and impugned proceedings/orders are set aside. There shall be no order as to costs. As a sequel, pending applications, if any shall stand closed.
