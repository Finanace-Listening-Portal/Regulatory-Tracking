Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Indian Herbs Specialties Private Limited Vs ACIT (ITAT Delhi) Section 14A Applies Where the AO Records Dissatisfaction With an Unsupported Self-Disallowance Before Invoking Rule 8D Background of the Case Indian Herbs Specialties Private Limited, engaged in the manufacture and sale of herbal products, held ₹82.71 crore in liquid mutual funds, of which approximately ₹75.37 crore was invested in dividend options. It earned exempt dividend income of ₹2,00,91,782 and voluntarily disallowed ₹36,000 under section 14A in its return. The company explained that it had invested surplus funds generated from business operations and had used no interest-bearing borrowings for the investments. During scrutiny, the Assessing Officer (AO) issued a notice under section 142(1) identifying the ₹36,000 disallowance and asking the company to justify it. The company attributed the amount to expenditure of ₹3,000 per month, covering staff time and related administrative costs such as telephone and stationery. The AO rejected the explanation and applied the amended Rule 8D, computing a disallowance of ₹38,82,011 at 1% of the relevant monthly average investment. After accounting for the amount already disallowed in the return, the disputed addition was ₹38,46,011. Assessee’s Objection to the Rule 8D Disallowance The assessee argued that section 14A(2) required the AO to examine its accounts and explain why its own computation was incorrect before applying Rule 8D. It maintained that the size of the investment portfolio and the amount of exempt dividend income could not establish a defect in the ₹36,000 disallowance. It also submitted that dividend credits were automated and that managing the investments required limited staff involvement. Before the first appellate authority, the assessee furnished a detailed working that placed the expenditure attributable to exempt income at approximately ₹10,400. It argued that the ₹36,000 already offered in the return was conservative. Before the Tribunal, it relied on the Bombay High Court’s decision in PCIT v. Tata Capital Ltd., ITA No. 1081 of 2018, dated 3 April 2024, on the requirement to record satisfaction under section 14A(2). Why the First Appellate Authority Sustained the Addition The first appellate authority held that the AO had recorded adequate dissatisfaction and that the statutory formula applied once this condition was met. It distinguished the assessee’s own favourable Tribunal order for AY 2013–14, pointing to the earlier version of Rule 8D and the fact that the earlier year involved an interest disallowance. For AY 2017–18, the dispute concerned the disallowance computed under the amended rule. The Revenue likewise argued before the Tribunal that the amended rule governed the year under appeal. Tribunal’s Ruling on Satisfaction Under Section 14A(2) The Tribunal read the section 142(1) notice and the assessment order together. It noted that the AO had expressly acknowledged the ₹36,000 self-disallowance in the notice and had called upon the assessee to substantiate it. Although the assessee had described the involvement of a staff member and referred to incidental expenses, it had furnished no detailed working or basis for the ₹36,000 figure during assessment. The Tribunal also noted that the detailed working was furnished for the first time in appeal and that its basis did not correspond to the disallowance actually made in the return. On the material available to the AO, the Tribunal found that the notice and assessment order adequately recorded the AO’s dissatisfaction. It distinguished the authorities cited by the assessee on the facts and upheld the application of amended Rule 8D. The grounds challenging the section 14A disallowance were accordingly dismissed. Employees’ PF and ESI Contributions The assessee separately challenged the disallowance of ₹1,48,064 towards employees’ PF and ESI contributions deposited late. The delays were 10 days and one day, respectively. The assessee had pleaded that the payments were made before the income-tax return filing deadline, but its representative made no submissions on this ground at the hearing. Following Checkmate Services (P) Ltd. v. CIT (2022) 448 ITR 518 (SC), the Tribunal dismissed this ground as well. The assessee’s appeal was dismissed in full. Author’s Comments The ruling turns on what the assessee placed before the AO during assessment. A self-disallowance gives the AO a figure to examine. A contemporaneous working gives the AO a basis to evaluate that figure against the accounts. Here, the company explained the nature of the expenditure, but the detailed calculation emerged only at the appellate stage and produced a different amount. That sequence mattered to the Tribunal’s finding that the AO had adequately recorded dissatisfaction. The decision is particularly relevant where an assessee attributes only a small portion of its administrative costs to a substantial investment portfolio. In such cases, a working that identifies the personnel involved, records the basis for allocating their time and reconciles the resulting amount with the return can make the claim verifiable. The use of surplus own funds addresses the funding of the investment. The allocation of staff and administrative expenditure calls for its own factual explanation. Vs CIT (Supreme Court), (2022) 448 ITR 518 FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. This appeal is filed by the assessee against the order of ld. Addl/JCIT (A), Udaipur [“Ld. CIT (A)”, for short] dated 01.01.2026 for the Assessment Year 2017-18 raising following grounds of appeal:- “1.1 That the learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) erred in law and on facts in confirming the disallowance of Rs.38,46,011/- made under section 14A read with Rule 8D, ignoring that the assessee had already made a bona fide and reasonable disallowance of Rs.36,000/- in the return of income. 1.2 That the learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) [CIT(A)] has erred in law and on facts in upholding the disallowance under section 14A, without appreciating that the Assessing Officer had not recorded any valid satisfaction as mandated under section 14A(2) with reference to the correctness of the assessee’s own disallowance working of Rs.36,000/-. The learned CIT(A) further erred in wrongly observing that no disallowance was made by the assessee, which is contrary to the facts available on record, thereby rendering the impugned order unsustainable. 1.3 That the learned CIT(A) erred in holding that the Assessing Officer had recorded “detailed and specific dissatisfaction”, whereas such dissatisfaction was based only on the quantum of investments & exempt income and not on any defect in the assesses computation having regard to the accounts. 1.4 That the learned CIT(A) failed to consider the facts that during the year only 38 mutual fund transactions took place, no mutual fund was redeemed, and the dividend income was credited automatically by the mutual funds without any active or effort by the assessee, showing that negligible administrative expenditure only was incurred. 1.5 That the learned CIT(A) erred in ignoring the detailed working furnished by the assessee showing that the actual expenditure attributable to earning exempt income was only about Rs.10,400/- and that the assessee had already disallowed a much higher sum of Rs.36,000/- on a conservative basis. 1.6 That the learned CIT(A) erred in law in treating the statutory computation under Rule 8D as overriding the basic condition of section 14A that only actual expenditure incurred in relation to exempt income can be disallowed. 1.7 That the learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) [CIT(A)] has erred in law and on facts in sustaining the disallowance of Rs.38,46,011/- under section 14A, which is wholly unjustified and not applicable to the facts of the appellant’s case. The learned CIT(A) failed to appreciate that all the investments were made out of the appellant’s own surplus funds and no interest-bearing borrowed funds were utilized for earning the exempt income. Accordingly, the disallowance sustained is grossly excessive, arbitrary and wholly disproportionate to the nature and extent of the investment activity carried out by the assessee. 1.8 The appellant prays that the disallowance u/s 14A read with Rule 8D be deleted in full and the self-disallowance of Rs.36,000/- be accepted. 2.1 That the learned CIT(A) has erred in law and on facts in confirming the disallowance of employees’ contribution towards ESIIEPF amounting to Rs.1,48,064/- (Rs.71,139 + Rs.76,925), on account of a marginal delay of only 10 days and 1 day in deposit during December 2016 and March 2017 respectively. The appellant submits that the said contributions were deposited before the due date of filing of return of income and the disallowance is harsh and unjustified considering the bona fide and minor nature of delay. The appellant further prays that relief may be granted in view of the beneficial intent reflected in the proposed amendments in Finance Bill, 2026. ” 2. In the above grounds of appeal, assessee has raised two issues relating to section 14A disallowance and disallowance of employees contribution towards ESI/PF. 3. At the time of hearing ld. AR of the assessee made submission only relating to section 14A of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (for short ‘the Act’) and not made any submission on the issue of employees contribution towards ESI/ PF. However, the ground raised by the assessee in which it was submitted that only a marginal delay of ten days and one day in depositing the same in December 2016 and March 2017 respectively. Following the decision of Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Checkmate Services (P) Ltd. Vs. CIT (2022) 448 ITR 518, Ground No.2.1 raised by the assessee is dismissed. 4. With regard to Grounds No.1.1 to 1.8 raised by the assessee, the relevant facts are, assessee filed the return of income on 30.10.2017 and the case was selected under CASS. Accordingly, notice under section 143(2) and 142(1) were issued and served on the assessee. The assessee is engaged in the business of manufacture and sale of herbal products. During assessment proceedings, the AO observed that the assessee has shown investment of Rs.82,71,80,285/- in liquid Mutual Funds under the heads cash and cash equivalent out of which Rs.75,36,91,383/- is invested in mutual funds (Dividend Option) and shown exempt income of Rs.2,00,91,782/-. The AO issued a notice under section 142(1) dated 30.11.2019 directing the assessee to justify and show cause as to why expenses relating to exempt income should not be disallowed as per the provisions of section 14A read with Rule 8D. In the same notice, he also observed that assessee has earned dividend income of Rs 2 crores and against the above exempt income, assessee has disallowed expenses of Rs.36,000/- only. However, as per the financial statement, the assessee has shown investment of Rs.82.71 crores in the mutual funds while provisions of section 14A read with Rule 8D should not be disallowed to the extent of Rs.54,85,901/-. In response, assessee filed detailed submissions and it was explained that assessee has invested the surplus funds from its operation in mutual funds and not utilized any interest bearing funds in acquiring the mutual funds. It was also submitted that a staff of the company was deputed for this purpose and salary of the staff per month of Rs.3,000/- i.e. total Rs.36,000/- was disallowed relating to the expenses incurred directly to earn the exempt income. It was prayed that no disallowance under section 14A read with Rule 8D is applicable in its case and relied on the decision of various courts. After considering the detailed submissions of the assessee, the AO rejected the same. The AO observed that the provisions of Rule 8D was amended with effect from 02.06.2016. Hence, comparing the facts of any other judgment is irrelevant due to change in legal position applicable from the assessment year 2017-18. He further observed that assessee contended that only expenditure incurred on earning exempt income may be disallowed and no direct or indirect expenditure incurred to earn exempt income is not tenable as no specific supporting evidence was furnished by the assessee to establish that no expenses were incurred in earning of the dividend income. He observed that it is pertinent that every activity of the assessee has some cost or expenditure involved in it. He also relied on the Circular No.5/2014 dated 11.02.2014 of the CBDT. By relying on the decision in the case of Daga Capital Management Pvt. Ltd. (Mumabi ITAT in ITA No.8057/Mum/2003 dated 20.09.2008) and Chem Investment Pvt. Ltd. (Special Bench Delhi ITAT – 121 ITD 318), he observed that the provisions are applicable even in case of no exempt income, which fully justifiable in case of exempt income and no expenses were disallowed by the assessee. Accordingly, the AO invoked the provisions of amended Rule 8D disallowed 1% of the monthly average of investment as determined by him at page 10 of the assessment order i.e. Rs.38,82,01,105/-. Accordingly he disallowed Rs.38,82,011/-. 5. Aggrieved with the above order, assessee preferred an appeal before the ld. CIT(A) and ld. CIT(A), after considering the detailed submissions of the assessee, sustained the addition by observing as under: “5.1.3 The contention of the appellant that limited manpower and few transactions justify the lower disallowance cannot override the statutory prescription. Once the AO records dissatisfaction under section 14A(2), the computation of disallowance becomes formula-driven and mandatory. This principle has been upheld by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Godrej & Boyce Manufacturing Co. Ltd. v. DCIT (2017) 394 ITR 449 (SC), wherein it was held that where the AO properly records dissatisfaction, Rule 8D has to be applied. 5.1.4 CBDT Section 14A of Income Tax Act, 1961″ target=”_blank” rel=”noopener”>Circular No. 5/2014 dated 11.02.2014 also clarifies that expenditure relatable to exempt income cannot be allowed and that investment decisions require substantial administrative and managerial involvement. The Circular emphasizes that even if the assessee claims that only minimal expenditure is incurred, Section 14A read with Rule 80 must be applied objectively. The appellant’s contention that only negligible effort was required, therefore, cannot be accepted. 5.1.5 The appellant’s reliance on the ITAT Delhi order for AY 2013-14 is misplaced because the facts, law, and investment profile in that year were materially different, as discussed below: Distinguishing AY 2013-14 (ITAT Order) vs. AY 2017-18 (Present Case) a. Different Rule 8D Applicability: AY 2013-14 – Old Rule 80 (interest component + 0.5% administrative expenses). AY 2017-18 – Amended Rule 80, prescribing mandatory 1% disallowance, no interest computation. Thus, the legal framework itself is different. b. Interest Disallowance was the issue in AY 2013-14 The ITAT deleted the interest disallowance because the assessee had sufficient own funds and investments were made out of those funds. In AY 2017-18 no interest disallowance has been made or is in dispute, making the ITAT reasoning irrelevant to the present computation. c. Quantum and Activity Level Distinct:  AY 2013-14 investments: approx. Rs.10 crore; exempt income: Rs.40 lakh  AY 2017-18 investments: approx. Rs.82 crore; exempt income: Rs.2 crore. The substantially higher scale of investment and administrative involvement distinguishes the present year from the earlier one. d. Satisfaction of AO: In AY 2013-14, ITAT held AO failed to record proper satisfaction. In AY 2017-18, AO has explicitly recorded detailed dissatisfaction, fulfilling statutory requirements. e. Nature of Investments: AY 2013-14 involved mixed and multiple investments requiring nexus analysis. AY 2017-18 involves structured mutual fund investments where Rule 8D(2)(i) applies strictly. Hence, the appellant cannot derive any benefit from the earlier ITAT order. 5.1.6 The claim of the appellant that investments were made out of surplus funds is also not relevant for A.Y. 2017-18 because the amended Rule 80 no longer features the “interest expense” component. Even if own funds exceed investments, the 1 % administrative expenditure disallowance continues to apply as held by Hon’ble Delhi High Court in PCIT v. Caraf Builders & Developers (P) Ltd. (2019) 414 ITR 122 (Del), which held that administrative expenses under Rule 80(2) apply regardless of use of interest-free funds. 5.1.7 The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Maxopp Investment Ltd. v. CIT (2018) 402 ITR 640 (SC) has held that section 14A applies even where investments are strategic or incidental. The appellant’s argument that limited effort was required cannot dilute the statutory mandate. Considering the substantial investments (Rs.82.24 crore average), the high exempt income (Rs.2 crore) and the statutory requirement under amended Rule 8D, the disallowance of rs.38,46,011/- computed by the AO at 1% of the annual average investments is correct and requires no interference. Accordingly, Ground No.1 is dismissed and addition made by the AO upheld.” 6. Aggrieved with the above order, assessee is in appeal before us. 7. At the time of hearing, ld. AR of the assessee brought to our notice disallowance made by the AO by applying Rule 8D. Ld. AR submitted that assessee had already made bona fide and reasonable disallowance of Rs.36,000/- in the return of income itself. He submitted that the AO had not recorded any valid satisfaction as mandated under section 14A (2) with reference to the correctness of the assessee’s own disallowance working of Rs.36,000/-. He submitted that since the assessee had made suo motu disallowance, the AO has not made proper satisfaction as mandated under section 14A(2) of the Act. He submitted that ld. CIT(A) upheld the order of the AO by wrongly observing that no disallowance was made by the assessee which is contrary to the facts available on record. He further submitted that ld. CIT(A) has observed that the AO has recorded detailed and specific dissatisfaction whereas such dissatisfaction was based on the quantum of investment and exempt income and he had not recorded any defect with regard to assessee’s computation. He further submitted that the mutual fund transaction was auto redeemed and dividend income was credited automatically in the bank account without any active or effort of the assessee. Therefore, negligible administrative expenditure only is incurred. In this regard, he brought to our notice page 25 of the ld. CIT(A)’s order which is the finding of the ld. CIT(A). He further submitted that assessee has filed the detailed workings before the ld. CIT(A) showing the actual expenditure incurred for earning the exempt income which is only Rs.10,400/- whereas assessee has already disallowed much higher amount of Rs.36,000/-. In this regard, he relied on the decision of Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of Pr. CIT-2 vs. M/s. Tata Capital Ltd. in ITA No.1081 of 2018 order dated 03.04.2024 for the issue of non-recording of satisfaction as mandated under section 14A(2) of the Act. 8. On the other hand, ld. DR of the Revenue submitted that the issue under consideration is disallowance of expenditure relating to exempt income for the assessment year 2017-18. He submitted that from the current assessment year, the Rule 8D is amended and new rule is applicable. The decision relied upon by the assessee is relating to pre-amended provisions which has no application. Therefore, he relied on the detailed findings of the lower authorities. 9. Considered the rival submissions and material placed on record. We observed that assessee has earned exempted dividend income of Rs.2,00,91,781.54 and disallowed an expenditure of Rs.36,000/- only for earning the above said exempt income. When the AO issued notice under section 142(1) asking the assessee to justify issuance to show cause why expenses should not be disallowed as per section 14A. In response, assessee has submitted that it has itself disallowed an amount of Rs.3,000/- per month i.e. total Rs.36,000/- in computation of the total income as declared in the ITR considering expenses related to exempt income out of staff salary and other expenses like stationery and telephone etc. We noticed that the AO has recorded in the notice issued u/s 142(1) itself that assessee has disallowed only Rs.36,000/- against the exempt income and he further observed at para 3.5 (ii) of the assessment order that no specific supporting evidence has been furnished by the assessee to establish that no expenses has been incurred in earning of the dividend income. With the above observation, the AO considered the amended provisions under Rule 8D with effect from Assessment Year 2017-18 and calculated the monthly average of opening and closing of the value of investment from which exempt income is not form part of the total income. As per the amended Rule 8D, he disallowed 1% of the monthly average investment. Before us, ld. AR made a detailed submissions that AO has not recorded satisfaction u/s 14A(2) with reference to the correctness of the assessee’s own disallowance working. We noticed that assessee has only disallowed Rs.36,000/- and informed the AO that only a staff was involved in this transaction and has considered staff salary and certain administrative expenses for disallowance. No detailed submissions and basis for suo motu disallowance were submitted before the AO. That being the case, there is no requirement for the Assessing Officer to record his dissatisfaction for suo motu disallowance. The AO based on the information available on record and the mandatory provisions applicable with effect from AY 2017-18 determined the disallowance under section 14A. The AO in fact directed the assessee to justify and show-cause as to why expenses should not be disallowed after observing that assessee had only disallowed Rs.36,000/-. From the observations made by the AO in notice issued under section 142(1) and the assessment order, we observed that AO has recorded the dissatisfaction. We further noticed that assessee has filed a detailed submissions of disallowance before the ld. CIT(A) only and the above detailed submissions were never submitted before the AO. We noticed that the basis of detailed disallowance submitted before ld. CIT (A) is not commensurate with the actual suo motu disallowance made by the assessee. From the record that satisfaction recorded by the AO to reject the expenses suo motu disallowed by the AO is proper as per section 14A(2) of the Act, therefore we are inclined to reject the submissions made by the assessee. The decisions relied upon by the assessee are distinguishable to the facts of the present case. Therefore, we are inclined to dismiss Grounds No1.1 to 1.8 raised by the assessee. 10.
