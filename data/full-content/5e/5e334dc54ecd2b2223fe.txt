Articles 3 min read Manappuram Finance's Stage 2 gold loans jump tenfold to 5%; lender shifts towards one year bullet loans Manappuram Finance's Stage 2 gold loan assets rose to around 5% of the portfolio in Q2 FY27, against about 0.5% in the previous quarter. Management said the level was much lower a year ago, but did not quantify it, and is now gradually shifting customers towards longer-tenure, one-year bullet loans. During the earnings call, management said Stage 2 levels were much lower in the year-ago quarter.Stage 2 assets are loans that have experienced a significant increase in credit risk but are not yet classified as non-performing assets. The increase, therefore, does not mean that 5 per cent of the gold loan book has turned into bad loans. Manappuram's gold loan gross non-performing assets (GNPA) stood at 1.04 per cent in Q2 FY27. Advt The company's consolidated gold loan AUM stood at Rs 60,640 crore at the end of September, up 6.4 per cent sequentially and 92.5 per cent year-on-year. Its average gold loan-to-value (LTV) ratio was 64.5 per cent, compared with 65.6 per cent in the previous quarter.Longer-tenure products to gain shareManagement attributed the rise in Stage 2 largely to customers getting accustomed to the newer product structures. It said early collection trends were encouraging and that the company had collected a fair amount from customers since the levels seen in September."These are all early reads and largely attributable to behaviour of customers getting used to this kind of product," the Group CFO Bhuvanesh Tharashankar said during the earnings call. He added that Stage 2 had already started trending down, based on the early readings, but fresh inflows into the category would continue.The company said it was difficult to predict the level at which Stage 2 assets would eventually normalise. Management did not commit to a timeline or indicate that the ratio would return to the 0.5 per cent level recorded in Q1.On the product mix, management said the portfolio continued to be largely made up of longer-tenure loans, with EMI-based and shorter-tenure loans forming a smaller part. It is gradually moving from three-month products to six-month and then one-year products.The lender plans to retain shorter-tenure loans for customers who have a clear short-term borrowing requirement. For customers who are not certain about when they would be able to redeem the gold, it intends to offer one-year products instead. Advt Management also said that around 60-65 per cent of customers with longer-tenure bullet loans paid interest promptly, even when there was no scheduled periodic payment. It did not see a change in this repayment behaviour.To address overdue interest and LTV shortfalls, the company sends notices and contacts customers, who then visit branches to pay the interest and bring their LTV back to current levels, management said. It added that this was an existing practice.Vehicle finance book continues to shrinkManappuram's vehicle and equipment finance business continued to see a contraction in its loan book, with AUM declining to Rs 2,169 crore in Q2 FY27 from Rs 4,057 crore a year ago, a fall of 46.5 per cent. It was down 15.3 per cent sequentially from Rs 2,562 crore in Q1. Advt The segment's GNPA ratio increased to 16.9 per cent in Q2 from 13.4 per cent in the previous quarter. The company has been prioritising collections and reassessing its strategy for the business rather than pursuing fresh disbursement-led growth.The investor presentation showed that the vehicle finance portfolio had declined from Rs 4,773 crore in FY25 to Rs 2,991 crore in Q4 FY26, before falling further over the first two quarters of FY27.
