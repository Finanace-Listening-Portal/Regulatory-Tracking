Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Manoranjan Dash Vs Commissionerate of CT GST (GSTAT Cuttack) Summary: The GST Appellate Tribunal, State Bench Cuttack, decided an appeal concerning input tax credit claimed for FY 2020-21 by M/s Mother Power House Limited, represented by its director, Manoranjan Dash. The appellant, engaged in supplying works contract services to the Government of Odisha and others, had availed ITC of ₹27,07,095.73, whereas the credit reflected in GSTR-2A was ₹15,70,871.43. Following an unanswered scrutiny notice and subsequent adjudication, a tax demand of ₹11,34,474 was raised, together with interest and penalty. The first appellate authority admitted additional credit on reconciliation and reduced the tax demand to ₹1,02,012, with interest of ₹98,680 and penalty as recorded in its demand table. The appellant approached the Tribunal seeking admission of the remaining credit and challenging the interest liability. The appellant submitted that it possessed the supporting invoices and that non-reflection of invoice details in GSTR-2A did not necessarily establish non-payment of tax by suppliers. Suppliers might have discharged tax through their returns or through DRC-03 while filing annual returns, and the recipient had no control over their reporting or payment. It relied on the provisional-credit mechanism under Section 41, questioned the use of Rule 36(4) for interest computation, and invoked Section 75(7) against interest not indicated in the show cause notice. It also argued that summary-level comparison of GSTR-2A and GSTR-3B, without identifying the specific unmatched invoices, prevented it from producing invoice-specific evidence of supplier tax payment. The Revenue maintained that actual payment of tax to the Government was mandatory and that the claimant bore the burden of establishing ITC eligibility. It cited Astha Enterprises, Maruti Enterprise and Bhandari Scrap Traders; the Tribunal observed that the constitutional validity of Section 16(2)(c) had not been challenged and that those authorities therefore required no examination or elaboration. Reading Sections 16(2)(c), 41 and the erstwhile Section 42 together, the Tribunal held that provisional credit remained subject to statutory conditions and did not become an absolute entitlement merely because it had been credited to the electronic credit ledger. Although it acknowledged that non-population of invoices was not conclusive proof of non-payment, it interpreted the applicable statutory scheme as treating non-disclosure and subsequent disclosure as the basis for reversal and reclaim of credit, with corresponding interest consequences. It noted that the discrepancy had been communicated through the scrutiny notice dated 19.02.2022 and that the appellant had failed to substantiate supplier tax payment despite opportunities at the adjudication and first appellate stages. The reconciliation statement itself admitted that invoices covering ITC of ₹1,02,011.28 had not been uploaded. Accordingly, the Tribunal declined a further remand on tax eligibility and confirmed the tax demand upheld in the first appellate order. It also held that the subsequent omission of Section 42 did not displace its application to FY 2020-21. On interest, the Tribunal distinguished between interest on the confirmed ineligible ITC and a separate levy concerning provisional credit exceeding the Rule 36(4) limit. The latter levy was deleted because it was outside the original show cause notice. For interest on the confirmed tax demand, the Tribunal directed fresh computation strictly under Section 42(8), requiring invoice-level identification of unmatched invoices, relevant dates and the input tax involved. It held that interest under that provision was mandatory and incidental to recovery of the ineligible credit, and explained the computation period by reference to availment and repayment through addition to output liability or payment of the demand. The appeal was thus disposed of by confirming the tax demand, setting aside and remanding the interest computation, and deleting the separate Rule 36(4)-based interest levy. No order as to costs was made. Cases Discussed Bhandari Scrap Traders v. Union of India, Supreme Court of India, order dated 24.07.2026; SLP(C) 2393/2026 as recorded in the supplied order. The Revenue cited the decision concerning the constitutional validity of Section 16(2)(c) and refusal to read it down. The Tribunal stated that constitutional validity was not challenged in the appeal and that the cited authorities required no examination or elaboration. Maruti Enterprise v. Union of India, Gujarat High Court, decision dated 01.05.2026. Cited by the Revenue for the constitutional validity of Section 16(2)(c); the Tribunal did not separately examine the constitutional issue. Astha Enterprises v. The Revenue relied on the decision for the requirement to satisfy the statutory conditions governing ITC, including actual payment of tax by the supplier. For deciding the issues enumerated as above, the statutory scheme is required to be examined with reference to the relevant provisions governing input tax credit in the Act and the Rules framed there under. Chapter 5 of the Act deals with input tax credit and under chapter 5 Section 16 provides for the eligibility conditions for taking input tax credit. The relevant portions of said section 16, reads as follows: – “(1) Every registered person shall, subject to such conditions and restrictions as may be prescribed and in the manner specified in section 49, be entitled to take credit of input tax charged on any supply of goods or services or both to him which are used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of his business and the said amount shall be credited to the electronic credit ledger of such person. (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in this section, no registered person shall be entitled to the credit of any input tax in respect of any supply of goods or services or both to him unless, – (a) he is in possession of a tax invoice or debit note issued by a supplier registered under this Act, or such other tax paying documents as may be prescribed; (b) he has received the goods or services or both. Explanation. — For the purposes of this clause, it shall be deemed that the registered person has received the goods or, as the case may be, services–– (i) where the goods are delivered by the supplier to a recipient or any other person on the direction of such registered person, whether acting as an agent or otherwise, before or during movement of goods, either by way of transfer of documents of title to goods or otherwise; (ii) where the services are provided by the supplier to any person on the direction of and on account of such registered person (c) subject to the provisions of section 41, the tax charged in respect of such supply has been actually paid to the Government, either in cash or through utilisation of input tax credit admissible in respect of the said supply; and (d) he has furnished the return under section 39 xxxxxx”. 10. On reading the aforesaid statutory provisions it transpires that, Section 16 (1) is creating the entitlement to take credit of input tax “charged on any supply of goods or services or both to him which are used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of his business” implying there by, the first and foremost condition for being eligible to ITC is the intent for using inward supply of goods and services in business ; and the extent of such entitlement is subject to some conditions and restrictions. However, this aspect having not been examined and relied upon in framing of the allegation and confirmation of the demand in dispute in the instant appeal, requires no examination. 11. Section 16(2) puts in place some mandatory conditions for being entitled to credit of input tax and the said mandatory conditions have been set forth in clause (a) (b) (c) and (d) of this said subsection. There is no allegation regarding nonfulfillment of the condition set forth in clause (a) (b) and (d), thus requiring no examination. 12. The remaining condition in clause (c) of section 16(2) makes payment of tax charged in any supply transaction mandatory for the recipient to be entitled to take credit of such input tax. While putting such a strong condition, the said clause is made subjected the provisions of section 41. Therefore, for proper understanding of the scheme, examination of section 41 is necessary. 13. Section 41 has been amended in the meanwhile, and prior to the amendment the said section 41 reads as follows: – “(1) Every registered person shall, subject to such conditions and restrictions as may be prescribed, be entitled to take the credit of eligible input tax, as self-assessed, in his return and such amount shall be credited on a provisional basis to his electronic credit ledger. (2) The credit referred to in sub-section (1) shall be utilised only for payment of self-assessed output tax as per the return referred to in the said sub-section.” 14. Section 41 entitles tax payers to take credit of eligible input tax as per self -assessment in the return with further mandate for crediting such self-assessed input tax in the return to his electronic credit ledger. While creating such entitlement, section 41 expressly makes it provisional and subjected to some conditions and restrictions. This implies that, credit of input tax allowed under section 41 of the Act is merely provisional and not free from the statutory conditions and restrictions. On conjoint reading of the provisions of section 16 (2) (c) and section 41m, it is made amply clear that while tax payer is allowed under section 41 to take credit of input tax paid on any inward supply, such credit is provisional and not absolute. Such credit is also subjected to the statutory conditions and restrictions. Thus, taking shelter of section 41 the appellant cannot skip the statutory conditions put in clause (a) (b) (c) and (d) of section 16 (2) of the Act including the primary condition in section 16 (1) of the Act. 15. The statutory scheme for availing input tax credit continued in section 42 and 43 of the Act as well, which provide for matching, reversal and reclaim of input tax credit. For deciding the issue in question, the provisions in sub section (3), (5), (7), (8) and (9) of section 42 are relevant. The relevant portions of the said section 42 read as follows: – “42. Matching, reversal and reclaim of input tax credit. – (1) xxxxxxxx (2) xxxxxxx (3) Where the input tax credit claimed by a recipient in respect of an inward supply is in excess of the tax declared by the supplier for the same supply or the outward supply is not declared by the supplier in his valid returns, the discrepancy shall be communicated to both such persons in such manner as may be prescribed. (4) xxxxxx (5) The amount in respect of which any discrepancy is communicated under sub-section (3) and which is not rectified by the supplier in his valid return for the month in which discrepancy is communicated shall be added to the output tax liability of the recipient, in such manner as may be prescribed, in his return for the month succeeding the month in which the discrepancy is communicated. (6) xxxxxx (7) The recipient shall be eligible to reduce, from his output tax liability, the amount added under sub-section (5), if the supplier declares the details of the invoice or debit note in his valid return within the time specified in sub-section (9) of section 39. (8) A recipient in whose output tax liability any amount has been added under sub-section (5) or sub-section (6), shall be liable to pay interest at the rate specified under sub-section (1) of section 50 on the amount so added from the date of availing of credit till the corresponding additions are made under the said sub-sections. (9) Where any reduction in output tax liability is accepted under sub-section (7), the interest paid under sub-section (8) shall be refunded to the recipient by crediting the amount in the corresponding head of his electronic cash ledger in such manner as may be prescribed: Provided that the amount of interest to be credited in any case shall not exceed the amount of interest paid by the supplier. (10) The amount reduced from the output tax liability in contravention of the provisions of sub-section (7) shall be added to the output tax liability of the recipient in his return for the month in which such contravention takes place and such recipient shall be liable to pay interest on the amount so added at the rate specified in sub-section (3) of section 50.” 16. On conjoint reading of section 41 and 42 of the Act, it transpires that, while section 41 allows tax payers to take credit of input tax provisionally without ensuring payment of tax paid to the suppliers, section 42 (5) mandates the recipients to add the amount of ITC not declared by the corresponding supplier to the output tax liability of the month specified in the said sub section. The mandate for adding the undeclared input tax (by the supplier) essentially implies refund of ITC availed against transactions which have not been declared by the corresponding suppliers. Here, the cause for refund of the undeclared ITC is simply failure on the part of the supplier to declare the transaction in the prescribed manner and not failure to pay tax. This scheme further stipulates in sub section 7 to reduce the output tax liability if the supplier declares the invoice in its valid return within the specified time i.e., before the due date for furnishing the return for the month of September or second quarter of the succeeding financial year. Thus, refund by way of enhancing output liability as mandated in sub section (3) above does not extinguish the entitlement fully, but rather suspends the entitlement up to the date the unpopulated invoices are declared by the corresponding suppliers. 17. Further, subsection (8) creates liability to pay interest at the rate specified in section 50 (1) of the Act on the amount refunded by way of enhancing the liability and such interest is to be calculated for the period commencing from the date of availing credit till the date of refund by way of addition to the output tax liability. Again, when a case for reduction in liability arises under subsection (9) due to uploading of unpopulated invoices by the supplier(s), there is a mandate for payment of interest to the recipient not exceeding the interest paid by the supplier due to delayed reporting of transactions followed by delayed discharge of liability. Thus, the mandate in subsection (9) secures the entitlement of recipients to re-avail credit of input tax with interest on discharge of tax liability by the supplier. 18. The scheme for recovery of interest under subsection (8) from the recipients and payment of interest under sub section (9), even though linked to non-disclosure and disclosure of the invoice details by the supplier, it essentially means and implies discharge of liability on the unreported transactions by the supplier. Indisputably, non-population of the invoice details in the portal is not a conclusive proof of tax having not been paid. But on holistic examination of the scheme of availing ITC under section 41 and reversal and reclaim of ITC under section 42 of the Act it transpires that, the statute recognises non-disclosure as non-payment tax and disclosure as payment of tax, and accordingly, the statutory provisions have been drafted providing for reversal of ITC (by enhancing the output tax liability) with interest in the event of non-disclosure of the invoice details by the supplier; and further allowing reclaim of ITC(by reducing the output tax liability) with interest, on subsequent disclosure of the invoice details by the supplier. 19. The analysis of section 41 and 42, as above, clarifies the question raised by the appellant questioning non-population of invoice details in GSTR 2A is not a proof of tax having not been paid by the supplier. Section 42 of the Act implicitly recognises non-disclosure of invoice details in the prescribed manner as non-payment of tax by the supplier and subsequent disclosure of the undisclosed invoices as payment of tax for which the statute mandates payment of interest by the supplier and refund of interest to the recipient. If it were not so, the statute would not have mandated for payment of interest and refund of interest to the recipient on non-discloser or disclosure of invoice details by the suppliers. Thus, the answer to the questioned raised by appellant is in affirmative. To make it clearer, as per the statutory scheme envisaged in section 42 of the Act, non-disclosure of invoice details in the prescribed form and manner means and implies tax having not been paid. 20. The analysis of section 42(8) also clarifies the time period for computing the interest liability commencing from the date of availing credit to the date on which the amount is added to output liability on non-payment of tax by the supplier. In this case in hand, the appellant having not refunded in eligible ITC by way of enhancing the output liability, the period of interest is to be computed from the date ITC is availed, albeit ineligibly, till the date of payment of the demand. To make it clearer, Section 41 of the allows credit of self-assessed eligible input tax to the electronic credit ledger and self-assessment tool is provided in the periodical return. Thus, the date of availing the ineligible credit is day after the due date for filing the periodical return in which credit of input tax against the un-populated transaction was availed. 21. In this context, a supplementary question may be raised because of subsequent omission of section 42 and also because no exercise was undertaken for matching of invoices and communicating the discrepancy to the recipient. Admittedly section 42 envisaged matching of invoice and communicating the discrepancy to the recipient affording opportunity for rectification. Nevertheless, as revealed from OIO, summary level matching of ITC available vis-a-vis ITC availed has been done and the discrepancy noticed has been communicated to the appellant in the notice issued under section 61 of the Act on 19.02.2022 which was much before the proceeding under section 73 of the Act was initiated. The aforesaid scrutiny seems to have been undertaken long after the period specified in section 42 (7) and section 39(9) of the Act for availing ITC on account of delayed reporting of invoice details by the suppliers. The appellant seems to have failed to avail the opportunity so afforded to explain the discrepancy at that stage. Thus, to some extent, the mandatory requirement for communicating the unmatched ITC has been met through return scrutiny under section 61 of the Act. 22. The appellant seems to have wasted the opportunity to explain the tax payment position in respect of the un-populated transactions both at the adjudication stage as well as the first appeal stage. While participating in the hearing of the impugned appeal the appellant has submitted a reconciliation statement of the amount of ITC availed during the material year of 2020-21 where in it has been categorically admitted that the invoices covering ITC amounting to Rs 1,02,011.28 have not been uploaded in the GST portal by the corresponding suppliers. Even at stage, no credible effort seems to have been made to prove payment of tax by those defaulting suppliers for which, the impugned appeal has been decided reducing the tax demand to Rs 1,02,011.28 due to non-fulfilment of the condition in section 16(2)(c) of the Act. In view of such factual back-ground further opportunity by of remand is not justifiable. 23. From the legislative history of the Act it is seen that, section 42 was omitted in 2022, but while omitting section 42, section 41 was also amended incorporating the provisions for reversal of ITC provisionally availed with applicable interest, in the event of non-payment tax by the corresponding suppliers, and reclaim of ITC by re-availing the reversed amount to the extent paid by the supplier. In essence, the provisions of reversal and reclaim of ITC with interest under the amended section 41 is equivalent to the provisions of addition and reduction of output tax liability under section 42 of the Act. In either case, the core idea is to recover the ITC from the recipient which has not been paid by the supplier. In view of the basic statutory scheme of reversal and reclaim continuing in the amended section 41 of the Act, there is no breach or deviation from the core scheme due to subsequent omission of section 42. Nevertheless, the demand in dispute in the instant appeal relates to the financial year 2020-21 and hence the provision of section 42, as it stood then, would apply to the present appeal. 24. The next contentious issue is in relation to applicability of Rule 36 (4) in the matter of determination of interest liability. Rule 36 (4), as it stood during the relevant year 2020-21 reads as follows: – “Input tax credit to be availed by a registered person in respect of invoices or debit notes, the details of which have not been furnished by the suppliers under sub-section (1) of section 37, in FORM GSTR-1 or using the invoice furnishing facility shall not exceed 5 per cent. of the eligible credit available in respect of invoices or debit notes the details of which have been furnished by the suppliers under sub-section (1) of section 37 in FORM GSTR-1 or using the invoice furnishing facility. Provided that the said condition shall apply cumulatively for the period February, March, April, May, June, July and August, 2020 and the return in FORM GSTR-3B for the tax period September, 2020 shall be furnished with the cumulative adjustment of input tax credit for the said months in accordance with the condition above. Provided further that such condition shall apply cumulatively for the period April, May and June, 2021 and the return in FORM GSTR-3B for the tax period June, 2021 or quarter ending June, 2021, as the case may be, shall be furnished with the cumulative adjustment of input tax credit for the said months in accordance with the condition above.” 25. Rule 36 (4), as above, simply puts a limit on availing provisional ITC in excess of the amount of ITC available in GSTR 2A. However, it does not validate or makes the amount of provisional ITC availed absolute. The amount of ITC availed provisionally continues to remain provisional, and is reversible under the erstwhile section 42 or under subsection (2) of the amended section 41 in the event of tax not paid by the supplier. Therefore, the additional leeway provided under rule 36(4) by way of additional ITC within the specified limit does not validate the provisional ITC availed in the event of non-payment of tax by the supplier. In the result interest liability is due from the date the ITC is availed ineligibly to the date the amount is refunded by way of addition to the output liability or otherwise. 26. In the impugned OIA, interest has been computed on two accounts- first on the amount of tax demand confirmed by holding the unmatched ITC as ineligible and second on the amount of provisional ITC availed in excess of the limit set in rule 36(4). Levy of interest on the second account was clearly not contemplated in the SCN and thus outside the scope of both the original adjudication as well as the impugned appeal requiring deletion for being outside the scope of the original SCN. Again, interest on the tax demand upheld in the impugned appeal having not been computed strictly as per the mandate in section 42(8) requires remand to the Adjudicating Officer for fresh determination of interest liability strictly as per section 42 (8) of the Act. 27. For computation of the interest liability strictly as per section 42 (8) of the Act, invoice level matching is required so as to specify the invoices not populated in GSTR 2A, the date of receipt of the invoice and the amount of input tax involved in such invoices so as to determine the date from which the interest is due in respect of a specified invoice. For identifying and specifying the invoices not populated, the date of such invoice, and the input tax related to such invoice for determining the interest liability as per section 42 (8) of the Act remand is warranted. 28. On the question raised placing reliance upon section 75 (7) of the Act it is made clear that interest liability to be computed under section 42 (8) of the Act is mandatory and incidental to recovery of the ITC ineligibly availed. Therefore, whether or not levy of interest was proposed in the SCN, it cannot be separated from recovery of ineligible ITC in view of the mandate for such recovery along with the amount of ineligible ITC availed by the recipient. Besides the mandate in section 42 is for the recipient availing ITC ineligibly to pay back the ITC with interest as per section 42(8) of the Act. In view of such statutory provision mandating recovery of the ineligible ITC with interest computed strictly as per section 42 (8), the correct approach would be to compute the interest liability strictly as per section 42(8) of the Act. 29. In view of the discussion above, the following orders are hereby passed: (i) amount of tax demand upheld in the impugned appeal order is hereby confirmed; (ii) the order relating to computation of the interest liability is set aside and remanded back for fresh computation strictly as per section 42 (8) of the Act after identifying and specifying the invoices not populated in the GST portal. (iii) Amount of interest levied placing reliance upon Rule 36(4) is hereby deleted since such levy was not contemplated in the SCN issued under Section 73. The appeal is disposed of accordingly. No order as to costs. The judgement is pronounced in the open Court on 08-10-2026.
