Articles 2 min read Fed hike, RBI moves hand India bonds a fifth weekly loss Indian government bonds have fallen for five consecutive weeks. A Federal Reserve rate hike and RBI liquidity measures emboldened bond market bears. Global yields reached multi-decade peaks, and the Fed raised rates by twenty-five basis points. The Reserve Bank of India sold bonds, draining significant liquidity from the system. This situation increases the likelihood of an RBI rate hike in October. Advt The Fed's widely expected 25-basis-point rate hike on Wednesday fortified its inflation-fighting resolve in investors' eyes, while also raising the chances of an RBI rate hike in October, traders said.The Fed's decision will add to calls for the RBI to hike in its October meeting to maintain India's interest rate differential with the US and mitigate any large potential foreign fund outflow, said Ajit Banerjee, president and chief investment officer at Shriram Life Insurance.The US 10-year Treasury yield probed 5.03% this week, its highest level since the 2008 financial crisis, spurring foreign investors to retreat from risk, including by unloading Indian government bonds that are part of global indexes.Meanwhile, the RBI, through its first open market operation bond selling in nine years, drained liquidity equivalent to nearly 0.2% of total bank deposits on Thursday.The RBI will likely step up these operations going forward, traders said, as a large liquidity surplus in the banking system can blunt monetary-policy transmission by curbing banks' need to borrow at the RBI's policy rate.RATESOvernight indexed swaps rose on firmer domestic rate hike wagers.For the week,the one-year rate leapt 4 bps to 6.0725%, the two-year rate rose 4 bps to 6.28% and the five-year rate added nearly 2 bps to 6.5875%.
