Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Utkrishta Shiksha Samaj Samiti Vs National Faceless Assessment Centre (ITAT Indore) Non-Appearance Cannot Replace a Reasoned Order: ITAT Revives Society’s Appeal Against ₹90.88 Lakh Addition The Indore Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) has restored an educational society’s appeal for fresh adjudication, holding that the CIT(A) must decide the issues on merits even when the taxpayer fails to appear. In Utkrishta Shiksha Samaj Samiti v. National Faceless Assessment Centre, the Tribunal also condoned a stated 378-day delay, subject to verification of the delay period and the explanation from the appellate record. The ruling concerns Assessment Year 2021-22 and an addition of ₹90,88,407 made in a best judgment assessment. However, the Tribunal neither deleted the addition nor granted exemption. It directed the first appellate authority to reconsider the dispute through a reasoned order after providing a reasonable opportunity of hearing. Returned Loss Replaced by Assessed Income of ₹86.17 Lakh The assessee society filed its return on 31 March 2022, declaring nil total income after reporting a loss of ₹4,70,655. Its case was selected for compulsory scrutiny under criteria covering taxpayers who continued claiming exemption or deduction despite the relevant registration or approval not having been granted, or having been cancelled or withdrawn. During assessment proceedings, the society did not comply with the Assessing Officer’s notices. A show-cause notice dated 7 December 2022, issued under Section 144, required a response by 10 December 2022. No response was received. Consequently, the Assessing Officer completed the assessment under Section 144 read with Section 144B on 22 December 2022. After making an addition of ₹90,88,407, the officer determined total income at ₹86,17,752, against the reported loss. Non-Compliance Continued Before CIT(A) The society challenged the assessment before the CIT(A), filing its first appeal with a four-day delay. However, it again failed to comply with appellate notices. The CIT(A) dismissed the appeal primarily because of this non-compliance, leaving the disputed additions without substantive appellate examination. Before the Tribunal, the society challenged the assessment and the addition, describing the latter as having been made on an estimation basis. It also alleged denial of a reasonable opportunity of hearing. Alternatively, the society sought relief under Section 10(23C)(iiiad), contending that it existed solely for educational purposes and not for profit, with aggregate annual receipts below ₹1 crore. These remained the assessee’s claims; the Tribunal did not determine their correctness. New Management Explained the 378-Day Delay The Tribunal appeal was filed on 13 May 2025, accompanied by an application seeking condonation of a stated 378-day delay. Through an affidavit dated 21 March 2025, the society explained that its newly elected President assumed office on 30 July 2024 and initiated steps to appoint a tax consultant to examine pending income-tax matters. According to the society, the new management discovered the adverse appellate order while reviewing those matters. It thereafter obtained records, sought professional advice and prepared the appeal. The delay, it submitted, was neither intentional nor deliberate. The Revenue opposed condonation, citing the society’s repeated failure to participate before both lower authorities. It argued that a change in management or subsequent appointment of a consultant could not, by itself, constitute sufficient cause for the prolonged delay. Earlier Defaults Did Not Conclusively Defeat Condonation The Tribunal acknowledged the society’s earlier non-compliance but held that this conduct did not conclusively answer whether sufficient cause existed for the subsequent delay. Examining the application under Section 253(5), the Bench observed that “sufficient cause” should receive a construction advancing substantial justice, provided the explanation was bona fide and did not reveal deliberate inaction, negligence or lack of good faith. Considering the management change and the subsequent steps described in the affidavit, the Tribunal adopted a liberal approach. It condoned the delay subject to verification of the period and the explanation covering it from the appellate record. Section 250(6) Requires Findings and Reasons On the appellate dismissal, the Tribunal emphasised the requirements of Section 250(6). A CIT(A)’s order must identify the points for determination, state the decision on those points, and provide reasons supporting that decision. Therefore, even where an appellant does not appear, the authority must consider the available record and dispose of the appeal according to law. Here, the additions had not been examined and adjudicated on merits, warranting fresh consideration. Registration Remand Did Not Establish Exemption The Tribunal also noted its separate order dated 10 August 2026, restoring the society’s Section 12AB registration dispute to the CIT(Exemptions). That development could affect the exemption claim, depending on the applicable provisions and assessment year. However, the Tribunal expressly clarified that the registration remand did not itself establish entitlement to exemption. Setting aside the CIT(A)’s order, it directed fresh adjudication on merits, reasonable hearing and a speaking order. The society must cooperate and avoid unnecessary adjournments. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes, with both the addition and exemption claim remaining open. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF ITAT INDORE 1. This is an Appeal filed by the Assessee under section 253 of the Income Tax Act 1961, [ herein after referred to as the Act for the sake of brevity] before this tribunal as & by way of a second Appeal. The Assessee is aggrieved by the order dated 05.02.2024 passed by the Ld. CIT(A) U/S 250 of the Act, which is herein after referred to as the “Impugned Order”. 2. The assessee is a society which filed its return of income on 31.03.2022 under section 139(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as “the Act”), declaring nil total income after reporting a loss of Rs. 4,70,655/-. 3. The case of the assessee was selected for compulsory scrutiny under the criteria relating to cases where registration or approval under various provisions of the Act, including sections 12A, 35(1)(ii)/(iia)/(iii) and 10(23C), had either not been granted or had been cancelled or withdrawn by the competent authority, but the assessee continued to claim exemption or deduction in its return of income. During the course of assessment proceedings, the assessee failed to comply with the notices issued by the learned Assessing Officer (AO). Consequently, a show-cause notice dated 07.12.2022 was issued under section 144 of the Act, requiring the assessee to furnish its explanation on or before 10.12.2022. However, no response was received from the assessee. In the absence of compliance, the learned AO completed the assessment under section 144 read with section 144B of the Act vide order dated 22.12.2022, determining the total income at Rs. 86,17,752/- as against the returned loss of Rs. 4,70,655/-, after making an addition of Rs. 90,88,407/-. 4. Aggrieved by the assessment order, the assessee preferred an appeal before the learned Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals) [CIT(A)]. The said appeal was filed with a delay of four days. During the appellate proceedings, the assessee failed to comply with the notices issued by the learned CIT(A). Consequently, the learned CIT(A), vide order dated 06.02.2024, dismissed the appeal on account of non-compliance by the assessee. 5. Being aggrieved by the aforesaid appellate order, the assessee has preferred the present appeal before the Tribunal on 13.05.2025, accompanied by an application seeking condonation of delay on the following ground of appeal: – 1. On the facts and circumstances of the case and applicable law. Ld. CIT(A) erred in sustaining the assessment order passed by Ld. AO u/s 144 which is contrary to the material on records and provisions of the Act, unjust and bad in law. 2. On the facts and circumstances of the case and applicable law, Ld. CIT(A) erred in sustaining the addition of Rs. 90,88,407 made on estimation basis 3. On the facts and circumstances of the case and applicable law, Ld. CIT(A) erred in sustaining the addition of Rs. 90,88,407 made on estimation basis without providing reasonable opportunity of being heard 4. On the facts and circumstances of the case and applicable law and without prejudice to the above grounds of appeal, both Ld. AO and Ld. CIT(A) erred in not granting the relief u/s 10(23C)(iiiad) more particularly when the assessee exists solely for educational purposes and not for profit and the aggregate annual receipts of the assessee are less than 1 crores 6. At the outset, we observe that the present appeal has been filed belatedly. As stated in the application, there is a delay of 378 days in filing the appeal before the Tribunal. In support of the application seeking condonation of delay, the assessee has filed an affidavit dated 21.03.2025, explaining the circumstances which resulted in the delayed filing of the appeal. It has been submitted that the newly elected President of the assessee society assumed office on 30.07.2024 and thereafter took the initiative to appoint a tax consultant for examining and attending to the pending income-tax matters of the society. 7. It has further been submitted that during the course of examining the pending tax proceedings, the newly elected management came to know about the appellate order dated 06.02.2024 passed by the learned CIT(A). Thereafter, the management initiated the process of obtaining the relevant records, seeking professional advice and preparing the appeal, which was eventually filed before the Tribunal on 13.05.2025. The assessee has submitted that the delay was neither intentional nor deliberate and has requested that the same be condoned in the interest of substantial justice. 8. Per contra, the learned Departmental Representative (DR) strongly opposed the application seeking condonation of delay. The learned DR submitted that the conduct of the assessee throughout the assessment as well as the first appellate proceedings clearly demonstrates continuous non-compliance with the statutory notices issued by the respective authorities. It was further submitted that the assessee failed to participate in the assessment proceedings despite being afforded opportunities by the learned AO. Similarly, the assessee did not appear or furnish any submissions before the learned CIT(A), resulting in the dismissal of its appeal. 9. The learned DR contended that a mere change in the management of the society or subsequent appointment of a consultant cannot, by itself, constitute sufficient cause for condonation of an inordinate delay in filing the appeal. In support of these contentions, the learned DR relied upon the orders passed by the lower authorities and the judicial decisions furnished before the Bench on 13.10.2026, opposing the condonation of delay. 10. We have heard the rival submissions of the learned representatives of both parties and carefully perused the material available on record, including the application for condonation of delay, the affidavit dated 21.03.2025 filed by the assessee, the orders of the authorities below and the judicial precedents relied upon by the learned DR. The preliminary issue arising for our consideration is whether the assessee has demonstrated sufficient cause for not presenting the appeal within the prescribed period of limitation, as contemplated under section 253(5) of the Act. 11. We observe that the assessee society remained non- compliant during the assessment proceedings as well as the proceedings before the learned CIT(A). The learned AO completed the assessment under section 144 read with section 144B of the Act, and the learned CIT(A) dismissed the appeal on account of non-compliance. We further observe that the assessee has attributed the delay in filing the present appeal to the change in the management of the society. As stated in the affidavit, the newly elected President assumed office on 30.07.2024 and thereafter initiated steps to appoint a consultant and examine the pending income-tax matters of the society. It is pertinent to note that the explanation furnished by the assessee is required to be examined in the context of the circumstances prevailing during the relevant period. Although the earlier non-compliance on the part of the assessee cannot be overlooked, the same, by itself, does not conclusively determine whether sufficient cause existed for the delay in filing the present appeal. 12. We are conscious of the settled legal position that the expression “sufficient cause” employed in the provisions relating to condonation of delay must receive a construction which advances substantial justice, provided the explanation offered is bona fide and does not disclose deliberate inaction, negligence or lack of bona fides. In the present case, the assessee has placed on record an affidavit explaining the change in management and the subsequent steps stated to have been taken for pursuing the pending appellate proceedings. Having regard to the explanation furnished, and subject to its acceptance as bona fide on examination of the record, we are inclined to take a liberal view in the matter of condonation of delay. 13. Having considered the totality of the circumstances, the explanation furnished by the assessee and the interest of substantial justice, we are of the considered view that the delay in filing the appeal deserves to be condoned, provided the period of delay and the explanation covering such period stand verified from the appellate record. Accordingly, upon such verification, the delay in filing the present appeal is condoned, and the appeal is admitted for adjudication. 14. Coming to the merits of the appeal, we observe that the learned AO completed the assessment under section 144 read with section 144B of the Act, determining the total income at Rs. 86,17,752/-, whereas the assessee had declared a loss of Rs. 4,70,655/-. 15. We further observe that the learned CIT(A) dismissed the appeal primarily on account of non-compliance by the assessee. Thus, the issues arising from the additions made by the learned AO have not been examined and adjudicated on merits by the learned CIT(A). It is pertinent to mention that section 250(6) of the Act requires the order of the learned CIT(A) to state the points for determination, the decision thereon and the reasons for such decision. Therefore, even in cases where the appellant fails to appear, the learned CIT(A) is required to dispose of the appeal in accordance with the statutory provisions after considering the material available on record. 16. We also take note of the fact that the dispute concerning the assessee’s registration under section 12AB of the Act was the subject matter of separate appellate proceedings before the Tribunal. In those proceedings, vide order ITA No:- 437/Ind/2026 dated 10.08.2026, the Tribunal set aside the matter and restored it to the file of the learned Commissioner of Income-tax (Exemptions) [CIT(E)] for reconsideration. The aforesaid development is relevant because the assessment proceedings in the present appeal originated from the issue concerning the assessee’s entitlement to exemption under the Act. However, we clarify that the order of the Tribunal dated 10.08.2026 does not, by itself, establish the assessee’s entitlement to exemption, and the same shall remain subject to the outcome of the proceedings before the learned CIT(E) and the applicable statutory provisions. 17. We further observe that the Tribunal, vide its separate order dated 10.08.2026, has restored the matter relating to registration under section 12AB of the Act to the file of the learned CIT(E). The outcome of those proceedings may have a bearing on the assessee’s claim for exemption, subject to the applicable provisions of law and the relevant assessment year. Considering the facts and circumstances of the case, and particularly the fact that the learned CIT(A) has not adjudicated the additions on merits, we deem it appropriate, in the interest of justice, to set aside the impugned appellate order and restore the matter to the file of the learned CIT(A) for fresh adjudication in accordance with law. 18. The learned CIT(A) shall examine the grounds raised by the assessee, the material available on record and the effect, if any, of the proceedings relating to registration under section 12AB of the Act. The learned CIT(A) shall afford reasonable opportunity of being heard to the assessee and thereafter pass a reasoned and speaking order in accordance with law. We further direct the assessee to cooperate with the appellate proceedings and comply with the notices issued by the learned CIT(A), without seeking unnecessary adjournments. We make it clear that we have not expressed any opinion on the merits of the additions made by the learned AO or on the eligibility of the assessee for exemption under the Act. 19. In view of the foregoing discussion, the delay in filing the appeal is condoned, subject to verification as indicated above. The impugned order passed by the learned CIT(A) dated 06.02.2024 is set aside, and the matter is restored to the file of the learned CIT(A) for fresh adjudication on merits in accordance with law, after providing reasonable opportunity of being heard to the assessee. 20. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes. Pronounced in open court on 22.10.2026.
