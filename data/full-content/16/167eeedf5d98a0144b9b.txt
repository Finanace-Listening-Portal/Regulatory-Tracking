Articles 3 min read India's informal economy grows at record pace in Jul-Sep, but paid job creation lags PTI Published On Nov 24, 2026 at 04:26 PM IST var news_msid = 136127779; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook New Delhi, India's unincorporated non-farm economy expanded at its fastest quarterly pace on record in July-September, but the surge was driven largely by owner-operated businesses rather than paid employment, pointing to an expansion in self-employment even as the creation of hired jobs remained relatively weak.The number of establishments rose 18.81 per cent from a year earlier to 9.47 crore, while employment increased 16.86 per cent to 15.03 crore, the National Statistics Office (NSO) said in its latest Quarterly Bulletin of the Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises. Both growth rates were the highest in the quarterly series. Advt The composition of employment, however, presents a more mixed picture. The number of hired workers increased by only about 5 per cent to 3.49 crore, while working owners accounted for nearly 62 per cent of the workforce, up from 59.98 per cent a year earlier.The figures suggest that the expansion of India's vast small-business economy is translating more rapidly into self-employment and owner-operated enterprises than into conventional wage-paying jobs.Growth was broad-based across rural and urban areas, although urban establishments expanded faster. The number of urban enterprises increased 22.05 per cent year-on-year, compared with 16.05 per cent in rural areas. Employment growth, however, was marginally stronger in rural areas at 17.52 per cent, against 16.30 per cent in urban centres.Services recorded the strongest expansion among the three broad sectors covered by the survey. Establishments in the 'other services' category grew 29 per cent, while employment increased by more than 33 per cent.Trade establishments rose 11.70 per cent, with employment increasing 8.36 per cent. Manufacturing establishments expanded 10.55 per cent, but employment growth was more modest at 3.52 per cent.Retail trade, repair and maintenance, land transport, personal services and apparel manufacturing were among the fastest-growing activities.The share of hired workers in total employment declined to 23.22 per cent, while working owners' share increased to 61.99 per cent from 59.98 per cent a year earlier. The number of working owners rose by roughly 21 per cent, based on the published employment shares. Advt The average number of workers per establishment also declined to 1.59 from 1.61, suggesting that the increase in enterprise numbers has not been accompanied by a corresponding rise in average establishment size.Women accounted for slightly more than 31 per cent of the workforce, compared with 28.70 per cent a year earlier, indicating an increase in their participation in the unincorporated non-farm sector.The survey also pointed to greater formalisation and digital adoption among unincorporated enterprises.The share of establishments registered with at least one government or other authority increased to 43.28 per cent from 37.20 per cent a year earlier. Around 80 per cent of establishments reported using cashless payment methods, while nearly 84 per cent used the internet for business purposes. Advt The NSO, however, noted that questions relating to technology adoption had been redesigned in the latest round, including the introduction of a separate question on online banking and payment gateways, which could affect comparability with previous estimates.The latest estimates come from a quarterly sample of 1,80,025 establishments spread across 6,002 survey units, substantially smaller than the sample used for the annual survey.The survey covers unincorporated manufacturing, trade and services enterprises but excludes construction. Its estimates therefore provide a picture of a specific segment of the economy and should not be interpreted as a direct measure of overall GDP or economy-wide employment growth. Advt Launched in 2021-22, the Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises has become an important source of information on businesses operating outside the corporate sector. Quarterly bulletins have been published since 2025.The latest annual survey, covering calendar 2025, estimated 7.92 crore establishments and 12.81 crore workers, broadly in line with the July-September 2025 quarterly estimates of 7.97 crore establishments and 12.86 crore workers.
