The judgment reinforces the need to follow the prescribed regulatory and contractual framework before invoking loss-absorption provisions. This could narrow the legal and procedural risk premium investors demand on AT-1 bonds, potentially improving appetite for fresh issuances and supporting secondary-market valuations. However, the instruments will continue to carry the risk of a valid write-down at the point of non-viability (PONV), discretionary coupon payments and issuer-specific credit stress. Advt The ruling could change how investors assess the risk of principal loss. The Yes Bank episode had become a defining example of the possibility that AT-1 investors could lose their entire investment during a bank reconstruction. The Supreme Court clarified that the authority to trigger a write-down at PONV rests with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), and cannot be exercised by a bank administrator in its place. This strengthens procedural safeguards for bondholders and could reduce the uncertainty premium attached to the possibility of an unauthorised write-down.The judgment does not, however, dilute the RBI’s authority to direct a write-down or conversion when the prescribed PONV conditions are met. Nor does it invalidate the loss-absorption framework under which AT-1 bonds are issued. The court's finding concerned the authority and procedure followed in the Yes Bank case, not the legitimacy of loss absorption itself. Investors therefore remain exposed to the fundamental risk that these instruments are designed to absorb losses when a bank becomes non-viable.The reassessmentFor the AT-1 market, the immediate implication could be a reassessment of the premium investors demand for legal and procedural uncertainty. If the verdict makes the rules governing write-downs more predictable, investors may be willing to accept a lower premium over other bank debt. This could support demand for fresh AT-1 issuances by financially stronger banks and potentially soften their borrowing costs. Market participants have indicated that the ruling could reduce the uncertainty premium and lead to repricing of these instruments.The benefit is unlikely to be uniform across issuers. AT-1 bonds are perpetual, subordinated instruments that rank below Tier 2 debt and senior liabilities. Their coupons are generally discretionary and non-cumulative, while their principal can be written down or converted under specified conditions. Investors therefore assess not just the legal provisions but also the issuing bank’s capital position, asset quality, earnings strength and ability to withstand stress. Advt Banks with stronger balance sheets and adequate capital buffers could benefit more from an improvement in investor confidence. They may be able to raise AT-1 capital at relatively lower coupons if the legal uncertainty premium narrows. Weaker issuers, on the other hand, may continue to face higher borrowing costs because their credit and capital risks remain unchanged. The verdict could consequently encourage greater differentiation in pricing across issuers rather than trigger a uniform movement in AT-1 yields.Investors may also scrutinise the terms of individual bond issues more closely. The Yes Bank judgment highlights the distinction between a pre-specified trigger in the bond documents and a PONV intervention by the RBI. Under the terms of the Yes Bank instruments, a specified common equity tier-1 (CET1) capital trigger gave the bank discretion to write down or convert the bonds. At PONV, the relevant authority rests with the RBI. Greater attention to the wording of information memoranda, the authority responsible for invoking each trigger and the procedures for implementing loss absorption could become an important feature of AT-1 investment decisions. Advt The verdict could also influence secondary-market valuations. The restoration of Yes Bank’s bonds has revived the value of instruments that had been written down to zero. But reinstatement of the Rs 8,415 crore principal does not mean immediate cash repayment. The bank’s management has said that the accounting adjustment would involve shifting the amount from reserves to borrowings or liabilities, without an immediate cash outflow. Based on its assessment as of September 30, reinstatement could reduce the bank’s CET1 ratio by around 240 basis points to 11.4%, while its overall Tier 1 capital ratio would remain around 13.7%. Advt The treatment of unpaid coupons remains unresolved. The Supreme Court said annual coupon payments would be governed by the respective information memoranda. Yes Bank’s bond documents provide for discretionary and non-cumulative coupons, and the bank is still assessing the implications of the judgment. Axis Trustee Services, representing bondholders, has sought restoration of the bonds and clarity on unpaid coupons. The distinction between restoration of principal and payment of coupons is important because the judgment does not make discretionary coupon payments unconditional.For fund managers, the case could make AT-1 valuations more sensitive to legal developments and the specific terms of each instrument. The restoration of the Yes Bank bonds demonstrates how a court ruling can change the value of a holding even years after it has been written down. Investors will have to factor in the issuer’s financial position, contractual provisions, the possibility of a valid write-down and the time required to resolve disputes.The longer-term impact on pricing will depend on whether the market views the judgment primarily as strengthening investor protection or exposing gaps in the execution of bank-resolution procedures. The ruling provides greater clarity on who can exercise the PONV write-down power, which could reduce one element of the risk premium. It does not remove the possibility of a lawful write-down or the credit risks attached to AT-1 instruments.The likely outcome, therefore, is not a blanket reduction in AT-1 yields but a more differentiated market in which legal clarity, contractual safeguards and issuer strength carry greater weight in pricing. For banks with strong credit profiles, improved investor confidence could support demand and lower issuance costs. For weaker lenders, the fundamental risks of loss absorption will continue to determine the premium investors seek.
