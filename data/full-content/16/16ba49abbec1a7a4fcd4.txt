Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Indira Chencharapu Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) A Timely Section 148A(b) Show-Cause Notice Cannot Save a Section 148 Notice Issued After the Six-Year Limitation Period The issue before the Tribunal Can the Assessing Officer issue a show-cause notice under Section 148A(b) before the six-year deadline and issue the reassessment notice under Section 148 after that deadline? The Hyderabad ITAT held that, for AY 2015–16, the first proviso to Section 149(1) preserved the six-year restriction under the pre-Finance Act, 2021 law. The decisive date was the date on which the Section 148 notice itself was issued. The earlier show-cause notice could not keep the reassessment proceedings alive beyond that limit. Facts and chronology The Assessing Officer determined the assessee’s total income at ₹55,89,300 on account of transactions in her bank account. The assessee disputed the treatment of the bank deposits as unexplained money under Section 69A. The CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, dismissed her appeal on limitation, leaving the merits of the addition unexamined. The Assessing Officer issued the Section 148A(b) show-cause notice on 26 March 2022. The six-year period under the erstwhile Section 149(1)(b) expired on 31 March 2022. The Assessing Officer passed the order under Section 148A(d) and issued the notice under Section 148 on 29 April 2022. Thus, the preliminary show-cause notice was issued within six years, while the notice necessary to reopen the assessment was issued after that period had expired. Condonation of delay at both appellate stages The assessee’s first appeal was delayed by 308 days, and her appeal before the Tribunal was delayed by 21 days. She filed separate affidavits explaining the delays. She was about 70 years old and depended on her son for her legal, financial and tax affairs. Her son, who had undergone a kidney transplant, returned from Canada for treatment and required regular dialysis. The assessee also produced a medical certificate in support of her explanation that she had suffered from enteric fever around the time the Tribunal appeal fell due. The ITAT found sufficient cause and condoned both delays, subject to payment of ₹5,000 to the Prime Minister’s National Relief Fund within one month from the date of the order. Jurisdictional ground admitted for the first time before the ITAT The assessee raised the limitation objection as an additional ground under Rule 11 of the ITAT Rules, 1963. The Revenue opposed admission because the ground had not been raised during assessment or before the CIT(A). The Tribunal found that the relevant notice and order dates were already on record and that adjudication required no fresh investigation of facts. Following National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. v. CIT (1998) 229 ITR 383 (SC), it admitted the ground and took it up first. Fifth and sixth provisos cannot extend the old six-year limit The Revenue argued that the fifth and sixth provisos to Section 149(1) saved the notice. The fifth proviso provided for exclusion of specified periods, including the time allowed to the assessee to reply to the show-cause notice. The sixth proviso provided for a minimum remaining period of seven days in the circumstances specified, after applying the exclusion. The Tribunal followed the jurisdictional Telangana High Court in Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Private Limited v. DCIT and its coordinate bench in Sudheer Parimala v. ITO. Under that interpretation, the fifth and sixth provisos operate when computing limitation under the amended Section 149(1). The first proviso imposes a separate restriction for the covered earlier assessment years by preserving the applicable limitation under the old law. The exclusions and extension under the amended provision cannot enlarge that old six-year period. The Tribunal also relied on ITO v. Sai Kumar Mateti, where the Supreme Court recorded the Revenue’s concession concerning the AY 2015–16 cases in that batch in light of Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal (2024) 469 ITR 46 (SC). The Supreme Court remitted the matters for verification of the assessment year and directed that, where they pertained to AY 2015–16, the notices be declared time-barred. Decision and author’s comments The ITAT quashed the Section 148 notice dated 29 April 2022, which invalidated the consequential reassessment. It allowed the assessee’s appeal without adjudicating the remaining grounds, including the merits of the Section 69A addition. The practical point is that an alleged escapement exceeding ₹50 lakh does not, by itself, permit reopening an earlier assessment year after the deadline preserved by the first proviso to Section 149(1). In a case governed by that proviso, the relevant comparison is between the actual date of issuance of the Section 148 notice and the last permissible date under the old law. Here, the five days between the show-cause notice and the expiry of limitation made no difference to the validity of the notice issued on 29 April 2022. The ruling also shows how a jurisdictional objection can dispose of an appeal where the dates needed to decide it are already on record. Cases Discussed National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. Vs CIT (1998) 229 ITR 383 (SC): A purely legal additional ground can be admitted where its adjudication does not require fresh investigation of facts. Shri Katterapu Gopalakrishna Reddy Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad), ITA No. 437/Hyd/2026, dated 16.09.2026: Relied upon by the assessee in support of the limitation objection. Sudheer Parimala Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad), ITA No. 758/Hyd/2025, dated 17.04.2026: Reassessment notice issued beyond the six-year limitation period was held invalid. Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Private Limited Vs DCIT (Telangana High Court), 2025 (11) TMI 1967: The fifth and sixth provisos to Section 149 cannot extend the limitation restriction preserved by the first proviso. ITO & Anr. Vs Sri Sai Kumar Mateti (Supreme Court), SLP (Civil) No. 8682 of 2024, dated 04.05.2026: Reassessment notices pertaining to AY 2015-16 in the relevant batch were directed to be declared time-barred upon verification. Union of India Vs Rajeev Bansal (2024) 469 ITR 46 (SC): Explained the operation of the first proviso to Section 149 and the protection against retrospective enlargement of reassessment limitation. Godrej Industries Ltd. Vs ACIT: The validity of a reassessment notice must be examined with reference to the law applicable on its date of issuance. Shree Cement Ltd.: The fifth and sixth provisos cannot extend the restriction prescribed under the first proviso to Section 149(1). Hexaware Technologies Ltd.: Extensions under the fifth and sixth provisos apply to the limitation prescribed under the amended substantive provision. Chandra Shekhar (Patna High Court): Distinguished in the quoted Telangana High Court judgment as involving limitation under Section 149(1)(a).
