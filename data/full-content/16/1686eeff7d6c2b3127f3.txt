Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); ITO Vs Varda Energy and Engineering (ITAT Raipur) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Raipur, dismissed the Revenue’s appeal for Assessment Year 2018-19 against the CIT(A)/NFAC order dated 24 November 2023, upholding deletion of three additions: ₹3,91,35,000 for alleged bogus purchases, ₹20,00,000 for share application money and ₹14,15,237 out of agricultural income. The assessee, a private limited company engaged in trading iron and steel, had returned income of ₹63,290. Reassessment followed information alleging purchase accommodation entries from Mega City Trade Link and Goldwright Vyapar Pvt. Ltd. and a share capital entry through Saloni Buildtech Pvt. Ltd. The Assessing Officer assessed total income at ₹4,26,13,530. For the purchases, the Revenue relied on investigation information, supplier non-compliance with summons, nominal profits and non-filing of returns. The assessee submitted that the steel purchases were capitalised in the work in progress of its maize plant, rather than claimed as expenditure in the profit and loss account. The Tribunal noted that the Assessing Officer had accepted work in progress of ₹12,21,96,352, which included the disputed purchases. No corresponding sum of ₹3,91,35,000 was found credited in the books; on these facts, section 68 could not be applied to the purchases. VAT returns, ledgers, confirmations, invoices and vehicle details supported the transactions, while the audited accounts disclosed no defects. The CIT(A)’s reasoning referred to Odeon Builders and Sanjay Dhokad; the reproduced discussion of Sanjay Dhokad also referred to Nikunj Eximp and distinguished NRA Iron & Steel. The ₹20 lakh share application addition rested on statements of J.P. Gupta and Raju Malik concerning entry operations and shell companies. The assessee produced incorporation documents, memorandum and articles, audited financial statements, bank statements and share application/allotment records. It explained that existing shareholders had renounced their rights and shares were issued to Saloni Buildtech. The Tribunal found that the documents establishing the investor’s identity, creditworthiness and transaction genuineness had not been disproved. Neither person had named the assessee as a beneficiary. Once the assessee discharged its initial onus, the Assessing Officer had to rebut the evidence, which he failed to do. The CIT(A)’s discussion referred to Oasis Hospitalities, Lovely Exports and Precision Finance. On agricultural income, the assessee declared ₹14,56,420 from 12 acres, reporting production of 23,650 kilograms, sales of ₹20,99,790 and expenses of ₹6,43,370. The Assessing Officer accepted only ₹41,183 and added the ₹14,15,237 difference, relying on regional crop prices, yield statistics and concerns about irrigation. The Tribunal upheld the CIT(A)’s findings that ownership records, production and sales details, and expense vouchers substantiated the claim. The land was irrigated through public canals, and the assessee grew maize seeds, whose price differed from ordinary maize. No contrary material was produced by the Revenue. All three deletions were sustained; the assessee’s legal grounds under Rule 27 were dismissed as not pressed. The order was pronounced on 25 September 2026. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT RAIPUR This appeal filed by the Revenue is directed against the order dated 24.11.2023 passed by the Ld.CIT(A)/NFAC, relating to assessment year 2018-19. 2. Facts of the case, in brief, are that the assessee is a private limited company engaged in the business of trading of iron and steels. It filed its original return of income on 29.09.2018 declaring total income at Rs.63,290/-. The return was processed under section 143(1)(a) of the Act. Subsequently, information was received from credible sources that the assessee was in receipt of bogus purchase bill without actual delivery of the goods to the tune of Rs.2,12,85,000/- from M/s Mega City Trade Link and Rs.1,78,50,000/- from M/s Goldwright Vyapar Pvt. Ltd. Further, the assessee was also in receipt of accommodation entry of Rs. 20,00,000/-from Shri J.P. Gupta through a shell company, namely, Saloni Buildtech Pvt. Ltd. The AO, therefore, initiated proceedings under section 147 of the Act and order under section 148A(d) of the Act was passed on 28.03.2022. Accordingly, notice under section 148 of the Act was issued on 30.03.2022. The assessee in response to same filed its return of income on 29.04.2022 declaring total income at Rs.63,290/-. 3. During the course of assessment proceedings, the AO observed that as per information received from the investigation wing of the Income Tax Department, M/s Mega City Trade Link was found indulging in the issuance of fraudulent GST invoices for passing input tax credit to other business entities. In view of this information, summon under section 131 of the Act was issued to M/s Mega City Trade Link. However, the party neither appeared nor made any submission. It was found on enquiry that M/s Mega City Trade Link has filed ITR for AY 2018-19 only, where the revenue from operation were shown at Rs.33,94,65,983/-. The profit before tax declared at Rs.67,672/- was very nominal in comparison to the turnover of the company. Further, the said company stopped filing ITR from AY 2018-19 onwards and had neither fixed assets nor any rental expenses. Therefore, it was concluded that the company did not have any real trading with stock in trade rather it was used to provide accommodation entries in the form of bogus purchase invoices without any physical delivery of goods. The assessee company has made purchase of Rs.2,12,85,000/- from M/s Mega City Trade Link. 4. The AO similarly noted that the assessee has also made purchases of Rs.1,78,50,000/- from alleged shell company M/s Goldwright Vyapar Pvt. Ltd. who has not filed any return of income for FY 2018-19. The AO further noted that the assessee has received share application money of Rs.20,00,000/- from M/s Saloni Buildtech Pvt. Ltd. The AO therefore asked the assessee to substantiate the purchases made from M/s Mega City Trade link and M/s Goldwright Vyapar Pvt. Ltd. and also to explain the identity and creditworthiness of these parties and genuineness of the transactions. The assessee, in response to the same furnished copy of the bank statement, computation of income, bank book, copy of register of members, copy of share application of M/s Saloni Build Tech Pvt. Ltd., copy of invoices from M/s Mega City Trade Link and M/s Goldwright Vyapar Pvt. Ltd. and also affidavits of above two parties. Subsequently, the assessee filed various details to substantiate the purchases made from the above two parties and share application money of Rs.20,00,000/-. 5. However, the AO was not satisfied with the arguments advanced by the assessee and made addition of Rs.3,91,35,000/- under section 68 of the Act on account of bogus purchases. Similarly, in absence of any documentary evidence regarding creditworthiness of the investor M/s Saloni Build Tech Pvt. Ltd. who have invested Rs.20,00,000/- towards share application money in the assessee company, the AO made addition of the same by invoking the provision of section 68 of the Act. While doing so he noted that the share allotment application was signed by Mr. Raju Malik in the capacity of Director of M/s Saloni Buildtech Pvt. Ltd who was a cash handler and in his statement before the Investigation wing has accepted that he was Director in many Shell companies and was in the business of providing accommodation entries. Considering all these facts and circumstance of the case, the AO treated the said amount of Rs. 20,00,000/- as unexplained credit in the books of the assessee and added the same to the total income of the assessee under section 68 of the Act. 6. It was also observed by the AO that the assessee has shown agricultural income of Rs. 14,56,420/- which was claimed as exempt income. The assessee was required to justify the same alongwith supporting documents. In response, the assessee furnished the submission wherein it has claimed to have 12 acres (i.e. 4.85 hectare) of agricultural land with production of 23,650 kg of maize with total sale of Rs.20,99,790/-. The assessee also claimed expenditure of Rs.6,43,370/-. The AO noted that the assessee has claimed yield per hectare at 88.79 quintal and production claimed by the assessee is 4876 kg. The AO noted that the said yield and sale rate claimed was at extremely higher price than the prevailing market rate in the region. Further, as per report on agricultural prices published by the Ministry of Agricultural and Farmers Welfare, the average prices of Maize in F.Y. 2017-18 was Rs.1425/- per quintal which was adopted by the AO and total sale of the assessee was arrived at Rs.1,27,866/-. After analyzing the multiple statistics of the area by the AO, the agricultural income of the assessee was determined at Rs.41,183/-. Also, from the report collected from the site of the Krishi Vigyan Kendra, Raipur, the AO noted that the land of the assessee was not irrigated and being a company, the assessee was not an expert in farming. Therefore, it was concluded by the AO that there is no reason for such higher yields than the average production. Since, the assessee had claimed the agricultural income at Rs.14,56,420/- and had credited the said amount as agricultural income in its books which was claimed as exempt, therefore, the difference of Rs.14,15,237/- was treated as unexplained credit in the books of the assessee and was added to the total income of the assessee for the year under consideration. 7. The AO, accordingly determined the total income of the assessee at Rs.4,26,13,530/-. 8. In appeal, Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC deleted all the additions. From the above, the following points seek to established identity of the party and genuineness of the transactions: – 1. Purchases from M/s Mega City Tradelinks and M/s Goldwright Vyapar Pvt. We have heard the rival arguments made by both the sides, perused the orders of the Assessing Officer and Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC and the paper book filed on behalf of the assessee. We have also considered the various decisions cited before us. The first issue raised by the Revenue in the grounds of appeal is regarding the deletion of addition of Rs.3,91,35,000/-made by the AO as unexplained cash credit on account of bogus purchases. A perusal of the record shows that the AO made addition of Rs.3,91,35,000/- by invoking the provision of section 68 of the Act on the ground that the assessee has made bogus purchases of Rs.2,12,85,000/-from M/s Mega City Trade Links and Rs.1,78,50,000/- from M/s Goldwright Vyapar Pvt. Ltd. The reason given by the AO for making the above addition is that M/s Mega City Trade Link did not appear nor made any submission in response to the summon issued under section 131 of the Act. Further, the revenue from operation has been shown at Rs.33,94,65,983/- whereas the profit declared is only at Rs.67,672/-, which is very nominal in comparison to the turnover of the company. Moreover, M/s Mega City Trade Link has stopped filing the return of income from AY 2018-19 onwards. Similar was the case with M/s Goldwright Vyapar Pvt. Ltd. The AO, therefore invoking the provision of section 68 of the Act made the addition. We find the Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC deleted the addition, the reasons of which have already been reproduced in the preceding paragraphs. We do not find any infirmity in the order of the Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC on this issue. As pointed out by the Ld. Counsel for the assessee the purchase of materials at Rs.3,91,35,000/- has not been debited to the P & L account but has been utilized for construction of the maize plant and has been shown in the work in progress which has been accepted by the AO. Further, no sum of money of Rs.3,91,35,000/- has been found credited in the books of account of the assessee and therefore, provision of section 68 of the Act, in our opinion, cannot be applied for purchases. Apart from this, we find the assessee, during the course of assessment proceedings has furnished VAT return, copy of ledger of M/s Mega City Trade Link and M/s Goldwright Vyapar Pvt. Ltd., confirmation from the above two parties, copy of invoices with details of vehicles for transportation etc. The assessee has maintained proper record of purchase and sale etc. and accounts of the assessee have been audited by the auditors who have not pointed out any defects. Under these circumstances and in view of the detailed reasoning given by ld.CIT(A)/NFAC we do not find any infirmity in the order of Ld. CTI(A)/NFAC deleting the addition. We, therefore, uphold the order of the Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC on this issue and the ground raised by the revenue is accordingly dismissed. 17. After hearing both the sides, we find the AO made the addition of Rs.20,00,000/- on the basis of statement of Mr. JP Gupta recorded on oath under section 132(4) of the Act, wherein he had stated that he is an entry provider and is running various shell companies. The AO has also relied on the statement of one Mr. Raju Malik, an employee of Mr. J.P. Gupta who is a dummy Director in various shell companies. We find Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC deleted the above addition the reasons of which have already been reproduced in the preceding paragraphs. We do not find any infirmity in the order of Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC on this issue deleting the addition. We find the ld.CIT(A) while deleting the same has noted that the assessee, during the course of assessment proceedings, as well as appellate proceedings has submitted all the necessary documentary evidences. He noted that the assessee during the assessment proceedings had submitted that the rights shares had been initially offered to the existing shareholders and they chose to renounce their rights in favour of other individuals/company. The assessee issued the Right Shares to M/s Saloni Build Tech Pvt. Ltd in the instant case. The various documentary evidences filed during the course of assessment proceedings substantiating the identity and creditworthiness of the share applicant and the genuineness of the transaction has not been proved to be false. Further, the above two persons have never taken the name of the assessee company as a beneficiary of such accommodation entry. Once the assessee fulfilled all the three ingredients of section 68 of the Act, the onus shifts to the AO to disprove the same. However, the AO in the instant case has not discharged the onus cast on him by disapproving the documentary evidences and proving that the transaction between the assessee and M/s Saloni Build Tech Pvt. Ltd. is not genuine and a sham transaction. Since, the assessee in this case has discharged the onus cast on it by producing all the relevant details substantiating the identity and creditworthiness of the share applicant and genuineness of the transaction, therefore, we do not find any infirmity in the order of the Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC deleting the addition. Accordingly, the same is upheld and the ground raised by the revenue on this issue is dismissed. 18. So far as the third issue is concerned, the same relates to the order of Ld. CTI(A) in deleting the addition of Rs.14,15,237/- made by the AO as unexplained cash credit under section 68 of the Act. 19. After hearing both the sides we find the AO made addition of Rs.14,15,237/- on the ground that the agricultural income declared by the assessee is much higher than the actual receipt and therefore, he brought to tax the same under section 68 of the Act. We find the Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC deleted the addition the reasons of which have already been reproduced in the preceding paragraphs. While doing so he had given a finding that the assessee during the course of assessment proceedings as well as appellate proceedings has produced details of land holdings (12 Acres) with ownership documents. The assessee has produced the details of the product wherein total production stands at 23650 kilograms and the total sales has been shown at Rs.20,99,790/-. The assessee has also substantiated the expenses for earning such agricultural income by producing relevant vouchers including expenses towards labour charges, tractor rent, seed cost, pesticides & fertilizers. He has also given a finding that the assessee has not grown or sold maize but has grown and sold maize seed whose price is higher than the crop. Since, the order of the Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC in the instant case is based on the various details submitted by the assessee during the assessment proceedings as well as appellate proceedings, therefore, in absence of any contrary material brought to our notice by the Ld. CTI-DR, we do not find any infirmity in the order of ld.CIT(A)/NFAC. Accordingly, the same is upheld and the ground raised by the revenue on this issue is dismissed. 20. The assessee has also raised certain legal grounds by invoking the provision of Rule 27 of the ITAT, Rules, 1963, However, the Ld. Counsel for the assessee did not press all these grounds. Accordingly, all these grounds are dismissed as not pressed. 21. In the result, appeal of the revenue is dismissed. Order pronounced in open court on 25th day of September, 2026.
