Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re KIMSHEALTH Executive Leisure Private Limited (NCLT Kochi) Summary : The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Kochi Bench, considered a joint first-motion application filed by KIMSHEALTH Executive Leisure Private Limited, the Transferor Company, and Spiceretreat Hospitality Services Private Limited, the Transferee Company, seeking directions concerning their proposed amalgamation under Sections 230–233 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with the Companies (Compromises, Arrangements and Amalgamations) Rules, 2016. The proposed scheme contemplated the transfer of the undertaking, assets and liabilities of the Transferor Company to the Transferee Company on a going-concern basis, with 1 April 2026 as the Appointed Date. The companies stated that the amalgamation would simplify their group structure, consolidate hospitality operations, improve cash management, eliminate duplicated administrative functions, reduce regulatory compliance costs and strengthen their combined financial and operational position. The Tribunal examined the corporate particulars, capital structures, financial statements, valuation documents and stakeholder positions of both companies. The Transferor Company, incorporated on 9 April 1999, was engaged in providing accommodation services at Thiruvananthapuram and had paid-up share capital of ₹61,21,760 as on 31 March 2026. The Transferee Company, incorporated on 20 September 2018, was engaged in hospitality and allied services, including food and beverage, catering and support services, and had paid-up share capital of ₹5,00,000. Both companies had approved the scheme through their respective Board resolutions dated 29 August 2026. The record also contained a valuation report dated 24 June 2026 issued by D and P India Advisory Services LLP and an auditor’s certificate dated 25 August 2026 concerning conformity of the proposed accounting treatment with Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013 and applicable accounting standards. Regarding stakeholder approval, the Tribunal noted that the Transferor Company had two equity shareholders and one unsecured creditor, all of whom had consented to the proposed scheme and dispensation of their respective meetings. The Transferee Company had seven equity shareholders, representing its entire equity shareholding, who had similarly consented. Neither company had any secured creditors as on 31 May 2026. However, the Transferee Company had 92 unsecured creditors, whose meeting was required to be convened. The Tribunal also recorded the applicants’ submissions that the proposed transaction did not require approval from the Competition Commission of India under Section 5 of the Competition Act, 2002, and that no investigation proceedings under the specified provisions of the Companies Acts, 1956 and 2013 were pending against either company. After examining the application and supporting documents, the NCLT expressed prima facie satisfaction regarding maintainability under Sections 230–232, territorial jurisdiction, Board approvals, stakeholder classification, valuation and accounting documentation. It also observed that the record did not prima facie disclose material suggesting that the proposed scheme was prejudicial to creditors or contrary to public policy. Accordingly, the Tribunal dispensed with the meetings of equity shareholders and unsecured creditors of the Transferor Company, dispensed with the equity shareholders’ meeting of the Transferee Company, and held that secured creditors’ meetings were unnecessary because neither company had secured creditors. However, it specifically directed the Transferee Company to convene a meeting of its unsecured creditors in accordance with law. The Tribunal directed that the unsecured creditors’ meeting be convened within 45 days of the order, with at least 30 days’ notice, at the Transferee Company’s registered office through physical or hybrid arrangements, including permissible electronic participation and voting. Dr. Geetha K.K. was appointed Chairperson with remuneration of ₹1,50,000, while Mr. Shawn Jeff Christopher was appointed Scrutinizer with remuneration of ₹75,000, excluding applicable taxes and expenses. The prescribed quorum required creditors present and voting representing at least 30% in number and 50% in value of unsecured creditors. The order also prescribed procedures for adjournment, proxies, publication of meeting notices in the Times of India and Mathrubhumi, service of notices and explanatory statements, and reporting of meeting results in Form CAA-4. The NCLT further directed service of notices on statutory and regulatory authorities, including the Regional Director, Registrar of Companies, Official Liquidator, Income Tax Department, Reserve Bank of India, Customs Department, GST Department, EPFO and ESIC, among others. The authorities were required to examine compliance with applicable laws and communicate their representations within 30 days of receiving notice, failing which no objection would be presumed. The applicants were directed to establish compliance through appropriate affidavits and subsequently present a petition in Form CAA-5 seeking sanction of the scheme. The Tribunal accordingly allowed the first-motion company application. Importantly, the order did not constitute final sanction of the amalgamation; the proposed scheme remained subject to the prescribed creditor meeting, statutory scrutiny and subsequent proceedings before the Tribunal. Rationale of the scheme: The amalgamation of Transferor Company with the Transferee Company would inter alia have the following benefits: 1. Simplification of group structure by eliminating multiple companies and thus enabling focus on core competencies and strengthening the position of the merged entity, by enabling it to harness and optimize the synergies of the two companies. 2. Greater efficiency in cash management of the Transferee entity, and unfettered access to cash flow generated by the combined business which can be deployed more efficiently to fund organic and inorganic growth opportunities, to maximize shareholder value. 3. Cost savings are expected to flow from more focused operational efforts, rationalization, standardization and simplification of business processes, productivity improvements, improved procurement, usage of common resource pool like human resource, administration, finance, accounts, legal, technology and other related functions, leading to elimination of duplication and rationalization of administrative expenses. 4. The amalgamation would help avoid duplication of regulatory and procedural compliances and consequently result into saving of time, resources and cost involved in such compliances. 5. Post amalgamation, the Transferee Company will have better financial and operational prospects. The amalgamation shall be beneficial and in the best interests of the shareholders, creditors, employees of the companies involved and all concerned. 11. Heard the arguments advanced by the Learned Counsel for the Applicant Companies and perused the documents on record, including the Scheme. 25. Upon perusal of the Company Application, the Scheme of Amalgamation and the documents placed on record, this Tribunal is prima facie satisfied that the Application is maintainable under Sections 230–232 of the Companies Act, 2013 and that the Registered Offices of the Applicant Companies are situated within the territorial jurisdiction of this Bench. The Scheme has been duly approved by the respective Boards of Directors of the Applicant No. 1 and the Applicant No. 2 Companies, vide resolutions dated 29.08.2026, and these resolutions are placed on record as Annexure E1 and E2. An Auditor’s Certificate dated 25.08.2026 of the Applicant No. 2, respectively, is annexed as Annexure F, certifying that the accounting treatment proposed in the Scheme is in conformity with Section 133 of the Act and the applicable Accounting Standards have been filed. 27. Accordingly, this Tribunal is satisfied that the statutory requirements, to the extent required for consideration at the first motion stage, have been complied with, subject to further scrutiny at the stage of consideration of the Scheme after meetings of the stakeholders/statutory reports, as the case may be. 29.
