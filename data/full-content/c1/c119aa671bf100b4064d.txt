Summary: Polkadot is a network in which specialised blockchains can use common security and exchange cross-consensus messages. DOT is the native token used for governance, staking and access to secure computation through coretime. This article explains the relay chain, parachains, XCM, Nominated Proof of Stake, nomination pools and OpenGov without presenting DOT as an investment. It also separates protocol mechanics from the Indian legal and tax position. For an Indian taxpayer, a disposal of DOT will ordinarily require virtual digital asset analysis even where consideration is another token rather than rupees. The special 30% transfer-income rate, restricted deductions and loss rules, and the 1% withholding mechanism require transaction-level records. Staking rewards, a later disposal, business income, GST, FEMA and PMLA questions are separate factual inquiries. Technical risks include validator slashing, smart-contract or bridge failures outside the relay chain, governance changes, key loss, custodial failure, liquidity and valuation. Readers should verify current protocol documentation and Indian law at the transaction date. Key Takeaways Show all 16 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); What Polkadot DOT Is and How Its Network Operates Polkadot is not a company share, bank deposit or sovereign currency. Its design connects application-specific blockchains, commonly described as parachains or rollups, to common infrastructure. The relay chain concentrates on consensus, validator coordination, data availability, core scheduling and shared security. Connected chains can implement different business logic while relying on the validator set and finality supplied by the wider network. This does not make every connected application equally safe: application code, administration, assets and interfaces can still fail independently. DOT is Polkadot’s native token. Official Polkadot documentation identifies three core functions: participation in on-chain governance, bonding for staking and purchasing coretime or otherwise accessing secure computation. These functions are protocol utilities, not promises of profit, repayment or a stable INR value. A token holder has no insured principal and no automatic claim on the revenue or assets of an operating company. Polkadot’s pooled model allows connected chains to draw on common relay-chain validators. Validators check parachain candidates and participate in finality, while collators collect parachain transactions and produce candidates. Shared security can reduce the burden of creating a new security market, but it does not remove execution, oracle, governance, wallet or economic-design risk at the parachain level. XCM means Cross-Consensus Messaging. The official wiki stresses that XCM is a format, not itself a transport protocol. It describes instructions that different consensus systems can interpret. A cross-chain transfer may involve a reserve location, fees, execution weight and destination-chain logic. Users should not equate a successful message with an unconditional legal or technical guarantee. Incorrect destinations, unsupported assets, insufficient execution fees and application defects can make recovery difficult or impossible. Coretime is the resource through which a rollup obtains access to Polkadot’s secure computation. DOT may be used to purchase coretime and reserve identifiers. This economic function has evolved from earlier parachain-slot auction arrangements. Articles should therefore avoid repeating outdated claims that every parachain must lock DOT in a long lease auction. Current official documentation and runtime state should be checked before describing access mechanics. DOT Staking, Nominated Proof-of-Stake and Governance Polkadot uses Nominated Proof of Stake. Validators operate infrastructure and participate in block production and finality; nominators bond DOT and back selected validators. Nomination pools provide a way for participants to combine stake. Protocol rewards are variable and are not interest on a deposit. They depend on era participation, validator commission, reward points, nomination status, protocol issuance and other live rules. Custodial services may add their own commissions, waiting periods and counterparty exposure. Stake can be exposed to slashing when a supported validator commits a punishable offence. Slashing can affect validators and nominators, and the impact may extend beyond the party operating the faulty infrastructure. Unbonding is also subject to the current protocol process and timing. A participant should confirm the actual bonding method, validator set, commission, slash history, reward destination and unbonding rules rather than relying on a promotional annual percentage figure. OpenGov permits DOT holders to propose and vote on referenda under multiple governance tracks. Governance can change protocol parameters, approve treasury spending and authorise runtime upgrades. Participation has operational consequences because conviction voting can lock tokens for periods linked to voting strength. Governance rights are not equivalent to shareholder rights under company law, and a successful referendum can change economic or technical assumptions. Polkadot Wallet Custody, Validator and Token Risks Self-custody concentrates control in the holder’s seed phrase and signing devices. Loss, phishing, malicious extensions, address substitution and compromised recovery material can lead to irreversible loss. Exchange custody adds insolvency, withdrawal, security and contractual risks. A user should test addresses with a small amount, confirm the network, use independent device security and keep recovery material offline. Screenshots alone are not a complete audit trail. Market liquidity and quoted price can change quickly. Staking may reduce immediate liquidity, while a centralised platform may suspend deposits or withdrawals during maintenance. A token labelled DOT on another network may be a wrapped representation rather than native DOT. The holder should document the issuing arrangement, reserve mechanism and bridge dependencies before treating wrapped and native units as interchangeable. Indian Tax on DOT Transfers Under VDA Provisions A transfer of DOT will ordinarily be examined under India’s virtual digital asset provisions. Income from transfer is generally subject to the special 30% rate, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. The computation generally permits only cost of acquisition and restricts set-off and carry-forward of a loss from transfer of a VDA. These rules can produce tax even where an overall portfolio has declined. A sale for INR is an obvious disposal, but a DOT-to-stablecoin trade, DOT-to-another-token swap, payment for goods or services, gift, or conversion into a different beneficial interest can also require analysis. The absence of a bank withdrawal does not establish that no transfer occurred. Each event should be recorded with the wallet or exchange, transaction hash, timestamp, units, fees, counterparty where known and a defensible INR valuation source. The 1% withholding mechanism for consideration on transfer of a VDA is separate from final tax. It generally applies to gross consideration paid to a resident, subject to statutory thresholds and procedural rules. The party responsible for withholding can depend on the transaction structure and whether an exchange facilitates it. TDS reflected in tax records is a credit; it does not replace the taxpayer’s gain computation or return obligations. The special transfer provision applies to a disposal; the receipt may also be taxable depending on the nature and regularity of activity, the rights obtained and the applicable head of income. A later sale can create a separate VDA transfer computation. Records should preserve the receipt-date value and subsequent disposal rather than using only the final bank amount. GST depends on whether there is a taxable supply, who supplies it, the recipient, consideration and place of supply. Operating validation infrastructure, providing technical services or charging a platform fee may require a supply-based analysis. Merely holding a token does not answer GST questions. Businesses should review registration, invoicing, export-of-service and reverse-charge issues with transaction facts. FEMA is separate from income tax. Using an overseas exchange, paying a foreign counterparty, receiving cross-border protocol rewards or operating foreign infrastructure can raise purpose and payment-channel questions. The Liberalised Remittance Scheme should not be described as blanket permission for overseas margin or margin-call remittances. FIU-IND registration is relevant to covered VDA service providers; it does not make DOT legal tender or guarantee a platform. Frequently Asked Questions Is DOT legal tender in India? No. DOT is a crypto-asset and not sovereign currency, insured money or a bank deposit. What does shared security mean? Connected chains use common relay-chain validators and finality, but their application code, assets and governance retain separate risks. Is XCM a bridge? XCM is a message format. Transport and execution depend on the participating consensus systems and configuration. Are staking rewards fixed? No. Rewards and their INR value depend on live protocol, validator, commission and market conditions. Can nominators be slashed? Yes. Stake backing a validator can be exposed when punishable validator behaviour occurs. Is a crypto-to-crypto exchange taxable? It can be a transfer even without INR settlement and should be valued and documented. Is 1% TDS the final tax? No. It is withholding on consideration and must be reconciled with the final VDA tax computation. Does FIU registration guarantee a platform? No. It is a compliance status for covered services, not investment protection or token approval. Key Takeaways Polkadot supplies shared security and cross-consensus coordination to specialised chains. DOT is used for governance, staking and access to coretime; it has no guaranteed INR value. Staking involves validator, slashing, custody, liquidity and governance risks. DOT disposals generally require 30% VDA-tax and 1% withholding analysis in India. Receipt, disposal, GST, FEMA and platform-compliance questions must be analysed separately.
