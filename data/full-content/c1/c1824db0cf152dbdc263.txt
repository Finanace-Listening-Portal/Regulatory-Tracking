Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Hemlata Jaiswal Vs Pacific Exports (GSTAT Lucknow) Summary: The Lucknow State Bench of the Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal allowed six departmental appeals concerning refund of accumulated Compensation Cess Input Tax Credit on coal used in manufacturing leather products. It set aside the Order-in-Appeal dated 5 July 2021 and restored the original refund rejection orders passed by the Deputy Commissioner, Commercial Tax, Sector-20, Kanpur. The Tribunal held that eligibility to avail ITC, its utilisation and entitlement to a cash refund are distinct statutory matters. Eligible credit does not automatically become refundable merely because it remains accumulated. The judgment body describes the respondent manufacturer as M/s Pacific Leather Pvt. Ltd., Kanpur. Coal was used as fuel to operate boilers and generate energy for production. Compensation cess was paid on coal purchases, whereas the finished leather products sold domestically were not subject to that cess. Six refund claims were filed under the inverted tax structure category for periods during 2018–19. The amounts listed in the operative order are ₹15,040, ₹11,704, ₹19,463, ₹10,321, ₹13,010 and ₹2,446, aggregating to ₹71,984. The Proper Officer rejected the claims, but the First Appellate Authority allowed them principally because the coal-related credit was eligible and was not blocked under Section 17(5) of the CGST Act. The Department challenged that approach under Section 112(3) of the CGST/UPGST Act, 2017. It argued that Section 54(3) allows refund only within the prescribed statutory categories and that the respondent had not established the conditions for an inverted tax structure refund. It also relied on the separate character of compensation cess credit under Section 11(2) of the Goods and Services Tax (Compensation to States) Act, 2017. The Department submitted that Circular No. 45/19/2018-GST dated 30 May 2018 and Circular No. 125/44/2019-GST dated 18 November 2019 dealt with cess refunds in the context of zero-rated supplies, whereas the disputed claims concerned domestic sales. The respondent maintained that the cess paid on coal was lawfully available as input tax under Section 2(g) of the Compensation Cess Act and the applicable ITC provisions. It argued that the restriction permitting cess credit to be utilised only against cess liability did not expressly prohibit refund. Since leather products did not bear compensation cess, the respondent attributed accumulation to the difference between the cess on inputs and the absence of cess on outputs. It further contended that administrative circulars could not override the parent statute and relied on decisions concerning accumulated credit refunds. The Tribunal explained that Section 54(3) does not confer an unrestricted right to cash refund. For a claim under the inverted tax structure category, the claimant must establish accumulation arising from the rate of tax on inputs being higher than that on output supplies and satisfy the exclusion relating to nil-rated or fully exempt supplies. The statutory test must be applied to the particular levy for which refund is sought. The mere existence of cess on coal and absence of cess on leather products did not, by itself, establish eligibility. The Tribunal noted the need to examine the status of the outward supplies for compensation cess purposes rather than equating credit accumulation with an established refund entitlement. The Tribunal accepted that the utilisation restriction in Section 11(2) should not, by itself, be read as an absolute prohibition on every refund claim. Nevertheless, the absence of an express prohibition did not create a right to refund outside an authorised statutory category. Section 16 and Section 17(5) govern availability and blocking of credit; they do not dispense with the independent conditions of Section 54(3). Similarly, Rule 89 and the inverted tax structure computation under Rule 89(5) operate within the parent provision and cannot independently create a substantive right to refund. The Tribunal also considered Notification No. 5/2017-Central Tax (Rate), dated 28 June 2017. It expressly declined to assume that the respondent’s leather products were covered by a notified restriction because the available material did not establish this. Even so, the absence of an applicable notification-based restriction could not itself establish refund eligibility. As regards the circulars, the record did not establish that the disputed credit was attributable to qualifying zero-rated supplies. The principle that circulars cannot override legislation was accepted, but it did not resolve the respondent’s failure to establish the statutory basis of these domestic refund claims. Patson Papers Private Limited v. Union of India, Atul Limited v. Union of India and Tata Steel Limited v. State of Jharkhand were considered in the context of export-related compensation cess refunds. The Tribunal distinguished those factual settings from the domestic supplies involved here. Those decisions did not eliminate the need to satisfy Section 54(3) or establish an automatic entitlement for domestic supplies. It concluded that the First Appellate Authority had inadequately distinguished credit admissibility from refund entitlement and had not established an independent statutory basis for the refunds. All six departmental appeals were allowed and the original rejection orders were restored. Cases Discussed Atul Limited v. Union of India — judgment dated 24.07.2025 (Gujarat High Court). Distinguished because it concerned compensation cess credit attributable to exports. Tata Steel Limited v. State of Jharkhand — judgment dated 03.04.2025 (Jharkhand High Court). Questions of Law The following questions arise for consideration and determination in the present Second Appeal: 2.1. Whether the First Appellate Authority was justified in allowing the refund of accumulated Input Tax Credit of Compensation Cess paid on coal used in the manufacture of leather products, where the outward supplies were domestic supplies not liable to Compensation Cess. 2.2. Whether the admissibility of Input Tax Credit under Section 16 of the CGST Act, 2017, read with Section 11(2) of the Goods and Services Tax (Compensation to States) Act, 2017, automatically entitles a registered person to refund of such credit under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act. 2.3. Whether accumulation of Compensation Cess ITC on account of the absence of Compensation Cess liability on outward supplies can, by itself, be treated as an inverted tax structure qualifying for refund under Section 54(3)(ii) of the CGST Act. 2.4. Whether the First Appellate Authority correctly interpreted Circular No. 45/19/2018-GST dated 30.05.2018 and Circular No. 125/44/2019-GST dated 18.11.2019 while allowing the disputed refund claim. 3. Grounds of Appeal 3.1. The Appellant has contended that the refund of unutilised Input Tax Credit is not an unrestricted entitlement and is governed by the specific statutory conditions prescribed under Section 54(3). Merely because Input Tax Credit has accumulated in the electronic credit ledger, it cannot be presumed that the registered person is automatically entitled to refund of such accumulated credit. 3.3. The Appellant has further stated that the Respondent was engaged in the manufacture and domestic sale of leather products, which were not liable to Compensation Cess. The accumulated Compensation Cess ITC arose on account of the purchase of coal used as fuel in the manufacturing process. However, the Respondent failed to establish that such accumulation satisfied the statutory requirements for refund under the inverted tax structure provisions. 3.4. The Appellant has submitted that Section 11(2) of the Goods and Services Tax (Compensation to States) Act, 2017 specifically restricts utilisation of Compensation Cess ITC towards payment of Compensation Cess. The Respondent’s claim related to domestic outward supplies, and the First Appellate Authority did not adequately distinguish the factual circumstances of the Respondent’s case from those covered by the said circulars. 3.6. Discussion and Findings 6.1 We have carefully considered the facts of the case, the grounds of appeal preferred by the Department, the submissions advanced by the learned Departmental Representative and the learned Counsel for the Respondent, the original Refund Rejection Orders and the impugned Order-in-Appeal dated 05.07.2021. 6.2 The dispute concerns the refunds claimed by M/s Pacific Leather Pvt. Ltd. towards accumulated Input Tax Credit of Compensation Cess paid on coal used as fuel in the manufacture of leather products during the tax period as mentioned above. 6.3 The material facts indicate that Compensation Cess was paid on the purchase of coal, whereas the Respondent’s finished leather products sold in the domestic market were not subject to Compensation Cess. The Respondent filed its refund applications under the inverted tax structure category. 6.4 we find that the central issue is whether the Respondent’s claim satisfies the statutory requirements for refund of unutilised Cess under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act, 2017. 6.5 Statutory conditions under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act, 2017 Section 54(3) of the CGST Act, 2017, provides, “(3) Subject to the provisions of sub-section (10), a registered person may claim refund of any unutilised input tax credit at the end of any tax period: Provided that no refund of unutilised input tax credit shall be allowed in cases other than— (i) zero rated supplies made without payment of tax; (ii) where the credit has accumulated on account of rate of tax on inputs being higher than the rate of tax on output supplies (other than nil rated or fully exempt supplies) …” 6.6 The above provision establishes that refund of unutilised ITC is subject to the categories and conditions prescribed by the legislature. It does not confer an unrestricted right to a cash refund merely because credit has accumulated in the electronic credit ledger. 6.7 The Respondent’s claim was made under the inverted tax structure category. Consequently, the Respondent was required to establish that the accumulation arose on account of the rate of tax on inputs being higher than the rate of tax on output supplies within the meaning of Section 54(3)(ii), and that the exclusion concerning nil-rated or fully exempt supplies did not apply. 6.8 In the present case, the accumulation of Compensation Cess ITC is attributed to the levy of Compensation Cess on coal and the absence of such levy on the finished leather products. However, the mere difference between the levy on an input and the absence of the same levy on an output does not, by itself, establish entitlement to refund under the inverted tax structure category. The relevant statutory test must be applied to the levy in respect of which the refund is claimed. Where the outward supply does not attract Compensation Cess, it is necessary to determine whether the outward supply is nil-rated or exempt for the purposes of that levy and whether the statutory exclusion under Section 54(3)(ii) of the CGST Act 2017 is attracted. 6.9 The Respondent has contended that coal was used as fuel in the manufacturing process and that Compensation Cess paid on such coal constituted eligible ITC. Reliance has been placed upon Section 16 read with Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017. However, we find that Section 16 prescribes the conditions for taking ITC, while Section 17(5) specifies categories of blocked credit. The fact that coal does not fall within the applicable blocked-credit provisions may support the Respondent’s contention regarding the admissibility of the underlying credit. 6.10 The statutory scheme distinguishes between the availment of ITC, its utilisation towards payment of the relevant tax or cess, and the refund of unutilised credit. These matters are governed by separate statutory provisions. Therefore, even assuming that the Respondent was entitled to avail the Compensation Cess ITC on coal, it was still required to establish an independent entitlement to refund under Section 54(3). 6.11 The First Appellate Authority placed undue reliance upon Section 17(5) without adequately examining the refund conditions. We find that the reasoning adopted in the impugned Order-in-Appeal is insufficient to sustain the grant of refund. 6.12 Section 11(2) of the Goods and Services Tax (Compensation to States) Act, 2017, provides for the application of specified provisions of the CGST Act and the IGST Act to Compensation Cess, subject to the conditions stipulated therein. The proviso to Section 11(2) states: “Provided that the input tax credit in respect of cess on supply of goods and services leviable under section 8, shall be utilised only towards payment of said cess on supply of goods and services leviable under the said section.” 6.13 The statutory restriction preserves the separate character of Compensation Cess ITC and confines its utilisation to payment of the corresponding Compensation Cess. 6.14 The Respondent has argued that this restriction concerns utilisation and does not expressly prohibit a refund. we accept that the utilisation restriction, by itself, should not be interpreted as an absolute prohibition on every refund claim. Nevertheless, the absence of an express prohibition does not create an independent right to refund in circumstances not authorised by the applicable statutory provisions. 6.15 The Respondent must establish that the claim falls within a refund category permitted by Section 54(3), read with the applicable provisions governing Compensation Cess. The inability to utilise Compensation Cess ITC against other tax liabilities cannot, by itself, confer an entitlement to a cash refund. 6.16 The First Appellate Authority did not adequately examine this distinction. The Department’s objection is, therefore, sustainable to the extent that the impugned order treated the existence of eligible Compensation Cess ITC as sufficient justification for granting the refund. 6.17 Rule 89 of the CGST Rules, 2017, prescribes the procedure for filing refund applications and the supporting particulars required for processing refund claims. Rule 89(5), as applicable to the relevant period, provides the formula for determining the maximum refund of unutilised ITC in cases of accumulation on account of an inverted tax structure. 6.18 The rule operates within the statutory framework of Section 54(3). It does not independently create a substantive right to refund where the conditions of the parent provision are not satisfied. 6.19 Accordingly, the Respondent must first establish that the claim falls within the category of refund permitted under Section 54(3). Only thereafter can the applicable computation and procedural requirements under Rule 89 be considered. 6.20 The First Appellate Authority was required to determine the statutory basis of the refund and then examine the applicable computation. The mere accumulation of Compensation Cess ITC cannot replace that exercise. 6.21 Notification No. 5/2017-Central Tax (Rate), dated 28.06.2017, as amended from time to time, concerns specified goods in respect of which refund of accumulated ITC is restricted under the inverted tax structure category. The applicability of this notification must be determined with reference to the description and classification of the outward goods, the relevant amendments and the tax period concerned. 6.22 The material presently available does not establish that the finished leather products manufactured by the Respondent fall within a specified category covered by the notification as applicable during the period as mentioned above. We find, therefore, does not base its conclusion upon an assumption that the notification applies to the Respondent’s goods. Nevertheless, the absence of an applicable notification-based restriction would not, by itself, establish a right to refund. The substantive requirements of Section 54(3) must independently be fulfilled. 6.23 The Respondent has relied upon Circular No. 45/19/2018-GST dated 30.05.2018 in support of its refund claim. Paragraph 5 of the circular concerns refund of unutilised Compensation Cess ITC on inputs where the final product is not subject to Compensation Cess. Paragraph 5.1 identifies the issue by reference to exporters manufacturing aluminium products using coal, where Compensation Cess is levied on coal but not on aluminium products. Paragraph 5.2 clarifies: “…a registered person making zero rated supply of aluminum products under bond or LUT may claim refund of unutilized credit including that of compensation cess paid on coal.” 6.24 The clarification must be read in the context in which it was issued. It addresses zero-rated supplies of aluminium products made under bond or LUT and does not expressly grant an unconditional refund entitlement for every domestic outward supply whose finished product is not subject to Compensation Cess. 6.25 The Respondent’s claim relates to domestic supplies of leather products. The record does not establish that the disputed amount is attributable to zero-rated supplies covered by the clarification. 6.26 The Respondent is correct in contending that an administrative circular cannot override the parent enactment. However, this principle does not itself establish that the disputed refund is admissible. 6.27 The First Appellate Authority did not adequately explain how the clarification concerning zero-rated supplies supported the refund claim arising from the Respondent’s domestic outward supplies. Its reliance upon the circular is, therefore, insufficient to sustain the impugned order. 6.28 The respondent relied on In Patson Papers Private Limited v. Union of India, the Gujarat High Court, by order dated 28.03.2025, considered refund claims concerning Compensation Cess paid on coal used in the manufacture of goods exported by the petitioners. The Court examined Section 54(3) of the CGST Act, Section 16 of the IGST Act, Section 11(2) of the Compensation Cess Act and the relevant CBIC circulars. 6.29 The Respondent cannot, therefore, rely upon the judgment as dispensing with the conditions of Section 54(3) in every case involving accumulated Compensation Cess ITC. The statutory conditions and factual circumstances of the present claim must be examined independently. 6.30 The Respondent relied on In Atul Limited v. Union of India, the Gujarat High Court, by judgment dated 24.07.2025, examined refund claims relating to accumulated Compensation Cess ITC on coal used in manufacturing goods exported by the petitioner. 6.31 The Court considered the relevant provisions of the CGST Act, the IGST Act and the Compensation Cess Act, together with the applicable circulars, in the context of zero-rated supplies. 6.32 The judgment is distinguishable because the Respondent in the present appeal has claimed a refund in respect of domestic outward supplies. The record does not establish that the disputed amount is attributable to exports or other qualifying zero-rated supplies. 6.33 Accordingly, the decision cannot be treated as establishing an automatic entitlement to refund under the inverted tax structure category for the domestic supplies involved in the present case. 6.34 The Respondent relied on In Tata Steel Limited v. State of Jharkhand, the Jharkhand High Court, by judgment dated 03.04.2025, considered a refund claim concerning Compensation Cess ITC on coal used in manufacturing goods exported under a Letter of Undertaking. 6.35 The Court set aside the rejection orders because the refund had been rejected on grounds found to be beyond the requirements of the applicable statutory provisions, rules and binding circulars. 6.36 The judgment concerned exported goods and the procedural requirements applicable to the refund claim in that case. It does not directly determine the admissibility of a refund arising from domestic outward supplies of leather products. The decision is, therefore, distinguishable on facts and does not dispense with the requirement to establish the statutory conditions of Section 54(3) in the present proceedings. 6.37 The above judgments demonstrate that refund of Compensation Cess ITC may be available in appropriate circumstances, particularly where the credit is attributable to qualifying zero-rated supplies. They also establish that statutory provisions and binding circulars must be correctly applied. However, the cases arose in materially different factual circumstances. They do not, on the facts presently establish, that the Respondent’s domestic refund claim is automatically admissible. 6.38 The Department’s appeal must accordingly be determined by applying Section 54(3) to the particular outward supplies and the precise refund category invoked by the Respondent. 6.39 The First Appellate Authority allowed the Respondent’s appeal principally on the ground that Compensation Cess paid on coal constituted eligible ITC and was not blocked under Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017. 6.40 However, the First Appellate Authority was also required to determine whether the accumulated credit fulfilled the statutory conditions for refund under Section 54(3). The impugned order does not adequately establish: 6.41 The First Appellate Authority thus failed to adequately distinguish between admissibility of credit and entitlement to refund. This constitutes a material deficiency in the application of the statutory refund provisions. 6.42 Upon a cumulative consideration of the facts, the statutory provisions, the applicable rules, the circulars and the judicial authorities, we find that the First Appellate Authority has not established, on the material presently available, an independent statutory entitlement to a refund claims under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act, 2017. 6.43 The fact that Compensation Cess was paid on coal used in the manufacturing process, and that the corresponding credit may otherwise be admissible, does not automatically confer a right to receive the accumulated amount as a cash refund. 6.44 The First Appellate Authority was required to establish that the accumulation fell within a category for which refund was permissible under Section 54(3), read with the applicable provisions governing Compensation Cess. The First Appellate Authority did not adequately record such a finding before allowing the refund. 6.45 Accordingly, the Department’s contention that admissibility of ITC and entitlement to refund claims are distinct statutory matters, is accepted. The impugned Order-in-Appeal is liable to be set aside. 6.46 We accordingly find that the Department’s challenge to the impugned Order-in-Appeal is sustainable. 7. ORDER In view of the foregoing findings and discussion, the we pass the following order: The following Appeals filed before Appellate Tribunal by the Department under Section 112(3) of the SGST Act, 2017, are hereby allowed.
