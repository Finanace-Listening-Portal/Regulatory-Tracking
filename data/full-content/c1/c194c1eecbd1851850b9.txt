The expansion is expected to improve the bank's priority-sector lending compliance and reduce its expenditure on acquiring priority-sector lending certificates (PSLCs). Its longer-term objective is to become a net seller of these certificates.The expansion is part of PNB's broader strategy to shift towards granular, higher-yielding advances. Its retail, agriculture and MSME (RAM) portfolio currently accounts for 54 per cent of its overall loan book. MSME advances are growing at around 20 per cent year on year, agricultural priority-sector lending at 18 per cent and core retail advances, excluding inter-bank participation certificates (IBPC), at 16.3 per cent, according to a report by Motilal Oswal Financial Services. Advt PNB is targeting overall loan growth of 12-13 per cent in FY27, supported by traction in the RAM segment. Its deposits grew 15.6 per cent in September 2026, aided by mobilisation of USD 2.7 billion through FCNR(B) deposits. Its credit-deposit ratio of around 74 per cent provides headroom to sustain lending momentum. Motilal Oswal estimates the bank's loan book will grow at a compound annual growth rate of 14 per cent between FY26 and FY28.The bank is simultaneously reshaping its corporate loan portfolio by running down low-yielding exposures, even as fresh sanctions remain strong in sectors such as renewable energy and data centres. The sustained reduction in its IBPC portfolio over the past couple of years has supported an improvement in overall portfolio profitability. The shift towards higher-yielding RAM advances is expected to improve portfolio diversification and support margins.Q3 NIM boostPNB expects a further improvement in net interest margins in the December quarter, following an expansion in global NIM to 2.58 per cent and domestic NIM to 2.74 per cent in the September quarter. Management expects global and domestic NIMs to improve by around 7-8 basis points and 9-10 basis points, respectively, in the December quarter. The bank is also targeting a 40-45 basis-point improvement in blended corporate loan yields from the earlier level of 7.28 per cent, with 20-25 basis points of improvement anticipated in the December quarter.However, rising deposit costs are expected to partly offset the improvement in lending yields. PNB anticipates a 7-8 basis-point increase in its cost of deposits in the December quarter. The entire FCNR(B) deposit mobilisation has already been utilised, limiting the scope for further incremental benefits from these funds. Advt The bank is also seeking to diversify its income beyond treasury gains and recoveries by scaling recurring fee-based businesses. Cash management services are expected to generate around Rs 250 crore in quarterly income, while its credit card business, with approximately 11.5 lakh cards, is already generating around Rs 200 crore in quarterly fees. PNB aims to reduce its cost-to-income ratio from around 50 per cent currently to 49 per cent by March 2027 and approximately 45 per cent over the next two to three years.Asset quality remains a key support to the bank's profitability outlook. Its gross and net non-performing asset ratios improved to 2.55 per cent and 0.25 per cent, respectively, in the September quarter, while its provision coverage ratio stood at around 97.3 per cent. The bank estimates a gross expected credit loss provisioning requirement of around Rs 8,000 crore. It has already built additional provisions of Rs 2,560 crore on NPA accounts and around Rs 1,100-1,150 crore on standard accounts, reducing the incremental provisioning requirement ahead of the ECL transition. Advt PNB expects normalised credit costs of around 25-30 basis points, incorporating the anticipated ECL-related provisioning impact. Recoveries are expected to moderate from around Rs 13,000 crore in FY27 to Rs 12,000 crore in FY28. Core operating profit, excluding recoveries from written-off accounts and treasury gains, grew around 29 per cent year on year in the September quarter. Management expects core pre-provision operating profit to grow around 25 per cent in FY27 and net profit to exceed Rs 21,000 crore.The bank has set a medium-term objective of achieving a return on assets of around 1.1 per cent by FY28. Motilal Oswal estimates RoA at 1.1 per cent in FY28 and has reiterated its Buy rating on the stock with a target price of Rs 160, based on 1.1 times estimated FY28 book value.
