Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); PCIT Vs Jay Kishore Choubey (Orissa High Court) Summary: The Orissa High Court, in PCIT Vs Jay Kishore Choubey, dismissed an income-tax appeal filed by the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax, Sambalpur, on account of the monetary limits prescribed by the Central Board of Direct Taxes for departmental appeals. The Revenue had challenged the order dated 29 November 2023 passed by the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Cuttack Bench, in ITA No. 02/CTK/2023 concerning Assessment Year 2010-11. The appeal was preferred under Section 260A of the Income Tax Act, 1961. In paragraph 10 of its memorandum of appeal, the Revenue stated that the disputed tax effect amounted to ₹1,74,62,851, which exceeded the monetary threshold prescribed under CBDT Instruction No. 17/2019 dated 8 August 2019. The Department also asserted that the case involved a substantial question of law and that the Assessing Officer and Joint Commissioner of Income Tax had recommended challenging the Tribunal’s decision. Appearing for the assessee, Senior Advocate Mr. Jagabandhu Sahoo submitted that the disputed tax effect was below the revised monetary limit prescribed by CBDT Circular No. 09/2024 dated 17 September 2024. The circular enhanced the monetary threshold for departmental appeals before High Courts to ₹2 crore. Accordingly, the assessee argued that the Revenue’s appeal was liable to be dismissed. The Department’s counsel, Mr. Avinash Kedia, did not dispute the revised monetary limits but drew attention to the substantive issue considered by the Tribunal. The Tribunal had referred to the Delhi High Court’s decision in Experion Developers (P.) Ltd., (2020) 115 taxmann.com 338 (Delhi) and held that mechanical approval without recorded reasons demonstrating independent application of mind would invalidate the initiation of reassessment proceedings under Sections 147 and 148 of the Income Tax Act. Thus, the underlying controversy concerned the validity of reassessment proceedings initiated on the basis of mechanical approval. After examining the Revenue’s own statement regarding the tax effect and considering the CBDT circular dated 17 September 2024, the Division Bench held that the disputed amount was below the prescribed monetary threshold for maintaining a departmental appeal before the High Court. Consequently, the appeal was dismissed without adjudicating the substantive controversy concerning mechanical approval for reassessment proceedings. Importantly, the High Court expressly clarified that dismissal of the appeal would not prevent the Revenue from raising the substantial question of law in another matter involving an identical issue. The Court also dismissed all pending interlocutory applications, if any, and made no order as to costs. The decision demonstrates the application of CBDT’s revised departmental litigation thresholds, while distinguishing dismissal on monetary-limit grounds from a judicial determination of the underlying legal issue. Cases Discussed 1. Experion Developers (P.) Ltd., (2020) 115 taxmann.com 338 (Delhi High Court). The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal referred to this decision while examining the validity of reassessment proceedings. As recorded in the Orissa High Court’s order, the Tribunal held that mechanical approval without reasons demonstrating independent application of mind would invalidate initiation of proceedings under Sections 147 and 148 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The Revenue referred to this issue before the High Court. However, the High Court did not decide the question on merits, dismissing the departmental appeal solely because the tax effect was below the prescribed monetary limit. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ORISSA HIGH COURT 1. This matter is taken up through hybrid arrangement. 2. This Income Tax Appeal is filed under Section 260A of the Income Tax Act, 1961 assailing the order dated 29.11.2023 passed in ITA No.02/CTK/2023 by the learned Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Cuttack Bench, Cuttack pertaining to the assessment year 2010-11. 3. It is brought to our notice that the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax, Sambalpur has filed the memo of appeal on various grounds. However, relevant fact pleaded in the appeal for the present purpose is quoted hereunder: “10. The tax effect of Rs.1,74,62,851/- is more than the threshold limit for filing of appeal before the Hon’ble High Court as laid down in CBDT Instruction No.17/2019 dated 08.08.2019 and also involves substantial question of law. Further, there is also merit in this case for filing appeal before the Hon’ble High Court of Orissa. The Assessing Officer as well as the Jt. CIT have suggested for filing of appeal before the Hon’ble High Court, as the decision of learned ITAT, Cuttack Bench, Cuttack is not acceptable.” 4. It is submitted by Mr. Jagabandhu Sahoo, learned Senior Advocate that the tax effect as stated in the memo of appeal is less than the monetary limit specified in the Circular No.09/2024 (F.No.279/Misc./M-74/2024-ITJ) dated 17th September, 2024 issued by the Central Board of Direct Taxes. The disputed amount being below the monetary limits specified therein for the purpose of filing an appeal before the High Court, the appeal deserves to be dismissed. 5. Mr. Avinash Kedia, learned Junior Standing Counsel for the Income Tax Department did not dispute the monetary limits so specified in the Circular dated 17th September, 2024; nonetheless, he submitted that the learned Income Tax Appellate Tribunal referring to the decision of the Hon’ble Delhi High Court in the case of Experion Developers (P.) Ltd., (2020) 115 taxmann.com 338 (Delhi) held that mechanical approval without ascribing any reason and demonstrating independent application of mind would render the initiation of proceeding under Section 147 read with Section 148 of the Income Tax Act invalid. 6. On perusal of the averments made in paragraph-10 of the memo of appeal and having taken note of the tenor of Circular dated 17th September, 2024, as the tax effect is less than monetary limit specified for the purpose of maintaining appeal before this Court, the appeal as preferred by the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax, Sambalpur stands dismissed. However, dismissal of appeal would not pose as impediment for raising substantial question of law in any other matter having identical issue. 7. In the result, the Income Tax Appeal along with pending interlocutory application(s), if any, stands dismissed. No order as to costs.
