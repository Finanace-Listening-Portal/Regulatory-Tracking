The proposed commission caps and expense of management (EoM) limits could put greater pressure on smaller banks, brokers and corporate agents, potentially reshaping competitive dynamics across the industry.Most large insurers believe they have sufficient flexibility in their existing cost structures and distribution models to navigate the proposed changes. However, the impact is likely to vary depending on channel and product mix. SBI Life and Canara HSBC are expected to be among the least affected, with the potential to gain market share as competitors recalibrate their distribution strategies, Motilal Oswal Financial Services said in a report. Advt Insurers broadly support the intent of the proposed reforms, which seek to improve distribution transparency, policyholder outcomes and affordability. However, they have called for a framework that balances distribution economics with insurance affordability and penetration. Some insurers also expect the final commission limits to be more accommodating than those outlined in the consultation paper.Agency, proprietary and large bancassurance relationships appear relatively better positioned under the proposed framework, while smaller banks, brokers and corporate agents could face greater pressure. HDFC Life, for instance, expects its agency channel to be the least affected, followed by banks, with brokers and corporate agents facing relatively higher impact.Canara HSBC's moderate commission rates, balanced product mix and expense ratio of about 18%, compared with the proposed 15% EoM threshold, position it to adapt to the final regulations. Any additional EoM headroom could allow the insurer to accelerate investments and growth. A 10% reduction in acquisition costs could expand its value of new business (VNB) margin by 2.9%.Its position in the credit life segment could also provide some insulation. Canara HSBC's bancassurance partner is also its promoter, with the insurance product helping protect the partner's loan book. This could preserve the partner's incentive to drive sales even if the commission structure changes. Advt SBI Life's distribution strength and ability to adapt to the revised framework could similarly help it gain market share as the industry adjusts to the new operating environment. The insurer reported the highest VNB margin in the industry at 28.5% in the second quarter of FY27, supported by continued momentum in protection products and launches in the non-participating and annuity segments, partly offset by the impact of input tax credit losses.Other insurers are also preparing to realign their cost structures. Max Life expects to achieve a 5-7% reduction in its EoM ratio through commission and GST optimisation, alongside a further 2-3% improvement through operating efficiencies over time. More than 90% of its business is expected to remain unaffected by the proposed changes, while it could benefit from counter-share gains at Axis Bank. Broker-led business and group credit life are relatively more exposed but account for a smaller share of its portfolio. Advt HDFC Life, with a current expense ratio of about 21%, could mathematically reach the proposed 15% EoM threshold. ICICI Prudential Life has reduced its cost-to-premium ratio, excluding GST, to 18.3% in the first half of FY27, reflecting cost discipline and preparedness for regulatory recalibration. LIC, meanwhile, expects the changes to create opportunities for better engagement with existing distribution partners.Significant impactThe impact on business volumes could nevertheless be significant. Lower distribution costs are expected to improve product-level profitability, but volumes could face pressure in the medium term, particularly in credit life. Insurers have stressed the importance of preserving the economics of credit life and annuity products, given their role in extending protection to underserved borrowers and customers. Advt Insurers are increasingly looking at technology, digitisation, artificial intelligence-led productivity and operating leverage to adapt to a lower-cost distribution environment. The industry is also preparing for further regulatory changes, including the transition to International Financial Reporting Standards and a risk-based solvency framework, which could improve transparency, global comparability and capital allocation.Individual annualised premium equivalent (APE) growth moderated in the second quarter of FY27, partly due to volatile equity markets affecting unit-linked insurance plan demand. October growth remained subdued, with the industry recording single-digit growth. However, growth is expected to recover towards a sustainable mid-teen trajectory, supported by a revival in ULIPs, sustained protection demand and improving guaranteed savings demand amid a rising interest rate environment.The brokerage maintained a positive view on VNB margin expansion, supported by a higher share of non-ULIP products and improving cost efficiency. Its preferred picks are Canara HSBC Life, with a target price of Rs 180 based on 1.7 times FY28 estimated price-to-embedded value, and SBI Life, with a target price of Rs 2,340 based on 2.1 times FY28 estimated price-to-embedded value.
