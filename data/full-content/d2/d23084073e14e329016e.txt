The system is being used for document and data verification, while the broader Udaan transformation has reduced loan turnaround times and centralised processing across the branch network.Samur.AI currently operates within Mahindra Finance's central processing centre (CPC) rather than the credit-decision-making layer, according to a report by Motilal Oswal Financial Services. The system performs document and data verification, including regional-language documents and highly variable dealer documentation, and provides a rationale when a case is flagged for human review. Advt The expansion of Samur.AI is part of the company's Udaan programme, which has moved lending from largely manual and decentralised processes towards a standardised, technology-driven operating model. The CPC rollout covered more than 1,300 branches in around 11-12 months, compared with the usual two-three years for such an industry implementation, the report said.CPC file volumes have increased around eight-fold, while cost per file has fallen to about 54 from a 100 base and turnaround time has declined to about 49 from 100. CPC headcount has also fallen from around 885 to about 465. The report said the numbers suggest automation is absorbing incremental processing volumes rather than merely shifting work between teams.Digital adoption has also sharply reduced loan processing times. Time to first offer has declined from around six-seven days to about 20 minutes, while the broader login-to-disbursement cycle has fallen from around 10-12 days to the same day or within two days for most cases.The lending workflow is supported by the M TEZZ field app, more than 13,000 business rules, Account Aggregator and bureau integrations, digital KYC, e-sign and e-mandates. Around 85-90% of recent originations and about 70% of the outstanding portfolio are now covered by e-mandates.Data miningMahindra Finance is also using 30 years of proprietary repayment data in its underwriting system. The historical data has been converted into customer personas and scorecards and combined with real-time bureau and banking information. This allows the company to assess selected new-to-credit customers even without a conventional bureau history. Advt Customer attributes including geography, landholding and crop mix can be mapped against historical repayment behaviour to generate an initial offer. However, the digital journey is not universal. Informal-income customers may still require physical business assessment, while customers seeking higher loan-to-value ratios can trigger additional field verification.Around 85% of cases are processed within the targeted turnaround time, with the balance requiring additional checks because of limited bureau or banking information or other exceptions.The faster decision-making is also supporting growth at dealerships. Tractor disbursements grew more than 45% in the previous year, compared with industry growth of around 20%, while Mahindra Finance's own OEM-linked growth was around 22%. Advt Collections have also become increasingly digital, with digital collections rising to 75-77%. E-mandate penetration in newly sourced portfolios has reached 85-90%, while the collection process is supported by AI-led calling for customer reminders and field-force prioritisation of high-value, high-probability contracts.The productivity gains are being redeployed towards growth and cross-selling. Branches are moving from a service-heavy model towards outbound sales and cross-selling across insurance, fixed deposits, mutual funds and personal loans.Insurance premium reached around Rs950 crore in FY26, while fixed-deposit mobilisation has doubled over the past two years. Personal loans are emerging as the next major cross-selling opportunity, with Suvidha serving tickets up to Rs3.5 lakh and PL Next targeting higher-ticket customers. Advt Mahindra Finance's roughly 500-member direct marketing team generates more than Rs200 crore of monthly business across wheels and the recently launched PL Next product. The company also has around 150,000-200,000 branch walk-ins, providing a further opportunity for cross-selling.
