Summary: BLS Polymers Limited filed its final prospectus with the Registrar of Companies, as recorded on SEBI’s website on 30 October 2026, following the closure of its initial public offering (IPO) on 29 October 2026. The book-built IPO, which opened on 27 October 2026, comprised a fresh issue of up to 1.70 crore equity shares at a final issue price of ₹150 per share, aggregating up to ₹255 crore, without any offer-for-sale component. The minimum application lot was 100 shares, requiring an investment of ₹15,000. The company, engaged in manufacturing customised polymer compounds for the power, telecommunications, railways, water distribution and oil and gas sectors, proposes to utilise the proceeds primarily for expanding its Goa manufacturing facility, meeting working capital requirements and general corporate purposes. According to its draft prospectus, revenue from operations increased to ₹328.44 crore in FY 2024-25 from ₹296.36 crore in FY 2023-24, while annual profit rose to ₹21.14 crore from ₹7.52 crore. However, significant risks include customer concentration, with the top ten customers contributing 64.44% of FY 2024-25 revenue, substantial raw-material costs, dependence on power and telecommunications customers, and execution risks associated with capacity expansion. Following the closure of bidding, applicants must monitor allotment, ASBA fund unblocking, demat credit and listing through official exchange and registrar channels. The proposed listing on NSE and BSE remains subject to completion of the prescribed procedures and exchange approvals. Investors should assess the company’s financial performance, utilisation of IPO proceeds, operational risks and applicable capital gains tax implications without relying on unofficial grey-market premiums or speculative listing-price predictions. What BLS Polymers does and where revenue concentration appears The offer documents describe BLS Polymers as a manufacturer of multiple grades of custom-formulated polymer compounds used in critical industries, including telecommunication, power, railways, water, and oil and gas distribution. Its Goa manufacturing facility is central to the proposed capital-expenditure object. The company also depends heavily on wire and cable polymer compounds and on a concentrated customer base, so an investor should connect revenue growth with concentration, raw-material costs and the ability to pass through price changes. The DRHP reported revenue from operations of ₹32,843.59 lakh in Fiscal 2025, ₹29,635.83 lakh in Fiscal 2024 and ₹31,461.61 lakh in Fiscal 2023. Profit for the year was ₹2,113.60 lakh, ₹752.20 lakh and ₹624.98 lakh respectively. Those figures show a sharp Fiscal 2025 profit increase, but they should not be extrapolated mechanically. The same document reported that the top ten customers contributed 64.44% of Fiscal 2025 revenue, while raw-material consumption represented 80.85% of revenue from operations. Power and telecommunication customers accounted for 81.59% of Fiscal 2025 revenue. How the ₹255 crore fresh issue is intended to be used Goa manufacturing expansion The DRHP allocated ₹6,984.32 lakh of net proceeds to expand the existing Goa manufacturing facility by increasing capacity for specified existing products. Capital expenditure can improve capacity but creates execution risks: equipment procurement, installation, regulatory clearances, commissioning, customer qualification and utilisation may not follow the original schedule. The final prospectus should be checked for the updated deployment schedule and whether any amount changed after the final issue price was determined. Working capital and general corporate purposes The draft objects allocated ₹7,500 lakh to working-capital requirements. The residual amount, after issue expenses and the specified objects, was earmarked for general corporate purposes within the regulatory ceiling. Working-capital funding supports inventory and receivables but does not by itself ensure profitable sales or collection. The DRHP showed Fiscal 2025 inventory and receivable holding periods of 65 and 76 days respectively, making conversion of funded working capital into cash a continuing monitoring point. Raw-material price, availability and pass-through risk Raw materials consumed were more than four-fifths of Fiscal 2025 revenue from operations. Polymer inputs can be affected by petrochemical prices, currency movements, supplier availability and quality. If sales contracts do not permit timely cost pass-through, margins can compress even when volumes rise. The percentage declined across the three reported years, but that historical trend is not a promise. Expansion and utilisation risk Fresh capital does not remove commissioning or demand risk. Delays can raise costs; low utilisation can depress returns on capital; and product qualification can postpone commercial sales. Investors should compare actual utilisation disclosures with the prospectus schedule rather than using the intended capacity as though it were operational. Listing, liquidity and price risk This is the company’s first public issue. There was no established public market before listing. The offer price does not guarantee an active market, liquidity, price stability or gains. Listing can occur below the issue price, and later prices can react to financial results, supply-demand conditions, lock-in expiries, sector cycles and broader markets. Grey-market quotations are unofficial and are deliberately excluded from this article. Allotment, unblocking, demat credit and grievance route After closure, the registrar and designated stock exchange finalise the basis of allotment under the issue framework. A valid application may receive full, partial or no allotment. ASBA blocks funds rather than transferring them at application; the amount corresponding to allotted shares is debited and the balance is unblocked. UPI applications depend on successful mandate acceptance and application validity. Applicants should check official status using the PAN and application details on the registrar or exchange portal and should not share OTPs or payment credentials with unsolicited callers. First verify the application number, PAN, demat beneficiary details and bank/UPI mandate status. Check allotment only on KFin Technologies, NSE or BSE official facilities when the issuer is available there. If the block remains after the official unblock date, contact the SCSB or bank with the application and mandate reference. For allotment or registrar-data discrepancies, raise a written ticket with KFin Technologies and preserve the acknowledgement. Escalate unresolved securities-market complaints through SEBI SCORES and use the exchange grievance channel where relevant. Confirm demat credit and the separate exchange trading notice before attempting to sell; a tentative listing date is not itself proof that trading has begun. Tax treatment for a successful allottee Receipt of shares on allotment at the issue price does not ordinarily create a capital gain merely because the securities enter the demat account. Tax consequences generally arise on a later transfer. For Tax Year 2026–27, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies. Subject to statutory conditions including recognised-exchange transfer and securities transaction tax, section 196 covers specified short-term listed-equity gains at 20%, while qualifying long-term listed-equity gains under the applicable provisions are taxed at 12.5% above the statutory threshold. Holding-period classification, cost, brokerage, exchange charges, STT, set-off rules, non-resident provisions and any grandfathering or corporate action must be checked for the actual transaction. STT is not generally deducted as a capital-gains expense where the statute prohibits it. An investor should retain the contract note, demat statement, allotment advice, bank debit record and expense ledger. A listing-day sale is not tax-free simply because the shares came through an IPO. Business-income treatment can apply where facts show trading activity rather than investment, and non-residents may have treaty, withholding and foreign-exchange questions requiring professional review.
