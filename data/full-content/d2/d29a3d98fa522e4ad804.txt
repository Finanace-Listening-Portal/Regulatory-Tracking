Articles 3 min read Banking’s next leap: Knowing all of us better Banking institutions are poised to leverage artificial intelligence to enhance customer understanding and service delivery. This transition seeks to replace inconsistent interactions with a seamless flow of insights in the banking sector. Notably, Bank of India is establishing data lakes for effective information centralization and analysis. Employees will partner with AI to improve operations while prioritizing data governance and security. For executive director Rajiv Mishra, that enormous customer base hints at what AI could make possible: banking that understands the needs of a village farmer as closely as those of a customer in Mumbai.Banking offers fertile ground for AI because so much of its information is structured. Transaction amounts, account balances, repayment dates and borrowing histories can be compared and analysed to identify patterns. These records could help banks assess creditworthiness, spot unusual activity and anticipate financial needs in near real-time. Advt Nitesh Ranjan, executive director at Union Bank of India, described this as a move from occasional interactions to an ongoing understanding of customers. “We will be moving from episodic banking to continuous intelligence, real-time banking – understanding customers as they do transactions and being very contextual to their requirements,” he said on our webinar recently.The first phase of banking’s digital transformation gave customers mobile banking, internet banking and digital payments. Ranjan said the next would embed intelligence into customer interactions. “AI and intelligent automation can enable hyper-personalised engagement, near-real-time credit decisions and faster fulfilment, and intelligent fraud monitoring,” he said.But there is a distinction between knowing something about a customer and understanding what it means. Building that understanding requires banks to bring their information together. Mishra said Bank of India had built a data lake, a central repository that brings data from different systems into one place. The aim is to give teams a consistent basis for analysis and decisions.“The foundation has to include data quality and consistency, data governance, privacy and security, access controls and system resilience,” Mishra said. Ranjan also stressed the need for “common business definitions” – so that information means the same thing across the bank. Advt Union Bank had taken almost 18 months to build its data system, Ranjan said. AI itself could help improve the information feeding these systems. “It’s better to use AI to clean the data rather than waiting for clean data before using AI,” he said.Alongside more usable data, banks need people who can put it to work. Rishi Aurora, managing partner at IBM Consulting India and South Asia, described a future in which employees work with AI agents – software that can carry out steps in a task.“People, software and agents working together to derive the most optimal outcome for the organisation – that is going to be the model,” Aurora said. “AI can take care of analysis, but empathy, judgement and creativity will play an integral role.” Organisations that make these capabilities work together, he said, will be most successful in adopting AI. The outcomes, he added, must be auditable and explainable, because agents will have access to sensitive data. Advt This makes banking knowledge valuable alongside technical expertise. Mishra said Bank of India was hiring specialists who need not have a background in financial services, then bringing them together with bankers to develop applications. Ranjan said Union Bank was developing “AI-aware bankers” through foundational and certification courses.Some of the expertise must come from outside. Ranjan said no single institution could independently master every capability across AI, cloud computing, cybersecurity and analytics. Technology partners could help banks connect systems, draw on experience from other markets and turn experimental applications into services that operate securely at scale. “Partners like IBM help us transfer those capabilities to our own internal employees,” Ranjan said. Advt Banking will become much more personalised. I am looking at how we serve a village farmer as well as a customer in Mumbai. But trust is something we cannot lose. Sophisticated frauds and biases can move as fast as our AI models. We need to innovate boldly, with responsible governance, because technology may change, but trust must remain our focus.Rajiv Mishra, Executive Director, Bank of IndiaThe next phase of banking will embed intelligence into customer interactions, making services more contextual, predictive and proactive. AI should help us understand customers better, serve them faster and protect them more effectively. But its value does not come from the model alone. It depends on trusted data, common business definitions, secure integrations, reliable context and a well-governed decision-making system.Nitesh Ranjan, Executive Director, Union Bank of IndiaPeople trust banks with their money, their identity and their data.
