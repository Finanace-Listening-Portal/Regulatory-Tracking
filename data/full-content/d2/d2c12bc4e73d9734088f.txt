Summary: Suspension of GST registration following an enforcement investigation raises important questions concerning the powers and responsibilities of the proper officer. Although enforcement authorities may investigate suspected fictitious transactions and recommend action, their findings do not automatically replace the statutory satisfaction required from the officer responsible for registration proceedings. The distinction between suspension under Rule 21A and cancellation under Section 29 is particularly important because a prior hearing is not invariably required before suspension, whereas final cancellation must comply with prescribed procedural safeguards. The Delhi High Court’s decision in Sant Ram v. Delhi State GST & Others illustrates the requirement of independent decision-making. Balaji Trading Co. addresses the need for timely adjudication where a taxpayer’s reply remains undecided, while Deepak Sharma highlights the commercial consequences of prolonged registration proceedings. The Karnataka High Court’s decision in Goutham Constructions further demonstrates the importance of examining evidence of genuine transactions while recognising the taxpayer’s responsibility to preserve replies and acknowledgements. Taxpayers facing suspension should identify the precise allegation, request disclosure of relevant material, provide transaction-wise evidence and seek revocation under Rule 21A(4) where justified. The proper officer must evaluate the evidence independently and reach a reasoned determination rather than mechanically implementing an enforcement recommendation. Table of Contents▸ 1. An enforcement report is the beginning of an enquiry, not the conclusion A taxpayer’s GST registration is suspended after an enforcement visit or communication. The taxpayer approaches the jurisdictional officer with invoices, bank statements and evidence of receipt of goods. The response is that enforcement has recommended the action and the jurisdictional office cannot do anything until enforcement gives clearance. That position requires scrutiny. Enforcement officers are entitled to investigate suspected fictitious transactions and communicate their findings. A report may contain substantial evidence warranting immediate protective action. But the officer exercising statutory registration powers must still examine whether the material attracts the applicable provision. The legal issue is independence of decision-making. A proper officer cannot substitute another authority’s instruction for the satisfaction that the law requires that officer to reach. The Delhi High Court applied this principle directly in Sant Ram v. Delhi State GST & Others. Nor should taxpayers assume that producing a few invoices automatically answers the investigation. The transactions must be explained as a whole. The department must examine the evidence; the taxpayer must supply evidence capable of examination. 2. First distinguish suspension from cancellation An effective challenge begins with the correct provision. Section 29 governs cancellation and suspension. Rule 21 specifies circumstances attracting cancellation. Rule 21A deals with suspension pending proceedings, and rule 22 governs the cancellation process. Under the current rule 21A (2), suspension does not invariably require a prior hearing. The earlier express requirement of a pre-suspension opportunity was removed in December 2020. It is therefore unsafe to argue that suspension is automatically void merely because the taxpayer was not heard beforehand. Final cancellation stands differently. Section 29(2) expressly requires an opportunity of being heard. The proper challenge should identify the actual defect: No relevant material connecting the taxpayer to a cancellation ground; Mechanical reliance on an enforcement communication; Failure to disclose the substance of the allegation; Failure to consider the reply; Continued suspension without timely determination; Cancellation without the prescribed opportunity or a reasoned decision. The absence of a compulsory pre-suspension hearing does not remove these other statutory responsibilities. 3. Sant Ram: another authority’s letter is not a sufficient reason by itself Case particulars Sant Ram v. Delhi State GST & Others Delhi High Court, W.P.(C) No. 15846/2023 Decision: 11 December 2023 Neutral citation: 2023: DHC:8838-DB. What happened? The show-cause notice proposed cancellation for the following stated reason: “Letter received from Deputy Commissioner, (A.E) vide letter no. 13553 dt 16.09.2022.” Registration was suspended from the notice date. Subsequently, it was cancelled retrospectively. The cancellation order described the reason as “Others.” The communication from the other authority was neither attached to the notice nor meaningfully explained in it. What did the Court decide? The Court held that the cancellation order was unsustainable because it was not supported by reasons. Paragraphs 11 to 14 are particularly important. The Court required the proper officer to arrive independently at satisfaction regarding the circumstances specified in section 29. It stated that an officer entrusted with discretion cannot act mechanically on another authority’s instructions. The cancellation order was set aside and restoration directed. The department remained free to take further steps in accordance with law if statutory violations were found. How should a taxpayer use it? This is the strongest case where the file shows that the jurisdictional officer merely implemented enforcement’s recommendation. A representation should identify: The notice’s reference to the external communication; Whether that material was supplied; Whether the taxpayer’s alleged conduct was identified; Whether the officer recorded an independent conclusion; Whether the order addressed the taxpayer’s records. The case does not prohibit reliance on enforcement evidence. It prohibits mechanical decision-making. That distinction should appear expressly in the submission. 4. Balaji Trading: a reply cannot remain undecided while suspension continues Case particulars M/s. Balaji Trading Co. v. The Commissioner & Another Delhi High Court, W.P.(C) No. 5169/2024 Decision: 10 April 2024 Neutral citation: 2024:DHC:2983-DB. What happened? The cancellation notice alleged that the business premises were found locked, the proprietor had not responded properly to a telephone call, and local enquiries suggested that the establishment opened only occasionally. The taxpayer stated that it had filed a detailed reply on 22 December 2023. Its grievance was that the reply had not been considered and registration continued under suspension. What relief was granted? The department stated that the notice would be adjudicated within two weeks. The Court directed the proper officer to decide it within that period after granting a personal hearing. The Court expressly reserved the parties’ rights and did not determine the merits. Why does it matter? This decision is useful where the taxpayer has answered the allegation but the proceeding has not moved forward. It supports a request for time-bound adjudication and hearing. It should not be cited as an order automatically quashing suspension or declaring that locked premises can never justify an enquiry. The two-week period was fixed in that case. It is not a universal statutory deadline. 5. Deepak Sharma: registration proceedings have immediate business consequences Case particulars Deepak Sharma v. Commissioner, Department of Trade and Taxes, Government of NCT of Delhi Delhi High Court, W.P.(C) No. 4922/2025 Order dated 17 April 2025. According to the available case report, registration was suspended following a cancellation notice. The Court recognised that cancellation could bring a business to a standstill and directed completion of the proceedings within 45 days, with a personal hearing. Notice of hearing was to be uploaded on the portal and communicated by email. This authority assists a request for prompt disposal where the registration restriction is disrupting an operating business. For a filed pleading or publication, the original order should be checked before quoting it verbatim. The account above is based on the published report. The 45-day direction was case-specific, not a general rule invalidating every suspension exceeding that period. 6. Goutham Constructions: the Karnataka decision must be read with its conditions Case particulars M/s. Goutham Constructions v. The Joint Commissioner of Central Tax GST Appeals-II & Others Karnataka High Court, W.P. No. 30119/2026 Decision: 29 September 2026 Neutral citation: 2026: KHC:53993. The taxpayer challenged cancellation and the subsequent rejection of revocation and appeal. The original notice referred to rule 21(b). During revocation proceedings, the department disclosed an allegation of ineligible ITC of ₹8,87,014 from M/s. S M Traders, described as non-existent or fake. The taxpayer offered to produce documents establishing genuine transactions. But it had not preserved its alleged reply to the original notice or the acknowledgement, and it had not answered the later revocation-stage notice. The Court took both sides of the matter seriously. It declined to quash the original notice in the circumstances, observing that an objection to vagueness should have been raised diligently and supported by records. However, it set aside the cancellation and subsequent proceedings and reopened the matter for examination of the taxpayer’s evidence. Paragraph 8 stated that if the taxpayer produced documents demonstrating genuineness, registration must be restored. What does it support? It supports an opportunity to establish genuine transactions and proper consideration of the factual position. It does not finally allow the ITC, declare the supplier genuine, or hold that all suspensions without prior hearing are unlawful. For Karnataka taxpayers, it is the relevant local authority among these cases. The Delhi decisions provide persuasive support on independent decision-making and timely disposal. 7. Choose the authority that answers the defect Defect in the proceeding Most useful authority Relief or proposition supported Officer acts only on another authority’s letter Sant Ram Independent satisfaction and a reasoned registration decision Reply remains undecided while suspension continues Balaji Trading Co. Time-bound adjudication with personal hearing Registration proceedings remain unresolved and affect business Deepak Sharma Prompt disposal and effective hearing, as reported Genuine-transaction evidence requires examination in a cancellation dispute Goutham Constructions Reopening for factual examination, subject to proof These cases should not be presented as if all four quashed an enforcement suspension. They concern different procedural defects and granted different relief. 8. A supplier-related allegation must be connected to the purchaser The taxpayer’s strongest submission is not that action against a purchaser is legally impossible. Rule 21(e) permits cancellation for ITC availed in violation of section 16 or the applicable rules. A serious allegation concerning fictitious purchases can therefore have registration consequences. The proper questions are: Which invoices are disputed? What evidence suggests non-receipt? When was the supplier found non-existent? What period does that finding concern? What records contradict the purchaser’s explanation? Which cancellation ground is invoked against the purchaser? A registration certificate and bank payment alone should not be presented as conclusive. Prepare the commercial trail: ordering, supply, transportation where applicable, receipt, accounting, payment and use or onward supply. The argument should be: “Examine this evidence before deciding the allegation,” not “The department cannot examine our purchases.” 9. Seek revocation of suspension separately Rule 21A (4) permits the proper officer to revoke suspension during pending cancellation proceedings where considered fit. A taxpayer may therefore submit a specific request rather than wait passively for final adjudication. The application should explain: What material answers the allegation; Why continued suspension is unnecessary on those facts; The immediate impact on genuine business; The taxpayer’s willingness to cooperate; The request for an independent, prompt decision. Where relevant, state that removal of the suspension need not prevent lawful investigation or determination of disputed liability. The representation does not itself remove the restriction. Until status changes, the taxpayer must observe the applicable consequences of suspension under rule 21A. 10. Suggested grounds for a professional representation The grounds should be adapted to the actual record, not copied indiscriminately. Ground I: No independent consideration The communication and subsequent action do not disclose an independent examination of the statutory ground against the registered person. An enforcement recommendation may supply information, but cannot replace the proper officer’s exercise of discretion. Sant Ram supports this proposition. Ground II: Allegation cannot be answered without particulars The registered person requests identification of the disputed invoices, periods, alleged contravention and material relied upon. A complete response will be submitted upon receipt, without prejudice to the documents already enclosed. Ground III: Reply and evidence remain unexamined The reply dated, acknowledged under reference, addresses the allegation through the indexed documents. A decision is requested after consideration of those records and a personal hearing. Balaji Trading Co. supports seeking prompt adjudication rather than allowing the matter to remain unresolved. Ground IV: Suspension should be reconsidered In view of the documents now produced, revocation of suspension under rule 21A(4) is requested. The taxpayer remains available for lawful verification and seeks a reasoned decision on this request. Rule 21A provides the direct statutory basis. 11. Preserve what was filed, not merely what was drafted Goutham Constructions demonstrates the cost of a missing reply and acknowledgement. Maintain one proceeding file containing: Notice and annexures; Reply actually submitted; Supporting records; Acknowledgement or ARN; Hearing communications; Written submissions; Requests for documents; Orders and portal status; Chronology of follow-up. An unsigned draft on the accountant’s computer does not establish submission. Nor should a portal complaint be treated as a substitute for a reply. Where submission fails, preserve the error and promptly communicate the problem through an appropriate documented channel. Conclusion An enforcement investigation may be justified. A suspension may also be legally permissible before a hearing. Neither proposition authorises an officer to stop thinking once an enforcement recommendation arrives. Sant Ram requires independent satisfaction. Balaji Trading Co. supports timely determination of an unanswered proceeding. Goutham Constructions requires factual examination while reminding taxpayers to preserve their own record. For the taxpayer, the effective course is to identify the precise defect, produce a transaction-wise explanation, request revocation of suspension where justified and insist on a reasoned determination. For the officer, the duty is to examine that material—not merely repeat that another wing has advised action. An enforcement communication may justify opening the file. It should not, by itself, decide the fate of the business.
