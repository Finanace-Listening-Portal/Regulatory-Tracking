} } Listen to This ArticleAxis Bank, the third largest private sector lender, reported a 38 per cent Year-on-Year (YoY) rise in net profit to ₹7,003 crore for the second quarter of 2026-27 (Q2FY27), supported by healthy growth in net interest income (NII) and decline in provisions. The lender’s NII – the difference between interest earned and expended - grew 12 per cent YoY to ₹15,371 crore during the quarter. Net interest margin (NIM) stood at 3.36 per cent, compared with 3.55 per cent in the year-ago quarter. Excluding the impact of foreign currency non-resident (FCNR-B) deposits, NIM stood at 3.47 per cent. The bank reported FCNR-B deposits of ₹1.018 trillion, including deposits backed by advances from its international branches and standby letters of credit. The management of the bank said that it will use the funds to retire bulk deposits or use it drive growth in advances. “FCNR (B) deployment is a combination of either we substitute high cost deposits which come up for renewal or we use it to continue to drive advances growth. So, we will continue to be adaptive to the environment in terms of how we use this. In this quarter itself, we used some of our FCNR (B) to retire or substitute the high cost deposits. There are multiple levers, both on cost of funds as well as on the asset mix side,” said Subrat Mohanty, Executive Director, Axis Bank during the media call. Other income slipped 1 per cent YoY to ₹6,531 crore, as trading income fell sharply to ₹85 crore from ₹498 crore a year earlier. Annualised credit cost declined to 0.45 per cent, from the year-ago level, as asset-quality indicators improved. The gross non-performing asset (GNPA) ratio declined to 1.16 per cent as of September 30, 2026, from 1.28 per cent a quarter earlier. The net NPA ratio fell to 0.35 per cent from 0.39 per cent. Gross slippages declined to ₹4,762 crore during Q2FY27, compared with ₹5,696 crore in the year-ago quarter and ₹5,566 crore in the preceding quarter.
