Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Manav Utthan Seva Samiti Vs CIT (Exemption) (ITAT Surat) Wrong Clause Selection Cannot Defeat 80G Approval Claim: ITAT Surat Orders Fresh Examination The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Surat Bench, in Manav Utthan Seva Samiti v. Commissioner of Income Tax (Exemption), Ahmedabad, held that inadvertent selection of an incorrect clause or code in an application constitutes a procedural lapse which should not, by itself, defeat a substantive claim for registration or approval. By its common order dated 30 October 2026, the Tribunal restored the assessee’s applications concerning Section 80G(5) approval and Section 12A registration for fresh consideration. It also condoned a 633-day delay in filing the appeal against the earlier rejection of 80G approval. Background: Existing Approval Followed by Incorrect Clause Selection The assessee held approval under Section 80G(5) effective from 28 June 2016. Following the introduction of the revised approval regime effective from 1 April 2021, it submitted an application on 26 March 2022. However, while filing the application, the assessee mistakenly selected clause (iv) of the first proviso to Section 80G(5), instead of clause (i) applicable to an institution having pre-existing approval. Consequently, the Department granted provisional approval on 4 April 2022. Thereafter, the assessee submitted another application on 30 September 2023 under clause (iii), seeking renewal/permanent approval. The CIT(E) rejected this application on 20 March 2024, holding that the prescribed filing deadline had expired on 30 September 2022, considering commencement of activities from 1 April 2022. The provisional approval was also cancelled. Assessee’s Submissions The assessee explained that the initial selection of the wrong clause had carried forward into the subsequent approval proceedings. It submitted that the revised statutory framework contained several clauses governing different situations, resulting in confusion among taxpayers and professionals. The incorrect selection was inadvertent, conferred no benefit on the assessee, and represented a rectifiable procedural defect. The assessee requested an opportunity to correct the applicable clause and have its application examined on merits. It also pointed out that rejection arising from such confusion could affect donors who had made substantial donations. The Revenue opposed the request, arguing that the assessee itself had selected the incorrect clause and that the rejection order contained no error warranting interference. ITAT: Procedural Error Should Not Defeat Substantive Claim The Tribunal observed that the revised approval framework involved multiple clauses and sub-clauses concerning provisional, final and renewal approvals. Difficulties in understanding these provisions had resulted in inadvertent clause-selection errors in several cases. It referred to decisions including Subhadra Foundation, Rambha Charitable Trust, North Eastern Social Research Centre, and Balaji Gau Sarakshan Sansthan. According to the Tribunal, these decisions consistently recognised that an inadvertent selection of an incorrect clause or code, by itself, was a procedural lapse which should not defeat a substantive claim for registration or approval. Following these decisions, the ITAT set aside the rejection order and directed fresh adjudication. The CIT(E) was required to permit rectification of the applicable clause or code, if necessary, and examine the application on merits without being influenced by observations in the earlier order. The Tribunal also directed the authority to pass a speaking order after providing an opportunity of hearing. Delay of 633 Days Condoned The appeal against the order dated 20 March 2024 was filed with a delay of 633 days. In its affidavit, the assessee explained that it had misunderstood the distinction between Section 12A registration and Section 80G approval. It believed that filing a fresh application would cure the defect and subsequently approached counsel after that application was also rejected. The Tribunal accepted that sufficient cause had been established. Exercising its power under Section 253(5) and referring to the Supreme Court’s decision in Collector, Land Acquisition v. Mst. Katiji, it adopted a justice-oriented approach and admitted the appeal. Section 12A Rejection Also Restored In the separate appeal concerning Section 12A registration, the Tribunal found that rejection was primarily attributable to non-submission of the required details and documents. Only one opportunity, dated 23 February 2026, appeared to have been granted before the rejection order dated 8 March 2026. Considering the significant consequences of registration proceedings, the Tribunal directed fresh consideration after reasonable and adequate hearing opportunities. We find that the Legislature has introduced a new regime for granting different types of approvals in section 80G(5) through TOLA w.e.f. 01.04.2021. The provisions contain multiple clauses and sub-clauses governing different situations of ‘provisional’, ‘final’ and ‘renewal’ of approvals. The taxpayers have faced difficulties in understanding and, therefore, inadvertent errors in selecting the correct/applicable clause while filing applications are not uncommon. There are cases all over India being dealt by benches of ITAT concerning the issues of wrong selection of clause/code. We find that in the undermentioned cases, the Benches of ITAT have examined such situations and have provided appropriate relief to the assessees by adopting a pragmatic approach: i. ITAT, Pune in Subhadra Foundation Vs. The Commissioner of Income-tax Exemption, Pune, ITA No. 1744 & 1745/PUN/2025, dated 09.09.2025 ii. ITAT, Mumbai in Rambha Charitable Trust, Mumbai Vs. Commissioner of Income-tax, ITA No. 5111/Mum/2024, order dated 29.11.2024 iii. ITAT, Kolkata in North Eastern Social Research Centre Vs. CIT(Exemption), Kolkata, ITA No. 741/Kol/2024, order dated 09.07.2024 iv. ITAT, Indore in Balaji Gau Sarakshan Sansthan Vs. CIT(Exemption), Bhopal, ITA No. 887 & 890/Ind/2025 14. These decisions consistently hold that an inadvertent selection of an incorrect clause/code, by itself, constitutes a procedural lapse and should not defeat a substantive claim for registration or approval.
