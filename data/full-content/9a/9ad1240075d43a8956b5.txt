Summary: The non-availability of a recent utility bill for the proposed registered office does not necessarily prevent incorporation of a company in India. However, promoters must distinguish between the correspondence address furnished during incorporation and the registered office required under Section 12 of the Companies Act, 2013. Under the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 and the SPICe+ incorporation framework, promoters may consider alternative premises, use an appropriate correspondence address during incorporation, or obtain the required utility bill and supporting documents from the premises owner. Section 12(1) requires every company to have a registered office capable of receiving and acknowledging communications within 30 days of incorporation. Section 12(2) separately requires verification of the registered office to be furnished to the Registrar within the same period. Rule 25 prescribes supporting documents, including evidence of utility services such as electricity, telephone or gas, not older than two months. Importantly, the utility bill need not necessarily be issued in the proposed company’s name; a bill in the owner’s name may be acceptable when supported by the prescribed ownership, occupancy and authorization documents. Where the correspondence address is different from the registered office, MCA’s SPICe+ FAQs clarify that Form INC-22 must be filed within 30 days of incorporation. Promoters should ensure that the address is genuine, the premises are legally available for use, and the necessary verification documents are obtained before the applicable filing deadline. These precautions can help avoid incorporation resubmissions, registration delays and subsequent compliance issues. A recent utility bill is an important document for verifying a company’s registered office. However, promoters may face difficulties obtaining one, particularly where the proposed company intends to operate from newly acquired premises, rented property, family-owned premises or an address where utility connections have not yet been activated. The registered office requirements under the Companies Act, 2013 provide a framework for establishing and verifying the company’s official address. The following alternatives may be considered, depending on the circumstances. Option 1: Use Alternative Premises With Complete Documentation Promoters may identify another suitable premises where the required address proof, recent utility bill and authorization documents are available. For example, the company may use a promoter’s residential property, a family-owned property or rented commercial premises, provided the company is legally authorized to use that address as its registered office. The following documents should ordinarily be arranged, as applicable: Proof of ownership or occupancy of the premises. Authorization or no-objection certificate (NOC) from the owner or authorized occupant. Applicable lease or rent agreement and supporting rent documentation. Recent utility bill showing the address of the premises, not older than two months. The address must be genuine and capable of receiving and acknowledging official communications. Option 2: Use a Correspondence Address During Incorporation The SPICe+ framework distinguishes between the correspondence address furnished during incorporation and the company’s registered office. The MCA’s FAQs on Incorporation and Allied Matters, particularly FAQ No. 66, clarify that Form INC-22 is not required separately where the company is registered at the same address as its correspondence address. However, where the registered office address differs from the correspondence address, Form INC-22 must be filed within 30 days of incorporation to intimate the registered office particulars. Accordingly, where the proposed registered office is not ready or its documents are unavailable, promoters may consider furnishing a valid correspondence address during incorporation, subject to the applicable SPICe+ requirements. The correspondence address should be in the same State in which the company proposes to establish its registered office, in accordance with the MCA’s SPICe+ instruction kit. Important: Using a correspondence address does not permanently dispense with the registered office verification requirements. The company must establish its registered office and furnish the prescribed verification within the statutory period. Option 3: Obtain the Utility Bill From the Premises Owner Where promoters intend to use rented or family-owned premises, they may request the property owner to provide a recent electricity bill, telephone bill, gas bill or other acceptable evidence of utility services. Under Rule 25 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014, evidence of utility services may be furnished in the name of the owner or relevant document holder, as applicable. Therefore, the utility bill need not necessarily be issued in the name of the proposed company. However, promoters should ensure that: The utility bill is not older than two months. The address appearing on the bill corresponds to the proposed registered office. The owner or authorized occupant has permitted the company to use the premises. The relevant ownership, occupancy or rental documents are available. The documents are consistent with the particulars furnished in the incorporation or registered office verification forms. A utility bill in the owner’s name, supported by appropriate authorization and premises documentation, may therefore satisfy the applicable requirements. Registered Office Verification: Statutory Requirements and Deadlines Section 12 of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule 25 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 govern the establishment and verification of the registered office. The principal requirements are: Section 12(1): Every company must have a registered office capable of receiving and acknowledging communications and notices within 30 days of incorporation. Section 12(2): Verification of the registered office must be furnished to the Registrar within 30 days of incorporation in the prescribed manner. Rule 25: Prescribes the manner of verification and supporting documents, including recent utility-service evidence. Form INC-22: Used for registered office verification where a separate filing is required, including where the registered office differs from the correspondence address furnished during incorporation. Promoters should distinguish between the legal deadline for establishing and verifying the registered office and the documentary requirements applicable when that verification is filed. The availability of a correspondence-address facility does not constitute an exemption from the statutory registered office obligations. Can a Company Be Incorporated Without a Utility Bill? The answer depends on whether the company is declaring and verifying its registered office during incorporation or intends to establish and verify it subsequently. If the correspondence address is also declared as the registered office in SPICe+, the prescribed registered office supporting documents, including a recent utility bill, are generally required. If the registered office is to be established after incorporation, promoters may use the correspondence-address mechanism, subject to the applicable incorporation requirements, and subsequently complete registered office verification through Form INC-22 within the statutory deadline. Thus, the absence of a utility bill at the initial stage should not be confused with a permanent exemption from producing the required evidence. Conclusion The absence of a recent utility bill need not automatically prevent company incorporation. Promoters may consider alternative premises, obtain a recent utility bill from the premises owner, or use the correspondence-address facility available under the SPICe+ framework. However, these alternatives must be implemented in accordance with Section 12 of the Companies Act, 2013, Rule 25 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 and the applicable MCA filing requirements. The essential distinction is that a company may have flexibility in furnishing its registered office particulars during incorporation, but it cannot avoid the prescribed registered office verification requirements. Proper planning, valid premises documentation, owner authorization and timely filing of Form INC-22, wherever applicable, can help promoters complete incorporation and registered office compliance without unnecessary delays.
