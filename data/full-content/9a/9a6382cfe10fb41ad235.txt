Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Venkata Siddi Raju Subramanyam Raju Konduru Vs ACIT (ITAT Chennai) Summary: The Chennai Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal considered the assessee’s challenges to denial of exemption under section 10(37) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 on enhanced compensation received from the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI), and to denial of long-term capital gain treatment on sale of office premises at Raigad. The source header identifies the appeal as ITA No. 767/Chny/2026 for AY 2016-17, decided on 18.08.2026. The record states that the assessee had originally filed a return declaring total income of Rs.49,94,600/-. The original assessment accepted the assessee’s treatment of the office-premises gain as long-term capital gain and the claim under section 10(37) concerning enhanced compensation. The Principal Commissioner subsequently exercised revisionary jurisdiction under section 263 and directed a fresh assessment. In the consequential assessment, the Assessing Officer treated the office-premises gain as short-term capital gain and denied section 10(37) exemption on the ground that documentary evidence of agricultural operations and other prescribed conditions had not been established. The CIT(A) upheld those conclusions. On the section 10(37) issue, the assessee submitted that the amount of Rs.5,23,29,323/- received during AY 2016-17 was enhanced compensation arising from the same compulsory acquisition by NHAI for which original compensation had already been examined and accepted as exempt. The assessee relied on the earlier scrutiny proceedings, departmental enquiry, Inspector’s verification, Lok Adalat and revenue records, including Chitta and Adangal. The Department contended, among other things, that Rs.1,21,29,533/- related to survey numbers it characterised as vacant residential plots and that, for the remaining compensation of Rs.4,01,99,790/-, the Adangal allegedly showed only limited cultivation. The Tribunal examined the earlier assessment record and found that the Assessing Officer had conducted an enquiry through the departmental Inspector regarding the agricultural character of the land. It also considered the revenue records and the registered conveyance documents. The Tribunal accepted the assessee’s contention that the conveyance schedule described the relevant property as dry agricultural land and that the Department’s characterisation as residential plots was contrary to the documentary record. It further held that the Adangal records supported agricultural operations. A material part of the Tribunal’s reasoning concerned units of measurement. It found that the Revenue had treated Adangal figures expressed in hectares as though they were cents. On the corrected conversion placed before the Tribunal, the land owned by the assessee totalled 233 cents, land acquired by NHAI totalled 150 cents, and the cultivated extent reflected in the Adangal totalled approximately 211 cents. The Tribunal held that this reconciliation showed agricultural operations on the lands acquired by NHAI and supported the section 10(37) claim. It therefore deleted the denial of exemption of Rs.5,23,29,323/- and allowed grounds 2 to 6. On the long-term capital gain issue, the assessee contended that the transaction had already been disclosed in the return and that the only mistake was its classification as short-term rather than long-term capital gain. The Department relied on the formal registered conveyance dated 13.02.2015 and contended that ownership arose only then, while the assessee relied on rights acquired from 01.07.1995 under the Memorandum of Understanding and on possession and other evidence. The Tribunal found that the original Assessing Officer had examined acquisition, possession, ownership and period of holding and had accepted long-term capital gain treatment. The Tribunal held that the controversy was not about a fresh deduction, exemption or relief omitted from the return. The capital-gain transaction itself had been disclosed and the gain had been offered to tax; the assessee had merely sought correct characterisation of the disclosed transaction. It distinguished Goetze (India) Ltd. v. CIT, noting the limitation recognised in that decision on the Assessing Officer’s power to entertain a new claim without a revised return, and relied on the appellate authorities’ jurisdiction as discussed in the cited precedents. The Tribunal also considered the Madras High Court decision in CIT v. Perlo Telecommunication & Electronic Components India (P.) Ltd., TCA No.413 of 2014 dated 20.09.2021, and the principle stated there concerning the distinction between a fresh claim and a revised computation and the jurisdiction of appellate authorities. It also referred to CBDT Circular No.14 (XL-35) dated 11.04.1955. On the facts before it, the Tribunal held that the gains were assessable as long-term capital gains as originally accepted by the Assessing Officer, deleted the addition, set aside the CIT(A)’s order on this issue and allowed grounds 7 to 9. Accordingly, the Tribunal allowed the assessee’s appeal. Cases Discussed Goetze (India) Ltd. v. CIT, 284 ITR 323 (SC) National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. v. CIT, 229 ITR 383 (SC) CIT v. He vehemently argued that such correction of an inadvertent error cannot be equated with making a fresh claim so as to attract the ratio of Goetze (India) Ltd (supra). 19. CIT and argued that the Tribunal has jurisdiction to examine a question of law arising from the facts already on record, notwithstanding that such a claim was not raised before the lower authorities. 20. He prayed that since the factual foundation for treating the gain as Long-Term Capital Gain already exists on record and has never been disputed on merits by either the Assessing Officer in the original assessment or by the Principal Commissioner in the revisionary proceedings, the benefit of Long-Term Capital Gain treatment cannot be denied merely on a procedural objection based on the filing of a revised return. He refers to CBDT Circular No. 14 (XL-35) dated 11.04.1955, and submits that the Central Board of Revenue categorically instructed that officers of the Department must not take advantage of an assessee’s ignorance and are duty-bound to assist taxpayers in securing all legitimate reliefs and benefits available under law. He argued that the Circular specifically emphasizes that tax authorities should draw the attention of the assessee to any refund or relief to which he is lawfully entitled, even if such claim has not been properly made. 21. Heard both the parties and perused the material available on record. We find the Assessing Officer who completed the original assessment under section 143(3) of the Act at page 94 to 96, we note that the AO had examined the facts relating to the acquisition, possession, ownership and period of holding of the property and accepted the assessee’s contention that the gain was assessable as Long-Term Capital Gain. We find that neither the Principal Commissioner in the order passed under section 263, nor the Assessing Officer in the consequential assessment proceedings, nor even the learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) in the impugned order has disputed the fundamental fact that the office premises at Raigad constituted a long-term capital asset. We find no finding anywhere the revisionary order nor in the consequential assessment order, the quantum appellate order disputing the date of acquisition, the period of holding, the assessee’s ownership, possession, or the factual basis on which the original Assessing Officer accepted the gain as Long-Term Capital Gain. We find the action of the Assessing Officer in the original assessment proceedings in correctly assessing the gain as Long-Term Capital Gain was in consonance with the Circular No.14 dated 11.04.1955 (supra). 25. In this regard, we find the judgment of the Hon’ble Madras High Court in CIT v. Perlo Telecommunication & Electronic Components India (P.) Ltd. (TCA No.413 of 2014 dated 20.09.2021), wherein the Hon’ble High Court was pleased to hold after considering Goetze (India) Ltd., (supra) that the restriction contained therein is limited to the powers of the Assessing Officer and does not affect the jurisdiction of the appellate authorities to entertain and adjudicate a claim. The Hon’ble Court further upheld the Tribunal’s finding that where the assessee merely furnishes a revised computation during the course of assessment proceedings, such claim cannot automatically be treated as a fresh claim and that substantive relief cannot be denied on mere technicalities. 26. Further, on a perusal of the original assessment order under section 143(3) dated 03.05.2018 at pages 94-96 of paper book would clearly demonstrate that the Assessing Officer did not mechanically accept the assessee’s claim merely on the basis of a letter filed during the course of assessment proceedings. On the contrary, the claim was accepted only after a detailed verification of the facts relating to the acquisition, possession, ownership and period of holding of the property. We find during the course of the original assessment proceedings, the assessee furnished complete details relating to the acquisition of the office premises bearing Nos. D-105 and D-106 situated in Nandavan Complex, C & D Wings CHS Limited, Raigad, Panvel Taluk and District, Maharashtra and assessee explained and we find that a) The said premises had been booked and purchased in the year 1995 from M/s. Parjat Developers for a total consideration of Rs.4.49,600/-. b) The Assessing Officer examined the details relating to the payment of sale consideration and specifically recorded that the consideration had been paid by the assessee on 01.07.1995 through cheque No.014056. c) The assessee further established that possession of the premises had been obtained after the issuance of the completion certificate by the competent authority during the period 1996-97. d) The assessee had also furnished several documents evidencing continuous possession and enjoyment of the property over a prolonged period, including copies of electricity bill payments, municipal tax receipts issued by the Panvel Municipal Council for various years commencing from 2004-05 up to 2013-14, and share certificates issued by the building association, we find above said documents establishes that the assessee was in continuous possession, enjoyment and control of the property for several years prior to its eventual sale. 27. Further, we find the Assessing Officer recorded a categorical finding in the original assessment that the assessee had become the legal and beneficial owner of the property from 01.07.1995 itself notwithstanding the fact that formal registration had taken place subsequently. We find the Assessing Officer concluded that the asset had been held by the assessee for a period far exceeding the statutory period prescribed for a long-term capital asset. Therefore, only issue raised by the Revenue throughout has been whether the Assessing Officer could have accepted the assessee claim of Long-Term Capital Gain on the basis of a letter filed during the assessment proceedings without the filing of a revised return of income or not? 28. We find that the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Goetze (India) Ltd. v. CIT has no application to the facts of the present case, as the assessee is not seeking to raise a fresh claim for deduction, exemption, allowance or relief which was omitted from the return of income. We find the transaction giving rise to the capital gain was duly disclosed as short term in the return and the resultant gain was offered to tax. The assessee merely pointed out that the gain had been inadvertently classified as Short-Term Capital Gain instead of Long-Term Capital Gain and requested that the same be assessed under the correct head based on the facts already available on record. Therefore, we hold this is not a case of fresh claim but only a case of correct characterization of an already disclosed transaction and the said gains, in our opinion, are assessable as Long- Term Capital Gains, as originally accepted by the Assessing Officer in the assessment completed under section 143(3) of the Act dated 03.05.2018 and the addition is deleted. Thus, order of the ld.CIT(A) is set aside and ground nos. 7 to 9 raised by the assessee are allowed. 29. In the result, appeal filed by the assessee is allowed.
