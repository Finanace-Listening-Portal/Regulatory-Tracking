OEM brokers and motor insurance service providers generated about Rs29,000 crore of motor premiums in FY25 and received nearly Rs7,050 crore in commissions, highlighting the widening gap between premium growth and distribution payouts. Advt The motor insurance squeezeIRDAI has proposed that distribution entities receive no commission on third party motor insurance for new vehicles, while agents and associates could receive 2.5 per cent. For own damage, personal accident and legal liability covers on new vehicles, the proposed commission limits are 5 per cent for distribution entities and 10 per cent for agents and associates.The proposals are part of a wider discussion paper on recalibrating insurance distribution economics, with stakeholder feedback due by October 25. The regulator's stated objective is to link remuneration more closely to the complexity of products and the effort involved in selling them, while reducing insurance distribution costs.The scale of the proposed change is particularly significant for motor insurance, where distributor commissions have increased much faster than premiums. The overall average commission rate in motor insurance was about 24 per cent in FY25, while the reported commission range across channels was 13-50 per cent.Dealer economicsThe proposed caps could put pressure on the economics of insurance sold through automobile dealers and OEM linked distributors, which have become an important distribution channel for new vehicle policies. The sharp reduction in remuneration could force distributors to reassess how they sell and service motor insurance. Advt The issue is also significant because third party motor insurance is mandatory. IRDAI's proposal would therefore substantially reduce the commission available to distribution entities on a product that customers are required to purchase, rather than one for which distributors generate demand through discretionary sales.Brokers push backInsurance brokers have opened a broader challenge to the proposed commission overhaul, arguing that hard caps could damage distribution economics without guaranteeing that the savings will be passed on to policyholders. The Insurance Brokers Association of India has sought an impact assessment covering policyholders, employment, public sector insurers and foreign investment, and has asked for the existing framework to continue until its scheduled 2028 review. Advt The association has also warned that the proposed changes could put at least one million jobs at risk over five years, excluding salaried employees of insurers. It has sought an extension of the consultation deadline and raised concerns that tighter commission limits could encourage alternative forms of distributor remuneration. These are estimates and concerns raised by the brokers' association, rather than projections by IRDAI.
