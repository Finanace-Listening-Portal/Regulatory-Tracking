Banks could see further acceleration in net interest income growth from current levels, even if headline loan growth moderates as their portfolios gradually move away from corporate lending.The replacement of high-cost wholesale funding with lower-cost term deposits, including through the FCNR route, is expected to provide additional support to margins. Frontline private banks and SBI are relatively better placed to benefit because of their sensitivity to interest rate movements and the composition of their loan portfolios, Kotak Institutional Equities said in a report. Advt The brokerage expects banks to benefit from a more favourable loan mix as corporate lending gradually moderates. It said some of the recent demand for corporate credit appears to be driven by higher working-capital requirements as businesses face elongated cash-conversion cycles and inflation. A gradual shift away from corporate lending could support margins, even if it results in some moderation in headline loan growth.Funding costs will also be important in determining the extent of margin improvement. Banks are replacing expensive wholesale funding with lower-cost term deposits, while the rate cycle could provide an additional tailwind. However, the magnitude and sustainability of any NIM expansion will depend on the duration and intensity of the rate cycle, as well as the prevailing deposit and funding environment.Earnings growthBanks under the brokerage's coverage reported 20% year-on-year earnings growth in Q2FY27, supported by operating profit growth of around 15% and a 25% decline in provisions. Net interest income grew approximately 15%.Public sector banks recorded around 20% earnings growth, while private banks posted growth of about 25%. Net interest margin trends, however, were mixed. Margins remained stable or improved at public sector banks, while most private lenders reported a decline, partly due to the impact of FCNR deposits. Loan growth remained strong across both categories, while treasury income contributions declined sequentially. Advt The brokerage expects the asset-quality cycle to remain favourable in the near term, supported by lower slippages and benign credit costs. It sees limited near-term asset-quality risk in banks' gold-loan portfolios, particularly at larger lenders, whose customer mix is skewed towards larger-ticket borrowers with relatively lower risk profiles.Headwinds from fee income and operating expenses are also expected to be significantly lower for frontline banks than for their peers. This, along with the potential improvement in margins and favourable asset-quality outlook, supports the brokerage's preference for frontline private lenders and SBI. Advt Despite a sharp rally in frontline private banks and SBI over the past few weeks, the brokerage considers their valuations attractive. It said the recent re-rating followed a period of underperformance or absolute declines in share prices. It remains watchful for opportunities among mid-tier banks but believes their prospective return profiles make it harder to turn constructive. It would continue to avoid regional banks because of their higher exposure to gold loans and relatively expensive valuations.Among banks with a higher share of microfinance institution exposure in their loan portfolios, the brokerage favours Bandhan Bank.
