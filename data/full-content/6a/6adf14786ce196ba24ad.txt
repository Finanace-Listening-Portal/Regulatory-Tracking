Articles 3 min read Around 25,000 bank employees to strike on Dec 3 over proposed IDBI Bank sale to Canadian firm Fairfax, likely disrupting 3,700 branches and transactions worth Rs 1,200 crore in Gujarat On December 3, bank employees and officers are set to join IDBI Bank staff in a nationwide strike. The protest targets the potential sale of IDBI Bank to Fairfax Financial Holdings, a Canadian entity. Union representatives emphasize the importance of public sector banks in safeguarding citizens' savings. Despite government assurances about maintaining employee benefits post-sale, widespread apprehension remains among various stakeholders following prior demonstrations against the sale. Advt The All India Bank Employees’ Association (AIBEA), All India Bank Officers’ Association (AIBOA) and Bank Employees Federation of India (BEFI) have called for a solidarity strike in support of IDBI Bank employees and officers.The decision comes despite the government's assurance that the service conditions and pension benefits of existing employees will remain protected after the transaction.The All India Bank Employees' Association (AIBEA), All India Bank Officers Association (AIBOA) and Bank Employees Federation of India (BEFI) have called for a solidarity strike in support of IDBI Bank employees and officers protesting the proposed transfer of control to a foreign investor.The unions termed the proposed sale unwarranted, arguing that public sector banks play a crucial role in safeguarding people's savings. They said IDBI Bank has deposits of around Rs 3.5 lakh crore and advances of Rs 2.5 lakh crore, taking its total business to approximately Rs 6 lakh crore.The December 3 strike comes after IDBI Bank employees and officers staged a two-day protest on November 2 and 3. The unions said the government had continued with the proposed sale despite the earlier agitation.The Union Cabinet approved the sale of a 60.72 per cent stake in IDBI Bank to a subsidiary of Fairfax Financial Holdings on October 14 for Rs 52,878 crore. Advt As part of the proposed transaction, the government will divest its 30.48 per cent stake in the bank, while the Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) will sell its 30.24 per cent holding. Following the transaction, the government's stake will come down to 15 per cent and LIC's shareholding to 19 per cent.However, DIPAM Secretary Arunish Chawla said in October that the share purchase agreement would safeguard the service conditions of existing employees and their pension benefits. He also said a separate pension fund had been set up for existing pensioners and would be fully maintained."Existing employees and existing pensioners should have no apprehensions, as their service conditions have been fully protected," Chawla had said. Advt The proposed disinvestment has also faced criticism from Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha Rahul Gandhi, who warned that privatisation could end reservation protections for SC, ST and OBC candidates. He also questioned the move despite IDBI Bank reporting a profit of over Rs 9,000 crore last year.The unions have also flagged concerns over the banking sector's handling of bad loans. They alleged that private corporate borrowers were responsible for large non-performing loans and that banks had incurred losses through loan write-offs.(With Agency Inputs) By Anjali Singh , TIMESOFINDIA.COM Published On Nov 24, 2026 at 03:50 PM IST Telegram Facebook Copy Link Comments What are your thoughts?
