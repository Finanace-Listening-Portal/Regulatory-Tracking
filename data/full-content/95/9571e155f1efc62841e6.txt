The sharp correction in gold prices, which has reduced collateral coverage on loans originated at higher valuations, could add to the pressure if borrowers fail to meet repayment obligations or restore prescribed loan-to-value (LTV) ratios. Advt Portfolio at risk (PAR) in the 31-90 days overdue category among gold-loan-focused NBFCs increased to 1.6 per cent in September 2026 from 1 per cent a year earlier, according to credit bureau CRIF India. The corresponding ratio for private banks rose to 2.2 per cent from 1.1 per cent, while other NBFCs recorded a sharper increase to 3.9 per cent from 1.2 per cent.However, the deterioration is not uniform across the sector. Public sector banks reported an improvement, with PAR in the 31-90 days category declining to 0.6 per cent from 1.2 per cent a year earlier. Overall gold loan PAR in the 31-180 days category also declined to 1.7 per cent in September from 2 per cent a year earlier, although the 31-90 days category increased sequentially to 1.3 per cent from 0.9 per cent in June.The emerging stress comes against the backdrop of rapid expansion in gold-backed lending. Outstanding gold loans grew 60.6 per cent year on year to Rs 23.3 lakh crore in September 2026, accounting for 12.6 per cent of total retail credit. Gold-loan-focused NBFCs have also expanded their share of outstanding gold loan portfolios to 16.9 per cent from 7.6 per cent two years earlier.The correction in bullion prices has added to the vulnerability. Gold prices fell more than 32 per cent from their January 2026 peak of around Rs 1.8 lakh per 10 grams to approximately Rs 1.2 lakh on November 20. The decline has pushed LTV ratios higher on loans originated when collateral valuations were significantly elevated, prompting lenders to seek additional collateral and part repayments from borrowers. Advt Company disclosures have also highlighted an increase in loans showing significant credit deterioration. Muthoot Finance reported Stage 2 loan assets of Rs 23,467 crore in September 2026, compared with Rs 534 crore a year earlier. Its Stage 3 loan assets increased to Rs 7,267 crore from Rs 2,977 crore over the same period. These figures cover the company's overall loan assets and are not confined to its gold loan portfolio.At Manappuram Finance, Stage 2 assets increased to around 5 per cent of its gold loan portfolio in the September quarter from approximately 0.5 per cent in the previous quarter. However, its gold loan gross non-performing asset ratio stood at 1.04 per cent, while the average LTV ratio remained at 64.5 per cent, indicating that the increase in early-stage stress has not yet translated into a comparable deterioration in recognised bad loans.Emerging vulnerabilities Advt Borrower-level indicators also point to emerging vulnerabilities. The share of very high-risk borrowers in the overall gold loan portfolio increased to 16.7 per cent in September 2026 from 14.8 per cent in September 2024. Among borrowers with loans of up to Rs 2.5 lakh, the proportion increased to 18.2 per cent from 15.7 per cent. Loans of up to Rs 1 lakh recorded the sharpest sequential deterioration in early delinquencies, with PAR in the 31-90 days category rising to 1.9 per cent in September from 1.2 per cent in June.The Reserve Bank of India has increased its monitoring of gold loan portfolios following the sharp correction in bullion prices. Large NBFCs have been asked to provide portfolio information at a higher frequency than the earlier monthly assessment, as the central bank tracks movements in LTV ratios and emerging repayment stress. Advt The RBI's revised gold and silver collateral lending framework, effective April 1, 2026, requires lenders to maintain prescribed LTV ratios throughout the loan tenure. The maximum LTV for consumption loans is 85 per cent for loans up to Rs 2.5 lakh, 80 per cent for loans above Rs 2.5 lakh and up to Rs 5 lakh, and 75 per cent for loans exceeding Rs 5 lakh. The framework also permits bullet repayment loans with a maximum tenure of 12 months.Lenders have responded by stepping up collections, seeking additional collateral and encouraging borrowers to make part repayments. Some have also tightened fresh lending and reduced the LTVs offered on new loans to limit exposure to further declines in gold prices.The December quarter will therefore be important in determining whether the increase in early delinquencies remains contained or translates into higher slippages, provisioning requirements and collateral auctions. A sustained recovery in gold prices could ease pressure on collateral coverage, but further declines could leave lenders with fewer options to recover outstanding dues from borrowers unable to provide additional security.
