(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal filed in the dispute concerning ₹1,58,59,294 in margin money maintained by Diamond Power and Infrastructure Ltd. for bank guarantees issued by Indian Overseas Bank. The supplied Supreme Court order records dismissal of the appeal in terms of a signed order and disposal of pending applications, without setting out substantive reasons. The underlying NCLAT proceedings concerned whether the bank could appropriate earmarked term deposits after commencement of the corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP), where the guarantees had already been invoked before the moratorium commenced. Read NCLAT Delhi Order: NCLAT Holds Pre-CIRP Bank Guarantee Invocation Valid Despite Post-Moratorium Margin Money Adjustment The guarantees were invoked between 21 April and 23 May 2018, before CIRP commenced on 24 August 2018. The bank recorded appropriation on 27 September 2018 and submitted a revised claim of ₹51,54,93,366 after adjusting the margin money. The successful resolution applicant, Consortium of GSEC Limited and Rakesh Shah, subsequently challenged the adjustment. On 24 April 2024, NCLT Ahmedabad directed the bank to reverse ₹1,58,59,294. The bank appealed under Section 61 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. The bank contended that the deposits were earmarked for beneficiaries and acquired the character of trust property upon invocation of the guarantees. The successful resolution applicant argued that appropriation after commencement of CIRP violated the Section 14 moratorium and the approved resolution plan. NCLAT examined Sections 3(31), 14 and 18 of the IBC, Section 126 of the Indian Contract Act and precedents addressing margin money and bank guarantees. It held that the guarantees had been invoked before CIRP, the corresponding margin money was no longer an asset of the corporate debtor, and the subsequent accounting adjustment did not constitute enforcement of security interest prohibited by the moratorium. It distinguished cases involving deposits remaining with banks after CIRP and found that the revised claim had been disclosed to the resolution professional and reflected in the information memorandum. NCLAT further held that ordering reversal after approval of the resolution plan would impermissibly disturb its settled terms. It allowed the bank’s appeal and set aside the reversal direction.
