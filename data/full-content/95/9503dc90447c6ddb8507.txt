Vs Joint Director (Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA, New Delhi) Third-Party Loose Sheets Fail to Prove Rs. 4.08 Crore FEMA Charge; Tribunal Reduces Penalties Summary: The Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA at New Delhi rejected a FEMA contravention allegation involving Rs. 4.08 crore against Shukla Forex Pvt. Ltd. and its Managing Director, finding that third-party loose sheets and an uncorroborated statement were insufficient to establish the charge. However, it upheld separate contraventions involving foreign transfers and unauthorised foreign currency purchases. Two other directors were exonerated because their involvement in managing the company or the contraventions was not established. The decision was delivered on October 8, 2026, in M/s. Shukla Forex Pvt. Ltd. & Others v. Joint Director, Directorate of Enforcement, Jalandhar, Appeal Nos. FPA-FE-71 to 74/JL/2015. The Bench comprised Members Shri Balesh Kumar and Shri Rajesh Malhotra. Background of the proceedings The appeals challenged an adjudication order dated September 21, 2015, imposing penalties under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA). The Enforcement Directorate’s investigation followed searches at the premises of persons allegedly engaged in hawala transactions. Documents recovered from Jeevan Kumar alias Happy contained entries referring to “Shukla”. ED linked those entries to Mohinder Pal Shukla, Managing Director of Shukla Forex Pvt. Ltd., an RBI-licensed money-changing business. Searches were subsequently conducted at the appellants’ premises on December 3, 2013. The allegations under Section 3(d) involved transactions totalling Rs. 4,51,46,775. Of this, Rs. 4,08,01,515 was calculated using documents recovered from Jeevan Kumar’s premises, while Rs. 43,45,260 related to documents recovered from the appellants’ own premises. A separate charge concerned unauthorised foreign currency purchases of Rs. 1,53,74,072.10 under Section 10(4), read with Section 42. Third-party documents insufficient to establish charge The appellants disputed the identification of the name “Shukla” appearing in the third-party records. They argued that the entries contained no adequate particulars linking them to Mohinder Pal Shukla. ED relied on the seized records and statements attributed to the Managing Director acknowledging transactions through Jeevan Kumar. Examining Section 39, which concerns presumptions relating to documents, the Tribunal observed that Jeevan Kumar was neither a co-noticee nor shown to be facing proceedings in the matter. It held that the loose sheets recovered from his premises could not be relied upon through that provision to impose penalties on the appellants for the alleged Rs. 4,08,01,515 contravention. The Tribunal also found that the surname alone, without an address or other identifying details, did not establish that the entries referred to the appellant. It concluded that the Managing Director’s solitary statement, without corroborative evidence, was insufficient to establish the alleged transactions. Accordingly, the charge concerning Rs. 4,08,01,515 was not sustained. Foreign transfer charge of Rs. 43.45 lakh upheld The Tribunal reached a different conclusion concerning entries recovered from the appellants’ own business premises. These documents reflected telegraphic transfers involving foreign bank accounts during September and November 2013, aggregating to Rs. 43,45,260. The appellants claimed that customers had merely been referred to HDFC Bank and that commission was received when transactions materialised. However, they produced no supporting documents correlating the seized entries with transfers processed through the bank. The Tribunal held that a general explanation about referrals could not rebut the specific records. It therefore upheld the contravention under Section 3(d), read with Section 42, against the company and its Managing Director. Unauthorised currency purchases and confiscation sustained ED also identified discrepancies between seized transaction records, reported purchases and the foreign currency stock found during the search. The Tribunal noted that the appellants failed to demonstrate any error in ED’s calculations through documentary evidence. It upheld the finding of unauthorised purchases totalling Rs. 1,53,74,072.10 under Section 10(4), read with Section 42. Confiscation of the foreign currency actually seized, valued at Rs. 28,43,322, was also confirmed. Two directors exonerated Amit Shukla and Raman Bala challenged their liability, submitting that they did not manage the company’s day-to-day affairs. The Tribunal found that ED had not controverted their claimed lack of involvement. It also failed to prove that the contraventions occurred with their consent or connivance, or were attributable to their neglect. The Bench held that their positions were nominal and that the business was actively managed by Mohinder Pal Shukla. Both directors were consequently exonerated under Sections 3(d) and 10(4), read with Section 42. Penalties substantially reduced Considering the reduced quantum of the Section 3(d) contravention and the confirmed confiscation, the Tribunal reduced the company’s penalties to Rs. 4.50 lakh under Section 3(d) and Rs. 3 lakh under Section 10(4). The Managing Director’s penalties were reduced to Rs. 1 lakh under each provision, read with Section 42. The company’s and Managing Director’s appeals were partly allowed, while the other two directors’ appeals were allowed. Any pre-deposit was directed to be adjusted against the reduced penalties. On the basis of above rival contentions, we considered the same. There are rough calculations done by the officers based on the loose sheets found from the premises of Sh. Jeevan Kumar @ Happy, in assessing the unauthorized amount for sum of Rs. 4,08,01,515/- alleged to be changed by appellant no.1. Therefore, the issue arises as to whether the said amount can be considered for assessing the amount of contravention against the appellants. The relevant Section pertaining to presumption of documents is Section 39 of FEMA, which is reproduced below: “39. Presumption as to documents in certain cases.— Where any document— (i) is produced or furnished by any person or has been seized from the custody or control of any person, in either case, under this Act or under any other law; or (ii) has been received from any place outside India (duly authenticated by such authority or person and in such manner as may be prescribed) in the course of investigation of any contravention under this Act alleged to have been committed by any person, and such document is tendered in any proceeding under this Act in evidence against him, or against him and any other person who is proceeded against jointly with him, the court or the Adjudicating Authority, as the case may be, shall— (a) presume, unless the contrary is proved, that the signature and every other part of such document which purports to be in the handwriting of any particular person or which the court may reasonably assume to have been signed by, or to be in the handwriting of, any particular person, is in that person’s handwriting, and in the case of a document executed or attested, that it was executed or attested by the person by whom it purports to have been so executed or attested; (b) admit the document in evidence notwithstanding that it is not duly stamped, if such document is otherwise admissible in evidence; (c) in a case falling under clause (i), also presume, unless the contrary is proved, the truth of the contents of such document.” Thus, as per second part of Section 39 of FEMA, the loose sheets recovered from premises of Jeevan Ram @ Happy and the amount of contravention calculated on the basis of those sheets to the extent of Rs. 4,08,01,515/- cannot be taken into consideration for imposition of penalty against the appellants, as Sh. Jeevan Ram @ Happy is neither a co-noticee, nor any proceedings are stated to be initiated against him, till date. Further, we are of the view that merely the name “Shukla” (without any address or other details) mentioned in the loose sheets recovered from the premises of Jeevan Ram @ Happy does not give any presumption as to the fact that the same belongs to the appellant no. 2, Mohinder Shukla, as there may be large number of Shuklas in various districts of Punjab. Thus, we are of the considered view that the solitary statement of the appellant Mohinder Pal Shukla in absence of any corroborative evidence cannot be considered to arrive at conclusion that he purchased foreign currency to the extent Rs.4,08,01,515/- from Jeevan @ Happy to arrange foreign currency equivalents in the accounts of Exporters/Importers in India by way of Telegraphic Transfers (TTs). Therefore, the charge w.r.t. the value of Rs. 4,08,01,515/- cannot be substantiated and the penalty cannot be imposed on this value. Issue no.1 is accordingly decided in favour of appellants. After hearing the rival submissions of both the parties, we reproduce the Section 10(4) of FEMA, 1999 herein below: “10. Authorised person.— ———————- 4) An authorised person shall, in all his dealings in foreign exchange or foreign security, comply with such general or special directions or orders as the Reserve Bank may, from time to time, think fit to give, and, except with the previous permission of the Reserve Bank, an authorised person shall not engage in any transaction involving any foreign exchange or foreign security which is not in conformity with the terms of his authorisation under this section. —————–” We are of the view that while replying with regard to the currencies recovered & seized during the course of the search of the M/s Shukla Forex Pvt. Ltd. as detailed in Table-F mentioned in the complaint, the Ld. counsel for appellant has made submissions that the difference in foreign currencies were due to the reason that currencies were purchased after the search was concluded by the department which resulted in increase in closing stock and duly reflected in FLM-1 for 03.12.2013. We find from record that the search of the business premises of M/s Shukla Forex Pvt. Ltd. commenced on 03.12.2013 at 04:15 pm and was concluded as late as 08:35 pm. Thereafter, the appellant no.2, Mohinder Pal Shukla also made a statement on 03.12.2013 itself. In view of the fact above, the submission of the appellant falls highly unlikely that after the search, the customer would come with foreign currencies at that late hour for purpose of exchange. This is also fortified with the fact that the INR 23,00,000/- found in his business premises was already seized by the ED and thus the appellant company was not in position to purchase the foreign currency. Moreover, Mohinder Pal Shukla on 02.12.2014, stated that the record pertaining to purchases of foreign currencies made by M/s Shukla Forex Pvt. Ltd. was seized by the Officers of Enforcement Directorate and thereafter no foreign currency was purchased. Therefore, it is established beyond doubt that the appellant company being authorized dealer in Foreign Exchange had failed to comply with general or special directions of the Reserve Bank and the seized Foreign Currency was purchased in contravention the Provisions of section 10(4) read with section 42 of FEMA, 1999 as brought out in the complaint. Ld. Counsel for the appellants failed to point out any irregularity in calculations made by the ED for not reflecting the transactions to the extent of Rs. 1,53,74,072.10, as detailed in para no. 12 above, by way of any documentary evidence. However, encashment certificates seized from his premises on 03.12.2013 and stock position of the foreign currencies as brought out in complaint as well as the statement of the appellant at Sr. No. 2 that he did not purchase any Foreign Currency on that day after the search, clearly indicated that the seized foreign currencies were purchased in violation of the guidelines. The charge of the contravention of Section 10(4) read with section 42 of FEMA, 1999 as brought out in the complaint against the appellants at S.No.1 & 2 for unauthorized purchase of foreign currencies to the tune of Rs. 1,53,74,072.10 is therefore established. We are satisfied with the detailed findings qua this issue made by the Ld. Adjudicating Authority in para (b) of the findings at internal page no. 28 & 29, in absence of any contrary evidence. Thus, this issue is decided against the appellant no. 1 & 2 and in favour of the respondent ED. Issue no.4 14. The Ld. counsel for the appellants submitted that Smt. Raman Bala (wife) & Amit Shukla (Son) whose statements were not recorded and who are not involved in day-to-day affairs of Shukla Forex. He stated that the other Directors namely, Smt. Raman Bala w/o Sh. Mahendra Pal Shukla and Amit Shukla S/o Mahendra Pal Shukla were not looking after the affairs of the said Company. Raman Bala is fifth class fail and is a housewife and she has no knowledge about the affairs of M/s Shukla Forex, as the affairs of the said Company were managed by her husband Mahendra Pal Shukla. Amit Shukla is tenth class fail and never looked after the day-to-day affairs of the M/s Shukla Forex and was only looking after the miscellaneous works of the company. 16. On the other hand, the Ld. Counsel for the respondent ED submitted that the appellant no. 3 & 4 are also liable for the contravention of the aforementioned provisions on account of statutory liability under Section 42 of FEMA,1999 being the directors of the company. He contended that they cannot claim any immunity from the contravention by taking the plea that they were not managing the affairs of the company. 17. We considered the rival submissions. Section 42 of FEMA is reproduced as under: “42. Contravention by companies.—(1) Where a person committing a contravention of any of the provisions of this Act or of any rule, direction or order made thereunder is a company, every person who, at the time the contravention was committed, was in- charge of, and was responsible to, the company for the conduct of the business of the company as well as the company, shall be deemed to be guilty of the contravention and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly: Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall render any such person liable to punishment if he proves that the contravention took place without his knowledge or that he exercised due diligence to prevent such contravention. (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1), where a contravention of any of the provisions of this Act or of any rule, direction or order made thereunder has been committed by a company and it is proved that the contravention has taken place with the consent or connivance of, or is attributable to any neglect on the part of, any director, manager, secretary or other officer of the company, such director, manager, secretary or other officer shall also be deemed to be guilty of the contravention and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly. Explanation.—For the purposes of this section— (i) “company” means any body corporate and includes a firm or other association of individuals; and (ii) “director”, in relation to a firm, means a partner in the firm.” The respondent ED during investigation failed to controvert the fact that at the relevant time appellant no. 3 Amit Shukla was tenth class fail and Appellant no. 4, Smt. Raman Bala was a housewife & an uneducated lady, or they were not managing the affairs of M/s Shukla Forex Pvt. Ltd., as stressed by the appellants. Thus, as per proviso to Section 42(1) of FEMA, they are not liable for contravention, as alleged. Section 42(2) is also not applicable against the applicants as the respondent ED failed to prove that the contravention has taken place with the consent or connivance of, or is attributable to any neglect on the part of, the appellants being directors. Hence, we are of the view that the charge against the appellants at S. No. 3 & 4, is not sustainable as they did not look after the affairs of the company. Thus, the appellant no 3 & 4 are entitled to be exonerated of the charges u/s Section 3 (d) as well as Section 10(4) r/w Section 42 of FEMA, 1999, given the fact that they were holding that position for the namesake and had no active role to play in the day-to-day business activities of the said company M/s Shukla Forex, rather it was solely and actively run by the appellant no. 2 since they were not active in the management of the company. Accordingly, issue no. 4 is decided in favour of appellant no. 3 & 4 and against the respondent ED. Conclusion: 18.
