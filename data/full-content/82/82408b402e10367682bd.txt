Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Elite Buildcon Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner (CESTAT New Delhi) Summary: Summary: The Karnataka High Court in S J Gupta And Co. Vs Assistant Commissioner of Commercial Taxes considered a writ petition challenging the rejection of a GST appeal through Form GST APL-02 dated 18 July 2026 on the ground that the petitioner had failed to furnish a certified copy of the adjudication order. The petitioner, represented by advocate M. N. Shankare Gowda, submitted that the appeal had been filed electronically in Form GST APL-01 through the GST portal. It was contended that when an appeal is filed online, the system automatically populates the impugned adjudication order based on the reference number entered by the appellant. Consequently, there was no requirement to separately furnish a certified copy of the order. The petitioner argued that rejection of the appeal solely for non-receipt of a certified copy was contrary to the amended procedural framework governing electronic GST appeals. The petitioner specifically relied upon the substituted Rule 108(3) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017, which came into effect on 26 December 2022. According to the petitioner, the first proviso to the amended rule requires submission of a self-certified copy of the impugned order only where the order is not automatically populated on the common portal at the time of filing the appeal. Where the adjudication order is already available electronically through the reference number, insisting upon a separate certified copy would defeat the purpose of the amended provision. The petitioner therefore maintained that the appellate authority had wrongly rejected the appeal despite the adjudication order being automatically populated during electronic filing. Mr. K. Hema Kumar, the learned Additional Government Advocate appearing for the respondents, did not dispute the legal proposition advanced on behalf of the petitioner. The Karnataka High Court allowed the writ petition, set aside the rejection of the GST appeal and restored the appellate proceedings. The Court observed that the appeal could not have been rejected merely on the ground of non-receipt of a certified copy of the adjudication order, particularly when the petitioner asserted that the order had been automatically populated on the GST portal during online filing. Accordingly, the Court directed the petitioner to appear before the Appellate Authority on 30 September 2026, without awaiting any further notice. As a precautionary procedural requirement, the petitioner was also directed to submit a self-certified copy of the impugned adjudication order on the first date of appearance. The ruling underscores that the amended Rule 108(3) must be applied while examining electronic GST appeals and that an appeal should not be rejected solely for non-submission of a certified copy where the impugned order is automatically available through the portal. Appellant as such do not provide any maintenance services and reflect the amount so collected as liability in their books of accounts. 4.4 This issue has been considered time and again by the Tribunal holding that service tax would not be leviable on such charges reference is made to the decision of Commissioner (Appeals) in appellant‘s own case for the period wherein as has been held: “7. I observe that the OIO has come to conclusion that the appellant having collected an amount from the prospective flat buyers towards management, maintenance or repair service, tax liability would arise; that the maintenance charges recovered from the prospective purchasers of flat in residential building as well’ as commercial building were liable to be taxed as the definition does not make any distinction on the basis whether the service has been rendered to the purchasers or owners of the residential units or commercial units. I find that the appellants are engaged in providing service of Construction of Complex Service and not in the business of maintenance or repair service, or management of immovable property. The appellants cannot be held as provider of maintenance or repair service as they are not charging anything on their own and amount so received available in their account current liabilities. In view of the factual findings and the issue being covered by the decision of hon’ble Tribunal, I hold that no service tax liability arise on the appellant under the category ‘Management, Maintenance or Repair Service’ for the amounts collected by them from the prospective flat owners.‖ 4.5 In case of KDP Infrastructure (2019 (22) GSTL 450(Tri.-All.) as follows: “3. The two issues are required to be decided in the present appeal. The first issue relates to inclusion of the amount collected by the appellant as IFMS. Revenue‘s contention is that the said collected amount would fall under the category of Management Maintenance and Repair Services‘ and would be liable to service tax separately. We note that the said amount collected by the appellant from the flat owners is towards the security for the purpose of maintenance of the building and to cover the eventual default made by any of the flat owners for payment of monthly maintenance charges. As per the agreement with the flat owners, the said amount is liable to be refunded to them within the period of Six months from the date of termination of the said agreement. The Adjudicating Authority observed that the genuineness of the said term is very much doubted inasmuch as the appellant had not produced any evidence to show that the said IFMS was ever refunded to anyone. We really fail to understand the said reasoning of the Adjudicating Authority. The amount is refundable in case of termination of the ownership agreement and if no such termination has taken place till date, the amount would not be refunded. As long as the provisions for refund of the said amount in the agreement itself is there, it has to be considered that the said amount is refundable and was towards security deposits and was not for the purpose of providing any services, so as to levy tax on the same. 4.
