Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Sanjeev Kumar Singh Vs Additional Commissioner (Orissa High Court) Summary: The Orissa High Court, in Sanjeev Kumar Singh Vs Additional Commissioner, CGST & Central Excise, Rourkela, Sundargarh & Ors., granted interim protection to the director of M/s Shri Ganapati Ores & Ispat Private Limited in a writ petition challenging a common show cause notice, a GST adjudication order dated 29 January 2025 and a recovery notice dated 19 March 2026 relating to financial years 2017-18 and 2018-19. The petitioner challenged the imposition of a substantial penalty under Section 122(1A) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, contending that the provision, which came into force on 1 January 2021, had been applied retrospectively to earlier financial years. The petition described the penalty as ₹24,13,54,968, whereas the subsequent account of the adjudication order recorded ₹24,31,54,968. The petitioner submitted that the common show cause notice had been issued to eleven noticees, comprising three distinct corporate entities and eight individuals, on allegations of wrongful availment of input tax credit through invoices issued by suppliers alleged to be non-existent. According to the petitioner, the proceedings improperly clubbed separate legal entities and individuals into a composite demand for tax, interest and penalties under Section 74 of the CGST Act. It was further contended that the adjudicating authority confirmed the demands without separately determining the liability of each noticee for each financial year and without uploading an individual Form GST DRC-07 under Rule 142. The Department’s Senior Standing Counsel sought time to obtain instructions. Without finally deciding the validity of the show cause notice, the retrospective applicability of Section 122(1A), or the legality of the composite demand, the Division Bench comprising Dr. Justice Sanjeeb K. Panigrahi and Justice Murahari Sri Raman directed that the adjudication order dated 29 January 2025 and the recovery order dated 19 March 2026 remain in abeyance until the next listing on 18 November 2026. The order is therefore an interim protective direction and not a final adjudication quashing the penalty. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ORISSA HIGH COURT 1. This matter is taken up through Hybrid Arrangement (video conferencing/physical mode). 2. The Petitioner, the director of M/s. Shri Ganapati Ores & Ispat Private Limited, has filed the instant Writ Petition assailing the common show cause notice (SCN) dated 08.07.2021, the common Order-in-Original (OIO) dated 29.01.2025, which demands a penalty of Rs.24,13,54,968/- retrospectively under Section 122(1A) of the GST Act, which came into force with effect from 01.01.2021, and the common recovery notice dated 19.03.2026, in respect of the period 2017-18 and 2018-19, as arbitrary, illegal and without jurisdiction. 3. Heard learned counsel for the parties. 4. Learned counsel for the Petitioner submits that Opposite Party No.2 issued the impugned common show cause notice (SCN) bearing No.07/2021-22 dated 08.07.2020 to a total of eleven noticees, including the Petitioner, alleging wrongful availment of input tax credit (ITC) on the strength of invoices issued by certain suppliers alleged to be non-existent. He further contends that the SCN impermissibly clubbed three distinct corporate entities and eight separate individuals, including the Petitioner and proposed a composite demand for tax, interest, and penalties for the financial years 2017-18 and 2018-19 under Section 74 of the CGST Act. 5. Learned counsel further contends that the Petitioner, along with the other noticees, duly contested the SCN. However, Opposite Party No.1 proceeded to pass the impugned common Order-in-Original (OIO) No.102/ADC-JC/GST/RKL/2024-25 dated 29.01.2025, confirming the demands on a composite basis without segregating or specifically quantifying the liability of each individual noticee for each financial year, and without uploading an individual DRC-07 under Rule 142 of the Rules. The OIO imposed a penalty of Rs.24,31,54,968/- on the Petitioner under Section 122(1A) of the CGST Act. 6. At this juncture, Mr. B. N. Prusty, learned Senior Standing Counsel appearing for the Department-Opposite Parties, seeks some time to obtain instructions in the matter. 7. List this matter on 18 November 2026. I.A. No. 21418 of 2026 8. Heard. 9. As an interim measure, it is directed that the order dated 29.01.2025 passed by the Additional Commissioner, GST & Central Excise Commissionerate, Rourkela (Opposite Party No.1) under Annexure-2 and the order dated 19.03.2026 passed by the Assistant Commissioner, GST & Central Excise, Rourkela-I Division (Opposite Party No.3) under Annexure-3, shall remain in abeyance till next date of listing of this matter. 10. Issue urgent certified copy of this order as per the Rules.
