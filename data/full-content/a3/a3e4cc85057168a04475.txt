Articles 6 min read Jimmy Tata accuses Noel of seeking 'back-door takeover' of Tata Sons, callsfor listing Jimmy N Tata has raised concerns over Noel Tata's attempts to consolidate family control within Tata Trusts. He believes this goes against the intentions of the trust's founders, who aimed to benefit the public. Disagreements have emerged regarding the governance of Tata Trusts and the reappointment of N Chandrasekaran as Tata Sons chairman. Jimmy has called for transparency through public listing and opposes any restructuring to evade regulations. PTI Published On Nov 17, 2026 at 07:59 AM IST var news_msid = 135907756; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook New Delhi, Jimmy N Tata, younger half-brother of the late Ratan Tata and a trustee of the Sir Ratan Tata Trust, has accused Tata Trusts Chairman Noel Tata of seeking to consolidate family control over the charitable institutions and, through them, Tata Sons, describing the developments as a potential "back-door takeover" of the conglomerate's holding company.The 85-year-old conveyed his views during a documented conversation with a fellow Tata Trusts trustee, who subsequently briefed other trustees, people with knowledge of the matter said.Jimmy, according to the people, said the induction of multiple members of the chairman's family into the trusts was inconsistent with the intentions of their founders, who had established the institutions to serve the public rather than function as private family vehicles. Advt "What has transpired in recent times is that the trusts are being filled with family members of the present Tata Trusts Chairman, thereby securing a degree of control over Tata Sons that was never the settlers' intention," he said."It is entirely inappropriate for more than one family member to serve on a given trust; doing so effectively confers ownership rights over the trusts' significant shareholding in Tata Sons at no consideration whatsoever."The intervention comes amid an intensifying dispute over the governance of Tata Trusts, which collectively hold about 66 per cent of Tata Sons, the holding company of the USD 277-billion Tata group, and disagreements over its future ownership structure, the reappointment of N Chandrasekaran as Tata Sons chairman and whether the holding company should be publicly listed.Tata Trusts Chairman Noel Tata and trustee Darius Khambata have opposed Chandrasekaran's reappointment for another five-year term and resisted listing Tata Sons, arguing that a public listing could undermine the distinctive governance structure of the Tata Group and the Trusts' ability to safeguard its long-term interests.Vice-Chairmen Venu Srinivasan and Vijay Singh, however, have backed the Tata Sons board's decision to reappoint Chandrasekaran and argued that the company must respect regulatory requirements, including the RBI's listing framework.Jimmy's remarks also follow Noel's appearance at the Hindustan Times Leadership Summit on November 14, where the Tata Trusts chairman defended keeping Tata Sons privately held, warning that a public listing could shift the group's priorities towards profits and away from philanthropy. Advt Jimmy, however, argued that the Sir Ratan Tata Trust (SRTT) and Sir Dorabji Tata Trust (SDTT) - two principal charitable trusts that together hold 51.54 per cent of Tata Sons - were established through the wills and deeds of their respective founders, who had no children and bequeathed their wealth and properties to create institutions for public benefit.Their stated objectives included alleviating poverty and safeguarding Indian culture, he said, adding that trustees were custodians of public charitable institutions rather than owners of family assets.He cited former Tata chairmen JRD Tata and Naval Tata as leaders who understood the responsibility attached to managing the trusts. Advt Jimmy also questioned changes to the Tata Sons Articles of Association around 2013-14, arguing that they had fundamentally altered the relationship between the charitable trusts and the operating business.Under the earlier framework, he said, the welfare trusts were kept separate from the commercial management of Tata Sons. The subsequent amendments, however, gave the Tata Trusts chairman what he described as "unnatural, dictatorial veto powers" and the authority to nominate directors, undermining the collective decision-making powers of the board.He also criticised differences in trustee tenure rules, alleging that fixed terms for some trustees, alongside exemptions for others, had weakened institutional independence and concentrated authority. Advt Citing the position of Venu Srinivasan, whose term on the Sir Ratan Tata Trust is due to expire in December, Jimmy alleged that Srinivasan was being targeted because he opposed the induction of Noel's son into the trust. He also referred to the earlier removal of Vijay Singh, alleging that it followed his publication of an article setting out factual concerns.He contrasted the current arrangements with an earlier tradition of appointing independent and eminent figures to the trusts, naming jurist Nani Palkhivala, industrialist Gautam Sarabhai, nuclear scientist Homi Sethna and physicist Homi Bhabha.The dispute has also spilled over into the leadership of Tata Sons, with Jimmy throwing his support behind Chandrasekaran and criticising Noel for opposing his proposed third term as executive chairman.Jimmy said Chandrasekaran had helped turn around Tata companies that were weighed down by debt, delivering record earnings and substantial dividend payouts to shareholders, including Tata Sons.He said Chandrasekaran had received a 4-1 majority at the Tata Sons board and unanimous backing through a resolution passed by Tata Trusts, but Noel had nevertheless sought to block his continuation by invoking the veto provisions in the amended Articles of Association."Despite Chandrasekaran receiving overwhelming support (including 4-1 in Tata Sons board and a unanimous resolution passed by Tata Trusts), Noel sought to block him arbitrarily by invoking the onerous veto provisions in the amended Articles of Association without providing any justification or accountability," Jimmy said.The latest differences revive memories of the bitter 2016 removal of Cyrus Mistry as Tata Sons chairman, which triggered one of India's most consequential corporate governance battles.Mistry, who had succeeded Ratan Tata in 2012, was removed by the Tata Sons board in October 2016. The decision led to a prolonged legal battle between the Mistry family-backed companies, which held an 18.37 per cent stake in Tata Sons, and the Tata group leadership.The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal initially ordered Mistry's reinstatement in 2019, but the Supreme Court overturned that decision in March 2021, upholding his removal. The court dismissed a subsequent review petition in May 2022.Interestingly, Shapoorji Pallonji Group, its second-largest shareholder with an 18.37 per cent stake, has backed a stock market float to unlock value from its investment.Jimmy has called for the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to enforce the listing of Tata Sons, arguing that a public float would bring greater transparency and statutory accountability to the group.The issue has acquired urgency following the RBI's September 2026 rejection of Tata Sons' application to surrender its registration as a core investment company. The company had been classified as an upper-layer non-banking financial company (NBFC) in 2022, triggering a three-year listing requirement that expired in September 2025.The RBI has retained Tata Sons within its upper-layer regulatory framework, strengthening the regulatory pressure for a listing. Tata Sons had previously sought to exit the framework after repaying substantial debt.Jimmy warned against any attempt to restructure Tata Sons into smaller entities to avoid the listing requirement, alleging that such a move would serve to preserve secrecy and unchecked control.He also cited the public listing of Tata Consultancy Services under Ratan Tata as an example of how the group had benefited from opening one of its flagship businesses to public investors."Any proposal or move by Noel Tata to break up Tata Sons into smaller entities to evade the RBI's threshold requirements for listing, is deeply damaging and contrary to public and shareholder interests," he said.He urged the RBI to impose strict timelines for listing and called on the government and the Charity Commissioner to intervene to safeguard the original objectives of the trusts.
