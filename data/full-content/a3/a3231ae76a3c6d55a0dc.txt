Summary: The weekly regulatory roundup for 16–22 November 2026 covers important notifications, circulars, advisories, advance rulings and judicial decisions relating to Income Tax, GST, Central Excise, Customs, DGFT, SEBI, MCA and IBC. Income Tax notifications grant exemptions to the Telangana Building and Other Construction Workers Welfare Board, Kerala Abkari Workers Welfare Fund Board and Joint Plant Committee, besides recognising the Sakhalin-1 Joint Venture and approving Tata Memorial Centre for scientific research. GST developments include corrections to the CGST Amendment Rules, introduction of Metro Rail in the e-way bill system and mandatory RCM e-invoicing from January 2027. Advance rulings address dealer incentives, rose water, unworked human hair and residential maintenance charges, while the Delhi High Court examines GST valuation of corporate guarantees. Central Excise notifications revise export duties on aviation turbine fuel and diesel. Customs updates include duty exemptions under the Diamond Imprest Authorisation Scheme, revised tariff values, notified customs ports and routes, and extension of compulsory registration deadlines for hard disk drives. DGFT measures cover cut flower imports, raw sugar import quotas, eligible export certifications, freight reimbursement and airfare assistance for exporters. SEBI has revised commodity derivatives margins and proposed changes to market infrastructure governance and FPI disclosures. MCA has proposed amendments to the CSR framework, while significant Supreme Court and NCLAT decisions address company restoration, merger approvals, rehabilitation schemes, resolution plans, gratuity and arbitration. GST GST, Corrigendum to Notification 05/2026 Dated 26th October 2026: The notification has issued 40 numbered corrections the Central Goods and Services Tax (Amendment) Rules, 2026, originally published vide G.S.R. 919(E) dated 26 October 2026. The important corrections include expressly requiring maintenance of books of accounts and records in the relevant provision, replacing references to permission with consent, and correcting the spelling of Aadhaar. Two provisos are inserted on page 122 specifying that FORM GST RFD-06 does not require revalidation by the proper officer, whereas FORM GST RFD-05 requires revalidation if the refund is not disbursed within the same financial year in which the payment order was issued. (Link: Notification 05/2026 Corrigendum Dated 18/11/2026) GSTN, Advisory on Introduction of Metro Rail as Mode of Transport in the E-Way Bill System: The advisory states that taxpayers and transporters can select Metro Rail as a mode of transport when generating an e-way bill or updating its Part-B details. The Notification 05/2026 dated 26 October 2026, had included metro rail under Rule 138(2A) of the CGST Rules with effect from 1st November 2026. (Link: GSTN Advisory Dated 20/11/2026) GSTN, Advisory on RCM E-Invoicing Mandatory from January 2027 for Domestic Supplies & Service Imports: The advisory states that vide Notifications 04/2026 and 05/2026 dated 26th October 2026, e-invoicing (IRN) becomes mandatory from 1st January 2027 for Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) self-invoice categories for eligible taxpayers (generally those with aggregate turnover above Rs 5 crore). It covers domestic supplies received from unregistered persons subject to RCM (RCMDOM) and Import of services subject to RCM (RCMIMP).The system will be enabled for voluntary IRN from 15th to 31st December 2026 and mandatory compliance follows. (Link: GSTN Advisory Dated 17/11/2026) AAAR, GST Applicable on Gold Coins and White Goods Given as Dealer Incentives: Case of Orient Cement Limited, AAAR Telangana Ruling Dated 10th November 2026. AAAR upheld the advance ruling that distribution of gold coins and white goods to cement dealers under promotional incentive schemes constitutes a taxable supply under the GST law. Input Tax Credit (ITC) on procuring these items is blocked. (Link: AAAR Telangana Ruling Dated 10/11/2026) AAAR, Rose Water for Religious Use not Exempt from GST: Case of TSR & Co, AAAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 10th November 2026. AAAR upheld the ruling, holding that the product “Pooja Panneer” (rose water) was correctly classifiable under HSN 3301 90 79 and was not eligible for exemption as puja samagri under Entry 167 of Notification 10/2025 Rate dated 17th September 2025. (Link: AAAR Tamil Nadu Ruling Dated 10/11/2026) AAR, Unworked Human Hair Procured under Temple Tenders Is Exempt from GST: Case of Muthu Mohan Doss P, AAR Telangana Ruling Dated 17th November 2026. AAR held that raw, unworked, and unsorted human hair procured through temple tenders and e-auctions is classifiable as goods under HSN 0501 and is exempt from GST. (Link: AAR Telangana Ruling Dated 17/11/2026) AAR, GST @ 18% on Entire Maintenance Charges Exceeding Rs 7,500/-: Case of Trendset Jayabheri Elevate Flat Owners Maintenance Mutually Aided Cooperative Society Limited, AAR Telangana Ruling Dated 30th October 2026. AAR ruled that monthly maintenance charges up to Rs 7,500 per member remain exempt from GST even if the society’s annual turnover exceeds Rs 20 lakh. However, if the monthly charge crosses Rs 7,500, 18% GST applies to the entire amount, while separately billed common-area electricity and water reimbursements at actuals are exempt under pure-agent rules. (Link: AAR Telangana Ruling Dated 30/10/2026) HC, GST on Corporate Guarantees Upheld, 1% Valuation not an Inflexible Minimum: Case of Sterlite Power Transmission Limited vs Union of India, HC Delhi Judgement Dated 17th November 2026. HC held that extending a corporate guarantee without consideration is a taxable service under Section 7(1)(c) and Schedule I, Entry 2 of the CGST Act, with the related entity (borrowing subsidiary) as recipient. It also ruled that the 1% per annum valuation is a rebuttable presumption, not an inflexible minimum. Taxpayers may prove a lower open market value using cogent, verifiable evidence. (Link: HC Delhi Judgement Dated 17/11/2026) C. The SAED rate has been substituted with Rs 12.50 per litre (Pre-revised Rs 11.00 per litre), effective from 16th November 2026. (Link: Central Excise Notification 57/2026 (T) Dated 16/11/2026) D. Custom Duty Customs, Exemption from Duty and IGST for Natural Diamond Imports Under DIA Scheme: The notification exempts natural cut and polished diamonds imported under the Diamond Imprest Authorisation (DIA) scheme from basic customs duty, additional duties, anti-dumping, countervailing, and safeguard duties, cesses, and IGST. The covered goods include natural cut, polished, semi-processed, half-cut, and broken diamonds weighing up to 25 cents (1/4 carat) each. Exporters must achieve a minimum 10% value addition in freely convertible foreign exchange through physical exports made after import, with no deemed exports allowed. (Link: Customs Notification 37/2026 Dated 20/11/2026) Customs- Chharchhum Bridge notified as India–Nepal Land Customs Route: The notification adds the Chharchhum motorable bridge at Dharchula as a notified land customs route for India–Nepal trade. The notified routes now covers the existing road connection and the newly added Chharchhum motorable bridge linking Dharchula, Uttarakhand to Darchula, Nepal. (Link: Customs Notification 95/2026 (NT) Dated 20/11/2026) Customs, Fixation of Tariff Value of Edible Oils, Brass Scrap, Areca Nut, Gold and Silver: CBDT notified the Tariff Values of Edible Oils, Brass Scrap, Areca Nut, Gold and Silver, which shall come into force w.e.f. 20th November 2026. The tariff value for crude palm oil is set at USD 1141 per metric ton, while gold and silver have tariff values of USD 1245 per 10 grams and USD 1496 per kilogram, respectively. The tariff value for areca nuts is fixed at USD 10433 per metric ton. (Link: Customs Notification 94/2026 (NT) Dated 19/11/2026) Customs, Ramayapatnam notified as Customs Port for Imports and Exports: The notification adds Ramayapatnam in Andhra Pradesh as a customs port authorised for unloading imported goods and loading export goods. (Link: Customs Notification 93/2026 (NT) Dated 16/11/2026) Customs, Common Adjudicating Authority appointed for Six Customs Notices Issued to OnePlus: The Principal Commissioner/Commissioner of Customs (Import), Air Cargo Complex, New Delhi, has been appointed as the common adjudicating authority for six show cause notices issued to OnePlus Technology India Pvt Ltd. (Link: Customs Notification 92/2026 (NT) Dated 16/11/2026) Customs, Implementation of the Diamond Imprest Authorisation (DIA) Scheme: The circular provides operational procedures to implement DIA Scheme. It covers duty-exempt imports of natural cut and polished diamonds weighing up to 25 cents each. Laboratory-grown diamonds are excluded. The scheme is restricted to Two-Star Export Houses (and above) meeting a minimum US$15 million three-year export threshold. The authorisations carry a 12-month import validity from issuance. Importers face a strict, non-extendable 18-month export obligation period. (Link: Customs Circular 48/2026 Dated 20/11/2026) Customs, Standalone Hard Disk Drive Registration Deadline Extended to May 2027: The instructions extends the implementation timeline for compulsory registration requirements on Standalone Hard Disk Drives under the Electronics and Information Technology Goods (Requirement of Compulsory Registration) Order. (Link: Customs Instructions 24/2026 Dated 12/11/2026) E. Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) DGFT, Cut Flower Imports Permitted through Chennai, Amritsar Airports and Kandla Seaport: The notification expands the permitted entry points for importing Cut Flowers under HS Code 0603 into India from Chennai Airport alone to Chennai Airport, Amritsar Airport, and Kandla Seaport. The import policy status remains Free, subject to mandatory Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) and plant quarantine requirements. (Link: DGFT Notification 50/2026 Dated 19/11/2026) DGFT, Application Window Opens for Tariff Rate Quota (TRQ) Raw Sugar Imports: The public notice relates to opening of one-time application window for the fresh allocation of 2.15 Lacs Metric Tonnes (MT) of raw sugar under TRQ Scheme. It is open to eligible millers and refiners capable of processing raw sugar, with an online application deadline of 27th November 2026. (Link: DGFT Public Notice 41/2026 Dated 18/11/2026) DGFT, Expansion of EPM Niryat Disha Eligible Certifications: The trade notice amends the Guidelines for Trade Regulations, Accreditation and Compliance Enablement under the Export Promotion Mission (EPM) Niryat Disha. It expands the list of eligible testing, inspection and certification entries in Annexure-I from 462 to 600, with the objective of facilitating wider coverage of support to MSME exporters. (Link: DGFT Trade Notice 37/2026 Dated 17/11/2026) DGFT, Expands LIFT Freight Reimbursement for MSME Exports: The trade notice amends the operational guidelines for Facilitating Logistics Interventions for Freight and Transport (LIFT)under the Export Promotion Mission (EPM) Niryat Disha. Eligible MSMEs across 308 low-export-intensity districts (spanning 21 States/UTs as specified in Annexure-I) can claim partial freight reimbursement for all exportable merchandise not on the negative list. The minimum distance required to qualify for inland freight reimbursement is reduced from 200 km to 100 km, with a maximum cap of 400 km. The shipments via inland waterways are also now eligible. (Link: DGFT Trade Notice 36/2026 Dated 17/11/2026) DGFT, Export Airfare Support Raised to 95% under EPM Niryat Disha: The trade notice amends Market Access Support (MAS) guidelines under the Export Promotion Mission (EPM) Niryat Disha. It expands airfare reimbursement for eligible Indian delegates at overseas trade events. The preceding-year export turnover cap for airfare support rises from Rs 75 lakh to Rs 50 crore. Economy class airfare reimbursement increases to 95% for Micro and Small enterprises and 80% for other eligible entities (including zero-turnover exporters). (Link: DGFT Trade Notice 35/2026 Dated 17/11/2026) F. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) SEBI, Review of Margin Framework for Commodity Derivatives Segment: The circular revises the margin framework for the commodity derivatives segment. It has lowered minimum Initial Margin (IM) and Margin Period of Risk (MPOR) floors for agricultural commodities, aligning them with non-agricultural commodities across all volatility categories. For low, medium and high-volatility commodities, the minimum IM are 6%, 8% and 10%, respectively. The corresponding minimum MPOR is 2, 2 and 3. (Link: SEBI Circular Dated 17/11/2026) SEBI, Consultation Paper on Strengthening Governance of Market Infrastructure Institutions: It covers stock exchanges, clearing corporations, and depositories under the SECC and D&P Regulations. The key proposals include Lowering the maximum age limit of Public Interest Directors (PIDs) from 75 to 70 years and reducing the cap on terms across MIIs from three to two, Restrict PIDs from serving on boards of MII associates and cap membership at four statutory committees, Revising term and tenure limits of Managing & Executive Directors (MDs/Eds), including a reported 12-year cumulative cap across MIIs and Ex officio board seat at AMC Repo Clearing Ltd. for AMFI’s chief executive. The feedback/ comments from stakeholders are invited. (Link: SEBI Consultation Paper Dated 20/11/2026) SEBI, Consultation Paper on proposal to review the Additional Disclosure Framework for Foreign Portfolio Investors: Currently, the Additional Disclosure Framework (ADF) requires FPIs holding over 50% of Indian equity AUM in one corporate group or exceeding Rs 50,000 crore in equity AUM to disclose ownership, economic interest, and control down to natural persons with zero threshold. The paper proposes to remove the ADF for Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs), Offshore Derivative Instrument (ODI) subscribers, and segregated portfolios. The beneficial-owner identification under Prevention of Money-laundering Rules (10% or 15% thresholds) remains unchanged. The feedback/ comments from stakeholders are invited. (Link: SEBI Consultation Paper Dated 20/11/2026) G. Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) MCA, Draft Notifications CSR Policy and Schedule VII: The two draft notifications relates to the Companies (CSR Policy) Amendment Rules and a revamped Schedule VII to the Companies Act. The Key changes include Making Voluntary Impact Assessments optional for all Section 135 companies, the CSR exclusions to include one-off events, financing government schemes, corpus contributions, and in-kind donations of a company’s own products or services (except during immediate disaster relief). It also proposes voluntary surrender and cancellation mechanisms for CSR-1 registrations, a 30-day deadline to report changes in particulars, and eligibility updates aligned with the Income Tax Act. The overhauled Schedule VII reorganizes eligible activities into 14 modernized items, covering mental health, climate action, and skill development, while mandating liberal interpretation. The feedback/ comments from stakeholders are invited. (Link: MCA Public Notice Dated 11/11/2026) NCLAT, Leasehold Asset Alone cannot Justify Company Restoration: Case of Radiant Inn Private Limited vs Registrar of Companies (RoC), NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 18th November 2026. RoC struck off the company in 2018, after non-filing of financial statements and annual returns following FY 2013–14. The applicant sought restoration to realise or transfer a claimed 95-year MIDC leasehold plot in Nagpur and close its affairs, stating it had no intention to resume business operations. NCLAT refused to restore the struck-off company under Section 252(3) of the Companies Act. (Link: NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 18/11/2026) NCLAT, Merger Approval cannot be Conditional on Unnecessary RBI and ED Certifications: Case of L-1 Identity Solutions Operating Company Private Limited, NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 4th November 2026. The appellate tribunal held that court-approved schemes of arrangement do not routinely require Reserve Bank of India (RBI) or Enforcement Directorate (ED) no-objection certificates (NOCs) in absence of allegations of violations under FEMA, PMLA, or RBI guidelines. (Link: NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 04/11/2026) H. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) SC, IBC cannot Override Binding BIFR Rehabilitation Scheme: Case of Ganesh Benzoplast Ltd vs State Trading Corporation of India (STC), SC Judgement Dated 16th November 2026. The applicant was declared sick under Sick Industrial Companies Act (SICA), had a rehabilitation scheme approved by the Delhi High Court in December 2015. STC later filed a Section 9 insolvency petition under the IBC over disputed dues. The apex court held that a sanctioned rehabilitation scheme for a sick company binds unsecured creditors, including contingent liability claimants, to its recovery limits. (Link: SC Judgement Dated 16/11/2026) NCLAT, Contingent Operational Creditor Claim cannot Reopen Implemented Resolution Plan: Case of Dharampal Premchand Ltd vs Jitendra Bhandari, NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 25th May 2026. The applicant claim was treated as a “contingent claim” based on a counterclaim raised by the Resolution Professional (RP), resulting in no payment under the resolution plan to him. The appellate tribunal upheld the finality of the implemented plan. It held that the appellant failed to establish fraud, suppression, collusion, or material procedural irregularity i.e. the grounds for appellate interference under Section 61 of IBC. (Link: NCLAT Delhi Judgement Dated 25/05/2026) I. Reserve Bank of India (RBI) No Notification/ Circular during the week. J. Miscellaneous SC, Upholds Gratuity with Interest for Acquitted Employees: Case of Delhi Development Authority (DDA) vs Rajender Kumar Sharma, SC Judgement Dated 16th November 2026. The apex court held that a pending State appeal against an acquittal, without a stay in operation, does not justify withholding a retired employee’s gratuity. It dismissed the DDA appeals and ordered the release of gratuity with applicable interest. (Link: SC Judgement Dated 16/11/2026) SC, Arbitrators cannot Rewrite Contract, Orders Refund with 9% Interest: Case of Shreeram Builders vs Upendra Kantilal Thanawala, SC Judgement Dated 29th October 2026. The apex court held that arbitrators violated Section 28(3) of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, by rewriting contractual terms, ignoring the agreed payment sequence, altering consideration rates, and granting relief beyond the contract. By invoking Article 142, SC directed the landowners to refund the amounts received under the agreement with 9% simple annual interest from each payment date.
