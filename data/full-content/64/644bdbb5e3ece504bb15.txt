Articles 9 min read DCB Bank Targets 18-20% annual asset growth, bets on SME and affordable housing to drive expansion: CFO DCB Bank is targeting 18 to 20% annual asset growth and is betting on SME business loans, gold loans, SME working capital financing, and affordable housing to drive its next phase of expansion, CFO Ravi Kumar told ETCFO. The bank has also planned capital raise of Rs 900 crore to Rs 1,500 crore, expected to close before the end of calendar year 2026. Further, the bank is targeting to cross 500 branch mark by the end of the calendar year.The bank is also actively working to reduce its cost of deposits Kumar said, having brought down the incremental credit spread it offers over large peers from 127 basis points eight quarters ago to around 79 basis points currently, by shifting its branch banking pitch from rate to relationship and technology. Advt On the macro environment, Kumar saidthat while a potential rate hike by RBI in its upcoming meet would benefit banks in the short term through immediate transmission, the deposit side would catch up quickly. He said DCB is not expecting meaningful treasury gains in the next three to four quarters given the bond yield environment, but has built enough momentum in core fee income to compensate. The bank is also deploying AI in its finance function for the first time, having just gone live with a product that monitors interoffice accounts across 300,000 accounts and millions of transactions for pattern anomalies.DCB Bank closed Q1FY27 with total assets crossing Rs 88,752 crore, up 14.6% and advances growing 17% year on year to Rs 59,951 crore year on year. The bank has a network of 480 branches presently spread across 20 States and UTs.Edited Excerpts: How are you reading the current liquidity environment and the rate hike outlook given the RBI policy and what the Fed has already done?What is worrisome more than anything is inflation. On liquidity, there is a lot in the system, we got about 190 odd million in FCNRB inflows, which is about a month's sourcing for us. So we are not sitting on excessive liquidity but we can calibrate our deposits accordingly. Advt My concern is that some of the banks which raised a lot of money may end up with some dilution in credit discipline and that could have a wider systemic impact. My hope is that all bankers keep this in mind, we are in a very good credit cycle and anything that disturbs that could be a potential challenge.On rate hikes, if they happen, the transmission will happen immediately, which is beneficial for banks in the short term. But the deposit side rates will also catch up and cost of deposits will go up.We are one of the banks with a higher cost of deposits, in the top five in descending order of cost. We want to change that. If you look at the last two years, we were offering about 127 basis points incremental credit spread over the composite average of six large banks, three PSUs and three private sector banks. We have brought that down to about 79 basis points and it went as low as 16 basis points at one point. Our philosophy is to keep reducing the peak rate so the overall cost of deposits comes down over time. We have moved from pitching rate as the first selling option to pitching relationship, banking services, and technology. Advt The bank had indicated a capital raise around Q2-Q3. What is the current plan, how much and in what instruments?Our board approved a Rs 1,500 crore tier one and Rs 200 crore tier two of total capital in our April AGM and shareholders approved that as well.We had two conditions we set for ourselves. First, we said we will not dilute below book value. Second, we made sure our performance improved every quarter so the market recognises that effort and we don't have to dilute below book. Thankfully the market price is now above book and we are in the process of triggering the raise.We may not raise the entire Rs 1,500 crore of tier one. We will probably be raising somewhere between Rs 900 crore to Rs 1,000 crore depending on the price. All options are open, preference allotment and QIP. The CEO has already said that we intend to close the fundraise before the end of calendar year 2026 and that remains the target. Advt What is the lending strategy and product mix going forward? Co-lending has come down?Co-lending is one of the spokes in our wheel and we punch much above our weight on co-lending as a percentage of overall asset book. But to ensure there is no undue concentration risk, we have capped the co-lending book at 15% of overall assets. That has been the policy for about a year now and it will remain.Our core products are business loans and loan against property, catering to self-employed micro and small SMEs. We intend to accelerate growth there. We also intend to grow gold loans, which is an important product for us. SME working capital loans are something we are now pushing aggressively, we have been focused largely on term loans so far and we believe it is now time in our journey to also fund the working capital cycle of the SME. The book is small right now but it will become meaningful as we grow.Construction finance in the mid-market affordable segment is doing well for us. We are not in the high-luxury CF space because that comes with much higher risk than the BTS segment. Priority sector loans, tractor and KCC loans, and microfinance to small and marginal farmers are also an important part of the mix.In terms of the current composition, business loans and home loans, which we call mortgages, together are about 48% of the book. Within that, business loans are about 60% and mortgages 40%. Co-lending is capped at 15%. Gold is about 7.5%. Corporate is restricted to 10% of the overall asset book, we are at about 7.5% now so there is some headroom, but we are very selective because at our cost of funds we don't necessarily attract the kind of corporates we want to deal with. Agri loans are another 15 to 18%.What is the asset book growth target in next two-three years?We have guided 18 to 20% asset growth and we stick to that. At that growth rate we are already one of the fastest growing banks. There is an aspiration to grow faster but different pools of capital and requirements come with that. Once the capital raise comes in we will revise the target and speak about it. But 18 to 20% is the guided number for now.On the liability side, we always want deposit growth to be slightly ahead of advances growth, 50 to 100 basis points higher, because we want to maintain higher liquidity. That has been our consistent approach and will remain so.On technology, what is happening at DCB on AI and agentic AI, and how much are you spending?Post-COVID we started a digital transformation programme for the bank and I chaired that committee for four years. There is a high-powered group cutting across business, operations, and technology that constantly looks at tech initiatives and where we are heading.We are very excited about AI internally. Beyond the initial use cases of customer communication, fraud, and risk management, the use cases will grow big time. We launched 12 new products in the current financial year, including one which has AI built in for customer onboarding. We are also using AI heavily in our Business Intelligence Unit to look at propensity of customers to buy various products, the timing, and where they are in their life journey. A lot of work is happening on that front.On cost — AI is not cheap and it is not going to get cheaper in the next few years before the cost gets optimised. We are conscious of it but this is a good cost to have. We are one of the higher tech spenders as a percentage of our opex, we did a study using IBM data on all banks about a year ago and we came out as one of the highest. We don't publish the exact number but that is where we stand.In the finance function specifically, we have just implemented an AI-based product for interoffice accounts monitoring. It is humanly impossible to look at all the transactions happening across the bank, we are talking about 300,000 accounts and millions of transactions. This product maps patterns and flags unusual items. We co-created it with a large technology company, sat down with them, gave them our inputs, and it went live about six to seven weeks ago. Too early to talk about the impact but I am very excited about it.Q: You are currently at 480 branches across 20 plus states and UTs. What is the expansion target for FY27 and beyond and which geographies will receive the bulk of new branch investments?We follow a calibrated approach on branch expansion. We plan to add about 25 to 30 branches every year — some years we do 23, some years we do 30, but it averages out to about 25 to 30. We are present across all states other than the seven northeastern states and Jammu and Kashmir.Our expansion strategy is to put more branches in places where we already exist, in the neighbourhood of where we already are. If a branch is doing well, the second, third, and fourth branches come in and around that city so that we become a cluster there. That is the thought process on branch expansion.We have significant digital reach and our products have a lot of digital reach, we are a digital-first business model. But some physical presence is still required despite all the investments in technology. At 25 to 30 branches a year, we should be at about 500 branches this year and slightly above that by FY27. On treasury and rupee depreciation, DCB has a significant GSEC book. How has the rate environment played out for you?The rate cycle dropped about six quarters ago. Personally I thought yields would be around 6.60 to 6.70%, if not lower. But this coincided with the tariff war and the West Asia conflict and sentiment was never really positive on the bond market. We only had one quarter where treasuries were in our favour and then GSEC yields went back up to 6.90 to 7.10, peaking at 7.40 if I am not mistaken. That doesn't help us. So we didn't really have a positive MTM sitting on our bond book.With a potential rate hike the whole picture changes again. We are not expecting much to come out of treasury in the next three to four quarters. But we have built enough momentum and inertia on our core fee income to compensate for any lack of treasury gains.We are slightly conservative on risk-taking on FX trading and GSEC trading. We are more passionate about core fee income and treasury gains are the icing on the cake, we don't run the bank based on treasury gains.
