CESTAT Kolkata considered an appeal filed by the Revenue challenging the adjudicating authority’s decision regarding the waiver of penalty on National Insurance Company Limited for wrongly availing CENVAT credit on various services. During the investigation, it was found that the respondent had incorrectly availed CENVAT credit, which was subsequently reversed when pointed out by the audit team. The respondent explained that it operated through offices at various locations and that its employees had inadvertently availed CENVAT credit due to insufficient knowledge of the applicable statutory provisions and rules. After examining the records and submissions, the adjudicating authority confirmed the demand, appropriated the amount already reversed and demanded interest. However, considering the facts and circumstances of the case, the authority refrained from imposing any penalty. Aggrieved by the non-imposition of penalty, the Revenue approached the Tribunal. The Tribunal observed that the respondent had admittedly reversed the wrongly availed CENVAT credit upon being pointed out. Considering these circumstances, CESTAT extended the benefit of Section 80 of the Finance Act, 1994 and held that the imposition of penalty was unwarranted. Accordingly, the Tribunal found no infirmity in the adjudicating authority’s order, upheld the waiver of penalty and dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. FULL TEXT OF THE CESTAT KOLKATA ORDER The Revenue is in appeal against the impugned order wherein the Ld.Commissioner refrained from imposing penalty on the respondent. 2. Facts of the case are that the respondent is an Insurance Company. They availed various services and taken cenvat credit thereon. During the course of investigation, it was found that the respondent has wrongly taken cenvat credit on various services, which was reversed on pointing out by Audit Team. The contention of the respondent is that as the respondent is having office at various locations and the employees are not fully aware of the provisions and rules, therefore, they have taken cenvat credit inadvertently by mistake and on pointing out, the same has been reversed. During the course of adjudication, the ld.;Adjudicating Authority gone through the records and various contentions made by the respondent, wherein it was found that the respondent has reversed the cenvat credit which was taken wrongly. The Adjudicating Authority confirmed the said demand and appropriated the amount reversed and also demanded interest, but the in the facts and circumstances of the case, refrained from imposing any penalty on the respondent. For not imposing penalty on the respondent, the Revenue is in appeal. 3. Heard the ld.A.R. for the Revenue and gone through the records. 4. We find, it is a fact on record that cenvat credit has been taken by the respondent wrongly and the same has been reversed on pointing out. In that circumstances, by giving the benefit under Section 80 of the Finance Act, 1994, we refrain from imposing any penalty on the respondent. In that circumstances, we do not find any infirmity in the impugned order and the same is upheld. 5. In the result, the appeal filed by the Revenue is dismissed.
