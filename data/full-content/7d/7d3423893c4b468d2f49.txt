(Karnataka High Court) Summary: The Karnataka High Court, Dharwad Bench, in M/s R M Chougule and Associated Vs Assistant Commissioner of Commercial Tax & Ors., considered a writ petition challenging rejection of a GST appeal solely on the ground of delay. The petitioner submitted that, notwithstanding payment of the entire tax liability, an assessment had been made against it and its appeal had been rejected on limitation grounds. The petition specifically sought quashing of appellate order No. GST-227/2024-25/B-491 dated 30 September 2024 and the rectification order dated 21 November 2023. The petitioner relied upon the Karnataka High Court’s earlier decision in Jai Hind Enterprises Vs Assistant Commissioner of Commercial Taxes & Ors., Writ Petition No. 107549 of 2024, decided on 21 January 2025. The Court also considered the position arising from the Division Bench decision in Writ Appeal No. 100608 of 2025, subsequently identified in related TaxGuru reporting as Yallappa Patil Vs Assistant Commissioner & Ors. The issue was whether the petitioner should be granted an opportunity to pursue the statutory appellate remedy despite the earlier rejection on limitation grounds. Comparable Karnataka High Court decisions on delayed GST appeals are discussed in Delayed GST Appeal to Be Heard on Merits Without Limitation Objection: Karnataka HC. Justice M. Nagaprasanna observed that the issue stood covered by the earlier judgment in Writ Petition No. 107549 of 2024 and the Division Bench decision in Writ Appeal No. 100608 of 2025. Accordingly, the Court partly allowed the writ petition and permitted the petitioner to file an appeal against cancellation of registration before the Appellate Authority within four weeks from receipt of the order. The Court directed that, if the appeal was filed within the permitted period, the Appellate Authority must consider it on merits and must not reject it on limitation grounds. However, the Court expressly stipulated that the benefit of the order would not be available if the petitioner failed to file the appeal within four weeks. Importantly, although the petitioner’s recorded grievance concerned an assessment and rejection of an appeal, the operative directions specifically refer to an appeal against cancellation of GST registration. The judgment therefore grants a time-bound opportunity to seek appellate consideration rather than finally deciding the underlying tax dispute. A subsequent Karnataka High Court judgment reproduced and applied this very order in Shivashakti Traders Vs Commercial Tax Officer. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT 1. Petitioner is before this Court seeking the following prayer: a. “Issue Writ of Certiorari or in the like nature of certiorari quashing the impugned order bearing no. Appeal No. GST-227/2024-25/B-491, dated 30/09/2024, passed by respondent no. 2. vide ANNEXURE-C, to the petition. b. Consequently or in the like nature of certiorari quashing the impugned order bearing no. ACCT/A-5/RECTIFICATION/2023-24 dated 21.11.2023, passed by respondent no. 1 vide ANNEXURE-A. c. Pass such other order of further orders as this Hon’ble Court may deems fit in the facts and circumstances of this case, in the interest of justice and equity.” 2. The learned counsel appearing for the petitioner submits that the petitioner notwithstanding the fact of payment of complete tax, the assessment is drawn against him, against which an appeal is preferred and the appeal is rejected on the sole ground of delay. 3. The learned counsel submits that the issue in the lis stands answered by the judgment rendered by this Court in Writ Petition No.107549/2024 disposed of on 21.01.2025. 4. In the light of the issue standing answered by this Court and that of the Division Bench in Writ Appeal No.100608/2025, the petition deserves to succeed in the same direction that is issued while disposing of Writ Petition No.107549 of 2024. 5. For the aforesaid reasons, the following: ORDER i. Writ petition is allowed in part. ii. The petitioner is permitted to file an appeal against the cancellation of registration before the Appellate Authority within four weeks from the date of the receipt of this order. iii. In the event, the appeal is preferred within four weeks as aforesaid, it shall be considered on its merit and not reject the appeal on the ground of limitation. iv. In the event, the petitioner would not prefer an appeal within four weeks as permitted above, the benefit of the order rendered in the subject petition would not be available to the petitioner. Ordered accordingly.
