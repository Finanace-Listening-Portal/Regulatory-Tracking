Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Sep 11, 2026 at 02:24 PM IST var news_msid = 134056171; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook MAS Chief Chia Der JiunArtificial intelligence could make financial decisions almost instantaneous, fundamentally changing how money is managed, invested and moved while putting established financial institutions under increasing competitive pressure, Chia Der Jiun, Managing Director of the Monetary Authority of Singapore, said at the Global FinTech Fest 2026.As AI adoption moves beyond experimentation across the financial sector, its impact could extend well beyond productivity gains. AI is increasingly being deployed in fraud detection, credit underwriting, risk management, compliance, marketing, customer service and document processing, while the next phase could see AI systems and agents make and execute financial decisions with significantly less human intervention. Advt AI could take friction out of financeAccording to Chia, AI has the potential to optimise cash flows, cash management, payments and investments, allowing decisions that currently take time to be made almost instantaneously.This could fundamentally alter how consumers and businesses interact with financial institutions. As AI agents become capable of assessing information, making recommendations and eventually executing transactions, the distinction between financial decision-making and financial execution could increasingly disappear.The shift could also intensify competition within the financial sector. Institutions would have to compete not only on products and pricing but also on the speed and quality of AI-driven financial services.Adoption moving beyond pilotsChia said AI adoption among financial institutions in Singapore has already moved beyond small-scale pilots, with use cases emerging across several parts of the financial value chain.Financial institutions are using AI for areas such as fraud detection, credit assessment, risk management and customer service. The Monetary Authority of Singapore has also been working with the industry to develop AI capabilities and encourage responsible adoption.The regulator has supported efforts to use AI and data more effectively to identify suspicious accounts and transactions, including collaboration across financial institutions. Advt Faster AI brings new risksHowever, the acceleration of financial decision-making also brings new risks. As AI systems become more autonomous, errors, cyberattacks or failures at critical technology providers could have consequences that spread quickly across the financial system.Chia highlighted the growing cyber threat alongside the increasing use of AI. High-severity vulnerabilities have risen sharply, while AI is also being used by attackers to make cyberattacks more sophisticated and scalable.At the same time, the concentration of AI capabilities among a relatively small number of technology and model providers could create additional systemic vulnerabilities for financial institutions. Advt Regulators face an AI governance challengeFor regulators, the challenge will be to allow financial institutions to capture the benefits of faster, more automated decision-making without compromising resilience, accountability and consumer protection.MAS has been developing governance frameworks and safeguards around generative and agentic AI, including controls around identity and authority, checks before actions are executed, and auditability.The larger shift, however, could be structural. If AI agents increasingly decide where money is held, invested or transferred, financial institutions could face a competitive environment where decisions happen at machine speed, making the ability to deploy trusted AI a potential differentiator as well as a regulatory challenge.
