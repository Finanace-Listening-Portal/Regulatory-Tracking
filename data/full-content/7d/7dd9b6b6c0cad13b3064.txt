EconomyAnalysts Divided On RBI, Centre's Move To Attract Foreign InvestorsAuthored by: Vikas KumarUpdated: Oct 19, 2026, 15:02 ISTASSOCHAM supports the policy changes, including tax exemptions for foreign portfolio investments, Bernstein warns that tax cuts alone won't ensure consistent foreign fund inflows. (Representational Image)follow usfollow usAnalysts remain divided over the effectiveness of measures taken by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and the Centre to attract foreign investors, with industry body ASSOCHAM welcoming the policy push while brokerage Bernstein cautioning that tax concessions alone may not revive sustained foreign fund inflows.ASSOCHAM President Nirmal k Minda, reacting to former RBI Governor Dr. D. Subbarao's observation that the rupee will ultimately find strength when India attracts durable capital flows based on fundamentals, said, The Government and RBI are already moving in the right direction and ASSOCHAM fully aligns with this approach.ASSOCHAM also welcome the Finance Ministry's investor-friendly steps including the Income-tax Amendment Ordinance, 2026 which has exempted FPI investments in Government Securities from tax on interest and capital gains with effect from April 1, 2026.Saurabh Sanyal, Secretary General ASSOCHAM said, "This forward-looking move will attract stable, long-term capital from pension funds, sovereign wealth funds and insurance companies."Dr. S P Sharma, Chief Economist ASSOCHAM on RBI's proactive package said the recent measures including the special USD window for public sector oil marketing companies, the Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve, tightening of forex derivative norms, OMO operations and the higher CRR requirement have helped the rupee recover nearly 2 percent from its recent lows. "The successful handling of the 133 billion dollar FCNR(B) mobilisation window that closed on August 31 is commendable," he noted.Reduction in LTCG tax insufficient to trigger FII inflows:Bernstein said a reduction in long-term capital gains (LTCG) tax would be desirable for a capital-starved economy, but would be insufficient on its own to trigger sustained foreign institutional investor (FII) inflows. A recovery in India's growth and earnings cycle remains critical, it added.Further, Bernstein cut its December 2026 target for the Nifty 50 to 25,000 from 26,000 earlier, citing a deteriorating macroeconomic backdrop, persistent crude oil and supply-chain pressures, and a lack of meaningful earnings upgrades.Bernstein’s revised Nifty target implies an upside of around 8.1 per cent from the current level. However, the brokerage cautioned that a further 4-5 per cent rally would take Indian equities out of their current valuation comfort zone, bringing them closer to recent averages at around 18.2 times forward earnings.Bernstein also sees limited scope for India to benefit materially from a near-term pause in the artificial intelligence (AI) trade.
