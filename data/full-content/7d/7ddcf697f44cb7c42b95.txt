Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Gudla Vanaja And Others Vs Assistant Commissioner (ST) And Others (Telangana High Court) Summary: The Telangana High Court Full Bench, deciding a reference in Gudla Vanaja and others v. Assistant Commissioner (ST) and others, held that show cause notices and adjudication orders electronically authenticated by the proper officer and uploaded on the GST common portal under Chapter XVIII of the CGST Rules are valid even where their PDF versions do not display a physical or digital signature. The batch arose from conflicting Division Bench decisions concerning allegedly unsigned GST notices and orders, including Bigleap Technologies and Solutions Private Limited v. State of Telangana, Silver Oak Villas LLP v. Assistant Commissioner (ST) and Sahithi Marketers v. Superintendent of Central Tax. Rejecting the objection to the reference, the Full Bench held that a larger Bench was the proper forum to resolve the conflicting views. The petitioners contended that signatures were mandatory under the prescribed forms and the Information Technology Act, 2000; that backend authentication or login credentials could not substitute authentication of the document received by the taxpayer; and that portal upload alone did not establish service. GSTN was impleaded and demonstrated the process of onboarding officers, registering digital signature certificates, authenticating and authorising access, creating notices and orders, and serving them through the portal. Examining Sections 3, 3A, 4 and 5 of the Information Technology Act, the applicable digital signature rules, and the statutory GST framework, the Court accepted that the electronic authentication mechanism ensured authenticity, integrity and tamper detection. It held that prescribed forms are subservient to the rules and cannot impose a requirement inconsistent with the governing scheme. Rule 26(3), situated in the chapter concerning registration, could not be applied in the manner urged to invalidate electronically authenticated demand and recovery documents. The Court further held that the methods of service under Section 169(1) are alternatives. Making an electronically authenticated notice or order available on the common portal constitutes valid service under Section 169(1)(d), and limitation runs from the date of upload rather than the date on which the taxpayer subsequently retrieves it. The common portal constitutes a designated computer resource for this purpose. The Court declined to accept the contrary interpretation advanced by reference to Bambino Agro Industries Limited v. State of Uttar Pradesh. It also held that the presumption of regularity attached to official electronic acts is rebuttable, but unsubstantiated allegations do not displace it; demonstrable abuse may be examined in appropriate proceedings. On document identification, the Court accepted RFN as a valid verification mechanism and treated Circular No.249/06/2025-GST dated 09.06.2025 as clarificatory. A separate DIN is unnecessary where a portal communication bears a verifiable RFN; absence of DIN or RFN would, however, render the document invalid. Other challenges, including alleged procedural non-compliance under Section 65, competence of the issuing officer, possible misuse of authority and certain service objections relating to the “Additional Notices and Orders” tab before 16.01.2024, were left open for the appropriate forum. The contrary position in Bigleap Technologies on the signature issue was held not to state the correct law. Since the petitions had remained pending for a considerable period, the Court granted the petitioners liberty to pursue statutory appeals within two weeks from 30.09.2026 and raise the other grounds left open.
