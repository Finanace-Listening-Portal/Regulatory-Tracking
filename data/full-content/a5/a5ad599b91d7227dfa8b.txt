Articles 2 min read Kotak MF suspends subscriptions in two funds holding Yes Bank AT1 bonds after SC ruling Kotak Mahindra AMC has suspended fresh subscriptions in Kotak Medium Term Fund and Kotak Credit Risk Fund, which hold Yes Bank AT1 bonds earlier valued at zero following their March 2020 write-off. The AMC is now reviewing the Supreme Court judgment restoring the bonds. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Published On Oct 16, 2026 at 10:49 AM IST var news_msid = 134983270; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Kotak Mahindra Asset Management Company has temporarily suspended subscriptions in two debt mutual fund schemes that hold Additional Tier 1 (AT1) bonds of Yes Bank, after the Supreme Court on Thursday quashed the lender’s Rs 8,415-crore write-off of the securities.The two schemes — Kotak Medium Term Fund and Kotak Credit Risk Fund — hold Yes Bank AT1 bonds that had been valued at zero following their write-off during the reconstruction of the private sector lender in March 2020, Kotak AMC said in a statement.The fund house said it had suspended subscriptions in both schemes earlier on Thursday as a precautionary measure to protect the interests of its investors. The Supreme Court's judgment in the matter was delivered later in the day. Advt “Kotak Medium Term Fund and Kotak Credit Risk Fund hold Yes Bank AT1 bonds that were valued at zero following their write-off during the reconstitution of Yes Bank in March 2020,” the AMC said.“Earlier today, we proactively suspended subscriptions in both funds as a prudent measure to safeguard the interests of our investors. The Honourable Supreme Court's judgment in the Yes Bank AT1 bond matter, delivered later in the day, has restored the Bonds. We are reviewing the judgment and will determine the appropriate course of action,” it added.Supreme Court rulingThe Supreme Court on Thursday upheld the Bombay High Court's January 2023 judgment that had quashed Yes Bank's write-off of Rs 8,415 crore worth of AT1 bonds undertaken as part of the bank's reconstruction in March 2020. A bench of Justices Dipankar Datta and Augustine George Masih dismissed appeals filed by Yes Bank, the Reserve Bank of India and the Centre. The Bombay High Court had held that the final reconstruction scheme approved by the Centre did not authorise the write-off of the AT1 bonds, and that the administrator had acted beyond his authority in doing so after the scheme had been finalised.
