Articles 3 min read AI can cut MSME underwriting to minutes, but credit decisions still need human oversight: NBFC leaders AI is helping NBFCs structure unorganised business records and speed up SME underwriting, but data quality, diverse business models and repayment risks continue to limit fully automated lending, industry leaders said at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Published On Oct 12, 2026 at 05:14 AM IST var news_msid = 134879821; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Artificial intelligence can help NBFCs expand credit access to small businesses by converting fragmented financial records into usable credit insights, but human judgment remains essential to assessing repayment capacity, industry leaders said.Speaking at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026, Jyothirlatha B, CTO & CIO, Godrej Capital; Naved Hussain, Head IT & BSG, Tyger Capital; and Raman Aggarwal, CEO, FIDC, discussed how AI is changing SME underwriting while highlighting the challenges of data reliability and diverse borrower profiles.AI speeds up assessment of unstructured dataJyothirlatha said AI could help underwriters analyse fragmented business records, identify transaction patterns and improve the consistency of credit assessments. However, fully automated decision-making remains difficult when financial information is incomplete or unreliable. Advt “Whether we are able to do complete decisioning using that, it is still a no. It is a co-pilot kind of thing,” she said, stressing the need for verification and human oversight.Hussain said AI was helping Tyger Capital convert handwritten records and other unstructured information into usable financial data. Tasks that once took credit managers days, such as structuring a small merchant’s records into a balance sheet, could now be completed in minutes.This could allow underwriters to spend more time assessing repayment capacity and structuring loans rather than manually processing documents.Cash-flow lending can widen credit accessAggarwal said the growing use of GST records, transaction histories and repayment data was shifting SME lending away from an excessive reliance on collateral towards assessing actual business cash flows.“NBFCs and MSMEs are made for each other,” he said, highlighting the sector’s role in financing businesses that may lack substantial assets or formal financial documentation.However, Jyothirlatha cautioned that historical transactions alone cannot establish whether a borrower will be able to repay in the future. Data quality, validation and the ability to understand changing business conditions remain critical. Advt Diverse businesses require tailored underwritingHussain said lenders could not apply identical credit models to every small business. A dairy farmer and a milk aggregator, for instance, have different cash-flow patterns and financing needs. Even two kirana stores may differ in margins, inventory practices and customer profiles.Jyothirlatha said Godrej Capital uses multiple scorecards to assess different SME borrower segments, reflecting variations in business characteristics and risk appetite.Aggarwal also stressed that many smaller businesses have limited digital records, making physical customer interactions important for verifying information and understanding local business conditions. Advt AI investment must make business senseThe panellists said AI adoption must be aligned with the economics of lending to smaller borrowers, where loan sizes may be modest but acquisition, verification and servicing costs remain high.Technology can lower processing costs and improve efficiency, but lenders must still assess risk-adjusted returns and select segments they can serve sustainably. Aggarwal said regulatory engagement, including mechanisms such as regulatory sandboxes, could help support responsible innovation.The broader opportunity for NBFCs is to use AI to make complex and underserved borrower segments more viable to finance. However, faster data processing cannot substitute for sound underwriting, reliable information and an understanding of local business realities.
