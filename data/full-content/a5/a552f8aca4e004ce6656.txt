Articles 3 min read CSB Bank board yet to discuss Fairfax-IDBI deal, CEO says Fairfax Financial Holdings’ proposed acquisition of IDBI Bank has put its existing stake in CSB Bank under review, with the RBI giving the Canadian group two years to either merge CSB with IDBI or sell its stake. However, CSB’s board has not yet discussed the transaction, its MD & CEO Pralay Mondal said. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Oct 24, 2026 at 01:37 AM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 135155370; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook CSB Bank’s board has not discussed Fairfax Financial Holdings’ proposed acquisition of IDBI Bank or the future of its stake in CSB, with MD & CEO Pralay Mondal saying the lender has also not received any deposit from the Canadian financial services group.“Fairfax has no deposit with the bank,” Mondal said during the bank’s Q2 FY27 analyst call, responding to a question on reports that Fairfax had a Rs 4,000–5,000 crore deposit with CSB Bank. He added that the Rs 5,000-crore related-party figure disclosed in CSB Bank’s annual report does not pertain to Fairfax.“There could be other transactions, but nothing to do with deposits,” Executive Director B.K. Divakara said during the call. Advt Mondal also said the CSB board has not discussed the IDBI transaction in its last two meetings and that the bank is not currently thinking through the implications of Fairfax’s proposed acquisition.“The second question on Fairfax, board have not even discussed this point in the last two board meetings,” Mondal said. He added that Fairfax has two years to decide on the two options available to it, while the IDBI transaction itself is yet to be completed.“At this point of time, they have the transaction to yet to complete, so it is too early to even discuss transaction,” Mondal said, pointing to pending share purchase agreements, regulatory approvals and other formalities.Fairfax has two years to decide on CSBThe comments come days after the Union Cabinet approved Fairfax-backed Canada Inc as the strategic buyer for a 60.72 per cent stake in IDBI Bank for Rs 52,878 crore. The Government of India is selling 30.48 per cent and LIC 30.24 per cent in the lender.Fairfax currently owns around 40 per cent of CSB Bank. Following the IDBI transaction, the group will have overlapping ownership of two banking licences, requiring it to decide on the future of its CSB holding.Fairfax Chairman and CEO Prem Watsa had said that the Reserve Bank of India has given the group two years to decide whether to merge CSB with IDBI Bank or sell its stake. “Merge or sell? Those are the two options we have,” Watsa had said, adding that Fairfax would consider the options carefully before taking a decision. Advt In a subsequent discussion on Fairfax’s India strategy, Watsa said CSB was not part of the announced IDBI-Digit-IIFL Capital structure and that the group would maintain its decentralised operating philosophy. Fairfax plans to bring its interests in Go Digit and IIFL Capital under IDBI, while CSB and its stake in IIFL Finance remain outside the announced structure.Fairfax has also said it expects IDBI’s existing management to continue, while proposing former CSB Bank CEO C.V. Rajendran as part of the leadership group and N.S. Kannan as chairman, subject to regulatory approvals.The lender reported an 18 per cent year-on-year increase in deposits and 22 per cent growth in gross assets in Q2 FY27. Gold loans grew 36 per cent while the corporate book expanded 44 per cent. SME growth remained moderate, with management saying that issues in the portfolio over the past year to year-and-a-half had largely been corrected and growth should start picking up from the quarter. Advt Mondal has also said CSB intends to become a more diversified bank rather than remain heavily dependent on gold loans. The bank is targeting a reduction in gold-loan share to around 50 per cent from 54 per cent, with a longer-term glide path towards 32 per cent by 2030.The bank’s management has maintained that its growth aspiration remains ahead of its current guidance of growing 30–50 per cent faster than the banking system. However, it expects the liability franchise to take time to build, with CASA growth and reduced dependence on foreign-currency funding and certificates of deposit expected to support faster growth from FY28 onwards.
