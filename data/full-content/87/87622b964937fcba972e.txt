Nine of the companies listed the same address at Poddar Court on Rabindra Sarani in central Kolkata. The concentration raises questions about the corporate and business structures behind these entities, but the RBI has not disclosed their individual ownership details, the grounds for cancellation or whether they have common promoters, directors or business links.The 49 cancellation orders were issued between October 1 and October 29, 2026. Of these, 38 were dated October 15, including 36 against companies registered in West Bengal. Across the entire list, 43 of the 49 companies had registered offices in West Bengal, including 41 in Kolkata. The remaining six were registered in Tamil Nadu (three), Maharashtra (one), Delhi (one) and Gujarat (one). These figures reflect registered office addresses published by the RBI and do not establish where the companies conducted their business. Advt The nine companies listing Room No. 303, third floor, Gate No. 2, Poddar Court, 18 Rabindra Sarani, Lalbazar, Kolkata, as their registered office are Alankrit Vyapaar, Dreamon Commercial, Emersec Holdings, Jemco Vanijya, Jet (India), M.L. Finlease, Nikita Electrotrades, Purvi Vanijya Niyojan and Strength Fincap Markets. Their certificates of registration were issued between 1998 and 2013. The common address is a verifiable feature of the RBI's list, but it is not evidence by itself of common ownership, control or financial activity.The cancellations were made under Section 45-IA(6) of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. The provision allows the RBI to cancel an NBFC's certificate of registration on specified grounds, including cessation of non-banking financial business, failure to comply with conditions attached to registration, failure to meet certain statutory conditions, non-compliance with RBI directions, and failure to maintain or produce prescribed accounts and records. It also covers certain cases involving a prohibition on accepting deposits. The RBI's announcement does not identify which statutory ground applied to each of the 49 companies. It would therefore be incorrect to describe them collectively as defaulters or regulatory violators.Surrender of registrationSeparately, 13 NBFCs and one housing finance company surrendered their certificates of registration. The RBI grouped these 14 cases into three categories: six entities had exited the non-banking financial institution business, five met the criteria for classification as unregistered Type I NBFCs, and three had ceased to exist as legal entities following amalgamation, merger, dissolution or voluntary strike-off. These voluntary surrenders are distinct from the 49 registrations cancelled by the RBI.The Type I exemption follows amendments to the RBI's NBFC registration directions issued on April 29, 2026, and effective from April 1, 2026. Eligible NBFCs with assets below Rs 1,000 crore that do not avail public funds or have customer interface, and intend to maintain that business model over the long term, are exempt from the registration requirement under Section 45-IA of the RBI Act. Existing eligible NBFCs could apply for deregistration within six months, by September 30, 2026. The RBI's November 16 announcement identifies five companies whose registrations were cancelled after they met the criteria for unregistered Type I NBFCs. Advt The exemption is not a blanket relaxation for smaller NBFCs. Public funds are broader than public deposits and include borrowings from banks, inter-corporate deposits and funds raised from outside sources. Customer interface includes lending, guarantees, account-based relationships and other customer-facing business activities. An entity intending to access public funds or have customer interface must obtain registration as a Type II NBFC before undertaking those activities. An entity with assets of Rs 1,000 crore or more must obtain Type I registration even if it does not access public funds or have customer interface. The RBI also requires the assets of multiple unregistered Type I NBFCs within a group to be aggregated for the Rs 1,000 crore threshold. Advt The exemption is only from the registration requirement under Section 45-IA; it does not remove the entities from all provisions of Chapter IIIB of the RBI Act. The RBI retains powers to issue instructions and take action under the applicable provisions of the Act.
