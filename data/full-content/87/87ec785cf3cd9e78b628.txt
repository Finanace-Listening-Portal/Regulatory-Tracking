Summary: Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) return filing is an important income-tax compliance requirement for businesses, employers, professionals and other persons responsible for deducting tax at source. TDS statements contain particulars of the deductor, deductee, PAN, TAN, payments, tax deductions and deposits made with the government. Different forms are prescribed depending on the nature of payments and residential status of the recipients. Under the Income-tax Act, 1961 framework, Form 24Q covers salary payments, Form 26Q covers specified non-salary payments to residents, and Form 27Q covers specified non-salary payments to non-residents. The filing process involves obtaining TAN where applicable, deducting tax at prescribed rates, depositing TDS within statutory timelines, preparing statements using the Return Preparation Utility (RPU), validating files through the File Validation Utility (FVU) and submitting the statements electronically. Deductors must also issue applicable TDS certificates to deductees. Delayed filing, incorrect reporting, non-deduction and delayed deposit of TDS can attract statutory fees, interest, penalties and disallowance of expenditure, subject to applicable provisions. Timely reconciliation of PAN details, challans, payment records and TDS statements can help prevent defaults and facilitate accurate tax-credit reporting. The article explains the essential requirements, persons responsible for filing TDS returns, different statement forms, electronic filing procedures and consequences of non-compliance. The statement includes the information of the deductor, deductee, payment amounts, and tax deducted, etc. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is a component of the Indian income tax system, implemented to collect tax at the source where income is generated and to deposit it with the Income Tax Department. This tax deduction applies to payments such as salaries, rent, interest, and professional fees. To ensure compliance with regulations and avoid penalties, it is essential to file TDS returns accurately and on time. This article provides a detailed overview of the importance of filing TDS returns, the associated process, due dates, penalties, and key points to keep in mind to ensure compliance with the rules. What is TDS Return Filing? Filing a TDS return typically involves submitting a statement to the Income Tax Department, which provides details of the tax deducted at source and deposited with the government. It comprises the information regarding- TAN Number of the deductor PAN of the deductees Amount Paid/Credited Tax Deducted and Deposited Requirements before filing TDS returns Before filing your TDS return, it is essential to have these requirements/details ready in advance: Correct TAN: Tax Deduction Account Number is crucial for the deductor. PAN: Ensure you have valid PAN information for both the deductor and the deductee, and the PAN details should match official records. Tax Challan: Collect the challan number, BSR code, and payment date if advance tax or TDS payments have been made. TRACES and ITD Portal: An active account is required on the TRACES or Income Tax e-filing portal. Who should submit TDS returns? Any individual or entity accountable for deducting tax at source (TDS) is required to file a TDS return. The parties obligated to file TDS returns include: 1. Companies and LLPs: All registered companies and Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs) should file TDS returns for payments such as salaries, professional fees, and rent. 2. People and Partnership Businesses: Individuals and partnership firms, including sole proprietorships, must file TDS returns if they make any payments subject to TDS. This typically applies to individuals making significant payments, such as rent exceeding Rs 50,000 per month, or businesses hiring contractors and freelancers. 3. Government Units: Government offices and departments are accountable for deducting TDS and filing TDS returns. 4. Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs): HUFs must file TDS returns if their turnover, gross receipts, or sales from a business or profession exceed the financial limit set by the Income Tax Act. 5. The key principle is clear: Filing a TDS return is crucial once TDS has been deducted. Types of TDS Returns TDS Returns can be classified into various types based on the type of payment and the deductor. The most common types are specified as; Form 24Q: This form is filed by employers every quarter, providing details of the TDS deducted from their employees’ salaries. Form 26Q: This form is used for filing TDS on all payments made to residents, excluding salary. This includes payments such as professional fees, rent, interest, commissions, etc. Form 27Q: It is used for filing TDS on all payments (excluding salaries) made to Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and foreign businesses. Procedure for E-filing TDS Return TDS returns can be filed online by following the mentioned steps. The process for filing TDS is as follows: Obtain a proper TAN: TAN is a unique 10-character alphanumeric number that is mandatory for all deductors. It must be mentioned on all documents related to TDS. Deduct TDS timely: Deduct the correct TDS percentage at the time of making a payment that is subject to TDS. Deposit TDS to the Government: The deducted TDS should be deposited with the government via a bank using Challan 281. TDS should be deposited by the 7th of the subsequent month. File TDS Return Quarterly Prepare TDS and TCS Return: On your accounting software, use a TDS Return Preparation Utility (RPU) to prepare the return file in the mentioned format. Confirm the File: Use the TDS File Validation Utility (FVU) given by NSDL to ensure the file does not contain any errors. Uploading the TDS Return: Proceed to the Traces portal and upload the validated file with the use of your TAN. Issue Form 16/16A to the Deductee: After filing the return, you must provide a TDS certificate (Form 16 for salary, and Form 16A for payments other than salary) to the person whose tax you have deducted. Penalties for Non-submission of TDS Return On failure to comply with the TDS return filing, the below-mentioned penalties can be imposed- Late filing fees- Under section 234E, a fee of Rs 200 per day is levied for any delay in filing a return; this fee continues to accrue until the total amount of the fee equals the total TDS amount. Non-filing Tax penalty: If the TDS return has not been filed by the deadline or filed with the wrong information, then the assessing officer can charge a penalty of Rs 10,000, which can increase to Rs 1,00,000 u/s 271H. Cost disallowance: Under Section 40(a)(ia), if TDS was required to be deducted but was not, or if it was deducted but not deposited with the government, then the entire expenditure will be deemed as your income. This could significantly increase your tax liability. Interest: For late deduction, a 1% interest will be charged per month, and late payments will attract interest of 1.5% (per month) on the TDS amount.
