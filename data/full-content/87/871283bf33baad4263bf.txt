Changes in the Hospitality Sector under GST 2.0 and the 57th GST Council Meeting – All You Need to Know (Practical FAQs – Second Edition, November 2026) Introduction: The 56th GST Council meeting brought in significant reforms for the hospitality sector under GST 2.0, effective from 22nd September 2025. GST rates on certain accommodation, beauty and wellness services were reduced, but input tax credit (ITC) on these services was restricted at the same time. The aim is to lower the tax paid by customers, but businesses now need to re-examine their pricing and compliance. While guests get immediate relief through lower tax, hotels and restaurants face challenges in managing ITC reversals, absorbing costs and protecting margins. A proactive approach to compliance and costing will therefore be critical during this transition. This FAQ-based note explains the legal, procedural and practical aspects of GST 2.0 for the hospitality industry. Second Edition (November 2026): The 57th GST Council meeting held on 8th October 2026 did not change the GST rates on hotel accommodation, restaurant or catering services. It did, however, make the following changes: hotels, restaurants and outdoor caterers paying GST at 5% can now avail limited ITC on procurements from suppliers in the same line of business; an option has been introduced for passenger transport by electric vehicles; the tax on delivery services has been shifted to e-commerce operators; and used cooking oil and other scrap have been brought under reverse charge, TDS and registration rules. These recommendations were notified through Notification Nos. 3 to 7/2026-Central Tax, Notification Nos. 2 to 10/2026-Central Tax (Rate) and Circular Nos. 257 to 266/2026-GST. Most of them apply from 1st November 2026. In this edition, Part A retains the FAQs on GST 2.0 (56th GST Council meeting). Questions whose answers have changed are marked [Updated – 2nd Edition], and the update is added at the end of the answer. Part B contains new FAQs on the changes arising from the 57th GST Council meeting. Part A – GST 2.0 (56th GST Council Meeting): Changes effective from 22nd September 2025 Q1. What is the effective date of the GST 2.0 changes for the hospitality sector? [Updated – 2nd Edition] A: The new rates and restrictions apply from 22nd September 2025, as per Notification No. 15/2025-Central Tax (Rate). Update (2nd Edition): The changes arising from the 57th GST Council meeting apply from 1st November 2026, except: e-invoicing of reverse charge self-invoices (from 1st January 2027), automatic suspension of registration for return defaults (from 1st April 2027), and the proposed amendments to the Act (which will apply only when enacted and notified). Please refer to Part B (Q.34 onwards) for the changes arising from the 57th GST Council meeting. Q2. What is the main GST change for hotels from 22nd September 2025? A: Room tariffs up to ₹7,500 per unit per day (or equivalent) now attract 5% GST without input tax credit (ITC), down from 12% with ITC. Tariffs above ₹7,500 continue to attract 18% with ITC. Q3. What does “without ITC” mean in this context? A: It means that for room tariffs up to ₹7,500 per unit per day, the hotel cannot claim ITC of the GST paid on inputs, input services and capital goods used for that service. So, although the GST rate is lower, the GST paid on inputs becomes a cost that the hotel must build into its pricing. Q4. Is input tax credit available to a business that resells accommodation services? [Updated – 2nd Edition] A:Up to 31st October 2026: ITC on accommodation services was available only where the value of supply of the unit of accommodation was above ₹7,500 per day. In all other cases, ITC was restricted. Unlike passenger transport services, accommodation services had no relaxation allowing ITC on procurements from the same line of business. From 1st November 2026: Under Notification No. 6/2026-Central Tax (Rate), a supplier of hotel accommodation paying GST at 5% can avail ITC on hotel accommodation services procured from another hotel in the same line of business. The ITC is capped at 2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST (5% in total). Any tax charged above this limit is not eligible. In our view, a business that resells accommodation as its own supply of accommodation services (HSN 9963) at 5% can avail ITC on rooms procured from hotels, subject to the 5% cap and the conditions of Section 16. Where the business only acts as an intermediary or tour operator and does not supply accommodation on its own account, this relaxation does not apply. ITC on all other goods and services used for supplying accommodation continues to be restricted (see Q.36 to Q.39). Q5. Can a recipient avail ITC on accommodation services where the applicable GST rate is 5%? A: GST 2.0 does not change the law for the recipient. As long as the services are received in the course or furtherance of business and all the conditions of Section 16 are met, the registered recipient can avail and use the ITC, subject to Section 17. Q6. Is there any change in the rate of tax for restaurants under GST 2.0? A: No, GST 2.0 does not change the rate of tax on restaurant services. However, the rate on most food and beverages for human consumption, when supplied as goods, has been rationalised to 5%. Q7. How are the time of supply and the rate of tax determined for transitional supplies (i.e., where the supply, invoice or payment took place on or before 21st September 2025 and the rest after that date)? A: Section 14 of the CGST Act, 2017 determines the time of supply, and hence the applicable rate, for such transitional transactions, as follows: Scenario Condition Time of Supply Applicable Rate Services supplied before 22nd September 2025 (i) Invoice issued & payment received after change Earlier of date of payment or Invoice New Rate (ii) Invoice issued before change & payment received after change Date of Invoice Old Rate (iii) Payment received before change & invoice issued after change Date of Payment Old Rate Services supplied after 22nd September 2025 (i) Payment received after change & invoice issued before change Date of Payment New Rate (ii) Invoice issued & payment received before change Earlier of date of payment or Invoice Old Rate (iii) Invoice issued after change & payment received before change Date of Invoice New Rate Q8. Does this amendment affect hostels and paying guest (PG) facilities? A: The 56th GST Council recommended, and the notification has amended, the tax rates for accommodation services classifiable under HSN 9963. Therefore, it is first necessary to determine whether the underlying supply is accommodation service under HSN 9963 or renting of residential property under HSN 9972. The GST liability should then be assessed accordingly. Q9. What GST applies to accommodation services provided by hostels and paying guest facilities? A: Accommodation services provided by hostels and paying guest facilities are exempt from GST where the value of supply is up to ₹20,000 per person per month, provided the accommodation is supplied for a continuous period of at least 90 days. GST 2.0 does not affect this exemption. Q10. What is the amendment proposed in the 56th GST Council meeting for restaurant services? A: Existing law:Restaurants operating in specified premises: can charge 18% and avail ITC. Restaurants operating in non-specified premises: must charge 5% without ITC.Proposed clarification: Stand-alone restaurants, including a restaurant that is an additional place of business but is independent of the specified premises, cannot declare themselves as specified premises. They must charge 5% without ITC. The option to charge 18% GST is therefore available only to restaurants operating within a property that qualifies as specified premises. Q11. What are specified premises? A: From 1st April 2025, a property has to determine every financial year whether it qualifies as specified premises. A property qualifies as specified premises for a financial year if it meets either of the following conditions: a) In the preceding financial year, any unit of accommodation in the premises was supplied at a value above ₹7,500 per unit per day or equivalent; or b) The registered person has voluntarily declared the property as specified premises (whether or not any unit of accommodation in it is supplied at a value above ₹7,500), between 1st January and 31st March of the preceding financial year (for an existing registration) or within 15 days of the acknowledgment of the registration application (for a new registration). Q12. What is the GST rate for a restaurant in specified premises serving guests staying in rooms valued up to ₹7,500, or walk-in customers? A: Food and beverages supplied by a restaurant operating within specified premises are taxed at 18%. So, even if the room tariff is taxed at 5% (as the value of the unit is up to ₹7,500 per day), the restaurant’s supplies are taxed at 18%, provided the property is classified as specified premises. Q13. What is the GST rate on complimentary breakfast provided along with the room? A: Where food and beverages are supplied as a package along with accommodation, the package is a composite supply. The principal supply in the package has to be identified, and the entire package is taxed at the rate applicable to that principal supply. Since accommodation is usually the principal supply, the GST rate applicable to accommodation will apply to the entire package, including food and beverages. Q14. Do hotels need to reverse ITC proportionately when they have both types of rooms (up to ₹7,500 and above ₹7,500)? [Updated – 2nd Edition] A: Yes. ITC can be availed only in proportion to the turnover from units charged at 18% GST. Units at 5% are treated as exempt supplies for ITC apportionment. The reversal should be made as per Rule 42 of the CGST Rules for inputs and input services, and as per Rule 43 for capital goods. Update (2nd Edition): From 1st November 2026, for 5% rooms, ITC on accommodation services procured from another hotel is allowed up to 5% (see Q.36 to Q.39). In our view, such ITC should be identified invoice-wise against the 5% rooms and availed to the extent permitted, rather than being pooled with common credit for apportionment under Rules 42 and 43. Q15. Why is ITC reversal required even though GST at 5% is paid on rooms up to ₹7,500? A: As per Explanation (iv) of Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate), where a rate is prescribed subject to the condition that credit of input tax charged on goods or services used in supplying the service has not been taken, such ITC-restricted turnover is treated as exempt turnover. Q16. How are additional bed charges treated for GST purposes in hotel accommodation? A: If the room tariff, including additional bed charges, exceeds ₹7,500 per day, GST is applicable at 18% with ITC. If it is ₹7,500 or less per day, GST is applicable at 5% without ITC. Q17. Is ITC reversal required for restaurants operating in specified premises because of GST 2.0? A: Restaurants operating within specified premises must charge GST at 18% with ITC. However, where such restaurants supply food to in-house guests under the American Plan or Continental Plan and the room value is up to ₹7,500, the related ITC may not be available because of Explanation (iv) of Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate), as amended. Therefore, ITC directly relating to such supplies cannot be availed, and common credit has to be reversed under Section 17 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rules 42 and 43. Q18. Has the rate of tax on banquet services (renting of premises along with food) changed under GST 2.0? [Updated – 2nd Edition] A: No, GST 2.0 does not change the rate of tax on banquet services. Banquet services are a composite supply of renting of premises together with food and beverages. The applicable GST continues to be: 18% with ITC – where the services are provided in specified premises or by a supplier located in specified premises. 5% without ITC – in all other cases. Update (2nd Edition): The rate remains unchanged. However, from 1st November 2026, a supplier of composite outdoor catering along with renting of premises (halls, pandals, shamianas) at non-specified premises, taxed at 5%, can avail ITC on input services procured from another outdoor caterer, capped at 5% (see Q.42 and Q.43). Q19. Under GST 2.0, is there any change in the rate of tax where a hall is rented out only for use of the premises (without food or catering)? A: No. Where the hall is given purely on rent without any food or catering, the supply is classified under Service Code 9972, and GST continues at 18% with ITC. Q20. Do we need to reverse ITC already claimed because of the GST rate cut? A: No. ITC already availed is a vested right. Reversal is required only if the supplies become wholly exempt, which is not the case here. Q21. Can existing ITC be used to pay GST at 5%? A: Yes. The restriction applies only to availing fresh ITC. Any balance in the electronic credit ledger can still be used to pay GST at 5%. There is no provision for the lapse of existing credit. Q22. Can a supplier of accommodation services choose to charge GST at 18% with ITC when the tariff per unit per day is ₹7,500 or less? A: No, this option is not available. As per Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate), where the value of accommodation per unit per day is ₹7,500 or less, GST must be charged at 5% without ITC. The supplier cannot opt to pay 18% with ITC. Q23. What is the GST treatment for beauty, spa, gym and physical well-being services in hotels? A: The GST rate has been reduced from 18% with ITC to 5% without ITC, even where these services are provided within specified premises. No ITC can be claimed on inputs, input services or capital goods used for these services. Q24. What GST rate applies to an e-commerce operator providing beauty and physical well-being services? A: It depends on the nature of the services supplied by the e-commerce operator. If it supplies beauty and physical well-being services itself, the rate is 5%. If it provides only support or facilitation services, the rate depends on the classification of those services. Q25. What are the changes in passenger transport services (like cab rentals by hotels)? [Updated – 2nd Edition] A: Where the cost of fuel is included in the consideration, the rate continues to be 5%, with ITC restricted except on services procured from another supplier in the same line of business. Alternatively, the supplier may charge GST with full ITC (on vehicle purchase, insurance, repairs, etc.); this rate has increased from 12% to 18%. Update (2nd Edition): From 1st November 2026, new entries cover transport of passengers by, and renting of, electric vehicles where the battery charging cost is included in the consideration. The supplier can opt for 5% with restricted ITC or 18% with full ITC (see Q.44). Further, the reverse charge entry for renting of passenger vehicles to a body corporate now applies where the supplier does not charge 18% (see Q.45). Q26. How are aerated and caffeinated beverages affected under GST 2.0? A: Earlier, they attracted 28% GST plus 12% cess. They now attract 40% GST, with no separate cess. When supplied as part of restaurant services, they are taxed at 18% in specified premises or 5% in non-specified premises. Q27. Do these changes affect liquor and tobacco sales in hotels? A: Liquor for human consumption remains outside GST and is taxed under State excise laws. Tobacco products continue to attract GST. Q28. How are package bookings (such as room with hall, or room with food) treated? A: Hotels need to segregate supplies carefully, as accommodation up to ₹7,500 and food and beverage, banquet or conference services may attract different GST rates. The ITC reversal rules also apply. Q29. Will customers see lower bills after GST 2.0? A: Yes, especially for rooms up to ₹7,500. However, since ITC is restricted, hotels may increase base prices to recover the lost credit, so customers may not get the full benefit of the 7-percentage-point rate reduction. Q30. What compliance challenges should hotels prepare for? [Updated – 2nd Edition] A: Hotels should prepare for the following: ITC tracking and reversal (Rule 42/43 apportionment). Repricing to balance higher costs against customers’ price sensitivity. Correct invoicing, by updating billing software and POS systems, to avoid disputes on the applicable rates. Separate tracking of revenue streams (rooms, restaurant, spa, etc.). Training staff on the new rates. Reviewing contracts with corporates, airlines and event clients to reflect the GST 2.0 changes. Tagging same-line procurements (rooms, restaurant or catering services from other suppliers) separately in the purchase register, to claim ITC up to the 5% cap from 1st November 2026. Updating vendor masters and tax codes for cab hire (reverse charge where the operator does not charge 18%), EV transport and delivery services. Generating e-invoices for reverse charge self-invoices from 1st January 2027, where e-invoicing applies to the hotel. Reconciling TDS credits on sale of used cooking oil and scrap with GSTR-7A, and regularly checking the GST registration status of vendors. Q31. What is the impact of these amendments on cross-charge transactions within hotel groups? [Updated – 2nd Edition] A: Up to 21st September 2025, specified premises had flexibility in valuing cross-charges between group entities because of the proviso to Rule 28 of the CGST Rules, 2017 read with Section 15 of the CGST Act, 2017. However, from 22nd September 2025, specified premises that have units of accommodation valued up to ₹7,500 per day, or that provide beauty and physical well-being services, can no longer use this proviso, as they are not entitled to full ITC. Such groups should therefore be careful when determining the value of supplies between related persons. Update (2nd Edition): Circular No. 260/06/2026-GST clarifies that common input services received at a central office for use by other units (distinct persons) must be distributed through an ISD. The reversal of ITC relating to 5% rooms or spa services is the responsibility of each recipient hotel, not the ISD (see Q.50). Groups should review their cross-charge and ISD arrangements together. Q32. What is the impact of these amendments on tour operators? [Updated – 2nd Edition] A: There are no direct amendments for tour operators. However, the lower GST rates in the hospitality industry discussed above will reduce the tour operator’s cost to the extent it books units of accommodation valued up to ₹7,500. Update (2nd Edition): From 1st November 2026, passenger transportation by helicopter on a seat-share basis, embarking from or terminating at an airport or helipad in the North-Eastern States or at Bagdogra (West Bengal), is exempt (Notification No. 7/2026-Central Tax (Rate)). Tour packages in these regions should be repriced accordingly. Tour operators who supply accommodation on their own account should also examine Q.4 and Q.36. Q33. What about bookings made before 22nd September 2025 where the stay or check-in is after 22nd September 2025? A: The rate depends on the time of supply rules. Since both the service and the invoice are after the rate change, the new rate applies. Part B – Changes on account of the 57th GST Council Meeting (effective from 1st November 2026, unless otherwise stated) Q34. What are the key changes for the hospitality sector arising from the 57th GST Council meeting, and from when do they apply? A: The 57th GST Council meeting held on 8th October 2026 recommended changes that have been notified through notifications and circulars dated 26th and 27th October 2026. The key changes relevant to the hospitality sector are summarised below: Change Notification / Circular Effective from ITC on hotel accommodation procured from another hotel (5% rooms), capped at 5% Notification No. 6/2026-CT(R), entry 7(i) 1 Nov 2026 ITC on restaurant services procured from another restaurant (non-specified premises) Notification No. 6/2026-CT(R), entry 7(ii) 1 Nov 2026 ITC on outdoor catering procured from another outdoor caterer, including composite catering with renting of premises, capped at 5% Notification No. 6/2026-CT(R), entries 7(iv) and 7(v) 1 Nov 2026 EV passenger transport and EV renting (battery charging included): option of 5% or 18% Notification No. 6/2026-CT(R), entries 8(vib), 10(ib), 10(ic) 1 Nov 2026 Reverse charge on renting of passenger vehicles aligned to 18% Notification No. 8/2026-CT(R) 1 Nov 2026 Delivery services through e-commerce operators: 5% without ITC, payable by the ECO Notification Nos. 6/2026-CT(R) and 9/2026-CT(R) 1 Nov 2026 Used cooking oil and scrap: reverse charge, registration and TDS Notification No. 4/2026-CT(R); Notification Nos. 6 and 7/2026-CT 1 Nov 2026 Post-sale discounts through credit notes Notification No. 3/2026-CT (Sections 153, 154 of Finance Act, 2026) 1 Nov 2026 Blocking of ITC – objection and hearing (Rule 86A) Notification No. 5/2026-CT 1 Nov 2026 E-invoicing of reverse charge self-invoices Notification No. 4/2026-CT 1 Jan 2027 Automatic suspension of registration for return defaults Notification No. 5/2026-CT 1 Apr 2027 ISD, registration and adjudication clarifications Circular Nos. 257, 258, 259 and 260/2026-GST Date of issue (26 Oct 2026) Q35. Has the rate of GST on hotel accommodation, restaurant services or outdoor catering changed after the 57th GST Council meeting? A: No. Room tariffs up to ₹7,500 per unit per day continue to attract 5% with restricted ITC, and tariffs above ₹7,500 attract 18% with ITC. Restaurant services in specified premises continue at 18% with ITC, and other restaurant services at 5% without ITC. The change is limited to the scope of the ITC restriction: ITC is now allowed on certain procurements from suppliers in the same line of business. Q36. A hotel charging 5% on its rooms books its guests in another hotel when it is overbooked. Can it claim ITC of the GST charged by the other hotel? A: Yes, from 1st November 2026. Under Notification No. 6/2026-Central Tax (Rate), hotels paying GST at 5% can avail ITC on hotel accommodation services procured from another hotel operating in the same line of business. Typical cases are overflow bookings, rooms block-booked from other properties for group bookings, and rooms sourced for guests during renovation. ITC on all other goods and services used for providing accommodation, such as furniture, rent, maintenance, housekeeping, linen and utilities, continues to be restricted. Q37. What if the hotel from which rooms are procured charges GST at 18% (room tariff above ₹7,500)? A: Where the procuring hotel supplies the rooms at a tariff of ₹7,500 or less (at 5%), ITC is restricted to 2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST (5% in total). The tax charged above this limit is not eligible and becomes a cost to the procuring hotel. In our view, such ITC cannot be availed by a hotel registered in a different State, so the relaxation is useful mainly for procurements within the same State. Q38. From when can ITC on same-line procurements be claimed, and what about invoices covering stays before and after 1st November 2026? A: The relaxation applies from 1st November 2026 and is not retrospective. In our view, ITC should be claimed only for inward supplies received on or after 1st November 2026. Where an invoice covers a stay that straddles 31st October and 1st November 2026, it is advisable to claim ITC only for the portion of the stay from 1st November 2026 and to document the working. Q39. Does the ITC on rooms procured from another hotel need to be reversed under Rule 42/43, since 5% turnover is treated as exempt turnover? A: As explained in Q.15, turnover taxed at 5% without ITC is treated as exempt turnover for Rule 42/43. However, the notification itself permits ITC on the same-line procurement used for supplying such rooms. In our view, this ITC should be identified invoice-wise as directly relating to the 5% supply and availed to the extent permitted (subject to the 5% cap), instead of being included in common credit and reversed. As there is no explicit clarification on this point, hotels should maintain clear records linking such procurements to the bookings for which they were used. Q40. What ITC is now available to restaurants operating in non-specified premises? A: From 1st November 2026, such restaurants can avail ITC on restaurant services procured from another restaurant operating in the same line of business, that is, restaurant services other than those provided at specified premises. ITC on raw materials, kitchen equipment, rent and housekeeping continues to be restricted. Two points require attention. First, restaurant services procured from a restaurant in specified premises (taxed at 18%) do not fall within the same line of business as defined. Second, food items purchased as goods, for example from a central kitchen, are inputs and not restaurant services, so the relaxation does not extend to them. Q41. Is there any change for catering services by Indian Railways, IRCTC or their licensees? A: Yes. Catering service providers supplying food and beverages on trains or at platforms can now avail ITC on inputs and input services procured from suppliers in the same line of business, namely Indian Railways, IRCTC or their licensees. ITC on other goods and services continues to be restricted. Q42. An outdoor caterer subcontracts part of an event to another caterer. Can it claim ITC on the sub-caterer’s invoice? A: Yes, from 1st November 2026. Outdoor caterers paying 5% at non-specified premises can avail ITC on input services procured from another outdoor caterer. Where the sub-caterer charges more than 5%, ITC is restricted to 5% (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST). ITC on raw materials, utensils, transportation and event space rentals continues to be restricted. Q43. A banquet or convention hall at non-specified premises provides the hall with food (composite supply at 5%) and outsources the food to an outdoor caterer. Is ITC available? A: Yes. Under entry 7(v) as amended, suppliers of composite outdoor catering along with renting of premises can avail ITC on input services procured from another outdoor caterer, capped at 5%. Banquets at specified premises taxed at 18% continue to be eligible for full ITC, as before. Q44. Hotels provide airport transfers using electric vehicles where the battery charging cost is included in the fare. What is the GST rate? A: New entries have been inserted for transport of passengers by electric vehicles and for renting of electric vehicles with operators, where the battery charging cost is included in the consideration. The supplier can choose either: 5% (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) with restricted ITC. ITC on the purchase of EVs and general expenses is not allowed, but ITC on services procured from another supplier in the same line of business is allowed, up to 5%; or 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST) with full ITC, subject to Sections 16 and 17. Hotels that own their EV fleet may find the 18% option better as ITC on the vehicles becomes available. Hotels that outsource transfers may prefer 5%. Where transfers are bundled into a room package, the composite supply principles in Q.13 continue to apply. A similar option is available for renting of EVs used for transport of goods. Q45. A hotel company hires cabs from proprietorship or partnership travel operators. Does reverse charge apply? A: Notification No. 8/2026-Central Tax (Rate) amends the reverse charge entry for renting of passenger vehicles (with fuel cost included) supplied by a person other than a body corporate to a body corporate. From 1st November 2026, reverse charge applies where the supplier does not charge CGST at 9% (18% in total). Earlier the reference was to 6% (12% in total), which no longer matched the rates after GST 2.0. Accordingly, if the cab operator charges 18% on its invoice, the hotel pays the GST to the operator. If the operator does not charge 18% (for example, it charges 5%), the hotel, being a body corporate, must pay GST under reverse charge and not to the operator. ITC on such tax is subject to Section 17(5)(b). Hotels should obtain a declaration of the rate charged from each cab vendor and update their tax codes. Q46. How do the changes on delivery services affect restaurants selling through food delivery platforms? A: From 1st November 2026, e-commerce operators (ECOs) are liable to pay GST on delivery services (other than postal and courier) provided through their platforms, except where the delivery service provider is itself liable to register (Notification No. 9/2026-Central Tax (Rate)). Delivery services, including delivery of goods sold through ECOs, attract 5% without ITC. Restaurant services (other than from specified premises) supplied through ECOs were already taxed under Section 9(5), with the ECO paying the tax. The delivery fee charged by the platform to the customer is the platform’s liability, and the restaurant does not pay GST on it. Where a restaurant delivers through its own staff and the delivery charge forms part of its order, it remains part of the restaurant’s own supply. Restaurants should check how the platform shows the delivery fee on its invoices and reports. Q47. Hotels and restaurants sell used cooking oil to collectors. What has changed? A: From 1st November 2026, used cooking oil (heading 1518), plastic waste (3915), tyre scrap (4004), metal scrap (Chapters 72 to 81) and e-waste (8549) are subject to new rules: Registered hotel or restaurant selling these goods: it continues to charge GST on its tax invoice. Where the buyer is registered and the contract value exceeds ₹2.5 lakh, the buyer deducts TDS at 2% (1% CGST + 1% SGST, or 2% IGST) under Section 51. The TDS is credited to the seller’s electronic cash ledger and should be reconciled with GSTR-7A. Unregistered eatery selling to a registered buyer: the buyer pays GST under reverse charge (Notification No. 4/2026-Central Tax (Rate)). Registration: the registration exemption for persons making only reverse charge supplies is no longer available to suppliers of these goods (Notification No. 6/2026-Central Tax). A supplier whose turnover exceeds the threshold must register. These rules also apply when hotels dispose of e-waste, plastic waste or metal scrap, for example after a renovation. Q48. Hotels give year-end volume rebates to corporate clients and travel agents. How should these be handled from 1st November 2026? A: Sections 153 and 154 of the Finance Act, 2026, in force from 1st November 2026, allow post-sale discounts to reduce the value of supply through a GST credit note, provided the recipient reverses the proportionate ITC. The earlier conditions that the discount be agreed before the supply and linked to specific invoices no longer apply. Corporate clients who availed ITC on room or banquet bills (see Q.5) must reverse ITC on the credit note. Similarly, where a hotel receives volume rebates from its own suppliers through credit notes, it must reverse the ITC it availed. Rate agreements with corporates and travel agents should include a clause requiring ITC reversal, and credit notes should be issued within the time limit under Section 34(2). Q49. Do hotels need to generate e-invoices for reverse charge self-invoices? A: Yes, from 1st January 2027, where e-invoicing applies to the hotel. Notification No. 4/2026-Central Tax extends e-invoicing to inward supplies from unregistered persons on which the recipient pays GST under reverse charge, including import of services. Typical hotel cases are subscriptions to overseas booking engines, channel managers and property management software, brand or marketing fees paid to overseas franchisors, rent paid to unregistered landlords of immovable property, and legal services from unregistered advocates. The department may question ITC where the self-invoice is not generated through the e-invoicing system. The reverse charge liability in Table 3.1(d) of GSTR-3B should be reconciled each month with GSTR-2B and the e-invoice data. Q50. How does Circular No. 260/06/2026-GST on Input Service Distributors (ISD) affect hotel groups? A: Where a head office or regional office receives common input services, such as brand marketing, loyalty programmes, central reservation systems, IT and audit, which are used by hotels registered as distinct persons, it must register as an ISD and distribute the credit only to the units to which it relates. A separate ISD registration may be taken for each business vertical or place of business in a State. The ISD must distribute the entire credit, showing eligible and ineligible ITC separately, in the month in which it ascertains that the credit is prima facie eligible after the invoices appear in GSTR-6A. Reversals under Sections 17(1), 17(2) and 17(4), including those relating to 5% rooms and spa services, are the responsibility of the recipient hotel and not the ISD. Invoices issued under Rule 54(1A) to transfer reverse charge credit to the ISD do not form part of turnover. Incorrect distribution attracts a penalty under Section 122(1)(ix) of ₹10,000 or the ITC distributed, whichever is higher. Q51. A hotel is opening a new property or adding a place of business. What has changed in registration? A: Under amended Rule 19, only a change in the principal place of business needs the officer’s approval, and it is deemed approved if the officer does not decide within 15 days (or 7 days from receipt of clarifications). Other amendments, such as adding an additional place of business, are approved automatically after portal validation. The amendment must be filed within 15 days of the change. Circular No. 257/03/2026-GST provides that officers must not demand documents beyond those prescribed in FORM GST REG-01, such as Udyam certificates or shop licences, and must not reject an application merely because the premises are residential or the electricity connection is domestic. This is relevant for homestays and serviced apartments. Aadhaar-authenticated applications are generally processed within 7 working days, and delays may be escalated to the jurisdictional Chief Commissioner or Commissioner. Q52. What should hotels note about the new rules on suspension and cancellation of registration for return defaults? A: Registration may be suspended automatically by the portal for non-filing of returns (6 consecutive months for monthly filers, 2 consecutive tax periods for quarterly filers) or for not furnishing bank account details under Rule 10A. In our understanding, automatic suspension applies from 1st April 2027. During suspension, tax invoices cannot be issued, and the registration is deemed cancelled if the defaults are not cured within 30 days. Hotels work with many small vendors such as laundries, housekeeping contractors and caterers. If a vendor’s registration is suspended or cancelled, ITC on its invoices may be at risk, so vendor registration status should be checked regularly. Q53. Can the department block the ITC of a hotel, and what remedy is available? A: Under amended Rule 86A, the proper officer (earlier only the Commissioner or an officer not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner) can block ITC through FORM GST DRC-26. The block lasts for one year or until the officer allows the credit earlier. The hotel can file an objection in FORM GST DRC-27, and the officer decides after a hearing through FORM GST DRC-28. Hotels should reconcile ITC with GSTR-2B and file objections promptly with supporting documents. Q54. What changes apply to small restaurants under the composition scheme? A: The due date of GSTR-4 (annual return) has been extended from 30th April to 30th June. FORM GST CMP-08 now shows goods, services and services supplied through an ECO under Section 9(5) separately. Composition taxpayers do not pay tax on Section 9(5) supplies, as the ECO pays it, but the value of such supplies counts towards aggregate turnover. Restaurants selling through food delivery platforms should therefore monitor their turnover against the composition threshold. Q55. Many hotels face show cause notices on specified premises, ITC reversals and classification. Do the new circulars provide any procedural safeguards? A: Yes. Circular No. 258/04/2026-GST requires: (a) a pre-notice intimation in FORM GST DRC-01A; (b) notices that disclose the basis for alleging fraud or suppression; (c) no parallel proceedings by CGST and SGST authorities on the same subject; (d) a maximum of three adjournments, with virtual hearings permitted; and (e) reasoned (speaking) orders that deal with each submission. Circular No. 259/05/2026-GST clarifies that the pre-deposit is 10% of the tax in dispute, and that it is refundable through FORM GST RFD-01 (“on account of order”) after a favourable order, even if the department appeals, unless the order is stayed. Hotels should reply to every notice in full, request a personal hearing in writing and maintain a litigation tracker. Q56. For Rule 42 reversal, do exempt supplies include room accommodation at 5%, passenger transport at 5%, etc.? A: Yes, in our view. Under Rule 42 the credit attributable to exempt supplies is D1 = (E ÷ F) × C2, where E is the aggregate value of exempt supplies and F is the total turnover of the registered person in the State during the tax period. The position on what goes into “E” is as follows: 1. Statutory meaning. Section 2(47) defines exempt supply as supply of goods or services that attracts nil rate of tax or is wholly exempt, and includes non-taxable supplies. Under Section 17(3), the value of exempt supply also includes supplies on which the recipient pays tax under reverse charge, transactions in securities, sale of land and, subject to Schedule II, paragraph 5(b), sale of building. A supply taxed at 5% is a taxable supply and is not exempt on these definitions. 2. Deemed treatment through the rate notification. Where Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate) prescribes a rate subject to the condition that credit of input tax on goods or services used in supplying the service has not been taken, Explanation (iv) of the notification applies (see Q.15). The turnover on which ITC is restricted is considered as exempt turnover for apportionment of credit. Therefore, the 5% “without ITC” supplies are included in “E” even though tax is actually paid on them. 3. What is included in “E” and what is not: Supply Treatment for Rule 42/43 Reason Room accommodation with value up to ₹7,500 per unit per day (5% without ITC) Included in E ITC-restricted turnover (Q.14, Q.15) Beauty, spa, gym and physical well-being services (5% without ITC) Included in E ITC-restricted turnover (Q.23) Restaurant services in non-specified premises, outdoor catering and banquets at 5% without ITC Included in E ITC-restricted turnover (Q.10, Q.18) Passenger transport (cab, airport transfer) at 5% without ITC Included in E ITC-restricted turnover (Q.25) Same supplies where the supplier is permitted to opt for 18% with ITC (cab hire, EV transport) Not included in E; taxable supply No ITC restriction applies once 18% is chosen (Q.25, Q.44) Rooms above ₹7,500, restaurant and banquet in specified premises, hall rent (18% with ITC) Not included in E; taxable supply ITC is not restricted Sale of liquor for human consumption Included in E Non-taxable supply, which is exempt supply under Section 2(47) (Q.27) 4. Order of computation. ITC on inputs and services used exclusively for 5% supplies is not availed at all (T2 in Rule 42(1)). ITC on goods and services used exclusively for 18% supplies is availed in full. Only the common credit (C2) is apportioned using the E ÷ F ratio, with 5% of C2 going to non-business use as D2. For capital goods, Rule 43 applies in the same way over the useful life of five years. 5. Value to be taken. The value of the 5% supplies in E and F is the transaction value under Section 15, excluding GST. The ratio is computed GSTIN-wise (State-wise) for each tax period, and the final recomputation for the financial year is made under Rule 42(2) by the due date of the September return, together with interest on any short reversal. 6. Illustration (figures are only for illustration). A hotel in a State has the following monthly turnover and common credit: Particulars Turnover (₹ lakh) In “E”? Rooms above ₹7,500, 18% 60 No Restaurant in specified premises, 18% 10 No Rooms up to ₹7,500, 5% without ITC 30 Yes Spa and gym, 5% without ITC 5 Yes Total turnover in the State (F) 105 Exempt value (E) = 30 + 5 35 If the common credit C2 is ₹10 lakh, D1 = (35 ÷ 105) × 10 = ₹3.33 lakh is reversed, and D2 = 5% of ₹10 lakh = ₹0.50 lakh is reversed towards non-business use. The balance is eligible credit. 7. Same-line ITC from 1st November 2026. As explained in Q.39, ITC on rooms, restaurant services or outdoor catering procured from a supplier in the same line of business should, in our view, be identified invoice-wise with the 5% supply and kept out of the common credit pool. Only the credit that remains common is subject to the E ÷ F ratio. 8. Points of caution. The deeming under Explanation (iv) applies for apportionment of ITC only. The 5% supplies continue to be taxable supplies for all other purposes, including payment of tax, invoicing and returns. Reversal is to be made through Table 4(B)(1) of GSTR-3B (reversal under Rules 42 and 43), and the working should be maintained for each month and for the annual recomputation. Q57. The 57th GST Council has also recommended ITC in the same line of business for gym and fitness services. Can gyms, spas and fitness centres avail this benefit from 1st November 2026? A: Not yet, in our view. As on the date of this edition, the benefit has been recommended but is not yet notified for gym and fitness services. 1. What the Council recommended. The 57th Council recommended limited ITC in the same line of business for restaurant and outdoor catering services, hotel accommodation services with value up to ₹7,500 per unit per day, and gym and fitness services, “in the same manner as is currently available” for passenger transportation, tour operator and renting of motor vehicle services. The recommendation names gym and fitness services. It does not name beauty, spa, salon or other physical well-being services. 2. What has been notified. Notification No. 6/2026-Central Tax (Rate), effective from 1st November 2026, amends the entries for hotel accommodation, restaurant services, railway catering, outdoor catering, electric vehicle transport and renting, and delivery services (see Q.34). The entry for beauty and physical well-being services, including gyms and health clubs, was not amended in this notification. A recommendation of the Council has no effect until it is given legal force by a notification under Section 11 of the CGST Act. Therefore, the condition that credit of input tax charged on goods or services used in supplying the service has not been taken, read with Explanation (iv), continues to apply to gym, spa and fitness services at 5%. 3. Practical consequence. Until the gym and fitness entry is amended: – ITC should not be availed in GSTR-3B on any procurement relating to gym, spa or fitness services, including services procured from another gym or fitness operator. – The turnover of these services continues to be treated as exempt turnover in the Rule 42/43 computation (see Q.56). – The position in Q.23 remains unchanged: 5% without ITC, even inside specified premises. 4. How the benefit is likely to work when notified. The recommendation refers to the manner in which ITC is available for passenger transportation and similar services. Based on the notification issued for hotel accommodation and outdoor catering, we expect that: – ITC will be limited to input services procured from another supplier of gym and fitness services (for example, a hotel that engages a third-party fitness operator to run its gym, or a fitness studio that engages another studio or trainer on a sub-contract basis). – ITC on equipment and other capital goods, rent, electricity, consumables, linen and housekeeping will continue to be restricted. – Credit may be capped where the supplier charges tax above 2.5% central tax (5% in total), as in the entries for hotel accommodation and outdoor catering. – Whether spas, salons and other beauty services will be covered depends on the wording of the amending entry, as they are not named in the recommendation. The entry has to be read when it is notified. This is our expectation on the pattern of the existing entries, and it should be confirmed against the notification when issued. 5. Related provisions. Section 17(5)(b) separately blocks ITC on inward supply of membership of a club, health and fitness centre (for example, gym memberships taken for employees) and, except for the same category of outward supply, on beauty treatment and health services. The Council recommendation concerns the supplier of gym and fitness services, and does not remove this block for recipients. Where gym access is provided free of charge to room guests as part of the room tariff, it is not a separate supply and is taxed along with the accommodation (see Q.13). 6. Recommended action. Tag purchases from other gym and fitness service providers separately in the purchase register, as suggested in Q.30, so that credit can be claimed promptly once the notification is issued. Since the relaxation for hotels and caterers has been made applicable only from 1st November 2026 and is not retrospective (see Q.38), no credit should be taken for earlier periods unless the notification specifically provides for it. Conclusion GST 2.0 marks a major shift for the hospitality industry. It gives customers immediate tax relief, but requires businesses to sharpen their compliance, costing and operations. With lower rates on accommodation and wellness services balanced by restrictions on ITC, hotels, restaurants and allied service providers must recalibrate their pricing, make accurate ITC reversals and strengthen internal systems. The transitional provisions, classification issues and compliance challenges call for advance planning and clear communication with clients and customers. Businesses that quickly align their practices with the revised framework will be better placed to protect margins, stay competitive and deliver value as GST continues to evolve. Second Edition: The 57th GST Council meeting has not altered the rate structure but has eased the ITC restriction on same-line procurements by hotels, restaurants and caterers, while adding new compliance on cab hire, delivery services, scrap and used cooking oil, and e-invoicing of reverse charge self-invoices. Hospitality businesses should update their purchase tagging, ERP tax codes and vendor checks from 1st November 2026 to benefit from the relaxation without inviting disputes. ****** For feedback or queries, please email the authors: CA.
