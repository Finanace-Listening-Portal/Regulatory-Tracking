(Delhi High Court) The Delhi High Court dismissed the Revenue’s appeal in ITA 741/2018 concerning Assessment Year 2012-13. The appeal challenged the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal’s order dated 1 January 2018 directing the exclusion of three companies from the transfer pricing comparables selected for Omniglobe Information Technologies (India) Pvt. Ltd. The assessee was a wholly owned subsidiary of a United States company and provided IT-enabled business process outsourcing services, including phone activation and local number portability. It filed its return declaring income of Rs. 2,01,87,160, while the Transfer Pricing Officer initially recommended an upward adjustment of Rs. 6,11,78,279. Following objections before the Dispute Resolution Panel, the proposed adjustment was reduced to Rs. 3,22,06,526. The assessee challenged the inclusion of Acropetal Technologies Ltd., BNR Udyog Ltd. and Informed Technologies India Ltd. before the Tribunal, which directed their exclusion. The Revenue argued that the Tribunal had improperly evaluated the profit margins of BNR Udyog. In particular, it disputed the comparison between BNR’s 40.62% margin in the relevant year and the Tribunal’s stated 24.86% average for surrounding years, pointing to a different segmental calculation in the TPO’s analysis. The assessee contended that functional comparability, rather than profit margin alone, was decisive. It relied on Rule 10B(2)(a) of the Income-tax Rules, 1962 and the Delhi High Court decision in Rampgreen Solutions Pvt. Ltd. v. CIT concerning the distinction between routine BPO and higher-end KPO services. Acropetal was found to provide specialised healthcare-related knowledge process outsourcing services involving electronic medical records and intellectual property. Its functional profile was materially different from the routine IT-enabled services provided by the assessee. BNR Udyog rendered medical transcription services. The Tribunal regarded these activities as functionally dissimilar to the assessee’s services. The High Court observed that, even if the Revenue’s criticism of the margin analysis had force, the decisive ground for exclusion was functional dissimilarity. Informed Technologies India Ltd. provided financial research, financial-content processing and data-management services. The Tribunal found these knowledge-based services distinct from the assessee’s routine transaction-processing functions. The High Court agreed that the Tribunal had examined the characteristics of the services as required by Rule 10B(2)(a). It held that no substantial question of law arose, declined to interfere with the Tribunal’s factual findings and dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. Cases Discussed Rampgreen Solutions Pvt. Ltd. v. The moot question therefore, which arose for the consideration for the statutory authorities, including the Tribunal, was whether the services offered by the three (3) entities were comparable to those of the respondent/assessee. 33. The extract from the impugned order would demonstrate that, insofar as the three (3) comparables are concerned, they were providing services which were different from the respondent/assessee. 34. Acropetal was rendering Knowledge Process Outsourcing (KPO) services. The Tribunal has said that it was engaged in medical transcription services, which compendiously has been given the name Electronic Medical Record. 35. Likewise, insofar as the BNR was concerned, the Tribunal once again returned the finding of fact that it was engaged in providing medical transcription services. 36. Similarly, insofar as Informed Technologies was concerned, the Tribunal notes that it provides financial research and data management services. More particularly, insofar as Informed Technologies is concerned, the Tribunal notes that it is a leading player in KPO services, which extend from financial content to financial industry. 37. We are in agreement with the submissions of the counsel for the respondent/assessee that Rule 10(B)(2)(a) required that, when carrying out a comparability analysis of international transactions, with an uncontrolled transaction, the reference point should be, inter alia, the services provided by the entities which are being compared. 38. The Tribunal has, in our view, carried out that exercise. We may note Digitally Signed By:PREM Signing Date:05.09.2023 that Mr Sharma, is perhaps, right to the extent that the Tribunal did not consider the analysis of operating margin vis-a-vis BNR, which the TPO had carried out. The difference in operating margins clearly arose because the respondent/assessee has included revenue and expenses from medical transcription services, which the TPO excluded, and thereby caused the difference between the operating margins in the period in issue and the average margin which BNR had registered. 39. In our view, fluctuation in margin was not the only reason that the Tribunal provided in excluding BNR as a comparable. The fact that BNR offered a service which was different from that the respondent/assessee offered was the principal reason why the Tribunal concluded that BNR had been wrongly included in the comparables. 40. Thus, for the forgoing reasons, we are of the view that no interference is called for with the order passed by the Tribunal. According to us, no substantial question of law arises for our consideration. 41. The appeal is accordingly dismissed.
