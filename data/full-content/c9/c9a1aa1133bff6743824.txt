} } Listen to This ArticleThe International Finance Corporation (IFC) extended a $150 million social loan to State Bank of India (SBI) to expand access to agricultural finance for rural women, the private-sector arm of the World Bank Group said on Friday. "The US$150 million social loan will empower around 864,000 rural women across India's agricultural value chains, including crop cultivation, animal husbandry, fisheries and food processing, by providing accessible agricultural financing," IFC said in a press release. "Up to 90 percent of the financing will support women borrowing through Self-Help Groups (SHGs) while the rest will fund women entrepreneurs graduating to individual Micro Enterprise Loans under SBI's Svayam Siddha Scheme," it added. The $150 million social loan to SBI marks IFC's first line of credit to a public-sector bank in India and its first investment in a state-owned bank in the country. The partnership also includes their first cross-currency swap executed under a recently signed International Swaps and Derivatives Association (ISDA) Master Agreement, the release said.
