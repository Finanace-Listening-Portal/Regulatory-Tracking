HDFC Bank is a leading private sector bank with consistent growth and operational performance across various cycles. Post-merger, the bank has become the second largest in terms of size with a diversified portfolio. HDFC Bank is the largest private sector bank with a loan book of ₹31 trillion. Meanwhile, the Nifty Bank index hit a new all-time high of 64,450, gaining 2.2 per cent on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) in Friday’s intraday deals, surging 4 per cent in the past two trading days. The sharp rally in private and public sector banks (PSBs) is aided by strong buying from foreign portfolio investors (FPIs). Thus far in November, the Nifty Bank index outperformed the market by soaring 17.8 per cent, as compared to a 11.7 per cent rally in the Nifty 50. According to the National Securities Depository Limited (NSDL) data, FPIs net bought a record number of shares worth ₹1.15 trillion ($12.21 trillion) in November. Meanwhile, HDFC Bank appears to be nearing an inflection point, with September 2026 quarter (Q2) margins at a trough, improving operating productivity, and accelerating retail/micro, small and medium enterprises (MSME) momentum setting the stage for a gradual recovery in earnings. The higher share of floating rate loans, along with an improving mix of better-yielding Retail/MSME loans and focused efforts to mobilise current account saving account (CASA) deposits, bode well from a margins standpoint. The appointment of Anup Bagchi as MD & CEO addresses the leadership overhang; however, his strategic direction, particularly on retail growth, deposit mobilisation and NIM improvement, will be key to sustaining the bank’s re-rating, the brokerage firm said in the Q2 result update. The brokerage firm expects return on asset (RoA) to improve to 1.8-1.9 per cent over FY27-29E. Analysts believe the risk-reward remains favourable, and with most risks largely addressed, the downside on the stock appears limited. The brokerage has a ‘Buy’ rating on HDFC Bank with a target price of ₹950 per share. “We expect RoA to remain around 1.8 per cent over FY27–28E, supported by gradual margin recovery, benign credit cost and improving operating efficiency. Stepping down of Kaizad, DMD, brings in near term uncertainty. Anup Bagchi’s appointment as MD & CEO marks a key leadership transition, with his forthcoming strategy on growth, margins and balance sheet optimisation remains awaited,” analysts at ICICI Securities said in the result update. Factoring all, the brokerage firm said it continues to value standalone bank at ~1.8x FY28E book value and ₹118 for subsidiaries, thus keeping target unchanged at ₹925.
