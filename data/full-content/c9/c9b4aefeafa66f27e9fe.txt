Nasar Vs Appellate Deputy Commissioner (State Tax) & Anr., partly allowed a writ petition challenging the imposition of interest and penalty arising from the wrongful availment of Input Tax Credit (ITC) under the GST law. The petitioner, proprietor of J.F. Traders, challenged an order dated 16 December 2025 issued in Form GST DRC-07 for the period April 2021 to March 2022. The petition sought deletion of interest of Rs.2,12,731 and penalty of Rs.30,879, with a consequential direction to restrict the liability to late fee of Rs.12,450. The petitioner submitted that the ineligible ITC had been availed inadvertently and was subsequently reversed without utilisation. Since the disputed credit had remained in the Electronic Credit Ledger and had not been used to discharge tax liability, the petitioner contended that neither interest nor penalty should be imposed. The State Tax Department opposed the petition, pointing out that the petitioner had admitted availing ineligible ITC. The Court distinguished the consequences of wrongful availment from those of actual utilisation. It held that the act of availing ineligible ITC justified the imposition of penalty. Although the penalty appeared to have been imposed under Section 73 of the applicable GST enactments, the Court observed that there was no material difference even if Section 122 were applied, because the penalty imposed was only Rs.10,000 each towards CGST and SGST. The Court also upheld the late fee imposed for belated filing of the return and found no reason to interfere with that demand. On the issue of interest, the Court accepted the legal distinction between merely availing ITC and utilising such credit. It observed that interest would not be leviable unless the petitioner had utilised the ITC. Accordingly, the interest component of the assessment required reconsideration. The Court partly set aside the impugned order only insofar as interest was concerned, subject to the petitioner paying the penalty and late fee within 30 days from receipt of a copy of the order. The matter was remanded to the tax authority to reconsider interest after providing a reasonable opportunity of hearing and to issue a fresh order within five months from payment of penalty and late fee. The writ petition was disposed of without costs, and the connected miscellaneous petitions were closed. The decision clarifies that reversal of unutilised ITC may provide relief from interest, but does not, by itself, eliminate penalty for wrongful availment or late fee for delayed compliance. Learned counsel for the petitioner submits that the petitioner inadvertently availed of Input Tax Credit. Upon realising the mistake, it is stated that such Input Tax Credit was reversed without utilisation. Because the petitioner did not utilise Input Tax Credit and the same was lying in the petitioner’s Electronic Credit Ledger, it is submitted that interest should not be levied. It is also contended that penalty should not be imposed under said circumstance. 3. Accepting notice for both the respondents, Mr.R.Parthiban, learned Special Government Pleader, submits that the petitioner has admitted that ineligible Input Tax Credit had been availed of. In those circumstances, he submits that the petitioner is liable to pay penalty and also late fee. 4. The act of availing of ineligible Input Tax Credit justifies the imposition of penalty. Such penalty appears to have been imposed under Section 73 of the applicable GST enactments. Instead, even if penalty had been imposed under Section 122 of the statute, there is no material difference inasmuch as only Rs.10,000/- each was imposed as penalty towards CGST and SGST. The imposition of late fee for belated filing of Return is also entirely in order and no interference is warranted. 5. As regards interest, as contended by learned counsel for the petitioner, interest would not be leviable unless the petitioner utilised the Input Tax Credit. Hence, the imposition of interest warrants reconsideration. Subject to the petitioner discharging the demand towards penalty and late fee within thirty (30) days from the date of receipt of a copy of this order, the impugned order is set aside partly only insofar as interest is concerned, and the matter is remanded for reconsideration. After providing a reasonable opportunity to the petitioner, a fresh order shall be issued on this aspect within five (5) months from the date of payment of penalty and late fee. 6. This Writ Petition stands disposed of on the above terms. There shall be no order as to costs. Consequently, the connected miscellaneous petitions are closed.
