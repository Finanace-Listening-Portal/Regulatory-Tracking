Summary: For listed entities to which the relevant SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations apply, the Integrated Filing–Governance for the quarter ended 30 September 2026 is ordinarily due within 30 days of quarter-end, making 30 October 2026 the relevant date. SEBI Circular No. SEBI/HO/CFD/CFD-PoD-2/CIR/P/2024/185 consolidated specified governance disclosures, and the SEBI master circular updated on 30 January 2026 carries the operative consolidated guidance. The National Stock Exchange’s current main-board compliance calendar expressly lists 30 October for the September quarter. The integrated submission combines the investor-grievance statement under Regulation 13(3), applicable corporate-governance reporting under Regulation 27(2), and specified quarterly material-event information. Stock exchanges use XBRL utilities; companies should follow the current utility, entity-specific applicability and exchange-specific validation rather than relying on historical separate filing formats. This article explains how to identify the applicable reporting scope, collect board and committee information, reconcile investor complaints, review prescribed Regulation 30 items and retain evidence of acceptance. It also distinguishes the 30 October governance deadline from the 21 October shareholding-pattern deadline and the later Integrated Filing–Financial timetable. It does not treat every reporting field as mandatory for every category of listed issuer. Table of Contents▸ What Is Due on 30 October 2026? Key Takeaways Show all 22 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); What Is Due on 30 October 2026? The National Stock Exchange’s main-board compliance calendar identifies Integrated Filing–Governance as a quarterly filing to be submitted within 30 days of the end of the quarter. For the quarter ended 30 September 2026, the calendar therefore shows 30 October 2026. The deadline is separate from the quarterly shareholding pattern under Regulation 31(1)(b), which the exchange lists for 21 October, and from Integrated Filing–Financial, which follows a different reporting timetable. Combining all three in one diary entry can result in a filing that is timely for one requirement but late for another. The legal foundation is the SEBI LODR framework, including SEBI Circular No. SEBI/HO/CFD/CFD-PoD-2/CIR/P/2024/185 dated 31 December 2024. The SEBI master circular updated on 30 January 2026 consolidates operative circular guidance. Stock-exchange FAQs and utilities explain how the reporting framework is implemented, but they do not displace the Regulations or a subsequent SEBI amendment. The compliance officer should retain a dated copy of the applicable primary materials and the utility version used for the September 2026 quarter. Three Core Disclosure Streams in the Integrated Filing Component Regulatory reference Working data source Main control Investor grievance statement Regulation 13(3) RTA records, SCORES and investor relations register Opening, received, disposed and closing complaints reconcile Corporate governance report Regulation 27(2), where applicable Board, committees, meeting and director registers Composition and dates match corporate records Specified quarterly event disclosures Regulation 30 / Schedule III as specified Secretarial event log, acquisitions and penalties Events are mapped to prescribed quarterly fields The integrated form is not merely a renamed corporate-governance report. It brings together selected periodic governance information that was previously supplied through separate reporting channels. The consolidated approach does not eliminate the underlying obligations to keep accurate registers, resolve complaints or disclose material events promptly where separate event-based disclosure remains mandatory. The quarterly form should therefore be a reconciliation of existing compliance evidence, not the first occasion on which an event is investigated. Investor grievances under Regulation 13(3) Start with the investor complaint register maintained by the registrar and transfer agent and the company’s investor relations team. Reconcile complaints pending at the beginning of the quarter, received during the quarter, disposed of and pending at quarter-end. Identify reopened cases and duplicate records before generating the filing numbers. A complaint shown as closed internally but still pending in an exchange or regulator system should be investigated rather than excluded by a manual adjustment. The evidence pack should preserve system exports and explanations for any difference. Corporate governance under Regulation 27(2) Where the corporate-governance reporting requirement applies, collect board and committee composition, category of directors, dates of appointment or cessation, meeting attendance and other fields required by the current utility. Use the effective date of each change rather than only the quarter-end snapshot where the form requires period information. The secretarial team should reconcile the report with stock-exchange corporate announcements, board minutes, committee charters and MCA records. Do not assume that the same reporting exemption or format applies to every main-board, SME, debt-listed or newly listed entity. Quarterly information connected with Regulation 30 SEBI’s integrated-filing circular identifies specified material events or information to be presented in the quarterly governance filing. Examples in the framework include acquisitions of shares or voting rights in unlisted companies crossing prescribed levels and specified lower-value fines or penalties. These fields are not an invitation to postpone disclosures that are independently material and reportable on an event basis. The legal and secretarial teams should maintain two registers: one for prompt Regulation 30 disclosures and another for quarterly information required in the integrated form. Applicability Must Be Tested, Not Assumed The issuer’s securities, listing segment, paid-up capital, net worth, market-capitalisation classification, date of listing and applicable LODR exemptions can affect which governance provisions and fields apply. A newly listed company may have a different starting point for certain disclosures. An SME entity that crosses a regulatory threshold may have a different transition analysis from a long-established main-board issuer. These questions should be resolved using the current Regulations, relevant SEBI circulars and the exchange’s instructions, not a generic internet checklist. The NSE issued FAQs through Circular NSE/CML/2026/16 dated 12 June 2026, including guidance on newly listed entities, SME threshold changes and market-capitalisation categories. TaxGuru carries the NSE FAQs on Quarterly Integrated Filing–Governance as a convenient reference to the exchange communication. Because the FAQs themselves state that they are guidance for the XBRL utility rather than a substitute for law, any conflict should be resolved by consulting the primary LODR text and subsequent circulars. XBRL Submission: Prepare a Controlled Data File Stock exchanges implement the integrated governance return through XBRL utilities. XBRL structures each item as a tagged data field, so an apparently minor choice of reporting category, date or yes/no response can cause validation failures or misleading public data. The compliance officer should use the latest utility made available by the relevant exchange, complete all required entity details and validate the data before upload. Reusing a prior-quarter utility without checking its version can introduce outdated tags or fields. The Bombay Stock Exchange’s April 2025 notice clarified that, from the March 2025 quarter, the Integrated Filing–Governance was to be submitted in XBRL and the earlier separate corporate-governance and investor-grievance formats were no longer required in that old form. TaxGuru reproduces the BSE integrated governance submission update. That operational instruction is relevant to format control, while current exchange portal directions must be checked again for the September 2026 quarter. Governance Reconciliation Before the Filing Is Signed Off Prepare a maker-checker workbook in which each disclosure field is linked to an identified source. For board and committee data, use secretarial records; for complaints, use RTA and SCORES information; for share acquisitions and fines, use the legal and investment registers. Record the responsible officer, source date, review status and explanation of any change from the preceding quarter. A simple quarter-on-quarter movement analysis often identifies missed director changes or a complaint balance that does not roll forward correctly. Utility version, acknowledgement and acceptance screen Multiple Listings, Resubmissions and Clarifications A company whose specified securities are listed on more than one exchange should check the reporting obligations and workflow of each relevant exchange. Do not assume that an acknowledgement on one portal proves completion on another. The BSE notice indicates that a clarification or resubmission requested by an exchange should be addressed through the exchange that raised it. The filing team should maintain a separate acknowledgement and status log for each exchange, with the time of final acceptance. An upload that fails validation should be corrected promptly. Where a revised submission is required, preserve both the original and corrected versions and a short note explaining the change. If a previously disclosed board change was entered with an incorrect effective date, the correction log should show the supporting board resolution or announcement. This is important because integrated filings are used by investors and market participants; a corrected XBRL should be traceable rather than silently replacing the company’s internal source data. Distinguish Governance Filing from Financial Reporting The exchange calendar lists Integrated Filing–Financial separately from the governance return. Financial reporting includes applicable financial results, related-party transaction information and other prescribed financial disclosures under their respective rules and timelines. An entity should not assume that uploading the governance XBRL also completes financial-result reporting. Conversely, a completed financial filing does not cure an omitted investor-grievance statement. Maintain separate controls for the 21 October shareholding pattern, 30 October governance return and the applicable financial filing date. Tax, GST and Accounting Consequences: Keep the Categories Separate Integrated Filing–Governance is a securities-law disclosure, not an income-tax return, GST return or certificate of tax compliance. Any fine, penalty, provision or contingent liability identified while preparing the governance report may separately require accounting recognition or disclosure under applicable Ind AS/AS, and its deductibility under income-tax law requires its own legal analysis. Do not infer that an amount disclosed to the exchange is automatically allowable as a business deduction or that a stock-exchange fee is exempt from GST. The nature of each charge and its supporting invoice determine the tax and accounting treatment. If a legal order or fine is relevant to the quarterly report, finance should reconcile the disclosed amount with the ledger and supporting order, while the secretarial team checks whether the same event was separately announced under Regulation 30. These cross-checks improve consistency between the governance filing, financial statements and public disclosures. They do not convert the XBRL return into a tax audit or require an audit opinion unless the applicable rule independently says so. Recommended Internal Filing Sequence for October 2026 A practical internal plan is to obtain RTA complaint figures and board data immediately after quarter close, run legal-event checks in the second week of October, validate the current XBRL in the third week and leave time for exchange queries before 30 October. These are internal controls, not regulatory deadlines. The company secretary should sign off the applicability analysis and final data, and a second reviewer should check the uploaded copy against the approved workbook. Save the final acknowledgement and status screen in a quarter-specific folder accessible for the next annual governance review. Before marking the task complete, confirm whether a subsequent SEBI or stock-exchange communication has modified the reporting requirement for the issuer’s category. The 30 October date is the ordinary calendar date supported by the NSE’s current main-board schedule; it should not be treated as a guarantee that every type of listed security or issuer follows identical provisions. Any exemption relied upon should be documented with the legal basis and reviewed when the company’s classification changes. Frequently Asked Questions 1. What is the ordinary due date for September 2026 Integrated Filing–Governance? The NSE main-board compliance calendar specifies 30 October 2026, being within 30 days after the quarter ended 30 September 2026. 2. Does the filing combine investor grievances and corporate governance? Yes. It consolidates the applicable Regulation 13(3) grievance statement, Regulation 27(2) governance information and specified quarterly Regulation 30 information. 3. Is the filing generally made in XBRL format? Yes, the exchange framework uses XBRL utilities. The current exchange-specific utility and validation requirements must be followed. 4. Is the 21 October shareholding-pattern deadline the same filing? No. The shareholding pattern under Regulation 31(1)(b) has a separate quarterly filing timeline. 5. Does every listed entity complete every governance field? No. The applicable LODR provisions and any entity-specific exemption must be examined before selecting required fields. 6. Does an integrated governance filing replace prompt Regulation 30 announcements? No. Separate event-based disclosures remain subject to their own timing and materiality requirements. 7. What if the exchange rejects the XBRL upload? Correct the validation error, resubmit as directed, and retain the revised file, acknowledgement and final acceptance evidence. Key Takeaways 30 October 2026 is the ordinary NSE calendar deadline for the September-quarter Integrated Filing–Governance. The integrated return brings together grievance, governance and specified quarterly event disclosures. Use current XBRL utilities and document entity-specific applicability and exemptions. Reconcile RTA complaints, board and committee records, legal events and portal data before submission.
