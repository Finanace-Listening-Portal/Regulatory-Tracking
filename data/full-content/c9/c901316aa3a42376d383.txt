Articles 2 min read Loan recovery calls: NBFCs seek RBI clarity on TRAI’s 1600 series rules Non-banking finance companies have sought clarity from the Reserve Bank of India on whether recovery calls fall under the service-call category permitted through the 1600 numbering series, amid concerns that customers increasingly block calls originating from the series, sources part of the discussion told ETBFSI. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Updated On Sep 4, 2026 at 11:31 AM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 133737944; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The issue was raised by NBFC CEOs and industry representatives during a meeting with RBI Deputy Governor S C Murmu on Thursday, where the industry flagged concerns around the implementation of the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India’s (TRAI) mandatory 1600-series framework for regulated financial entities.“Sales calls should be outside the 1600 series. We fully agree with that. But whether recovery calls fall under service calls is still a question. That needs to be clarified,” a source told ETBFSI.The 1600 series was allocated by the Department of Telecommunications (DoT) for service and transactional voice calls by Government and BFSI entities. TRAI, in November 2025, subsequently mandated a phase-wise migration of RBI-, SEBI- and PFRDA-regulated entities to the series, with the objective of allowing consumers to distinguish legitimate calls from regulated financial institutions from spam, promotional and potentially fraudulent calls. Advt For RBI-regulated entities, commercial banks were required to adopt the 1600 series by January 1, 2026, while large NBFCs with asset size above Rs 5,000 crore, payments banks and small finance banks were given a February 1, 2026 deadline. Remaining NBFCs, co-operative banks, regional rural banks and other entities were required to migrate by March 1, 2026.RBI regulated entities covered by the mandate are not permitted to initiate service or transactional voice calls from numbers other than those allocated under the 1600 series, even where they have explicit or inferred customer consent.TRAI's stated objective is to prevent promotional calls from being made under the guise of service or transactional calls and to give BFSI entities a distinct identity that allows consumers to make informed decisions about whether to answer a call.However, NBFCs said the framework does not clearly address recovery communications. Lenders typically make large volumes of calls to borrowers regarding overdue payments, repayment schedules and other recovery-related matters, and the industry wants to know whether such calls qualify as service calls under the 1600 framework.“The distinction between a sales call, a service call and a recovery call is important. I agree that a sales call should not be made through 1600. But whether a recovery call is considered a service call is not clear,” the NBFC CEO said.The concern is compounded by the possibility of customers blocking calls originating from the 1600 series. Industry participants said that while the dedicated series is intended to help consumers identify genuine financial-sector calls, borrowers may still block such numbers as unwanted or spam, particularly when they receive repeated recovery calls. Advt “If you are doing mass recovery calls and people are blocking those numbers, the whole purpose is lost,” the source said.According to the industry, the problem becomes particularly acute when a borrower does not respond to repeated calls from a lender’s 1600 number.“Imagine a 1600 call. If the customer does not pick up, how will a banker communicate? If we try once or twice and the number is blocked, can we then call from our normal number? That is the question,” the CEO said.
