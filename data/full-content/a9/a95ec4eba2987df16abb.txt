Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Vs My Home Constructions Private Limited (ITAT Hyderabad) Section 14A Disallowance Cannot Exceed Exempt Income Under the Pre-Amendment Law: ITAT Hyderabad Hyderabad ITAT upholds the ceiling for AY 2018–19 Can the Assessing Officer disallow ₹5.35 crore under Section 14A when the company earned only ₹56.79 lakh in exempt income? In DCIT Vs My Home Constructions Private Limited, ITA No. 2352/HYD/2025, decided on 20 November 2026, the Hyderabad ITAT held that, under the law applicable to AY 2018–19, the disallowance could not exceed the exempt income earned during the year. The Tribunal upheld the CIT(A)’s order restricting the disallowance to ₹56,79,327 and dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. It also held that the Explanation inserted by the Finance Act, 2022 could not be applied retrospectively to that year. How the Assessing Officer arrived at ₹5.35 crore The company filed its return declaring income of ₹137.45 crore. During scrutiny, the Assessing Officer asked it to explain why expenditure relating to exempt income should not be disallowed. The company maintained that it had incurred no expenditure to earn exempt income. The Assessing Officer noted that the company held investments of approximately ₹601.98 crore at the beginning of the financial year and ₹469.11 crore at the end. It had also incurred interest expenditure of ₹5.63 crore. Taking the view that the expenditure attributable to exempt income could not be determined from the accounts, the officer invoked Rule 8D, as amended by CBDT Notification No. 43/2016 dated 2 June 2016, and computed a disallowance of ₹5,35,54,388 using the 1% investment-based formula. Why the CIT(A) restricted the disallowance Before the CIT(A), the company argued that it had made the investments out of its own surplus funds and had used its interest-bearing borrowings to acquire commercial vehicles and construction equipment. The CIT(A) found that the company had furnished no fund-flow statement to substantiate that claim. The appellate authority also rejected the broader contention that the company had incurred no expenditure, direct or indirect, in relation to exempt income. The CIT(A) nevertheless accepted that the computation should take into account the eligible investments yielding exempt income. On the figures furnished by the company, the recalculated amount came to ₹1,87,50,530. That figure still exceeded the ₹56,79,327 actually earned from tax-free bonds. The CIT(A) therefore capped the disallowance at the latter amount. The relief arose from the ceiling on disallowance, even though the CIT(A) had rejected the company’s explanation about the source of its investments. Rule 8D computation remained subject to the ceiling The Revenue relied on CBDT Circular No. 5/2014 dated 11 February 2014, which took the position that Section 14A read with Rule 8D could apply even in a year in which the taxpayer earned no exempt income. It also relied on the 2016 amendment to Rule 8D. The Tribunal framed the issue more narrowly. The company had earned exempt income, and the question was whether the disallowance for that year could exceed the amount actually earned. The Bench drew support from CIT Vs Chettinad Logistics Pvt. Ltd. (2017) 248 Taxman 55 (Mad.) and noted that the Supreme Court had dismissed both the Revenue’s special leave petition and its review petition. It also referred to PCIT Vs Oil Industry Development Board (2019) 262 Taxman 102 (SC) in connection with the Delhi High Court’s ruling. Applying the pre-amendment law, the Tribunal held that the disallowance could go only up to ₹56,79,327, regardless of the higher figure produced by the Rule 8D calculation. The Finance Act, 2022 amendment applies prospectively The Explanation to Section 14A, inserted by the Finance Act, 2022 with effect from 1 April 2022, addresses cases where expenditure is incurred in relation to exempt income even though such income has not accrued, arisen or been received during the relevant previous year. The Tribunal held that the Explanation could not be applied retrospectively to AY 2018–19. For this conclusion, the Bench relied on PCIT Vs Era Infrastructure (India) Ltd. (2022) 114 CCH 219 (Delhi) and PCIT Vs Keti Constructions (2024) 162 taxmann.com 278 (MP). The assessment year was therefore decisive. The Revenue’s reliance on the amended Rule 8D did not displace the ceiling the Tribunal applied under the version of Section 14A governing that year. Author’s comments The order illustrates why a Rule 8D working and the amount ultimately disallowable can differ. In this case, the Assessing Officer arrived at ₹5.35 crore, and the calculation based on eligible investments brought that figure down to ₹1.87 crore. The exempt-income ceiling reduced the sustained disallowance further to ₹56.79 lakh. The last reduction followed from the legal ceiling, independently of the dispute over which investments entered the calculation. For disputes concerning earlier years, the ruling also shows the value of placing the actual exempt income and its source on record. Here, the tax-free bond income supplied a definite figure against which the Tribunal could test the disallowance. The decision on that ceiling is expressly tied to the pre-amendment law applicable to AY 2018–19. Cases Discussed: Jasper Industries Pvt. Ltd. FULL TEXT OF THE ORIGINAL JUDGMENT/ORDER The present appeal filed by the Revenue is directed against the order passed by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeals Centre, Delhi (for short, “CIT(A)”), dated 30/10/2025, which in turn arises from the order passed by the Assessing Officer (for short, ‘AO’) under section 143(3) r.w.s 144B of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (for short, ‘the Act’), dated 28/04/2021, for the assessment year 2018-19. The Revenue has assailed the impugned order on the following grounds of appeal raised before us: 1) The Ld. CIT(A) erred in not appreciating the relevant provisions of section 14A of the Act which was introduced by the Finance Act, 2001 with retrospective effect from 01.04.1962. 2) The Ld CITIA) erred in not appreciating the CBDT Circular No.5/2014 dated 11.02.2014, wherein the CBDT has clarified that Rule 8D read with section 14A of the Act provides for disallowance of expenditure even WHARE taxpayer in a particular year has not earned any exempt income. 3) The Ld. CITIA) erred in not appreciating the modification of Rule 8D of the Income Tax Rules, 1962 w.e.f. 02.06.2016, based on which AO has drawn his satisfaction. 4) The appellant craves leave to add to, alter, amend OR withdraw any of the above grounds of appeal at the time of hearing. 2. Succinctly stated, the assessee company had e-filed its return of income for AY 2018-19 on 29/09/2018 declaring an income of Rs.1,37,45,42,730/-. Subsequently, the case of the assessee company was selected for complete scrutiny assessment through CASS for verification of multiple issues, which, inter alia, included verification of the expenses that it had incurred for earning of exempt income. 3. During the course of the assessment proceedings, the AO called upon the assessee company to explain as to why disallowance of the expenses incurred for earning of exempt income may not be made per the mandate of section 14A of the Act. In response, it was submitted by the assessee company that as it had not incurred any expenditure for earning of exempt income, therefore, the provisions of section 14A of the Act were not attracted. However, the AO did not find favour with the aforesaid explanation of the assessee company. The AO observed that the assessee company during the subject year had investments at the beginning and at the end of Financial Year (FY) of Rs.601,97,71,360/- and Rs.469,11,06,286/- respectively on which it had earned exempt income of Rs.56,79,327/-. The AO taking cognizance of the fact that the assessee company had paid interest of Rs.5,62,58,925/-, therein relied upon the CBDT Notification No.43/2016, dated 02/06/2016, and holding a conviction that the expenditure incurred by the assessee company on earning of the exempt income was not discernible from its accounts for the year under consideration, therein worked out the disallowance under section 14A of the Act @ 1% of its investment at Rs.5,35,54,388/-. Accordingly, the AO vide his order passed under section 143(3) r.w.s 144B of the Act, dated 28/04/2021 after making the aforesaid disallowance under section 14A of the Act, determined the income of the assessee company at Rs.1,42,80,97,100/-. 4. Aggrieved, the assessee company carried the matter in appeal before the CIT(A). Insofar as the assessee company’s contention that as the investments made in the exempt income yielding investments were sourced out of its self-owned surplus funds and the entire amount of the interest bearing funds were utilized for acquiring commercial vehicles and construction equipment, the same did not find favour with the said First Appellate Authority. It was observed by him that as the assessee company had failed to file any fund flow statement, therefore, its claim that no interest bearing funds were diverted for making investment in the exempt income yielding shares/securities remained unsubstantiated. It was further observed that the assessee company to support its aforesaid claim was obligated to prove based on demonstrative evidence that on the date on which the investments in the exempt income yielding shares/securities were made, it had sufficient interest free funds with it, which would have supported its claim that there was no diversion of interest bearing funds for non-business purposes. The CIT(A) further observed that the claim of the assessee company that no expenditure was incurred for earning of the exempt income also did not merit acceptance. Elaborating on his view, it was observed that the expenditure incurred by the assessee company like administrative expenses, depreciation, financial cost, operating cost, notional cost, etc., had a direct or indirect nexus with the fungible funds available with the assessee company that were utilized for the purpose of investment and earning exempt income. Accordingly, the CIT(A) was of the view that the fungible funds available with the assessee company could not be dissociated from the revenue generating apparatus of the assessee company by a watertight margin, as a result whereof its claim that none of the expenses were directly or indirectly related with the specified funds invested in the exempt income generating shares/securities did not merit acceptance. However, the CIT(A) found favour with the Ld. AR’s contention that the AO while calculating the average investments ought to have considered the eligible investments yielding exempt income for the purpose of computing the disallowance under section 14A of the Act, as a result whereof the impugned disallowance as per the details furnished by the assessee company worked out at Rs.1,87,50,530/-. However, the CIT(A) taking cognizance of the fact that the assessee company during the subject year had only earned exempt income of Rs.56,79,327/- from tax free bonds, restricted the disallowance to the said extent and partly allowed the appeal. 5. The Revenue being aggrieved with the order of the CIT(A) has carried the matter in appeal before us. 6. We have heard the Learned Authorized Representatives of both parties, perused the orders of the authorities below and the material available on record, as well as considered the judicial pronouncements that have been pressed into service by the Learned Authorized Representative to drive home his contentions. 7. Shri Mohan Babu, Learned Senior Departmental Representative (for short, “Ld. Sr-DR”) at the threshold of hearing of the appeal took us through the facts of the case and the specific grounds raised on which the impugned order passed by the CIT(A) has been assailed before us. Elaborating on his contention, the Ld. Sr-DR took us through the ground of appeal No.2, wherein the impugned order has been assailed on the ground that the CBDT Circular No.5/2014, dated 11/02/2014 contemplates that a disallowance under section 14A r.w. Rule 8D is to be made even in a case where the assessee may not have earned any exempt income. Also, the Ld. Sr-DR submitted that the CIT(A) had erred in not appreciating the modification of Rule 8D w.e.f 02/06/2016 based on which the AO had computed the disallowance under section 14A of the Act. 8. Per contra, Shri P Vinod, Advocate, Learned Authorized Representative (for short, “Ld. AR”) for the assessee company, submitted that the CIT(A) had rightly restricted the disallowance made by the AO under section 14A of the Act to the extent of the exempt income earned by the assessee company during the year under consideration. The Ld. AR to buttress his contention relied upon the order passed by the Tribunal in the case of Jasper Industries Pvt Ltd vs. Deputy Commissioner of Income Tax, Circle-2(1), Hyderabad in ITA No.1357/Hyd/2025, dated 10/12/2025. 9. We have given thoughtful consideration to the contentions advanced by the Learned Authorized Representatives of both parties in the backdrop of the orders of the authorities below. 10. We find that the issue involved in the present appeal boils down to the solitary issue, i.e., as to whether or not the CIT(A) is right in law and facts of the case in restricting the disallowance made by the AO under section 14A of the Act to the extent of the exempt income earned by the assessee company during the subject year. 11. We find substance in the Ld. AR’s claim that the CIT(A) had rightly observed that the disallowance under section 14A of the Act could not have exceeded the amount of the exempt income of Rs.56,79,327/- that was earned by the assessee company during the year under consideration. Our aforesaid view is supported by the judgment of the Hon’ble High Court of Madras in the case of Commissioner of Income Tax Vs. Chettinad Logistics Pvt. Ltd. (2017) 248 Taxman 55 (Mad.). The Special Leave Petition (SLP) (Civil) No.16194 of 2018 filed by the department before the Hon’ble Apex Court against the aforesaid order of the Hon’ble High Court of Madras in the case of Chettinad Logistics Pvt. Ltd. (supra) has been dismissed by the Hon’ble Apex Court in CIT Vs. Chettinad Logistics (P) Ltd. (2018) 95 taxmann.com 250 (SC). Rather, we find that even the review petition filed by the revenue has also been dismissed by the Hon’ble Apex Court vide its order passed in Commissioner of Income Tax (Central) Vs. M/s. Chettinad Logistics Pvt. Ltd. (2019) 105 CCH 226 (SC). Also, we find that the Hon’ble Apex Court has taken the same view in the case of Principal Commissioner of Income Tax Vs. Oil Industry Development Board (2019) 262 Taxman 102 (SC), wherein the order of the Hon’ble High Court of Delhi in the case of Principal Commissioner of Income Tax Vs. Oil Industries Development Board (2018) 101 CCH 452 (Del. HC) was approved. 12. We thus, in terms of our aforesaid deliberations, are of the firm conviction, that pursuant to the aforesaid orders of the Hon’ble Supreme Court the issue involved in the present appeal is no more res integra and principally agree with the Ld. AR that the disallowance under the pre-amended Section 14A of the Act could not have been made by the AO in excess of the exempt income of Rs.56,79,327/- earned by the assessee company during the subject year. 13. Before parting, we may herein observe, that though the legislature vide its amendment made available on the statute by the Finance Act, 2022 has inserted an “Explanation” to Section 14A of the Act, as per which, notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in the Act, the provisions of Section 14A shall apply in a case where the income, not forming part of the total income under the Act, has not accrued or arisen or has not been received during the subject year and the expenditure has been incurred during the said previous year in relation to such exempt income, but the same is effective from April 1, 2022 and cannot be presumed to have retrospective effect. Our aforesaid view is fortified by the judgment of Hon’ble High Court of Delhi in the case of Pr. CIT Vs. Era Infrastructure (India) Ltd. (2022) 114 CCH 219 (Delhi) and that of the Hon’ble High Court of Madhya Pradesh in Pr. CIT Vs. Keti Constructions (2024) 162 taxmann.com278 (MP), wherein it has been held that the amendment made available on the statute vide the Finance Act, 2022 in Section 14A is effective from 01.04.2022 and cannot be permitted with retrospective effect. 14. We thus, in terms of our aforesaid observations uphold the CIT(A) order and dismiss the appeal filed by the Revenue. Order pronounced in the open court on 20th November, 2026.
