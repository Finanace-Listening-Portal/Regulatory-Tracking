Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); SBI (erstwhile SBBJ) Vs ITO (TDS) (ITAT Jaipur) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Jaipur Bench, allowed the appeal of SBI (erstwhile SBBJ) for AY 2017-18 against its treatment as an assessee in default under Sections 201(1) and 201(1A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 for not deducting tax on LTC payments involving foreign travel. The Assessing Officer’s order dated 28 July 2023 concerned LTC of ₹2,21,156 and raised a demand of ₹1,15,984, comprising TDS of ₹63,034 and interest of ₹52,950. The CIT(A) confirmed that order. The bank’s grounds also challenged proceedings against the erstwhile merged entity, the Assessing Officer’s jurisdiction, manual affixation of DIN, the inquiry procedure and the failure to proceed against employees. The Tribunal’s operative reasoning, however, centred on the binding judicial directions that prevented deduction of tax during the relevant period. The Revenue relied on State Bank of India Vs ACIT, Civil Appeal No. 8181 of 2022, decided on 4 November 2022, which held that journeys involving a foreign leg fall outside Section 10(5) and that the employer must deduct tax under Section 192(1). The Tribunal accepted that this judgment lays down the governing law on taxability and the employer’s statutory obligation. It noted that the Supreme Court had rejected SBI’s plea of bona fide mistake because employees’ travel details were available when the bank settled their LTC claims. The Tribunal nevertheless distinguished the factual setting of that decision, concerning AY 2013-14, from the later years covered by the Madras High Court’s interim directions. Following the bank’s withdrawal of overseas LTC facilities through its circular dated 15 April 2014, the High Court stayed the circular and, on 16 February 2015, clarified that payments made pursuant to the interim order would not constitute income enabling the bank to deduct tax at source. The clarification also stated that employees would be liable to pay tax if the writ petition was dismissed. The order traced the subsequent writ dismissal, appellate proceedings and directions restraining recovery from employees. Relying on the Kerala High Court’s decision in ITA No. 45 of 2025 and coordinate Bench decisions, including State Bank of India Vs ITO, ITA No. 1817/Ahd/2026, the Tribunal held that the Supreme Court’s interpretation of the law and the bank’s inability to deduct tax under binding court orders operate in different factual contexts. The judicial restraint was a vital fact that could not be ignored when determining default. Since the present facts and assessment year were identical to those considered in the earlier Tribunal decisions, SBI could not be held to be an assessee in default under Section 201. The appeal was allowed; the ruling did not hold that foreign-travel LTC was exempt under Section 10(5). Cases Discussed State Bank of India Vs ITO (ITAT Ahmedabad); ITA No. 1817/Ahd/2026; 25/08/2026. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee is a branch of State Bank of India which had provided Leave Fare Concession (LFC) to its employees and had treated the same as exempt under section 10(5) of the Income-tax Act (“the Act”) while computing TDS under section 192 of the Act. Accordingly, notices were issued to the assessee asking it to explain as to why it should not be treated as an assessee in default under section 201(1) and liable for interest under section 201(1A) of the Act. 4. In response, the assessee submitted that the LFC benefit was granted strictly in accordance with section 10(5) of the Act read with Rule 2B, as the designated place of travel was within India and the reimbursement was restricted to the fare of the shortest route within India. The assessee further submitted that there is no explicit prohibition in the Act or Rules against a foreign leg in the course of such travel. The assessee also relied upon industry practice, guidelines issued by the Indian Banks’ Association, and various judicial precedents to contend that the assessee had acted under a bona fide belief and therefore could not be treated as an assessee in default. At the outset, we note that the issue on merits regarding allowability of exemption under section 10(5) of the Act in cases where the journey involves a foreign leg now stands concluded against the assessee by the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court dated 04.11.2022. There is no dispute on this legal position and the same is duly acknowledged. However, the limited controversy before us is whether, in the peculiar facts of the present case, the assessee can be treated as an “assessee in default” under section 201(1) of the Act for non-deduction of tax at source during the relevant period. 13. The contention of the assessee has consistently been that during the year under consideration, it was bound by the interim orders passed by the Hon’ble Madras High Court in W.P. No.11991 of 2014, wherein vide order dated 16.02.2015 it was specifically clarified that the LFC payments would not amount to income so as to enable deduction of tax at source and further that if the writ petition was ultimately dismissed, the employees would be liable to pay tax. The assessee has submitted that in view of such binding judicial directions, it could not have deducted tax at source and any such deduction would have amounted to disobedience of the order of the Hon’ble High Court. 14. We find considerable merit in the aforesaid contention of the assessee. The interim directions of the Hon’ble Madras High Court were in force during the relevant previous year and the assessee, being a party to the proceedings, was duty bound to comply with the same. The obligation under section 192 of the Act to deduct tax at source cannot be read in isolation and must yield to binding judicial orders. Therefore, the failure to deduct tax in such circumstances cannot be equated with a default contemplated under section 201(1) of the Act. 15. We further find that an identical issue has been considered by the Co-ordinate Bench of the Tribunal in the case of State Bank of India in ITA No.514/Agr/2024, wherein after considering the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court as well as the interim orders of the Hon’ble Madras High Court, ITAT held that the assessee bank could not be treated as an assessee in default since it was bound to follow the interim directions of the Hon’ble High Court. The Tribunal categorically observed that the assessee had no option but to comply with the orders of the Hon’ble High Court and non-deduction of tax in such circumstances could not invite the rigours of section 201(1) and 201(1A) of the Act. 16. More importantly, the Hon’ble Kerala High Court in ITA No.45 of 2025 (order dated 18th November 2025) has examined this issue in detail and has held in favour of the assessee. The relevant findings of the Hon’ble High Court, which have a direct bearing on the issue before us, are reproduced below for ready reference: “The interim order granted by this Court is explained to the effect that any amount paid to the petitioner towards LTC or re-imbursement of LTC pursuant to the impugned order would not amount to income so as to enable the Bank to deduct tax at source. It is made clear that if the writ petition is dismissed, the employees are liable to pay tax on the amount paid by Bank.” “It is only when the appellant-assessee, after having a liability to deduct tax, fails to do so, the question of invoking Section 201 of the Act and treating it as an ‘assessee in default’ arises. Here, the Madras High Court found, prima facie, that the amount paid would not be the income of a payee so as to deduct tax. Therefore, we are of the opinion that the provisions of Section 201(1) of the Act are not attracted to the case at hand.” “The appellant-assessee was under an obligation not to deduct tax at source and therefore, the assessee could not be held to be assessee in-default for non-deduction of tax at source on impugned LFC payments.” 17. The Hon’ble High Court has thus clearly held that where the assessee was restrained by judicial orders from deducting tax at source, the provisions of section 201 of the Act cannot be invoked and the assessee cannot be treated as an assessee in default. 18. In the present case also, the facts are materially identical. The assessee was operating under the binding interim directions of the Hon’ble Madras High Court during the relevant period and therefore could not have deducted tax at source. The subsequent decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court, though settling the issue on merits, cannot retrospectively fasten liability under section 201(1) of the Act for a period during which the assessee was acting in compliance with judicial orders. 19. We also find force in the argument of the assessee that the scheme of section 201 of the Act itself contemplates that a person can be treated as an assessee in default only when there is a failure to deduct tax in spite of a legal obligation to do so. In the present case, such legal obligation stood eclipsed by the interim directions of the Hon’ble High Court. 20. In view of the above discussion, respectfully following the decision of the Hon’ble Kerala High Court in ITA No.45 of 2025 and the decision of the Co-ordinate Bench in ITA No.514/Agr/2024, we hold that the assessee cannot be treated as an assessee in default under section 201(1) of the Act for the impugned period. Consequently, the interest charged under section 201(1A) also does not survive. 21. Accordingly, we direct the Assessing Officer to delete the demand raised under section 201(1) and 201(1A) of the Act.” 9. We are in agreement with the above decision of the co-ordinate bench of the Tribunal. Since the assessees were under legal obligation to comply with the orders of the Hon’ble Madras High Court (supra), hence the assessees, herein, cannot be termed as assessees in default under section 201(1) of the Act for the impugned period and interest cannot be charged from them under section 201(1A). Accordingly, the demand raised by the Assessing Officer under section 201(1) and 201(1A) of the Act, is hereby set aside. 15. A perusal of the above judgement reveals thatall decisions holding the assessee to be not an assessee in default are based on appreciation of facts peculiar to the assessment years before it. All these decisions record the following chronology of events/facts:- That SBI had been providing reimbursement of LTC to its employees even for foreign travel. Pursuant to circular dated 15.04.2014 issued by the Bank, the LTC as regards overseas travel facilities to its employees stood withdrawn. This circular was challenged before the Hon’ble Madras High Court at the instance of the Association of employees of the Bank. The Hon’ble Madras High Court vide order dated 25.04.2014 stayed operation of the circular. Later on the stay order was sought to be modified/clarified by issuing interim order dated 16.02.2015 in writ petition No.11991 of 2014 clarifying that during the pendency of the stay of the circular issued by the Bank ,the Bank would be paying LTC to its employees and vide the interim order, it was explained that any amount paid to the petitioners towards LTC or reimbursement of LTC would not amount to income so as to enable the bank to deduct tax at source. The interim order made it clear that if the writ petition was dismissed, the employees would be liable to pay tax on the amount paid by the Bank.And pursuant to the above stated interim direction of the Hon’ble Madras High Court, SBI and its various branches was barred from deducting TDS on the remittance to their employees during the FY 2015-16 till the disposal of the writ petition. The writ petition filed by the Association of Employees of the Bank was ultimately dismissed on 24.06.2022. Against the aforesaid order WA No. 1653 of 2022 was filed wherein vide order dated 08.08.2022 interim directions were issued by the Hon’ble High Court interdicting/prohibiting assessees’/Banks from making recovery from the salary of the employees. Ultimately the Division Bench of the Hon’ble Madras High Court vide order dated 08.06.2023 set aside the order passed by the Single Bench and the matter was remanded to the Bank Authorities for fresh consideration in respect to grant of LTC to cover foreign travel on merits in accordance with law. The judgment of the Hon’ble Madras High Court was again challenged by the assessee Bank before the Hon’ble Apex Court vide order dated 28.08.2023 passed in Special Leave to Appeal (C) No(s). 16734/2023 while issuing notices to the respondents, restrained the bank from making recoveries from employees during the pendency of the matter. 16. Taking note of the above chronology, the Courts held that in view of the decision of the Hon’ble Madras High Court and the Hon’ble Apex Court, the assessee bank was under the legal obligation not to deduct TDS on the reimbursement of LTC paid to its employees. Even the Hon’ble Kerala High Court held that the assessee SBI , under the circumstances was under obligation not to deduct tax at source. 17. Considering therefore both the decision of the Hon’ble apex court and that of the Hon’ble Kerala High court and the ITAT, what is derived is that the Hon’ble apex court decision was for A.Y 2013-14 , deciding the issue of liability of the assessee bank to deduct TDS on LTC after interpreting the law applicable and was rendered on 04-11-2022. 18. The decision of the ITAT and the Hon’ble Kerala High Court pertain to different assessment years i.e. AY 2016-17 and AY 2017-18 and have held that the assessee to be not in default for not deducting TDS not by applying the law in this regard, but by noting that the assessee was prevented by the order of the Hon’ble Madras High Court from deducting TDS. 19. The basis and the context in which both the decisions have been rendered are completely different. The Hon’ble Apex Court has interpreted the law while holding the assessee to be liable to deduct TDS, while the decisions of the ITAT and the Hon’ble Kerala High Court have noted the inability of the assessee to deduct TDS on account of the order of the Hon’ble Madras High Court and accordingly held the assessee to be not an assessee in default. The decision of the Hon’ble Apex Court does undoubtedly lay down the law, but at the same time for holding the assessee in default the fact that the assessee was precluded from deducting TDS, noted by the ITAT and the Hon’ble Kerala High Court also, is a very vital fact and cannot be ignored. Therefore, though the law as laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court prevails and the assessee is liable for deducting TDS on the LTC paid for foreign travel, however at the same time if it is prevented from doing so, being bound by the order of the Hon’ble Madras High Court, the assessee cannot be faulted for not having deducted the TDS as held by the Hon’ble ITAT and the Hon’ble Kerala High Court. 20. The facts of the present case and the assessment year involved in the present case being identical to that dealt with by the ITAT in its decisions earlier the said decisions will apply following which I hold that the assessee cannot be held to be an assessee in default u/s 201 of the Act. 21. With the above observations and findings, I decide the issue in favour of the assessee and allow the appeal of the assessee. 22. In effect, the appeal of the assessee is allowed.
