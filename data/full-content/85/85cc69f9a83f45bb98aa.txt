Articles 3 min read RBL Bank Q2 FY27 profit nearly triples to Rs 482 crore on loan growth, FCNR(B) gains RBL Bank’s advances rose 42 per cent year on year to Rs 1.42 lakh crore and deposits growing 34 per cent to Rs 1.56 lakh crore, while net profit nearly tripled to Rs 482 crore. The headline balance-sheet growth was partly supported by the bank’s mobilisation of Rs 32,472 crore through the RBI’s FCNR(B) swap facility, against which its international banking unit extended Rs 10,309 crore in loans. Excluding loans extended against FCNR(B) deposits, management said underlying loan growth was around 30 per cent, while deposit growth excluding FCNR(B) stood at 6 per cent. Loans provided against FCNR(B) deposits stood at Rs 10,309 crore as of September 30, according to the investor presentation. Advt Subramaniakumar said the bank’s 42 per cent advance growth should be viewed in the context of the FCNR(B)-linked component, while underlying growth remained strong. “Excluding that, we are still growing at about 30 per cent,” Subramaniakumar said. RBL Bank said it would continue to raise deposits in line with its deployment requirements, with a greater focus on retail term deposits and granular liabilities.“We will raise deposits only to the extent of deployment. We will not be raising deposits just for the sake of raising deposits,” Subramaniakumar said, adding that retail term deposits had grown 16 per cent during the quarter, compared with 22 per cent in the previous quarter. The bank’s average deposits grew 24 per cent year-on-year during the quarter, while the average CASA ratio stood at around 24 per cent. End-period deposits stood at Rs 1,562.72 billion. Its wholesale advances grew 56 per cent year-on-year, while secured retail advances increased 52 per cent. Unsecured retail advances grew 6 per cent. The management said it expects the approximately 30 per cent underlying loan growth to remain the baseline going forward.“Our baseline is around 30 per cent. We will maintain the baseline which we have achieved in March and June,” the CEO said. The bank plans to deploy the liquidity raised through FCNR(B) mobilisation and its capital raise into highly rated corporates, wholesale opportunities and secured retail lending. Advt Subramaniakumar said the bank had initially deployed part of the short-term liquidity into short-term loans, rather than leaving the funds in money-market instruments, and would gradually transition this into medium- and long-term lending.“We have deployed into short-term loans instead of putting it in money market. Over a period of time, we will convert that into medium and long-term,” he said. How did Q2 FY27 fare for RBL Bank?RBL Bank’s net profit rose 170 per cent year-on-year to Rs 482 crore, while net interest income grew 37 per cent to Rs 2,131 crore. Net interest margin stood at 4.61 per cent, with core fee income increasing 12 per cent. Asset quality also improved, with gross NPA declining to 1.02 per cent from 1.30 per cent in the previous quarter and net NPA at 0.29 per cent. Credit cost stood at 63 basis points, including an 11-basis-point standard asset provision.
