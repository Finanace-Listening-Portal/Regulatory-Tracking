Summary : Ind AS 12 permits a deferred tax asset for unused tax losses and unused tax credits only to the extent it is probable that future taxable profit will be available for utilisation. Recent losses are themselves strong contrary evidence. An entity with a history of losses therefore needs sufficient taxable temporary differences or convincing other evidence before recognising the asset. A board-approved profit forecast alone is not automatically enough: the analysis must respect the tax law’s entity, head-of-income, expiry and set-off restrictions, reconcile accounting projections to taxable profit, and avoid counting the same taxable source twice. The recognised amount must be reviewed at every reporting date, written down when utilisation is no longer probable, and increased only when new evidence supports recovery. This guide develops an audit-ready recognition file covering loss-ageing, reversal schedules, forecast reliability, tax-planning opportunities, tax-rate measurement, disclosure and subsequent reassessment. It also distinguishes the Ind AS 12 probability test from the different virtual-certainty language used in AS 22, because applying the wrong framework can materially misstate both tax expense and net assets. Key Takeaways Show all 23 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Recognition Begins with the Tax Attribute, Not the Accounting Loss A loss in the statement of profit and loss is not automatically an unused tax loss. The deferred tax analysis begins with the tax return or legally supportable computation: the nature of the tax loss or credit, the tax year of origin, amount available, expiry date, permitted set-off, ownership conditions and any restrictions following changes in business or shareholding. The balance must be reconciled to assessments, returns and notices. Disputed or unfiled attributes should be separately identified rather than included as certain recoverable amounts. Ind AS 12 paragraph 34 requires recognition only to the extent that future taxable profit is probable. Paragraph 35 explains that unused tax losses are strong evidence that taxable profit may not be available. When recent losses exist, recognition is limited to available taxable temporary differences or convincing other evidence of sufficient future taxable profit. The conclusion must be specific to the same taxation authority and taxable entity and to the period in which utilisation is legally possible. Utilisation model and sensitivity analysis Four Ind AS 12 Criteria Structure the Probability Assessment Taxable Temporary Differences Must Reverse in the Right Window A deferred tax liability or other taxable temporary difference can support recognition when it relates to the same taxation authority and taxable entity and will reverse in a period in which the loss or credit can be used. A gross deferred tax liability balance is not sufficient evidence by itself. The schedule should show expected reversal dates, the character of taxable income, applicable set-off restrictions and whether the tax attribute expires before the reversal. Reversals already used to support another deferred tax asset cannot be counted again. Forecast Taxable Profit Must Be Distinguished from Accounting EBITDA Budgets commonly start with revenue, EBITDA or profit before tax. The recognition test concerns taxable profit after relevant tax adjustments. The forecast should bridge accounting profit to taxable income, including depreciation differences, disallowances, exempt income, carried-forward items and reversal of temporary differences. It should exclude future tax benefits arising from the same deductible differences being assessed; otherwise the model becomes circular. Loss Causes Should Be Identifiable and Unlikely to Recur A one-time plant shutdown, non-recurring litigation cost or defined start-up phase may support a recovery case if the underlying evidence shows that the cause has ended. Merely labelling a cost “exceptional” is not convincing. The file should identify the event, quantify its tax effect, show why it will not recur and reconcile subsequent actual performance. Persistent pricing weakness, customer concentration, under-utilisation or repeated forecast misses weigh against recognition. Tax Planning Opportunities Must Be Lawful and Feasible Ind AS 12 permits consideration of tax-planning opportunities that create or increase taxable income in the period of utilisation. The opportunity must be practically available, economically rational and within management’s control. A hypothetical asset sale, group transaction or change in financing is not enough when approvals, ownership, marketability or commercial intent are absent. Any action should be analysed for all tax consequences, transaction costs and accounting effects. Convincing Other Evidence Requires More Than an Approved Forecast The standard does not prescribe a closed evidence list. Strong evidence may include non-cancellable customer contracts, committed order books with demonstrated margins, regulated tariffs already approved, executed cost reductions, contractual price resets, post-reporting performance consistent with the plan, or a reliable history of forecasting taxable results. Evidence should exist at the reporting date or appropriately illuminate conditions at that date. New conditions arising later are not backdated simply to support recognition. Management should test forecast reliability using prior budgets versus actual outcomes. Large unexplained misses, repeated forecast extensions and dependence on unapproved capital or restructuring weaken the case. The forecast horizon should not extend beyond what can be estimated reliably merely because tax losses expire later. Sensitivities should cover revenue, margin, timing, exchange rates, financing costs and the utilisation sequence. A deferred tax asset is recognised only for the amount that remains probable under the supportable scenario, not necessarily the full legal balance. The schedule should operate year by year because legal expiry and forecast profit occur through time. Where losses do not expire, timing still matters because forecast uncertainty increases with the horizon and because management must substantiate utilisation. The tax rate used is the enacted or substantively enacted rate expected when the asset is realised, consistent with Ind AS 12. Measurement is not discounted even when utilisation is expected several years later. Reassess Recognised and Unrecognised Assets at Every Reporting Date Write Down When Recovery Is No Longer Probable The carrying amount of a deferred tax asset is reviewed at each reporting date. If forecast profit declines, a major customer is lost, the expiry window shortens or a tax position changes, the asset is reduced to the amount still probable. The write-down is recognised in the same location as the underlying tax effect, subject to the standard’s presentation requirements. Delaying the review until the loss legally expires can overstate net assets for several periods. Recognise Previously Unrecognised Amounts Only on New Support An improvement in trading conditions, signed contracts or the emergence of taxable temporary differences may justify recognition of a previously unrecognised asset. The memo should identify what changed since the prior reporting date, why the evidence is stronger and how much additional utilisation is now probable. A reversal of an earlier write-down is limited to the amount supported by the current assessment. Disclosure and Governance for Loss-Related Deferred Tax Assets When recognition depends on future taxable profit and the entity has suffered a loss in the current or preceding period in the relevant jurisdiction, Ind AS 12 requires disclosure of the amount of the deferred tax asset and the nature of the evidence supporting recognition. Other disclosures include unused tax losses and credits for which no deferred tax asset is recognised, together with expiry information where relevant. The notes should be consistent with going-concern disclosures, impairment models, liquidity forecasts and segment outlook. Tax return and assessment reconciliation signed by the tax owner. Loss-ageing and expiry schedule by legal category and jurisdiction. Taxable temporary-difference reversal schedule with no double counting. Accounting-profit to taxable-profit bridge for every forecast year. Prior-forecast accuracy analysis and downside sensitivities. Board or audit-committee approval of assumptions and disclosures. Quarterly trigger list for customer loss, legal change, expiry and forecast underperformance. Do Not Import the AS 22 Recognition Threshold into Ind AS 12 Entities applying Ind AS 12 use its probability model and the specific stronger evidence discipline when recent losses exist. AS 22, applicable in a different accounting framework, uses reasonable certainty and, for unabsorbed depreciation or carry-forward losses, virtual certainty supported by convincing evidence. The terminology and mechanics should not be blended. The accounting framework applicable to the reporting entity must be identified before preparing the memo, and group reporting packages should reconcile differences where components use another framework. Frequently Asked Questions Does an unused tax loss automatically create a deferred tax asset? No. Recognition is limited to the amount for which sufficient future taxable profit is probable under Ind AS 12. Why do recent losses require stronger support? They are strong evidence that future taxable profit may not be available, so taxable temporary differences or convincing other evidence is required. Can a board-approved business plan support the full asset? Approval alone is not decisive. The plan must translate into reliable taxable profit before expiry and be supported by evidence and forecast performance. Can a deferred tax liability support recognition? Yes, when the related taxable temporary difference belongs to the same taxation authority and entity and reverses while the loss or credit can be used. Should the future taxable-profit model include the benefit being tested? No. Taxable profit should be evaluated without circularly including future tax benefits arising from the deductible differences under assessment. Are deferred tax assets discounted? No. Ind AS 12 does not permit discounting of deferred tax assets or liabilities. What happens when the forecast deteriorates? The recognised asset is written down to the amount that remains probable. The assessment is refreshed at each reporting date. Is the Ind AS 12 test the same as AS 22 virtual certainty? No. They are different accounting frameworks with different recognition language and should not be mixed. Key Takeaways Recognition is based on legally available tax attributes and probable utilisation, not the accounting loss alone. Recent losses demand taxable temporary differences or convincing other evidence. Forecasts must be converted to taxable profit and tested against expiry and set-off rules. Every reporting date requires a fresh review of recognised and unrecognised assets. The amount and nature of supporting evidence may require specific disclosure. ***** Disclaimer: This article is an educational accounting guide to selected deferred-tax-asset requirements in Ind AS 12 and does not provide an accounting conclusion, tax opinion, valuation, audit opinion or legal interpretation for any entity.
