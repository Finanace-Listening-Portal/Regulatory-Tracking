Banks, NBFCs, brokers and agents are seeking more time to adjust to the proposed commission caps, warning that steep cuts could undermine distribution economics.Bank and non-bank insurance distributors are seeking a three-to-five-year transition to the proposed commission caps, as the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (Irdai) prepares to overhaul insurance distribution costs. The Finance Industry Development Council (FIDC), which represents NBFCs, has proposed reducing commissions on credit-linked insurance from the current 45-49% to 25% over five years, instead of an immediate reduction to the proposed caps of 2% for single-premium credit-life policies, 2.5% for multi-year credit-life policies and 5% for credit-health policies. Advt The Confederation of General Insurance Agents Associations of India has sought at least three years, while the Insurance Brokers Association of India has proposed a phased transition aligned with the five-year timeline for insurers' expenses of management (EoM) limits. Smaller and newer insurers have also sought around three years to adjust their cost structures and distribution networks.Irdai has received more than 650 submissions on its consultation paper, Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution, and extended the deadline for feedback to October 31 from October 25. The proposals include product- and channel-specific commission caps, tighter EoM limits, restrictions on indirect remuneration and a ban on compulsory bundling of insurance with loans.Insurers could gain, but policyholder benefits are uncertainLower commissions could reduce insurers' acquisition costs and improve margins, particularly for companies dependent on banks, NBFCs and brokers for distribution. However, the savings will not automatically translate into lower premiums. Unless insurers pass them on through pricing, improved benefits or better service, a significant part of the gain could accrue to shareholders.The proposed EoM limits add another layer to the cost reduction exercise. Life insurers would have to bring EoM down to 15% of gross direct premium income within two years and 12.5% within five years. For general insurers, Irdai has proposed shifting the benchmark from gross written premium to domestic gross direct premium income and reducing the limit from 30% to 20% over five years. Advt The regulator is also seeking greater transparency in distributor remuneration and tighter controls on incentives linked to sales volumes. The proposals include restrictions on volume- and reward-linked incentives for bank and NBFC employees selling insurance, as well as stronger accountability for mis-selling and clawbacks of commissions.Distributors face a sharp resetThe impact could vary significantly across distribution channels. NBFCs selling credit-linked insurance alongside loans face particularly steep cuts. FIDC estimates that distribution costs account for around 32% of premiums, including customer acquisition, technology, branch infrastructure, compliance and servicing. It has warned that the proposed limits could make the business unviable for some lenders and force them to scale down or exit insurance distribution. Advt Brokers have warned that the proposed changes could reduce their revenues by 60-70%, affecting employment and the viability of smaller distribution businesses. Banks could also face pressure on bancassurance income, particularly where insurance distribution contributes significantly to fee-based revenue.Individual agents face a different challenge. Lower payouts could affect their earnings and the incentive to sell smaller policies that require considerable customer engagement relative to the premium earned. Industry bodies have also warned that reduced remuneration could weaken last-mile distribution, particularly in rural and semi-urban markets. Advt The regulator's intervention follows a sharp rise in distribution costs. Commissions paid through general insurance brokers increased 173% between FY23 and FY25, while premiums sourced through them grew 37%. Average commission rates rose from 8.5% to 17% over the same period. The proposed framework seeks to link payouts more closely to product complexity and the effort required to sell and service policies.The eventual impact will depend on the final commission structure and how insurers and distributors adjust their business models. Lower selling costs could improve affordability, but a withdrawal of distributors from less profitable segments could restrict access to insurance. The challenge for Irdai is to curb excessive commissions and mis-selling without weakening the distribution network needed to expand insurance penetration.
