(ITAT Delhi) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Delhi Bench, dismissed the Revenue’s appeals concerning PNB Gilts Ltd. for Assessment Years 2012-13 and 2013-14 and upheld the relief granted by the CIT(A). For AY 2012-13, the Assessing Officer had made disallowances of ₹4.26 crore under Section 14A read with Rule 8D, ₹30.61 lakh under Section 36(1)(iii) and ₹6.56 crore towards diminution in the market value of securities held as stock-in-trade. The Tribunal noted that the assessee, an RBI-registered NBFC and Primary Dealer, held the relevant securities as stock-in-trade in its ordinary securities-trading business. It had interest-free funds of ₹577.64 crore, substantially exceeding the securities yielding exempt income, and also earned net positive interest income. The Tribunal further observed that a valid and reasoned satisfaction was necessary before invoking Rule 8D and that the availability of sufficient interest-free funds supported the presumption that the securities were acquired from such funds. The separate disallowance under Section 36(1)(iii) was also unsustainable because the borrowings were used for business purposes and the same interest expenditure could not effectively be disallowed twice. Regarding diminution in value, the Tribunal accepted that the securities were consistently valued at cost or market value, whichever was lower, following RBI guidelines and recognised accounting principles. The resulting ₹6.56 crore diminution represented a permissible valuation loss rather than an unascertained provision. As the facts and grounds for AY 2013-14 were identical, the findings for AY 2012-13 were applied mutatis mutandis, and both Revenue appeals were dismissed. Key Takeaways Sufficient interest-free funds supported deletion of the interest disallowance under Section 14A read with Rule 8D(2)(ii). Rule 8D could not be mechanically invoked without recording proper dissatisfaction after examining the assessee’s accounts. Interest on borrowings used for the securities-trading business remained deductible under Section 36(1)(iii). The same interest expenditure could not effectively be disallowed under both Section 14A and Section 36(1)(iii). Diminution in securities held as stock-in-trade was allowable where valuation at cost or market value, whichever was lower, was consistently followed in accordance with RBI norms. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Delhi Bench, dismissed both Revenue appeals concerning PNB Gilts Ltd. for Assessment Years 2012-13 and 2013-14. The appeals arose from orders of the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)-7, New Delhi, which had partly allowed the assessee’s appeals against assessment orders passed under section 143(3) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The assessee is a Non-Banking Financial Company registered with the Reserve Bank of India and engaged in trading in Government securities, debt instruments and market instruments. For AY 2012-13, the assessee had declared income of Rs.25,77,78,259/-. The Assessing Officer made additions of Rs.4,26,85,000/-, Rs.30,61,000/- and Rs.6,56,24,000/-. The CIT(A) sustained a disallowance of Rs.34.25 lakhs but deleted the balance disallowance of Rs.3,92,60,000/- under section 14A, besides deleting the additions of Rs.30,61,000/- under section 36(1)(iii) and Rs.6,56,24,000/- towards diminution in market value of stock-in-trade. On the section 14A issue, the assessee submitted that the shares and bonds yielding exempt income were held as stock-in-trade in the ordinary course of its securities trading business. The exempt income comprised dividend of Rs.4,78,249/- and interest on tax-free bonds of Rs.4,15,80,043/-. The assessee contended that earning exempt income was incidental to its business and that no expenditure had been incurred for earning such income. It also submitted that borrowings were mandatorily invested in accordance with RBI guidelines and that sufficient interest-free funds were available. The Tribunal recorded that the CIT(A) had relied upon the availability of interest-free funds of Rs.577.64 crores against the securities generating exempt income. The assessee also relied upon Maxopp Investment Ltd. v. CIT and the distinction recognised in respect of shares held as stock-in-trade, as well as CIT v. Walfort Share & Stock Brokers (P.) Ltd.. Reliance was also placed on Godrej & Boyce Manufacturing Company Ltd. v. Deputy Commissioner of Income-tax concerning the requirement of recording proper satisfaction before applying Rule 8D. The assessee further relied upon the principle that where sufficient interest-free funds are available, investment is presumed to have been made from such funds. In support, reliance was placed on CIT v. Reliance Utilities & Power Ltd., CIT-2, Mumbai v. HDFC Bank Ltd., CIT v. Reliance Industries Ltd. and Pr. CIT v. Ashok Apparels (P.) Ltd. The assessee also pointed out that its interest/discount income of Rs.17,528.20 lakhs exceeded interest expenditure of Rs.12,567.26 lakhs, resulting in a net surplus of Rs.4,960.94 lakhs. In relation to section 36(1)(iii), the assessee contended that borrowed funds had been used exclusively for business purposes and that securities were held as stock-in-trade. It argued that the mere earning of tax-free income during business operations did not establish diversion of borrowed funds for non-business purposes. The assessee also contended that the same interest expenditure could not be disallowed both under section 14A and section 36(1)(iii). The Tribunal recorded the issue as covered by the assessee’s own case, DCIT v. PNB Gilts Ltd., ITA No.810/Del/2015 for AY 2010-11. The third issue concerned the Rs.6,56,24,000/- provision for diminution in the market value of stock-in-trade. The assessee explained that the provision was created at year-end and reversed in the immediately succeeding year, in accordance with its regular accounting policy. Securities were consistently valued at cost or market value whichever was lower. The assessee, being a Primary Dealer regulated by RBI, relied upon RBI guidelines requiring valuation of securities and recognition of net depreciation where the market value was lower than cost. The assessee relied upon section 145A and the Supreme Court decision in United Commercial Bank v. CIT [1999] 240 ITR 355 (SC), submitting that diminution arising from valuation of stock-in-trade at cost or market value whichever is lower constituted an allowable business loss. After considering the rival contentions, the Tribunal held that the issues were covered by the assessee’s own case and the judicial principles relied upon. It rejected the Revenue’s grounds and dismissed the appeal for AY 2012-13. Since the facts and grounds for AY 2013-14 were stated to be identical, the Tribunal applied its observations for AY 2012-13 mutatis mutandis to ITA No.7434/Del/2017. Consequently, both Revenue appeals were dismissed. Cases Discussed Maxopp Investment Ltd. v. Commissioner of Income Tax, New Delhi [2018] 402 ITR 640 (SC). CIT v. Walfort Share & Stock Brokers (P.) Where the market value at the end of the year is lower than the cost, the difference is recognized as diminution in the value of stock-in-trade, which represents a legitimate business loss. It is also clarified that such diminution is computed on the actual market value as on the balance sheet date. 6.19. The allowability of such diminution in value of securities held as stock-in-trade stands settled by judicial precedents, including the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in United Commercial Bank v. Even while invoking Rule 8D, the Assessing Officer failed to provide cogent reasons for rejecting the factual submissions made. The alternate plea raised during assessment for limited disallowance under Rule 8D(2)(iii) cannot operate as estoppel against law. It is trite that there is no estoppel against statute, as held by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in CIT v. C. Parakh & Co. (India) Ltd. [1956] 29 ITR 661 (SC) and by the Division Bench in CIT v. Bharat General Reinsurance Co. Ltd. [1971] 81 ITR 303 (Delhi). Tax can be levied only in accordance with law, as mandated by Article 265 of the Constitution. 7.6 The AO also disallowed an amount of Rs. 30.61 lacs under Section 36(1)(iii) of the Act holding that the Assessee has earned substantial tax-free interest income and therefore, use of funds to earn tax free income cannot be allowed. The AO also failed to take into account the fact that Assessee had sufficient capital and reserve and surplus amounting to Rs. 577.64 crores which is in excess of amount of bonds and shares on which tax-free income has been earned and therefore, it is to be presumed that the tax-free income is earned on account of interest free funds available with the Appellant. The issue under consideration also stands covered by Assessee own case DCIT v. PNB Gilts Ltd. (ITA No. 810/Del/2015 (Λ.Y. 2010-11). 7.7 The AO disallowed the provision for diminution in market value of the stock amounting to Rs. 6,56,24,000/-. In this regard, it was submitted by the Assessee that the said provision is created at the end of the year and immediately reversed in the next year and it is in the normal course of business as per the regular accounting policies followed by the Appellant. Following the accounting standards and regular accounting policies followed by the Appellant, the stock in trade is valued at cost or market value whichever is lower, as also accepted under section 145A of the Act. Any change in the value at the end of the year is reversed in the immediately succeeding year to bring it back to the book value. 7.8 The allowability of such diminution in value of securities held as stock-in-trade stands settled by judicial precedents, including the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in United Commercial Bank v. CIT [1999] 240 ITR 355 (SC), wherein it has been held that banks are entitled to value their stock-in-trade at cost or market value whichever is lower, and the resultant diminution represents an allowable deduction. 7.9 The depreciation in value of securities provided in the books of account represents a legitimate valuation loss of stock-in-trade, computed in accordance with RBI guidelines, recognized accounting principles, and the consistent method of accounting followed by the assessee. Therefore, the same has rightly been claimed as a deduction while computing the taxable income. 8. In view of the above, grounds of appeal are rejected. Appeal of the Revenue is dismissed. Year 2013-14. 10. In the final result, both the appeals filed by the Revenue are dismissed. Order is pronounced in the Open Court on 25.03.2026.
