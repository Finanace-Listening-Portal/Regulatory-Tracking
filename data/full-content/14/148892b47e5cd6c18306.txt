Articles 4 min read Cabinet approves Rs 52,878 crore IDBI Bank sale to Fairfax arm in India’s largest financial-sector FDI The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs has approved the strategic sale of IDBI Bank to a Fairfax Group arm, Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said. The government and Life Insurance Corporation of India will retain a combined 34% stake in the restructured entity. Vaishnaw called it India’s largest financial-sector FDI. ET Online Published On Oct 14, 2026 at 04:34 PM IST var news_msid = 134940508; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Union Cabinet on Wednesday approved the sale of a 60.72% stake in IDBI Bank to Fairfax Financial Holdings’ subsidiary, M/s 10955230 Canada Inc., for Rs 52,878.08 crore, paving the way for a major restructuring of the lender. The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) approved the Canadian entity as the strategic buyer, according to an official press note.The transaction involves the sale of the government’s 30.48% stake and Life Insurance Corporation of India’s (LIC) 30.24% holding in IDBI Bank. Following the proposed transaction, the government will retain a 15% stake and LIC 19%, taking their combined holding to 34%. Advt Once completed, the transaction will be the largest foreign direct investment (FDI) in India’s financial sector, Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said.Fairfax arm wins competitive bidding processThe strategic sale followed a two-stage competitive bidding process overseen by a multi-layered decision-making mechanism comprising the Inter-Ministerial Group, the Core Group of Secretaries on Disinvestment and an empowered group of Cabinet ministers.The government issued a preliminary information memorandum inviting expressions of interest on October 7, 2022. Five parties initially submitted expressions of interest, of which four qualified under the prescribed eligibility criteria. Two ultimately submitted technical and financial bids.Prospective bidders were required to secure clearance from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) under its “fit and proper” assessment process and obtain security clearance from the Ministry of Home Affairs before participating in the bidding stage.The initial bids, invited in the financial year 2025-26, fell short of the reserve price of Rs 94.42 per share and were not approved. The government subsequently invited fresh sealed bids reflecting the qualified bidders’ best offers in FY27.Two independent professional valuation agencies assessed the bank using separate methodologies. The average fair valuation was Rs 75.99 per share, which served as the benchmark for evaluating the bids. Fairfax’s successful bid of Rs 81 per share was above the benchmark as well as the fair value estimates of both agencies, and was consequently considered reasonable and approved by the CCEA, the press note said. Advt The successful bidder will have to present its credentials to the RBI and submit a roadmap for complying with the regulatory conditions prescribed by the central bank. The CCEA has also delegated powers to the empowered decision-making mechanism to undertake minority stake sales of the government’s residual 15% holding through the open market, in accordance with the applicable procedure. LIC will manage its remaining stake in line with its internal procedures and regulatory requirements.IDBI Bank’s financial health improvesIDBI Bank was established as a development financial institution in 1964 and converted into a commercial bank in 2004. The RBI has categorised it as a private-sector bank since January 21, 2019, while the government’s holding has remained below 51% since 2019. Advt The lender had faced financial stress and required repeated capital infusions from the government and LIC. Between 2010 and 2020, the government infused around Rs 25,000 crore into the bank.The RBI placed IDBI Bank under its Prompt Corrective Action framework on May 5, 2017, imposing restrictions because of its non-performing assets and losses. The central bank lifted the restrictions on March 10, 2021, subject to conditions and continued monitoring.The bank’s asset quality has since improved. Its gross non-performing assets declined from 31.78% in September 2018 to 2.32% in March 2026, while net NPAs fell from 17.30% to 0.15% over the same period. Its capital adequacy ratio rose from 6.22% in 2018 to 26.65% in March 2026. Advt IDBI Bank reported a net profit of Rs 9,513 crore in FY26. Its total business stood at Rs 6,27,149 crore as of September 30, 2026, marking an 18% increase over a year earlier, according to the official factsheet.The restructuring will not alter the RBI’s regulatory and prudential oversight of the bank. Deposits will continue to be insured up to Rs 5 lakh per depositor under the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation framework.IDBI Bank will also continue to provide financial inclusion, social security and pension schemes, including the Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana, Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana, Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana and Atal Pension Yojana. Its micro-credit and livelihood support programmes, including loans under the Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana, will continue.
