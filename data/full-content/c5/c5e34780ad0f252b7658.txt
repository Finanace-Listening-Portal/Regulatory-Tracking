(Photo: Shutterstock)Vrinda Goel New Delhi 3 min read Last Updated : Oct 14 2026 | 11:14 AM IST @media (min-width:1100px) and (max-width:1280px) { .storymeta .meta-info { flex: 0 0 45%; } } Listen to This ArticleBank of Baroda (BoB) on Wednesday responded to a lawsuit filed by NMC Healthcare group founder Bavaguthu Raghuram Shetty seeking $15 billion in damages. In an exchange filing, the bank said it had seen media reports of a suit said to have been filed by Shetty in the Bombay High Court on October 13, 2026. The bank said it had not been served with the suit and could not comment on the contents of the pleadings, which had not been tested in court. It added that if and when it was served, it would defend the suit firmly and take all necessary steps to protect its interests. What’s the lawsuit about? BR Shetty, founder of the NMC Healthcare group, filed a lawsuit in the Bombay High Court on Tuesday seeking $15 billion in damages from state-owned Bank of Baroda and 25 other defendants, alleging that the bank and former NMC executives enabled the diversion of funds, concealed liabilities and subsequently pursued him on guarantees bearing forged signatures. Shetty has sought $5 billion each in direct and consequential damages against the defendants jointly and severally, and a further $5 billion from BoB for alleged reputational damage and loss of goodwill. He has also sought interest at 18 per cent a year. The suit names BoB and 10 of its officers, whom Shetty alleges hold assets acquired using diverted funds.
