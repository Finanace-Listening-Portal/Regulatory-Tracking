Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Azure Photovoltaic Private Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) Short Deduction of TDS Does Not Warrant Disallowance U/s 40(a)(i) or Revision U/s 263 on That Ground: ITAT Delhi Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Delhi Bench, held in Azure Photovoltaic Private Limited Vs Deputy Commissioner of Income Tax, Circle 1(1), Delhi that an alleged shortfall in tax deducted at source did not justify disallowance of interest under Section 40(a)(i) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Since the Assessing Officer could not lawfully make that disallowance on the ground of short deduction, omitting it did not make the assessment erroneous for purposes of Section 263. The Tribunal allowed the company’s appeal and set aside the revisional order. Facts and Assessment Proceedings Azure Photovoltaic Private Limited, part of the Azure Power Group, was engaged in setting up power plants and generating power. For Assessment Year 2020–21, the company declared nil total income after setting off ₹9,33,48,920 in brought-forward unabsorbed depreciation against income of an equal amount. The return was selected for complete scrutiny. The issues flagged included high creditors or liabilities, international related-party lending or borrowing, unsecured loans, foreign outward remittances and capital gains or income from the sale of property. The Assessing Officer issued notices under Sections 143(2) and 142(1), and the company furnished its responses. The assessment order dated 31 October 2023, passed under Section 143(3) read with Sections 144C(3) and 144B, made an addition of ₹4,03,23,401 on account of a transfer pricing adjustment. Grounds for Revision Under Section 263 The Principal Commissioner of Income Tax (PCIT) issued a show cause notice on 10 November 2025. After considering the company’s reply, the PCIT set aside the assessment by an order dated 30 March 2026 and directed the Assessing Officer to pass a fresh order after conducting enquiries and giving the company an opportunity to be heard. The principal direction was to disallow ₹13,98,27,316 under Section 40(a)(i). The company had deducted tax at 7.5% on the interest payment, while a separate order dated 29 March 2024 under Sections 201(1) and 201(1A) applied a rate of 18.93%. The PCIT also directed the Assessing Officer to deny carry-forward of interest of the same amount under Section 94B and to enquire into the difference between turnover reported in the income tax return and the GST returns. Assessee’s Challenge to the Revisional Order The company argued that the Assessing Officer had completed the assessment after detailed enquiries and that the PCIT had failed to establish the conditions for revision. It disputed the application of Explanation 2(a) and 2(b) to Section 263, contending that the PCIT had relied on the separate TDS order without conducting an independent examination. The company had already challenged that order before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals). The company argued that the rate dispute concerned short deduction of TDS and therefore could not support disallowance under Section 40(a)(i). It also contended that the proposed disallowance overlapped with the transfer pricing adjustment already made in the assessment, raising a risk of double disallowance. On the turnover issue, it maintained that the difference arose from accounting treatment and was revenue neutral. The Departmental Representative relied on the PCIT’s order. Tribunal’s Findings on Short Deduction of TDS The Tribunal followed the coordinate bench decision in Azure Green Tech Private Limited Vs DCIT, which addressed a similar dispute about TDS on interest. In that decision, the coordinate bench applied the Delhi High Court’s ruling in PCIT Vs Future First Info Services Private Limited, 447 ITR 299 (Delhi). The High Court had held, in the context of Section 40(a)(ia), that short deduction of tax did not warrant an expenditure disallowance and that a shortfall could be addressed under Section 201. The coordinate bench applied the same reasoning to interest disallowance under Section 40(a)(i). The distinction mattered because tax had admittedly been deducted at 7.5%. Even on the PCIT’s premise that 18.93% applied, the allegation was that the company had deducted too little tax. The Tribunal could therefore decide the validity of the revisional direction without determining the correct withholding rate. The cited coordinate bench decision also treated directions concerning Section 94B carry-forward and initiation of Section 271C penalty proceedings as consequential to its conclusion on the principal disallowance. Enquiry Required to Justify Revision The Tribunal also referred to PCIT Vs Delhi Airport Metro Express Private Limited, where the Delhi High Court held that the PCIT had to undertake the basic enquiry needed to establish the alleged error before exercising revisional jurisdiction. That precedent addressed the need for the PCIT to substantiate the alleged defect in the assessment before directing the Assessing Officer to revisit it. Applying these precedents, the Tribunal held that the PCIT had erred in exercising jurisdiction under Section 263. On 20 November 2026, the Delhi Bench set aside the revisional order and allowed the assessee’s appeal in ITA No. 6208/DEL/2026. Cases Discussed: Azure Green Tech Private Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi); ITA Nos. 6198 & 6199/DEL/2026; 02/09/2026. The coordinate Bench held that short deduction of tax at source could not justify disallowance of interest under Section 40(a)(i) and consequently could not provide a valid basis for revision under Section 263. The High Court held that short deduction of TDS could be addressed under Section 201 instead of making a disallowance under Section 40(a)(ia). A Co-ordinate Bench of the Tribunal in order dated 02.09.2026 in ITA No. 6198 and 6199/Del/2026 Page No. 262 to 268 read as under: “9. From the above, it could be seen that ld PCIT had invoked revision jurisdiction effectively for effecting disallowance of interest u/s 40a(i) of Act on account of short deduction of tax at source on interest paid to Azure Power Energy Ltd, Mauritius. It is not in dispute that assessee had deducted tax at source @7.5 % on interest paid to the said party. The case of the ld PCIT is TDS should have been done by the assessee @18.88 %. Now, the short question that arises for our consideration is as to whether any disallowance under section 40a(i) of the Act per se could be made for short deduction of tax at source. This issue is no longer res integra in view of the decision of Hon’ble Jurisdictional Delhi High Court in the case of PCIT Vs. Future First Info Services Private Limited reported in 447 ITR 299 (Del). The relevant operative portion of the said judgement is reproduced below:- “5. Further, this Court is of the opinion that in cases of short deduction of TDS, disallowance under Section 40a(ia) of the Act cannot be made and the correct course of action would have been to invoke Section 201 of the Act. On similar facts, the Calcutta High Court in CIT vs SK Tekriwal [2012 SCC Online CAL 12147] dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. The relevant para of the said judgement is reproduced herein below: “We are satisfied that the order under challenge is a just order. The reasoning appearing at paragraph 6 of the judgment and/or order under challenge reads as follows: “In the present case before us the assessee has deducted tax u/s. 194C(2) of the Act being payments made to sub-contractors and it is not a case of non-deduction of tax or no deduction of tax as is the import of section 40a(ia) of the Act. But the revenue’s contention is that the payments are in the nature of machinery hire charges falling under the head ‘rent’ and the previous provisions of section 194I of the Act are applicable. According to revenue, the assessee has deducted tax @ 1% u/s. 194C(2) of the Act as against the actual deduction to be made at 10% u/s. 194I of the Act, thereby lesser deduction of tax. The revenue has made out a case of lesser deduction of tax and that also under different head and accordingly disallowed the payments proportionately by invoking the provisions of section 40(a)(ia) of the Act. The Ld. CIT, DR also argued that there is no word like failure used in section 40(a)(ia) of the Act and it referred to only non-deduction of tax and disallowance of such payments. According to him, it does not refer to genuineness of the payment or otherwise but addition u/s. 40(a)(ia) can be made even though payments are genuine but tax is not deducted as required u/s.40(a)(ia) of the Act. We are of the view that the conditions laid down u/s.40(a)(ia) of the Act for making addition is that tax is deductible at source and such tax has not been deducted. If both the conditions are satisfied then such payment can be disallowed u/s. 40(a)(ia) of the Act but where tax is deducted by the assessee, even under bonafide wrong impression, under wrong provisions of TDS, the provisions of section 40(a)(ia) of the Act cannot be invoked. Here in the present case before us, the assessee has deducted tax u/s. 194C(2) of the Act and not u/s. 194I of the Act and there is no allegation that this TDS is not deposited with the Government account. We are of the view that the provisions of section 40(a)(ia) of the Act has two limbs one is where, inter alia, assessee has to deduct tax and the second where after deducting tax, inter alia, the assessee has to pay into Government Account. There is nothing in the said section to treat, inter alia, the assessee as defaulter where there is a shortfall in deduction. With regard to the shortfall, it cannot be assumed that there is a default as the deduction is not as required by or under the Act, but the facts is that this expression, ‘on which tax is deductible at source under Chapter XVII-B and such tax has not been deducted or, after deduction has not been paid on or before the due date specified in sub-section (1) of section 139’. This section 40(a)(ia) of the Act refers only to the duty to deduct tax and pay to government account. If there is any shortfall due to any difference of opinion as to the taxability of any item or the nature of payments falling under various TDS provisions, the assessee can be declared to be an assessee in default u/s. 201 of the Act and no disallowance can be made by invoking the provisions of section 40(a)(ia) of the Act.” (emphasis supplied)” 10. Respectively following the same, we hold that there cannot be any disallowance u/s 40a(i) of the Act on the subject mentioned transaction of payment of interest by the assessee to Azure Power Energy Ltd, Mauritius. The order u/s 201 of the Act has already been passed in the hands of the assessee which is agitated by the assessee before the ld CIT(A). Since, there cannot be any disallowance that could be legally made u/s 40a(i) of the Act by the ld AO for short deduction of tax at source, the order of the ld AO could not be construed as erroneous warranting revision u/s 263 of the Act on merits of the case. Hence, the direction of the ld PCIT to disallow interest u/s 40a(i) of the Act is hereby dismissed. 11. In view of our aforesaid decision, the other directions of ld PCIT to deny carry forward of interest disallowed u/s 94B of the Act; to record clear findings regarding the components of disallowance said to be ineligible for carry forward; to take steps to initiate penalty proceedings u/s 271C of the Act for alleged short deduction of TDS, would become consequential. Hence, the grounds raised by the assessee for AY 2020-21 are hereby allowed.” 8.4 Hon’ble High Court of Delhi in order dated 05.09.2017 titled as PCIT Delhi Vs. Delhi Airport Metro Express Pvt. Ltd. in Para No. 11 read as under:- “In the considered view of the Court, this can hardly constitute the reasons required to be given by the PCIT to justify the exercise of jurisdiction under Section 263 of the Act. In the context of the present case if, as urged by the Revenue, the Assessee has wrongly claimed depreciation on assets like land and building, it was incumbent upon the PCIT to undertake an inquiry as regards which of the assets were purchased and installed by the Assessee out of its own funds during the AY in question and, which were those assets that were handed over to it by the DMRC.
