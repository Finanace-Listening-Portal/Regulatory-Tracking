Summary: The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has introduced significant reforms to food business registration and licensing in 2026, changing the turnover thresholds, validity requirements and compliance procedures applicable to Food Business Operators. With effect from 1 April 2026, the general turnover limit for FSSAI registration has increased to ₹1.5 crore, State licensing generally covers turnover above ₹1.5 crore and up to ₹50 crore, and Central licensing applies to businesses exceeding ₹50 crore, subject to special activity-specific criteria. The reforms also provide perpetual validity for registrations and licences, eliminating periodic renewal applications while retaining applicable annual fees, returns and food safety obligations. Qualifying street food vendors registered under the Street Vendors Act, 2014, may also benefit from deemed registration. Choosing the appropriate authorisation requires considering both annual turnover and the actual nature of the food business. Home kitchens, restaurants, cloud kitchens, manufacturers, retailers, importers and e-commerce food operators may face different eligibility conditions and documentation requirements. Businesses must accurately identify their activities and operating premises, prepare the relevant identity, constitution, premises and technical records, and apply through the Food Safety Compliance System (FoSCoS). Existing operators must review migration requirements and ensure that current licences reflect their activities. Compliance responsibilities continue after approval, including hygiene, supplier controls, storage, traceability, applicable annual returns and regulatory payments. The article explains the revised registration and licensing framework, application documents, practical compliance requirements, common mistakes and frequently asked questions. A home bakery, restaurant, cloud kitchen, grocery shop or food manufacturing unit must also identify the food safety requirements applicable to its operations. One of the first questions business owners ask is whether they need FSSAI registration, a State Licence or a Central Licence. Choosing the correct category matters because the application, documents, fees and continuing obligations depend on the business activity and applicable eligibility conditions. The 2026 changes have revised important turnover thresholds and validity provisions. Businesses should therefore check current requirements before relying on older articles, certificates or application checklists. What Is FSSAI? FSSAI stands for the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India. It is the national authority established under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 to regulate food safety and prescribe standards for food products. The law covers activities across the food supply chain, including manufacturing, processing, storage, distribution, sale and import. Depending on the activity and eligibility conditions, a Food Business Operator, commonly called an FBO, must obtain the appropriate registration or licence. FSSAI approval should be understood as part of a continuing compliance responsibility. Receiving a certificate does not establish that every product, label or daily practice automatically complies with the law. The operator remains responsible for the food handled and sold. Why FSSAI Compliance Matters Food safety problems can arise at any stage of a business. Ingredients may arrive in poor condition, food may be stored incorrectly, cleaning procedures may be incomplete or packaging may provide inaccurate information. A business can reduce these risks by combining the correct authorisation with clear operating procedures. Employees should understand how to receive ingredients, prevent contamination, monitor storage and respond to complaints. Compliance also supports commercial relationships. Distributors, marketplaces, institutional customers and other buyers may examine a business’s food safety documents before working with it. Accurate records help the business explain its operations and demonstrate accountability. Important FSSAI Changes in 2026 Revised Turnover Thresholds For new applications submitted from 1 April 2026, the general turnover-based categories are registration for eligible businesses with annual turnover up to ₹1.5 crore, State licensing for turnover above ₹1.5 crore and up to ₹50 crore, and Central licensing for turnover above ₹50 crore. These figures are a starting point for assessment. Certain activities have special eligibility conditions, so turnover should not be considered in isolation. A business should identify its actual activity before selecting an application category. For example, a small food importer cannot assume that registration is sufficient merely because its turnover is below ₹1.5 crore. The importer category has separate Central licensing requirements. Perpetual Validity The 2026 reforms introduced perpetual validity for registrations and licences. Under the revised provisions, an authorisation remains valid unless suspended, cancelled or surrendered, removing the need for repeated renewal applications. However, perpetual validity does not mean that the business can disregard annual fees, applicable returns or food safety requirements. Management should continue monitoring its obligations and maintain the authorisation’s active status. Existing businesses should check the applicable transition arrangements and their FoSCoS records. An older certificate should not simply be ignored because the validity provisions have changed. Migration for Existing Businesses A business holding a State Licence may fall within a different turnover category after the revised thresholds. However, the category should be changed through the applicable migration process rather than by making an internal assumption. The business should review its activity, current authorisation and portal instructions before proceeding. Turnover-based migration does not remove special-category requirements that may independently apply. Relief for Qualifying Street Vendors The reforms provide deemed registration for qualifying street food vendors already registered under the Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act, 2014. This provision should not be interpreted as an automatic exemption for every roadside food seller. The vendor must fall within the relevant conditions. Hygiene and sanitary requirements continue to apply. What Is FSSAI Registration? FSSAI registration is the authorisation route available to eligible businesses within the registration category. Under the revised general turnover band, qualifying businesses with annual turnover up to ₹1.5 crore may use this route. Registration is commonly relevant when assessing smaller food operations. However, the business must still check its activity-specific eligibility. A home-based operation, retail shop and manufacturing unit should not automatically be treated as identical. For example, a home baker may prepare and sell cakes directly to customers. Another business may purchase finished cakes from a manufacturer and resell them. Their activities differ, and the application should describe the actual arrangement. Registration does not reduce the importance of safe food handling. Small businesses must still manage ingredient quality, cleanliness, storage and other applicable requirements. What Is an FSSAI State Licence? A State Licence generally applies to eligible businesses with annual turnover above ₹1.5 crore and up to ₹50 crore, subject to the relevant activity-specific conditions. The application should identify what happens at the premises. A business that processes, manufactures or repacks food should not describe itself only as a retailer if that description leaves out a significant activity. Preparation may include business constitution documents, premises records, responsible-person information and applicable technical documents. The exact checklist depends on the selected activity. A State Licence should also be reviewed when operations change. New products, additional activities or a change of premises may require modification or another regulatory step. What Is an FSSAI Central Licence? A Central Licence generally applies above the revised ₹50 crore turnover threshold. It also applies to certain specified activities irrespective of turnover, including importers, e-commerce operators and trader or merchant exporters. The important distinction is between the business’s size and its regulatory role. A small importer still operates as an importer. Similarly, a platform facilitating food transactions may have obligations different from those of an individual restaurant listed on it. Businesses should avoid choosing Central licensing simply because they consider it a more prestigious certificate. The application route should follow the applicable eligibility conditions and accurately describe the operation. FSSAI Registration vs State and Central Licence The main difference lies in eligibility, the category of authorisation and the supporting requirements. Registration generally applies to eligible businesses within the lower turnover band, while State and Central licences cover the relevant higher bands and specified activities. The categories should not be viewed only as three price options. Selecting registration to save fees can create problems where licensing is required. Equally, obtaining a licence under the wrong activity does not necessarily cover the business’s actual operations. A suitable assessment should answer three questions: what does the business do, where does it operate and which current eligibility provisions apply? Does a Home Kitchen Need FSSAI Approval? Operating from home does not automatically exempt a food business. A person regularly preparing meals, cakes, snacks or other food for sale should assess the appropriate registration or licensing category. The application should identify the actual operating premises. Using an unrelated address merely because the documents are easier to obtain can create inconsistencies. Home businesses should also consider practical controls. Ingredients need suitable storage, food preparation areas need cleaning arrangements and household activities should not introduce contamination into commercial food preparation. FSSAI Requirements for Cloud Kitchens and Restaurants A cloud kitchen prepares food for delivery, while a restaurant may serve customers on-site, offer takeaway or provide both services. Their application details should reflect the food-service activities actually undertaken. The absence of customer seating does not remove a cloud kitchen’s food safety responsibilities. Ingredient handling, preparation, storage, packaging and dispatch all need attention. Where one operator manages multiple brands or locations, the authorisation structure should be reviewed carefully. A common brand name should not be assumed to cover every operating premises automatically. FSSAI Requirements for Online Food Businesses Online food businesses should distinguish between the seller, manufacturer, kitchen, warehouse and platform operator. These roles can involve different activities and eligibility conditions. A business selling packaged food through a marketplace should assess the underlying food activity rather than assume that the marketplace’s approval covers its operations. Similarly, owning a website does not by itself explain the complete regulatory position. The application should reflect whether the business manufactures products, gets them manufactured by another party, stores them or simply resells them. Documents Required for FSSAI Registration Applicant Photograph and Identity Proof The official registration checklist includes a photograph and government-issued photo identification. Applicants should provide clear copies and enter their particulars consistently. Differences in spelling or unexplained identity details can lead to questions. Reviewing the documents before submission is easier than correcting several connected records later. Business Address Proof Proof of the business activity address is required where it differs from the address on the photo identification. The address should correspond to the place where the business actually operates. Where premises are rented or shared, the supporting arrangement should be clear. Applicants should check the current checklist rather than assume that every address document will be accepted for every situation. Business Activity Information Before applying, prepare an accurate description of the food business, products and relevant turnover information. This information helps determine the appropriate category. A description such as “food business” may be too broad to explain the operation. Preparing meals, manufacturing spice blends and reselling sealed products are different activities. Documents Required for an FSSAI Licence Business Constitution Documents Depending on the legal structure, relevant records may include incorporation documents, a partnership deed or other documents establishing the business’s constitution. These should match the applicant details entered in the application. Where a trade name differs from the legal name, the relationship should be explained accurately. Authorisation and Responsible-Person Details The application may require details of the person authorised to act for the business and the person responsible for relevant operations. Management should ensure that the nominated people understand their responsibilities. A name entered only to complete a form may create difficulties when authorities seek information or arrange an inspection. Premises Documents Proof of possession of premises should support the operating address. The documentation may differ depending on ownership, tenancy or another lawful arrangement. Applicants should compare the address across the application, premises documents and technical records. Minor differences should be resolved or explained before submission. Layout and Machinery Information Manufacturing-related applications may require a layout plan and machinery information. These records should describe the actual facility and the operations proposed there. A useful layout makes the movement of ingredients, personnel, waste and finished products understandable. Generic drawings that do not reflect the premises can weaken the document package. Water Analysis and Food Safety Records Depending on the activity, water analysis reports and a food safety management plan may be required. Additional documents can include procurement details, manufacturer authorisation and a recall plan. These requirements are activity-specific. A retailer should not be given the same technical checklist as a processing plant without checking whether each document applies. How to Apply Through FoSCoS Step 1: Identify the Business Activities Describe what the business makes, prepares, stores, distributes or sells. Identify the premises and any outsourced manufacturing arrangement. This initial exercise helps reveal activities that might otherwise be left out. A company may consider itself a brand owner while also undertaking storage, distribution or relabelling functions. Step 2: Check Eligibility Review the current category requirements using turnover and activity information together. Consider whether a special category applies before selecting registration or licensing. Where uncertainty remains, resolve the classification before paying fees or commissioning technical reports. An incorrect starting point can affect the entire application. Step 3: Prepare Supporting Documents Collect the applicable records and compare names, addresses and responsible-person details. Use complete, readable documents. Where information has changed, keep supporting records explaining the change. Avoid submitting different versions without making clear which one is current. Step 4: Submit and Monitor the Application Complete the application through FoSCoS, upload the relevant documents and make the applicable payment. Retain the acknowledgement and monitor communications. If a clarification is requested, respond within the stated period with accurate information. A response should address the query directly rather than simply repeat the original submission. Step 5: Review the Approval After approval, check the business name, premises and approved activities. Correct errors through the applicable process. Management should also retain access to the portal account and application records. Important regulatory information should remain accessible to the business. Food Safety Compliance After Approval Hygiene and Cleaning Food businesses should follow the applicable hygiene and sanitary requirements. Cleaning responsibilities should be assigned, and staff should know which areas and equipment require attention. A written schedule is useful when someone checks that the work has been completed. Repeated problems should lead to corrective action rather than repeated entries in a register. Ingredient and Supplier Controls Businesses should know where their ingredients and finished products come from. Purchase records, supplier information and batch details help investigate complaints and identify affected stock. When replacing an ingredient or supplier, assess whether the new material remains suitable for the product. A lower purchase price should not be the only consideration. Storage and Product Handling Storage arrangements should help staff recognise damaged packaging, expired goods and products needing particular temperature conditions. The June 2026 amendment provides specific relief concerning production records for non-manufacturing businesses and the specified FIFO/FEFO clause for retailers. These changes do not remove the responsibility to prevent unsafe or expired food from being sold. Staff Awareness Employees should understand the tasks relevant to their role. A person receiving deliveries needs different instructions from someone preparing food or handling customer complaints. Practical training can use real examples, such as a damaged ingredient bag, a refrigeration failure or an incorrect label. Employees should know when to stop work and seek guidance. Complaint Handling and Traceability Maintain a clear process for recording complaints and investigating potential food safety concerns. Record the product, purchase details, batch information where available and action taken. Traceability records can help the business identify whether a problem affects one item or a wider batch. Management should be able to locate this information promptly. Applicable Annual Returns and Payments Businesses should check whether annual returns apply to their category. For businesses required to file the relevant annual return, the preceding financial year’s return is generally due by 31 May through FoSCoS, subject to applicable directions. Annual fee obligations should also be monitored. Perpetual validity should not be interpreted as permission to disregard continuing payments or filing requirements. Common Mistakes to Avoid A frequent mistake is using older turnover limits without checking the revised position. Another is selecting a category solely by turnover while overlooking an activity that requires different treatment. Businesses also create problems by using an unrelated premises address, leaving out activities or allowing application queries to remain unanswered. After approval, the main risk is assuming that the certificate completes all compliance work. Operations, documents and authorisation details should remain aligned as the business grows. Conclusion Choosing between FSSAI registration, a State Licence and a Central Licence requires understanding how the food business operates. Turnover is an important factor, but the nature of activities, operating premises and special eligibility conditions must also be reviewed. Manufacturers, restaurants, home kitchens, importers and online food businesses may have different requirements. Selecting the appropriate category and preparing consistent documents helps businesses avoid application errors and establish a sound basis for their food safety compliance responsibilities. The 2026 reforms make it essential to review current turnover thresholds, validity provisions and continuing obligations. Businesses should maintain accurate records, monitor applicable payments and filings, and reassess their authorisation whenever operations change. Safe handling, proper storage and staff awareness remain equally important after approval. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ’s) Q1. What is the difference between FSSAI registration and a licence? Ans. FSSAI registration and licences are different authorisation categories for food businesses. The appropriate category depends on annual turnover, business activity and special eligibility conditions. Business owners should assess their actual operations before selecting registration, a State Licence or Central Licence. Q2. What is the general FSSAI registration turnover limit in 2026? Ans. For eligible new applications submitted from 1 April 2026, the general FSSAI registration threshold is annual turnover up to ₹1.5 crore. However, businesses must also examine their activity because special categories may require a licence regardless of their turnover level. Q3. When does a food business need a State Licence? Ans. A State Licence generally applies to eligible food businesses with annual turnover above ₹1.5 crore and up to ₹50 crore under the revised thresholds. The final category must also reflect activity requirements, operating arrangements and any applicable special eligibility conditions. Q4. Can a Central Licence be required below ₹50 crore turnover? Ans. Yes. Central licensing can apply below ₹50 crore when the business falls within a specified category, such as food importing. Turnover alone does not determine eligibility. Applicants should check the requirements for their actual activity before choosing an authorisation category. Q5. Does a home kitchen need FSSAI registration or licensing? Ans. Yes. A home kitchen selling food commercially should assess its FSSAI registration or licensing requirements. Operating from residential premises does not automatically establish an exemption. The appropriate category depends on the activity, applicable turnover conditions and any relevant special provisions. Q6. Does an online marketplace’s approval cover its food sellers? Ans. No. A marketplace’s authorisation does not automatically cover every food seller using its platform. Sellers must assess their own manufacturing, storage, distribution or food service activities and obtain appropriate authorisation. Their application should accurately identify the business and operating premises. Q7. Does perpetual validity remove annual FSSAI fee payments? Ans. No. Perpetual validity removes repeated renewal applications under the revised provisions, but annual fee obligations remain relevant. Businesses must also meet applicable food safety and filing requirements. Existing operators should review their portal status and follow the relevant transition instructions. Q8. Are FSSAI documents the same for every food business? Ans. No. Document requirements vary according to the business activity, legal structure and application category. A manufacturer may need technical records that a retailer does not. Applicants should follow the current checklist for their selected activity and submit consistent supporting information. Q9. Does every food business need to file an annual return? Ans. No. Annual return requirements do not apply identically to every food business. Operators should check whether their category must file the relevant return. Where applicable, submission is generally due by 31 May, subject to current directions and any announced extensions. Q10. Should FSSAI authorisation be reviewed when the business expands? Ans. Yes. Businesses should review their FSSAI authorisation when adding premises, introducing activities or experiencing changes affecting eligibility. Management should assess whether modification, migration or another regulatory action is necessary. Keeping authorisation details aligned with actual operations helps prevent avoidable discrepancies.
