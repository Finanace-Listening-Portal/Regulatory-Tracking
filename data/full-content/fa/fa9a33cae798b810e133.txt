Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Raj Enterprises Vs DCIT (ITAT Surat) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Surat Bench, comprising Shri Pawan Singh, Judicial Member, and Dr. Arjun Lal Saini, Accountant Member, decided three connected appeals involving Raj Enterprises and the Deputy Commissioner of Income Tax for Assessment Years 2009-10 and 2010-11. The appeals arose from the common order dated 15 February 2016 passed by CIT(A)-4, Surat. Raj Enterprises, a partnership firm engaged in property development, challenged the rejection of its books of account and substantial additions made towards alleged unaccounted receipts from its Water World Project. The assessee’s appeals were registered as ITA Nos. 1166 and 1167/Ahd/2016, while the Revenue’s cross-appeal was ITA No. 1164/Ahd/2016. Since the factual background and legal issues were substantially common, the Tribunal consolidated the appeals and treated AY 2010-11 as the lead case. The dispute originated from a survey conducted under Section 133A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, on 6 January 2010 at the assessee’s business premises. During the survey, five pocket diaries and other documents were found and impounded, and statements of the firm’s partners, Rajeshbhai Pengawala and Gobarbhai Gondalia, and accountant, Ritesh Gheewala, were recorded. According to the Revenue, the impounded material indicated receipt of unaccounted cash consideration from the booking and sale of plots in the Water World Project. During the survey, the assessee admitted additional undisclosed income of Rs.1.50 crore for FY 2009-10, relevant to AY 2010-11. The disclosed amount was subsequently included in the return filed on 15 October 2010, declaring total income of approximately Rs.1.61 crore. However, the Assessing Officer considered that the disclosure did not fully account for the alleged unrecorded transactions and proceeded to estimate additional undisclosed income. The Assessing Officer relied on entries in the impounded diaries, statements recorded during the survey and selected transactions involving individual plots to estimate the actual selling price at Rs.1,500 per square yard, compared with an average registered sale consideration of approximately Rs.417.97 per square yard. Applying the estimated difference of Rs.1,082.03 per square yard to 411 plots, each having an average area of 212.91 square yards, the AO calculated alleged unaccounted receipts of Rs.9,46,84,128. After deducting Rs.1.50 crore already disclosed by the assessee, the AO made an additional assessment of Rs.7,96,84,128 for AY 2010-11. The AO also rejected the books of account under Section 145(3), alleging incomplete accounting records, discrepancies in the area of land developed, insufficient details regarding plot transactions and failure to satisfactorily explain the impounded documents. The assessment was completed under Section 143(3), following directions issued under Section 144A. The assessee disputed the rejection of its books and the adoption of a uniform selling price of Rs.1,500 per square yard. It contended that the statements recorded during the survey had been incorrectly interpreted and that certain persons whose statements were relied upon had subsequently retracted their statements. The assessee explained that the Water World Project occupied only part of the land acquired by the firm and that other land parcels were not under development during the relevant period. It further submitted that the AO had failed to distinguish completed sales from mere bookings and had ignored cases where bookings were subsequently cancelled and advances refunded. According to the assessee, income from real estate transactions could not automatically be recognised merely because booking advances were received, particularly when the relevant sale deeds had not been executed. The assessee also challenged the attribution of certain diary entries to its project and alleged that adequate opportunity had not been provided to examine the persons whose statements were used against it. Before the CIT(A), the assessee argued that the AO’s computation resulted in commercially unrealistic profits. It submitted that the estimated additions, when combined with income already declared, implied a profit margin exceeding 90% of the computed sales, which was unreasonable for a property development business. The assessee therefore sought deletion of the entire addition or, alternatively, taxation of only the reasonable profit component of any established unaccounted receipts. The CIT(A), however, upheld the rejection of the books of account and the estimated selling rate of Rs.1,500 per square yard, relying on the statements, impounded diaries and promotional material discovered during the survey. Nevertheless, the appellate authority recognised that the entire estimated difference between recorded and alleged actual consideration could not necessarily represent taxable profit. Accordingly, the CIT(A) directed computation of undisclosed income at 50% of the estimated difference, thereby granting partial relief to the assessee. Both the assessee and the Revenue challenged these findings before the Tribunal. Before the ITAT, the assessee challenged the evidentiary basis of the additions, particularly the reliance on survey statements without adequate corroboration or effective opportunity for cross-examination. It argued that the books of account were audited and that an isolated discrepancy or disclosure of additional income during survey proceedings could not automatically justify rejection of the entire accounting records. The assessee relied on several judicial precedents concerning the evidentiary value of statements recorded during surveys, the legal consequences of retracted statements and the requirement that additions towards undisclosed income must be supported by reliable evidence. It also questioned the exercise of powers under Section 131(1) during survey proceedings without satisfying the statutory conditions. The Revenue, on the other hand, defended the assessment by submitting that the impounded diaries, statements of partners and accountant, promotional material and other circumstances collectively established receipt of unaccounted consideration from plot transactions. After considering the rival submissions and examining the assessment records, the Tribunal held that the rejection of books of account and estimation of plot sale consideration were not sustainable on the available evidence. It observed that the AO had not adequately investigated the alleged higher consideration by examining purchasers or obtaining independent corroborative material from the Sub-Registrar. The Tribunal also considered the evidentiary limitations of statements recorded during survey proceedings, particularly where such statements were retracted or were not supported by independent evidence. The Tribunal referred to judicial precedents dealing with the powers exercisable under Section 131(1) during survey proceedings and the requirement of legally admissible material for making additions. It further recognised that plot prices in a development project may vary according to location, size, booking date and other commercial considerations. Consequently, adopting a uniform selling rate of Rs.1,500 per square yard for all plots, without adequate transaction-specific evidence, could not justify the disputed additions. Consequently, the ITAT Surat allowed the assessee’s grounds challenging rejection of the books, estimation of plot sale consideration and the related undisclosed-income addition for AY 2010-11. Having granted relief on the principal grounds, the Tribunal considered adjudication of the remaining additional grounds unnecessary. The Revenue’s cross-appeal, ITA No. 1164/Ahd/2016, challenging the relief granted by the CIT(A), was dismissed. Regarding AY 2009-10, the Tribunal observed that the assessee had raised substantially similar grounds and that the material facts were common to both assessment years. Applying the principle of consistency, it also allowed ITA No. 1166/Ahd/2016 with similar directions. Thus, the consolidated order pronounced on 31 March 2022 resulted in both assessee appeals being allowed and the Revenue’s appeal being dismissed. The ruling emphasises that rejection of books and additions based on alleged on-money receipts must be supported by legally admissible and corroborated evidence rather than unsupported survey statements or generalised assumptions about property sale prices.
