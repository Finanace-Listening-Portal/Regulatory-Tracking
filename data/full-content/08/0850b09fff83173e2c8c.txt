Articles 4 min read RBI DG Murmu meets FIDC, NBFC CEOs; industry hopeful of partial ban on revolving credit RBI DG S C Murmu recently held a meeting with NBFCs and the Finance Industry Development Council (FIDC), having already received 350 feedbacks from the industry on central bank's draft proposal of banning revolving credit. Exemption to Supply chain financing has garnered repeated attention during the discussion, with one of the senior industry participant saying final guidelines may come in another two months. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Updated On Sep 4, 2026 at 11:27 AM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 133737739; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India may reconsider its proposed blanket ban on revolving credit facilities by non bank lenders after receiving feedback that the draft could affect a much wider section of the NBFC industry than initially envisaged, sources said.RBI Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu recently held a meeting with NBFCs and the Finance Industry Development Council (FIDC) to discuss the proposed restrictions on revolving credit, with the central bank listening to concerns raised by lenders, according to people aware of the discussions.The RBI had initially envisaged the impact of the proposed ban to be limited to around three to four NBFCs. However, feedback received during the consultation has indicated that a significantly larger section of the industry could be affected, prompting the central bank to consider whether a blanket prohibition is appropriate, sources said. Advt “Initially, the impact was seen as being limited to three or four NBFCs. But the feedback has shown that a much larger section of the industry could be impacted. RBI may therefore look at a partial restriction rather than a blanket ban,” an NBFC CEO said.The contours of any changes are yet to be finalised, the person added and all the suggestions discussed will be sent to RBI by Confederation of Indian Industry (CII).The development comes after substantial industry feedback on the draft. Around 30-40 lenders have submitted their responses to FIDC, while the RBI has received around 350 responses on the proposed revolving credit restrictions, according to sources.“RBI is still receiving a lot of views. The purpose of putting out a draft is to take the public view, absorb the feedback and then convert it into regulation,” another NBFC CEO said.The RBI's proposal had raised concerns across the NBFC industry over the scope of its definition of revolving credit and its potential impact on products and lending structures that are not necessarily intended to perpetually roll over credit.The central bank's concern behind the proposal is linked to the credibility and creditworthiness of borrowers, sources said. The proposed framework seeks to ensure greater discipline around the repayment and continuation of credit facilities rather than allowing credit limits to remain available on a revolving basis. Advt However, NBFCs have argued that there are several products where limited redraw or replenishment within an existing sanctioned facility does not necessarily amount to the kind of revolving lending that RBI is seeking to restrict.“The issue is that there is something in between a conventional term loan and what RBI may be defining as revolving credit. There are products that technically may not fit neatly into the definition, and that is where the industry has sought greater clarity,” an industry professional said.The industry has also pointed to the potential impact on working-capital and other lending products if the definition is applied too broadly. Advt “RBI has been quite positive in the discussions and is listening to the points being raised by the industry. That gives us confidence that the feedback will be considered before the final regulation,” an NBFC CEO said.The issue is not new, with NBFCs having raised concerns around the treatment of revolving credit with the RBI in earlier discussions as well. However, the latest draft has brought the issue into sharper focus by putting the proposed restrictions in writing.“There were discussions on the same issue even earlier, but there were no guidelines in writing. The moment something is put into a regulation, the grey areas become much more important because businesses have to comply with the exact wording,” an industry professional said. Advt The consultation process is therefore expected to play a key role in determining whether RBI retains the blanket restriction or introduces exemptions or a more targeted prohibition.Sources said the final guidelines could take another two months to be issued, giving the central bank time to assess the responses received from lenders and industry bodies.“The industry is not opposed to the objective of preventing evergreening or ensuring borrower credibility. The concern is with a blanket definition that could capture products which are not being used for that purpose,” an NBFC CEO said.Supply Chain FinancingNon bank lenders have repeatedly clarified on whether RBI’s draft proposal takes into account supply chain financing as well. One of the NBFC CEO told ETBFSI that supply chain financing as a model is well monitored through GST data of the borrowers and to add, loans disbursed directly go to the supplier and not to the borrower.
