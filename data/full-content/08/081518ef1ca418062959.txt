Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Krishna and Company Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) Section 148 Notice for AY 2015–16 Is Time-Barred After 31 March 2022 Even If the Section 148A Show-Cause Notice Was Issued Earlier Background of the Case The Hyderabad Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held that a notice under Section 148 issued on 26 April 2022 for AY 2015–16 was barred by limitation, even though the Assessing Officer had issued the show-cause notice under Section 148A(b) on 22 March 2022. The six-year period under the erstwhile Section 149(1)(b), preserved by the first proviso to the amended Section 149(1), expired on 31 March 2022. The Tribunal quashed the notice, which vitiated the consequential reassessment. The reassessment involved an addition of ₹67 lakh under Section 68 in respect of bank deposits. Krishna and Company contended that the two-partner firm had dissolved in September 2011 upon the death of one partner. It submitted that the business was subsequently taken over by Shankar Sakhla, who reported the transactions in his own returns, although the bank account continued in the firm’s name. The assessee also challenged the jurisdiction of the officer issuing the notice and raised objections concerning the opportunity of hearing. Limitation Ground Admitted for the First Time Before the ITAT The assessee raised the limitation objection as an additional ground before the Tribunal under Rule 11 of the Income Tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963. The Revenue opposed admission because the assessee had raised the objection neither during assessment nor before the CIT(A). Relying on National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. v. CIT [1998] 229 ITR 383 (SC), the Tribunal admitted the ground. The dates and material necessary to decide whether the notice was time-barred were already on record, and adjudication required no fresh factual investigation. The Tribunal therefore took up the additional ground first, since its determination could dispose of the appeal. First Proviso Preserves the Earlier Six-Year Cut-Off Under the reassessment provisions applicable to the notice, amended Section 149(1)(b) permitted reopening beyond three years and up to ten years in specified cases involving alleged escaped income of ₹50 lakh or more. For assessment years beginning on or before 1 April 2021, the first proviso imposed a further restriction. The Assessing Officer had to determine whether a notice could still have been issued on the relevant date under the limitation provisions that stood immediately before the Finance Act, 2021. For AY 2015–16, the assessment year ended on 31 March 2016, and the six-year period expired on 31 March 2022. Although the Assessing Officer issued the preliminary show-cause notice on 22 March 2022, both the order under Section 148A(d) and the notice under Section 148 were issued on 26 April 2022. The Tribunal applied the limitation test to the date of issuance of the Section 148 notice. Fifth and Sixth Provisos Cannot Extend the Earlier Cut-Off The Revenue argued that the fifth and sixth provisos to Section 149(1) extended the available time. The fifth proviso excludes specified periods, including the time allowed to the assessee to respond to the Section 148A(b) notice. The sixth proviso provides a minimum period of seven days for passing the Section 148A(d) order where the time remaining after the prescribed exclusion is seven days or less, with a corresponding extension of limitation. The Tribunal followed the jurisdictional Telangana High Court’s decision in Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Private Limited v. DCIT, which held that these provisions operate when computing the three-year or ten-year period under amended Section 149(1). The six-year cut-off preserved through the first proviso continued to apply independently. Consequently, the time allowed for the preliminary proceedings could not save the Section 148 notice issued after that cut-off. Judicial Precedents and Final Outcome The Tribunal also followed its coordinate bench decision in Sudheer Parimala v. ITO, which had quashed a Section 148 notice dated 15 April 2022 for the same assessment year. It referred to ITO v. Sai Kumar Mateti [2026] 187 taxmann.com 225 (SC), where the Supreme Court directed the jurisdictional High Courts to treat the notices in that batch as time-barred upon confirming that the cases related to AY 2015–16, in light of Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal [2024] 469 ITR 46 (SC). The Tribunal allowed the assessee’s appeal on limitation. The grounds concerning the firm’s dissolution, the authority issuing the notice, the opportunity of hearing and the merits of the ₹67 lakh addition were left unadjudicated. Author’s Comments The practical point is to verify both notice dates from the record. A Section 148A(b) show-cause notice may have been issued while time remained, as it was here. The decisive question is whether the Section 148 notice itself was issued within the period preserved by the first proviso. The amount alleged to have escaped assessment and the exclusions under the amended regime enter the limitation analysis, but neither displaces the earlier statutory cut-off where the first proviso applies.
