Summary: Tata AIA Life Insurance Sampoorna Raksha Supreme is a non-linked, non-participating, pure-risk individual life insurance product issued by Tata AIA Life Insurance Company Limited. The current product page, brochure, policy wording and customer information sheet identify it as UIN 110N160V06. It is not an investment fund and does not participate in insurer profits. Its principal purpose is protection, although one of its four benefit options returns a defined percentage of premiums at maturity and another can pay a scheduled income while life cover continues under a reducing-benefit design. The four choices—Life, Life Plus, Life Income and Credit Protect—do materially different jobs. A proposer should therefore read the benefit illustration and policy schedule, not infer cover from the product name. The brochure also permits a lump-sum death benefit, a staggered stream for up to 60 months, or a combination, subject to the chosen payout plan. An inbuilt Payor Accelerator Benefit advances part of the death benefit after an accepted terminal-illness claim; it is not a separate additional sum assured. Riders, where selected and accepted, are separate contracts with additional premium and their own exclusions. If the life assured dies while the policy is in force during the term, the stipulated death benefit is paid according to the selected payout plan, reduced by any Payor Accelerator amount already paid. If the life assured survives to maturity, no additional benefit is payable. That distinction matters: the Life option should be evaluated as risk transfer, not as a savings return. Life Plus: death cover with a conditional return at maturity Life Plus follows the same protection logic but adds a maturity amount equal to 105% of total premiums paid toward the benefit option, excluding modal loading, if the life assured survives the term and the policy has not terminated earlier. The wording does not promise 105% of every rupee collected: taxes, rider premiums, underwriting extras and modal loadings may sit outside the defined base. A buyer should compare the signed benefit illustration with the policy definition of total premiums paid. Life Income: income payments reduce the effective death cover Life Income combines death protection with monthly income beginning after the policy anniversary following the chosen start age of 55, 60 or 65. The income rate is selected at inception within the contractual range and cannot later be changed. Crucially, the effective sum assured for death-benefit computation is the base sum assured less total regular-income payouts due up to that time. The income is therefore not a free addition to an unchanged death cover; it progressively uses part of the economic benefit. Credit Protect: a reducing cover structure Credit Protect is designed around a changing effective sum assured. It is not a promise that an outstanding loan will automatically be discharged, and the nominee remains the policy claimant unless valid assignment or other arrangements apply. Loan balance, cover schedule and lender rights can diverge. Borrowers should separately confirm assignment, repayment dates and whether the effective cover matches the debt profile. The annualised-premium and total-premium definitions exclude specified amounts such as taxes, rider premiums, underwriting extras or modal loading. The Credit Protect benefit is its effective sum assured on the date of death. These tests must be read with the policy schedule because additional cover tranches, previous terminal-illness payments and income payouts can change the final amount. Payor Accelerator is an advance, not extra cover On an accepted terminal-illness diagnosis meeting the contract definition, the Payor Accelerator Benefit pays 50% of base sum assured for Life or Life Plus, or 50% of effective sum assured for Life Income or Credit Protect. The policy can continue for the remaining cover, but due premiums remain payable. Later death benefits are reduced by the amount already advanced. The brochure defines terminal illness by reference to an incurable condition and medical opinions indicating a greater than 50% chance of death within six months; the insurer may independently assess the claim. This benefit is unavailable where the product is sourced under the Point of Sales route. Lump sum versus staggered benefit A policyholder may direct that all or part of the death benefit be paid as a staggered stream, with annual, half-yearly, quarterly or monthly frequency for a selected period up to 60 months. The brochure uses a 4% annual discount rate when valuing the staggered portion at inception. During the payout period the nominee may request commutation of future instalments, with the contractual discount basis then applying. Families should retain the policy schedule because it records the elected proportions and frequencies. Underwriting, disclosures and policy continuity Medical and financial underwriting The plan is not guaranteed issue. Age, occupation, income, health, tobacco use, travel, avocations and existing cover can influence acceptance, premium, exclusions or cover limits. A calculator output or sales quote is provisional. The proposer should answer the insurer’s questions personally and completely, correct inaccurate pre-filled answers before signing, and keep copies of the proposal, medical reports, benefit illustration and payment record. Material misstatement or suppression can affect a claim under the Insurance Act and policy terms. Grace, lapse, revival and reduced paid-up status The policy document governs grace periods, lapse, revival, surrender and reduced paid-up rights. These outcomes vary by premium-payment pattern and option. Paying within the grace period can keep cover operational subject to deductions described in the contract; missing the grace deadline can cause lapse or change benefits. Revival is not automatic: evidence of insurability, arrears, interest or underwriting may be required. A policyholder considering discontinuance should request a written value statement rather than assuming premiums will be refunded. Suicide exclusion and other claim conditions The policy wording contains a suicide exclusion for death within the specified period from commencement of risk or revival, with the contractual payment generally linked to a percentage of total premiums paid or available surrender value, subject to the applicable version and in-force status. Rider claims have additional disease, waiting-period and exclusion language. No base-product summary can substitute for the exclusion clauses, definitions and endorsements in the issued policy. Claims, complaints and documents a family should organise A nominee should notify Tata AIA promptly through its claim channel and obtain an acknowledgement. Typical documents include the claim form, original or electronic policy record, death certificate, claimant identity and bank evidence, medical and hospital records where relevant, and police or post-mortem papers for accidental or unnatural death. The insurer may seek other material reasonably required to assess the event. The advertised four-hour express-claim facility is narrow: the insurer’s page limits it to specified non-early, non-investigation branch claims, up to a stated sum assured, with complete documents and a working-day cut-off. It is not a universal settlement guarantee. Keep nominee and contact details current, and tell the nominee where the policy schedule is stored. Preserve the proposal form and medical disclosures; disputes often turn on what was represented at inception. Use the insurer’s grievance channel first and record the complaint reference number. If unresolved, escalate under the insurer’s grievance matrix and then use the IRDAI Bima Bharosa portal or the Insurance Ombudsman where jurisdictional conditions are met. Do not surrender original records without retaining scans and proof of submission. GST and income-tax treatment in Tax Year 2026–27 GST on the individual life policy Notification No. 16/2025-Central Tax (Rate), effective 22 September 2025, exempts life-insurance services supplied by an insurer to an insured who is not a group. Government FAQs confirm the exemption for individual life policies and distinguish taxable group policies. Sampoorna Raksha Supreme is described as an individual product, so premiums for the qualifying base policy are generally within the exemption. However, the invoice should be checked for riders or separate supplies, and timing rules can matter for amounts received around the effective date. Premium deduction and policy proceeds For Tax Year 2026–27 the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies. The Income Tax Department explains that section 123 preserves the ₹1.5 lakh aggregate deduction for specified savings instruments, including qualifying life-insurance premium, structurally replacing old section 80C. This deduction is unavailable under the default concessional regime under section 202. Eligibility can also depend on the prescribed relationship, premium-to-capital-sum conditions and other statutory requirements; the amount paid is not automatically the amount deductible. Treatment of policy receipts depends on the nature of the payment, issue date, premium thresholds and statutory exclusions, while death benefits receive distinct treatment.
