Summary: Indian Overseas Bank’s IOB Eighty Plus term deposit is a named deposit arrangement for an eligible customer who is at least 80 years old when the account is opened. The bank’s product page states an additional 0.25 percentage point over its senior-citizen rate, producing a total 0.75 percentage point addition over the applicable ordinary rate. For deposits below ₹1 crore, the scheme permits terms from six months to 120 months and specified joint operation with a spouse when the super-senior customer is the primary holder. This guide uses the bank’s rate table displayed on 22 October 2026, explains the separate premature-closure and loan rules, and places the product within current TDS and DICGC principles without treating the highest displayed rate as a recommendation or guaranteed renewal outcome. The age condition is tested when the account is opened and must be supported by valid know-your-customer documentation carrying the date of birth or another acceptable proof of age. The product-specific benefit is therefore not based merely on reaching age 80 at some point during an existing deposit’s tenure. The bank states that the depositor receives an additional 0.25 percentage point over the rate offered to senior citizens. Because IOB’s retail-rate page gives senior citizens an additional 0.50 percentage point over the ordinary rate for eligible deposits, the total increment for an eligible super senior citizen under this product is 0.75 percentage point over the applicable ordinary rate. The product page limits the deposit amount to below ₹1 crore; the minimum amount follows the underlying term-deposit scheme selected. Depositors should verify the receipt, maturity instructions and whether the selected underlying scheme pays interest periodically or on a cumulative basis. The effective annual yield can differ from the nominal annual rate because of the interest-payment and compounding convention. Do not compare only the 7.35 percent headline The highest derived rate in the displayed eligible slabs is 7.35% per annum for 444 days, but that observation is not a recommendation. A depositor may need a different maturity date, periodic income, liquidity or ownership arrangement. Premature closure can reduce the return, while renewal occurs at the rate and terms then in force rather than automatically repeating the original rate. The depositor should compare the receipt’s deposit type, interest frequency, maturity value and closure conditions instead of treating the nominal rate as a complete measure of outcome. Ownership, spouse operation and nomination The product page permits the deposit in the super-senior citizen’s own name or jointly with the spouse. It lists self, either-or-survivor and first-or-survivor modes, while requiring the eligible super-senior citizen to be the primary account holder. These expressions govern operation and payment mechanics; they do not by themselves answer every question about beneficial ownership, tax attribution, succession or estate rights. Funding source, joint-holder capacity and nomination should be documented consistently. Nomination is available and is particularly important for an elderly depositor, but nomination is not a substitute for complete succession planning. The depositor should ensure that the nominee name, relationship and contact information are accurate, keep the nomination acknowledgement, and review it after family or legal changes. A mandate or joint-operation instruction should also be distinguished from nomination: one may assist operation during life, while the other addresses the bank’s payment process after death, subject to applicable law and claims. Premature closure changes the realised return IOB’s displayed foreclosure charges IOB’s retail-rate page displays foreclosure charges for domestic term deposits. It states nil charge for deposits up to ₹15,000, 0.50% for deposits from ₹15,001 to ₹5 lakh, and 1.00% for deposits above ₹5 lakh and below ₹3 crore. The product amount ceiling is below ₹1 crore, so the first three bands are relevant. The precise interest payable on premature closure also depends on the period the deposit actually remained with the bank and the bank’s applicable rules; the contracted maturity amount should not be used as the expected closure proceeds. Loan facility under the product The product page states that a loan may be available up to 90% of the deposit amount for the depositor, including a joint deposit with the spouse, and excludes third-party loans. It displays interest at 1.00 percentage point above the deposit rate for a demand loan and 1.50 percentage points above the deposit rate for a cash-credit facility. Availability remains subject to the bank’s documentation, lien, repayment, operational and credit processes. A loan avoids immediate premature closure, but it is not free liquidity. Interest accrues on the borrowing, the deposit is marked as security, and default or maturity interactions can affect net payment. The borrower should compare the full borrowing cost and expected duration with the quantified premature-closure outcome. For a joint deposit, all required consents and the operating mandate should be confirmed. The 90% figure is an upper product parameter, not an entitlement to a particular disbursement. TDS and income-tax treatment for an 80-plus depositor TDS is collection at source, not the final tax Interest on a bank term deposit is generally taxable under the applicable income-tax law according to the depositor’s facts. The bank aggregates interest for TDS purposes in accordance with the governing provisions and its records. For a resident senior citizen, the current threshold commonly applicable to bank interest is ₹1,00,000 for a financial year. Crossing the threshold does not mean only the excess is relevant for TDS, and remaining below it does not make the interest exempt from income tax. An eligible resident may submit the prescribed declaration for non-deduction only when the legal conditions are genuinely satisfied; age alone is insufficient. PAN status, estimated total income, tax regime, other interest, deductions, exemptions and return-filing obligations can affect the result. NRO and NRE deposits follow different tax and TDS rules, so the resident-senior threshold should not be assumed for a non-resident account merely because the customer is over 80. Reconcile certificates and accrued interest The depositor should reconcile the bank’s interest certificate, account statement, Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement with the income reported in the return. A cumulative deposit can accrue income before cash is received at maturity, depending on the applicable method and law. Joint naming does not automatically split taxable interest equally; beneficial ownership and funding are relevant. Where tax has been deducted in excess, the remedy is generally through the income-tax process rather than treating the deduction as the final liability. DICGC cover and concentration at one bank Indian Overseas Bank is a bank, so eligible deposits are governed by the deposit-insurance framework administered by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation. DICGC insurance is up to ₹5 lakh for each depositor in the same right and same capacity at the same bank, including principal and interest. Balances held in the same right and capacity across branches of that bank are aggregated; opening multiple receipts or using multiple branches does not create a separate ₹5 lakh cover for each receipt. Deposits held in genuinely different rights or capacities are treated under the DICGC rules, but labels should not be engineered without a real legal basis. A joint account combination may be treated separately only under the applicable combination and capacity rules. The ₹5 lakh figure is an insurance ceiling, not an investment limit, a guarantee of immediate access in every scenario, or a guarantee of every contractual return. Accrued interest counts within the ceiling, so a principal amount of exactly ₹5 lakh can leave part of interest outside the insured total. Opening and maturity checks for the depositor or family 1. Review maturity instructions before the due date because renewal will use the terms and rate then applicable. Frequently Asked Questions on IOB Eighty Plus 1. Is a 79-year-old who turns 80 during the term eligible at opening? No. The bank’s product condition requires the customer to have completed 80 years as on the date the deposit account is opened. 2. Is 7.35 percent available for every permitted tenure? No. It is the derived super-senior rate for the displayed 444-day slab. Other eligible tenures use their own ordinary rate plus the applicable 0.75 percentage point total addition. 3. Can the deposit be opened jointly with an adult child? The product page specifies joint operation with the spouse and requires the eligible super senior to be the primary holder. A different relationship should not be assumed to qualify without written confirmation from IOB. 4. Does no TDS mean the interest is tax-free? No. TDS is a collection mechanism. The depositor must determine taxable income and final liability under the law applicable to the relevant year. 5. Is each IOB fixed-deposit receipt insured up to ₹5 lakh? No. DICGC aggregates eligible deposits at the same bank held by a depositor in the same right and capacity, including principal and interest, across branches. 6. Is a loan against the deposit always better than premature closure? No. The loan has an interest cost and lien, while closure can involve a rate adjustment and charge. The depositor should compare bank-provided calculations for the expected period of need. 7. Will the same rate continue on automatic renewal? Not necessarily. Renewal normally occurs under the rate, product availability and terms in force on the renewal date. The maturity instruction and renewed receipt should be checked. Key Takeaways IOB Eighty Plus is for a primary depositor who has completed 80 years on the account-opening date. The product adds 0.25 percentage point over the senior rate, giving a total 0.75 percentage point addition over the eligible ordinary rate. The product amount must be below ₹1 crore and the term can run from six to 120 months. The displayed 444-day rate derives to 7.35% per annum for an eligible super senior, but other tenures carry different rates and no rate is an editorial recommendation. Premature closure charges are amount-based and the interest for the actual run period must also be considered. The product lists loans up to 90% for self or spouse-joint deposits, subject to bank rules and a lien.
