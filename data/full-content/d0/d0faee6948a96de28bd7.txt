Summary : Motilal Oswal Flexi Cap Fund is an open-ended equity scheme investing across large-cap, mid-cap and small-cap companies. The flexibility is at the portfolio-manager level: investors do not select the market-cap allocation themselves. This article explains the scheme’s current official features, how the benchmark and portfolio mandate should be read, the distinction between direct and regular plans, exit-load mechanics, equity-oriented-fund taxation and the records investors should review. It does not compare returns, rank the scheme or recommend an investment. The official scheme page describes it as an open-ended dynamic equity scheme investing across large-cap, mid-cap and small-cap stocks. Its investment objective is long-term capital appreciation through a diversified portfolio across sectors and market-cap segments, but the objective is not an assurance of returns. The scheme was allotted on 28 April 2014 and uses the Nifty 500 Total Return Index as its benchmark. The manager can alter exposure based on valuation, business quality, liquidity and conviction, subject to the scheme document and regulatory category rules. That freedom can help the portfolio respond to opportunity, but it also means the investor’s exposure may differ materially over time. The name “flexi cap” is therefore not a promise of automatic diversification or lower volatility. Benchmark comparison must use total return and a suitable period The Nifty 500 TRI reflects a broad Indian equity universe and includes reinvested dividends. A fair comparison should use the scheme plan and option actually held, the same start and end dates, and the total-return benchmark. Point-to-point results can be sensitive to market cycles. Rolling periods, downside experience, portfolio turnover and consistency with the mandate can provide context, but none can predict future returns. A scheme can underperform or lose capital even when it follows its stated process. Portfolio Concentration, Market-Cap and Liquidity Risks A portfolio may be diversified by number of securities yet remain concentrated in selected industries, business models or investment themes. Mid-cap and small-cap shares can have lower market depth and wider bid-ask spreads than large companies, particularly during market stress. A manager attempting to reduce a position may affect the execution price. Large-cap exposure does not remove valuation risk, and a broadly diversified benchmark does not prevent an actively managed scheme from having a materially different sector or stock profile. What investors should read in the monthly portfolio The monthly portfolio disclosure should be used to identify the largest holdings, sector weights, market-cap mix, cash position and derivatives, if any. Investors should compare successive months rather than infer a permanent strategy from a single factsheet. Material portfolio changes may reflect subscriptions, redemptions, corporate actions or the manager’s view. Overseas securities, if held under the current mandate and regulatory limits, add currency, market-hours, custody and foreign-regulatory risks. Portfolio changes and turnover disclosures Trading cost and strategy change questions Direct and Regular Plans: Same Portfolio, Different Expense Path Direct and Regular plans participate in the same underlying scheme portfolio but have different expense structures because the Regular plan includes distribution-related costs. Their net asset values and long-term outcomes therefore diverge. The lower-cost plan is not automatically appropriate for every investor: some investors choose regulated advisory or distribution support, while others execute independently. The decision should be based on service needs, competence, conflict management and the current total expense ratio, not a stale rate quoted in an article. TER and transaction costs are reflected differently The total expense ratio is charged to the scheme and reflected in NAV rather than invoiced separately to each unit holder. The AMC’s reviewed page displayed a 0.87% TER, but TER can change and differs by plan. Portfolio transaction costs, securities transaction tax and other scheme expenses are governed by regulation and disclosure rules. Investors should check the latest daily TER disclosure for the exact plan and not apply a Direct-plan rate to a Regular-plan holding. Entry, Redemption, Switch and Exit-Load Mechanics The official page states a minimum application amount of ₹500 in multiples of ₹1. Purchase and redemption processing depend on a valid application, applicable cut-off time, funds availability, KYC status and the transaction channel. A switch is generally treated as a redemption from one scheme or plan and a purchase into another. It can therefore trigger exit load and tax even though no cash reaches the investor’s bank account. Exit load is lot-specific, not account-wide The current exit-load disclosure is 1% when units are redeemed or switched out on or before 365 days from allotment and nil after 365 days. Each purchase or systematic-investment instalment has its own allotment date. Redemption is ordinarily matched to units under the applicable operational method, so a single redemption can contain lots with different holding periods. The exit load is deducted in the redemption valuation; it should not be confused with income tax or TER. Indian Tax Treatment of Equity-Oriented Mutual Fund Units The scheme is ordinarily analysed as an equity-oriented fund while it satisfies the statutory equity-investment conditions. Under the current regime reviewed for this article, gains on units held for not more than 12 months are generally short-term and taxed at 20%, subject to surcharge and health and education cess. Gains on units held for more than 12 months are generally long-term and taxed at 12.5% to the extent the aggregate eligible long-term capital gains exceed the annual ₹1.25 lakh threshold, again subject to applicable surcharge and cess. Current law and the investor’s entire portfolio of eligible gains must be checked at the time of filing. Losses, dividends and switches need separate records A capital loss is subject to statutory set-off and carry-forward rules and timely return filing. Dividend distributions, if any, are generally taxable in the investor’s hands at applicable rates, with withholding depending on current provisions and thresholds. A switch can create a taxable gain or loss without a bank credit. Non-resident taxation, treaty claims, withholding, minor holdings, business holdings and changes under the Income-tax Act, 2025 require tailored advice. Consolidated account statements and capital-gain reports should be reconciled with bank and transaction records. Suitability Questions Without Converting the Review into Advice A flexi-cap equity scheme exposes capital to market movements and is generally unsuitable for money needed on a fixed near-term date. The scheme’s current riskometer disclosure, portfolio, expense ratio and investment horizon should be read before each material decision. Investors should consider emergency liquidity outside the fund, their ability to tolerate a sustained drawdown, overlap with other equity funds, nomination and transmission arrangements, and whether they understand the plan and option selected. Past performance and a well-known AMC do not eliminate loss risk. Download the current SID, KIM, factsheet, addenda, TER and portfolio before investing. Confirm the exact plan, option, folio ownership, KYC and bank mandate. Measure overlap with existing large-, mid- and small-cap holdings. Track every purchase lot for exit-load and capital-gain holding periods. Use the latest riskometer and portfolio, not a historic label or return chart.
