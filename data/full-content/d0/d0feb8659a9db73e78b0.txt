Articles 3 min read Payments draw $44.2 bln as AI reshapes fintech funding; insurtech investment falls to $3.7 bln: Report Global fintech investment is increasingly concentrating around payments, AI and infrastructure, while funding for traditional insurtech models is becoming more selective. According to a recent report by KPMG, payments attracted $44.2 billion in H1 2026, while insurtech investment fell to $3.7 billion across just 124 deals. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Sep 7, 2026 at 07:07 PM IST var news_msid = 133883698; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Global fintech investment is undergoing a sharp reshaping, with payments emerging as the biggest magnet for capital while insurtech funding slows and AI increasingly determines where investors place their bets, according to KPMG's Pulse of Fintech H1 2026 report.Investment in the global payments sector surged to $44.2 billion in the first half of 2026, more than double the $20.2 billion invested during the whole of 2025.The surge was driven significantly by consolidation and large-scale transactions, underlining how the payments ecosystem is increasingly moving towards scale, infrastructure and multi-rail transaction capabilities. Advt Payments dominate fintech dealmakingThe payments boom was led by some of the largest fintech transactions globally, including Worldpay's $24.3 billion deal and the $13.5 billion Total System Services corporate divestiture.The scale of these transactions demonstrates how large, established payments infrastructure companies are becoming increasingly important to the next phase of fintech consolidation.The report said investor interest is also expanding beyond conventional payments into embedded finance, AI-driven payments and agentic commerce, where AI systems could increasingly participate in shopping and payment workflows.For banks and financial institutions, this could mean the next wave of fintech competition increasingly shifts towards infrastructure that makes payments faster, more automated and integrated into broader business workflows.Insurtech investment slows to $3.7 billionThe picture was significantly different for the global insurtech sector.Insurtech attracted $3.7 billion in investment across 124 deals in H1 2026, compared with $9.8 billion in investment during 2025.If the current pace continues, the report said deal volumes could fall to their weakest level since 2016.The slowdown comes after insurtech investment reached a four-year high in 2025, although that growth was driven largely by a limited number of large transactions rather than broad-based funding momentum. Advt The largest insurtech deal in the Asia-Pacific region during H1 2026 was a $41.6 million funding round raised by India-based insurance distribution platform Turtlemint.AI puts pressure on traditional insurtech modelsA major reason behind the changing investment environment is the rapid adoption of AI by insurance companies.According to KPMG, insurers are increasingly choosing to work directly with large global technology companies for AI capabilities rather than depending solely on smaller niche insurtech startups.This is forcing insurtech companies to demonstrate deeper industry expertise, proprietary data capabilities, regulatory understanding and specialised solutions for complex insurance problems. Advt Investors are also increasingly favouring asset-light models over companies carrying underwriting risk or requiring large amounts of capital.The shift could have important implications for India's insurance technology ecosystem, particularly as insurers increasingly digitise distribution, underwriting, claims and customer servicing.Regtech attracts $2.9 billion as compliance becomes a technology opportunityAnother area attracting growing attention is regulatory technology.Regtech companies attracted $2.9 billion globally in H1 2026, even as the number of deals fell to 133, the lowest level since 2017. Advt The trend reflects increasing investor interest in mature companies working on areas such as AML, KYC, financial crime prevention and risk intelligence.AI is also becoming central to the regtech opportunity, with financial institutions increasingly looking for technology that can automate compliance processes and improve risk management.However, KPMG noted that smaller pure-play regtech companies are facing growing competition from larger technology platforms capable of bundling AI-enabled compliance solutions with broader enterprise offerings.Fintech capital moves towards scale and infrastructureThe broader message from the report is that fintech investment is becoming increasingly selective.Capital is moving away from generic growth stories towards businesses that can demonstrate scale, strong data advantages and clear commercial applications.Payments companies are benefiting from consolidation and infrastructure demand, while AI is reshaping investment across banking, insurance, compliance and financial operations.For the BFSI ecosystem, the shift means technology investment is increasingly moving towards solutions that can reduce costs, improve risk management, automate workflows and strengthen customer engagement.
