} } Listen to This ArticleThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on Friday allowed white-label ATM operators to purchase cash directly from its issue offices in wholesale lots, besides sourcing it from lenders other than their sponsor banks, in a move that could make it easier for these operators to replenish cash. The RBI said in a circular that operators can purchase cash in quantities exceeding 100,000 pieces, and in multiples thereof, of any denomination, against full payment. They can also source cash from scheduled commercial banks, cooperative banks and regional rural banks. A white-label is an automated teller machine set up, owned, and operated by a non-bank entity rather than a bank. As on end October, there were 2.17 lakh bank managed ATMs and Cash Recycling Machines (CRM) while WTA were 36,000 according to latest RBI data. The directions, effective immediately, consolidate the existing regulatory framework for white-label ATMs. Operators are required to maintain a minimum net worth of ₹100 crore and ensure ATM deployment is weighted towards semi-urban and rural areas. The minimum deployment ratio across metro, urban, semi-urban and rural centres is 1:2:3. RBI also allowed white-label ATM operators (WLAOs) to have multiple sponsor banks and offer interoperable cardless cash withdrawals (ICCW) using Unified Payments Interface (UPI) for customer authorisation.
