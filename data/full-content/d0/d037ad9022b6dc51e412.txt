A tax-saving fixed deposit is a type of bank deposit that qualifies for a tax deduction when specific conditions are met. Like a regular fixed deposit, it earns interest at a rate set by the bank when the deposit is opened. The key difference is its five-year lock-in period and eligibility for a deduction under the applicable tax rules. These deposits can suit people who want to set aside money for several years while earning interest at a known rate. Here are the main features: Fixed returns: The interest rate is set when the deposit is opened and applies according to the bank’s terms. Five-year lock-in: The deposited amount remains invested for at least five years. Tax deduction: Eligible investments may qualify for a deduction of up to INR 1.5 Lakh, subject to the applicable tax regime and conditions. Choice of interest payout: Depending on the bank, investors may be able to choose between cumulative and periodic interest payouts. A tax-saving FD is different from a regular online Fixed Deposit with a short tenure. The tax benefit applies only to deposits that meet the prescribed conditions. How Does a Tax-Saving FD Reduce Taxable Income? Under the Income Tax Act, 2025, which came into force on 1 April 2026, Section 123 covers deductions for specified payments and investments listed in Schedule XV. Eligible tax-saving term deposits can qualify under these provisions, subject to the stated conditions. The combined deduction for eligible investments under this provision is capped at INR 1.5 Lakh for the tax year. For example, if an eligible taxpayer invests INR 1 Lakh in a qualifying tax-saving FD and has sufficient unused deduction limit, the investment may reduce the income considered for tax by INR 1 Lakh. The actual tax saved depends on the person’s taxable income, applicable tax rates and eligibility. The tax benefit is subject to the tax regime selected. The new tax regime under Section 202 does not generally allow this deduction for tax-saving FDs. Eligible taxpayers who use a regime that permits the deduction should check the applicable conditions before investing. It is also important to remember that the deduction applies to the qualifying investment, not to the interest earned on the FD. Interest income is generally taxable according to the investor’s applicable tax rules. How Can You Open a Tax-Saving FD Online? Product availability, rates and terms may vary by issuer, so verify the details shown at the time of booking. How is Interest on a Tax-Saving FD Taxed? The tax deduction on the original investment does not make the interest tax-free. Interest earned on a tax-saving FD is generally added to the investor’s taxable income and taxed according to the applicable rules. Keep these points in mind: Interest income: Include the interest in your tax calculations for the relevant year, following the applicable accounting and tax rules. Tax deducted at source (TDS): A bank may deduct TDS when the relevant legal conditions and thresholds are met. TDS is a tax credit, not necessarily the final tax payable. Records: Retain FD statements, interest certificates and tax records to help prepare your income tax return. Tax planning: Consider both the deduction on the investment and the tax payable on the interest when estimating the overall benefit. The final outcome depends on your total income, tax regime and other eligible deductions. If your tax position is complex, consult a qualified tax professional. Conclusion Tax-saving fixed deposits can help eligible taxpayers combine predictable interest with a deduction on qualifying investments. Before opening one, check the five-year lock-in, interest rate, tax regime and treatment of interest income. Compare offers carefully and invest only money you can leave untouched until maturity. A well-chosen FD can support your savings plan while helping you make informed tax decisions under the Income Tax Act, 2025.
