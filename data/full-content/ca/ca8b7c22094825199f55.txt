Global deposits grew 11-12 per cent year on year, while domestic deposits rose 10 per cent ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Sep 12, 2026 at 12:35 PM IST var news_msid = 134114654; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The bank is prioritising profitable growth, with advances up 18 per cent and a Rs 50,000 crore credit pipeline, while remaining comfortable with around 15 per cent credit growth.Canara Bank aims to protect NIM in the 2.5-2.6 per cent range through better loan pricing, lower funding costs and a more productive balance-sheet mix.The bank expects around Rs 12,000 crore of incremental ECL provisions and plans to absorb the impact largely over two years, backed by strong capitalisation.Gold loans remain a key growth area, with the Rs 2.59 lakh crore portfolio expected to grow 30-35 per cent as Canara Bank expands beyond its southern markets.Canara Bank has garnered $5.8 billion in FCNR(B) deposits and plans to retire high-cost deposits over the coming months, as the lender seeks to strengthen its liability franchise and improve net interest income growth.The bank is also focusing on individual savings and retail term deposits rather than relying on CASA alone to reduce funding costs. Global deposits grew 11-12 per cent year on year, while domestic deposits rose 10 per cent, according to a report by Motilal Oswal Financial Services.Domestic CASA stood at 29.7 per cent and the bank aspires to improve the mix slightly in FY27. Individual savings deposits grew around 12.5 per cent, while current account deposits increased by more than 11 per cent year on year, with both growing ahead of broader industry trends. Advt Management believes banks with a higher reliance on bulk deposits stand to benefit more from the FCNR(B) funding opportunity. Canara Bank will look to retire high-cost deposits over the coming months, which the report said will help improve NII growth.The bank is broadly comfortable with an optimal credit-deposit ratio of around 80-82 per cent, compared with 79 per cent currently. Its deposit base is expected to grow at around 11 per cent annually over FY26-28, driven by retail deposits through CASA and retail term deposits.Pursuing profitable growthOn the asset side, Canara Bank is prioritising profitable growth over absolute loan growth. Advances grew 18 per cent year on year, while RAM credit rose 21 per cent, taking RAM's share of domestic advances to around 59 per cent from 58 per cent a year earlier.The bank has a credit pipeline of around Rs 50,000 crore, comprising Rs 18,000 crore of sanctioned but undisbursed proposals and Rs 32,000 crore of proposals under consideration. Management remains comfortable with credit growth of around 15 per cent year on year while protecting margins.Net interest margin stood at 2.52 per cent in the first quarter of FY27. The bank is targeting a NIM of 2.5-2.6 per cent, with management focused on improving the underlying economics of both assets and liabilities. The yield on advances is around 8 per cent, compared with around 6.9 per cent on investments, leaving room to improve the loan mix as the CD ratio moves towards the bank's preferred range. Advt Canara Bank is also focused on avoiding lending below 7 per cent and securing better pricing on incremental business. A higher share of MCLR-linked loans is expected to provide support as the rate cycle progresses, while the RBI's new MCLR pricing policy is seen as favourable in a potential upcycle.Asset quality continued to improve, with gross NPA declining to 1.57 per cent from 1.84 per cent in the fourth quarter of FY26 and net NPA falling to 0.36 per cent. Provision coverage ratio strengthened to 94.8 per cent, while annualised credit costs remained controlled at 0.49 per cent.The bank estimates incremental provisions of around Rs 12,000 crore from the expected credit loss transition, with additional uncertainty around investment-book mark-to-market requirements. Management intends to absorb the impact largely over two years. With CRAR at 17.17 per cent and CET1 at 12.91 per cent, it believes the bank has sufficient capital headroom to manage the transition without raising fresh capital. Advt Canara Bank is also focusing on productivity, with digitisation and branch decongestion forming an important part of its strategy. The bank has around 10,000 branches and plans to add another 250, but wants branches to spend more time on business development by shifting routine processes towards digital channels. Operating expenses are growing at around 10-11 per cent year on year.Gold loans remain a major strength, with the portfolio at around Rs 2.59 lakh crore, including roughly Rs 1.51 lakh crore of agriculture gold loans and Rs 1.07 lakh crore of retail gold loans. Retail gold loans are growing faster and account for roughly 40 per cent of the portfolio. Management expects the gold loan book to grow 30-35 per cent, with scope to expand beyond southern markets, including the northern region, while keeping loan-to-value ratios at 60-65 per cent. Advt Management continues to target FY27 return on assets comfortably above 1 per cent, although the ECL transition and a rise in steady-state credit costs could dampen future profitability ratios. The bank expects to make efforts to pass on at least part of the impact to borrowers.
