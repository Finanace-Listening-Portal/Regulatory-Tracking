Articles 3 min read RBI DG Poonam Gupta says ‘fair case’ for rupee to appreciate from current levels RBI Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta said the rupee’s 13.1 per cent depreciation since March 2025 could be a temporary phenomenon, pointing to an expected improvement in India’s current and capital accounts and saying there is a “fair case” for the currency to not just stabilise but potentially appreciate from current levels. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 23, 2026 at 07:52 PM IST var news_msid = 134440635; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The rupee’s cumulative depreciation over the past year and a half could prove temporary as India’s balance of payments position improves, Reserve Bank of India Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta said on Wednesday, and that there is a “fair case” for the currency to appreciate from current levels.Gupta, speaking at the 13th SBI Banking & Economics Conclave, said India’s current account deficit should shrink further in the coming years, while the capital account could turn more favourable, plausibly from later in the current financial year.“The recent oil price and gold price shocks have pushed CAD temporarily higher,” Gupta said, noting that the capital account surplus had fallen short of the current account deficit in the last two years. Advt India recorded a negative balance of payments of about USD 5 billion in 2024-25 and USD 23.6 billion in 2025-26, she said.However, Gupta said India’s CAD has traditionally remained small and has declined as a share of GDP over time. Net services exports and remittances remain key structural strengths and are large and resilient enough to absorb the merchandise trade deficit, keeping the current account deficit below 1 per cent of GDP, she said.The rupee has cumulatively depreciated by 13.1 per cent on a point-to-point basis between March 31, 2025 and September 16, 2026, Gupta said.However, she said several factors point towards an improvement in India’s external position.Gupta expects the current account deficit to shrink further, with India’s traditional strengths persisting and new ones emerging, including growing success in merchandise exports.India’s dependence on imported oil is also expected to decline through alternative sources of energy and efforts to find domestic oil reserves, which would further reduce oil demand as a share of GDP, she said. She also pointed to the potential stabilisation of oil prices once the ongoing conflict resolves.At the same time, the rest of India’s trade basket is responding well to new trade opportunities, with the positive impact of recent free trade agreements expected to materialise, Gupta said. Advt On the capital account, she said several factors point towards a more favourable outlook. These include stretched valuations elsewhere, the AI-led investment boom potentially saturating, strong domestic macroeconomic fundamentals, high real and nominal GDP growth, a gathering domestic investment cycle and healthy bank and corporate balance sheets.She also pointed to continuing measures to attract different forms of capital into India, a trend increase in foreign direct investment and the eventual inclusion of Indian bonds in more global indices.“The AI story is, in effect, an ‘unspent force’ for us,” Gupta said, adding that India could leverage the potential of AI just as it has leveraged digital innovation, while avoiding the associated risks. Advt Gupta also highlighted the impact of the RBI’s special capital-flow measures implemented in June this year, saying they had resulted in a “meaningful BOP surplus” for the year.“This reflects India’s unique ability to attract large inflows at a very small country premium,” she said.“With the RBI remaining committed to ensuring orderly conditions in the foreign exchange market, and having the wherewithal to meet decades worth of CAD, or the net BOP deficit, the current market dynamics do not appear especially well-founded,” she said.“If anything, there seems to be a fair case for the rupee to not just stabilise but perhaps even appreciate from the current levels,” Gupta said. Advt She added that this was also what market analysts had been anticipating when the capital-flow measures were first announced.
