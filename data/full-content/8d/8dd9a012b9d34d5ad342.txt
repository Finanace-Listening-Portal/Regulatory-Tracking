(Gujarat High Court) Summary: The Gujarat High Court considered a writ petition filed by Champion Exim Company challenging the reassessment proceedings initiated for Assessment Year 2018-19 under the Income-tax Act, 1961. The petitioner had filed its return of income declaring total income of ₹84,380, which was accepted through an intimation under Section 143(1) dated 6 February 2019. Subsequently, the Income Tax Officer, Ward-3(1)(1), Rajkot, issued a show-cause notice dated 19 March 2022 under Section 148A(b), proposing issuance of a reassessment notice under Section 148. However, the petitioner contended that the show-cause notice had been sent to an incorrect email address and was consequently never received. The petitioner became aware of the proceedings only when the order dated 28 March 2022 passed under Section 148A(d) was received by post. Since the petitioner had been deprived of an opportunity to submit its objections before the Assessing Officer, it challenged the order dated 28 March 2022 and the consequential notice dated 29 March 2022 under Section 148. The petitioner argued that initiating reassessment proceedings without properly serving the statutory show-cause notice constituted a violation of the principles of natural justice and rendered the consequential proceedings legally unsustainable. During the hearing, the petitioner submitted that its correct email address and mobile number had already been communicated to the Income Tax Department and that the Department’s failure to maintain updated electronic records could not justify proceeding against the assessee without providing an effective opportunity of hearing. The Revenue, through an additional affidavit-in-reply dated 7 April 2025, acknowledged that the petitioner had updated its email address and that the Central Processing Centre had successfully used the updated address for communicating the intimation under Section 143(1). However, the updated email address had not been reflected in the Income Tax Business Application (ITBA) system because of non-updation of the departmental system. Learned Senior Advocate appearing for the petitioner confirmed that the updated email address was correct and operational. The Division Bench comprising Justice Bhargav D. Karia and Justice Pranav Trivedi considered these submissions and the Department’s acknowledgment of the discrepancy in its electronic records. The central issue was whether the petitioner had been afforded a meaningful opportunity to respond to the statutory notice under Section 148A(b) before the Assessing Officer proceeded to pass an adverse order under Section 148A(d). The Court considered the circumstances surrounding the incorrect email communication and the resulting denial of an opportunity of hearing. The Gujarat High Court accordingly quashed and set aside the order dated 28 March 2022 passed under Section 148A(d) and the consequential reassessment notice dated 29 March 2022 issued under Section 148. The Court remanded the matter to the Income Tax Officer, Ward-3(1)(1), Rajkot, with directions to permit the petitioner to submit its reply to the original show-cause notice issued under Section 148A(b). The Assessing Officer was further directed to provide the petitioner an adequate opportunity of hearing and thereafter pass a fresh de novo order under Section 148A(d) in accordance with law. The entire exercise was required to be completed within twelve weeks from the date of receipt of a copy of the High Court’s order. With these directions, the writ petition was disposed of and notice was discharged. The Court did not adjudicate the substantive merits of the proposed reassessment or determine whether the income allegedly escaping assessment justified reopening. The decision highlights the importance of proper communication of statutory notices through the assessee’s updated contact details and reinforces that failure of departmental electronic systems to reflect a registered email address cannot deprive an assessee of the opportunity to respond before adverse reassessment proceedings are undertaken. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF GUJARAT HIGH COURT 1. Heard learned Senior Advocate Mr. Prakash Shah with learned advocate Mr. Dhaval Shah for the petitioner and learned Senior Standing Counsel Mr. Karan Sanghani for the respondents. 2. By this petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, the petitioner has prayed for the following reliefs: “(A) Your Lordships be pleased to issue a Writ of Certiorari or a writ in the nature of Certiorari or any other writ, order or direction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India calling for the records pertaining to the petitioner’s case and after going into the validity and legality thereof be pleased to quash and set aside the impugned order dated 28.03.2022 (Annexure F) passed by Respondent No.3 and impugned notice dated 29.03.2022 (Annexure G) issued by Respondent No.3; (b) Your Lordships be pleased to issue a writ of Mandamus or any other appropriate writ in the nature of Mandamus ordering and directing the respondents to forthwith refrain from taking any steps or proceedings pursuant to and in furtherance of or in implementation of the impugned order dated 28.03.2022 (Annexure F) passed by Respondent No.3 and impugned notice dated 29.03.2022 (Annexure G) issued by Respondent No.3. (c) Your Lordships be pleased to issue a Writ of Prohibition or any other writ order or direction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India prohibiting the Respondent No.3 from taking any further steps or proceedings pursuant to or in furtherance of or in implementation of the impugned order dated 28.03.2022 (Annexure F) passed by Respondent No.3 and the impugned notice dated 29.03.2022 (Annexure G) issued by Respondent No.3; (d) That pending the hearing and final disposal of the above petition, this Hon’ble Court be pleased to restrain the Respondents by themselves, their successors in office, subordinates and servants and agents by an interim order and injunction of this Hon’ble Court from taking any further steps or proceedings pursuant to or in furtherance of or in implementation of the impugned order dated 28.03.2022 (Annexure F) passed by Respondent No.3 and the impugned notice dated 29.03.2022 (Annexure G) issued by Respondent no.3; (e) Ad-interim relief in terms of prayer (d) above may kindly be granted; (f) Such other and further order or orders as may be deemed just and proper in the facts and circumstances of the present case may kindly be granted.” 3. The petitioner-Company filed its return of income declaring total income of Rs. 84,380/- for the Assessment Year 2018-19. It is the case of the petitioner that email ID and Mobile Number were duly intimated to the Income Tax Department and intimation under section 143(1) of the Income Tax Act,1961 [for short ‘the Act’] was given on 06.02.2019 accepting the return of income. 3.1 Thereafter, a notice under section 148A(b) of the Act was issued on 19.03.2022 by the respondent No.3-The Income Tax Officer, Ward-3 (1)(1), Rajkot, calling upon the petitioner to show the cause as to why the notice under section 148 of the Act should not be issued. However, it is the case of the petitioner that as the said notice was communicated to the petitioner on wrong email ID i.e. [email protected]., the same was not received by the petitioner. 3.2 It is the case of the petitioner that when the impugned order dated 28.03.2022 passed under section 148A(d) was communicated to the petitioner by post, only then the petitioner came to know about the issuance of notice under section 148A(b) of the Act. The petitioner therefore, approached its Chartered Accountant. 3.3 It is the case of the petitioner that as the show-cause notice was not received, the petitioner could not file its reply to the same and therefore, the impugned order dated 28.03.2022 passed under section 148A(d) as well as the notice dated 29.03.2022 issued under section 148 of the Act for the Assessment Year 2018-19 are in violation of the principles of natural justice and are liable to be quashed and set aside. The petitioner has also prayed that the respondent may be directed to give an opportunity of hearing to the petitioner to file reply to the show-cause notice and thereafter, pass a fresh order under section 148A(d) of the Act. 4. Learned Senior Advocate Mr. Prakash Shah for the petitioner reiterated the facts and explained in detail as to why the notice was not served upon the Email address which was registered with the Department as different Email addresses were available on record which were not reflected in the system correctly. 5.On the other hand, learned Senior Standing Counsel Mr. Karan Sanghani for the respondent referred to the additional affidavit-in-reply filed on behalf of the respondent on 07.04.2025 wherein, it is stated that the respondent has updated his Email ID: [email protected] upon which, intimation under section 143(1) has been sent by CPC but the same was not reflected in the ITBA system due to non- updation of the system. 6. Learned Senior Advocate Mr. Prakash Shah also confirmed that the aforesaid Email ID of the petitioner is correct and is in operation as on today. 7. Considering the above submissions, impugned order dated 28.03.2022 under section 148A(d) and the notice dated 29.03.2022 under section 148 of the Act are hereby quashed and set aside and the matter is remanded back to the respondent No.3 so as to provide an opportunity to the petitioner-assessee to file a reply to the notice under section 148A(b) and thereafter, give an opportunity of hearing to the petitioner and pass a fresh de novo order under section 148A(d) of the Act. Such exercise shall be completed within a period of Twelve weeks from the date of receipt of copy of this order. 8. With the aforesaid direction, the petition is disposed of. Notice is discharged.
