Summary: Kotak Flexicap Fund was renamed Kotak Flexi Cap Fund effective 26 August 2026. The change resolves the ledger’s historical candidate name; it does not create a new underlying scheme or reset unit holding periods. Kotak Flexi Cap Fund is an open-ended dynamic equity scheme that can invest across large-cap, mid-cap and small-cap companies. The official scheme page reports assets under management of ₹53,086 crore as at 30 September 2026, identifies Nifty 500 TRI as the Tier I benchmark and Nifty 200 TRI as the Tier II benchmark, and classifies the scheme as Very High risk. Its September portfolio showed 69.64% large-cap, 25.05% mid-cap, 3.81% small-cap and 1.50% debt and money-market exposure; these are a dated snapshot, not fixed allocation promises. The current minimum investment shown is ₹100. The exit-load structure permits redemption or switch-out of up to 10% of the initial investment within one year without load, charges 1% on the excess within one year, and is nil after one year, subject to the rate applicable when units were allotted. Direct and Regular plans use the same portfolio but different expenses. This article explains portfolio flexibility, operations and current equity-oriented fund tax without recommending a plan, option or investment decision. Sector Weights Reveal Concentration That a Category Label Hides The same dated page showed banks at 22.81% of the portfolio, followed by chemicals and petrochemicals at 7.03%, retailing at 6.48%, pharmaceuticals and biotechnology at 6.41%, and aerospace and defence at 5.34%. A broad index benchmark does not prevent sector concentration. Sector weights should be read with issuer-level holdings, cash, derivatives and the investor’s other assets, rather than treated as a forecast that the largest sector will outperform. AUM Is Scale, Not Safety The official AUM was ₹53,086 crore and average AUM ₹52,832 crore as at 30 September 2026. Large AUM can support operating scale, but it can also affect trade size and portfolio flexibility. It does not insure units, cap losses or guarantee liquidity in stressed markets. Mutual-fund assets are held for unit holders under the trust structure and remain exposed to the market value and liquidity of portfolio securities. Performance Tables Need Matching Plan and Period The displayed historical returns are for Direct Plan Growth and use the relevant plan inception. The scheme’s original inception date is 11 September 2009, while the Direct Plan Growth option began on 1 January 2013. A Regular Plan investor should not substitute Direct Plan results because recurring expenses differ. Point-to-point and rolling returns answer different questions, and neither predicts the next holding period. Riskometer Can Change With the Portfolio The current Very High classification is based on the portfolio as at 30 September 2026. The Riskometer is a standardised risk indicator, not a maximum-loss estimate. It can change as portfolio composition and regulatory methodology change. Equity valuation, sector concentration, mid-cap liquidity, issuer governance, derivative, settlement and operational risks remain even when a prior period’s return was positive. ₹100 Minimums Do Not Answer Suitability or Affordability The current page displays ₹100 as the initial and additional investment minimum and also lists SIP purchases from ₹100. A low minimum makes transaction entry accessible; it does not make the scheme low-risk, guarantee diversification across an investor’s overall portfolio or prove that a five-year horizon will produce a gain. The page indicates an ideal horizon of five years and above, but an investor may still face a loss at any redemption date. NAV and Unit Count Must Be Read Together NAV is the per-unit value after scheme assets and liabilities. A higher NAV does not make a fund more expensive in the way a price-to-earnings multiple might, and a lower NAV does not make another fund cheaper. Investment value depends on units multiplied by NAV. Applicable NAV follows SEBI cut-off and funds-realisation rules; a platform timestamp alone may not determine the allotment NAV if funds or KYC arrive later. Growth and IDCW Have Different Cash-Flow Records Growth retains income and realised gains within the scheme until redemption. IDCW permits distribution from distributable surplus when declared; it is not fixed interest or an assured dividend. A payout reduces NAV to the extent of the distribution and applicable effects. Reinvestment creates new units with their own cost and date. The investor should verify available options, default rules and tax consequences in the current SID and transaction form. The Exit Load Uses a Ten Per Cent Free Band The current load structure states that redemption or switch-out of up to 10% of the initial investment amount within one year from allotment carries no exit load. Units redeemed or switched out above that limit within one year carry 1%, while redemption or switch-out after one year carries nil exit load. The applicable load is based on the rate prevalent when the units were allotted. Each purchase or SIP instalment therefore needs its own lot date. A Switch Is Not an Administrative Relabelling A switch between schemes, plans or options is generally processed as redemption of old units and purchase of new units. It can trigger exit load, Securities Transaction Tax and capital-gains tax even if no cash reaches the bank account. The August 2026 rename is different: it changed the scheme name without requiring the investor to switch. A distributor or platform should not describe a plan switch as necessary merely because the name changed. Redemption Timing Depends on a Valid Request An open-ended structure allows valid redemption requests on business days, but payment depends on transaction cut-off, bank validation, lien or pledge status, KYC, folio operation and applicable timelines. Market stress affects NAV through portfolio prices and liquidity. Exit load is deducted under the scheme rule and is separate from STT and income tax. The investor should keep the transaction confirmation and final account statement. Direct and Regular Plans Share Assets but Deduct Different Expenses Direct and Regular plans belong to the same scheme and hold one common portfolio. The Direct Plan excludes distributor commission and normally has a lower total expense ratio. The Regular Plan includes distribution-related expenses. Because expenses are charged daily to scheme assets, plan NAVs and long-term realised returns can diverge even though the securities are the same. The official page displayed an expense ratio of 0.61 for the Direct Plan data set as at 30 September 2026 and states that the figure includes base expense ratio plus GST thereon. TER is dynamic and plan-specific. An investor should use the AMC’s live TER disclosure for the chosen plan on the decision date rather than copy a dated page value. Distributor commission, transaction platform arrangements and advice should be evaluated separately from portfolio quality. Equity-Oriented Fund Tax Applies Lot by Lot Kotak Flexi Cap Fund is ordinarily treated as an equity-oriented fund when it satisfies the statutory domestic-equity condition. For transfers under the current regime, units held for not more than 12 months generally produce short-term capital gain. Where the equity-oriented fund and Securities Transaction Tax conditions are met, the special short-term rate is 20%, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. The holding period is measured for each allotment lot. Long-Term Gain Shares an Aggregate ₹1.25 Lakh Threshold Qualifying gain on units held for more than 12 months is generally taxed at 12.5% on the aggregate qualifying long-term gain above ₹1.25 lakh for the tax year, subject to statutory conditions, surcharge and cess. The threshold is shared across covered listed equity shares, equity-oriented fund units and business-trust units; it is not a separate exemption for every scheme or folio. Grandfathering can matter for units acquired before 1 February 2018. IDCW and Non-Resident Cases Need Separate Checks IDCW is taxable in the recipient’s hands at the applicable rate, and payer withholding rules and thresholds should be checked for the current tax year. Reinvestment does not erase the income event and creates a new acquisition lot. Non-resident investors can face special withholding, residential-status, treaty and repatriation analysis. Capital gain, exit load, STT, stamp duty and TDS are different items and should not be merged in one effective-rate assumption.
