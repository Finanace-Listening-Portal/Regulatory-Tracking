Info Systems Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) Section 57(iii) Disallowance Cannot Be Sustained Where an Expense Already Added Back to Business Income Is Claimed Only Once Under Other Sources Interest Income and Corresponding Expenditure Shifted Together C.E. Info Systems Limited earned interest income of ₹15,98,10,859, which was included in its profit and loss account. For income-tax purposes, the company excluded the gross interest from its business income computation and offered it separately under “Income from Other Sources.” At the same time, it added back ₹1,59,81,086 to business income, representing expenditure allocated to earning the interest. It then deducted the same amount from the interest income under section 57(iii). The amount represented 10% of the interest income. The company explained that it had allocated a portion of common administrative, banking, documentation, professional and finance-management costs to its interest-earning activity. According to the company, the two entries merely shifted the expenditure from one head of income to another. The same amount had been added back under the business head before being claimed under other sources. Why the Assessing Officer and CIT(A) Rejected the Claim During scrutiny, the Assessing Officer called for documentary evidence of the expenditure and an explanation of its nexus with the interest income. Finding the explanation and supporting evidence inadequate, the AO disallowed ₹1,59,81,086 and added it to the total income. The CIT(A) acknowledged that the computation contained both the add-back under business income and the deduction under other sources. However, the CIT(A) rejected the argument that the adjustment was tax-neutral and treated the deduction as an unsupported estimate. The assessee’s expense statement allocated percentages ranging from 1% to 20% across different expenses, but the CIT(A) found that the entries lacked supporting evidence and did not establish how the expenses related to earning interest. The addition was accordingly sustained. Assessee’s Argument Before the Tribunal The assessee took the Tribunal through the relevant pages of its computation. It explained that the starting figure for business income was the profit disclosed in the profit and loss account, which already reflected both the interest receipts and the expenditure. When the company took the receipts out of business income, it also reversed the corresponding expenditure in that computation. Both amounts were then taken into account in computing income from other sources. The assessee also invoked the principle of consistency, pointing to earlier assessments in which it said the Department had accepted the same method of allocation. It cited CIT v. Excel Industries Ltd. (2013) 358 ITR 295 (SC) in support. The Tribunal ultimately decided the issue by examining the computation for the year under appeal. Tribunal’s Illustration Shows Why the Addition Failed The Tribunal referred to section 14, which provides for computing income under different heads. It found that the company had removed both the interest income and the related expenditure from its business computation before taking those figures into account under other sources. On examining the two computations together, the Tribunal found no additional expenditure claim. The Tribunal illustrated the point with a profit of ₹100, which included interest income of ₹15 and related expenditure of ₹1.50. Removing the gross interest and adding back the expenditure produced business income of ₹86.50. Under other sources, deducting ₹1.50 from ₹15 produced income of ₹13.50. Together, the two heads still yielded ₹100. This showed that the company had deducted the expenditure only once across the two heads. On those facts, the Tribunal rejected the lower authorities’ treatment of the entry as a separate claim requiring separate documentation. It held that the adjustment reflected a head-wise recomputation of amounts already in the profit and loss account and deleted the addition of ₹1,59,81,086. Delay of 41 Days Condoned The Tribunal also condoned the 41-day delay in filing the appeal. The company stated on affidavit that it had received no alert on its registered email addresses or mobile numbers and no physical communication of the appellate order. It learnt of the order when a subsequent assessment order for AY 2024–25, dated 3 March 2026, referred to it. The Tribunal accepted the explanation as reasonable cause and proceeded to decide the appeal on merits. The appeal was allowed. Author’s Comments The deciding fact was the matching add-back under business income. Once that entry is traced to the deduction under other sources, the effect of the assessment becomes clear. The AO’s adjustment denied the deduction under other sources while retaining the add-back under business income, increasing total income by the very amount that the assessee had shifted between the two heads. The ruling is fact-specific. The 10% figure came from the company’s allocation for the year, and the Tribunal granted relief on the strength of the matching entries in its computation. In a similar dispute, the most direct way to demonstrate the treatment is to reconcile the profit and loss account, Schedule BP and Schedule OS, showing where the same expenditure was added back and where it was claimed. Brief facts of the case are, assessee filed their return of income on 30.11.2022 declaring total income at Rs 14,36,59,467/-. The return was processed under section 143(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (for short ‘the Act’). Subsequently, the case was selected for complete scrutiny under CASS. Accordingly, notices under section 143(2) and 142(1) were issued and served on the assessee. In response, assessee filed the relevant details as called for. During assessment proceedings, the AO observed from computation of income from other sources and he noticed that assessee had claimed deduction of Rs.1,59,81,086/- under section 57 of the Act against income from other sources. A separate notice under section 142(1) dated 26.12.2023 was issued and the assessee was asked to furnish reason for deduction under section 57 with documentary evidence and nexus with income from other sources. In response, assessee filed computation of total income. On perusal of the computation of total income, he observed that assessee has shown interest income of Rs.15,98,10,859/- under the head ‘Income from other sources’. At the same time. assessee also debited an amount of Rs.1,59,81,086/- on account of expenses incurred. Such deduction under the head income from other sources prima facie seems to be violation of provisions of section 57. AO further issued notices asking for the assessee to explain the above claim of deduction under section 57 of the Act. Since there was no proper explanation from the assessee, even at the time of discussion through video conference as requested by the assessee, he observed that assessee failed to file any documentary evidences to claim the above deduction under section 57. Accordingly, he disallowed the above claim of the assessee and added to the total income of the assessee. 5. Aggrieved with the above order, assessee preferred an appeal before the ld. NFAC Delhi and filed detailed submissions. Before ld. The same has been offered to tax under the head “Income from Other Sources.” In its computation of business income, the Appellant excluded this interest income and, correspondingly, added back 10% – Rs.1,59,81,086/- representing estimated expenditure attributable to earning this interest income. The same amount was then claimed as deduction u/s 57 (iii) to ensure accurate head-wise classification. This treatment was tax-neutral, as the total taxable income remained unaffected – it only ensured proper allocation between “Business Income” and “Income from other sources.” It is respectfully submitted that this figure forms part of the assessee’s total claim under section 57 amounting to Rs.1,60,87,694/-, as per ITR-6 (Acknowledgement No.828647301301122, Page no. 59 of ITR-6). Legal Position Supporting the Claim The claim is fully supported by settled judicial principles: 1. CIT v. Rajasthan State Warehousing Corporation (2000) 242 ITR 450 (SC) Where income falls under different heads, reasonable apportionment of expenses between such heads is permissible and should be allowed. 2. CIT V. Bombay Samachar Ltd. (1969) 74 ITR 723 (Bom) Where the same set of expenses pertains to multiple activities, reasonable apportionment is permissible Accordingly, the ad-hoc 10% allocation is fully supported in law and consistent with accounting prudence. 4. Application to the facts/Revenue-Neutral Nature of Adjustment 4.1 The Appellant has already added back the same Rs.1,59,81,086/- under the Business Head” ensuring no double deduction or reduction in overall taxable income. 4.2 Thus, even if the claim u/s 57(1)(iii) is ignored, there will be no impact on total taxable income, making the adjustment revenue-neutral. 4.3 Courts have consistently held that when there is no loss to Computational differences do not warrant disallowance. 4.4 It is respectfully submitted that in the assessee’ ‘s own regular assessment case for earlier assessment years A.Y.s 2010-11, 201 1-12. 2013-14, 2014-15. 2016-17, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2020-21, 2021-22 and 2022-23 the Department has Consistently examined and accepted the identical methodology of allocating and claiming proportionate expenses under section 57 against interest and dividend income. The deductions were duly allowed in those years, thereby establishing a settled and accepted position in favour of the assessee. In the absence of any change in the underlying facts or legal provisions, a deviation from this established position would be contrary to the principle of Consistency recognised by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in CIT v. Excel Industries Ltd. (2013) 358 ITR 295 (SC) and Berger Paints India Ltd. v. CIT (2004) 266 ITR 99 (SC). Hence, the disallowance made by the Assessing Officer results only in duplication of the same amount already taxed under another head, which is neither warranted under the Act nor results in any addition to the total income. 5. CIT(A) dismissed the plea of the assessee by observing as under :- “6.1 The facts of the case, in brief are that the appellant has claimed an expense of Rs.1,59,81,086/- being 10% of the interest income, as expenses u/s 57(ii) of the Act against the interest earned Rs.15,98,10,859/-. In its appeal, in Form no.35, the appellant had raised only one ground, that is disallowance of expenditure of Rs.1,59,81,086/-. During the course of appellate proceedings, the appellant has made submissions which are reproduced above. In its submission dated 01.11.2025, the appellant has stated “In its computation of business income, the appellant excluded its interest income and correspondingly added back 10% – Rs. 1,59, 81, 086/- representing estimated expenditure attributable to earning this interest income. The same amount was then claimed as deduction u/s 57(iii) to ensure accurate head wise classification. This treatment was Tax Neutral as the total taxable income remained unaffected. It only ensured proper allocation between ‘Business Income’ and ‘Income from Other Sources“, 6.2 It is seen from the computation of total income submitted during appellate proceedings that the appellant has added back an amount of Rs.1,59,81,086/- being expenses incurred on interest income. Further, an amount of Rs.15,98,10,859/- has been reduced from the resultant business income as interest income which is computed separately as income from other sources. In its computation of income from other sources, the appellant has reduced, inter-alia, an amount of Rs.1,59,81,086/- from interest income as expenses related to earning of interest income. The appellant had stated this treatment of interest income and expenses for earning interest income is Tax Neutral and does not affect the total income. This statement of the appellant is factually and legally incorrect as the interest income has been taken out of computation of business income and thereafter separately computed as income from Other Sources. There is no element of Tax Neutrality in this arrangement. Thus, it is clear that the appellant has reduced an amount of Rs.1,59,81,086/- being 10% of the interest income (Rs. 15,98,10,859/-) on estimated basis. 6.3 There is no logic to this claim of estimate deduction. Even the Act provides that only the expenses attributable to the earning of interest income can be claimed as deduction against the interest income. Section 57(ii) of the Act which provides for such deductions is reproduced hereunder: “57. (i) (ii) (iii) Any other expenditure (not being in the nature of laid out or of capital expenditure) laid out or expanded wholly and exclusively for the purpose of earning such income. 6.4 Further, no proviso/explanation to this sub-section provides for claim of estimated additions. That means that the expenses allowable u/s 57(ii) of the Act has to be quantified strictly on the basis of the evidences submitted w which lead to the fact that such expenses are made wholly and exclusively Tor the purpose of earning the interest income. In the present case, the appellant has claimed the expenses on estimated basis @10%. The appellant has with its submission dated 01.11 2025 enclosed one Annexure-D which gives the list of expenses and the appellant has in the last column estimated the expenses attributable to interest income. It is seen from the said Annexure-D that the appellant has allocated various percentages ranging from 1% to 20% of the expenses attributable to the interest income. Moreover, no evidences of any of the expenses in Annexure-D are provided. Also, it is not clarified from expense as to how these expenses are attributable to the earning of interest income. Under these circumstances, it is held that the amount of Rs.1,59,81,000/- claimed as expenses and attribute to interest income are not allowable and the additions made by the AO is justified.” 7. Considered the rival submissions and material placed on record, we observed that the assessee normally has to disclose income from various heads as per section 14 of the Act. Accordingly, assessee has disclosed income from business by taking the profit declared by the assessee from the profit and loss account and readjusted the above said profit declared in the profit and loss account to disclose actual business income earned by the assessee by eliminating the other sources of income and expenditure relating to other heads of income. In that process, we noticed that assessee has eliminated the income as well as expenses relating to income from other sources so that assessee can determine separately income from other sources. Accordingly, once assessee has removed the income and expenditure relating to income from other sources from the profit and loss account, the assessee has determined the actual income from other sources by disclosing the interest income separately and from that above, the relevant interest expenditure disallowed while computing the income from business and the same was claimed as expenditure under the head income from other sources. In our considered view, assessee has not claimed any additional expenditure anywhere in the computation of income. To demonstrate the same, we give the following example :- (i) Profit as per Profit & Loss A/c Rs.100.0 (ii) Less : Interest income (separately determined for income from other sources) Rs. 15.0 * Rs. 85.0 (iii) Add : Interest expenses disallowed by the assessee of Rs.1.5 Rs. 1.5 ** Income from the head “Business” (A) Rs. 86.5 Calculation of Income from other sources (separately) Interest Income Rs.15.0 * Less : Interest expenses Rs. 1.5 ** Income from other sources (B) Rs.13.5 Total income of the assessee Business Income (A) Rs.86.5 Income from other sources (B) Rs.13.5 TOTAL : Rs.100 12. From this example, it is clear that assessee has recomputed the income for the year under consideration by eliminating the income and expenditure relating to the head income from other sources and recomputed the same separately. Therefore, there is no undisclosed income or expenditure claimed by the assessee by recalculating various heads of income to disclose the relevant incomes under different heads. Therefore, the findings of the lower authorities are not proper and assessee cannot bring on record separate documents for claiming the interest expenditure as directed by the AO. Therefore, we do not see any reason to sustain the addition made by the AO. 13.
