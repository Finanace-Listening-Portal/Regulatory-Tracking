(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court, in S S Overseas Vs Union of India & Anr., dismissed Special Leave Petitions challenging the Allahabad High Court’s decision concerning the requirement to issue speaking orders upon finalisation of customs assessments. After condoning the delay and hearing the petitioner’s counsel, the Supreme Court declined to interfere with the impugned judgment and order. The underlying dispute concerned the reassessment of imported polyester knitted fabrics, where the importer had accepted the redetermined customs value in writing and subsequently sought speaking orders. The Allahabad High Court had held that, in view of the express exception under Section 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962, a speaking order was unnecessary when the importer had confirmed acceptance of reassessment in writing. The Supreme Court’s order contains no independent examination of the statutory provisions or detailed reasons for declining interference. Consequently, the High Court’s dismissal of the writ petitions remained undisturbed. Read HC Judgment in this case: No Speaking Order Required When Importer Accepts Customs Reassessment: Allahabad HC Background of the Customs Reassessment Dispute The dispute originated from several writ petitions filed by S S Overseas before the Allahabad High Court, with Writ Tax No. 881 of 2022 treated as the leading petition. The importer had filed Bills of Entry declaring imported goods as mixlot/stocklot polyester knitted fabric and had self-assessed the customs duty under Section 17(1) of the Customs Act, 1962. During verification under Section 17(2), the Customs authorities observed that the declared values were lower than contemporaneous import values of similar goods. They sought additional information regarding the composition, dimensions, GSM, colouring, coating and intended use of the imported fabric to determine its appropriate classification and valuation. According to the Customs Department’s counter-affidavit, the importer submitted letters accepting redetermined values ranging from USD 1.85 to USD 1.96 per kilogram. The importer also requested that no show cause notice or speaking order be issued. Provisional Assessment and Finalisation of Bills of Entry The Customs authorities considered testing necessary to establish whether the imported fabric was prime material or stocklot/mixlot goods. Pending test reports, the Bills of Entry were provisionally assessed against bonds and bank guarantees. The bank guarantees covered the differential duty exposure between the accepted reassessed values and the proposed value of USD 2.85 per kilogram for prime goods. Following receipt of the test reports, no difference in duty liability was found that required departure from the provisional assessment. The Bills of Entry were therefore finally assessed at the values previously accepted by the importer. The authorities also returned the bonds and bank guarantees for cancellation. Despite having accepted the reassessed values, the importer approached the Allahabad High Court seeking directions requiring the Customs Department to issue speaking orders in respect of the finalised Bills of Entry. Customs Department’s Submissions The Customs Department opposed the writ petitions principally on the ground that the importer had expressly accepted the redetermined values in writing. It relied on the importer’s letters, which were produced with its counter-affidavit, to establish that the reassessment had been accepted and that the importer had specifically requested the authorities not to issue show cause notices or speaking orders. The Department also alleged that the petitioner had failed to disclose these letters while filing the writ petitions. This was an allegation advanced in the counter-affidavit; the High Court’s operative reasoning rested on the documentary evidence of written acceptance. The Department further referred to the Customs (Finalization of Provisional Assessment) Regulations, 2018, notified through Notification No. 73/2018-Customs (N.T.) dated 14 August 2018. Under Regulation 6(3), a speaking order following principles of natural justice is required where final assessment is contrary to provisional assessment. Regulation 6(4) deals with cases where final assessment confirms provisional assessment and requires the officer to ascertain the importer’s or exporter’s acceptance on record and communicate the finalisation date in writing. Allahabad High Court’s Findings on Section 17(5) The Allahabad High Court examined the statutory framework governing customs self-assessment, verification and reassessment under Section 17. The Court noted that Section 17(4) permits the proper officer to reassess duty where the original self-assessment is incorrect. However, Section 17(5) specifically excludes from the requirement of a speaking order those reassessment cases where the importer or exporter confirms acceptance of the reassessment in writing. The High Court found that the documents produced with the counter-affidavit established that S S Overseas had itself confirmed acceptance of the reassessment in writing. Consequently, the Court concluded that there was no occasion for the proper officer to issue a speaking order under Section 17(5). Finding no merit in the petitions, the Allahabad High Court dismissed all the connected writ petitions. Supreme Court’s Decision on the Special Leave Petitions Aggrieved by the High Court’s decision, S S Overseas approached the Supreme Court through Special Leave Petitions. The Supreme Court condoned the delay and heard counsel appearing for the petitioner. It thereafter recorded that it was not inclined to interfere with the impugned High Court judgment and order and dismissed the Special Leave Petitions. The Supreme Court also directed that any pending applications stand disposed of. Importantly, the Supreme Court did not deliver a reasoned judgment independently interpreting Section 17(5), nor did it expressly affirm every aspect of the High Court’s reasoning. The substantive interpretation concerning written acceptance and the requirement for a speaking order is contained in the Allahabad High Court’s judgment, which was left undisturbed. Legal Significance The decision highlights the importance of an importer’s written acceptance in customs reassessment proceedings. Where an importer expressly accepts reassessment in writing, the statutory exception under Section 17(5) may dispense with the requirement to issue a speaking order on that reassessment. The case also illustrates the need to distinguish between the statutory treatment of reassessment under Section 17 and the procedures governing finalisation of provisional assessment under Section 18 and the 2018 Regulations. The Supreme Court’s dismissal of the Special Leave Petitions leaves the Allahabad High Court’s decision operative between the parties, but should not be presented as a separately reasoned Supreme Court declaration of law under Article 141. Conclusion The Allahabad High Court held that S S Overseas could not insist upon a speaking order under Section 17(5) after expressly accepting the customs reassessment in writing. The Supreme Court subsequently declined to interfere with that decision and dismissed the Special Leave Petitions. The central principle emerging from the High Court judgment is that written acceptance of reassessment attracts the exception to the speaking-order requirement under Section 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962.
