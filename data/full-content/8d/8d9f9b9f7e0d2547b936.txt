Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Shivish Bio Bag LLP (GST AAR Gujarat) The Gujarat Authority for Advance Ruling examined the classification and applicable GST rate on biodegradable and compostable carry bags manufactured by Shivish Bio Bag LLP, Morbi. The applicant manufactured Bio Carry Bags using compostable polymer materials or biodegradable plastic substitutes and had been classifying them under HSN 39232990. It sought clarification on whether the bags should be classified under Chapter 39, covering plastics and articles thereof, or Chapter 48, covering paper and paperboard products. The applicant also sought confirmation that the products were eligible for the concessional GST rate of 5% under Entry No. 319 of Schedule I to Notification No. 09/2025-Central Tax (Rate), effective from 22 September 2025. The applicant submitted that its carry bags were environmentally sustainable products capable of biodegradation or composting under prescribed conditions. It relied on ISO 17088:2021 certification, quality certificates, sales invoices and technical documentation to substantiate the biodegradable nature of the products. According to the applicant, Entry No. 319 specifically covers paper sacks, paper bags and biodegradable bags falling under Chapters 39 and 48. It argued that the entry was intended to extend the concessional GST rate to biodegradable bags irrespective of whether they were manufactured from paper or compostable plastic materials. The Authority examined the nature of the raw materials and relevant headings of Chapter 39 of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975. Since the bags were manufactured from compostable polymers or biodegradable plastic substitutes, it held that Chapter 39 was applicable rather than Chapter 48. After examining the tariff headings, the Authority concluded that the products were appropriately classifiable under residual tariff item 39269099, covering other articles of plastics. The classification differed from the applicant’s existing classification under HSN 39232990. For determining the GST rate, the Authority examined Entry No. 127 of Schedule II, which generally covers other articles of plastics under Heading 3926 at 18%, and Entry No. 319 of Schedule I, which covers paper sacks, paper bags and biodegradable bags under Chapters 39 and 48 at 5%. It also referred to discussions during the 54th GST Council meeting concerning environmentally friendly alternatives to conventional plastic bags. The Authority interpreted the inclusion of biodegradable bags in Entry No. 319 as extending the reduced rate to qualifying biodegradable plastic bags, notwithstanding their classification under Chapter 39. The Authority considered an interim test report issued by the Institute of Petrochemicals Technology concerning material supplied by the applicant’s sister concern. The sample contained blends of polylactic acid (PLA) and polybutylene adipate terephthalate (PBAT). The disintegration test showed that only 8.1% dry mass remained after 84 days, against the prescribed maximum of 10%. The aerobic biodegradation test showed 92.34% biodegradation after 133 days, exceeding the prescribed threshold of 90% within 180 days. However, the Authority also noted that certain tests, including heavy-metal analysis, seed germination and earthworm toxicity testing, remained incomplete and that the report was interim rather than final. The Authority ultimately ruled that the applicant’s biodegradable or compostable carry bags were classifiable under HSN 39269099 and covered by Entry No. 319 of Schedule I to Notification No. 09/2025-Central Tax (Rate). Accordingly, the supplies attract 5% GST, comprising 2.5% CGST and 2.5% SGST. Importantly, the ruling expressly made this treatment conditional upon the validity of the applicant’s claim that the products were biodegradable. Thus, the concessional rate was not granted unconditionally merely because the products were described or marketed as compostable bags. Let us first take up the Entry No.319 which reads as “Paper sacks/bags and biodegradable bags” and covers Chapters 39 & 48 of the First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 (51 of 1975). On verification, we find that this entry was not a part of any of the 6 schedules covering the earlier Notification No.01/2017- Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017 but has been newly inserted in Schedule-I of existing Notification No.09/2025-Central Excise (Rate) dated 17.09.2025 (which superseded the earlier Notification No.01/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017). Further, as we find that there is a conjunction ‘and’ between ‘Paper sacks/bags’ & ‘biodegradable bags’, we find it necessary to find out the relevance of usage of the conjunction ‘and’ in the context of the said entry. In this context, the first question that naturally arises in the mind is whether paper sacks/bags also need to be biodegradable bags in order to be covered in this entry. We also need to find out whether this entry covers paper sacks/bags as well as bags which are biodegradable in nature. It, therefore, becomes mandatory to know the background which necessitated the insertion of the said entry in the present Notification which superseded the earlier Notification No.01/2017- Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017. 10.1 For the purpose, we find it obligatory to refer to the minutes of the 54th meeting Ofthe GST Council held on 09.09.2024 under the Chairpersonship Of the Hon’ble Union Finance Minister, in order to unearth the background which could have possibly culminated in the insertion of Entry No.319 in Notification No.09/2025- Central Tax (Rate) dated 17.09.2025. During the course of the meeting, when the Joint Secretary, TRU-1 stated that the Fitment Committee, in the 53rd GST Council meeting had recommended maintaining status quo regarding the rate on paper sacks which have always attracted a GST rate of 18%, the Hon’ble Minister from Maharashtra informed/stated that along with several other States, they have implemented a ban on plastic bags to promote eco-friendly and environmental friendly alternatives and were encouraging the use of paper sacks and paper bags across various municipal corporations and urban areas; that in line with the recommendation made in the 53rd GST Council meeting to reduce the GST rate on corrugated boxes made of paperboard to 12%, the GST rate on paper sacks should also be reduced to 12%; that such a reduction would further the goal of increasing the use of paper sacks and paper bags over plastic alternatives in urban and semi-urban areas, thus supporting eco-friendly and environmental friendly materials and products. Accordingly, the Fitment Committee was urged to consider this request. Subsequently, the Hon’ble member from Meghalaya informed that in addition to what the Hon’ble Minister from Maharashtra mentioned, they are also promoting biodegradable bags made from Starch and numerous industries are being set up for these bags, which while resembling plastic, are actually made from starch and decompose automatically within 90 days. The Hon’ble Member emphasized to also include this category in the proposal under examination. The Hon’ble Chairperson suggested assigning a separate HSN code for the biofriendly category of bags and urged the Hon’ble Member from Meghalaya to provide the necessary details. Ultimately, the Fitment Committee recommended to maintain statu§—quo on paper bags and recommended that for the prospective period, the rate be reviewed by the Fi Committee with inputs from the states Of Maharashtra and Meghalaya. 10.2 Therefore, what appears to be forthcoming from the above discussions and the subsequent insertion of Entry No.319 ( ‘Paper sacks/bags and Biodegradable bags’ covering Chapter 39 & 48) in Schedule-I of Notification No.09/2025-Central Tax (Rate)dated 17.09.2025 with a reduced GST liability of 5% is that the aspect of taxability of both paper sacks/bags as well as biodegradable bags have been taken into consideration by the Government, culminating in the insertion of the said Entry covering both paper sacks/bags and biodegradable bags. The very purpose of the Government in inserting this entry appears to be to promote the production of paper sacks/bags and bio-degradable bags which are eco-friendly and environmental friendly because paper decomposes completely within a few months and does not leave toxic micro plastics in the environment. Further, the purpose of inclusion of Chapter 39 in the said entry appears to be to encourage production of plastic bags which are biodegradable in nature, eco-friendly and environmental friendly. Biodegradable plastic bags are eco-friendly alternatives to conventional plastic that break down naturally through microorganisms into water, carbon dioxide, and biomass and unlike traditional plastics, they are manufactured from renewable plant-based materials or specialized polymers rather than fossil fuels, leaving no toxic or micro plastic residues behind. 10.3 Reference is made to the EU Policy Framework on Biobased, Biodegradable and Compostable Plastics, which explains that plastics referred to as “biodegradable” are designed to decompose at the end of their life by the conversion of all their organic constituents (polymers and organic additives) mainly into carbon dioxide and water, new microbial biomass, mineral salts and, in the absence of oxygen, methane. For such biodegradation to take place, suitable conditions in the receiving environment and sufficient time are necessary, in addition to the characteristics of the plastic material. The said framework further explains that “compostable plastics” are a subset of biodegradable plastics designed to biodegrade under controlled conditions, typically through industrial composting in special facilities for composting or anaerobic digestion. 10.4 The applicant has also submitted that Bio Carry Bags (presently classified by them under HSN 39232990) are manufactured by using compostable polymer materials, and/or biodegradable plastic substitutes and are specifically designed to be environmentally sustainable and capable of undergoing biodegradation/composting under prescribed conditions and are manufactured in compliance with ISO 17088:2021 Standard, which prescribes specifications for compostable plastics.
