(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India dismissed the Special Leave Petition filed by the Commissioner of Customs against the order granting relief to Nanak Electronics Pvt. Ltd. in a dispute concerning refund of Special Additional Duty of Customs (SAD). The Supreme Court condoned the delay and referred to its order dated 04.07.2023 in SLP (C) Diary No. 23340/2023. It declined to interfere with the impugned order, dismissed the petition and disposed of pending applications. The underlying dispute arose from the rejection of the importer’s SAD refund claim on the ground of limitation under Notification No. 102/2007-Customs. The Commissioner of Customs (Appeals) had allowed the refund claim by order dated 18.10.2017, relying on the Delhi High Court’s judgment in Sony India Pvt. Ltd. v. Commissioner of Customs, 2014 (304) E.L.T. 660 (Del.). CESTAT subsequently dismissed the Department’s appeal on 20.02.2018. Before the Delhi High Court, the Department sought to rely on the contrary view taken by the Bombay High Court in CMS Infosystem Ltd. v. Union of India, 2017 (349) E.L.T. 236 (Bom.). It also pointed out that the Supreme Court had dismissed the Special Leave Petition against Sony India on delay while leaving the question of law open. However, the Delhi High Court observed that its decision in Sony India continued to bind coordinate benches. It referred to its subsequent decisions in Commissioner of Customs v. S.R. Traders and Commissioner of Customs v. Tanvir Trading Import. The Delhi High Court initially disposed of the appeal by applying directions from another case but subsequently recalled that order after recognising that the issues were different. On reconsideration, it followed Sony India and dismissed the Department’s appeal. The Supreme Court’s subsequent dismissal of the Special Leave Petition left that result undisturbed. The Supreme Court’s brief order does not independently set out a detailed interpretation of the SAD refund limitation provisions.
