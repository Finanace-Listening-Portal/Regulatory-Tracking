(ITAT Delhi) Section 149 Grandfathering Bars Time-Barred Reassessment Even Where Alleged Escaped Expenditure Exceeds ₹50 Lakh Search and Alleged Diversion of Event Payments The Delhi ITAT held that the wider scope of section 149(1)(b), which covered qualifying escaped income represented by expenditure, remained subject to the grandfathering protection in the first proviso to section 149(1). Where the earlier limitation had expired and the Department failed to establish the statutory asset condition for reopening beyond the ordinary six-year search block, allegations of expenditure exceeding ₹50 lakh could not revive the proceedings. The Tribunal also examined whether the disputed expenditure was deductible under section 37(1). Hero MotoCorp engaged SALT Experience & Management Pvt. Ltd. and related entities to organise promotional and business events. Following a search on 23 March 2022, the Assessing Officer alleged that payments made against event invoices were routed through intermediaries to finance charter flights for the company’s Chairman and Managing Director. For AY 2015–16, the AO disallowed ₹88.67 crore towards domestic events, ₹2.98 crore towards foreign travel and ₹4.02 crore relating to the FIFA World Cup event in Brazil. The CIT(A) upheld the reopening and granted partial relief on the disallowances. The ₹50 Lakh Threshold and the Earlier Asset Condition The notice under section 148 for AY 2015–16 was issued on 2 March 2023. The Tribunal rejected the assessee’s objection based solely on the expiry of three years. Under section 149(1)(b), as amended by the Finance Act, 2022, income represented by expenditure relating to a transaction, event or occasion could qualify for the extended period where the alleged escapement amounted to, or was likely to amount to, ₹50 lakh or more. The decisive issue was whether the notice also satisfied the first proviso to section 149(1). The ordinary six-year limitation applicable to AY 2015–16 had expired on 31 March 2022. Reopening beyond that period under the earlier search framework required satisfaction of the asset condition in the fourth proviso to section 153A(1). Bank statements and transaction records could trace payments through intermediaries, but the Department had failed to show that the alleged escaped income was itself represented by a qualifying asset. The amount of the proposed disallowance could not establish that statutory fact. Applying the Delhi High Court’s reasoning in Smart Chip (P.) Ltd. v. ACIT [2025] 476 ITR 389 (Del.), the Tribunal quashed the reassessments for AYs 2013–14, 2014–15 and 2015–16. The notices for AYs 2016–17, 2017–18 and 2019–20 survived the limitation challenge. Domestic Event Expenditure Requires Event-Specific Examination On merits, Hero MotoCorp furnished invoices, cost break-ups, photographs, invitations, event agendas and contemporaneous communications. The CIT(A) accepted that the events took place, but sustained ₹8.06 crore for four identified events in the lead year. The Tribunal found that the alleged money trail had not been reliably reconciled with the specific amounts debited to the profit and loss account. Uncertainty over the amount allegedly diverted could not justify disallowing the entire expenditure on those events. The Tribunal deleted the amount sustained, upheld the relief already granted by the CIT(A), and declined a further remand. The AO had already had opportunities during assessment and remand proceedings to verify the evidence and identify discrepancies. Fixed Charter Cost Cannot Be Split by Headcount The AO accepted the Chairman’s trip to Colombia and the United States as official but attributed two-thirds of the travel expenditure to accompanying family members. The Tribunal found that the charter cost was fixed and that the Department had shown neither an incremental charter cost nor any separately identifiable family expenses borne by the company. It therefore deleted the entire ₹2.98 crore disallowance. Referring to Hero Honda Motors Ltd. v. JCIT [2006] 103 ITD 157 (Del.), the Tribunal examined the travel in its commercial context. Goodwill, brand positioning and business relationships were relevant to commercial expediency, alongside the accepted official purpose of the trip and the evidence concerning the actual cost. FIFA World Cup Expenditure and the Missing Invoice Line The Tribunal also deleted the ₹4.02 crore disallowance relating to the Brazil event. The absence of a separate charter line in the event invoices and the vendor’s failure to respond under section 133(6) did not establish that the amount represented personal travel. The company had furnished contemporaneous event records, and the Department had failed to link the third-party data extract to the specific expenditure claimed. All assessee appeals were allowed and all Revenue appeals were dismissed. Author’s Comments A reassessment notice may satisfy the ₹50 lakh threshold and still fail on limitation. Where the earlier search provisions govern the limitation inquiry, the recorded material must show how the alleged escapement meets the applicable asset test. On deductibility, the order illustrates the value of retaining event-wise evidence and cost records. Once those records establish the business purpose and support the amount claimed, an allegation of personal diversion must be tested against the specific debit sought to be disallowed.
