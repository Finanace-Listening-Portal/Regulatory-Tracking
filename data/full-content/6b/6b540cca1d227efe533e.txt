(Calcutta High Court) Summary: The Calcutta High Court, in Shankar Prasad Gupta Vs The State of West Bengal & Ors., quashed an ex parte GST adjudication order dated 10 August 2023 and a consequential recovery notice dated 27 February 2026 concerning alleged input tax credit (ITC) mismatch for the period July 2017 to March 2018. Justice Smita Das De held that the authorities had failed to follow the mandatory verification procedure prescribed under Circular No. 183/15/2022-GST dated 27 December 2022 before confirming a demand arising from differences between FORM GSTR-3B and FORM GSTR-2A. The Court directed fresh consideration of the petitioner’s representation after examining the supporting documents and providing a reasonable opportunity of hearing. The petitioner contended that the demand was contrary to paragraph 4 of Circular No. 183/15/2022-GST. Under that procedure, the proper officer must first obtain details of invoices on which ITC was availed in FORM GSTR-3B but which did not appear in FORM GSTR-2A. The officer must thereafter ascertain whether the registered person satisfies the applicable conditions under Section 16 of the CGST Act, including possession of the relevant tax invoice or debit note, receipt of goods or services, and payment of the value of supply together with the applicable tax to the supplier. According to the petitioner, these verification steps were not undertaken before the demand was confirmed. The petitioner further submitted that the show-cause notice had not been served upon him, preventing him from filing a reply. Consequently, the adjudicating authority proceeded ex parte. Reliance was placed on the Division Bench decision in Goutam Bhowmik Vs State of West Bengal & Ors., (2024) 158 taxmann.com 399 (Calcutta), concerning personal hearing before passing an adverse order. The petitioner argued that the adjudication order was illegal and contrary to the principles of natural justice. After considering the rival submissions and examining the materials on record, including the screenshot of the show-cause notice and the consequential ex parte order, the High Court found that the petitioner had established a prima facie case warranting interference. The Court specifically observed that the impugned orders had been passed without compliance with paragraph 4 of Circular No. 183/15/2022-GST. The prescribed procedure required the proper officer to seek the relevant invoice details and verify fulfilment of the conditions under Section 16 before confirming an ITC mismatch demand. The Court found that this exercise did not appear to have been undertaken. Accordingly, the High Court quashed and set aside the adjudication order dated 10 August 2023 and recovery notice dated 27 February 2026. The petitioner was directed to submit a comprehensive representation with supporting documents before respondent No. 4 within four weeks. The authority was directed to consider the representation in light of Circular No. 183/15/2022-GST and pass a reasoned order in accordance with law after granting a reasonable opportunity of hearing. The decision was to be communicated within one week thereafter. The petitioner was also directed not to seek unnecessary adjournments. Cases Discussed Goutam Bhowmik Vs State of West Bengal & Ors., (2024) 158 taxmann.com 399 (Calcutta High Court) — Cited by the petitioner in support of the requirement of personal hearing before passing an adverse order. The present judgment records the petitioner’s reliance on this precedent but bases its operative interference on non-compliance with Circular No. 183/15/2022-GST. Appellant Was Represented by: Mr. Himangshu Kumar Ray, Mr. Saptak Sanyal, Mr. Subhasis Podder, Ms. Shiwani Shaw FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CALCUTTA HIGH COURT 1. Affidavit of service filed in Court today by the petitioner is taken on record. 2. The petitioner is engaged as tentile prouning and trading under the name and style of “G. S. Fabrics” and the petitioner is proprietorship concern duly registered under the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (hereinafter “CGST Act) and the West Bengal Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017(hereinafter referred to as WBGST Act). [Paragraph 2 is to be read subject to the correction order dated 12.10.2026 reproduced below, substituting “The petitioner is engaged as textile processing” for the original opening words.] 3. The issue involved herein is whether the ex parte assessment order dated 10.08.2023 passed under Section 73 of the CGST Act/WBGST Act is sustainable in law, particularly in light of circular of 183/15/2022-GST Dated 27th December, 2022 stating to reconciliation of ITC between FORM GSTR-3B and FORM GSTR-2A. 4. The learned Counsel appearing for the petitioner submits that a show-cause notice was issued on 13.06.2023 for the period of July, 2017 to March 2018 by raising a demand of Rs. 8,11,565/-, on account of alleged mismatched of ITC, including Tax, interest and penalty. 5. The said demand is contrary to Circular No. 183/15/2022-GST dated 27th December, 2022. Reliance is placed upon paragraph 4 of the said circular, “that before raising any demand on account of ITC mismatch, the proper officer is mandated to” which is reproduced below: “The proper officer shall first seek the details from the registered person regarding all the invoices on which ITC has been availed by the registered person in his FORM GSTR 3B but which are not reflecting in his FORM GSTR 2A. He shall then ascertain fulfillment of the following conditions of Section 16 of CGST Act in respect of the input tax credit availed on such invoices by the said registered person: (i) That he is in possession of a tax invoice or debit note issued by the supplier or such other taxpaying documents; (ii) That he has received the goods or services or both; (iii) That he has made payment for the amount towards the value of supply, along with tax payable thereon, to the supplier”. The said procedure was not followed in the present case. 6. It is submitted that since the show-cause notice was not served upon the petitioner. As a result the petitioner had no knowledge of the same and were unable to file a reply. Consequently an ex parte order of adjudication order dated 10.08.2023 was passed by the respondent no.4. 7. In this context petitioner relies upon a Division Bench judgment in the case of Goutam Bhowmik vs. State of West Bengal & Ors., reported in (2024) 158 taxmann.com. 399 (Calcutta), regarding personal hearing before passing of an adverse order. 8. It is therefore contended that the adjudication order is ex facie illegal, in violation of principles of natural justice, and a liable to be set aside. 9. Learned Counsel appearing for the respondent authorities vehemently opposes the submission of the petitioner and submits that no illegality and/or irregularity has been committed by the respondent authorities. Learned counsel further submits that as per Section 169(1)(d) of the CGST/WBGST Act, 2017 uploading any notices or orders in the common portal constitutes valid service. In the present case, the show-cause notice was duly uploaded on the portal. Since the petitioner did not file any reply to the show-cause notice, the adjudicating authority was justified in passing the ex parte order. 10. It is further submitted that an opportunity of personal hearing was afforded to the petitioner which was not availed. Therefore, the petitioners cannot now claim violation of natural justice. 11. After hearing the rival contention of the parties and upon perusal of the materials on record, this Court is of the view that the petitioner has been able to make out a prima facie case warranting interference at this stage. 12. This Court has taken judicial note of the screenshot of the show-cause notice at pages 71 to 73 of the writ petition and also the consequential order passed ex parte by the respondent authorities. 13. It appears that the impugned orders have been passed without adhering to the procedure prescribed in paragraph 4 of circular no. 183/15/2022-GST dated 27th December, 2022. The mandate of the said circular requires the proper officer to first seeks details and verify fulfillment of the conditions of Section 16 before confirming any demand on account of ITC mismatch. Such exercise does not appear to have been undertaken. 14. In view of the above observations, the writ petition is disposed of on the following grounds: a. The order of adjudication dated 10.08.2023 and Recovery Notice dated 27.02.2026 are hereby quashed and set aside. b. The petitioner is directed to file a comprehensive representation before respondent no. 4 within four weeks from date along with all supporting documents. c. Respondent no. 4 shall consider and dispose of the said representation in light of circular no. 183/15/2022 GST dated 27.12.2022 by passing a reasoned order in accordance with law after affording a reasonable opportunity of hearing to the petitioner. Such decision shall be communicated to the petitioners within a week thereafter. d. The petitioner shall not seek any unnecessary adjournments before the adjudicating authority. e. The adjudicating authority shall be at liberty to take an independent decision on merits without being influenced by any observations in this order. 15. With the aforesaid observations and directions, the writ petition is disposed of without going into the merits of the case. 1. The matter has appeared in the list under the heading ‘To Be Mentioned’ for correction of the order dated 6th October, 2026. 2. In the first line of paragraph 2 at page 1, the sentence shall read as ‘The petitioner is engaged as textile processing’ in place and stead of ‘The petitioner is engaged as tentile prouning’. 3. The order dated 6th October, 2026 is corrected accordingly. The remaining part of the said order will remain unaltered. 4. Let this order be read along with the order dated 6th October, 2026. 5. Urgent photostat certified copy of this order be supplied to the parties, if applied for, as early as possible.
