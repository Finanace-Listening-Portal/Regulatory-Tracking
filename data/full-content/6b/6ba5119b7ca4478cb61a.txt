NFAC/2019-20/10467237 by the ld Commissioner of Income Tax(Appeals), NFAC(DELHI) [hereinafter referred to as the “CIT(A)] u/s. 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as the ‘Act), for the A.Y. 2020-21, wherein ld CIT(A) has dismissed assessee’s appeal, against the addition of Rs. 12,20,000/- made on account of unaccounted cash and added u/s 69A r.w.s. 115BBE of the Act in the total income of the assessee, vide assessment order dated 30.03.2025 passed u/s 147 of the Act. 3. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee is an individual and is engaged in the business of transport logistics services and is the proprietor of M/s Chauhan Road Lines. Assessee filed his return of income for A.Y. 2020-21 on 10.01.2021, declaring total income of Rs. 42,53,970/. A search and seizure action was carried out in the case of Bansal Group of Bhopal u/s 132 of the Act on 18.11.2022. During the search, various incriminating documents/ loose papers were found and seized by the department. During the course of analysis of seized data, it was revealed that Bansal Construction Works Pvt. Ltd had given cash of Rs. 12,20,000/- to Chauhan Road Lines (Prop. Ganga Singh Chauhan), which was treated as escaped income in the hands of the assessee. The reassessment proceedings were initiated u/s 147 of the Act after obtaining approval from the competent authority u/s 151 of the Act. Notice u/s 148 of the Act was issued on 26.02.2024. Assessee filed return of income in response thereof on 09.01.2025. Statutory notices u/s 143(2) and 142(1) of the Act were also issued and served upon the assessee, seeking assessee to furnish his explanation. Assessee filed reply and denied to have received any cash from Bansal Construction Works Pvt. Ltd during the relevant financial year. The ld assessing officer noted that the assessee had business transactions with Bansal Construction Works Pvt. Ltd through banking channels amounting to Rs. 1,24,31,818/- and observed that the said payment of cash by Bansal Construction Works Pvt. Ltd to the assessee may not be ruled out as detected during the course of search and seizure, thus added the said unaccounted cash of Rs. 12,20,000/- u/s 69A r.w.s. 115BBE of the Act in the hands of the assessee. 4. Aggrieved, assessee preferred an appeal before ld CIT(A), who dismissed assessee’s appeal, confirming the assessment order in verbatim. 5. Appellant assessee has preferred this second appeal on the following grounds: “1) That the Learned CIT(A)/Assessing Officer erred in facts and circumstances of the case in making addition of Rs 12,20,000/- to the income of the assessee. 2) That the Learned Assessing Officer did not provide any material on which he relied upon in making addition of Rs 12,20,000/- 3) Any other point will be raised at the time of hearing.” 6. Appellant assessee neither responded nor moved any adjournment request. Perused the records and heard ld Sr DR for the respondent revenue. The matter is being adjudicated on merits. 7. The main point for determination under appeal is as to whether ld CIT(A) has erred in confirming the addition of Rs. 12,20,000/- as unaccounted cash in the hands of assessee u/s 69A r.w.s.115BBE of the Act? 8. On perusal of records, it transpires that the reassessment proceedings were initiated against the appellant assessee on the basis of the information consequent upon the search and seizure action dated 18.11.2022 conducted on Bansal Group of Bhopal u/s 132 of the Act, suggesting to have found certain incriminating documents/ loose papers (from the premise of third party), on the basis of which assessee was alleged to have received a cash amount of Rs. 12,20,000/- from Bansal Construction Works Pvt. Ltd. The assessment order does not speak as to what kind of incriminating document/ loose paper was found during the said search. Nothing is on record to suggest that the copies of the said incriminating documents/ loose papers were ever furnished to the appellant assessee? Ld assessing officer has added the impugned amount in the case of assessee merely on the basis of guess work stating that since the assessee had the business relations with the Bansal Construction Works Pvt. Ltd and had transaction amounting to Rs. 1,24,31,818/- through banking channels, therefore, the payment of impugned amount in cash by Bansal Construction Works Pvt. Ltd to assessee may not be ruled out. The foundation of said detection is not disclosed in the assessment order as to what was the document and what was printed or written material on the said documents. We do not find even a prima facie documentary/ oral evidence to sustain the impugned addition, hence the same stands deleted. The impugned order confirming the impugned addition is thus unsustainable. The aforesaid point is accordingly determined in positive in favour of the appellant assessee and against the respondent revenue. This appeal is liable to be allowed. ITA No. 144/Agr/2026 9. This appeal has been preferred against the impugned order dated 22.12.2025 passed in Appeal No NFAC/2020-21/10467240 by the ld Commissioner of Income Tax(Appeals), NFAC(DELHI) u/s. 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, for the A.Y. 2021-22, wherein ld CIT(A) has dismissed assessee’s appeal, confirming the addition of Rs. 10,45,000/- made on account of unaccounted cash and added u/s 69A r.w.s. 115BBE of the Act in the total income of the assessee, vide assessment order dated 30.03.2025 passed u/s 147 of the Act. 10. The facts and issue involved in this case are exactly similar and identical to the one involved in ITA No. 143/Agr/2026 except that in this case, an addition of Rs. 10,45,000/- has been made as unaccounted cash in the hands of the assessee as against the addition of Rs. 12,20,000/- made in ITA No. 143/Agr/2026. Our findings in ITA No. 143/Agr/2026 shall mutatis-mutandis apply in this case also. This appeal is thus liable to be allowed. 11. In the result, the assessee’s appeals viz ITA No. 143&144/Agr/2026 are allowed.
