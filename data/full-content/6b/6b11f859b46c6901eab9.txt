Final approval pending However, the RBI’s in-principle approval does not allow PPSL to immediately commence wallet operations. The company can issue prepaid payment instruments, including wallets, only after receiving final authorisation or a Certificate of Authorisation from the central bank. The RBI has laid down certain conditions that PPSL must fulfil before securing final approval. It has advised the company to undertake a system audit, including a cyber security audit, through an auditor empanelled with CERT-In or a qualified information systems auditor. The audit must cover the requirements specified under the RBI’s Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank Payment System Operators, dated November 5, 2026. The company has also been asked to ensure compliance with the RBI’s April 6, 2018, circular on storage of payment system data.Story continues below this ad Six-month deadline The RBI has stipulated a six-month deadline from the date of its letter for submission of the system audit report. Failure to submit the report within this period will result in the in-principle authorisation automatically lapsing. In such a situation, the central bank will not consider granting final authorisation thereafter. The RBI has further advised PPSL that any change in the company’s shareholding or ownership should be guided by its July 4, 2022, directions on obtaining prior approval in cases involving the takeover or acquisition of control of non-bank payment system operators, and the sale or transfer of payment system activities. Regulatory backdrop The RBI had earlier taken action against Paytm Payments Bank Ltd primarily because of persistent regulatory non-compliance, weaknesses in its systems and controls, and serious supervisory concerns. The matter developed over several years, culminating in the major restrictions announced on January 31, 2024. The RBI cancelled the banking licence of Paytm Payments Bank on April 24, 2026.
