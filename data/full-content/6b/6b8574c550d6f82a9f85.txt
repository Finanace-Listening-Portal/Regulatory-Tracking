Pen Mart (CESTAT Kolkata) Summary: The Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), Kolkata, dismissed the Revenue’s appeals concerning the classification and valuation of imported ice-making and ice-cream-making machinery. The respondent, M/s J.B. Pen Mart, had imported six consignments between December 2017 and May 2018, declaring the goods under Customs Tariff Heading (CTH) 8438 80 90. However, the Customs Department reclassified the machinery under CTH 8418 69 90 and enhanced the declared assessable values, resulting in an aggregate differential customs duty demand of ₹12,27,374.98. The Commissioner of Customs (Appeals) set aside the revised classification and valuation, prompting the Revenue to approach the Tribunal. The Tribunal examined the mandatory requirements of Section 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962, which requires a speaking order when reassessment differs from the importer’s self-assessment, unless the importer accepts the reassessment in writing. In the present case, no speaking order had been issued explaining the revised tariff classification or enhanced valuation. Although the importer paid the additional customs duty, the payment was made under protest to avoid demurrage and fulfil business commitments. The Tribunal held that such payment could not be treated as written acceptance of reassessment. Relying on judicial precedents, including HPL Chemicals Ltd. v. Commissioner of Central Excise, Commissioner of Customs (Port), Kolkata v. Radha Trading and Sigma Power Products Pvt. Ltd., it concluded that failure to follow the prescribed reassessment procedure and principles of natural justice rendered the Department’s action unsustainable. Regarding tariff classification, the Tribunal considered the distinction between machinery used for manufacturing ice cream and equipment principally designed for refrigeration. The Revenue relied on the HSN Explanatory Notes and an advance ruling concerning Mitora Machinex Pvt. Ltd. to support classification under CTH 8418. However, the respondent produced earlier appellate orders dated 11 May 2012, 3 December 2018 and 23 July 2025, supporting classification under CTH 8438. The Tribunal also noted that the Department had treated comparable machinery as classifiable under heading 8438 in earlier proceedings and had permitted subsequent imports by the respondent under that heading. In view of these inconsistent departmental positions, the absence of a speaking order and the earlier classification decisions, the Tribunal held that the Revenue could not selectively apply different tariff headings to comparable goods. Accordingly, it upheld classification under CTH 8438 for the disputed imports. On the separate issue of customs valuation, the Tribunal found that the Department had failed to disclose any reliable evidence, comparable import data or legally sustainable methodology justifying enhancement of the declared transaction values. The assessing authority had neither established valid grounds for rejecting the invoice values nor issued the speaking order required under Section 17(5). The Tribunal agreed with the Commissioner (Appeals) that arbitrary enhancement without supporting evidence violated the Customs Valuation Rules, 2007, and the principles governing transaction value under Section 14 of the Customs Act. It also noted the Revenue’s inconsistent approach to pursuing appeals despite the monetary limits under the National Litigation Policy. Consequently, CESTAT Kolkata upheld the Commissioner (Appeals)’ orders, set aside the reassessment under CTH 8418 and the enhanced customs valuation, and dismissed all the Revenue’s appeals. The facts of the case are that the respondent filed various bills of entries declaring the goods as imported „Mixed lot of Polyester Knitted Fabrics‟ from the manufacturers of China and sought clearances of the goods. We find that after filing the Bills of Entry declaring the value of the imported goods the Proper Officer sought to assess the goods under section 17(4) of the Customs Act, 1962 and assessed accordingly. In terms of section 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962, the Proper Officer is duty-bound to pass a speaking order for enhancement of the declared value within 15(fifteen) days of the passing of the order under section 17(4). Admittedly, no such order was passed by the adjudicating authority after passing the order under section 17(4) of the Act. Therefore, on this ground itself the enhanced value is not sustainable. Moreover, the Ld.Commissioner(Appeals) has examined the issue in detail and observed as under:- “8. In the instant cases, the appellant imported mixed lot of Polyester Knitted Fabric, for which they declared the value in the bills of 3 Customs Appeal No.76519 of 2018 entry. The values declared by the appellant in the impugned Bills of entry, as mentioned on previous page, were enhanced by the assessing officer. The appellant paid duty in respect of impugned Bills of entry in order to clear the goods early to avoid heavy demurrage. 9. I observe that the appellant did not receive assessment order u/s 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962 (hereinafter, referred to as “the Act”). I note that Section 17(5) of the Act states that: “Where any re-assessment done under sub-section (4) is contrary to the self-assessment done by the importer or exporter regarding valuation of the goods, classification exemption or concessions of duty availed consequent to any notification issued therefore under the Act and in cases other than those where the importer or the exporter, as the case may be, confirms his acceptance of the said re-assessment in writing, the proper officer shall pass a speaking order on the re assessment within fifteen days from the date of re-assessment of the bill of entry or the shipping bill as the case may be.” In the instant case, I note that no speaking order has been issued in terms of the said Section 17(5) of the Act, ibid. This by itself is contrary to the provisions of the Act. However, Section 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962 stipulates that a speaking order has to be passed within 15 days if the re-assessment is done contrary to appellant’s claim unless the said re-assessment is accepted by the importer in writing. It is on record that the bills of entry were re-assessed. Naturally, the speaking orders were required to be issued within 15 days thereof as per Section 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962. There is no dispute that no speaking order was passed in this case. In the circumstances, in absence of a valid speaking order as per Section 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962 for re-assessment made by the department contrary to appellant’s claim, re-assessment is liable to be set aside. Hon’ble High Court at Calcutta in the matter of M/s. Sigma Power Products Pvt. Ltd. [2017-TIOL-597-HC-KOL-CUS] held as under: “The order made by the proper officer on the bills of entry in changing the classification and value enhancement is an order of re-assessment attracting the provisions of sub section (5) of section 17 of the Act as urged by the appellant what who appears to be the case from the affidavit filed by the Revenue. Hence, the conclusion has to be that the proper officer was required to pass a speaking order on the re-assessment within 15 days of the re-assessment of the bills of entry. No speaking order was passed. In the circumstances, we have no hesitation to set aside the re assessment. In view of the aforesaid, there is no scope for a contrary finding. The writ petition thus succeeds to that extent.” Similar order was also passed by Hon’ble Tribunal in the matter of C.C. -vs- M/s Mittal Processors Pvt. Ltd. (2013(293) E.L.T. 384 (Tri Mumbai)). Therefore, the impugned re- assessments are liable to be set aside on this ground alone. 14. I find that that there were no ground in respect of the present appeals to doubt the transaction value and to assess the duty liability on the enhanced value, as done by the assessing authority. 15. Next, we take up the point made by the appellant about the Orders In Appeal passed in the past holding that the machines would be classifiable under CTA 8438. 27. In another OIA, The Commissioner (Appeals) subsequent to the period of present dispute, vide OIA No. KOL/CUS/PORT/DC/274/2025 DATED 23.07.2025, has held as under: The appellant, on the other hand, has argued that the classification of the goods under heading 84388090 is not new and has been consistently accepted in the past in their own case. They have also referred to an Advance Ruling in the case of M/s Mitora Machinex Pvt. Ltd., which supports their classification. 16. I find merit in the appellant’s contentions. The classification of identical goods under heading 8438 has already been upheld by this office in past orders, and the same benefit has been extended to the same importer. The department has not produced any evidence that the previous classification orders have been set aside. Mere pendency of an appeal does not affect the validity of those orders. In absence of a stay or contrary judicial decision, the settled classification should not be disturbed. The Advance Ruling cited by the department does not pertain to this specific importer, and classification may vary based on product configuration and usage. I find that the goods imported by the appellant are complete Ice Cream Making Machines and Ice Cube Machines, which are specially designed for preparing and dispensing edible products. These machines do not function merely as parts or accessories of any other larger machinery. As per the Explanatory Notes to the HSN and the classification principles under the Customs Tariff, such fully functional machines used for preparing or vending food or beverages are more appropriately covered under heading 8438. The appellant’s classification under CTH 84388090, therefore, appears reasonable and well supported. 18. Further, I find that the department has relied upon heading 8418, which covers refrigerators, freezers, and other similar cooling equipment. However, the machines in question are not ordinary cooling units but are specifically manufactured for the production and delivery of soft serve ice cream and ice cubes. These are not used for general refrigeration purposes. In such cases, heading 8438, which deals with machinery for the industrial preparation of food or drink, is more specific and should be preferred over a general heading like 8418. 19. In view of the above discussion, I am of the opinion that the classification adopted by the appellant under CTH 84388090 is appropriate for the goods in question. The machines are complete units meant for food preparation and vending, and are not general-purpose cooling devices falling under CTH 84186990. Accordingly, I hold that the impugned classification under CTH 84186990 is not sustainable. Therefore, the impugned Order-in-Original is set aside and the appeal filed by the appellant is allowed with consequential relief. 28. In this regard a reference has been made to Notes 3 and 4 to Section XVI of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, which reads as follows:- “3. Unless the context otherwise requires, composite machines consisting of two or more machines fitted together to form a whole and other machines designed for the purpose of performing two or more complementary or alternative functions are to be classified as if consisting only of that component or as being that machine which, performs the principal function “4. Where a machine (including a combination of machines) consists of individual components (whether separate or interconnected by piping, by transmission devices, by electric cables or by other devices) intended to contribute together to a clearly defined function covered by one of the headings in Chapter 84 or Chapter 85, then the whole falls to be classified in the heading appropriate to that function.” Perceived from the aforesaid viewpoint/statutory interpretation vis-à-vis the catalogue and technical literature in respect of the impugned goods, I find that the imported goods are correctly classifiable under tariff item 843880 90 in terms of Notes 3 and 4 to Section XVI of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, particularly when it is a composite ice-cream making machine and that the imported continuous freezer is an integral part of the complete ice-cream making machine. The decision of Hon’ble CESTAT in the case of M/s. Milk Foods Ltd. (supra), is squarely applicable to this case. I, therefore, set aside the order of assessment by the lower authority and direct that the goods be assessed under tariff item 843880 90. 31. I note that Section 17(5) of the Act states that: “Where any re-assessment done under sub-section (4) is contrary to the self-assessment done by the importer or exporter regarding valuation of the goods, classification or concessions of duty availed consequent to any notification issued therefore under this Act and in cases other than those where the importer or the exporter, as the case may be, confirms his acceptance of the said re-assessment in writing, the proper officer shall pass a speaking order on the re- assessment, within fifteen days from the date of re-assessment of the bill of entry or the shipping bill, as the case may be.” Therefore, Section 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962 stipulates that a speaking order has to be passed within 15 days if the re-assessment is done contrary to appellant’s claim unless the said re-assessment is accepted by the importer in writing. To Summarize: (a) Non-following of principles of natural justice for arriving at the enhanced value and re-classification of goods by issuing Order under Section 17(5) proves fatal to the Revenue‟s case. (b) The Revenue cannot pick and choose the course of further appeal filing in respect of the same goods, by not filing Appeals in some case citing National Litigation Policy and filing the appeals in some other cases. This shows that Revenue has no fixed Policy as to what is to done in which case. (c) Total confusion prevails so far as the classification of the goods in question is concerned. In 2008, 2012, 2018 and 2025 the Revenue has classified the goods in question under CET 8438 and in the present imports during 2017-2018 they have classified the same under CET 8418. In such a scenario of confusion, the benefit of such confusion goes to the appellant. Hence, we hold that the correct classification applicable is CET 8438, in respect of the present appeals. (d) As has been discussed in detail by the Commissioner (Appeals), no reason has been given for enhancement of the 27 value. Further no details have been given as to how the enhanced value has been arrived at. Hence, we hold that the Commissioner (Appeals) has rightly set aside the enhanced value. 47.
