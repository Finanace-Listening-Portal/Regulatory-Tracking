Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Satyendra Kumar Jain Vs Initiating Officer & DCIT (Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA, New Delhi) Deposits Under Void IDS Declarations Can Constitute Benami Property Where Statutory Ingredients Are Established Background and proceedings: The Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA, New Delhi, comprising Members Balesh Kumar and Rajesh Malhotra, decided three appeals challenging the Adjudicating Authority’s order dated 25 February 2020 confirming provisional attachment of ₹5,30,10,000. Vaibhav Jain and Ankush Jain had deposited ₹3,71,25,000 and ₹1,58,85,000 under the Income Declaration Scheme, 2016 (IDS), relating to undisclosed income connected with share capital and share premium investments. Satyendra Kumar Jain separately faced income-tax demands of ₹6,92,45,248 for AY 2011-12 and ₹3,88,54,355 for AY 2012-13. His letters dated 27 June 2018 sought adjustment of the deposits made by the two declarants against his personal tax demands. The Initiating Officer treated the money as benami property and attached it under the Prohibition of Benami Property Transactions Act, 1988. Appellants’ arguments: The appellants denied that Satyendra Kumar Jain had supplied the money or was its beneficial owner. They argued that his letters had been selectively read and were intended only to prevent taxation of the same income in different hands. Vaibhav Jain and Ankush Jain asserted that they declared their own income and made the deposits from their own funds. They disputed whether the Department had established the twin statutory ingredients under Section 2(9)(A): consideration provided by one person for property held by another and an immediate or future direct or indirect benefit to the person providing consideration. They also questioned the investigation, approval and reasons recorded for the provisional attachment. Character of IDS deposits: The appellants argued that amounts deposited as tax, surcharge and penalty had entered the Government’s custody and could no longer be property held by a benamidar. They relied on Section 191 of the Finance Act, 2016, which provides for non-refundability of payments made under the specified IDS provisions. They also contended that such payments could not be adjusted against another assessee’s outstanding demands. The Tribunal considered whether money deposited under the Scheme could nonetheless be benami property in the particular circumstances, focusing on the legal effect of the invalidity of the underlying declarations. Void declarations: The Principal Commissioner of Income Tax had declared the IDS declarations of Vaibhav Jain and Ankush Jain illegal and void on 9 June 2017. The Delhi High Court upheld that action on 21 August 2019 in W.P.(C) Nos. 6541/2017 and 6543/2017, finding misrepresentation and suppression of material facts. Under Section 193 of the Finance Act, 2016, such a declaration is void and deemed never to have been made under the Scheme. The Tribunal relied on the High Court’s findings and held that the disputed sums, although lying with the Department, could not be treated as tax paid pursuant to valid IDS declarations. Adjustment letters and beneficial arrangement: The Tribunal examined the letters dated 27 June 2018 and considered the request to adjust Vaibhav Jain’s and Ankush Jain’s deposits against Satyendra Kumar Jain’s tax demands unambiguous. It reasoned that such adjustment could directly or indirectly benefit him by reducing his liabilities. Along with earlier attachment and adjudication findings and the invalidation of the declarations, the letters supported an inference that Satyendra Kumar Jain had provided the consideration. The Tribunal found the elements of Section 2(9)(A) satisfied and identified Vaibhav Jain and Ankush Jain as benamidars and Satyendra Kumar Jain as beneficial owner. Retrospective application: The appellants contended that the 2016 amendments to the benami legislation could not apply to earlier transactions. Relying on Manjula & Ors. Vs D.A. Srinivas, the Tribunal distinguished declaratory, procedural, curative and machinery-oriented provisions from penal provisions. It held that the relevant attachment, adjudication and confiscation machinery could operate retrospectively or retroactively, including proceedings under Sections 27 to 29. It expressly clarified that Chapter VII penal provisions creating new offences or enhancing punishment could not be applied retrospectively. The Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA, New Delhi, has upheld the attachment of ₹5,30,10,000 deposited under the Income Declaration Scheme, 2016, holding that the amount constituted benami property after the underlying declarations were declared illegal and void. The Tribunal identified Vaibhav Jain and Ankush Jain as benamidars and Satyendra Kumar Jain as the beneficial owner. It relied on the invalidation of the declarations, earlier benami proceedings and letters seeking adjustment of the deposits against Satyendra Kumar Jain’s personal tax demands. The common order was delivered on 29 October 2026 by Members Balesh Kumar and Rajesh Malhotra, dismissing the three connected appeals against the Adjudicating Authority’s order dated 25 February 2020. Background of the disputed deposits Vaibhav Jain and Ankush Jain made declarations under IDS 2016 concerning undisclosed income associated with investments in share capital and share premium of certain companies. The disputed deposits comprised ₹3,71,25,000 and ₹1,58,85,000, aggregating to ₹5,30,10,000. Separately, income-tax demands of approximately ₹6.92 crore and ₹3.89 crore were raised against Satyendra Kumar Jain for Assessment Years 2011-12 and 2012-13. Through two letters dated 27 June 2018, he requested adjustment of the amounts deposited by Vaibhav Jain and Ankush Jain against those demands and sought protection against recovery of the balance. The Initiating Officer treated the deposits as benami property and provisionally attached them. The Adjudicating Authority subsequently confirmed the attachment. Appellants disputed beneficial ownership Satyendra Kumar Jain argued that his letters had been read selectively. According to him, they expressly denied beneficial ownership and merely sought to prevent taxation of the same income in different persons’ hands. The appellants maintained that Vaibhav Jain and Ankush Jain had declared their own undisclosed income and paid the amounts from their own funds. They submitted that the Department had failed to establish that the money originated from Satyendra Kumar Jain or that the statutory requirements of Section 2(9)(A) of the Prohibition of Benami Property Transactions Act, 1988 were satisfied. They also challenged the adequacy of the inquiries and procedural compliance preceding attachment. Can money deposited as tax remain benami property? A principal objection was that money already deposited with the Government could no longer be property held by the alleged benamidars. The appellants relied on Section 191 of the Finance Act, 2016, which provides that tax, surcharge and penalty paid pursuant to an IDS declaration are not refundable. They argued that the deposits had become government revenue and could neither be treated as privately held benami property nor adjusted against another person’s tax liability. The Tribunal rejected that argument in the specific circumstances of the case. Invalid declarations were central to the decision The Tribunal noted that the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax had declared the IDS declarations illegal and void on 9 June 2017. The Delhi High Court upheld that decision on 21 August 2019, finding suppression and misrepresentation of material facts. The Tribunal referred to Section 193 of the Finance Act, 2016, under which a declaration made through misrepresentation or suppression is void and deemed never to have been made under the Scheme. Consequently, it held that the disputed amounts, although still lying with the Department, could not be treated as tax paid under valid IDS declarations. This finding distinguished the deposits from payments supported by an effective declaration. Adjustment request indicated the alleged arrangement The Tribunal examined the letters dated 27 June 2018 and found the request for adjustment against Satyendra Kumar Jain’s liabilities unambiguous. In its assessment, the arrangement could have conferred a direct or indirect benefit upon him by reducing his outstanding tax demands. The Tribunal further inferred that the consideration had been provided by him, relying on the adjustment request together with the earlier orders and findings concerning the declarations. It therefore concluded that the requirements of Section 2(9)(A) were fulfilled. The appellants’ contrary explanation was not accepted. Retrospective application objection rejected The appellants also contended that the amended benami framework, effective from 1 November 2016, could not apply to earlier transactions. Relying on Manjula & Ors. v. D.A. Srinivas, the Tribunal distinguished between procedural, curative and machinery provisions and penal provisions. It held that the relevant attachment, adjudication and confiscation machinery could apply retrospectively or retroactively. However, new offences or enhanced punishment could not operate retrospectively. The Tribunal expressly preserved that limitation concerning the penal provisions under Chapter VII. Decision and significance The Tribunal dismissed all three appeals and upheld the finding that the attached ₹5.30 crore constituted benami property. Pending applications were also disposed of. The order turns on the void declarations, earlier findings and inferred beneficial arrangement. It should not be read as holding that every IDS deposit, or every request for adjustment of another person’s payment, automatically constitutes a benami transaction. Cases Discussed Manjula & Ors. Vs D.A. Srinivas (Supreme Court): Relied upon on retrospective application of procedural and machinery provisions and prospective operation of penal provisions. Vaibhav Jain and Ankush Jain, W.P.(C) Nos. 6541/2017 and 6543/2017 (Delhi High Court), 21.08.2019: Relied upon for invalidation of IDS declarations for suppression and misrepresentation of material facts. Union of India & Ors. Vs Ganpati Dealcom Pvt. Ltd. (Supreme Court): Department referred to recall of earlier judgment on 18.10.2024.
