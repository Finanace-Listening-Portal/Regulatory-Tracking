Ramesh (Madras High Court) Settlement Benefits Accepted: Madras High Court Upholds Restraint on Communications Against IPO Introduction The Madras High Court, in G. Saravanan v. D. Ramesh and Others, OSA(CAD) No. 122 of 2026, decided on 10 November 2026, upheld an interim injunction restraining communications to regulatory authorities in breach of a mediated settlement. The Court held that a party who had substantially acted upon a settlement and received consideration could not obstruct its implementation merely by filing a suit alleging fraud. However, it expressly directed that the underlying suit be decided independently on its merits, without being influenced by the appellate observations. Background of the Dispute The dispute arose between directors and promoters of NTL India Limited. Saravanan and the other principal parties also held interests in other entities. Their differences were referred to mediation before Justice N. Kirubakaran, a retired Madras High Court judge. The parties executed a Settlement Agreement dated 13 August 2024. The settlement involved transfers of shareholding and other rights. The Court recorded that Saravanan received an aggregate consideration of ₹13 crore, including consideration connected with transferring his shareholding in NTL India. The company subsequently filed a Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) for a proposed listing on the BSE SME platform. Suit and Interim Injunction The plaintiffs alleged that Saravanan had violated the settlement. They sought ₹2.20 crore in damages and a permanent injunction against breaches, including communications concerning the proposed public issue. Pending disposal of the suit, they requested an interim restraint against communications with BSE, the Registrar of Companies, SEBI and other authorities, where those communications contravened settlement obligations. A Single Judge granted the injunction on 30 March 2026. Saravanan challenged that order before the Division Bench under the Commercial Courts Act, 2015, read with the Letters Patent. Appellant Alleged Fraud and Undervaluation Saravanan argued that he had separately challenged the settlement on grounds including fraud and undue influence. He sought restoration of his 33.34% shareholding and retransfer of properties and rights surrendered under the settlement. His principal grievance concerned valuation: according to him, shares valued at ₹50 each were subsequently sold at ₹410 each. Although he acknowledged receiving ₹13 crore, he contended that he had also transferred valuable immovable properties. He therefore disputed the suggestion that he had obtained an advantage. He further argued that an IPO would create third-party rights, potentially prejudicing his claims. Allegations of Fraud Required Evidence The Division Bench noted that the settlement emerged from mediation involving several hearings and had been substantially implemented. Whether it was induced by fraud or undue influence required proper evidence. Those allegations could not be treated as established merely because a suit had been instituted. Having acted upon the arrangement and received consideration, Saravanan could not prevent the other parties from enjoying its benefits solely by challenging it. The Court held that the settlement remained binding unless the alleged fraud was established in accordance with law. This reasoning concerned the interim position; it did not finally determine whether the settlement was legally unimpeachable. Express Valuation Clause Was Significant The Court placed particular emphasis on Saravanan’s agreement not to raise claims concerning the settlement consideration being low or reduced when compared with market value. Having expressly accepted that term, he could not rely on the valuation grievance to prevent the other parties from proceeding with the proposed IPO. The judgment therefore highlights the significance of specific valuation acknowledgments and waivers in negotiated commercial settlements. A later dispute over consideration must be examined alongside the obligations expressly accepted, rather than in isolation from the settlement. Section 28 Argument Was Rejected Saravanan relied on Section 28 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, arguing against the restriction on approaching authorities. The High Court held that the provision could not assist him in the circumstances. It distinguished an agreement restraining pursuit of legal remedies from his attempt, after accepting settlement consideration, to obstruct the other parties’ public-issue process. The Court also found that his conduct did not satisfy the requirement of clean hands. This conclusion should be understood within the particular settlement and injunction before the Court. The judgment did not announce a general prohibition against regulatory complaints or decide that every contractual restriction on communications is enforceable. Appeal Dismissed, Suit Remained Open The Division Bench found no infirmity in the interim order and dismissed the appeal without costs. The connected miscellaneous petition was closed. Crucially, the Court clarified that the suit must be decided on its own merits, uninfluenced by the observations in its judgment. Accordingly, the decision neither finally rejected the fraud allegations nor constituted regulatory approval of the IPO. It maintained the interim restraint against communications contrary to the settlement obligations. Practical Implications The ruling underscores the importance of settlement finality, consistent conduct and carefully drafted valuation clauses. Parties challenging an implemented settlement should substantiate fraud or undue influence through evidence. Merely instituting proceedings does not automatically suspend contractual obligations or justify interference with the other parties’ commercial steps. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT OF MADRAS HIGH COURT Challenging the order made by the learned Single Judge of this Court in O.A.No.127 of 2026 in C.S.(Comm.Div.) No.49 of 2026, dated 30.03.2026, restraining the defendants in the said suit from communicating with the BSE, RoC, SEBI or other authorities in contravention of obligations undertaken under the Settlement Agreement until the disposal of the suit, the 1st defendant has filed the present Appeal. 2. For the sake of convenience, the parties will be referred to as per their rank in the suit. 3. Following are the brief facts of the case : The plaintiffs 1 and 2 and the 1st defendant were Directors and promoters of the 3rd plaintiff Company. They were also shareholders of other entities. In view of certain disputes that arose between them, mediation proceedings were conducted before the Hon’ble Mr. Justice N.Kirubakaran, a Retired Judge of this Court, wherein, the parties arrived at a settlement and a Memorandum of Settlement dated 13.08.2024 (hereinafter referred as “Settlement Agreement”) came to be executed between the parties. 4. Thereafter, it appears that the 3rd plaintiff Company has filed a Draft Red-Herring Prospectus (DRHP) in relation to a proposed Initial Public Offer (IPO) in the SME Board of the Bombay Stock Exchange. When that process is pending before the concerned authorities, the present suit came to be filed by the plaintiffs seeking damages and for other reliefs as under : “(a) direct defendant No.1 to pay a sum of Rs.2,20,00,000/- as damages to plaintiff Nos.1 and 2 for gross violation of the Settlement Agreement dated 13.08.2024. (b) grant a permanent injunction restraining the defendants from committing any breach of the settlement agreement dated 13.08.2024 settling all the disputes as evidenced in the mediation report dated 21.11.2025 submitted by Hon’ble Justice N.Kirubakaran (retired) either by communicating to the Stock Exchanges, ROC or other authorities concerning the steps taken by plaintiff No.3 for Initial Public Offer through the Stock Exchanges or committing any breach thereof either in writing, orally, or any other mode of communication.” 5. In the said suit, the present application has been taken out by the plaintiffs in O.A.No.127 of 2026 to restrain the defendants from communicating with the BSE, RoC, SEBI or other authorities in contravention of obligations undertaken under the Settlement Agreement, until the disposal of the suit. 6. Learned Single Judge of this Court, after considering the submissions of the learned counsel on either side, allowed the said application by impugned order dated 30.03.2026. 7. Challenging the said order of the learned Single Judge, the present Appeal has been filed. 8. Learned Senior Counsel appearing for the appellant would submit that the appellant has filed a suit in C.S.No.49 of 2026 challenging the very Settlement Agreement, dated 13.08.2024, to declare it as null and void; to restore the appellant’s shareholding of 33.34% in the Company and re-transfer all properties and rights parted under the Settlement Agreement; and for permanent injunction, on the ground of fraud. According to him, when the very Settlement Agreement is under challenge in the suit filed by the appellant, the learned Single Judge ought not to have restrained the appellant from approaching the authorities. According to him, the shares have been valued at Rs.50/- per share and later, the same have been sold at Rs.410/- per share. Hence, according to him, the entire agreement is vitiated on the ground of fraud. He would further submit that, though the appellant has received a sum of Rs.13 Crores, he has already transferred several valuable immovable properties. Such being the position, it is his contention that the agreement reached between the parties itself is a result of fraud. Therefore, according to him, if permission is granted to go for public issue, third party rights will be created and the appellant’s right will be affected. Therefore, it is his contention that the appellant cannot be restrained from addressing before the concerned authorities in view of Section 28 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. 9. Whereas, learned counsel appearing for the respondents 1 to 3 would submit that the very settlement has been reached by way of mediation in the presence of a retired Judge of this Court, after several hearings. According to him, having taken advantage of the settlement, now, for some reason or the other, the appellant is preventing the respondents from public issue, with an ulterior motive. Therefore, he would submit that the learned Single Judge has rightly restrained the appellant and thus, prays for dismissal of this Appeal. 10. In the light of the above submissions, the only point that arises for consideration in this Appeal is whether the learned Single Judge is right in restraining the appellant from communicating with BSE, RoC, SEBI or other authorities ? 11. We have perused the entire materials available on record. 12. It is not in dispute that, in respect of the disputes that arose in the Company in which the appellant and the respondents 1 and 2 were Directors, a Settlement Agreement, dated 13.08.2024, came to be executed in mediation proceedings in the presence of a retired Judge of this Court, Hon’ble Mr. Justice N. Kirubakaran. The Minutes of the proceedings held between May 2024 and 06.10.2025 were also taken note of by the learned Single Judge. The appellant has received an aggregate sum of Rs.13 Crores as consideration of the Settlement Agreement inter alia in relation to the transfer of his shareholding in the 3rd plaintiff to the other plaintiffs. Though it is now submitted by the appellant that he has also transferred the immovable properties worth about Rs.13 Crores and according to him, there is no benefit received, the fact remains that the settlement has been acted upon. Once the mediation proceedings have been conclusively established and certain benefits have been taken by the appellant, now, it appears that the appellant has filed a suit challenging the Settlement Agreement on the ground of fraud, undue influence, etc. Whether such settlement is induced by fraud or undue influence, etc., is a matter of evidence and it has to be tested only by way of proper evidence. Once parties to the settlement have taken advantage and acted upon substantially on the basis of the settlement, now, the appellant cannot turn around by merely filing a suit challenging the settlement and prevent the other shareholders or other Directors from enjoying the fruits of the settlement and to go for public issue also. 13. Though much emphasis has been made on Section 28 of the Contract Act, we are of the view that Section 28 cannot be pressed into service in the present case. Section 28 can be invoked only when there is an agreement between the parties restraining the other from pursuing any legal remedy. Whereas, in the case on hand, having taken advantage of the mediation agreement and received the consideration, the appellant is now trying to prevent the other parties to the agreement from processing further by way of public issue, which itself indicates that the appellant has not come to the Court with clean hands. It is also to be noted that, in the Settlement Agreement, the appellant has clearly agreed that he will not make any claim with regard to the consideration arrived at in the settlement, whether it is low or reduced when compared to the market value. Having specifically agreed to such term, the appellant cannot prevent the other parties to the settlement from going for public issue (IPO). In any event, as long as the alleged fraud is not established in the manner known to law, the settlement is binding on the parties. Hence, we do not find any infirmity in the order of the learned Single Judge. 14. Accordingly, this Original Side Appeal is dismissed. No costs. Consequently, connected miscellaneous petition is closed. It is made clear that the suit has to be decided on its own merits without being influenced by any of the observations made by us in this judgment.
