ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Published On Sep 14, 2026 at 11:57 PM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 134246620; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The government on Monday notified that banks and system providers cannot impose any direct or indirect charge on Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions of up to Rs 2,000 and payments made using RuPay-powered debit cards.In a gazette notification issued on September 14, the Ministry of Finance, exercising powers under the amended Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, specified the two electronic modes of payment that will remain protected from charges.The notification covers debit cards powered by RuPay and Unified Payments Interface transactions up to Rs 2,000.It further said, “No bank or system provider shall impose, whether directly or indirectly, any charge upon a person making or receiving a payment by using the electronic modes of payment specified above.” Advt The notification comes after weeks of debate over whether the government was preparing to bring back merchant discount rate (MDR) on UPI transactions after keeping the payment mode under a zero-MDR regime since January 2020.The debate gathered pace last month after amendments to the Payment and Settlement Systems Act changed the earlier blanket statutory protection against charges on specified digital payment modes. The amended provision empowers the Central government to specify, through notification, the electronic payment modes or transactions on which banks and system providers cannot levy charges.The latest notification is the first such specification under the amended framework and formally protects UPI transactions up to Rs 2,000 and RuPay debit card payments from any direct or indirect charges. The notification, however, does not prescribe any MDR or charge for UPI transactions above Rs 2,000.Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman had earlier sought to allay concerns around UPI charges, saying that consumers would not be made to pay for everyday UPI transactions and that any future MDR, if introduced, would apply only to a limited set of merchant transactions. The Finance Ministry had also said that any future MDR would be nominal and would not apply to everyday transactions undertaken by citizens. Advt The government had subsequently clarified that small merchants would also remain outside the scope of any such charges, while stressing that the legislative amendment was an enabling provision and did not, by itself, impose MDR on UPI.
