Summary: The Supreme Court, in State of Bihar vs Phulpari Kumari, decided on 2 November 2026, clarified that the permissible limits for retaining gold jewellery under CBDT Instruction No. 1916 dated 11 May 1994 cannot be treated as proof of its lawful acquisition or explained source. The case arose from a disproportionate assets investigation against a public servant, where the Patna High Court had quashed the proceedings by relying on the CBDT instruction and the Finance Ministry’s clarification dated 1 December 2016. The Supreme Court rejected this reasoning, holding that guidelines restricting seizure of jewellery during income-tax searches do not establish the source of funds used to acquire it. The matter was consequently restored for trial, leaving other defences open to the accused. The CBDT guidelines ordinarily protect jewellery weighing up to 500 grams for a married woman, 250 grams for an unmarried woman and 100 grams for a male family member from seizure during a search, but these limits do not grant immunity from scrutiny or taxation. The discussion further explains the tax treatment of jewellery received from relatives, on marriage or through inheritance under Section 56(2)(x), the capital gains implications of selling gold and the importance of maintaining purchase invoices, bank payment records, gift documentation, inheritance evidence and valuation reports. The central lesson is that possession within the prescribed jewellery limits does not eliminate the taxpayer’s responsibility to explain the source of acquisition when legally required. Supreme Court Ruling: Why the 500-Gram Gold Limit Is Not Enough Arjuna (Fictional Character): Krishna, what was the case about? Krishna (Fictional Character): Arjuna, in 2015 a disproportionate assets case was registered against a public servant in Bihar. She approached the Patna High Court, relying on CBDT Instruction No. 1916 dated 11th May 1994 and the Finance Ministry Press Release dated 1st December 2016, which allow a married woman to hold 500 grams of gold. The High Court agreed and quashed the case. But the Supreme Court called this reasoning totally untenable. It held that showing a circular does not amount to explaining how the gold was acquired, and sent the case back for trial. The accused can still raise other defences, but the “500 gram shield” is gone. CBDT Instruction No. 1916: Gold Seizure Limits Explained Arjuna (Fictional Character): Krishna, then what do these circulars really say? Krishna (Fictional Character): Arjuna, they are only guidelines for officers conducting a search under Section 132 of the Income Tax Act. During a search, gold up to 500 grams per married woman, 250 grams per unmarried woman and 100 grams per male member is ordinarily not seized. The 2016 Press Release further clarified that jewellery bought from disclosed income, exempt income such as agricultural income, reasonable household savings or inheritance is not taxable, whatever the quantity. So the limit protects gold from seizure, not from questions. Income Tax on Gifted, Inherited and Sold Gold Jewellery Arjuna (Fictional Character): Krishna, what about gold received as gifts or inherited, and gold sold later? Krishna (Fictional Character): Arjuna, under Section 56(2)(x), gold received from relatives, on the occasion of marriage, or through inheritance is fully exempt. But gold from a non-relative on any other occasion becomes taxable if its value exceeds Rs. 50,000 in a year. When jewellery is sold after 24 months, the gain is taxed as long term capital gain at 12.5%; within 24 months, it is taxed at slab rates. In both situations, the bill or valuation report decides how smoothly the taxpayer can prove the cost and source. Documents Needed to Prove the Source of Gold Jewellery Arjuna (Fictional Character): Krishna, so what should a taxpayer keep ready? Krishna (Fictional Character): Arjuna, every gram needs its paper trail: jeweller’s invoices with bank payment proof, gift letters and wedding photographs for wedding gold, and a will or valuation report for inherited jewellery. Taxpayers with income above Rs. 50 lakh must also report jewellery in Schedule AL of the ITR. Take Mrs. A, who holds 400 grams, received at her wedding and bought from her salary, with gift letters and invoices in place. She can face any officer calmly. Mrs. B holds the same gold with no papers and simply says “the circular allows it.” After this judgment, that answer will not work. Conclusion: Gold Seizure Protection Is Not Proof of Lawful Acquisition Arjuna (Fictional Character): Krishna, what is the lesson to remember? Krishna (Fictional Character): Arjuna, a circular may keep gold safe in the locker, but only proof keeps the taxpayer safe in the file. So this wedding season, buy gold with joy, but collect the bill with equal care. In the eyes of the law, it is not the weight of the gold that counts, but the weight of the proof behind it!
