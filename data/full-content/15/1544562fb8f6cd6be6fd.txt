Articles 5 min read OFS dominates IPO fundraising as shareholders cash out ₹48,260 crore across 15 financial-services listings An analysis by Tracxn of 15 IPOs between 2023 and September 2026 shows that 11 combined fresh capital with offers for sale, while just two were entirely fresh issues; NSDL and ICICI Prudential AMC were entirely OFS offerings. Of the ₹70,470 crore raised through these IPOs, approximately ₹48,260 crore came from OFS, while fresh issues accounted for ₹22,209 crore. In other words, existing shareholders sold shares worth more than twice the amount of fresh capital raised by the companies.The distribution of IPO structures reinforces the trend. Of the 15 offerings, 11 combined a fresh issue with an OFS, two were entirely OFS, and only two raised capital exclusively through fresh issues. Advt The findings suggest that IPOs have increasingly served a dual purpose: providing companies with growth capital while allowing promoters and private-equity investors to monetise their holdings. However, the distinction is important because money raised through an OFS goes to the selling shareholders, not to the company whose shares are being listed.The dataset covers core fintech businesses as well as adjacent financial-services companies, including non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), housing finance companies and asset managers.StructureNo. of IPOsof which core fintechof which adjacentTotal issue (INR cr)Fresh capital (INR cr)OFS size (INR cr)OFS % of issueLargest issue (INR cr)Avg. issue (INR cr)Pure OFS (100% OFS)21114,614.3-14,614.3100.0%10,602.77,307.1Fresh issue only (0% OFS)2111,317.01,317.0-0.0%745.0658.5Mixed (fresh + OFS)115654,538.420,892.233,646.261.7%15,511.94,958.0Total157870,469.622,209.248,260.468.5%15,511.94,698.0Mixed issues dominate, with OFS accounting for ₹33,646 croreMixed offerings were the most common structure, accounting for 11 of the 15 IPOs and a combined issue size of approximately ₹54,538 crore. Of this, ₹33,646 crore was attributable to OFS, while ₹20,892 crore came from fresh issues.OFS accounted for about 61.7% of the total size of these mixed offerings, with fresh capital contributing the remaining 38.3%.Several large transactions illustrate the trend. Groww’s ₹6,632 crore IPO in November 2025 included ₹5,572 crore of OFS, accounting for about 84% of the issue. The company raised ₹1,060 crore through fresh shares.HDB Financial Services’ ₹12,500 crore IPO in July 2025 comprised a ₹10,000 crore OFS by promoter HDFC Bank and a ₹2,500 crore fresh issue. OFS therefore accounted for 80% of the offering. Advt Tata Capital’s ₹15,512 crore IPO in October 2025 comprised approximately ₹8,666 crore of OFS and ₹6,846 crore of fresh capital. The secondary component accounted for around 56% of the issue.The pattern was also visible in earlier offerings. Go Digit General Insurance raised ₹2,615 crore through a combination of ₹1,125 crore in fresh capital and approximately ₹1,490 crore in OFS in May 2024. Pine Labs’ ₹3,900 crore IPO in November 2025 comprised ₹2,080 crore of fresh capital and ₹1,820 crore of OFS.While the proportion varies across companies, the common feature is that fresh capital and investor exits are being accommodated within the same public offering. Advt Pure OFS offerings account for ₹14,614 crore; fresh-only issues remain limitedThe dataset records two entirely OFS-based offerings, together worth approximately ₹14,614 crore. Neither company received fresh capital through these transactions.ICICI Prudential Asset Management Company accounted for the larger share, with an issue size of approximately ₹10,603 crore. Its December 2025 IPO was entirely an OFS by Prudential Corporation Holdings.The other was the ₹4,011 crore IPO of National Securities Depository Ltd (NSDL) in August 2025. The offering comprised shares sold by existing shareholders, including NSE, SBI, IDBI Bank, Union Bank of India, HDFC Bank and SUUTI. NSDL did not receive any proceeds from the sale of these shares. Advt At the other end of the spectrum, only two offerings in the dataset were entirely fresh issues: One MobiKwik Systems and Anand Rathi Share and Stock Brokers.MobiKwik’s ₹572 crore IPO in December 2024 was entirely fresh capital, with no OFS component. The company had initially proposed a larger ₹700 crore issue in its draft prospectus.Anand Rathi Share and Stock Brokers raised ₹745 crore through a fresh issue in September 2025, without an OFS component.Together, the two fresh-only offerings raised ₹1,317 crore. That represents less than 2% of the total ₹70,470 crore issue value across the dataset.The comparison highlighted how uncommon a completely primary offering has been in this sample. Most issuers have either provided an exit route to existing shareholders or combined such exits with capital raising for the business.OFS activity accelerates in 2025 as large financial-services listings drive fundraisingThe annual distribution shows that 2025 was the biggest year for OFS proceeds in the dataset.The two entirely OFS-based offerings, NSDL and ICICI Prudential AMC, together accounted for ₹14,614 crore. Mixed offerings added another ₹26,058 crore of OFS, taking total OFS proceeds for the year to approximately ₹40,672 crore.Fresh capital raised through the financial-services IPOs in the dataset during 2025 amounted to about ₹13,231 crore. OFS proceeds were therefore more than three times the fresh capital raised during the year.In 2024, OFS proceeds from mixed offerings stood at approximately ₹6,766 crore, marginally exceeding the ₹6,757 crore raised through fresh issues. In 2023, the two mixed offerings recorded in the dataset, Muthoot Microfin and India Shelter Finance, included ₹600 crore of OFS against ₹1,560 crore of fresh capital.The 2026 figures through September reflect a smaller sample, with Turtlemint Fintech Solutions’ proposed ₹883 crore offering comprising ₹661 crore of fresh capital and ₹222 crore of OFS. The final issue price and listing outcome should be confirmed before using the transaction as a completed IPO.The larger trend is clear: mixed offerings have become the predominant structure in the sample, while large OFS-led transactions have contributed substantially to the overall value of fundraising.For companies, the share of fresh capital determines how much money is available to strengthen the balance sheet, fund lending or expand operations. For investors, an OFS provides a route to realise gains or reduce exposure without the company issuing additional shares for that portion of the offering.The distinction is particularly relevant for fintech and new-age financial businesses, where early-stage investors and private-equity funds may have held stakes for several years before a public listing. A large IPO, therefore, does not necessarily translate into an equally large infusion of capital into the underlying business.
