(Calcutta High Court) Summary: The Calcutta High Court, in Nithil Agarwal Vs State of West Bengal & Ors., set aside an ex parte GST adjudication order dated 31 May 2023 and a consequential recovery notice dated 14 October 2025, holding that uploading a show cause notice only under the “Additional Notice and Orders” tab of the GST portal, without separate intimation to the taxpayer, constituted a violation of the principles of natural justice. The petitioner had challenged the adjudication order passed under Section 73 of the West Bengal Goods and Services Tax Act and the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. A show cause notice in Form GST DRC-01 dated 10 August 2022 had demanded tax, interest and penalty. The petitioner submitted that the notice was uploaded under the additional notices tab, resulting in the absence of actual intimation and preventing him from filing a reply. He contended that the proceedings and the resulting order were contrary to the statutory procedure and principles of natural justice. The State opposed the petition, maintaining that the petitioner had been granted sufficient opportunities to defend his case. After examining the available records, the Court found that the petitioner had established a prima facie case. It specifically observed that the notice had been uploaded only under the additional notices tab and that no separate intimation had been given, leaving the petitioner unable to respond. The Court consequently quashed the adjudication order and recovery notice and directed the petitioner to submit a reply to the original show cause notice within two weeks. The authority was directed to consider the reply within four weeks of its receipt, pass a fresh reasoned and speaking order after providing an opportunity of hearing, and communicate the decision within a further week. The writ petition was disposed of without determining the underlying tax liability. By a subsequent order dated 13 October 2026, the Court corrected a typographical error in paragraph 4 of its original order dated 6 October 2026 by deleting the words “the appeal has been rightly dismissed on limitation”. The remaining portion of the original order was expressly left unchanged, and the correction order was directed to be treated as part of the original order. Appellant Was Represented by: Mr. Himangshu Kumar Ray, Mr. Saptak Sanyal, Mr. Subhasis [‘odder, Ms. S.Shaw. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CALCUTTA HIGH COURT 1. Affidavit of service filed in Court today be kept with the record. 2. The present petition has been filed challenging inter alia, the legality and validity of the ex parte adjudication order dated 31.05.2023 passed by the respondent No.4 passed under Section 73 of the West Bengal Goods and Services Tax Act(hereinafter referred to as the said ‘WBGST Act’) and the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017(hereinafter referred to as the said ‘CGST Act’). 3. Learned counsel for the petitioner submits as follows; i. That a show cause notice in Form DRC 01 dated 10.08.2022 has been issued to the petitioner demanding tax, interest and penalty. ii. That said notice has been uploaded on the GST portal under the tab ‘Additional Notice and Orders’. Due to this, the petitioner did not get any actual intimation and hence could not respond to the show cause notice. iii. That the entire proceedings and the impugned order are contrary to law and have been passed in violation of principles of natural justice and without due compliance with the procedure prescribed under the said Act. 4. Learned counsel appearing for the State respondents opposed the writ petition and submitted since the petitioner has been granted ample opportunities to defend his case, the appeal has been rightly dismissed on limitation. 5. Having heard the parties and upon perusing the records made available this Court observes as follows; i. The petitioner has been able to make a prima facie case. ii. The fact that show cause notice has been only uploaded under the ‘tab Additional Notice and Orders’ and no separate intimation has been given therefore the petitioner has been unable to reply to the same. This constitutes a violation of natural justice. 6. In view of the above this Court directs as follows: a. The adjudication order dated 31.05.2023 and consequential recovery notice dated 14.10.2025 are hereby quashed and set aside. b. The petitioner is directed to file a reply to the show cause notice dated 10.08.2022 before the concerned authority within two weeks from date. Such reply shall be considered within four weeks from the date of receipt of the reply by passing a fresh reasoned and speaking order in accordance with law upon affording an opportunity of hearing to the petitioner. Such decision shall be communicated to the petitioner within a week thereafter. 7. With the above observations and directions this writ petition stands disposed of. 8. Since no affidavit in opposition has been called for, the allegations contained in the writ petition are deemed to have been denied and not admitted. 9. Urgent Photostat certified copy of this order be supplied to the parties, if applied for, as early as possible. 1. The matter has come up for correction of the order dated 6.10.2026. A typographical error crept in the said order. In the fourth para of the said order the words ‘the appeal has been rightly dismissed on limitation’ be deleted. 2. Remaining part of the order dated 6.10.2026 shall remain same. 3. This order be treated as a part of the order dated 6.10.2026 4. Department to take necessary steps.
