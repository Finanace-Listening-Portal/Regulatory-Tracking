Summary: Term life insurance is intended primarily to provide a financial benefit to beneficiaries if the life insured dies during the policy term, subject to the policy conditions and applicable law. A low quoted premium is not sufficient reason to select a plan. Buyers should estimate the family’s income-replacement requirement, debts, education costs and available assets, and then compare the resulting cover requirement with affordable premiums and a suitable policy duration. Complete and accurate disclosures concerning health, habits, occupation, income and other insurance are critical. Policy documents should be examined for exclusions, waiting periods where applicable, premium-payment obligations, riders, nomination and claims procedures. This article explains practical selection criteria, an illustrative cover calculation, common purchasing errors and steps families can take to keep the policy effective and make future claims easier. Key Takeaways for Policy Buyers Show all 22 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); What Term Life Insurance Does—and Does Not—Cover A conventional pure term policy promises a specified death benefit when the insured dies during the covered term, provided the contractual and legal requirements are satisfied. It generally does not build an investment corpus or provide a maturity payout if the insured survives, unless the chosen product expressly includes a return-of-premium or other feature. Such variants may cost more than comparable pure-risk cover. Compare the actual benefit schedule, rather than assuming every policy sold as “term insurance” has identical features. Term insurance addresses a financial protection need, not an investment-return target. It can help surviving family members meet routine expenditure, loan repayments and planned future obligations, but it cannot prevent every financial difficulty. Coverage should be reviewed alongside emergency savings, health insurance and the family’s overall financial position. Calculate an Appropriate Sum Assured Begin with the family’s future expenditure rather than a multiple of salary alone. Estimate essential annual spending, the period for which dependants need support, education and other major goals, outstanding loans, existing savings and other reliable sources of income. The approach is consistent with the broader principle of assessing dependants’ needs, liabilities and financial assets before buying. Illustrative Family Protection Calculation Suppose a household estimates ₹12 lakh a year of essential family expenditure for the next 15 years, ₹35 lakh in outstanding loans and ₹25 lakh for education and other future goals. A simple undiscounted calculation is ₹12 lakh × 15 + ₹35 lakh + ₹25 lakh = ₹2.40 crore. If ₹40 lakh of existing liquid assets is earmarked for these needs, the illustrative shortfall is ₹2 crore. This is only a starting point: inflation, investment returns, changing family expenses, taxes, insurance already in force and the timing of cash flows can materially alter the requirement. Do not automatically subtract retirement assets or an emergency reserve that cannot prudently be used by dependants. Similarly, do not assume that a jointly held loan will disappear on death. Review contractual loan obligations and the family’s ability to service them. The final sum assured should be financially sustainable, because an unaffordable policy that lapses may fail precisely when protection is needed. Choose Policy Duration and Premium-Payment Structure The cover period should reflect the years in which dependants rely on the insured’s earnings and the time needed to discharge significant liabilities. A very short term can leave a family exposed after expiry; a very long term may increase cost without adding proportionate protection. Reassess when children become financially independent, loans are repaid or household responsibilities change. Regular Pay, Limited Pay and Single Premium Regular-pay policies ordinarily require premiums throughout the selected payment period. Limited-pay policies collect premiums over fewer years, while single-premium products require an upfront payment. Compare the full cash outflow, not merely the instalment amount. A limited-pay quote can look attractive after the payment period ends, but earlier instalments may be substantially higher. Consider cash-flow resilience, the insurer’s grace-period and revival provisions, and the consequences of missed payments. Make Complete and Accurate Proposal Disclosures Answer all proposal questions truthfully and completely, including those concerning past or present medical conditions, medication, investigations, smoking or tobacco use, alcohol habits, hazardous activities, occupation, income and other insurance. The importance of material disclosure in insurance proposals cannot be overstated. Do not rely on an agent to guess answers or omit facts on the assumption that the insurer will discover them independently. Read the completed proposal before signing or digitally accepting it, and retain the proposal copy, declarations, medical reports and communications. Where a question is unclear, obtain clarification in writing. A telemedical interview or simplified application does not by itself eliminate the duty to answer accurately. Claims and the insurer’s ability to question a policy must be evaluated under the applicable policy terms and law, including the relevant provisions of the Insurance Act, 1938. Compare Exclusions, Riders and Product Conditions Review the actual policy wording, benefit illustration where relevant and customer information sheet. The IRDAI Insurance Products Regulations, 2024 provide regulatory context, but the individual contract still determines the benefits purchased. Check the precise wording of exclusions, the suicide-related provision, commencement and expiry dates, conditions for additional benefits, and any premium waiver. Evaluate Riders Separately Accidental death, critical illness, disability-related and waiver-of-premium riders may address different risks. Their triggers, definitions, benefit limits, survival requirements, exclusions and claim evidence can differ substantially. A critical illness rider is not necessarily a substitute for comprehensive health insurance, and an accidental death rider does not necessarily enhance every type of death claim. Buy a rider only where its added benefit justifies its price and overlaps are understood. Compare Insurers Beyond the Lowest Quoted Premium Compare equivalent age, health status, sum assured, policy term, payment frequency and riders across insurers. Examine the scope and time period of published claims metrics, service quality, complaint handling, policy servicing channels and financial disclosures. A claim settlement ratio is a useful indicator but is not a promise that an individual claim will be paid. A ratio based on claim counts may tell a different story from one based on claim amounts. Confirm that the policy is issued by the intended insurer and that any intermediary is appropriately authorised. Verify the final premium after underwriting, because an initial online quote may change following medical or financial assessment. Compare taxes and any additional charges reflected in the payable amount, and avoid judging plans solely by promotional discounts. Nomination and Family Claim Readiness Provide accurate nominee details and update them after relevant family changes. The applicable legal effect of nomination, including beneficial nominee provisions, should be considered under Section 39 of the Insurance Act, 1938 and the circumstances of the policy. The IRDAI policyholder protection framework is also relevant to insurer servicing and policyholder information. Tell trusted family members that the policy exists, who the insurer is, the policy number, sum assured, renewal method and where documents can be found. Keep the policy schedule, proposal copy, premium receipts, nomination acknowledgement, identification details and claims contact information accessible. Do not share passwords or sensitive credentials insecurely. After a death, the claimant should notify the insurer promptly and follow its documented requirements; additional documents may be requested depending on the circumstances. Tax Treatment Should Not Drive the Cover Decision Tax deductions for eligible life insurance premiums and exemption of qualifying policy proceeds depend on the law applicable to the relevant year, the tax regime, statutory conditions and the nature of the payment. Section 80C and Section 10(10D) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 may be relevant for periods governed by that Act, subject to applicable conditions and exceptions. For FY 2026–27, verify the corresponding provisions and transitional rules under the law in force before claiming a benefit. Do not assume every premium is deductible or that every receipt is exempt. Common Term Insurance Purchasing Mistakes Choosing the cheapest quote without comparing policy terms and benefit definitions. Using a fixed salary multiple without examining debts, dependants and available assets. Withholding health or tobacco information or allowing an intermediary to complete the proposal inaccurately. Assuming every rider pays on every disability, illness or death. Choosing a premium schedule that may be difficult to maintain. Failing to review nomination details and leaving family members unaware of the policy. Assuming a claim settlement statistic guarantees the outcome of a particular claim. Frequently Asked Questions About Term Insurance Is term insurance the same as an investment plan? No. Pure term insurance primarily provides a death benefit during the policy term; it ordinarily has no maturity value. Products with return-of-premium features should be compared separately. How much life cover should a salaried person buy? There is no universally correct salary multiple. Assess dependants’ expenditure, loan obligations, future goals, existing assets and affordability, and model inflation and timing. Can a smoker purchase term insurance? A smoker may be eligible subject to underwriting and the insurer’s terms. Tobacco use must be disclosed accurately, even if it results in a different premium. Does a medical test guarantee claim payment? No. Medical testing is part of underwriting and does not override policy conditions, truthful disclosure requirements or applicable law. Are term insurance riders compulsory? No. Select riders based on their specific benefits, definitions, exclusions, price and whether other insurance already addresses the risk. Can a nominee automatically receive every claim? A nominee’s rights and the insurer’s payment obligations depend on applicable law, policy terms, valid nomination and claim circumstances. Nomination should be checked and kept current. What happens if a premium is missed? Grace periods, lapse and revival rules depend on the policy and applicable regulations. Check the schedule promptly and contact the insurer rather than assuming cover continues. Should I buy solely because of tax savings? No. The primary purpose is family protection. Tax consequences depend on the law, regime and policy conditions applicable to the relevant financial year. Key Takeaways for Policy Buyers Choose term insurance by first measuring the family’s protection gap, then comparing the exact death benefit, duration, premium affordability, exclusions, underwriting requirements, riders and servicing standards. Complete proposal disclosures personally and accurately. Retain documents, maintain premium payments and keep nominees informed.
