Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Devi Steel Traders Vs State of Telangana (Telangana High Court) Telangana High Court Grants Liberty to GST Taxpayer to File Appeal Against Recovery Order Under Section 79 Summary: The Telangana High Court examined a writ petition challenging a recovery order dated 26 September 2026 passed under Section 79 of the Telangana Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, concerning Assessment Year 2017-18. The petitioner contended that the show cause notice had not been served and that the recovery order came to its knowledge only after receiving information from the bank authorities. It further submitted that the impugned order had merely been uploaded under the additional notices tab of the GST portal. The Revenue opposed the writ petition on the ground of delay, relying on the Supreme Court decision in Assistant Commissioner (CT) LTU, Kakinada v. Glaxo Smith Kline Consumer Health Care Limited. The petitioner subsequently sought liberty to file a statutory appeal along with a delay condonation application under Sections 107(1) and 107(4). The High Court declined to examine the merits of the dispute and permitted the petitioner to approach the appellate authority within two weeks, accompanied by the delay condonation application and statutory pre-deposit. The petitioner was permitted to raise all available factual and legal grounds. The Court clarified that the appellate authority would consider the reasons for delay and, if satisfied, decide the appeal on merits. The writ petition was disposed of without costs. The High Court neither quashed the recovery order nor expressly condoned the delay. Cases Discussed: – Assistant Commissioner (CT) LTU, Kakinada & Ors. Vs Glaxo Smith Kline Consumer Health Care Limited (Supreme Court), (2020) 19 SCC 681. – Relied upon by the Revenue while opposing the writ petition on the ground of delay and availability of statutory appellate remedy. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF TELANGANA HIGH COURT Sri Lingala Sudheer, learned counsel for the petitioner. Sri Swaroop Oorilla, learned Special Government Pleader for State Tax appears for respondent Nos.1 to 3. 2. In the present case, the order dated 26.09.2026 passed under Section 79 of the Telangana Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, and Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (for short ‘the Act’), are under challenge. The instant Writ Petition has been filed on 27.10.2026. The petitioner has taken a plea that he came to know of the impugned order only after the bank authorities have informed him. The matter relates to Assessment Year 2017-18. Therefore, the matter is not belated. This Court may entertain the Writ Petition on the ground that the petitioner was not served with the show cause notice and not aware of the proceedings initiated by the department. Moreover, the impugned order was merely uploaded on the additional notices tab of GSTIN Portal. As such, it was not in the notice of the petitioner. 3. Learned counsel for the respondents has opposed the prayer at the outset on the ground of huge delay in preferring this Writ Petition. He has relied upon the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Assistant Commissioner (CT) LTU, Kakinada, v. Glaxo Smith Kline 1 Consumer Health Care Limited. 4. Learned counsel for the petitioner therefore seeks liberty to the petitioner to prefer an appeal with a delay condonation application in terms of Section 107(1) read with Section 107(4) of the Act. He submits that the appellate authority may be directed to consider the question of delay sympathetically in view of the reasons explained in the delay condonation application. 5. Having regard to the facts and circumstances as noted above, this Court is not inclined to enter into the merits of the issue as the petitioner is being allowed liberty to approach the appellate authority. He may approach the appellate authority within a period of two (2) weeks with a delay condonation application and statutory pre-deposit. It is open for the petitioner to take all such grounds in law and on facts in the appeal. Needless to say, the appellate authority would consider the question of delay and if he is satisfied with the reasons explained in the delay condonation application, he shall decide the case on merits. 6. The instant Writ Petition is disposed of accordingly. There shall be no order as to costs. Miscellaneous applications pending, if any, shall stand closed.
