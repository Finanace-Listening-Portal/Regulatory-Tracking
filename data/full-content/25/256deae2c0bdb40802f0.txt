Brick Field (GSTAT Agra) Summary: The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), Agra Bench, comprising Judicial Member Ajeet Singh and Technical Member Vivek Kumar, dismissed four appeals filed by Jitendra Kumar, Assistant Commissioner, Special Investigation Branch, State Tax, Etawah, against appellate orders cancelling confiscation-related penalties and fines imposed on four brick-field operators. The appeals involved M/S R.V. Brick Field, M/S Kisan Ent Bhatta, M/S Shri Shiv Sakti EIT Bhatta and M/S Gatimaan Brickfield. During inspections conducted in 2025, the department found substantial quantities of bricks and, in one case, coal allegedly unaccounted for in the books of account. The officers initiated proceedings under Section 130 of the CGST/UPGST Acts and imposed penalties and fines. The first appellate authority set aside these orders, holding that stock discrepancies discovered during inspections should be addressed through tax determination proceedings under Sections 73 or 74 rather than confiscation under Section 130. The Tribunal examined the common question of whether the Revenue could maintain an appeal seeking restoration of confiscation fines when the first appellate authority had already quashed the underlying proceedings and penalties. It noted that the Revenue’s four appeals were confined to the fines and did not challenge the findings invalidating the Section 130 proceedings. The Tribunal considered the statutory framework under Section 35(1) and Section 35(6), which governs the maintenance of accounts and determination of tax on unaccounted goods. It held that where excess or unaccounted stock is discovered during a survey, the proper officer must determine the tax liability through the mechanism prescribed under Sections 73 or 74. The Tribunal relied upon three Allahabad High Court decisions, namely Dinesh Kumar Pradeep Kumar, Shree Om Steels and V. K. Electricals, which established that stock discrepancies found during surveys should not be dealt with by directly invoking confiscation provisions under Section 130. Applying these principles, the Tribunal concluded that the adjudicating officers had wrongly initiated confiscation proceedings against the brick-field operators instead of following the prescribed tax determination procedure. Consequently, the first appellate authority had correctly quashed the original orders, including their demands for penalties and fines. Since the underlying confiscation proceedings themselves had been held unlawful, no separate question regarding the survival of the fines remained open. The Tribunal also criticised the Commissioner for directing the filing of appeals in a perfunctory manner without proper consideration of the facts and applicable law. Answering the common question in the negative, the Tribunal dismissed all four Revenue appeals as devoid of merit and upheld the relief granted to the taxpayers. Cases Discussed V. K. Electricals Vs Additional Commissioner Gr-II and Another (Allahabad High Court) – Writ Tax No. 957/2024, decided on 23.08.2024. Relied upon. The Tribunal referred to the High Court’s ruling that where excess stock is discovered during a survey, proceedings should be initiated under Sections 73/74 of the CGST/UPGST Acts rather than under Section 130 read with Rule 120. This supported the conclusion that confiscation proceedings against the brick-field operators were legally unsustainable. Dinesh Kumar Pradeep Kumar Vs Additional Commissioner, Gr-II and Others (Allahabad High Court) – Writ Tax No. 1082 of 2022, decided on 25.07.2024. Relied upon. The High Court held that excess stock discovered during a survey should be addressed through proceedings under Sections 73/74 of the CGST/UPGST Acts and not through confiscation proceedings under Section 130. The Tribunal adopted this principle in rejecting the Revenue’s appeals. Shree Om Steels Vs Additional Commissioner Gr. II and Others (Allahabad High Court) – Writ Tax No. 1007 of 2022, decided on 19.07.2024. Relied upon. The High Court ruled that excess stock discovered during a survey requires tax and penalty proceedings under Sections 73 or 74 rather than invoking the confiscation provisions of Section 130. The Tribunal applied this reasoning in upholding the cancellation of confiscation-related demands. FULL TEXT OF THE ORIGINAL ORDER OF GSTAT AGRA 1. These four appeals have been filed by Sh. Before embarking to the facts of the appeals it is worthy to note that in all the four matter under consideration, learned proper officer had imposed penalty and fine u/s 130(2) in lieu of confiscation of seized goods. In first appeals filed by respondents herein original order passed by proper officer were set aside by holding that seizure and confiscation cannot be made if, like in the present matters, goods were found unaccounted and instead thereof department should launch proceedings u/s 73/74 of CGST/UPGST, act, all the four appeals have been directed by the revenue only to the extent of amount of fine imposed by adjudicating officer. 11. We have heard the learned authorized representative of appellant and perused the records of appeals uploaded on the GSTAT Portal, the common question for determination before us may be formulated as under- Whether an appeal may be filed against order of fine only where amount of penalty in lieu of confiscation of goods has been set aside by first appellate authority? FINDINGS WITH REASONS 12. As quoted by First Appellate authority in its order, in case of M/s Dinesh Kumar Pradeep Kumar V/s Additional commissioner, Gr-II and others writ tax no. 1082 of 2022 date of decision 25.07.2024, it was held by Allahabad High court that whereon survey of business premises excess stock of goods was found which triggered the initiation of the proceedings u/s 130 of CGST act against petitioner then proceedings u/s 73/74 of CGST/UPGST act, should be initiated and not proceedings u/s 130 of the UPGST/CGST, Act read with rule 120 of the rules framed under the act. 13. Further in a recent case M/s Shri Om steels vs Additional Commissioner Gr. II and others writ tax no. 1007 of 2022 dated 19.07.2024, It is also held that if excess stock is found during a survey, tax and penalty proceedings must be initiated under Section 73 or 74 of the GST Act, rather than invoking the harsh confiscation provisions under Section 130. 14. Section 35(1) of the CGST, Act provides that all the registered persons are required to keep and maintain at the principal palace of business a true copy and correct account of things specified in clause (a) to (f). The second proviso to this section and rule 56 and 57 makes it further necessary to keep the said documents in electronic form. Further section 35(6) of the CGST, act enumerates the consequences if such person fails to keep his account for the goods or services, the proper officer is empowered to determine the amount of tax payable on the goods or the services which are unaccounted for as if such goods or services had been supplied by such person and the provisions of section 73 or 74 shall mutates mutandis apply for determination of the said tax. Thus, by bare perusal of the section 35(1) and 35(6) conjointly makes it clear that proper officer is empowered to determine the taxes payable and while determine the said tax he is bound to determine the same in-accordance with the provisions section 73 and 74 of CGST act/ UPGST act. 15. Apart from above, in case of V. K electricals vs Additional commissioner Gr-II and another writ tax no. 957/2024 date of decision 23.08.2024, Allahabad High court has held that in case of excess stock found at the time of survey proceeding u/s 73/74 of CGST/UPGST, Act should be initiated and in place or in addition to launching proceedings u/s 130 read with rule 120 unsustainable and liable to be quashed. 16. Thus, on going through the principle laid down in above quoted ratios established by Hon’ble Allahabad High court as well as on bare perusal of section 35 of CGST/UPGST, Act, it is crystal clear that where at the time of survey of business premises excess or unaccounted goods are found the proper officer is only empowered to initiate proceedings u/s 73/74 of CGST/UPGST, Act, against business entity concerned, he is not authorised by law to initiate proceedings u/s 130 and levy the penalty in view of confiscation of goods. In the present matters same is the position where on the survey of business premises which were brick fields excess/unaccounted stocks of bricks and coal are said to be found but instead of initiation proceedings u/s 73/74 learned proper officer initiates the proceedings u/s 130 of the CGST Act, which is not sustainable in law and by holding so and quashing the order passed by Adjudicating officer, Learned First Appellate Authority has rightly allowed the first appeals filed by taxpayers. First Appellate Authority has also rightly quashed the proceedings-initiated u/s 130 of CGST Act, by proper officer resulting set aside the demand of penalty and fine. 17. Interestingly, Appellants in this bunch of four appeals have not challenged the findings of first appellate authority in respect of quashing the proceedings and amount of penalty-imposed u/s 130 of CGST act, but they have only stated that as question of imposing fine by first appellate authority has left open therefore, appellants have requested to this tribunal that amount of fine imposed by adjudicating officer be maintained. 18. We are unable to understand that how question of imposing the amount of fine by adjudicating officer is said to be left open where learned first appellate authority has quashed and set aside the order-in-original passed by learned A.O by holding that launching of proceedings u/s 130 of CGST/UPGST, Act, was not backed by law. In second appeal before Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal, commissioner of State Tax department is required to direct his subordinate officer to file the appeal by going through the facts of the case and law applicable thereto. In our opinion learned commissioner has directed the appellants to file the appeals in perfunctory manner and without application of mind, which cannot be appreciated. 19. Therefore, in view of discussion of facts of the cases in hand and law applicable thereto, by answering the question formulated in negative, we are of the considered opinion that all these four appeals are liable to be dismissed as sans merit, which this bench directs. 20. There is no pending interim application in the appeals. 21. Order is pronounced in open court on this day of 17th of September, 2026. 22. Copy of order be communicated to all concerned, as per rules.
