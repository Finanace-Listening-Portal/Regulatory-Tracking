Articles 3 min read GFF 2026: SEBI turns to AI, analytics for predictive supervision as market risks move faster: Tuhin Kanta Pandey India’s securities market can scale rapidly without scaling risks at the same pace if innovation is accompanied by stronger resilience and trust, SEBI chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said at Global FinTech Fest 2026. As AI, tokenisation and quantum technology reshape capital markets, SEBI is moving towards predictive supervision and testing tokenised corporate bonds under its D-MAT 2.0 pilot. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Updated On Sep 10, 2026 at 05:48 PM IST var news_msid = 134008773; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Innovation must scale without scaling risk at the same pace, Securities and Exchange Board of India chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said, as he outlined the regulator’s approach to artificial intelligence, tokenisation and quantum technology at Global FinTech Fest 2026 on Thursday.Pandey said India’s securities market has grown rapidly with technology playing a central role through digital onboarding, electronic payments, electronic trading and settlement, and the ability to trace transactions to the ultimate investor.However, as markets become larger, faster and more interconnected, the nature of regulatory risk is also changing, he said. Advt “A vulnerability in one part of the ecosystem can travel much faster,” Pandey said, underlining the need for resilient infrastructure and regulatory capabilities that can identify emerging risks early.SEBI moves towards predictive supervisionAs market participants adopt advanced technologies at greater speed and scale, regulators must be able to supervise markets with comparable sophistication, Pandey said.SEBI is using data, analytics and AI to move beyond simply automating supervision towards identifying patterns that may not be visible through traditional regulatory methods.At SEBI, the objective is to make supervision increasingly predictive and capable of identifying emerging risks early, he said.The regulator is also working on implementing IOSCO’s supervisory toolkit for AI use in the Indian securities market, with the aim of strengthening risk management and supporting an agile AI governance framework.Tokenised bonds pilot tests integrated market architecturePandey said SEBI and other market institutions are also exploring how tokenisation could change the architecture of India’s securities market.A pilot project on tokenisation of corporate bonds under D-MAT 2.0 is examining whether distributed ledger technology can bring the security and settlement legs closer together, enable faster and potentially atomic settlement, and automate parts of asset servicing. Advt The pilot combines tokenised securities with digital settlement assets through the central bank digital currency and smart-contract functionality, while building on existing market infrastructure and preserving legal certainty around ownership.Over the preceding days, three issuers had issued tokenised corporate bonds under D-MAT 2.0, led by depositories CDSL and NSDL and supported by BSE, MSEI and NSE, Pandey said.The project brings together market infrastructure institutions, NPCI, banks, issuers, investors and regulators.The broader objective is to explore a market architecture where securities, settlement and servicing become more integrated and programmable. Advt Quantum resilience cannot waitPandey also flagged quantum computing as a future cybersecurity risk for capital markets.While the quantum threat to existing cryptography may not yet represent an immediate operational problem, migration to quantum-safe systems cannot begin only after the threat becomes real, he said.The transition requires prioritisation of critical systems, crypto-agility and a phased move towards post-quantum cryptography.SEBI’s Cyber Security and Cyber Resilience Framework already recognises quantum computing as a potential cybersecurity threat, and the regulator has embedded quantum resilience into its cybersecurity strategy in alignment with India’s National Quantum Mission. Advt Pandey said India has an opportunity to go beyond being a large market and early technology adopter.Its scale, digital public infrastructure, market institutions and regulatory experience can allow it to experiment at meaningful scale and contribute to global thinking on building securities markets that are both innovative and resilient.“Growing fast and growing safe are not competing ambitions,” Pandey said, adding that sustainable market growth requires innovation to be accompanied by trust and trust to be sustained by resilience.Click here to read our coverage of the Global FinTech Fest 2026 By Anushka Sengupta , ETBFSI Published On Sep 10, 2026 at 05:36 PM IST Telegram Facebook Copy Link Comments What are your thoughts?
