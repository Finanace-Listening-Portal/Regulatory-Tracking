The microfinance portfolio grew 8% sequentially in the September quarter, while gross slippages in the segment fell to 3.3% from 4.6% in the previous quarter.Management flagged drought conditions, fuel prices, inflation and upcoming state elections as risks to watch. Changes to microfinance underwriting introduced last year are paying off, while the bank is taking a calibrated approach to growth in view of drought declarations in the three states, according to a report by JM Financial. Advt Asset quality improved across the bank’s lending segments in the second quarter of FY27. Gross and net slippage ratios fell 31 basis points and 37 basis points, respectively, from the previous quarter, while credit cost declined 26 basis points to 1.40%. In microfinance, the proportion of loans overdue by 1–90 days fell to 0.6% from 1% in the June quarter.Around 89% of the microfinance book is covered under the Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU). About 73% of borrowers have a lending vintage of more than three years, while 51–52% have a single loan facility.The recoveryThe recovery in microfinance comes as the bank shifts from balance-sheet repair towards growth. Advances grew 11% sequentially, led by wholesale lending, which rose 10%. Retail advances grew about 4% sequentially excluding FCNR(B)-related effects, supported by home loans, which rose 12%, loans against property, up 7%, and business banking, up 10%.IndusInd Bank is targeting a 1% exit return on assets in FY27. Management expects to recover the 12-basis-point sequential decline in normalised net interest margin over the next two quarters, supported by a shift towards retail assets and the full-quarter benefit of repaying certificates of deposit. However, it expects some increase in deposit rates and tighter liquidity in the second half of FY27.JM Financial retained its ‘ADD’ rating but cut its target price to Rs 950 from Rs 1,130, factoring in the potential impact of draft insurance commission regulations. It estimates the bank’s return on assets at around 1% in FY28 and FY29, below private-bank peers, and has also flagged the upcoming expected credit loss transition as a risk.
