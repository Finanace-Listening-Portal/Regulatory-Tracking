Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Aman Sarin Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) WhatsApp Chat Alone Cannot Sustain an Addition U/s 69A Without a Finding That the Assessee Owned the Alleged Cash Search Proceedings and the ₹5 Lakh Addition A search under section 132 was conducted on the Sushen Mohan Gupta Group on 2 January 2020. The seized digital material included WhatsApp communications concerning Aman Sarin, who was a person other than the searched person. The Assessing Officer issued a notice under section 153C on 24 August 2022. Against the returned income of ₹53,82,770, the assessment completed on 20 March 2024 determined income at ₹58,82,770 after adding the alleged cash receipt. The CIT(A) confirmed the assessment. Ownership Is a Mandatory Condition Under Section 69A The Assessing Officer relied on a WhatsApp message from Amar Sarin to Aman Sarin to infer that the latter received ₹5 lakh in cash. The assessee disputed the inference and argued that the amount did not appear in the alleged chats. He further contended that no money was found with him and that the Assessing Officer had nowhere found him to be its owner. The Tribunal held that ownership is an essential condition for invoking section 69A. The provision concerns money, bullion, jewellery or another valuable article that the assessee is found to own, which is unrecorded in the books, if any, and for which the assessee offers no satisfactory explanation about its nature and source. The Tribunal emphasised that the essential conditions of a deeming provision cannot themselves be presumed. On the facts, no money was found with the assessee, and the Revenue could point to no finding in the assessment order establishing his ownership of the alleged cash. Following the Supreme Court’s decision in D.N. Singh v. CIT, Central, the Tribunal directed deletion of the entire addition. The absence of a finding on ownership was decisive. Assessment Was Within the Time Allowed Under Section 153B The assessee argued that the assessment had to be completed by 31 December 2023, applying the nine-month period under the first proviso to section 153B(1). The Tribunal applied clause (ii) of the third proviso, which prescribed a twelve-month period for the search in question. It compared the period calculated from the end of the financial year in which the last search authorisation was executed with the period calculated from the end of the financial year in which the seized material was handed over to the other person’s jurisdictional Assessing Officer. The later deadline governed. On the record before it, the Tribunal took 24 August 2022, the notice date, as the presumed handover date. Twelve months from the end of FY 2022–23 expired on 31 March 2024. The assessment dated 20 March 2024 was therefore within time. Common Satisfaction Note and Cross-Examination Although the satisfaction note covered AYs 2014–15 to 2020–21, the seized messages carried timestamps falling in FY 2019–20, corresponding to the year under appeal. The Tribunal found that the note identified the seized material and recorded its bearing on the assessee’s income. Relying on Indian National Congress v. DCIT, 463 ITR 431 (Delhi), it upheld the consolidated note in the circumstances of this case. The Tribunal also rejected the cross-examination objection. It recorded that Amar Sarin’s statement was supplied on 23 January 2024, that two show-cause notices followed and that the record contained no request to cross-examine him during assessment. Actual Search Date Governs the 1 April 2021 Cut-Off The assessee argued that receipt of the material in August 2022 should be treated as the search date, bringing the case within the bar under section 153C(3). The Tribunal explained that CIT v. Jasjit Singh and PCIT v. Ojjus Medicare Pvt. Ltd. dealt with the deemed date under the first proviso to section 153C(1) for identifying the assessment years covered. For section 153C(3), the Tribunal applied the actual date of initiation of the search, which was 2 January 2020. It also recorded that the operative order of the Madras High Court single judge in Harigovind v. ACIT, relied on by the assessee, had been stayed by a Division Bench. Author’s Comments The decision illustrates the separate inquiries under sections 153C and 69A. A dated WhatsApp message can bear on the determination of income for a particular assessment year and support the satisfaction required under section 153C. To sustain an addition under section 69A, the assessment must go further and identify the evidence establishing that the assessee owned the specific money sought to be taxed. In this case, the same digital material survived scrutiny at the jurisdictional stage, but the inference drawn from it fell short of proving the statutory condition for the addition. A careful analysis of the provisions of law u/s 153B of the Act, shows that the assessee’s arguments deserves to be rejected. The law mandates two different dates for completion of assessment in cases of search. For assessee covered under the provision of section 153A, the law provides for limitation of twenty one months from the end of Financial year in which the last of the authorisation for search u/s 132 was executed. For assessee covered under the provisions of section 153C, the law provides for limitation of twenty one months from the end of Financial year in which the last of the authorisation for search u/s 132 was executed or nine months from the end of financial year in which books of account or documents or assets seized or requisitioned are handed over under section 153C to the Assessing Officer having jurisdiction over such other person, whichever is later. The law however, treats the prescribed time limit for searches taking place in Financial year 2019-20 differently. Under the provisions of the sub- clause (ii) of the 3rd proviso to section 153B of the Act, where search section 153C, the period of limitation for making the assessment or reassessment under shall be the period of twelve months from the end of financial year commencing on 01.04.2019 in which the last of the authorisation for search u/s 132 was executed or twelve months from the end of the financial year in which books of account or documents or assets seized or requisitioned are handed over under section 153C to the Assessing Officer having jurisdiction over such other person, whichever is later. In the instant case, the search u/s 132 took place on 02.01.2020, and there is no material placed before us to suggest that the last of the authorization took place beyond the financial year 2019-20. Therefore, twelve months from the end of the financial year, in which books of account or documents or assets seized or requisitioned are handed over under section 153C, is to be computed. The notice u/s 153C was issued on 24.08.2022, hence the presumption is that books of account or documents or assets seized, was handed over to the AO of the non-searched person i.e., the assessee on 24.08.2022 which is FY 2022-23. Computing twelve months from the end of FY 2022-23, i.e., 31.03.2023, gives the limitation date of 31.03.2024 for completion of assessment in the case of the assessee. In the given facts of the instant case, the assessment under section 153C was to be completed by 31.03.2024. As the assessment under section 153C was made on proviso to section 153B of the Act, the assessment completed on 20.03.2024 is considered as within the prescribed time. Ground 1 dismissed. 12. On merits, the assessee has raised the grievance in ground 9 that the AO invoked the provisions of section 69A without meeting the applied only in the case if the assessee is found to be the owner of money, bullion, jewellery and any other valuable asset. It is submitted that there was no discovery of any money, bullion, jewellery or any other valuable asset of like nature as stipulated in section 69A of the Income Tax Act and the AO never alleged that the assessee is owner of the money. The assessee relied on the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of M/s D.N Singh Vs. CIT, Central 1023 LiveLaw (SC) 451 dated 16.05.2023 in the context of section 69A of the Act. 20. We find that in the instant case, the AO made addition of Rs 5 lakh u/s 69A on the strength of whatsapp message from Amar Sarin to the assessee. We agree with the assessee that deeming provisions under section 69A can be applied only in the case if the assessee is found to be the owner of money, bullion, jewellery and any other valuable asset and it is settled law that to invoke deeming provisions in the Income Tax Act, no presumptions can be made regarding the existence of the essential conditions within the deeming provisions. We note, in the instant case, it is admitted fact that no money was found on the assessee. It is also not controverted by the Revenue that there is any finding of the AO showing the assessee as “owner” of the money. We are therefore of the considered view that the mandatory requirements of section 69A is not fulfilled in the instant case. Following the decision of the hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of M/S D.N Singh Vs. CIT, Central (supra), we hold that since the ingredients of section 69A is not satisfied, no additions can be made in the case of the assessee and accordingly we direct the AO to delete the said addition. Ground 9 is allowed. 21. In the result, appeal filed by the assessee in ITA No.3073/Del/2026 is partly allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 16th November, 2026.
