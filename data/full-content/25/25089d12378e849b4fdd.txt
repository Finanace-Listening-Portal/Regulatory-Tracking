Summary: Hedera is a public distributed ledger that uses the hashgraph consensus algorithm rather than a conventional linear blockchain. Its native cryptocurrency, HBAR, pays network fees and contributes stake weight to consensus. Hedera currently exposes consensus, token and smart-contract services, while permissioned mainnet consensus nodes are operated by organisations participating in the Hedera Council. The network is open source, but governance, node admission, treasury decisions and protocol upgrades remain material parts of its risk profile. The Council reports a fixed total supply of 50 billion HBAR, divided between released and unreleased classifications; that accounting should not be confused with a promise about liquidity or value. For an Indian holder, an HBAR transfer can trigger the virtual-digital-asset tax regime and one-percent withholding even when consideration is received in another token rather than rupees. This article separates protocol facts from custody claims, service-provider compliance and investment assertions. From the resulting hashgraph, nodes can calculate how other nodes would vote on event order without sending a separate ballot for every decision. Consensus timestamps are then used to establish a deterministic order. The network describes this design as asynchronous Byzantine fault tolerant. That property concerns the consensus model under stated assumptions; it does not make every application, smart contract, token, wallet or custodian secure. HBAR is the native unit used to pay for network transactions and services. Fees are expressed by the network in US-dollar terms and converted into HBAR through the network’s exchange-rate mechanism. A stable fee schedule in dollar terms reduces one type of developer uncertainty, but the HBAR amount charged can vary with the exchange-rate value and transaction type. Users should read a transaction preview and current fee schedule rather than assume a figure copied from an older example. Council Governance Shapes Nodes, Pricing, Upgrades and Treasury The Hedera Council is a multi-member limited liability company whose members participate in network governance. Council materials describe member responsibilities that include voting on network pricing, software updates and treasury transfers, as well as operating nodes and signing governance transactions. Members have equal voting rights under the governance design and normally serve limited terms. This structure is materially different from anonymous permissionless node admission, token-holder voting or governance by a single corporate operator. Council membership does not give a member an economic ownership right in the treasury HBAR. Council documentation distinguishes governance interests from rights to treasury distributions. Readers should therefore avoid inferring that the presence of recognised organisations is a guarantee of HBAR’s value, application performance, regulatory treatment or redemption. Council composition, committee policy, member terms and node-admission arrangements can change over time. Fifty billion HBAR is a governance-controlled total supply The Council’s treasury report dated 6 October 2026 states that the network launched with a total supply of 50 billion HBAR and that changing total supply requires unanimous Council consent under the LLC agreement. It classifies HBAR as released once transferred to an account operationally controlled by someone other than the Council; unreleased holdings are further described as allocated or unallocated. Those labels are treasury-accounting categories. They do not represent circulating-market depth, free float, vesting enforceability or a forecast of future distributions. It does not automatically prove that the off-chain statement was true, that the sender had authority, or that personal data was lawfully processed. Application designers remain responsible for identity, content validation, access control, privacy and retention. Public immutability can make accidental disclosure difficult to remedy. Token Service manages native fungible and non-fungible tokens Hedera Token Service can create and administer native tokens with configurable keys and controls such as supply, pause, freeze, wipe and KYC features where selected by the issuer. Those controls vary by token. A token appearing on Hedera is not the same asset as HBAR and is not endorsed by Hedera merely because it uses the service. Prospective users must inspect token identifiers, administration keys, supply rules and issuer terms rather than relying on a name or ticker. Smart Contract Service supports EVM-compatible execution The Smart Contract Service supports Solidity and Ethereum tooling through an EVM-compatible environment. Compatibility reduces migration friction but does not eliminate contract bugs, upgrade keys, oracle failures, bridge risk or differences in network semantics. Hedera’s own FAQ notes that user-deployed contracts and example contracts require their own auditing. Contract addresses, source verification and authority controls should be checked before any approval or transfer. Account Keys and Token Identifiers Drive Practical Custody Risk A Hedera account can be controlled by cryptographic keys or more complex key structures. Anyone who obtains a sufficient signing key can generally authorise transactions, while lost recovery material can make assets inaccessible. Account IDs, aliases, EVM addresses and token IDs are related but not interchangeable labels. A sender should confirm the exact identifier expected by the receiving service and whether a memo or token association is required. Self-custody puts signing and recovery on the holder. Custodial ownership instead depends on the platform’s contract, internal ledger, segregation arrangements and withdrawal process. A custodian may aggregate HBAR in omnibus accounts, and an on-chain balance does not by itself prove a particular customer’s legal entitlement. Users should verify the exact contracting entity, Indian compliance status where applicable, withdrawal terms, incident history and complaint route. Foreign regulation alone does not establish permission to serve Indian residents. Stake affects node weight in consensus and may qualify an account for rewards when current eligibility conditions are satisfied. Staked HBAR is not necessarily locked in the way some other proof-of-stake systems lock assets, but rewards, maximum rates, thresholds, timing and eligibility are governed by current network parameters and Council decisions. A displayed annual percentage is not fixed interest, a bank deposit or a promise of future return. Custodial staking adds another layer. The service provider may control node choice, reward sharing, payout timing and withdrawal. The provider’s use of the word staking does not prove that a customer’s HBAR is individually delegated on-chain. Tax treatment of rewards can depend on receipt, control, valuation and subsequent disposal. Indian users should preserve reward dates, quantities, INR valuation method and the relationship between custodial credits and on-chain events. Indian HBAR Transfers Fall Within the VDA Tax Framework HBAR is ordinarily within India’s statutory virtual digital asset definition. Section 115BBH applies a 30% rate to income from transfer of a VDA, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. The special computation generally allows only cost of acquisition, not other expenditure or allowance, and prohibits setting off a VDA transfer loss against other income or carrying it forward under that provision. Selling HBAR, swapping it for another token or spending it can be a transfer even if no rupees enter a bank account. Section 194S withholding is not the final tax Section 194S generally requires one-percent withholding on consideration paid to a resident for a VDA transfer, subject to statutory thresholds and mechanics. Current Income Tax Department guidance refers to an annual threshold of ₹50,000 for a specified person and ₹10,000 for other payers. Exchanges and token-to-token transactions can involve special collection procedures. TDS is a credit and does not replace the holder’s computation of consideration, cost and taxable income. A wallet transfer still needs an audit trail A transfer between two wallets under the same beneficial ownership may not itself be a disposal, but the taxpayer must be able to establish common ownership and reconcile network fees, dates and balances. Gifts, rewards, airdrops, staking credits and bridge transactions require separate analysis. Records should include transaction hashes, account IDs, platform statements, invoices, INR valuation sources and TDS certificates. FIU-IND registration of a service provider addresses AML duties; it is not government approval of HBAR and does not insure customer assets. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Is Hedera a conventional blockchain? No. Hedera uses the hashgraph data structure and gossip-about-gossip with virtual voting to reach ordered consensus. It is still a distributed ledger with software, node and governance risks. 2. What is HBAR used for? HBAR pays network fees, transfers value and supplies stake weight to nodes. Those utilities do not guarantee market demand, price appreciation or liquidity. 3. Can anyone operate a Hedera mainnet consensus node today? The network is public to use and open source, but current mainnet consensus nodes are permissioned and operated by Council participants under the network’s present model. 4. Can the 50 billion HBAR total supply be changed? The October 2026 Council treasury report says a change requires unanimous Council consent under the LLC agreement. That governance threshold is not an impossibility guarantee. 5. Are all tokens created through Hedera Token Service equivalent to HBAR? No. Native tokens have separate token IDs, issuers and administrative keys. HBAR is the network’s native cryptocurrency. 6. Does native staking guarantee a fixed yield? No. Reward parameters, eligibility and distribution can change, and a custodial provider may apply separate terms. Staking is not a bank deposit. 7. Is an HBAR-to-token swap taxable in India? It can be. A token swap can constitute a VDA transfer requiring INR valuation, gain computation and separate withholding analysis.
