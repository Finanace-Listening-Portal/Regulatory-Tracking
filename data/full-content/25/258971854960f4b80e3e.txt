} } Listen to This ArticleThe Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI’s) outstanding net short dollar position in the forward market surged to a new record of $239.2 billion at the end of September, from $200.06 billion at the end of August, according to the latest data released by the central bank. While net shorts with a maturity of more than one year increased to $199 billion from $176.6 billion, those in the three-month to one-year tenor stood at $13.22 billion at the end of September. The sharp rise in the net short position in recent months was driven by a special concessional swap window for banks to mobilise foreign currency non-resident (bank), or FCNR(B), deposits, which was operationalised on June 8. Although the window closed on August 31, banks were allowed to enter into swaps under it until September 11. “The RBI’s recent slew of foreign exchange measures and tighter market access have steadied the spot market, with the rupee rebounding around 2 per cent from its recent low of 96.85 per dollar,” said Madhavi Arora, lead economist at Emkay Global. “This heavy short position also limits the RBI’s room to cap severely dislocated and elevated forward premia.” The 12-month dollar-rupee forward premium rose by more than 120 basis points in October, increasing the cost of hedging future dollar payments. The premium, which reflects the difference between forward and spot exchange rates, rose to 4.57 per cent on Friday from 3.36 per cent at the end of September.
