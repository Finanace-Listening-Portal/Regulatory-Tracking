Summary: The Income-tax Act, 2025, effective from 1 April 2026, replaces the Income-tax Act, 1961, introducing a substantially simplified legislative structure. The number of sections has been reduced from 819 to 536, while chapters have been consolidated from 47 to 23. Although the tax rates and slabs remain unchanged, numerous commonly used provisions have been renumbered, requiring taxpayers, chartered accountants and tax professionals to update their references. The new legislation also introduces the concept of a single “Tax Year” in place of the earlier distinction between previous year and assessment year. The transition requires particular attention because returns for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) and proceedings relating to earlier years continue under the Income-tax Act, 1961, whereas income arising during Tax Year 2026-27 onwards falls under the new legislation. Important changes include Section 80C becoming Section 123, Section 80D becoming Section 126, Section 87A becoming Section 156, Section 115BAC becoming Section 202, Section 44AB becoming Section 63 and Section 139 becoming Section 263. Similarly, the presumptive taxation provisions under Sections 44AD, 44ADA and 44AE are consolidated under Section 58. One of the most significant structural changes concerns TDS and TCS provisions. Salary TDS under Section 192 is now covered by Section 392, while several other TDS provisions, including Sections 194C, 194I and 194J, are consolidated into the table-based Section 393. TCS under Section 206C moves to Section 394. The article also provides comparative tables covering salary exemptions, house property, business deductions, capital gains, unexplained income, set-off and carry-forward of losses, Chapter VI-A deductions, rebates, return filing and revised income-tax forms. The section mapping serves as a practical reference for preparing tax returns, conducting audits, drafting assessment replies, advising clients and updating compliance documentation. Since some published concordance tables contain conflicting references, practitioners should verify the applicable provisions against the enacted Income-tax Act, 2025, Income-tax Rules, 2026 and official CBDT concordance before relying on a particular section number in any statutory filing or professional opinion. Practical tips for the transition Search the parent section.Sub-clauses were reorganised far more than sections. Look up 80C, not 80C(2)(xviii). Renumbered does not always mean unchanged.Some provisions were amended while moving. Read the new text before assuming. Update your templates.Engagement letters, notice reply formats and checklists that cite old numbers need two versions for the next few years: one for AY 2026-27 and earlier, one for Tax Year 2026-27 onwards.
