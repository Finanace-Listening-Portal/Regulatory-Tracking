Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Shilpaben Shah & Ors. Vs Jasmin Pravinchandra Shah (Gujarat High Court) Summary : The Gujarat High Court, in Shilpaben Shah & Ors. Vs Jasmin Pravinchandra Shah, considered two connected arbitration petitions arising from disputes concerning M/s. Corus Metal, a partnership firm constituted under a deed dated 29 June 2020 between late Sanjay Pravinchandra Shah and the respondent, each holding a 50% share. The partnership deed provided that the firm was a partnership at will and contained an arbitration clause covering disputes relating to the firm. The petitioners alleged that the respondent had executed an agreement to sell the firm’s GIDC land situated at Dholka, Ahmedabad, on 1 January 2022 for ₹1.81 crore and subsequently conveyed the property to M/s. Sachi Steel Solution Private Limited through a registered sale deed dated 27 August 2024 without remitting the deceased partner’s 50% share of the consideration. Following Sanjay Pravinchandra Shah’s death on 4 December 2025, the petitioners contended that the two-partner firm stood dissolved by operation of law. They sought settlement of accounts, distribution of partnership assets and recovery of the deceased partner’s capital balance of ₹74,73,305 reflected in the audited balance sheet as on 31 March 2025. The petitioners invoked the arbitration clause by issuing a notice dated 9 May 2026 under Section 21 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, which was served on 13 May 2026. They proposed the appointment of a sole arbitrator, but the respondent refused to concur through a reply dated 5 June 2026, contending that the disputes were non-arbitrable and that the partnership deed had ceased to operate following the partner’s death. The petitioners consequently approached the High Court under Section 11(5) seeking appointment of an arbitrator. During the proceedings, the respondent’s counsel opposed the petitions on the ground of accord and satisfaction. The High Court rejected that objection in view of the Supreme Court’s decision in SBI General Insurance Co. Ltd. Vs Krish Spinning, 2024 SCC OnLine SC 1754. Both parties thereafter agreed that a suitable arbitrator could be appointed through the Gujarat High Court Arbitration Center. Examining Section 11(8) of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, the High Court observed that an appointing court must consider the qualifications required under the parties’ agreement and the factors necessary to secure an independent and impartial arbitrator. Referring to the Supreme Court judgments in Central Organisation for Railway Electrification v. ECI SPIC SMO MCML (JV) A Joint Venture Company, 2024 INSC 857, and Voestalpine Schienen GmbH v. Delhi Metro Rail Corporation Ltd., (2017) 4 SCC 665, the Court emphasised the importance of broad-based arbitrator panels incorporating not only judges and advocates but also engineers and other qualified professionals capable of addressing technical disputes. The Court also referred to the empirical study Arbitrator Appointments in Numbers, published by the Milon K. Banerji Centre for Arbitration Law, NALSAR University of Law, Hyderabad, in October 2026. According to the study, women accounted for only 29.97% of 2,283 court-made arbitrator appointments for which gender information was recorded, while technical experts represented merely 3.10% of appointments where professional background was available. The Court considered this under-representation significant and encouraged parties, counsel and arbitral institutions to widen the pool of eligible arbitrators without compromising competence, integrity or independence. Accordingly, Justice D.N. Ray allowed the arbitration petitions and appointed Justice Mauna M. Bhatt, Former Judge of the Gujarat High Court, as the sole arbitrator to resolve the disputes between the parties under the Arbitration Centre (Domestic and International), High Court of Gujarat Rules, 2021. Both parties were directed to be governed by those Rules. The Registry was instructed to communicate the appointment order to the arbitrator forthwith by Speed Post. The Court also disposed of any pending connected applications and made no order as to costs. The judgment is significant for applying the limited judicial scrutiny applicable at the arbitrator-appointment stage while expressly drawing attention to the need for greater participation of qualified women and technical professionals in arbitration. Cases Discussed 1. SBI General Insurance Co. Ltd. Vs Krish Spinning, 2024 SCC OnLine SC 1754 (Supreme Court) — Relied upon to reject the respondent’s objection based on accord and satisfaction at the stage of appointment of an arbitrator under Section 11 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. 2. Central Organisation for Railway Electrification v. ECI SPIC SMO MCML (JV) A Joint Venture Company, 2024 INSC 857 (Supreme Court, Constitution Bench) — Cited in footnote 1 with reference to paragraphs 36 and 38 concerning the statutory considerations governing the appointment of an independent and impartial arbitrator. 3. Voestalpine Schienen GmbH v. Delhi Metro Rail Corporation Ltd., (2017) 4 SCC 665 (Supreme Court) — Cited in footnote 2 for the principle that arbitrator panels should be broad-based and include suitably qualified professionals beyond judges and lawyers. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF GUJARAT HIGH COURT 1. Heard Mr.Aditya A. Gupta, learned advocate for the petitioners and Mr.Padmaraj K. Jadeja learned advocate for the respondent. 2. Issues raised in Arbitration Petition No. 119 of 2026 and Arbitration Petition No. 123 of 2026 are substantially identical and they were heard together. For the sake of convenience, Arbitration Petition No. 119 of 2026 is treated as the lead petition, and the decision rendered herein shall govern the outcome of the connected petition as well. 3. The factual matrix giving rise to the lead petition is as under:- 3.1 The petitioners’ case, in nuce, is that late Shri Sanjay Pravinchandra Shah and the respondent constituted a partnership firm, M/s. Corus Metal, under a Partnership Deed dated 29.06.2020, each holding an equal 50% share. Clause 9 of the Deed makes the partnership at will, while Clause 14 provides for reference of all disputes relating to the firm to arbitration. 3.2 The firm owned Gujarat Industrial Development Corporation (GIDC) land bearing Survey No. 1954, Sub-Plot No. 402, at Village Dholka, District Ahmedabad. According to the petitioners, the respondent executed an agreement to sell (banakhat) dated 01.01.2022, for a consideration of Rs. 1,81,00,000/-, and thereafter conveyed the said land to M/s. Sachi Steel Solution Private Limited by a registered Sale Deed dated 27.08.2024, without remitting the deceased’s 50% share of the consideration. Shri Sanjay Pravinchandra Shah passed away on 04.12.2025, and the petitioners contend that the two-partner firm thereupon stood dissolved by operation of law. 3.3 The petitioners thereafter sought settlement of accounts and distribution of assets, including the deceased’s capital of Rs.74,73,305/- as reflected in the audited balance sheet as on 31.03.2025, which the respondent declined. 3.4 The petitioners therefore invoked Clause 14 by a notice dated 09.05.2026, under Section 21 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 (hereinafter referred to as “the Act, 1996”), served on 13.05.2026, proposing a name of the sole arbitrator. By a reply dated 05.06.2026, the respondent declined to concur with the proposed nomination and contended, inter alia, that the disputes are non-arbitrable and that the Deed ceased to operate upon the death of the partner. 4. Being aggrieved and dissatisfied with the refusal of the respondent to refer the dispute to arbitration, the petitioners herein, have preferred the present petition under Section 11(5) of the Act, 1996, with the prayer to appoint the arbitrator. 5. Mr. Padmraj K. Jadeja, learned counsel for the Respondent has opposed the petition on the ground of accord and satisfaction. However, in view of a catena of decisions of the Hon’ble Supreme Court including SBI General Insurance Co. Ltd. Vs. Krish Spinning reported in 2024 SCC OnLine SC 1754, the said objection is unsustainable. At this stage, both the parties agreed that a suitable Arbitrator may be appointed by the Gujarat High Court Arbitration Center. 6. Section 11(8) of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 reads as under:- “(8) the Chief Justice or the person or the institution designated by him, in appointment of an arbitrator, shall have due regard to- any qualifications required of the arbitrator by the agreement of the parties; and other consideration as are likely to secure the appointment of an independent and impartial arbitrator.” 7. Section 11(8) of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 enjoins the appointing Court to have due regard to the qualifications agreed upon by the parties and to such considerations as are likely to secure the appointment of an independent and impartial arbitrator.1 The Hon’ble Supreme Court has emphasized that panels of arbitrators ought to be “broadbased”, comprising not only judges and lawyers but also engineers and other professionals, so that the technical aspects of a dispute may be suitably resolved by utilizing their expertise.2 8. The empirical study Arbitrator Appointments in Numbers, however, examining court-made appointments in cases registered in the year 2025 before select constitutional courts, records that, of 2,283 appointments for which gender was recorded, only 29.97% went to women, while technical experts accounted for a mere 3.10%.3 Without derogating from the primacy of competence, integrity and independence, this Court is of the considered view that such under-representation merits due attention. Parties, counsel and arbitral institutions are, therefore, encouraged to widen the pool from which arbitrators are drawn, so as to include duly qualified women and technical experts. 9. Accordingly, the following order is passed:- ORDER (i) Petition is allowed. (ii) Hon’ble Mrs. Justice Mauna M. Bhatt, Former Judge, High Court of Gujarat, having address at:- D-45, Aryaman Bungalows, Thaltej-Shilaj Road, Shilaj, Thaltej, Ahmedabad – 380059, Mobile No. 9825039195 and Email : [email protected], is hereby appointed as the sole Arbitrator to resolve the disputes between the parties in accordance with the Arbitration Centre (Domestic and International), High Court of Gujarat Rules, 2021. Both Parties would also be governed by said Rules. (iii) Registry to communicate this order to the sole Arbitrator forthwith by Speed Post. (iv) Consequently, all pending connected application/s, if any, stands disposed of. No order as to costs. Delhi Metro Rail Corporation Ltd., (2017) 4 SCC 665, paras 7, 26, 29 and 30. 3 Milon K.
