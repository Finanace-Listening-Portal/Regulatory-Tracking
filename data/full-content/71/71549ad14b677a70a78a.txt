Chidambaram Vs Principal Director of Income Tax (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madras High Court, in Karti P. Chidambaram Vs Principal Director of Income Tax (Investigation) and connected writ petitions, examined the validity of proceedings initiated under Section 153C read with Section 153A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, following searches conducted at the premises of third parties. The petitioners challenged the notices on grounds including lack of jurisdiction, limitation, alleged misuse of statutory powers and violations of natural justice. The Court considered whether proceedings under Section 153C could be initiated when reassessment proceedings under Sections 147 and 148 had already commenced on the basis of information obtained during the search. The dispute originated from the sale of approximately 5.11 acres of land by Karti P. Chidambaram to Agni Estates and Foundations Private Limited during financial year 2013–14 for a stated consideration of ₹15.33 crore. The petitioner maintained that the transaction had been disclosed in his return for Assessment Year 2014–15 and examined during the original scrutiny assessment. Following a search at the purchaser’s premises in July 2018, the Income Tax Department obtained notebooks and recorded statements that allegedly indicated additional cash consideration. The Assessing Officer consequently initiated reassessment proceedings under Section 148 in August 2018, alleging escaped income of ₹6,38,75,000. Subsequently, the Department issued notices dated 16 December 2019 under Section 153C, requiring returns for six assessment years from AY 2013–14 to AY 2018–19. The petitioner contended that the Department had relied on substantially the same seized materials for both sets of proceedings. According to the petitioner, the reassessment proceedings were approaching their limitation deadline of 31 December 2019, and the initiation of Section 153C proceedings was an attempt to extend the available period for assessment. The petitioner also challenged the subsequent show-cause notices and questioned the proposed taxation of alleged undisclosed consideration across different assessment years. The petitioners argued that once the Assessing Officer had invoked Sections 147 and 148, he could not abandon those proceedings and initiate action under Section 153C on the same material merely to overcome limitation. They further submitted that the alleged cash consideration related to the transfer of land in financial year 2013–14 and therefore could not be indiscriminately attributed to other assessment years. The petitioners invoked the principles of natural justice, the requirement of proper satisfaction before exercising jurisdiction under Section 153C, and judicial precedents concerning reassessment, writ jurisdiction and the validity of search-related assessments. The Revenue defended the proceedings on the basis that the seized documents and information obtained during the search disclosed transactions requiring examination under the statutory search-assessment framework. It maintained that the provisions governing reassessment and assessments of persons other than the searched person operated under distinct statutory conditions. The Court examined the respective statutory provisions, the satisfaction recorded by the authorities, the materials relied upon, and the objections concerning overlapping proceedings, jurisdiction and limitation. It also considered the scope of judicial interference at the notice stage, before completion of the assessment proceedings. The Madras High Court ultimately dismissed the batch of twelve writ petitions, declining to invalidate the proceedings under Section 153C on the jurisdictional and procedural grounds advanced by the petitioners. The judgment addressed the distinction between reassessment and search-related assessment proceedings and the statutory powers available to the Income Tax Department. The ruling did not itself determine the final taxable amount of the alleged undisclosed consideration; the substantive assessment issues remained matters for adjudication under the applicable statutory procedure. The decision was pronounced by Justice S.M. Subramaniam on 5 July 2021.
