(Supreme Court of India) Supreme Court Prioritises Refund of Verified PACL Payments Where Plot Allotments Cannot Be Completed Summary: The Supreme Court held that, in the PACL investor restitution proceedings, the Court could direct repayment of verified amounts with 8% interest where completing the original plot transaction would prolong recovery and involve unresolved questions of title. The Court protected eligible allottees who had paid the full price or more than half through banking channels, subject to the conditions in its order. It also preserved the right of cash-paying claimants to approach the appropriate civil court for recovery or declaration. How the PACL Plot Dispute Reached the Supreme Court SEBI concluded that PACL had collected money from the public through an unregistered collective investment scheme and directed it to wind up the schemes and refund investors. The Securities Appellate Tribunal upheld SEBI’s order. During the appeals, the Supreme Court constituted the Justice (Retd.) R.M. Lodha Committee on 2 February 2016 to liquidate PACL’s land assets and facilitate equitable refunds. The present order dealt with applications concerning plot allotments in Sectors 100 and 104 of Mohali and in Bathinda. With the assistance of chartered accountants, the Committee examined bank records and classified the claims according to the mode, amount and timing of payment. The Supreme Court accepted the classification and issued directions for each category. Full Bank Payment Qualifies for Sale or Possession Certificates For Category I, the Court directed the Recovery Officer under Section 28A of the SEBI Act to issue sale certificates or no-objection certificates to the listed allottees within eight weeks. Where verified full payment had been made but neither a possession certificate nor a sale deed had been issued, the Recovery Officer was also directed to issue the necessary possession certificate within the same period. Thus, eligible allottees could obtain relief even where the transaction paperwork remained incomplete. Substantial Part-Payment Allows Completion Subject to Conditions For eligible Category II allottees who had paid more than 50% through banking channels, the Committee must provide a statement of the contractual balance and the contribution towards internal development works within eight weeks. The allottee must pay both amounts through banking channels within 30 days of receiving the statement. The Recovery Officer must then issue the sale certificate or no-objection certificate within eight weeks of receiving the outstanding payment. An allottee who misses the deadline may apply once, giving reasons, for an extension of up to 30 days. If payment still remains outstanding, the plot will be treated as unsold and the verified amount refunded on Category III terms. The operative directions also exclude allottees in the specified annexures who made any payment after 2 February 2016 and apply the refund directions to them. Less Than Half Paid Through Banks Entitles Allottees to Refund With Interest For Category III, comprising allottees who paid less than 50% through banking channels, some applicants offered to pay the balance at prevailing market or circle rates. The Court considered the time already spent on the proceedings and the uncertainties involved in resolving competing claims, clearing title and transferring property. In exercise of its extraordinary jurisdiction, it prioritised returning the money that the Committee had verified. The Court directed refund of the auditor-certified amount with 8% interest, payable within three months from the successful auction of the concerned plot. Once that payment is made, the allottee’s claim relating to the plot stands fully satisfied. The same refund mechanism applies to covered post-freeze banking payments under Category IV. Cash Payments and Transfers During the Proceedings The Court rejected the claims of Category IV cash-paying allottees for allotment or refund in these proceedings. It nevertheless held that they could approach the appropriate civil court for recovery or declaration. The question of which defendants should be impleaded was left for further clarification after hearing SEBI and the amici curiae. For Category V, where allotment rights were sold during the pendency of the proceedings, relief follows the category that would have applied to the original allotment. The Committee must extend the corresponding benefit to those applicants. Author’s Comments The distinction between verification of payment and entitlement to the plot explains the outcome. A bank record could establish how much an allottee paid, while the payment threshold and the freeze date determined whether the Court would allow completion or direct a refund. For instance, an allottee listed in Category II who crossed the 50% threshold but made an instalment after the freeze date falls under the refund directions. The auction-linked deadline is equally significant. The three-month period runs from the successful auction of the particular plot, and payment of the verified amount with interest brings the allotment claim to an end. These directions operate within the Supreme Court’s PACL restitution framework, where the Court sought to protect eligible plot claims while securing a practical route to repayment for other verified claimants. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF THE SUPREME COURT 1. This order disposes of Interlocutory Applications regarding claims filed by plot holders/allottees of PACL at Sector 100 and 104 of Mohali, and at Bhatinda. 2. The present dispute arises from the regulatory action taken by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (“SEBI”) against PACL on the ground that its business model, under which it collected money from public/investors under various investment schemes, constituted an unregistered Collective Investment Scheme (“CIS”). 3. In 1999, SEBI issued notices directing PACL to either obtain registration under the SEBI (CIS) Regulations, 1999, or to wind up its operations. Asserting that its activities were confined to real estate dealings and did not constitute CIS activity, PACL initiated writ proceedings before the High Court of Rajasthan. By its judgment dated 28.11.2003, the High Court allowed the said writ petition, holding that PACL’s activities did not satisfy the statutory requirements of a CIS, and consequently quashed the notices issued by SEBI. In the appeal filed by SEBI, by its order dated 26.02.2013, this Court disposed of the appeal by setting aside the judgment of the High Court and remanded the matter to SEBI, directing that the original 1999 notices be treated as show-cause notices and that SEBI should conduct a de novo adjudication to determine whether its business activities fell within the ambit of a CIS. 4. Pursuant to the said remand, SEBI passed final orders concluding that PACL was, indeed, running an unregistered CIS and directed its promoters and directors to refund the monies collected from investors and to wind up all existing schemes. This order was upheld by the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT). It is in this context that the present Civil Appeals against this judgment and order of SAT are ongoing. 5. By an order dated 02.02.2016, this Court constituted the Justice (Retd.) R.M. Lodha Committee (“the Committee”), which was entrusted with the dual mandate of liquidating the Company’s land assets and ensuring the equitable refund of investor funds. In furtherance of these duties, the Committee retrieved and scrutinized title deeds from the CBI, engaged necessary experts for valuation and auditing, and also formulated and executed modalities for the sale of immovable properties and then verified and disbursed investors’ claims. 6. The Committee’s proposal for appointment of Shri R. S. Virk, District Judge (Retd.), to examine a large number of applications, objections, grievances, and representations related to Committee’s proceedings was accepted by this Court’s order dated 15.11.2017. Subsequently, many such applications were heard and disposed by Shri R.S. Virk following due procedure. In the course of these proceedings, a large number of Interlocutory Applications (IAs) were filed by various parties before us. 7. For the purpose of disposal of the present IAs it may be stated that the Committee examined the records relating to the residential projects that PACL had at Sectors 100 and 104, Mohali, and at Bhatinda. The Committee found that Sectors 100 and 104 at Mohali, together, had 2032 units and Bhatinda had 1078 units, and that many were allotted or sold before 02.02.2016. 8. By order dated 04.03.2020, this Court, while considering I.A No. 150831 of 2023 (filed by State of Punjab) and I.A No. 105681 of 2019 (filed by Pearl City Mohali Residents Welfare Association), directed disclosure of the names of the allottees of plots in Sectors 100 and 104 in Mohali, the dates on which the respective agreements were entered into, the mode and dates of payment, and whether the original allottees remain members of the Association or have transferred their rights. Pearl City Mohali Residents Welfare Association filed an affidavit dated 04.10.2024, supplying the necessary details regarding allottees of Sector 100 and 104, Mohali. By order dated 17.10.2024, this Court asked the Committee to verify the claims with the assistance of an auditor. 9. Thereafter, the Committee, after duly considering the claims with the assistance of chartered accountants, filed a Final Report on 28.02.2025. After having considered the matter, this Court, by its Order dated 02.04.2025, requested the Committee to suggest a further course of action. 10. We have duly considered the submissions made on behalf of the Category III allottees as well as the alternatives suggested by the learned Amici. The Supreme Court is exercising extraordinary jurisdiction that it has assumed in 2016. More than a decade has passed since then, and yet, this Court has been struggling to resolve this issue. Committees have been constituted, and various reports have been submitted and considered sporadically, within the limited time this Court has. These proceedings are not in the context of regular original or appellate jurisdiction that this Court exercises. It is necessary for this Court to conclude these proceedings at the earliest and at the same time, ensure that the investors are restituted in a reasonable manner. We cannot afford to convert these proceedings into suits for specific performance or public law proceedings for restitution and rehabilitation. Taking a practical and pragmatic view, it is necessary to take difficult decisions to ensure that the monies paid are returned with reasonable interest. The primary obligation in the present proceedings is to secure, to the extent possible, the return of monies to persons whose claims have been duly examined and verified by the Committee. 23. Further, this Court cannot afford to complicate the process of securing at least the repayment of the monies invested by the applicants, together with reasonable interest, by attempting to devise mechanisms for the fulfilment of their original commercial transactions. Such an exercise may, in fact, leave the respondents without even recovering the monies they paid. Having regard to the considerable passage of time, this Court cannot hazard a probable relief where plans and directions to make balance payments, resolution of existing claims and disputes over title, transfer of unencumbered property, and creation of fresh title, will all happen with certainty. The best this Court can do now is conclude the 17 proceedings and ensure that the applicants are repaid the monies they invested, with reasonable interest. 24. In view of the above, we accept and adopt the suggestion (iii) of the Amici Note and issue the following directions – i. The Committee is directed to refund to each allottee under this Category, as mentioned in Annexure 3, 6 and 9, the amount paid by them, as certified by the auditors and recorded in the Report, with interest at the rate of 8%. ii. The refund, together with the interest payable, shall be made within three months from the date of successful auction of the concerned plot. iii. Upon payment of the aforesaid amount, the claim of the concerned allottee in respect of the plot shall stand fully satisfied, and no further claim in relation to such allotment shall survive. D. Category IV – Full or Part-payment Made in Cash (Unverified) or Payments Made Through Banking Channels after 02.02.2016 25. The Committee in its Report dated 19.04.2025 recommended that plots and allotments in Category IV, i.e., those where either full or part- payment was made through cash, and those where payments were made after 02.02.2016 through banking channels, be treated as unsold inventory and sold through public auction, with no refund being made to the applicants. The Amici Note, at para 12, supports this recommendation. 18 26. If the alleged payments made in cash by members of this Category are verifiable, then there is no justification whatsoever to bar them from adopting such legal remedies as may be available to them in law. This Court will certainly not prevent such allottees from exercising such remedies. With this concern, even though the Committee has suggested that no refund is to be made to the Category IV applications, we enquired of the Amici if they can file civil suits for recovery. 27. In compliance, the learned Amici have submitted a short note. It is suggested that a suit by an allottee is not necessarily barred. However, they pointed us towards Order dated 02.05.2016 wherein this Court allowed a prayer in IA No. 5/2026 filed by SEBI in the present Civil Appeal (13301/2015) in which they sought the following – “a) pass an order directing that no Civil Court or other Authority or Forum shall entertain any suit or other proceeding in respect of any claim or related matter(s) pertaining to PACL Ltd. and/or its Directors/ Promoters/ group Companies/ entities/ individuals etc., arraying therein as parties/ Defendants/ Respondents the Justice (Retd). R.M Lodha Committee (in the matter of PACL Ltd.), and/or its Chairman and/or its Members and/or the Securities and Exchange Board of India and further no injunction shall be granted by any Court or other Authority or Forum in respect of any action taken or to be taken by the Justice (Retd.) R.M Lodha Committee (in the matter of PACL Ltd.) and/or its Chairman and/or its Members and/or the Securities and Exchange Board of India, with respect to claims and/ or matter(s) relating to investments/ deposits etc. in/ with PACL Ltd. or its Directors/ Promoters/ Group Companies/ Entities/ Individuals etc;” 28. Therefore, they suggest that there is no embargo to maintain suits against PACL Ltd, and that the embargo is only against impleading designated statutory and monitoring entities such as Justice (Retd.) R. M. Lodha Committee, the Chairman of the Committee, the individual members of the Committee, and SEBI from being arrayed. 29. We have also considered the detailed submissions by the learned counsels appearing for Category IV allottees. They can be summarised as – 29.1 The Committee’s Report dated 19.04.2025 arbitrarily classifies purchasers based on the mode and timing of payment, without considering settled possession, genuineness of cash payments, or the actual circumstances of individual transactions. They seek physical verification of plots and supporting documents. 29.2 Transactions which have been acknowledged by PACL, accompanied by delivery of possession, cannot be reopened merely because sale deeds were not executed. Their claims involve disputed questions of fact, possession and title, which require adjudication by a competent forum rather than a paper-based verification exercise. 29.3 They propose that their claims be determined by a Retired Judge or Recovery Officer under Section 28A of the SEBI Act, following notice, hearing, physical survey and examination of evidence, with a reasoned order. Alternatively, they seek liberty to approach the competent Civil Court at Mohali for declaration and specific performance, notwithstanding the existing orders dated 02.02.2016 and 25.07.2016. 30. Having considered the matter in detail, we reject the IAs filed by Category IV allottees, who paid in cash, seeking allotment or refund. However, we are of the opinion that they will be entitled to approach the appropriate Civil Court by filing suits for recovery/declaration. To provide clarity and certainty on which contesting defendants to implead, the matter may be posted for hearing the learned Amici and SEBI, and for clarification of para 13 of the order dt. 02.02.2016. 31. In respect of Category IV applicants who made payments through banking channels, but after the freezing date of 02.02.2016, the Amici have suggested that the same view taken for Category III applicants be applied. We accept this recommendation and issue the following directions – i. The Committee is directed to refund to each allottee the amount paid by such allottee towards the concerned plot, as certified by the auditors and recorded in the Report, with interest at the rate of 8%, ii. The refund, together with the interest payable, shall be made within three months from the date of successful auction of the concerned plot. iii. Upon payment of the aforesaid amount, the claim of the concerned allottee in respect of the plot shall stand fully satisfied, and no further claim in relation to such allotment shall survive. 32. The directions in paragraph 31 above shall also apply to those individuals in Annexures 2, 5 and 8 who have made payments through banking channels after 02.02.2016. E. Category V Applicants 33.
