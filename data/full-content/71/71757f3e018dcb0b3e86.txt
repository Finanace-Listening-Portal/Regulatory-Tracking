Agriculture GNPAs could increase to Rs 1.9 lakh crore to Rs 2 lakh crore by March 2028 from Rs 1.41 lakh crore in June 2026, with most of the stress expected to emerge in FY28.The monsoon shortfall could add 70 to 80 basis points to the agriculture GNPA ratio by March 2028, with public sector banks (PSBs) likely to bear most of the increase. Including farm loans restructured in drought-hit areas that remain classified as standard assets, stressed agriculture advances could reach 6% to 6.3% of the sector’s loan book, according to CareEdge Ratings. Advt The agriculture GNPA ratio is expected to ease to 4.6% to 4.8% by March 2027, supported by strong credit growth and write-offs, before rising as unpaid kharif crop loans affected by deficient rainfall turn into NPAs. In an adverse scenario involving a poor rabi harvest, larger state loan waivers and a sharper slowdown in bank lending as interest rates rise, the ratio could reach 6.1% to 6.4% by March 2028. A good rabi crop and wider restructuring under the Reserve Bank of India’s natural calamity framework could keep it at 4.7% to 4.9%.Agriculture had the highest GNPA ratio among major sectors in June 2026, compared with 1.48% for industry, 1.47% for services and 0.96% for retail loans. Although agriculture accounted for 12.9% of SCB advances, it contributed 37.5% of their total GNPAs. PSBs held about three-fourths of agriculture GNPAs.Scanty rainfallThe 2026 southwest monsoon delivered 759.4 mm of rainfall, 87% of the long-period average of 868.6 mm, the weakest performance since 2015. The shortfall was concentrated in states with substantial farm lending: about 68% of SCB agriculture credit is in states that received rainfall at least 20% below normal, compared with 48% in 2015.Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Maharashtra and Kerala, which together account for about 48% of SCB agriculture credit, received 20% to 30% less rainfall than normal. Punjab recorded a 41% shortfall, while Haryana and Rajasthan received 23% to 24% less rainfall than normal. Four states have declared drought so far, and these states account for about a third of SCB agriculture credit. Advt The impact on bank asset quality is likely to emerge with a lag because crop loans generally become NPAs when payments remain overdue for two crop seasons, or one crop season for crops that take more than a year to grow. Other agricultural loans, including those for dairy and poultry, become NPAs after 90 days of overdue payments. Some stress could emerge from early 2027, while kharif crop loans not repaid after the 2026 harvest are likely to turn into NPAs mainly in the second half of 2027.The decline in the agriculture GNPA ratio in recent years has not reflected a comparable reduction in the stock of bad loans. The ratio fell 522 basis points from its March 2020 peak of 10.33% to 5.11% in June 2026, even as agriculture GNPAs increased from Rs 1.28 lakh crore to Rs 1.41 lakh crore. Agriculture advances more than doubled over the period, while banks wrote off 16% to 18% of the agriculture GNPAs held at the start of each year during FY23 to FY25. Advt Restructuring under the RBI’s natural calamity framework could defer recognition of some stressed loans as NPAs. Eligible loans must have been standard and not overdue by more than 30 days when the calamity occurred. Restructured loans remain classified as standard assets, subject to an additional 5% provision, and are unlikely to turn into NPAs until the moratorium ends and repayments fall overdue again, likely after March 2028.State loan waivers remain a key downside risk. Between 2014-15 and 2018-19, ten states announced farm loan waivers worth about Rs 2.4 lakh crore. The GNPA ratio rose sharply between FY18 and FY20 during this period, reaching 10.33% in March 2020. The report notes that some borrowers who could repay held back payments in anticipation of relief.
