Summary : Canara Robeco Emerging Equities Fund is not the scheme’s current name. The same underlying scheme is now Canara Robeco Large and Mid Cap Fund, and the official Scheme Information Document preserves the former name and scheme code CANA/O/E/LMF/05/02/0010. It is an open-ended equity scheme investing in both large-cap and mid-cap stocks, benchmarked to the NIFTY Large Midcap 250 Total Return Index. The stated allocation places 35% to 65% in large-cap equities and 35% to 65% in mid-cap equities, with up to 30% in other equity, debt and money-market instruments and up to 10% in InvITs, subject to the current offer document. September 2026 official data reported month-end AUM of ₹24,578.35 crore, 96.21% in equities, a very-high scheme riskometer and significant sector and issuer exposure. The exit load was 1% for redemption or switch-out within one year and nil afterwards. Costs differ between Direct and Regular plans and can change; the live TER disclosure must be checked before acting. For resident investors, equity-oriented-fund capital gains can attract 20% short-term tax or 12.5% long-term tax above the ₹1.25 lakh aggregate threshold, subject to STT and individual facts. This is a scheme guide, not an investment recommendation or return forecast. Plans, Options, Minimums, TER and Exit Load Direct and Regular plans The scheme offers Direct and Regular plans with Growth and income-distribution-cum-capital-withdrawal options described in the offer documents. The Direct plan excludes distribution commission and therefore has a lower expense ratio than the corresponding Regular plan. An investor should not infer that lower expenses remove market risk. Option labels also matter because an IDCW payment can include income distribution and capital withdrawal and is not a guaranteed dividend. Official July 2026 factsheet data showed base expense ratios of 0.49% for Direct and 1.37% for Regular. The live TER page uses the post-June 2026 disclosure framework, separating base expense ratio, brokerage, transaction cost and statutory levies, and TER can change daily. The July figures are therefore dated evidence, not a current quote. The investor should capture the live plan-specific TER on the transaction date. Minimums and exit load The July 2026 factsheet stated an initial lump-sum minimum of ₹5,000 and subsequent purchase minimum of ₹1,000, with monthly SIP starting at ₹1,000 and quarterly SIP at ₹2,000, each subject to permitted multiples and current transaction rules. The exit load was 1% when units were redeemed or switched out within one year from allotment and nil after one year. Loads are applied lot by lot and can affect switches as well as cash redemptions. The current SID, KIM and transaction statement should be checked before execution. Risk Factors Beyond the Riskometer The very-high riskometer is consistent with a predominantly equity portfolio containing both large and mid-cap stocks. Mid-cap securities can have wider price swings and lower trading liquidity than the largest companies. Active stock selection introduces portfolio-manager and concentration risk. AUM changes can affect trading needs, while large redemptions may require portfolio sales. Debt and money-market exposure, if used, introduces credit, duration and liquidity risk. InvIT exposure introduces leverage, asset-operation and distribution variability. The scheme is not a fixed deposit and does not provide capital protection, a guaranteed return or DICGC insurance. Past returns, rankings, star ratings, recent AUM growth or a successful historical name do not establish future outcomes. Investors should review the latest portfolio, rolling-period performance, drawdowns, tracking against the stated benchmark, turnover, expense disclosures and their own investment horizon rather than rely on a single month or return table. Tax Treatment for Resident Investors Because the scheme is intended to maintain equity-oriented-fund status, a resident investor’s redemption or switch can qualify for the equity capital-gains rules when statutory conditions, including STT where required, are satisfied. Units held for not more than 12 months are generally short-term. Section 111A taxes qualifying short-term capital gains at 20% for transfers on or after 23 July 2024, plus applicable surcharge and cess. Units held for more than 12 months are generally long-term, and section 112A taxes aggregate qualifying long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh at 12.5%, plus surcharge and cess. IDCW and switch transactions IDCW receipts are generally taxable in the investor’s hands at the applicable slab rate and may be subject to TDS under the current threshold and status rules. A switch from one plan, option or scheme to another usually involves a redemption and fresh purchase for tax purposes, even when no cash reaches the bank account. Each folio and tax lot should be tracked with allotment date, cost, units, load, STT, transaction value and gain. Non-residents, minors, trusts, businesses and investors using special regimes may require different analysis.
