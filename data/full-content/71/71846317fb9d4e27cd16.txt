Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Chhaganbhai Nanubhai Butani Vs ITO (ITAT Surat) Section 148 Notice for AY 2015–16 Issued During the TOLA Extension After 1 April 2021 Is Time-Barred Summary: The Surat Income Tax Appellate Tribunal quashed a reassessment for AY 2015–16 where the original notice under section 148 was issued on 24 April 2021, invoking the extension under the Taxation and Other Laws (Relaxation and Amendment of Certain Provisions) Act, 2020 (TOLA). The Assessing Officer subsequently followed the procedure under section 148A and issued a fresh notice on 31 July 2022. Applying the binding Gujarat High Court decisions and the Revenue’s concession recorded by the Supreme Court in Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal, the Tribunal held that the reassessment was barred by limitation and without jurisdiction. Facts of the Case The assessee, an individual, filed his return for AY 2015–16 declaring a total income of ₹7,67,540. Based on information concerning certain financial transactions during the relevant year, the Assessing Officer initiated reassessment by issuing a notice under section 148 on 24 April 2021. The officer relied on the extended time limit under TOLA to issue the notice after the new reassessment provisions had come into force on 1 April 2021. Following the Supreme Court’s decision in Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal, the Assessing Officer passed an order under section 148A(d) and issued a fresh notice under section 148 on 31 July 2022. The reassessment order dated 15 May 2023, passed under section 147 read with section 144B, determined the total income at ₹2,71,55,280 after making an addition of ₹2,63,87,740. The assessee challenged both the validity of the reopening and the addition before the Commissioner (Appeals), who granted no relief. Earlier Tribunal Order Recalled on the Limitation Ground The Tribunal initially dismissed the assessee’s appeal on 23 January 2026. That order was recalled on 28 October 2026 in M.A. No. 37/SRT/2025 because Ground No. 1, which challenged the reassessment as time-barred, had remained unadjudicated. On rehearing, the Tribunal took up that ground first. Its decision on limitation would determine whether the Assessing Officer had jurisdiction to make the reassessment and whether the addition required examination on merits. Supreme Court Concession and Gujarat High Court Rulings The assessee relied on Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal (2024) 469 ITR 46 (SC). In paragraph 19(f), the Supreme Court recorded the Revenue’s concession that notices issued on or after 1 April 2021 for AY 2015–16 would have to be dropped because they fell outside the period covered by TOLA. The assessee also cited two decisions in which the jurisdictional Gujarat High Court applied that position to notices for the same assessment year. In Mayurkumar Babubhai Patel v. ACIT (2025) 176 taxmann.com 25 (Gujarat), the original notices bore the date 31 March 2021, but were issued after the new regime came into force. The subsequent notices under section 148A(b), issued pursuant to Ashish Agarwal, also came after 31 March 2022. The High Court held that the fresh section 148 notices issued in July 2022 were time-barred. The decision therefore took account of the actual date of issuance as well as the timing of the subsequent proceedings. In Sorathia Mahesh Veljibhai HUF v. ITO (2025) 179 taxmann.com 54 (Gujarat), the High Court explained that, for AY 2015–16, the three-year period expired on 31 March 2019, before the period covered by TOLA, while the six-year period expired on 31 March 2022, after that period. The High Court quashed the section 148 notice issued during the TOLA extension between 1 April 2021 and 30 June 2021. Tribunal Quashes Reassessment in Full The Tribunal found that the dates in the assessee’s case brought it squarely within the Gujarat High Court rulings. The original notice dated 24 April 2021 was issued during the TOLA extension, and the consequential notice dated 31 July 2022 was issued after 31 March 2022. The Tribunal accordingly allowed the jurisdictional ground, quashed the reassessment order and allowed the appeal. The challenge to the addition on merits became infructuous. Author’s Comments The ruling illustrates why compliance with section 148A and limitation under section 149 must be examined separately. The procedure followed after Ashish Agarwal addressed how the Assessing Officer was to proceed with the transitional notice. The statutory time limit still governed whether a fresh notice could validly be issued. In this case, Rajeev Bansal and the jurisdictional High Court rulings resolved that question in the assessee’s favour. For similar transitional cases, the starting point is to place the assessment year, the actual date of issuance of the original notice and the date of the fresh notice in sequence. Mayurkumar Babubhai Patel shows why the date printed on the original notice may require verification against the record of issuance. The recall proceedings also illustrate the importance of securing a ruling on a specific jurisdictional ground. Here, adjudication of that ground disposed of the entire reassessment, including the addition of more than ₹2.63 crore. Cases Discussed 1. Union of India Vs Ashish Agarwal (Supreme Court); Civil Appeal No. 3005/2022 and connected appeals; 04/05/2022; AY: Multiple assessment years. Citation: 2022 SCC OnLine SC 543. The Supreme Court’s transitional directions were followed by the Assessing Officer before issuing the fresh notice. 2. Union of India Vs Rajeev Bansal (Supreme Court); (2024) 469 ITR 46; 03/10/2024; AY: Multiple assessment years. Also reported at 167 taxmann.com 70 (SC). The Tribunal relied on the Revenue’s concession recorded in paragraph 19(f). 3. Mayurkumar Babubhai Patel Vs Assistant Commissioner of Income-tax (Gujarat High Court); Special Civil Application No. 3154 of 2022 and allied matters; (2025) 176 taxmann.com 25; AY: 2015–16. The Gujarat High Court considered notices dated 31 March 2021 but actually issued after 1 April 2021, and the subsequent proceedings initiated after the limitation period. 4. Sorathia Mahesh Veljibhai HUF Vs Income-tax Officer (Gujarat High Court); (2025) 179 taxmann.com 54; AY: 2015–16. The Tribunal reproduced the High Court’s discussion of the Revenue’s concession and the limitation applicable to AY 2015–16. 5. Deepak Steel and Power Ltd. Vs CBDT (Supreme Court); Civil Appeal No. 5177 of 2025; 02/04/2025; AY: 2015–16. Referred to in the Gujarat High Court extract reproduced by the Tribunal. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT SURAT Feeling aggrieved by order of first appeal dated 11.07.2025 passed by learned Commissioner of Income-Tax (Appeals)-National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi [“CIT(A)”], which in turn arises out of assessment-order dated 15.05.2023 passed by learned Assessment Unit of Income-tax Department [“AO”] u/s 147 r.w.s. 144B of Income-tax Act, 1961 [“the Act”] for Assessment-Year [“AY”] 2015-16, the assessee has filed this appeal. 2. Briefly stated, the facts of case are like this: The assessee-individual filed his return of income of AY 2015-16 declaring a total income of Rs.7,67,540/- u/s 139 which was duly assessed. Subsequently, on the basis of information in his possession revealing certain financial transactions done by assessee during the relevant year, the Ld. AO issued initial notice dated 24.04.2021 u/s 148 to re-open assessee’s case u/s 147 on the strength of the time-limit extended by The Taxation and Other Laws (Relaxation and Amendment of Certain Provisions) Act, 2020 [“TOLA”]. Since a new set of provisions for re-assessment u/s 147 was introduced in Income-tax Act w.e.f. 01.04.2021, there arose controversies with regard to the issuance of notices u/s 148 after 01.04.2021 and the litigation travelled to Hon’ble Supreme Court in Ashish Agarwal (2022 SCC Online SC 543) in which the Hon’ble Supreme Court gave certain directions. Accordingly, after considering the decision of Ashish Agarwal, the Ld. AO issued a fresh notice dated 31.07.2022 u/s 148 to assessee for making assessment u/s 147 [after completing the procedure of section 148A(d)], which culminated into passing of an order of re-assessment dated 15.05.2023. By this order of re-assessment, the Ld. AO re-assessed assessee’s total income at Rs.2,71,55,280/- after making an addition of Rs. 2,63,87,740/-. Aggrieved by of re-assessment, the assessee carried matter in first-appeal and challenged the legality of re-assessment framed u/s 147 as well as the merits of addition made by AO. However, the Ld. CIT(A) did not grant any relief. Hence, the assessee has come in next appeal before ITAT. 3. Originally, this appeal was disposed of by co-ordinate Bench of ITAT, Surat vide order dated 23.01.2026 wherein the co-ordinate bench dismissed assessee’s appeal while upholding the order of CIT(A). Subsequently, the said order was recalled in M/A No. 37/SRT/2025, order dated 28.10.2026 since the Ground No. 1 raised by assessee raising a legal issue which goes to the root of the matter, remained unadjudicated. Accordingly, this appeal has come before us. 4. The Ground No. 1 raised by assessee reads as under: “1. On the facts and circumstances of the case as well as law on the subject, the learned CIT(A) has erred ion confirming the actin of assessing officer in after limitation period. The ground is covered by the decision of Supreme Court in the case of Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal 167 taxmann.com 70 (SC).” 5. Thus, in Ground No. 1, the assessee is challenging the legality of re-assessment framed by Ld. AO u/s 147. The assessee is claiming that that the Ld. AO issued initial notice dated 24.04.2021 and subsequent notice dated 31.07.2022 for making re-assessment u/s 147 for AY 2015-16 under consideration but the Income-tax Department has already conceded in subsequent decision in Union of India Vs. Rajeev Bansal (2024) 469 ITR 46 (SC) that the notices issued on or after 01.04.2021 for AY 2015-16 shall have to be dropped as they will not fall for completion during the period prescribed under “TOLA”. The relevant para 19(f) of Rajeev Bansal is re-produced below for an immediate reference: “19. (f) The Revenue concedes that for the assessment year 2015-2016, all notices issued on or after April 1, 2021 will have to be dropped as they will not fall for completion during the period prescribed under the Taxation and other Laws (Relaxation and Amendment of Certain Provisions) Act, 2020.” 6. Ld. AR for assessee submitted that following the above decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court in Rajeev Bansal, the Hon’ble Gujrat High Court has also quashed the notices issued by assessing authorities u/s 148 in (i) Mayur Kumar Babubhai Patel Vs. Assistant Commissioner of Income-tax (2025) 176 taxmann.com 25 (Gujrat) and (ii) Sorathia Mahesh Veljibhai HUF Vs. Income-tax Officer (2025) 179 taxmann.com 54 (Gujrat). The relevant paras of Sorathia Mahesh Veljibhai are re-produced below: “8. The matter was carried to Hon’ble Supreme Court. Hon’ble Supreme Court in case of Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal [2024] 167 taxmann.com 70/301 Taxman 238/469 ITR 46 (SC) decided the issues raised with regard to delay as per the provisions of section 149 of the Act which has come into operation after 01.04.2021 as well as validity of sanction granted under section 155 of the Act. 9. During the course of hearing before the Hon’ble Apex Court, Revenue conceded to the effect that so far as Assessment Year 2015-2016 is concerned, Revenue could not have issued the notices under section 3(1) of TOLA as considering the time period as prescribed under section 149 of the Act with effect from 01.04.2021, three years would be over on 31.03.2019 which is prior to coming into force of TOLA and six years would be completed on 31.03.2022 which is after operation of TOLA. In such circumstances, notices for Assessment Year 2015-2016 are held to be invalid by Hon’ble Apex Court in case of Rajeev Bansal (supra). XXX 16. This Court also in case of Mayurkumar Babubhai Patel v. Asstt. CIT [2025] 176 taxmann.com 25 (Gujarat)/Special Civil Application No.3154 of 2022 and allied matters) has held as under: “15. Considering the facts of the case, it is not in dispute that the respondent-Assessing Officer has issued the notice under section 148A(b) of the Act after the period of six years were over on 31.03.2022. As observed by the Hon’ble Apex Court in case of Deepak Steel and Power Ltd(supra) and in view of the concession made by the Revenue before the Apex Court for the Assessment Year 2015-16, all the notices issued on or after 01.04.2021 will have to be dropped as they would not fall for completion during the period prescribed under the Taxation and Other Laws (Relaxation and Amendment of Certain Provisions) Act, 2020 and therefore, nothing further is required to be adjudicated in the matters as the notice so far as the present petitions are concerned, though dated 31.03.2021, admittedly have been issued after 01.04.2021. 16. It is also not in dispute that the notices under section 148A(b) have been issued pursuant to the decision of the Hon’ble Apex Court in Ashish Agarwal (supra) dated 04.05.2022 admittedly after 31.03.2022. Therefore, on both counts, the notices issued under section 148 of the Act dated 27/28/29.07.2022 would be time barred.” 17. In view of above, for the foregoing reasons, the petition is allowed. The impugned notice issued under section 148 of the Act for Assessment Year 2015-2016 is held to be invalid as same was issued during the extended period from 01.04.2021 to 30.06.2021 under TOLA. 18. Petition is accordingly disposed off.” 7. Thus, we find that the issue is no longer res integra. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Rajeev Bansal (supra) has recorded the Revenue’s concession in para 19(f) that all notices issued on or after 01.04.2021 for AY 2015-16 are liable to be dropped. Further, the Hon’ble Gujarat High Court in Mayurkumar Babubhai Patel (supra) and Sorathia Mahesh Veljibhai HUF (supra), following Rajeev Bansal and Deepak Steel & Power Ltd., has held that notices issued after 01.04.2021 for AY 2015-16 are barred by limitation. Admittedly, in the present case, the original notice u/s 148 was issued on 24.04.2021 and the consequential notice after the decision in Ashish Agarwal was issued on 31.07.2022. Therefore, respectfully following the aforesaid binding precedents, we hold that the impugned re-assessment proceedings are barred by limitation and without jurisdiction. Hence, the impugned re-assessment order passed by Ld. AO in present case is hereby held to be bad in law and quashed. Ground No. 1 raised by assessee is thus allowed. 8. Since we have quashed the reassessment proceedings and the consequential re-assessment order itself on the ground of lack of jurisdiction, the other ground raised by the assessee challenging the additions on merits has become infructuous and is, therefore, not adjudicated upon. Further, the earlier order of the Co-ordinate Bench dated 23.01.2026, which has been re-called vide order dated 28.10.2026 in M.A. No. 37/SRT/2025, shall not subsist. 9. Resultantly, this appeal is allowed.
