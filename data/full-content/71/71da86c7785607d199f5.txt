Summary: HDFC Nifty 100 Equal Weight Index Fund is an open-ended equity index scheme seeking to replicate the Nifty 100 Equal Weight Total Returns Index (TRI), subject to tracking error. Unlike a conventional market-cap-weighted Nifty 100 portfolio, its benchmark periodically resets constituent weights towards equality, changing the sources of concentration, turnover and rebalancing risk rather than removing equity-market risk. HDFC Mutual Fund’s September 2026 Fund Facts, reporting August 2026 information, states assets under management of ₹534.40 crore, an inception date of 23 February 2022, Regular and Direct plans with Growth option, a ₹100 minimum purchase/additional purchase amount, nil entry/exit load and annualised tracking error of 0.08% for both plans. The scheme’s September Fund Facts and the AMC’s September Index Insider publication display different expense-ratio figures for the same August reporting period: 0.88%/0.42% (Regular/Direct) in Fund Facts versus 1.00%/0.55% in Index Insider. The discrepancy is disclosed rather than silently resolved; investors should use the AMC’s current statutory TER report for the relevant transaction date. The fund’s riskometer is Very High, and passive replication does not guarantee benchmark returns. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, applicable from 1 April 2026, qualifying short-term gains on equity-oriented fund units are generally taxed at 20% under section 196, while qualifying long-term gains above the aggregate ₹1.25 lakh threshold are generally taxed at 12.5% under section 198, subject to securities-transaction-tax and other statutory conditions. This article separates historical AMC disclosures from legal tax rules and contains no recommendation or return prediction. Key Takeaways Show all 25 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Scheme Identity and What “Equal Weight” Means HDFC Nifty 100 Equal Weight Index Fund is a named, open-ended passive mutual-fund scheme. Its investment objective, as stated by HDFC Mutual Fund, is to generate returns commensurate before fees and expenses with the performance of the Nifty 100 Equal Weight Index TRI, subject to tracking error. The objective is not a contractual promise to match the index or protect capital. The scheme is different from HDFC Nifty 100 Index Fund, which follows a market-cap-weighted benchmark, and different from HDFC Nifty 50 Index Fund, which follows a narrower large-company universe. How equal weighting changes the portfolio The Nifty 100 Equal Weight benchmark assigns broadly equal index weights to its 100 constituents at periodic rebalance dates. The AMC states that the benchmark is rebalanced quarterly to restore equal weights. In a market-cap-weighted index, the largest companies generally command greater weights. Equal weighting reduces single-stock weight concentration at a rebalance but can raise exposure to the smaller constituents within the Nifty 100 universe and can create more turnover when prices diverge between rebalances. The equal-weight label should not be interpreted as equal exposure at every moment. Daily market movements change actual weights, cash and corporate actions cause temporary deviations, and a passive fund may use permitted instruments to manage flows. The investor bears full equity-market risk, possible sector tilts and the operational costs of periodic index changes. The index composition itself changes under its methodology; the AMC must respond to additions, deletions and corporate actions. Tracking Error, Tracking Difference and Rebalancing Tracking error is not the same as underperformance Tracking error measures the variability of the difference between the scheme and benchmark returns over an observation period. Tracking difference describes the level of return difference between the scheme and the benchmark over a stated period. A small tracking error does not guarantee the fund earns the benchmark return; a relatively stable drag from expenses can coexist with low variability. Both measures depend on the exact plan, data window, frequency and benchmark total-return convention. The September Fund Facts reports annualised tracking error of 0.08% for Regular and 0.08% for Direct based on daily rolling returns over the previous twelve months. This is a backward-looking calculation, not a maximum tracking error for the future. Cash holdings, corporate actions, market liquidity, securities lending, rebalancing execution, taxes and transaction costs can influence tracking. The fund’s strategy is passive but still requires operational execution and controls. Equal-weight rebalance can increase turnover Quarterly equal-weight resets can require selling constituents whose market values have risen relative to peers and buying those whose relative weights have fallen. The mechanism changes portfolio weights and transaction costs. It is not a prediction that equal weighting will outperform market-cap weighting. The two benchmarks can diverge materially when a small number of the largest stocks drive the market or when the broader large-cap group moves differently. A benchmark methodology document and the AMC portfolio statement are needed for a date-specific sector or constituent analysis. Riskometer and Investor-Level Risks The scheme’s August 2026 riskometer is Very High. The label is a regulatory risk disclosure, not a probability estimate or return forecast. An investor can suffer material capital loss despite the scheme being diversified across approximately 100 benchmark constituents. Market drawdowns, valuation compression, liquidity events, concentrated sectors, index methodology changes and operational tracking deviations all remain possible. Units of an equity-oriented mutual fund generally receive the special equity capital-gains treatment if the fund meets the statutory equity-investment definition and the relevant securities transaction tax (STT) conditions are satisfied. Under section 196, qualifying short-term capital gains are generally taxed at 20% plus applicable surcharge and cess. Under section 198, qualifying long-term capital gains exceeding the aggregate ₹1.25 lakh threshold are generally taxed at 12.5% plus applicable surcharge and cess. The ₹1.25 lakh limit is an aggregate statutory threshold for eligible gains, not a separate allowance for each scheme or redemption. Units of equity-oriented funds are generally long-term when held for more than twelve months; units held for twelve months or less are ordinarily short-term. Each SIP instalment is a separate acquisition lot, so a single redemption can contain both short-term and long-term lots. STT on redemption is relevant to the statutory concessional treatment. The precise holding period, transfer date, cost and grandfathering provisions where applicable should be checked before finalising a tax return. Growth Option, Distributions and TDS The September Fund Facts identifies Growth option under both Regular and Direct plans. Growth units do not ordinarily make periodic income-distribution payments merely because the underlying portfolio receives company dividends. Those dividends and other income affect the scheme’s NAV according to fund accounting. A redemption or switch can create a taxable capital-gains event even though the investor receives no separate distribution. If any future scheme addendum changes options or distributions, the operative terms must be checked. The Income-tax Act, 2025 section 393(1) includes withholding rules for income in respect of mutual-fund units, subject to statutory thresholds and recipient status. That distribution-income withholding rule should not be casually applied as a 10% TDS on every Growth-option redemption: capital-gains withholding and non-resident payment rules must be considered separately. NRIs should also examine tax residency, applicable DTAA provisions, account route, repatriation conditions and the payer’s withholding obligations. SEBI 2026 Regulations and Mandatory Investor Disclosures The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 were notified on 14 January 2026 and came into force on 1 April 2026. The framework governs mutual-fund operations and disclosures alongside SEBI circulars and scheme documents. For a specific index scheme, the SID, KIM, portfolio disclosures, TER reports, riskometer, scheme factsheet and material addenda are the relevant primary records. A marketing presentation or older factsheet cannot supersede a newer legally operative addendum. Passive investing does not remove the need to understand the scheme’s investment objective, replication limits, benchmark composition, permitted derivatives, expense rules, securities lending policy, liquidity and tax consequences. The scheme name does not make the investment low-risk or tax-exempt. Neither SEBI registration of the mutual fund nor benchmark licensing is an assurance of performance, principal protection or suitability for any particular investor. Accounting and Recordkeeping for Investors An individual holding mutual-fund units as investments should maintain the unit allotment statements, consolidated account statements, bank payments, SIP dates, cost of each lot, redemptions, STT evidence and capital-gain statements. For a company or other reporting entity, accounting depends on the applicable framework and business purpose. AS 13 may be relevant under Indian GAAP; entities applying Ind AS should evaluate Ind AS 109 classification, fair-value measurement and income-statement/OCI treatment as appropriate. The tax computation and accounting carrying value need not be identical. A reconciliation should distinguish units purchased, bonus or corporate-action units if any, switches, redemptions and closing units. Expense ratios are reflected in scheme NAV and should not be duplicated as an investor-level deduction from capital gains. A switch between schemes can be a transfer for tax purposes even when the money remains within the same fund house. Any scheme-specific exemption from exit load is a separate commercial matter from income-tax treatment.
