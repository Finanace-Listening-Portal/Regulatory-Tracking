Articles 3 min read SBI-led consortium advances Vodafone Idea loan; NaBFID to approve ₹4,000 cr, PNB and Union Bank to decide Vodafone Idea's proposed Rs 35,000 crore consortium financing has made further advances, with atleast Punjab National Bank, Union Bank of India expected to decide its exposure by the end of the month, bankers aware of the discussions have told ETBFSI. Infrastructure financier NaBFID is also expected to approve a Rs 4,000 crore exposure shortly. Advt SBI has completed its appraisal of the proposed financing and shared the assessment with other consortium members, which will use it as the basis for their respective internal evaluations and approvals.A person familiar with PNB's assessment said the bank would participate in the proposed lending arrangement, although the amount it would commit had not yet been finalised. The bank is awaiting the appraisal details from SBI to take the matter through its internal approval process.The proposed financing is part of Vodafone Idea's broader funding plan to support its network expansion and capital expenditure requirements. The telecom operator has outlined a Rs 45,000 crore capital expenditure programme over three years to strengthen its 4G coverage and accelerate its 5G rollout.Under the consortium lending arrangement, SBI, as the lead bank, undertakes the appraisal of the proposed facility and shares its assessment with other participating lenders. However, each bank is required to take its own credit decision and sanction its respective exposure.Once the participating lenders finalise their respective limits and complete their internal sanction processes, the consortium will move towards joint documentation, including execution of the necessary financing agreements. The lead bank will then coordinate the release of the facility in consultation with the other lenders. Advt During Vodafone Idea’s Q4 FY26 earnings call in May, CEO Abhijit Kishore had said the company was deeply engaged with an SBI-led consortium comprising public sector banks, private banks and foreign lenders, and was confident of closing the discussions quickly. The proposed debt package comprises Rs 25,000 crore in funded facilities and Rs 10,000 crore in non-funded facilities.During the company's Q1 FY27 earnings call in August, Kishore provided a further breakdown of the funding arrangement, identifying three lender groups, six to seven public sector banks led by SBI, Indian private-sector banks and foreign banks through external commercial borrowings (ECBs). Advt He had said that the company had made substantial progress across the three funding channels and had raised its first Rs 6,400 crore tranche, including Rs 1,183 crore through warrant proceeds and debt funding, including non-funded facilities from foreign and Indian private lenders.
