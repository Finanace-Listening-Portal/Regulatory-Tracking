Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 14, 2026 at 08:32 AM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 134231425; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Public sector banks have raised billions of dollars through the Reserve Bank of India's special FCNR(B) deposit window, with several lenders mobilising significantly more than their initial targets as the scheme drew a stronger than expected response from overseas depositors, even though the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) swap window closed a month prior. Second largest public sector lender Bank of Baroda has mobilised around USD 8 billion through FCNR(B) deposits, exceeding its initial target of USD 4-5 billion, as per a senior bank official. While Bank of India has raised around USD 2.33 billion against its earlier target of USD 1.2 billion. Central Bank of India, which had targeted USD 400 million, has mobilised around USD 760 million. Bank of Baroda's latest mobilisation is nearly twice the upper end of its original target. Advt The lender planning to use most of the funds to replace high cost bulk deposits and bonds while taking a balanced approach to incremental lending. “We are sitting having debts at 7.3, 7.4, debts means bulk deposits and also bonds retiring, bonds falling due, coming down. And this fund is available to us at 6.5, then to add operational expenses 6.6 say max,” Bank of India had told ETBFSI.Bank of India’s guidance on credit growth has remained unchanged at 15-17 per cent despite the additional liquidity. The incremental funds are expected to be utilised by the end of the third quarter of FY27.Indian Bank has also mobilised around USD 2.3 billion through FCNR(B) deposits under the special concessional swap facility. The funds are being used for rupee liquidity, lending and reducing the bank's reliance on bulk deposits, according to people aware of the development.The mobilisation comes against a record response to the RBI's special USD-INR swap facility. Banks mobilised USD 127.22 billion through FCNR(B) deposits by August 31, accounting for the overwhelming majority of the USD 136.37 billion raised through the three components of the facility. Overseas foreign currency borrowings contributed USD 5.26 billion, while external commercial borrowings accounted for USD 3.89 billion. Advt The RBI had initially allowed banks to mobilise fresh FCNR(B) deposits under the concessional swap facility until September 30. However, with FCNR(B) mobilisation touching USD 52.3 billion by August 13, the central bank advanced the closure of the window by a month to August 31, citing the “encouraging response” and resultant forex inflows.For Bank of Baroda, the mobilisation is substantially higher than the target the lender had outlined in July. The bank had initially targeted USD 4-5 billion through FCNR(B), Medium Term Notes and overseas foreign currency borrowings by September-end. MD & CEO Debadatta Chand had told ETBFSI that the FCNR(B) inflows were expected to replace nearly Rs 17,000-18,000 crore of bulk deposits. Advt Central Bank of India has similarly mobilised around USD 760 million against its original target of USD 400 million, nearly twice the amount it had planned to raise. The lender had initially expected to achieve the target by September-end, but the RBI's early closure of the FCNR(B) window brought the mobilisation timeline forward.Central Bank's mobilisation was driven by its International Banking Unit at GIFT City, through which it offered leverage against FCNR(B) deposits. The bank had around 75,500 NRI customers with nearly 1.97 lakh accounts and had set up dedicated NRI desks and outreach mechanisms to drive mobilisation. Advt SBI Chairman Challa Sreenivasulu Setty, who said the large FCNR(B) inflows should not result in abnormal lending. He said banks would take three to four months to deploy the funds and emphasised that lending would remain responsible.
