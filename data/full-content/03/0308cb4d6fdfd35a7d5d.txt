Advt Read more: Indian exporters push for early US trade deal, see 17% tariff giving edge over Asian rivals"Even if you're getting a concession of 17 per cent, which is giving you a 1 per cent advantage over Southeast Asian economies, they are not going for the FTA," Sahai said.He highlighted that countries such as Vietnam could potentially secure lower tariff rates through their own trade negotiations with the US, thereby putting Indian exporters at a competitive disadvantage."So I think the task is not easy for the negotiators. Every industry would like the best option for them," he said.Sahai suggested that the government should prioritise sectors based on their contribution to India's exports and employment generation while negotiating sector-specific tariff concessions with the US.Read more: How Donald Trump's proposed fresh tariff salvo for India compare with rival nationsHe also sought clarity on whether the proposed duties would be imposed if the bilateral trade agreement is concluded at a tariff rate of 17 per cent and whether existing exemptions for sectors such as pharmaceuticals and smartphones would continue.On the potential financial impact of the proposed tariffs, Sahai estimated that Indian exporters could face an additional burden of around 8 per cent if the US imposes a 25 per cent tariff and the historical practice of sharing the tariff burden continues."In the past, in most of the cases, it was a principle of one-third, one-third, one-third. One-third was shared by the Indian exporter, one-third by the importer, and one-third by the consumer," he said. Advt "If that applies and assuming the tariff is 25 per cent, probably an additional burden of 8 per cent will come," he added.While businesses with higher profit margins may be able to absorb the additional costs, exporters operating on thin margins could face difficulties in continuing shipments to the US, he said.However, Sahai noted that Indian textile and leather exports for the upcoming summer fashion season were unlikely to face an immediate impact, as shipments had either already been dispatched or were scheduled to be shipped by February.Responding to US allegations of excess manufacturing capacity in India, Sahai argued that Indian manufacturers were expanding production capacity to meet future domestic demand rather than to dump products in overseas markets. Advt "When industry is creating capacity, they have vision for 10 years, 15 years down the line," he said, questioning the rationale for imposing tariffs across export sectors over alleged excess capacity in a few industries.Sahai urged exporters not to panic and called for using the available window for negotiations and dialogue with the US."Let the industry not panic. And the good thing is the industry is not panicking. They are concerned, and their concern is quite genuine also. Let us use whatever two months' window is available for further negotiation and dialogue," he said.
