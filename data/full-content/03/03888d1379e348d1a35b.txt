Summary: The National Financial Reporting Authority has stated that Form NFRA-2 for the reporting period 2025-26 must be filed by 30 November 2026. Rule 5 of the NFRA Rules, 2018 places the annual-return obligation on every auditor referred to in Rule 3, so the first task is to map every audit report signed for an entity within NFRA’s governed classes to the correct filing auditor. This guide explains the scope screen, return-level data controls, review of network and engagement information, portal preparation, submission evidence and treatment of uncertain or incomplete records. It does not assume that every statutory auditor files NFRA-2, or that a firm can safely omit a governed engagement because a client or prior-year list did not flag it. The notice directs filers to the NFRA-2 e-form portal. This is a current compliance event, not a general annual-calendar estimate or an extension inferred from an earlier year. Rule 5 of the National Financial Reporting Authority Rules, 2018, as amended in 2019, requires every auditor referred to in Rule 3 to file the annual return in Form NFRA-2 on or before 30 November every year. The form is an auditor return, not a company annual filing. The reporting file should therefore be organised around the auditor or audit firm and the audit reports falling in the relevant reporting period, with entity-level data feeding the return. It also covers an unlisted public company meeting any prescribed financial threshold: paid-up capital of at least ₹500 crore, annual turnover of at least ₹1,000 crore, or aggregate outstanding loans, debentures and deposits of at least ₹500 crore as at 31 March of the immediately preceding financial year. Each limb should be tested separately because satisfying one threshold is sufficient; a turnover conclusion cannot cure an omitted borrowing test. Regulated sectors, statutory bodies and referred entities The governed classes also include insurance companies, banking companies, electricity-generation or supply companies, companies governed by a special Act, and specified bodies corporate incorporated by an Act. The 2019 amendment clarifies the banking-company expression for this purpose. Rule 3 further reaches a company, body corporate, person or class referred to NFRA by the Central Government in public interest. The engagement team should record the category relied on instead of using a single yes-or-no field without reasons. Foreign subsidiaries and associates require a consolidation test A body corporate incorporated or registered outside India can be within scope when it is a subsidiary or associate of an Indian governed entity and its income or net worth exceeds 20 per cent of the consolidated income or consolidated net worth of that Indian company or body corporate. The test requires current consolidation data and relationship analysis. An overseas entity should not be excluded merely because its local statutory audit is performed outside India. A billing ledger alone can miss no-fee work, group instructions, component assignments or reports billed in a different period. Conversely, a client master may contain dormant entities for which no report was issued. The reconciliation should retain both additions and exclusions with documented reasons. For a firm with branches or network arrangements, establish whether the legal filing auditor is the individual, partnership, Indian LLP or foreign LLP identified in the appointment and report. The form seeks identity, registration and contact information and engagement-level particulars. Network branding does not by itself decide which legal person reports the engagement. Changes in firm constitution, merger, PAN, registration number or contact person should be matched to effective dates and portal records. Resolve duplicates and joint audits explicitly Joint audits, reissued reports and multiple reports for the same entity can create apparent duplicates. A control key should combine the governed entity, financial period, report type, signing date and filing auditor. The team should determine whether each record represents a separate report or a corrected version. Deleting a duplicate without retaining the original-to-final trail can make the return irreconcilable to the signed-report archive. Validate return fields with engagement evidence, not estimates NFRA-2 captures extensive information about the auditor and the audit practice, including governed entities, audit reports and professional arrangements. The preparer should use a controlled data workbook with source-owner, source-document, reviewer and exception fields. Numeric totals must reconcile across the entity schedule and the return, while descriptive responses should be checked against engagement letters, independence records, quality-management documentation and signed reports. If a fee, staffing or report field cannot be supported, the answer is not to insert a convenient approximation. Escalate the gap, obtain the best available primary record and document the basis permitted by the form. Prior-year responses should be used only as a comparison because the reporting population, engagement partner, fees, hours, modifications and firm particulars may have changed. A roll-forward control should highlight every copied field for confirmation. Confirm that the relevant auditor profile, email and mobile access are current; test the required authentication and signing process; and check whether the portal accepts the prepared data format. Browser, credential or validation errors discovered on the deadline day can consume the review window. Technical issues should be supported by screenshots, timestamps, error text and helpdesk correspondence. A successful upload is not the same as a completed filing. The authorised person should review the final rendered form or preview, confirm declarations, complete submission and preserve the acknowledgement, service-request or reference number and a copy of the filed return. The acknowledgement should be tied back to the reviewed data file with a locked version number so that later corrections are not confused with the submitted population. Post-filing review closes the compliance loop After filing, inspect the acknowledgement for the correct auditor and reporting period, archive evidence and update the statutory-compliance register. Any incomplete or inconsistent submission should be escalated against the portal’s available correction mechanism and the Rules. NFRA has previously published provisional lists of firms that did not file or filed incomplete forms, demonstrating that submission quality and completeness are distinct from merely attempting access. A defensible NFRA-2 file records judgments as well as the form The closing file should contain the current NFRA deadline notice, Rules 3 and 5, the scope matrix, report population reconciliation, entity-threshold evidence, data workbook, reviewer sign-offs, portal screenshots, filed form and acknowledgement. It should also identify unresolved matters and the person responsible for follow-up. This package allows the firm to explain why an engagement was included or excluded without reconstructing the decision from memory. Where scope is genuinely uncertain, obtain technical or legal advice before omitting the engagement. A client’s view that it is outside NFRA, an absence from a provisional list, or a prior-year omission is not determinative. The governing facts and the current Rules control. The final reviewer should confirm that the two same-day editorial topics and the deposit article have no factual overlap with this compliance return; NFRA-2 is an auditor-regulation filing, not a tax return or financial-product disclosure. Frequently Asked Questions on NFRA-2 for 2025-26 Who files Form NFRA-2? Every auditor referred to in Rule 3 must file the annual return under Rule 5. The filing obligation is attached to the auditor or audit firm, not filed as an ordinary company ROC return. What is the official last date for the 2025-26 reporting period? NFRA’s current notice states 30 November 2026. Does every statutory auditor have to file NFRA-2? No. The engagement must relate to a company or body corporate within Rule 3. The scope decision should be documented from current facts. Is every unlisted public company covered? No. The Rule 3 financial thresholds and other governed categories must be tested. Meeting any applicable threshold is sufficient. Can the prior-year return simply be rolled forward? No. It is a comparison aid only. The population, report dates, entity facts, fees, firm particulars and other fields must be freshly verified. What evidence proves filing? Retain the submitted-form copy and the portal acknowledgement or reference number tied to the final reviewed dataset. What if a portal error occurs near the deadline? Capture time-stamped evidence and raise the issue through the official support route immediately, but do not assume a technical ticket automatically extends the statutory deadline. Key Takeaways NFRA has expressly fixed 30 November 2026 as the NFRA-2 deadline for reporting period 2025-26. Rule 3 scope must be tested engagement by engagement before compiling the auditor-level return. Reconcile signed reports to engagement, client and billing records; do not rely on one system. Validate every material field with current evidence and independently review carried-forward information.
