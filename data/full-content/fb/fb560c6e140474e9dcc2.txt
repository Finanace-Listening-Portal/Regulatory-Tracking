(File Pic)Subrata Panda Mumbai 3 min read Last Updated : Nov 19 2026 | 11:03 PM IST @media (min-width:1100px) and (max-width:1280px) { .storymeta .meta-info { flex: 0 0 45%; } } Listen to This ArticleMis-selling of financial products often stems from sales targets and variable pay structures rather than actions at the branch level, said S C Murmu, deputy governor (Dy Guv) of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). He urged financial institutions to examine whether their incentive structures reward sales volumes or customer outcomes, and how these are measured. “Look hard at incentives. Mis-selling rarely begins at the branch counter. It begins with a sales target and a variable pay scheme. Ask what your incentives actually reward: volumes or customer outcomes. And ask how you measure each,” Murmu said at the 11th annual Gatekeepers of Governance Summit 2026 in Mumbai. Mis-selling has remained a concern across India’s financial sector, particularly in the distribution of insurance policies, and mutual fund (MF) and other third-party products through banks and financial institutions. Murmu also highlighted the significant regulatory changes awaiting banks from April 1, 2027, when the new prudential framework is scheduled to come into effect. The transition will involve the introduction of expected credit loss (ECL)-based provisioning, implementation of the remaining Basel III standards, and adoption of a new standardised approach to credit risk. Under the ECL framework, banks will have to make provisions for losses they expect to incur rather than only for losses that have already materialised. The framework will replace the existing incurred-loss-based provisioning approach. The changes will make the banking system more sensitive to risk and place greater responsibility on boards, Murmu said.With the shift towards principle-based regulation, boards will have greater flexibility in determining their institutions’ risk appetite. Murmu also highlighted the importance of investments in technology and people, saying these were no longer optional for financial institutions. He also pointed to changes in the RBI’s governance directions for banks, which came into effect on October 1, 2026.
