The drawdown has reduced the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) intervention buffer, but the remaining stock is still substantial. The central bank has stepped up measures to ease dollar demand and curb speculative activity, helping the rupee rise nearly 1 per cent to Rs 95.77 per dollar on October 12. The key question is how long the RBI can sustain its defence if high oil prices and capital outflows continue to weigh on the currency. Advt The reserves fell by $12.95 billion in the week ended October 2, following a decline of $18.34 billion in the previous week. Foreign currency assets, the largest component, dropped by $10.66 billion to $604.75 billion, while gold reserves fell by $2.29 billion to $106.41 billion. The four consecutive weekly declines underline the pressure on India's external balances, although changes in the dollar value of foreign currency assets and gold can also reflect valuation effects rather than actual intervention.At $734.60 billion, India's reserves remain a substantial cushion against external shocks. The government estimated in April that reserves of $700.9 billion provided around 11 months of import cover, based on the balance of payments. That estimate is a useful benchmark, rather than a current calculation: the latest reserve figure is for October 2, and import cover changes with both reserves and the import bill. The Department of Economic Affairs' April review also showed that reserves covered 94 per cent of external debt outstanding at end-December 2025.More recent RBI data put India's external debt at $778.2 billion at end-June 2026, up $15.4 billion from end-March. At end-June, the ratio of reserves to total external debt stood at 85.9 per cent, down from 90.6 per cent at end-March. These debt and reserve-adequacy measures use different reference dates from the October reserve figure, but they show why the pace of reserve depletion matters alongside the headline stock.The RBI cannot treat the entire reserve stock as an amount available to sell without limit. Intervention has to be weighed against future import payments, external debt obligations, capital flows and the need to retain confidence in India's capacity to meet foreign currency requirements. Nor does every dollar decline in reported reserves represent a dollar sold in the market: valuation movements in non-dollar assets and gold also affect the published figures. Advt RBI shifts from intervention to reducing dollar demandOn October 10, the RBI announced measures intended to ease pressure on the rupee. From October 12, it began meeting the daily dollar requirements of Indian Oil Corporation, Bharat Petroleum and Hindustan Petroleum through a special arrangement, moving a major source of recurring dollar demand away from the spot market. It also tightened rules governing certain foreign exchange derivative transactions, including restrictions on rebooking cancelled contracts and a reduction in the threshold for transactions without underlying exposure.The measures helped trigger a sharp recovery in the rupee on October 12 with the currency strengthening by about 1 per cent to Rs 95.77 per dollar, its strongest single-day rally in six months. The move came after a period of sustained pressure from high oil import costs and foreign portfolio outflows. The rally, however, does not by itself establish that the underlying demand for dollars has eased permanently. Advt What could test the RBI's firepower?The outlook depends heavily on crude oil prices, global interest rates and foreign investment flows. Higher oil prices increase India's dollar requirements, while elevated US yields can make dollar assets more attractive and weigh on capital flows to emerging markets. Continued foreign portfolio selling would add to the demand for dollars and could force further intervention or additional policy measures.The RBI's recent steps may reduce immediate spot-market pressure, but they cannot indefinitely offset persistent external imbalances. A durable easing would require a combination of lower dollar demand, stronger foreign currency inflows and more supportive global conditions. If those factors fail to improve, the central bank could face a trade-off between slowing the rupee's depreciation and preserving its reserve buffer. Advt For now, the reserves give the RBI meaningful capacity to smooth disorderly currency movements. The more important indicators to watch are the pace of future reserve declines, the rupee's response to the new measures and whether oil-related dollar demand and capital outflows begin to moderate.
