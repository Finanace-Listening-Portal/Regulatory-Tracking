Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Ashok George Vs State Tax Officer (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madras High Court, in Ashok George Vs State Tax Officer, dismissed a writ petition challenging an ex-parte GST assessment order dated 15 December 2025 for FY 2021-22 under Section 73 of the TNGST/CGST Act, 2017. The petitioner, a former director of Ankasummor Foods Private Limited, contended that the company’s business operations had ceased in 2024 due to severe financial distress and that the assessment proceedings initiated in 2025 had culminated in an ex-parte order. The principal grievance was that, while reconciling Form GSTR-9C, the assessing authority had allegedly treated the company’s consolidated PAN-India turnover as turnover attributable exclusively to its Tamil Nadu GST registration. According to the petitioner, turnover relating to separate GST registrations in Karnataka and Telangana had been wrongly included, potentially resulting in double taxation. The petitioner maintained that tax on the actual Tamil Nadu turnover of ₹5.03 crore had already been discharged, whereas the disputed assessment quantified CGST of ₹45,34,291 and SGST of ₹45,34,291, aggregating to ₹90,68,582. The petitioner therefore sought quashing of the assessment and remand for fresh consideration. The respondent submitted that the statutory show-cause notice and reminders had been served through the GST portal and that physical service had also been attempted through registered post. Examining the record, the High Court found that Form GST DRC-01 had been uploaded on 26 September 2025, followed by three reminders, including a notice dispatched through RPAD on 24 October 2025, which was returned undelivered on 4 November 2025. The Court held that closure of business did not relieve a registered taxpayer of the obligation to inform the jurisdictional GST authority and formally seek surrender or cancellation of registration. It observed that the petitioner could not rely on its own failure to comply with these obligations as an excuse for non-participation in assessment proceedings. The Court also appreciated the respondent’s efforts to effect physical service and considered the petitioner’s failure to receive the notices indicative of an attempt to avoid service. Regarding the alleged inclusion of turnover belonging to other States, the High Court observed that the petitioner’s claim concerning the balance-sheet turnover of ₹11.46 crore and the tax already discharged on Tamil Nadu turnover involved disputed questions of fact. Such matters required examination of returns, records and supporting evidence and could not appropriately be adjudicated in extraordinary writ jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution. The Court declined to grant an automatic remand merely because the assessment had been completed ex parte, particularly when statutory notices had been issued and reasonable steps taken to serve them. Holding that the petitioner should challenge the quantified demand before the statutory Appellate Authority, the Court dismissed the writ petition without costs. It nevertheless allowed W.M.P. No. 49852 of 2026, dispensing with production of the original impugned order, and closed the connected miscellaneous petition. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF MADRAS HIGH COURT The present Writ Petition has been filed challenging the assessment order passed by the respondent, along with the consequential demand notice issued in Form GST DRC-07 bearing Reference No. ZD331225210769F dated 15.12.2025. 2. The learned counsel appearing for the petitioner submits that the petitioner closed its business operations in the year 2024 due to severe financial distress. Consequently, the assessment proceedings initiated in the year 2025 culminated in the impugned ex-parte order. He further submits that the petitioner was not in a position to pay the alleged tax liability. It is contended that as far as the taxable turnover within the State of Tamil Nadu is concerned, the petitioner has already discharged its tax liability. The learned counsel argues that the impugned assessment order suffers from a patent jurisdictional error; while reconciling Form GSTR-9C, the respondent erroneously treated the PAN-India consolidated turnover of the entire company, including turnovers relating to distinct GST registrations in Karnataka and Telangana, as though it represented the turnover of the Tamil Nadu GST registration alone. If the impugned order is permitted to stand, it would result in double taxation, compelling the petitioner to pay CGST and SGST in Tamil Nadu on the very same turnover upon which GST has already been discharged in Karnataka and Telangana. Such an assessment is wholly beyond the territorial jurisdiction of the GST authorities of the State of Tamil Nadu. Therefore, he prays that the impugned order be set aside and the matter be remanded to the respondent for fresh consideration on merits. 3. Per contra, the learned Additional Government Pleader appearing for the respondent submits that show-cause notices and reminders were duly served upon the petitioner through the GST portal as well as via RPAD. Hence, no fault can be attributed to the respondent. He, however, submits that subject to the petitioner depositing 25% of the disputed tax amount, this Court may consider remanding the matter to the respondent for fresh consideration. 4. Heard the learned counsel appearing for the petitioner and the learned Additional Government Pleader (Taxes) appearing for the respondent, and perused the materials available on record. 5. On a perusal of the record, it is manifest that the respondent uploaded the Show Cause Notice in Form GST DRC-01 on the common portal on 26.09.2025. Thereafter, three reminders were issued, including a physical notice sent via RPAD on 24.10.2025, which was returned as “undelivered” on 04.11.2025. 6. The primary contention of the learned counsel for the petitioner is that the business was closed down in the year 2024. In the event a business is closed down, a statutory duty is cast upon the taxpayer to intimate the status of closure to the jurisdictional authority concerned and to formally apply for the surrender/cancellation of the GST registration. The petitioner cannot plead its own omission as an excuse to evade assessment. The respondent has taken all reasonable and legal steps to serve the notice by uploading it on the portal and by dispatching it through RPAD. This Court appreciates the diligence shown by the respondent in attempting service via physical post. The failure to receive these documents points towards a deliberate attempt by the petitioner to avoid service for reasons best known to it, presumably to evade the tax liability. 7. The petitioner now contends that the PAN-India turnover was erroneously taken into consideration. During the course of arguments, it was submitted that the petitioner has discharged tax on the actual Tamil Nadu turnover amounting to Rs.5.03 Crores. However, in the impugned proceedings, the respondent authority has quantified the tax liability at Rs. 45,34,291/- towards CGST and Rs. 45,34,291/- towards SGST. Even in an ex-parte assessment, unless the regular tax liability is fully discharged, the calculations are bound to reflect the returns on record. Here, the tax amount stands quantified based on the available data. The petitioner disputes this quantification and attempts to argue the merits of the case on factual grounds. Furthermore, regarding the balance sheet reflecting a turnover of Rs. 11.46 Crores, the petitioner claims this belongs to branches outside Tamil Nadu. These are highly disputed factual matrices that cannot be adjudicated by this Court in the exercise of its extraordinary writ jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India. 8. This Court is also not inclined to accept the request for remand, particularly in view of the fact that the respondent had uploaded the statutory notice on the GST portal and had also taken steps to serve the same through RPAD. The petitioner, having failed to respond to the notices and having not taken appropriate steps to intimate the closure of its business to the GST authorities, cannot, at this stage, seek an automatic remand of the proceedings. Under such circumstances, this Court declines to remand the matter. The appropriate course of action available to the petitioner to challenge the quantified assessment is to prefer a statutory appeal before the Appellate Authority. 9. With the above observations, this Writ Petition stands dismissed. There shall be no order as to costs. W.M.P. No. 49852 of 2026 filed to dispense with the production of the original impugned order dated 15.12.2025 is allowed. Consequently, connected Miscellaneous Petition is closed.
