A 30-second to one-minute window is becoming more realistic as both payment infrastructure and fraud networks accelerate.The problem is particularly acute with mule accounts. Money received from a victim can be rapidly transferred through several accounts before reaching the wider fraud network, making recovery increasingly difficult once the funds have moved beyond the first receiving account.India recorded 24.02 lakh financial cyber-fraud complaints involving Rs 22,495 crore in 2025, according to government data. Advt From rules to behaviourThe discussions pointed to a shift away from conventional rule-based monitoring towards systems that assess customer and transaction behaviour in real time.A fraudulent transaction may not look suspicious on its own. However, a sudden change in device, transaction velocity, beneficiary behaviour or links with other accounts can provide signals that the payment is part of a larger fraud network.This is making fraud detection increasingly a network-level problem, requiring banks and other financial institutions to combine information about accounts, devices, mobile numbers and transaction patterns.The RBI has been developing tools in this direction. Its Innovation Hub developed MuleHunter.ai, an AI/ML-based system designed to identify potential mule accounts, and the central bank has said the tool will be expanded across more banks.Real-time intelligence becomes criticalGovernment initiatives are also moving towards prevention rather than recovery.The Department of Telecommunications' Financial Fraud Risk Indicator (FRI) allows financial institutions to assess in real time the likelihood that a mobile number is associated with cybercrime or financial fraud. Banks and UPI service providers are using the risk signals to flag or stop transactions involving high- and very-high-risk numbers.The government said FRI had helped prevent suspected fraud losses of Rs 5,043.73 crore by August 2026, up sharply from Rs 660 crore in its first six months.The system draws intelligence from multiple sources, including citizen reports, the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal, telecom operators, banks and financial institutions, and shares risk information across the financial ecosystem.False positives remain a challenge Advt Faster intervention, however, also raises the risk of blocking legitimate transactions.Banks cannot simply stop every payment that deviates from a customer's normal behaviour. Excessive alerts and transaction blocks could undermine the convenience that has driven the rapid adoption of digital payments.The challenge, therefore, is to identify combinations of risk signals that justify intervention while allowing legitimate transactions to proceed.This becomes even more important as fraudsters use increasingly sophisticated techniques, including AI-enabled impersonation and deepfakes, while payment systems themselves become faster.For banks, the implication is clear: fraud prevention is moving from a largely post-transaction investigation to a real-time risk-management function.
