CIT’s Approval Cannot Validate Section 148 Notice Issued Beyond Three Years Under Section 151(ii): ITAT Jodhpur Summary: The Jodhpur Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held that prior sanction from the specified authority under Section 151(ii) is a mandatory jurisdictional requirement for issuing a Section 148 notice beyond three years from the end of the relevant assessment year under the law applicable to the case. Since the Assessing Officer (AO) obtained approval from the Principal Commissioner of Income-tax (Pr. CIT), who was not a specified authority for that period, the Tribunal quashed both reassessments. Facts of the Case The lead appeal was filed by Smt. Devashree Chhaparwal as the legal heir of Late Shri Manish Chhaparwal. The Assessing Officer reopened the assessment for AY 2019-20 on the basis of information alleging that a donation to the Manavadhikar National Party was an accommodation entry through which the assessee had claimed a deduction under Section 80GGC. The Assessing Officer issued a show-cause notice under Section 148A(b) on 30 March 2023. After rejecting the objections to reopening, the Assessing Officer passed an order under Section 148A(d) and issued a notice under Section 148 on 6 April 2023. The legal heir subsequently informed the Assessing Officer that the assessee had died on 4 July 2022 and offered the original return in response. Relying on search findings and statements recorded in the cases of the recipient political parties, the Assessing Officer disallowed the Section 80GGC deduction and made a further ₹60,000 addition for estimated commission at 3%. The CIT(A) upheld the reassessment and the additions. Date of Section 148 Notice Determined the Competent Authority The assessee argued that the Section 148 notice dated 6 April 2023 was issued after three years had elapsed from the end of AY 2019-20. Approval had been obtained from the Pr. CIT (Central), Jaipur, although the notice required sanction from an authority specified under Section 151(ii). The Revenue relied on the search findings to support the disallowance of the donation claim. The Tribunal examined the notice and assessment records and confirmed that the Pr. CIT had granted the approval. Under Section 151(ii), as applicable on the notice date, the specified authorities were the Principal Chief Commissioner, Principal Director General, Chief Commissioner or Director General, subject to the statutory conditions. The approval from the Pr. CIT therefore failed to satisfy the statutory requirement. Sanction From the Specified Authority Is a Jurisdictional Safeguard The Tribunal held that where Parliament designates the authority competent to sanction reopening, the statutory designation must be strictly followed. Approval from an authority outside Section 151(ii) cannot confer jurisdiction on the Assessing Officer to issue a valid notice under Section 148. The Bench relied on the Delhi High Court decisions in Ganesh Dass Khanna v. ITO and Communist Party of India (Marxist) v. Income Tax Department on the requirement for sanction from the competent authority. Both Appeals Allowed on Jurisdictional Grounds The ITAT quashed the reassessment in the lead appeal and applied the same reasoning to Smt. Devashree Chhaparwal’s separate appeal, which involved identical facts and issues. Both appeals were allowed. The merits of the Section 80GGC deduction and the estimated commission additions were left open as the reassessments had been quashed on jurisdictional grounds. First, we take up assessee’s appeal ITA No.243/Jodh/2026 as filed by the legal heirs of the deceased assessee for Assessment Year (AY) 2019-20. This appeal is directed against an order dated 23.01.2026 as passed by the Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), Udaipur-2 [CIT(A)] in the matter of an assessment as framed by Ld. AO u/s 147 on 27.03.2024. 2. From case records, it emerges that the assessee filed regular return of income on 29.10.2019 at Rs.20.65 Lacs. Subsequently, pursuant to receipt of certain information alleging that the assessee obtained accommodation entry in the form of donation to political party namely Manavadhikar National Party (MNP) on 18.01.2019 and claimed deduction u/s 80GGC, the case was reopened and show-cause notice was issued by Ld. AO u/s 148A(b) on 30.03.2023. The assessee’s objection to reopening stood rejected vide order u/s 148A(d) on 06.04.2023 and reopening notice u/s 148 was issued on 06.04.2023. The legal heir of the deceased assessee, vide letter dated 18.04.2023, informed that her husband left for heavenly abode on 04.07.2022 and offered the original return of income as filed by the deceased assessee. 3. During the course of assessment proceedings, upon being confronted, the allegations of Ld. AO were opposed by way of written submissions on behalf of the deceased assessee. However, going by the search findings in the case of payee political parties and statements recorded therein, the deduction claimed by the assessee u/s 80GGC was denied. On such donation, Ld. AO estimated commission expenditure of 3% to make another addition of Rs.0.60 Lacs while framing the assessment. 4. The Ld. CIT(A) disposed-off first appeal on 23.01.2026. However, a corrigendum order was passed on same date to correct discrepancies in uploading of order. The Ld. CIT(A) rejected all the legal grounds as well as confirmed assessment on merits against which the assessee is in further appeal before us. 5. The Ld. AR, at the outset, raised a pertinent legal ground to state that reopening approval has been accorded by Ld. Pr. CIT whereas as per extant statutory provisions of Sec.151, such approval was required to be accorded by Ld. Pr. CCIT since the case was reopened beyond three years. To support the same, our attention has been drawn to order passed by Ld. AO u/s 148A(d) and reopening notice issued u/s 148 on 06.04.2023 which has been kept in the paper-book. The Ld. Sr. DR, on the other hand, stated that search findings clearly indicated the assessee made fake donations and made a wrongful claim which should be upheld. 6. Since the legal issue contest the very validity of assessment proceeding, we take up the same first. We find that the assessee has challenged the validity of the reassessment proceedings on the legal ground that the notice issued u/s 148 was without valid sanction of the specified authority as prescribed u/s 151(ii) of the Act. It is contended that the impugned notice was issued after expiry of three years from the end of the relevant AY (AY being 2019-20 and notice u/s 148 issued on 06.04.2023) whereas the Assessing Officer had obtained approval from the Principal Commissioner of Income-tax (Central), Jaipur (Pr. CIT), instead of the Principal Chief Commissioner of Income-tax (Pr. CCIT), as mandatorily required under the applicable statutory provisions. 7. The relevant provisions of Sec.151 as amended by the Finance Act, 2021, w.e.f. 01.04.2021 specifically provides for two different authorities depending upon the period elapsed from the end of the relevant assessment year. The sub-section (i) envisages approval of specified authority in case of reopening within 3 years. However, sub-section (ii) provides that to reopen the case beyond three years, the specified authority to accord such approval of reopening would be Principal Chief Commissioner or Principal Director General or where there is no Principal Chief Commissioner or Principal Director General, Chief Commissioner or Director General. It is quite apparent that the approval granted by the Pr. CIT is not as per the mandate of law. The statute makes a clear distinction between the authorities competent to grant approval in the two situations. 8. In the present case, admittedly, the relevant assessment year is 2019-20 and notice u/s 148 has been issued on 06.04.2023 which is beyond three years from end of the said assessment year. In such a situation, in terms of Section 151(ii) of the Act, the approval for issuance of the notice was required to be obtained from the Principal Chief Commissioner of Income-tax or the other competent authority specified therein. However, upon perusal of the notice issued u/s 148 of the Act and the relevant assessment records, we find that the Assessing Officer has obtained approval from the Principal Commissioner of Income-tax (Pr. CIT) and not from the Principal Chief Commissioner of Income-tax (Pr. CCIT) as required. In our considered opinion, the approval so obtained does not satisfy the mandatory requirement of Section 151(ii) of the Act. The expression “specified authority” used by the Legislature cannot be interpreted loosely or extended to include an authority which has not been designated under the relevant statutory provision. When the Legislature has specifically prescribed the authority competent to grant sanction, such statutory prescription is required to be strictly complied with. The requirement of obtaining prior approval from the specified authority is not a mere procedural formality. It is a mandatory jurisdictional safeguard imposed by the Legislature to ensure that the power of reopening an assessment after the expiry of three years is exercised only after due consideration and approval by the higher-ranking authority designated under the statute. Consequently, approval granted by an authority not specified u/s 151(ii) cannot confer jurisdiction upon the Assessing Officer to issue a valid notice u/s 148 of the Act. 9. Our view is duly supported by the recent decision of Hon’ble Delhi High Court in the case of Ganesh Dass Khanna v/s ITO (191 Taxmann.com 945) holding that where notice u/s 148 and order under section 148A(d) were issued beyond three years from end of relevant assessment year and prior approval was granted by Principal Commissioner instead of Principal Chief Commissioner as mandated by section 151, such approval was not valid and notice and order were to be quashed. The Hon’ble Court followed its earlier decision in the case of Communist Party of India (Marxist) Vs. ITD (174 Taxmann.com 925). Respectfully following the same, we quash the assessment order and allow this first and foremost legal ground as urged by Ld. AR. The assessee succeeds on legal grounds. The impugned addition stands deleted. All the other grounds have been rendered mere academic in nature. The appeal stand allowed. ITA No.244/Jodh/2026 10. The facts in this appeal are pari-materia the same. The donations made by the assessee stood disallowed along with commission income. The assessment stood confirmed by Ld. CIT(A) against which the assessee is in further appeal before us on similar grounds. Facts being identical, taking the same view, we quash the assessment order. Our adjudication, as above, would mutatis mutandis, apply to this appeal also. Conclusion 11. Both the appeals stand allowed in terms of our above order.
