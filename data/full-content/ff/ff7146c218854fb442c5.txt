The three lenders have recorded compound annual growth rates of 22-33% in disbursements and 29-55% in assets under management (AUM) over the past two years, albeit on a relatively low base.The challenge for these emerging lenders is to replicate the growth trajectory of Bajaj Finance without compromising asset quality or returns. Their current valuations of 1.6-2.6 times one-year forward price-to-book value and 15.3-21.1 times forward earnings remain substantially below Bajaj Finance's multiples during its high-growth phase in FY14-19, when its AUM grew at more than 30%. The comparison and stock rankings are based on balance-sheet resilience and profitability, with capital adequacy and asset-quality buffers taking precedence over growth, according to a report by Elara Capital. Advt The strengthsThe three lenders have benefited from access to relatively low-cost capital, supported by strong parentage, with their Tier-I capital levels ranging between 14.1% and 19.3%. Their cost of borrowing is competitive with industry leaders or declining, providing room to expand lending. The brokerage expects operating leverage, supported by expanding distribution networks and improving branch productivity, to be a key driver of profitability as the lenders scale up their businesses.Branch vintage is emerging as an important determinant of operating efficiency. Urban branches with an operating history of five-six years are approximately 18 times as productive as branches less than six months old and eight times as productive as those aged between six months and one year. More than 50% of the branch networks of Piramal Finance and Aditya Birla Capital are less than four years old, while over 80% of Poonawalla Fincorp's branches are less than three years old, largely reflecting its expansion in gold lending.Despite investments in distribution and technology, the three lenders have reduced their operating expenditure-to-AUM ratios by 20-115 basis points over the past two years. Their lending businesses' PPOP return on AUM is expected to improve by 30-220 basis points over FY26-29, indicating the potential for operating leverage as newer branches mature and contribute more to business volumes.Asset quality remains central to whether the lenders can sustain their expansion. Retail lending conditions have improved, with non-banking financial companies, including housing finance companies, recording a 15-105 basis point decline in 31-90 day delinquencies across key retail segments over the past two years, barring auto loans excluding two-wheelers. NBFCs have also recorded a 6-65 basis point greater improvement than the broader industry in personal loans, gold loans and two-wheeler financing. Advt The improving industry trend is broadly reflected in the asset-quality performance of the three lenders, although their relatively young loan books have yet to undergo a prolonged credit cycle. Piramal Finance has maintained broadly stable credit costs and Stage 2+3 assets, with both indicators remaining within a relatively narrow corridor of approximately 40-50 basis points over the past eight quarters in its growth businesses. Aditya Birla Capital and Poonawalla Fincorp have also recorded improvements in asset-quality indicators, with the report citing ranges of approximately 35-200 basis points and 145-290 basis points, respectively. Advt Piramal Finance stands out for its relatively conservative lending mix, with secured loans accounting for approximately 79% of its AUM. The lender has also seen declining disbursement yields across around 90% of its unsecured exposure, which the report views as favourable for customer selection. Its standard asset cover in growth businesses stands at approximately 1.3%, while an additional capital adequacy buffer of around 760 basis points provides further balance-sheet protection.The valuation gap with Bajaj Finance nevertheless reflects a substantial difference in profitability. During FY14-19, Bajaj Finance combined AUM growth of more than 30% with a return on assets of 3.5% and return on equity of 20%. The three emerging lenders are currently valued at 1.6-2.6 times forward book value, against 4.6 times for Bajaj Finance, while their forward price-to-earnings multiples range between 15.3 and 21.1 times compared with 25.7 times for the established lender. Advt Their price-to-earnings growth ratios of 0.2-0.5 times also compare with 0.6 times for Bajaj Finance during its earlier high-growth phase. The valuation discount indicates that the emerging lenders have yet to establish the profitability and consistency associated with Bajaj Finance's established franchise, despite their growth ambitions.The movement in Stage 2+3 assets and credit costs will remain important monitorables as the lenders expand their retail franchises. Key risks include asset quality underperforming industry trends, weaker loan growth, lower-than-expected operating cost synergies and regulatory changes weighing on profitability.
