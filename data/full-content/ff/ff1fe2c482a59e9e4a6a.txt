A bond is a debt instrument through which an investor lends money to a government, company, or other entity for a specified period. In return, the issuer pays periodic interest, known as the coupon, and repays the principal amount on maturity. Bond investments are commonly used to generate regular income, preserve capital, and reduce overall portfolio volatility. Government Bonds Government bonds are issued by the Central Government or State Governments to raise funds for public expenditure. Since they are backed by the government, they are generally considered one of the top bond investment options. Key features Backed by the Government of India or State Governments Lower credit risk compared to most corporate issuers Fixed interest payments in most cases Available in different maturities Suitable for conservative investors Advantages High level of credit safety Regular income through coupon payments Suitable for long-term wealth preservation Considerations Offer relatively lower yields than corporate bonds Market prices may fluctuate if sold before maturity Corporate Bonds Corporate bonds are issued by companies to raise capital for business operations, expansion, or refinancing existing debt. Since these bonds carry varying levels of credit risk, they often offer higher potential returns than government securities. Key features Issued by public and private companies Available across different credit ratings Fixed or floating coupon options Stock exchange listing and tradability in the secondary market Advantages Potential for higher yields Wide range of issuers and maturity options Opportunity to diversify across sectors Considerations Credit risk depends on the issuer’s financial strength Lower-rated bonds may carry a higher risk of default Investors should review credit ratings and financial metrics before investing in the potential top corporate bond options Non-Convertible Debentures (NCDs) Non-Convertible Debentures (NCDs) are debt instruments issued by companies that cannot be converted into equity shares. They generally offer fixed interest payments and have a predetermined maturity date. NCDs may be secured or unsecured, depending on the terms of the issue. Investors should review ratings assigned by recognised credit rating agencies before investing. Yield Compare the bond’s yield instead of focusing only on the coupon rate. Yield provides a better indication of the bond’s overall return based on its market price. Investment Horizon Choose bonds with maturities that align with your financial goals and liquidity requirements. Interest Rate Risk Bond prices generally move inversely to interest rates. Longer-duration bonds tend to experience greater price fluctuations when interest rates change. Liquidity If you may need to sell your investment before maturity, consider bonds that have relatively higher trading volumes in the secondary market. Which Bond Investment Option Should You Choose? The right bond investment depends on your financial objectives and risk appetite. Investors prioritising capital preservation may prefer government bonds. Those seeking higher income potential may consider high-quality corporate bonds. Investors looking for potentially higher fixed returns may evaluate NCDs after assessing the issuer’s creditworthiness. Rather than relying on a single bond category, many investors choose to diversify across different issuers and maturities to balance risk and return. Conclusion Government bonds, corporate bonds, and non-convertible debt all have different investment objectives. A government bond is safer in terms of credit rating, but a corporate bond/NCD may have higher return potential for a higher credit risk. Knowing the issuer’s financial strength, credit rating, maturity and yield may help you make better investment decisions.
