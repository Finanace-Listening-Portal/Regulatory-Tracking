The company conducts bureau checks on all gold loan customers.The lender plans to expand its gold loan network to around 400 branches, with new outlets expected to break even in about 13 months.Poonawalla Fincorp is avoiding volatile segments such as microfinance, agriculture, two-wheeler and micro-LAP lending, citing credit, political and regulatory risks.The company aims to grow 35-40% in FY27, with gold loans, branch expansion and cross-selling supporting growth. Cross-selling is expectedto contribute 4-5% to disbursement growth over time.Poonawalla Fincorp is building its gold loan business around longer-tenor, equated monthly instalment (EMI)-based products, with about 56% of its gold loan book linked to three-year repayment schedules, unlike the predominantly short-tenor bullet repayment model followed by non-banking finance companies (NBFCs). The company also conducts bureau checks on all gold loan customers as it seeks to expand its secured lending portfolio while maintaining tighter underwriting standards.The strategy is part of Poonawalla Fincorp's broader shift towards secured lending, with gold loans emerging as a key growth product. The company plans to drive expansion through new branches and higher productivity at existing outlets, while investing in radio frequency identification (RFID) technology and AI-based tracking. It expects to have around 400 gold loan branches, with new branches projected to break even in about 13 months, JM Financial said in a report following a meeting with the company's senior management. Advt Poonawalla Fincorp's three-year EMI-based gold loans contrast with the industry's predominantly bullet repayment products, which typically have tenors of three, six or 12 months. Management said about 56% of its gold loan book comprises three-year EMI-based loans, while all gold loan customers undergo bureau checks. The company has also indicated that other NBFCs are increasingly advising borrowers to opt for longer-tenor products.The lender charges interest rates of 16-16.5% on its three-year EMI-based gold loans, while the weighted average yield on its gold loan portfolio is about 17.5%. Management identified branch proximity as the key driver of customer acquisition in the gold loan business, rather than yield.Boosting infrastructureThe company is also investing in branch infrastructure and technology to support the expansion. Gold loan branches typically have three to four employees, with most recent hiring in the company directed towards this business. The planned expansion is expected to be supported by improved productivity at existing branches.The RBI's 75% loan-to-value (LTV) requirement is also catching out lenders relying on bullet-repayment gold loans, with the management notes flagging an impact on Stage 2 assets. The report, however, does not quantify the impact on individual lenders.Poonawalla Fincorp is avoiding several lending segments that management considers highly volatile, including microfinance, agriculture-related loans, two-wheelers and micro-loans against property (micro-LAP). Management cited political and regulatory risks and potential write-offs among the reasons for avoiding these segments. Advt It has separately flagged risks in micro-LAP, where losses are recognised over two years, compared with six months for personal loans. Management believes this longer recognition period can obscure the underlying risk of the product, which it considers riskier than unsecured lending despite being treated as secured by the industry.The company is targeting growth of 35-40% in FY27, with gold loans, branch expansion and cross-selling expected to support the next phase of growth. Management expects the recently established cross-selling engine to contribute 4-5% to disbursement growth over time as it deepens relationships with existing customers.
