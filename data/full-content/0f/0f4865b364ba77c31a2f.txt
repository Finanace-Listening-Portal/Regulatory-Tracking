Articles 4 min read HDFC Bank may sell down, securitise home loans at right time as price competition intensifies: Jagdishan HDFC Bank’s home loans will remain a “main pillar” for customer acquisition, outgoing CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan said, as intense price competition has made the product quite commoditised, and that Anup Bagchi (incoming CEO) and his team potentially exploring a “far more alternate strategy on selling down or securitising” home-loan assets at the right time. And with a brand of HDFC, I think this is something that is going to be the main pillar and product for customer acquisition going forward. Because along with it comes a long relationship, sticky primary relationship, which is what is going to salivate over the medium to long term,” Jagdishan said during HDFC Bank’s Q2 FY27 media call on Saturday. Advt However, the bank has been unwilling to chase home-loan growth purely through pricing, given the intensity of competition in the segment.“But having said that, I think somewhere down the line, the price competition has been rather intense on this particular product. It's become quite commoditized. So we have been happy to sort of let some of these deals go where the margin doesn't make any sense. We are not necessarily competing on the price front,” he said.HDFC Bank’s mortgage book stood at Rs 9.21 lakh crore as of September 2026, growing 7.5 per cent year-on-year and 2.2 per cent sequentially. Mortgages accounted for around 28 per cent of the bank’s advances mix in September, according to its Q2 FY27 investor presentation.The bank’s gross advances stood at Rs 32.20 lakh crore at the end of September, up 16.3 per cent year-on-year, while advances under management were at Rs 33.07 lakh crore, up 15.3 per cent.Jagdishan said the economics of the home-loan relationship extend beyond the loan itself, with customers acquired through mortgages increasingly using HDFC Bank for other products and their primary banking needs.“Almost about 95 plus percentage of our home loan customers who we have acquired over these three years have got multiple products and surely a savings account. And the balances in the savings account build has been extremely much higher than the normal savings account that we would have done,” he said. Advt At the portfolio level, including the home-loan book inherited from HDFC Ltd, around 60% of customers now pay their EMIs through HDFC Bank accounts, he said.“So, you know, almost about as we speak at a portfolio level, including the ones that we have inherited from HDFC Limited, almost about 60 percent of them pay EMI through our HDFC bank accounts now. So that is a pretty healthy one,” Jagdishan said.This, according to Jagdishan, is the reason the bank remains committed to the home-loan business despite the pressure on pricing.“We will stay in this space, but we will be very careful about what we need to do in terms of being competitive or should we compete with some of the banks which have a very low pricing as well. Having said that, you're right, what we are looking for is the relationship of the upsell to a home loan customer,” he said. Advt Sell-down could change pricing equationJagdishan said incoming CEO Anup Bagchi and his team could explore a more active asset sell-down or securitisation strategy, subject to market conditions.“We probably, and Anup is here, Anup and his team probably will think about trying to have far more alternate strategy on selling down or securitizing some of the home loan assets as in when we see the right market at the right time,” he said.A consistent ability to offload home-loan assets could allow HDFC Bank to compete more aggressively with larger lenders on pricing, Jagdishan said.“So, if that happens and if there is a strategic and a very consistent offload that is possible, then I'm sure then they will be rather intense in competing with some of the larger banks, even from a pricing perspective,” he said. Advt For now, however, the bank remains conservative on the pricing it is willing to offer because a significant portion of the home-loan book continues to sit on its balance sheet.“But as of now, since we are holding a lot on our balance sheet, we are a little bit conservative in terms of what is the threshold pricing that we would not like to go below,” Jagdishan said.HDFC Bank continues to operate with a substantially larger mortgage franchise following the merger with HDFC Ltd. The investor presentation shows that mortgages accounted for 29 per cent of the bank’s advances mix in September 2023, shortly after the merger, and 28 per cent in September 2026.
