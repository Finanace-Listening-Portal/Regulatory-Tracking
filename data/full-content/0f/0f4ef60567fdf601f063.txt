Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); RR Polymers Vs Commissioner of GST & Central Excise (CESTAT Chennai) Summary: The Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), Chennai, in M/s RR Polymers Vs Commissioner of GST & Central Excise, set aside a service tax demand raised on early-payment discounts received by the assessee from its principal, M/s Haldia Petro Chemicals Ltd. The Revenue had treated the discounts as consideration for Business Auxiliary Service (BAS) under Section 65(19)(iv) of the Finance Act, 1994, alleging that the assessee was providing procurement-related services to customers by making payments to the supplier before collecting the corresponding amounts from buyers. The Tribunal held that the statutory provision did not cover cash discounts in the manner alleged by the Department and that the essential ingredients of procurement of inputs for a client had not been established. It further found that the extended period of limitation could not be invoked merely because the relevant receipts were identified during scrutiny of the assessee’s financial records. The assessee, M/s RR Polymers, was engaged in providing two categories of services to M/s Haldia Petro Chemicals Ltd., namely Clearing and Forwarding (C&F) Agency services and Del Credere Agency services. Under the C&F arrangement, RR Polymers received, stored, warehoused and forwarded High Density Polyethylene belonging to Haldia Petro Chemicals. Under the Del Credere Agency arrangement, it procured orders from prospective buyers, while Haldia supplied the goods directly to those customers. The assessee received a commission of ₹550 per metric tonne for C&F services and ₹350 per metric tonne for Del Credere Agency services. It maintained that service tax was regularly discharged on these commissions. Separately, the supplier offered cash discounts where payments were made before the stipulated due dates. During an audit, the Department noticed that RR Polymers had received early-payment incentives which were reflected as discount receipts in its financial accounts. The Revenue alleged that, in certain transactions, the assessee paid Haldia Petro Chemicals on behalf of the ultimate buyers before receiving payment from those buyers and retained the resulting discounts. According to the Department, this arrangement amounted to a separate activity of procuring goods for customers, over and above the assessee’s existing C&F and Del Credere Agency services. The Department also noted that the assessee used overdraft facilities and enhanced bank credit limits to make advance payments to the supplier. On this basis, it treated the discounts as consideration for BAS and issued a show cause notice dated 19 October 2012, invoking the extended period of limitation for the disputed period from 1 April 2007 to 31 March 2011. The Revenue contended that the nature of a transaction, rather than the nomenclature adopted in the books of account, determined its taxability. It alleged that the assessee’s early payments effectively facilitated procurement of goods for customers and that the discounts retained by the assessee constituted remuneration for such services. The Department further asserted that the assessee had not disclosed the incentive receipts in its ST-3 returns and had thereby suppressed material facts. The adjudicating authority accepted this reasoning and concluded that the discounts represented taxable consideration under Section 65(19)(iv) of the Finance Act, 1994. The demand was subsequently upheld in the appellate proceedings, resulting in the appeal before CESTAT. The assessee disputed the Department’s characterisation of the transactions. It submitted that its commission income was separately identifiable and had already suffered service tax. The early-payment discounts were conditional benefits arising only when the assessee made payments to the supplier before the due date. For this purpose, it sometimes used borrowed funds through overdraft arrangements, thereby incurring bank interest. The assessee argued that the discount compensated for the financing cost and commercial risk associated with making early payments and could not automatically be treated as consideration for a separate taxable service. It also maintained that such discounts were not earned on every invoice and that the Department had failed to establish any direct connection between the discounts and an identifiable procurement service rendered to customers. The Tribunal examined the scope of Section 65(19)(iv) of the Finance Act, 1994, which dealt with procurement of goods or services that were inputs for a client. It observed that the Department had not identified the particular inputs allegedly procured for the client or demonstrated how the assessee’s advance payment activity satisfied the statutory description. The Tribunal further noted that there had been no cross-verification of payments made by RR Polymers to Haldia Petro Chemicals against the dates on which the ultimate customers made their payments. Consequently, the Department had not established that the early-payment incentives arose from a taxable procurement activity. The Tribunal held that merely advancing payment to a supplier did not, by itself, constitute procurement of inputs for a client within the meaning of BAS. On limitation, the Tribunal observed that the disputed period extended from 1 April 2007 to 31 March 2011, whereas the show cause notice was issued only on 19 October 2012. The Department had invoked the extended period under Section 73(1) of the Finance Act, 1994 on the allegation that the assessee had knowingly suppressed receipt of the incentives. However, the Tribunal found that the Department had itself discovered the relevant receipts from scrutiny of the assessee’s annual financial records. It held that the circumstances did not justify an allegation of suppression, particularly when the transactions were reflected in the financial records and the Department had not otherwise established the taxability of the receipts. The Tribunal also noted the absence of a specific and logical finding explaining why the early-payment incentives were liable to service tax beyond the bare assertion that they fell under BAS. Accordingly, the CESTAT Chennai allowed the appeal and set aside the impugned order. The Tribunal concluded that the Revenue had failed both to establish the substantive service tax liability on the early-payment discounts and to justify invocation of the extended period of limitation. Consequential benefits, if any, were directed to follow in accordance with law. The decision illustrates that the classification of a receipt as taxable consideration requires examination of the actual activity performed and the relevant statutory definition. It also reinforces that the extended limitation period cannot be sustained merely by asserting suppression where the material transactions are traceable to the assessee’s financial records and the Department has not established the necessary basis for alleging deliberate non-disclosure. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CESTAT CHENNAI Brief facts as are forthcoming from the OIO are as under: M/s.RR Polymers are providing services to M/s.Haldia Petro Chemicals Ltd. (hereinafter referred to as M/s.Haldia) under two categories i.e. (i) C&F Service and (ii) Del Credere Agents Service [DCA]. – As a C&F Agent, M/s.RR polymers was providing the services of receiving, storing/warehousing and clearing / forwarding of good i.e. High Density Polyethylene belonging to M/s.Haldia. – As a Del Credere Agent, they procured orders from the prospective buyers (final customers) on behalf of M/s.Haldia and M/s.Haldia delivered the goods directly to the buyers. M/s.RR Polymers was paid with commission by M/s.Haldia for the services rendered by them. In respect of rendering service in the category of ‘C&F Agent’, they were paid a commission of Rs.550/- per MT. In respect of rendering service in the category of ‘Del Credere Agent’, they were paid a commission of Rs.350/- per MT. 2. The case of the Revenue is that from the record of the assessee and during the course of audit of their accounts, it was found that the assessee had received early payment incentive from their suppliers. The OIO also reveals that there was an Agreement of Consignment Stockiest and as per the Del Credere Agreement with M/s.Haldia; all discounts, as per M/s.Haldia’s Policy, claimed by the appellant on behalf of the customers in respect of sales made should be passed on immediately to the customers by the appellant on receipt of the credit advice from M/s.Haldia. The Revenue appears to have found out that in certain cases, the appellant itself would make payments to M/s.Haldia on behalf of end-customers and thereby retain the discount given by M/s.Haldia meant for its customers, for which the payment by the customers was received at a later date. It was thus the case of the Revenue that, the above activity of the appellant making payments to M/s.Haldia by due date on behalf of ultimate customers and the collection of the same from the customers at a later date by the appellant, had resulted in the job of procurement of goods for their customers from M/s.Haldia, in addition to the activities of rendering service as C&F Agent and Del Credere Agent to M/s.Haldia. It was further observed by the Revenue that the appellant had also raised temporary loans through O.D. facility, raised credit limits with banks and settled the payments in advance for which, the collection was made from the customers at a later date, which appeared to be the early payment receipts shown in the P&L Account as discount receipts on which the appellant was liable to pay service tax under the category of “Business Auxiliary Service” [BAS for short]. Accordingly, a SCN dt. 19.10.2012 came to be issued by invoking the extended period of limitation for the reasons that the information relating to receipt of incentive in the name of discounts was within the exclusive knowledge of the appellant; the appellant had fully and knowingly suppressed the above facts from the knowledge of the department by not declaring the receipt of incentive in its ST–3 returns. In its reply, the noticee/appellant appears to have replied that they were the approved Consignment Stockiest Agent as well as Del Credere Agent for M/s.Haldia, they would get commission at fixed rates on which they were discharging their service tax liability regularly, 2.1 It was contended that as DC Agent type of sale, they procured orders from customers and M/s.Haldia would send the goods directly to the buyers; the customers would make payment directly to M/s.Haldia and for procuring orders, they were paid a commission of ₹350 per MT; 2.2 In so far as their consignment agency was concerned, they were paid a commission of ₹550 per MT of High Density Polyethelene; 2.3 In respect of both the above types of service/sales, settlement of price to the supplier was their responsibility and that the supplier offered cash discount if payment was advanced before the due date. 2.4 From the various clauses of the Agreement some of which are extracted in the Order-in-Original, there is no dispute between the parties. The only grievance of the Revenue is that in relation to procurement of goods for customers, the appellant was providing a facility of settling payments to M/s.Haldia first and collecting from its customers at a later date, which activity was felt to be covered under ‘BAS’ under the provisions of Section 65(19)(iv) of the Finance Act, 1994. The same was felt appropriate since according to the Revenue, the nomenclature of the discount or a ledger head was not important, rather what was important was the nature of transaction. Revenue further suspected that the early payment incentive or other discounts were not the discounts offered by the service provider to the service recipient on provision of services but in fact, the consideration given by the service recipient to the service provider which were reflected as income for the service provider was earned for the provision of service. There is also an observation as to the admission of the Appellant that it took advantage of cash discounts offered by M/s.Haldia, they raised temporary loans through OD facility and raised credit limits with banks, settled payments in advance, etc. for which the appellant was receiving early payment incentives regularly, which was credited to the discount receipts and payments account. The above discussion prompted the Adjudicating Authority to conclude that the appellant had provided service to its customers in relation to procurement of goods and received considerations from M/s.Haldia by keeping the discounts meant to final customers and hence the early payment discount and incentive received by the appellant for provision of the services fell under ‘Business Auxiliary Service’ and hence the shortcut notice was correctly issued demanding the service tax thereon. 3. Heard Sri Satish Chandrasekaran, ld. Advocate for the appellant and Smt. O.M. Reena, ld. Addl. Commissioner, for the Respondent; we have carefully perused the orders of lower authorities as well as the SCN. 4. The issue to be decided is, “whether the Revenue was justified in invoking the extended period of limitation in fastening the service tax liability under BAS on the appellant ?”. 5. Ld. Advocate appearing for the appellant would contend that para 20 of the OIO itself indicates that the Revenue has not disputed the fact that they had raised temporary loans through OD facility and raised credit limits with banks and settled the payments in advance and only later on, collected the price from the final customers so that the appellant would get the cash discount. The early payment discount would be adjusted towards the bank interest which they availed as OD, which was not possible for the appellant to pass on the benefit to the end-customer as they had to pay the appropriate interest on the said amount. In any case, the early payment was paid by the appellant only and had the appellant received the advance from such end customers, only then would it have passed on the early payment discount to such end customers. Moreover, the early payment discount received from its principal could not be equated with commission so as to attract service tax liability since, admittedly, the appellant had discharged the service tax liability on the commission which was received separately. Hence, there was no link between the commission paid to the appellant at fixed rates for the sale amount and cash discount offered in the name of incentive for making early payments. Early payments were not made in every bill and only in such cases where they made early payment, such discounts were offered which would happen only when they received sale amount in advance or at an early date from the end-customers. 6. Per contra, ld. Additional Commissioner relied on the findings in the impugned order. 7. We find that it is not the case of the Revenue that in every case where the early payment incentive is received, the end-customers had in fact made payments in advance or at an early date. But there is no cross verification as to the payments made in advance by the appellant vis-à-vis the payments made by the end customers. 8. It is a case where the Revenue is charging the appellant for the amount received as early payment incentive under ‘BAS’, but Section 65(19)(iv) does not cover any discounts in the form of cash. Moreover, the above section covers ‘procurement of goods or services’ which are inputs for the client, but here, what is that input is not specifically brought out on record in order to fasten the tax liability on the appellant. Mere passing of the payment, that too in advance would not ipso facto amounts to ‘procurement of inputs’ for the client. 9. The period of dispute is from 01.04.2007 to 31.03.2011, for which the show cause notice dated 19.10.2012 came to be issued, by invoking the larger period of limitation, for which the finding given in the OIO is that the receipt of incentive was noticed only on the scrutiny of annual financial records of the appellant. The same was therefore held to have been knowingly suppressed, to justify the issuance of the show cause notice under Section 73(1) of the Finance Act, 1994. When clearly the above facts are picked up from the scrutiny of the financial records of the appellant, there cannot be an allegation as to suppression since the suppression would be of something which was not disclosed anywhere in the financial records, which was unearthed by the Revenue, which is not the case here. There is also no specific logical finding that the early payment incentive received by the appellant was liable to service tax other than mentioning that the same was liable to service tax under BAS. Hence, we are of the view that the Revenue has failed to make out a case for invoking the larger period of limitation and that too, to demand service tax which the appellant was not established to be liable to pay. In view of the above discussion, we do not find any merit in the demand based against the appellant which came to be upheld in the order and hence, the impugned order deserves to be set aside which we hear by do. 10. In the result, the appeal stands allowed with consequential benefits, if any as per law.
