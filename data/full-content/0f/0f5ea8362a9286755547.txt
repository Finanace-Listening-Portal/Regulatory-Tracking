(GST AAR Telangana) Summary: Muthu Mohan Doss P., a Hyderabad-based trader in human hair, sought an advance ruling on the GST treatment of raw, unworked and unsorted hair collected from Telangana temples. Under the public tenders or e-auctions, the successful bidder pays a lump sum for a fixed period and collects whatever hair devotees offer during that period. The tenders fix neither a guaranteed quantity nor a price per kilogram. Against tender notices requiring payment of 18% GST on the bid amount, the applicant asked whether the transaction was a supply of goods or services, whether any exemption applied, and whether the lump-sum structure affected the exemption. The Authority admitted the application after examining the records and hearing the applicant’s representative on 17 November 2026. The applicant submitted that the bid amount paid for the hair itself. Ownership passed on collection, and the bidder then removed the hair and traded in it. On that basis, the applicant characterised the right to collect as the means of delivery and the uncertain quantity as the purchaser’s commercial risk. It claimed classification under HSN 0501, specifically Customs Tariff Item 0501 00 10, and cited the 2017 central and integrated tax exemption notifications along with the corresponding Telangana notification. The Authority examined Serial No. 20 of Notification No. 10/2025-Central Tax (Rate), which exempts intra-State supplies of unworked human hair, whether or not washed or scoured, and waste of human hair. It observed that the entry imposes no additional conditions. It also referred to two Madras High Court orders concerning temple tenders for collecting human hair, including the 2025 order that interfered with the clause requiring auction participants to pay 18% GST and the November 2026 order reiterating that position. The Authority ruled that procurement under the tender agreement was a supply of goods under HSN 0501 and that the exemption applies where the goods answer the description in the notification. It separately confirmed that the procurement of unworked and unsorted hair remains exempt where the bid is a lump sum for a fixed period, the quantity is neither specified nor guaranteed, and no per-kilogram price is fixed. Cases Discussed Muthu Mohandoss v. The Commissioner, Hindu Charitable and Religious Endowments & Ors. — W.P. No. 44706 of 2026 and W.M.P. Nos. 50203 and 50205 of 2026, decided on 2 November 2026 (Madras High Court). The Authority cited this order as reiterating the earlier ruling against requiring auction participants to pay 18% GST for collection of human hair. Muthu Mohandoss v. The Commissioner, HR&CE & Ors. — W.P. No. 20200 of 2025, decided on 6 June 2025 (Madras High Court). The Authority reproduced the finding that participants in the Arulmigu Renugambal Amman Temple auction could not be required to pay 18% GST and that the Court interfered with Clause 23 of the auction notice. M/s. Muthu Mohan Doss P., Hyderabad – 500033, Telangana (GSTIN: 36AJQPM2007G1ZA) has filed an application in FORM GST ARA-01 under Section 97(1) of TGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 104 of CGST/TGST Rules. 2. At the outset, it is made clear that the provisions of both the CGST Act and the TGST Act are the same except for certain provisions. Therefore, unless a mention is specifically made to any dissimilar provisions, a reference to the CGST Act would also mean a reference to the same provision under the TGST Act. Further, for the purposes of this Advance Ruling, the expression ‘GST Act’ would be a common reference to both CGST Act and TGST Act. 3. It is observed that the queries raised by the applicant fall within the ambit of Section 97 of the GST ACT. The Applicant enclosed copies of challans as proof of payment of Rs. 5,000/- under SGST and Rs.5,000/- under CGST towards the fee for Advance Ruling. The Applicant has declared that the questions raised in the application have neither been decided nor are pending before any authority under any provisions of the CGST/TGST Act’2017. The application is, therefore, admitted after examining it and the records called for and after hearing the applicant as per section 98(2) of TGST Act’2017. 4. BRIEF FACTS OF THE CASE: 4.1 M/s. Muthu Mohan Doss P., Hyderabad having GSTIN: 36AJQPM2007G1ZA, Plot No. 257/A, Road Number 72, Prashasan nagar, Hyderabad, Hyderabad, Telangana – 500033. 4.2 The applicant M/s. Muthu Mohan Doss P. are in the human hair trade. They take part in public tenders / e-auctions conducted by temples for the right to collect the hair offered by devotees (tonsured hair). They bid a lump-sum amount for a fixed tender period, and during that period we collect whatever hair is offered. The quantity is not specified or guaranteed, and no price per kilogram is fixed. The hair so collected is raw- un worked and unsorted. 4.3 Temple tender notices have been insisting that bidders pay 18% GST on the bid amount. Their position is that what they procure is unworked human hair, which is goods under tariff heading 0501, and that such goods are fully exempt from GST. 5. QUESTION RAISED: 5.1 Whether the procurement of unworked and unsorted human hair from the temple under the tender agreement constitutes a supply of goods under HSN 0501 or a supply of services? 5.2 If classified as goods, whether such supply is exempt from GST under the exemption notification applicable to unworked human hair during the relevant contractual period? 5.3 Whether procurement of unworked and unsorted human hair for a fixed tender period against a lump-sum bid, where the quantity is neither specified nor guaranteed and no price per kilogram is fixed, qualifies as a supply of exempt goods under HSN 0501? 6. PERSONAL HEARING: The authorized representative Sri S.Gnaneshwar, Tax Consultant, appeared before the AAR on 17.11.2026 and reiterated their averments in the application submitted. 7. INTERPRETATION OF THE APPLICANT: 7.1 The applicant has submitted his interpretation question wise raised by him in the application for clarification which are as under: Question 1 – Goods or services? Whether the procurement of unworked and unsorted human hair from the temple under the tender agreement constitutes a supply of goods under HSN 0501 or a supply of services? Our answer: It is a supply of goods. What we pay for and what we receive is the hair itself. Ownership of the hair passes to us when we collect it, and we carry it away and trade in it. The temple renders no service to us. The “right to collect” is only the way the goods are delivered; it is not a separate service. Question 2 – Is it exempt? If classified as goods, whether such supply is exempt from GST under the exemption notification applicable to unworked human hair during the relevant contractual period? Our answer: Yes. Notification No. 2/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017, Serial No. 30, Heading 0501 – “Human hair, unworked, whether or not washed or scoured; waste of human hair” – exempts these goods from the whole of the central tax. The corresponding SGST notification issued by Telangana, and Notification No. 2/2017-Integrated Tax (Rate) (also Sl. No. 30) for inter-State supplies, give the same exemption. Our goods are raw, unworked and unsorted, so they fall squarely within this entry (customs tariff item 0501 00 10). Question 3 – Does the lump-sum, no-fixed-quantity structure change anything? Whether procurement of unworked and unsorted human hair for a fixed tender period against a lump-sum bid, where the quantity is neither specified nor guaranteed and no price per kilogram is fixed, qualifies as a supply of exempt goods under HSN 0501? Our answer: Yes, it is still a supply of exempt goods. The pricing method (lump sum instead of per kg) and the uncertainty in quantity do not change the nature of what is supplied. The consideration is paid only to obtain the hair. Uncertain quantity is a commercial risk we take as the buyer; it does not turn a sale of goods into a service. 7.2 DISCUSSION & FINDINGS: 8.1 The applicant submitted that he has obtained a contract for collecting human hair from temples in the State of Telangana. The applicant will take part in public tenders / e-auctions conducted by temples for the right to collect the hair offered by devotees (tonsured hair). It was also brought to the notice that the applicant bids a lump sum amount for a fixed tender period and during this period the applicant will collect whatever hair is offered by devotees (quantity is not specified or guaranteed). Further, no price is fixed for kilogram. The hair so collected is raw-unworked and unsorted. 8.2 Government notification: The Government of India, Ministry of Finance, Department of Revenue Notification No. 10/2025 Central Tax (Rate) grants exemption under GST on intra-State supplies of goods the description is specified in the Schedule. In the said Schedule, Sl.No. 20 reads as under: S.No. Chapter / Heading Description of goods 20. 0501 Human hair, unworked, whether or not washed or scoured; waste of human hair 8.3 The above notification does not impose any additional conditions for grant of exemption against the above entry. 8.3 Case law (i) Hon’ble Madras High Court in W.P. No. 20200 of 2025 decided on June 6, 2025, (Muthu Mohandoss v. The Commissioner, HR&CE & Ors.), regarding a tender issued by the Arulmigu Renugambal Amman Temple in Padavedu, Tiruvannamalai District. In this order, Justice N. Anand Venkatesh ruled that the temple cannot collect 18% GST from participants bidding in the public auction for the collection of human hair. Case Background & Dispute On May 22, 2025, the Executive Officer of Arulmigu Renugambal Amman Temple, Padavedu, issued a public re-auction/tender notice for the collection of human hair during the Aadi season festival (Fasli 1435, covering July 1, 2025, to September 7, 2025). The Dispute: Clause 23 of the tender notification mandated that auction participants pay 18% GST. The petitioner challenged this specific clause via a Writ of Certiorari. Order of the Hon’ble Court: “6. Taking iProfilento consideration the notification issued by the Central Government as well as the Circular issued by the Commissioner of HR&CE, it is quite apparent that collection of human hair does not attract any GST and the present auction notification pertains to collection of human hair. Therefore, the participants in the auction cannot be insisted to pay 18% GST. Accordingly, clause 23 alone is interfered and it is made clear that the 2nd respondent will not insist for the payment of 18% GST from the participants in the public auction.” (ii) The Hon’ble High Court at Madras has reiterated the same decision and gave a similar order in WP No. 44706/ 2026 dt. 2.11.2026. 9. In view of the foregoing, we rule as follows: In view of the above discussion, the questions raised by the applicant is clarified as below: Question Ruling 1. Whether the procurement of unworked and unsorted human hair from the temple under the tender agreement constitutes a supply of goods under HSN 0501 or a supply of services? Supply of goods under HSN 0501. 2. If classified as goods, whether such supply is exempt from GST under the exemption notification applicable to unworked human hair during the relevant contractual period? Exempted if the goods is : Human hair, unworked, whether or not washed or scoured; waste of human hair 3. Whether procurement of unworked and unsorted human hair for a fixed tender period against a lump-sum bid, where the quantity is neither specified nor guaranteed and no price per kilogram is fixed, qualifies as a supply of exempt goods under HSN 0501? Yes, exempted.
