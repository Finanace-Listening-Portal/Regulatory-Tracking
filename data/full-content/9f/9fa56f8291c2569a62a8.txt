Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Geetaben Lalitbhai Vasoya Vs ITO (ITAT Rajkot) Even a 3,488-Day Delay in Filing a Tax Appeal Can Be Condoned When the Assessee Establishes Sufficient Cause ITAT Rajkot Rajkot ITAT Condones Delay of More Than Nine Years The Rajkot Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal condoned a delay of 3,488 days in filing an appeal before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals). The Tribunal accepted the assessee’s explanation after examining the combined effect of prolonged illness in the family, documented financial distress and the tax consultant’s failure to communicate the adverse assessment order. It took into account the assessee’s chronology and supporting records. Having condoned the delay, the Tribunal set aside the CIT(A)’s order and remanded the disputed additions to the Assessing Officer for fresh adjudication. Reassessment Concerned Cash Gifts and Sale of Gold Ornaments The Assessing Officer completed the reassessment under Section 143(3) read with Section 147 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, on 29 March 2016. He treated cash gifts of ₹16 lakh from the assessee’s grandfather-in-law and ₹15 lakh from her father, as well as ₹9,59,500 from the sale of gold ornaments, as unexplained cash credits under Section 68. The assessee disputed the additions and sought to rely on agricultural land records to support the donors’ financial capacity. She also contended that the Assessing Officer had drawn an adverse inference without independently examining the donors. The CIT(A) refused to condone the delay and dismissed the first appeal at the admission stage. Consequently, the objections to the additions were never examined on merits in the first appeal. Affidavit Explained the Delay and the Steps Taken in 2025 The assessee stated on affidavit that her then authorised representative had handled the assessment and had conveyed that the proceedings had concluded and that she need take no further action. She said she remained unaware of the adverse order and the resulting demand of approximately ₹24.18 lakh until she received a departmental letter dated 3 September 2025. The assessee requested a copy of the assessment order on 8 September 2025 and produced her written request bearing the Department’s acknowledgement. According to her affidavit, she received the order on 30 October 2025 and filed the appeal on 15 November 2025, just 16 days after obtaining the copy. This chronology placed before the Tribunal specific dates against which it could assess both her explanation for the delay and her conduct after learning of the demand. The assessee also explained that she had been caring for her mother-in-law, who was diagnosed with cancer in 2015 and died in August 2020 after prolonged treatment. Her family also faced severe business losses, loan defaults and bank auctions. The Tribunal examined the medical and death certificates and the documentary evidence of financial distress. The Revenue maintained that the long delay reflected negligence or deliberate inaction. The Tribunal found the explanation convincing and concluded that the delay was neither wilful nor attributable to negligence on the assessee’s part. Sufficiency of the Explanation Governs Condonation The Tribunal relied on Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag v. Mst. Katiji, (1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC), for a practical application of the expression “sufficient cause” in furtherance of substantial justice. It also relied on Mool Chandra v. Union of India, Civil Appeal Nos. 8435–8436 of 2024, where the Supreme Court held that a delay deserves condonation, irrespective of its length, if the explanation constitutes sufficient cause. On the effect of professional error, the Tribunal referred to Indian Gospel Fellowship Trust, (2010) 194 Taxman 167 (Madras), and Vijay Vishin Meghani, (2017) 86 taxmann.com 98 (Bombay). Those decisions supported condonation where an assessee’s bona fide reliance on professional advice explained the delay. In the present case, the consultant’s failure to communicate the order was considered alongside the family circumstances and documentary evidence. Tribunal Restores the Additions to the Assessing Officer The Tribunal noted that the CIT(A) had dismissed the appeal without examining the disputed additions. The assessee sought an opportunity to produce additional evidence before the Assessing Officer, and the Revenue raised no objection to that course. The Tribunal accordingly remitted the issues to the Assessing Officer for fresh consideration, giving the assessee liberty to produce supporting material. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes, leaving the tax treatment of the gifts and jewellery sale proceeds for fresh determination. Author’s Comments Under Section 249(3), the enquiry is whether sufficient cause prevented timely filing. Here, the affidavit connected the explanation to identifiable events, and the acknowledged request of 8 September 2025 supported the assessee’s account of what she did after learning of the demand. Her filing within 16 days of receiving the order also helped explain why the Tribunal accepted her claim of bona fide conduct. The condonation secured a hearing on the additions. The assessee still has to substantiate the genuineness of the gifts, the donors’ financial capacity and the source of the jewellery sale proceeds in the fresh proceedings. (1987) 167 ITR 471 wherein it was held that “the expression “sufficient cause” employed by the legislature is adequately elastic to enable the Courts to apply the law in a meaningful manner which subserves the ends of justice. In the case of Mool Chandra vs Union of India (Civil appeal No. 8435, 8436 of 2024 dated 05-08-2024) it was held that it is not the length of delay that would be required to be considered while examining the plea for condonation of delay, it is the cause for delay which has been propounded will have to be examined. If the cause for delay would fall within the four corners of “sufficient cause”, irrespective of the length of delay same deserves to be condoned. The Hon’ble Jurisdictional High Court of Gujarat in the case of Gujarat State Fertilizers & Chemicals Ltd. (2006) 283 ITR 149 (Guj. It is necessary that a liberal approach is adopted in such matters, so as to ensure that substantive rights are not defeated on the basis of technicalities or limitation. I note that Hon’ble Supreme Court has clearly laid down the legal position that in the matter of condonation of delay, the cause of the delay and not the length of delay should be considered. In this regard, I rely upon the relevant portion of the Hon’ble Supreme Court judgment in the case of Mool Chandra Vs. Union of India & ANR. (Civil Appeal Nos. 8435 -8436 of 2024) wherein it was held as under: “It is not the length of delay that would be required to be considered while examining the plea for condonation of delay, it is the cause for delay which has been propounded will have to be examined. If the cause for delay would fall within the four corners of “sufficient cause”, irrespective of the length of delay same deserves to be condoned.” 12. I note that the delay was neither wilful nor due to any negligence on assessee’s part. It was purely unintentional and arose due to circumstances beyond assessee’s control, as detailed above. I find that substantial justice should prevail over procedural technicalities. In the matter of condonation of delay, a pragmatic and liberal approach should be taken. The Supreme Court also held the same in the case of Investment Trust v. Ujagarsingh that unless mala fides or gross negligence is evident, delays should be condoned, and appeals should not be dismissed solely on technical grounds of delay and they should be ordinarily decided on merits. I also rely on the quote and the following observation of Hon’ble M. P. High Court in the case of Steel Ingots (86) Taxmann 440). “The eventual destination of every litigation is justice, and as such technicality should not be permitted to prevail as speed breaker in the course of dispensation of justice.” 13. I note that the reasons given in the affidavit for condonation of delay were convincing and these reasons would constitute reasonable and sufficient cause for the delay in filing this appeal. Having heard both the parties and after having gone through the affidavit as well the delay condonation, application, I am of the considered opinion that in the interest of justice, the delay deserves to be condoned. I, accordingly, condone the delay in filing the appeal before ld.CIT(A). 14. Since, I have condoned the delay in filing the appeal before Ld. CIT( A) and noted that Ld. CIT(A) did not adjudicate the issue ‘on merit’. The learned Counsel for the assessee submitted before me that assessee wants to submit additional documents and evidences to prove his claim before the assessing officer, therefore, learned Council, prays the Bench that matter may be restored back to the file of the assessing officer for fresh adjudication, on merit. 15. The ld. DR for the Revenue debarred from objecting the stand of the ld. Counsel. That is, Ld. DR did not raise any objection, if the matter is restored back to the file of the assessing officer for fresh adjudication, on merit. 16. I note that in the assessee’s case under consideration, the assessment was carried out u/s 147 of the Act and the impugned order passed by the ld. CIT(A), is an ex parte order and non-speaking order, therefore, I do not wish to make any comments on the merits of the grounds raised by the assessee. Considering the above facts, I note that assessee has not given sufficient opportunity of being heard and could not plead his case successfully before the ld. CIT(A). The learned CIT(A) did not adjudicate the issue on merit and dismissed the appeal of the assessee on account of delay. I note that the ld. CIT(A) did not discuss the assessee’s case on merits based on the material available before him hence it is a violation of principle of natural justice. I note that it is settled law that principles of natural justice and fair play require that the affected party is granted sufficient opportunity of being heard to contest his case. I also note that assessee wants to submit additional documents and evidences before the assessing officer to prove his claim and therefore, Ld. Counsel for the assessee also prays before the Bench that matter should be restored back to the file of the assessing officer, hence, I am of the view that the matter should be remitted back to the file of assessing officer for de novo adjudication. 17. Therefore, I accept the prayer of the Ld. Counsel for the assessee and hence, I set aside the order of Ld. CIT(A) and remit the issues back, to the file of the assessing officer, for fresh consideration by the assessing officer, with a liberty to the assessee to prove his case by producing sufficient evidence/material to the satisfaction of the assessing officer. For statistical purposes, the appeal of the assessee is allowed. 18. In the result, appeal filed by the assessee, is allowed for statistical purposes.
