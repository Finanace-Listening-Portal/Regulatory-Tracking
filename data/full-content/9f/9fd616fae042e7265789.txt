Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Nov 3, 2026 at 05:20 PM IST var news_msid = 135495130; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook * IBA working on structured information sharing on cybersecurity incidents* Setty says incidents should be shared immediately across banks, regulators and security agencies* AI-led attacks are a growing concern as digitalisation expands the banking sector’s “attack surface area”* Recent incidents at Bank of Baroda, AU Small Finance Bank and Spandana Sphoorty highlight the evolving cyber-risk landscapeState Bank of India (SBI) Chairman and Indian Banks’ Association (IBA) Chairman CS Setty said the banking industry is working on structured information sharing around cybersecurity incidents following a recent cyber incident at SBI, as the increasing use of artificial intelligence (AI) is creating new risks for banks.SBI is still conducting a root-cause analysis of the incident to identify the vulnerabilities that led to the breach and the remediation required. The bank has also undertaken a comprehensive cybersecurity drill, including API and vulnerability assessments.SBIhad received alerts on October 16 from its threat intelligence service provider, the Defence Cyber Agency and the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) about suspected exposure of customer data on the dark web. A preliminary analysis of around 375,000 records found online identified 3,897 customer records containing personally identifiable information. Advt The information included names, registered mobile numbers, dates of birth, email and residential addresses, along with branch and customer identifiers and metadata linked to loan and lead records. SBI activated its cyber incident response and containment protocols and appointed an independent CERT-In-empanelled cybersecurity agency to investigate the nature and extent of the exposure. The bank has said the incident had no material impact on its core business operations, financial performance or business continuity.Setty said the incident has further strengthened the focus on cybersecurity across the banking system, with the IBA working on an AI-related cybersecurity framework that includes structured information sharing as a key area.“AI attacks are a serious concern. As we digitalise, the attack surface area also increases,” Setty said during SBI’s Q2 FY27 media interaction.He said digital adoption and security need not be mutually exclusive, but the industry needs to work collectively to protect trust in the financial system.“One of the key focus areas is structured information sharing. If there is an incident happening, that should be immediately shared across the banking system so that others can take necessary measures,” Setty said.He added that information also needs to be shared with regulators and relevant security agencies so that the wider financial system can respond to emerging threats. Advt AI versus AISetty said the nature of cyber risks is changing with the increased use of AI, making it important for banks to assess emerging vulnerabilities before deploying AI tools directly into production.SBI, he said, is currently not using front-end AI models extensively, while adoption of such models across the banking and financial services sector is still limited. The IBA is working towards an industry-level sandbox where front-end AI models can be tested in a regulated and controlled environment before wider deployment.“You cannot deploy AI tools straight into production. You have to test it separately, identify the vulnerabilities quickly and then patch them,” Setty said. Advt Setty also said the banking sector needs to increasingly use AI tools to counter AI-enabled threats.“We need to put in AI tools, fighting AI with AI tools,” Setty said.He said cybersecurity spending would not be a constraint for SBI and that the bank would continue to invest in emerging technologies and security capabilities. SBI already has state-of-the-art security operations and network operations centres, while the number of people, tools and expenditure have never been a limiting factor for the lender.Apart from SBI's recent incident, Bank of Baroda, AU Small Finance Bank and Spandana Sphoorty Financial have also faced cyber-related incidents or reported cybersecurity concerns in recent months.
