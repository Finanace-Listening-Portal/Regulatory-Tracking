Similar Supplies, Different GST Rates: 14 Pairs of Goods and Services Easily Confused After GST 2.0 Summary: The introduction of GST 2.0 reforms effective from 22 September 2025 simplified the principal tax slabs but did not eliminate classification complexities arising from differences in tariff headings, product descriptions, packaging conditions, value thresholds and specific exclusions. Under Notification No. 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate) and Notification No. 10/2025-Central Tax (Rate), apparently similar goods may attract GST at Nil, 5%, 18% or 40%, depending upon their precise classification and the conditions prescribed in the relevant entries. This article examines 14 comparisons, comprising 13 pairs of goods and one pair of services, covering packaged drinking water and flavoured beverages, non-carbonated and carbonated fruit drinks, namkeen and pan masala, processed tea and unprocessed tea leaves, fresh and dried chillies, packaged and loose pulses, toilet soap and liquid body wash, shampoo and other hair preparations, shaving products, tractors, motorcycles, footwear, garments and hotel accommodation. Each comparison identifies the applicable GST rate, HSN or SAC classification, relevant notification schedule and entry, and the determining factor responsible for the difference. Particular attention is given to the ₹2,500 per-piece or per-pair thresholds, the definition of pre-packaged and labelled goods, the 350 cc motorcycle threshold, specific tariff exclusions and the treatment of hotel accommodation under the GST amendments effective from 1 November 2026. The article also explains a five-step verification process involving the Customs Tariff, complete notification descriptions, item-specific conditions, the applicable date of supply and documentation of the selected entry. Incorrect classification may result in short-payment of GST, interest, penalties and recurring errors in invoices and GST returns. The analysis is based on the rates stated to be applicable on 16 November 2026 and emphasises that taxpayers should verify the precise statutory entry rather than rely exclusively on a general rate chart or the classification of a similar product. Since 22 September 2025 most goods have been taxed at 5% or 18%, a short list at 40% and a few precious goods at special rates, and many essentials are exempt. Which of these applies depends on the exact entry in the rate or exemption notification, and goods that look alike, or share a heading, often sit in different entries. A trader who copies the code of a similar product, or picks a rate from a summary chart, can be off by anything from 5 to 35 percentage points. This article lists 14 such pairs (13 of goods and one of a service), with the notification and entry for each, and a short method to confirm the code. Rates are those in force on 16 November 2026, after the amendments that took effect on 1 November 2026. 1. Why Similar Goods End Up at Different Rates Most mix-ups come from one of four causes: A different heading or chapter.Fresh chillies are vegetables of Chapter 7; dried, crushed or ground chillies are spices of Chapter 9. The products are close; the entries are not. A tariff item carved out of a broad heading.Heading 2106 has a 5% entry for namkeen (2106 90), but pan masala, tariff item 2106 90 20, is carved out at 40%. A condition written into the entry.For example: pre-packaged and labelled, a sale value per pair or piece, an engine capacity, or a value per unit of accommodation per day. An exclusion.Many entries carry an exclusion in square brackets, “[other than …]”. Tea is 5% “[other than unprocessed green leaves of tea]”; the 18% soap entry excludes “toilet soap in the form of bars, cakes, moulded pieces or shapes”. Reading only the first half of an entry gives the wrong rate. 2. The 5% rate is mandatory: Explanation (a) in entry 7(vi) covers item (i), so a hotel cannot choose 18% with credit instead. It comes with a credit condition: no input tax credit on the goods and services used. From 1 November 2026 there is one exception. Credit is allowed on hotel accommodation bought from another supplier of hotel accommodation (the same line of business), but only up to tax at 2.5% central tax (5% in all). The notification’s own illustration: where the other hotel charged ₹900 central tax at 9% on a ₹10,000 room, credit of only ₹250 can be taken. Points That Cause the Most Errors Value tests apply item by item.For footwear it is the sale value of each pair, and for garments of each piece, not the total of the invoice. One invoice can carry both 5% and 18% lines of the same heading. “Pre-packaged and labelled” is a defined condition.The rate notifications define it as commodities intended for retail sale, of not more than 25 kg or 25 litres, that are “pre-packed” under section 2(l) of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 and are required to carry its declarations. The same pulses can be Nil loose or in a 50 kg sack, and 5% in a retail pack of up to 25 kg, so the item master needs two rate lines for one code. Named items are closed lists.Hair oil and shampoo, or shaving cream, shaving lotion and aftershave lotion, are 5% because the entry names them. In these two headings a product that is not named falls in the 18% entry (Sch. II, entry 62 or 64). Check each heading: in 2106, for example, the residual entry is itself 5%. The rate of the period, not of today.Rates and thresholds before 22 September 2025 were different, and pan masala moved from 28% plus compensation cess to 40% only on 1 February 2026. For an older supply, use the entry in force at its time of supply (section 14 where the rate changed). 4. Confirming the Code in Five Steps 1. Start from the tariff, not from a rate chart. The rate notifications say that the rules for the interpretation of the Customs Tariff, including its Section and Chapter Notes and General Explanatory Notes, apply to them. Find the heading from the description of the goods. 2. Read the whole entry. Check the words in any “[other than …]”, any value, size or packaging test, and, for services under 11/2017-CT(R), the condition column. 3. Apply the test to each item. Value per pair or piece, packaging of each pack, engine capacity of each vehicle. 4. Check the date. Use the entry in force at the time of supply. Where supply, invoice and payment fall on different sides of a change, section 14 of the CGST Act decides. 5. Record the notification and entry against the item, so a later reviewer can see why the rate was chosen. For a schedule-wise reference, see Notification 9/2025-CT(Rate): Complete HSN-Wise GST Rate Chart Effective 22nd Sept 2025. 5. Why It Is Worth the Effort Tax short-paid because the wrong notification entry was applied is recoverable under section 73 or 74 of the CGST Act (section 74A for 2024-25 onwards), with interest under section 50 and penalty. The HSN code also has to appear on tax invoices: under Notification 78/2020-Central Tax, at least 4 digits on B2B invoices where aggregate turnover in the preceding financial year is up to ₹5 crore, and 6 digits on all invoices above that. A code copied from a similar product puts a wrong number on every invoice and every GSTR-1, so the error repeats until someone checks the entry. ***** Sources: Notification 9/2025, 10/2025, 19/2025 and 1/2026-Central Tax (Rate); Notification 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate), as amended, including by 15/2025 and 6/2026-Central Tax (Rate); Notification 3/2025-Compensation Cess (Rate); Notification 78/2020-Central Tax; Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, sections 14, 50, 73, 74 and 74A.
