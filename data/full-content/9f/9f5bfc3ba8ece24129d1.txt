Articles 2 min read MoSPI's household income survey nearly two-thirds complete, completion expected by March 2027: Saurabh Garg The household income survey, which began in April 2026, is progressing well, with nearly two-thirds of the exercise completed. Separately, MoSPI is working to improve district-level GDP estimation and incorporate data as an asset into GDP calculations in the next base revision. People are participating and our focus obviously is to have a better idea of the different deciles that people belong to. We are not interested in the individual incomes and the work is going on fine and we expect it to be completed by March," Garg said during a media interaction.The survey was started in April 2026 and is aimed at providing a better understanding of household income distribution across different income deciles. Advt Focus on improving regional economic dataAlongside the household income survey, MoSPI is also working towards improving the quality and accuracy of economic data at the state and district levels.All states are working towards rebasing their gross state domestic product (GSDP), with the exercise expected to be completed over the next few months, Garg said.On district domestic product (DDP), Garg said several states already produce estimates based on an allocation methodology. However, the ministry is working to improve the methodology to make these estimates more accurate."Some states have already started it but we are hopeful that hopefully by next year many more states would have an improved methodology," he said.The exercise is part of the broader effort to strengthen the availability and accuracy of economic data at the sub-national level.'Data as an asset' to become part of GDP calculationsIndia will incorporate data as an asset into its GDP calculations in the next base revision, with the methodology and underlying details currently being worked out, Garg said.The System of National Accounts 2025 (SNA 2025) focuses on recognising data as an asset, and India is working towards incorporating this into its national accounts framework."We are a data rich country and the fact that the System of National Accounts 2025 focuses on data as an asset is something the mechanics, the details are being worked out and when the next base revision is done we will have data as an asset as an integral part of calculation of the GDP," he said. Advt He also highlighted the government's focus on digital public infrastructure, pointing to the Digital India initiative launched in 2015 and the subsequent digitisation of processes and service delivery across government and other sectors.
