Twenty thousand rupees. Cross that line in a financial year, and a brand that hands an influencer free products or paid trips has to deduct tax at source on the value under section 194R, in force since July 2022. For creators, that turned a loose world of barter deals into entries in the Annual Information Statement. For those of us who prepare their returns, it created a new reconciliation problem, and most creator files we see are not ready for it. It does not tell you what the creator delivered for it. That gap matters more than it looks. The CBDT’s clarification in Circular 12 of 2022 drew the line clearly: a product sent for promotion and returned afterwards is not a benefit, while a product the creator keeps is. So the same unboxing reel can sit on either side of the line depending on what happened to the phone after filming. When a client says “I sent that one back”, the next question is what we can show. Then there is the money side. Paid collaborations are business income in the creator’s hands, and once turnover from services crosses twenty lakh in a year (ten lakh in special category states), GST registration follows. A creator doing thirty brand deals a year cannot reconstruct all of them from memory in July. Why the posts themselves belong in the file Contracts and invoices go missing less often than the actual deliverable does. The reel or Story is the proof that the work was done, that it went live on a particular date, and that it carried the disclosure the ASCI guidelines on influencer advertising expect. And deliverables disappear. Stories are gone from public view in twenty-four hours. Brands ask for posts to be archived when a campaign ends. Accounts get hacked, renamed, or deleted. A creator who could pull up last March’s sponsored reel in thirty seconds is a much easier client to defend in a notice than one who says “it was on my account somewhere”. So the practical advice we now give every influencer client is boring and effective: save a copy of each sponsored post or Story on the day it goes live, together with a screenshot of how it appeared, and file both next to the invoice. Ways to capture a sponsored post, ranked for record-keeping There are four common routes. Ranked by how well they hold up as evidence and how likely a busy creator is to actually use them: A browser downloader, saved on the day. The simplest option is fastdl, an instagram download video page that runs in the browser. The creator pastes the post’s link and gets the reel, photo, carousel, or Story as a file, on a phone or a laptop, with nothing to install and no login to hand over. It works for public posts, which is what sponsored content is by definition. One deliverable takes under a minute, which is the only reason creators keep doing it. Screenshots of the post as published. Not a substitute for the file, but the only capture that shows the caption, the Paid partnership tag or the #ad label, and the date the way the audience saw them. Pair one with every saved file. Instagram’s own data export. Under Accounts Center, the Download your information option builds an archive of everything on the account, for a chosen date range. Complete and official, and a sensible year-end backup. Useless on the day a Story runs, because the export takes time to prepare and arrives as one large bundle that someone then has to sort through. Toolzu. Another browser downloader that does not ask for registration. A reasonable fallback if the first choice is unavailable for some reason; the routine is identical. The ranking is not about which tool is cleverer. A file captured the same day beats a perfect archive requested eleven months later. A filing routine that survives scrutiny Keep it to one folder per brand per financial year. Inside it, four things for each deliverable: the contract or the brief, including whether products are to be returned the invoice, or the brand’s valuation of the benefit if there was no invoice the saved post or Story file, named with the date and the brand the screenshot showing the caption and the disclosure label When the AIS arrives, match each 194R entry to a folder. Anything that does not match is a conversation to have with the client in May, not with the department later. For products that went back, add the courier receipt or the brand’s acknowledgment. That single page is what separates a returned item from income. What we tell creators who push back The usual objection is time. Thirty brand deals, some with three or four Stories each, sounds like a lot of saving. It is about the same effort as taking the screenshot they already take to send the brand as proof of delivery. Most brands ask for that anyway. The difference is that this copy ends up in a folder the creator controls, instead of scrolling away in a WhatsApp chat with an agency. The creators who adopt it rarely go back.
