Articles 4 min read GFF 2026: India could produce multiple $100 bln fintech companies by serving global markets: Razorpay’s Harshil Mathur India could produce multiple $100-billion fintech companies by taking its expertise in real-time payments and the complexity of its domestic market to emerging economies, Razorpay CEO and co-founder Harshil Mathur said. Speaking at Global FinTech Fest 2026, Mathur said markets across Southeast Asia and other emerging regions are beginning to replicate India’s real-time payments journey, creating a significant export opportunity for Indian fintech companies. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Updated On Sep 9, 2026 at 03:39 PM IST var news_msid = 133958479; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook India’s fintech ecosystem could produce multiple $100-billion companies by taking its expertise in real-time payments and the complex use cases developed in the domestic market to emerging economies, Razorpay CEO and co-founder Harshil Mathur said at the Global FinTech Fest 2026 on Wednesday.Mathur said India’s leadership in real-time payments has given its fintech companies a head start as several emerging markets begin building their own instant payment ecosystems.He argued that Indian fintechs have an advantage because they have already operated in one of the world’s most complex payment markets, where multiple instruments, languages, customer segments and use cases coexist at scale. Advt “We feel proud that 49% of all real-time payments in the world are happening through India. But I hope other parts of the world will capture a lot of that growth. They also see real-time payments growing there,” Mathur said.“That is going to create a massive opportunity for Indian companies to go and capture that growth. And I believe a lot of large multi-billion-dollar companies could come out of India because they will be able to capture their growth in other parts of the world," he added.‘Very hard to imagine $100-billion fintech by serving India alone’Mathur said the scale of the global opportunity is substantially larger than what can be captured by focusing exclusively on India.“It’s very hard for me to imagine today that a $100-billion fintech company could come just serving India. But I can imagine totally that a $100-billion company could come out of fintech by serving multiple parts of the world,” he said.According to Mathur, the evolution of real-time payments in markets such as Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand is creating conditions similar to those that helped UPI take off in India.Several emerging economies are now building instant payment systems, while card penetration remains relatively limited, he said. This could create an opening for companies with experience in real-time payments to become technology and payments partners in these markets. Advt “If you have solved for so many use cases and built a complex depth of product that is required to serve the complexity of India, we are in the best place to serve more of these markets,” Mathur said.Razorpay has already expanded into markets including Malaysia and Singapore, while other Indian payments companies are pursuing similar international opportunities.Mathur said the opportunity is particularly significant across emerging markets in Southeast Asia, where rising internet penetration, lower card penetration and growing demand for real-time payments could recreate some of the conditions that drove UPI’s growth in India. Advt India’s fintech edge is its complexityMathur compared India’s opportunity in fintech with the way US internet companies benefited from being early to a large internet market.He said the depth of use cases and problems solved by Indian fintech companies could become an exportable advantage as other countries move towards real-time payments.“Every company built in India has captured the market here. The amount of use cases you have seen, the amount of problems that you have solved, the way you have seen the ecosystem shape up when real-time payments seized the growth curve that UPI showed—when any other market implements real-time payments and starts capturing growth there, you'll be in a better place to solve it,” Mathur said. Advt He added that Western companies that have historically specialised in card payments may not necessarily have the same advantage in emerging markets that are moving towards real-time payment architectures.Mathur pointed to Brazil’s Pix as an example of another market where real-time payments have scaled rapidly by addressing problems similar to those faced by India.The opportunity, he said, will depend not only on technology but also on policy and regulatory support and collaboration among private players.“You need to have these ingredients, then you need to have the right policymakers and regulators to create the support system, and then you need to have the private players working together to drive the outcome,” he said.AI opens another fintech frontierMathur also highlighted artificial intelligence as a major opportunity for fintech, pointing to Razorpay’s payments-focused foundational AI model, Vulkan.The company has trained the model using around 4 billion transactions, applying transformer technology to payment data to identify relationships between transaction signals and predict outcomes.Mathur said early testing has shown an 8-10% increase in payment success rates, a fourfold reduction in fraud and a 40% increase in personalised checkouts.He said the model could eventually be used across multiple payments use cases, including fraud detection, transaction success and personalisation.At the same time, Mathur stressed that the rapid adoption of AI is forcing companies to rethink cybersecurity.“If the attackers are using models, you have to use models to defend. If you are missing out, you are going to lose,” he said.According to Mathur, companies can now deploy AI models across their infrastructure and codebases to identify vulnerabilities before they are exploited, something that was far more difficult when cybersecurity depended predominantly on human testers.He said the industry should accelerate investment in defensive AI rather than slow down innovation because of emerging risks.The broader opportunity, Mathur said, is for Indian fintech companies to take the expertise built through a decade of solving complex domestic financial problems and apply it to the next wave of global digital finance.Click hereto read our coverage of the Global FinTech Fest 2026.
