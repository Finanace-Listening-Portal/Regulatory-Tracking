Vs ACIT (ITAT Mumbai) No Section 14A Disallowance Without Exempt Income: ITAT Mumbai in Vodafone Case; Vodafone Gets Relief on Roaming Charges, Distributor Discounts and Interest: ITAT Mumbai; ITAT Mumbai Deletes Vodafone’s AMP Transfer Pricing Adjustment, Remands Royalty Issues; Vodafone Wins Major Tax Relief Under Sections 14A, 36(1)(iii) and 40(a)(ia): ITAT Mumbai Summary: The Mumbai Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), comprising Accountant Member S. Rifaur Rahman and Judicial Member Rahul Chaudhary, adjudicated three connected appeals involving Vodafone India Ltd., subsequently merged into Vodafone Idea Ltd., for Assessment Years 2011–12 and 2012–13. The appeals involved disputes concerning disallowances under Sections 14A, 36(1)(iii) and 40(a)(ia), deduction under Section 80-IA, and transfer pricing adjustments relating to advertisement, marketing and promotion (AMP) expenditure, brand royalty, technology support charges and reimbursement of employee expenses. For Assessment Year 2011–12, the Assessing Officer had assessed total income at ₹478,20,94,710 against the returned income of ₹235,15,11,079. The Tribunal examined the rival submissions, assessment records and earlier decisions involving the assessee. On the disallowance of ₹357,23,70,000 under Section 14A read with Rule 8D, the Tribunal observed that the assessee had not earned any exempt income during the relevant previous year. Following the Bombay High Court’s decision in PCIT v. Red Chillies Entertainment Pvt. Ltd. and its own decisions in Vodafone’s earlier assessment years, the Tribunal held that no disallowance under Section 14A was permissible in the absence of exempt income. Accordingly, the entire addition was deleted. The Tribunal also deleted the disallowance of ₹19,35,01,258 under Section 36(1)(iii) relating to interest on capital work-in-progress. It observed that investments in telecom network infrastructure were intended to improve service quality within the existing operational area and did not necessarily constitute an extension of business. Following its earlier decisions in the assessee’s own case, it allowed the interest deduction. The Tribunal next considered the disallowance of ₹30,95,03,786 relating to roaming charges under Section 40(a)(ia). The Assessing Officer had alleged that payments to other telecom operators attracted TDS under Section 194J because human intervention was involved in setting up and maintaining telecommunications infrastructure. Vodafone argued that roaming services were automated and did not involve human intervention in the actual transmission of calls. The Tribunal relied on its earlier decisions involving Vodafone and the ruling in Vodafone East Ltd. v. ACIT, which held that such roaming payments did not attract TDS under Sections 194C or 194J. Consequently, the disallowance was deleted, and the corresponding challenge raised by the Revenue was rejected. Another issue concerned ₹47,17,99,596 in discounts extended to distributors of prepaid SIM cards, recharge vouchers and talk time. The Assessing Officer treated these discounts as commission requiring deduction of tax under Section 194H and consequently made a disallowance under Section 40(a)(ia). The assessee maintained that its distributors operated under principal-to-principal arrangements, purchasing prepaid products at discounted prices without receiving commission payments. The Tribunal considered earlier decisions in the assessee’s own case and judicial precedents, including Bharti Airtel Ltd. v. DCIT and Reliance Communications Infrastructure Ltd. It held that the discounts did not constitute commission liable to TDS under Section 194H and deleted the disallowance. The Tribunal also examined the assessee’s claims for deduction under Section 80-IA, including income associated with its eligible telecom undertaking, and granted relief in accordance with the applicable findings. Regarding transfer pricing, the Tribunal examined adjustments of ₹22,01,14,350 relating to AMP expenditure, ₹7,97,68,155 concerning brand royalty, ₹1,31,43,772 relating to technology support charges and ₹93,12,637 concerning reimbursement of salary and related expenses. On AMP expenditure, the Tribunal considered whether the Revenue had established an international transaction between Vodafone and its associated enterprise warranting a separate adjustment. It deleted the AMP adjustment in accordance with the applicable judicial principles. However, the disputes concerning brand royalty, technology support charges and reimbursement of employee-related expenditure required further factual examination. The Tribunal restored the relevant issues to the Assessing Officer or Transfer Pricing Officer for fresh consideration, directing the authorities to examine supporting evidence and provide the assessee an opportunity of being heard. For Assessment Year 2012–13, the Tribunal considered several issues similar to those arising in the preceding year. It deleted the disallowances under Section 14A and Section 36(1)(iii), along with the disallowance concerning discounts extended to prepaid distributors. It also considered the deduction claimed under Section 80-IA and the treatment of other income relating to the eligible business. The transfer pricing adjustment concerning brand royalty was restored for fresh adjudication. Similarly, the adjustment of ₹11,23,53,754 relating to reimbursement of expenses was remanded for examination of the underlying transactions and supporting documents. The Tribunal also issued directions concerning the assessee’s claim for appropriate credit of tax deducted at source. The Tribunal further examined the Revenue’s appeal concerning ₹2,05,35,800 paid to the Department of Telecommunications, which the Assessing Officer had disallowed under Section 37(1). Following the relevant findings in earlier proceedings, the Tribunal rejected the Revenue’s challenge. Other consequential issues concerning interest, tax credits and penalty proceedings were dealt with through appropriate directions, while grounds challenging the initiation of penalty proceedings under Section 271(1)(c) were treated as premature. By its order pronounced on 17 May 2024, the ITAT partly allowed the assessee’s appeal for Assessment Year 2011–12, dismissed the Revenue’s appeal for that year and allowed the assessee’s appeal for Assessment Year 2012–13 for statistical purposes. The decision granted substantial relief on disallowances relating to exempt income, interest expenditure, roaming charges and distributor discounts, while directing fresh examination of specified transfer pricing issues.
