The bank’s CET1 ratio was 13.7 per cent as of 30 September 2026, compared with 14 per cent at the end of June. The overall capital adequacy ratio was 14.5 per cent at the end of September, compared with 15.1 per cent at the end of June. The Supreme Court on 15 October upheld the Bombay High Court's judgment setting aside the March 2020 write-down. It dismissed the appeals filed by the bank, the administrator, the Reserve Bank of India and the Union of India. Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer Vinay Tonse said that, as of 30 September, reinstating the principal would have a pro forma impact of about 240 basis points on CET1, including profits. The bank's overall tier 1 ratio is expected to stay around 13.7 per cent. Banodkar called a CET1 ratio of 11-11.5 per cent "not ideal" and said the bank would want more comfortable capital levels irrespective of what the regulator says. He said, however, that the bank has buffers and the benefit of deferred tax assets, and that whether to raise capital now or later is a commercial evaluation. He added that a security receipts portfolio, with a face value of about ₹1,500 crore and a net asset value (NAV) of about ₹2,000 crore but zero carrying value, is another lever to unlock capital. In its analyst presentation, the bank said its capital position remained well above the minimum regulatory requirements and it was evaluating various options to further strengthen its capital buffers
