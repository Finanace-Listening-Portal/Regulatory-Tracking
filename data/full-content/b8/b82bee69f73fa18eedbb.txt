} } Listen to This ArticleShares of state-owned Bank of Baroda (BoB) fell 3.47 per cent to ₹229.35 on the BSE on Wednesday after B R Shetty, founder of NMC Healthcare, filed a $15 billion lawsuit against the state-owned lender and 25 other defendants in the Bombay High Court, alleging fund diversion, concealed liabilities and forged personal guarantees. BoB said it had not been served with the suit and could not comment on pleadings that remained untested in court. “If and when the Bank is served, the Bank will defend the suit firmly and take all steps necessary to protect its interests,” it said. The bank said the allegations were also the subject of proceedings before the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) Court, where judgment was reserved against Shetty and former NMC chief executive Prasanth Manghat in proceedings involving fraud, wrongful trading and fraudulent trading. The proceedings against BoB were discontinued following a settlement with NMC Group’s joint administrators. BoB said it was pursuing recovery and enforcement proceedings against Shetty in India and the UAE for outstanding dues of about ₹2,800 crore and had obtained several decrees against him. The Karnataka High Court restrained Shetty from dealing with or alienating his assets in 2021. In subsequent proceedings before the National Company Law Tribunal, Shetty asserted that he was insolvent and unable to meet the bank’s dues, BoB said. The bank added that a similar suit filed by Shetty in the Supreme Court of the State of New York was dismissed in July 2024.
