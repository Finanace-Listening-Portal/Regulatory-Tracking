Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Madhao Kulkarni Vs ITO (ITAT Nagpur) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Nagpur SMC Bench, in Madhao Kulkarni Vs ITO, allowed a deduction of ₹16,41,772 under Section 54 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, holding that delayed registration of a residential property could not defeat the exemption where the assessee had made the relevant purchase payments within the prescribed statutory period. The appeal concerned Assessment Year 2015-16 and arose from a reassessment order dated 23 March 2023 passed under Sections 147 read with 144B, which was subsequently upheld by the CIT(A)/NFAC on 27 March 2025. The assessee, a retired employee and senior citizen, had not originally filed a return for the assessment year. Following information concerning the sale of immovable property for ₹62 lakh, the Assessing Officer initiated reassessment proceedings. The assessee filed his return on 28 February 2023 declaring total income of ₹9,38,280 after claiming Chapter VI-A deductions of ₹1,61,599. The assessee held a 50% share in the property sold and attributed sale consideration of ₹31 lakh to his share. After claiming indexed acquisition cost of ₹14,58,228, he disclosed long-term capital gains of ₹16,41,772 and sought deduction under Section 54 on account of investment in another residential property. The Assessing Officer denied the exemption because the purchase deed was registered during FY 2020-21, approximately five years after the original transfer. According to the Assessing Officer, the investment was outside the period permitted under Section 54. The assessee contended that the purchase payments had been made during FY 2014-15 and that the builder or developer had delayed registration for reasons beyond his control. Examining the registered purchase deed and the payment particulars recorded therein, the Tribunal found that the assessee had issued cheque No. 824967 dated 22 November 2014 for ₹2 lakh and cheque No. 116182 dated 10 February 2015 for ₹22.25 lakh, both drawn on Corporation Bank, Nagpur. The remaining ₹35,000 was paid at registration. The Tribunal observed that the long-term capital gain of ₹16,41,772 was undisputed and that the recorded payments demonstrated compliance with the investment conditions under Section 54. It held that subsequent registration of the sale deed would not prevent the assessee from claiming a genuine deduction when the necessary statutory requirements had been fulfilled. Since the relevant facts were already available before the lower authorities, the Tribunal rejected the Revenue’s request to remit this issue for fresh examination and directly allowed the deduction of ₹16,41,772. On the separate claim of ₹1,61,599 under Chapter VI-A, the assessee submitted that the deductions were reflected in Form 16 issued by his employer, LIC. However, the Tribunal found that Form 16 had not been furnished before it and the precise nature of the deductions could not be established. It observed that deductions relating to PF/EPF could be considered on the basis of Form 16, whereas claims concerning life insurance premiums or other eligible payments would require appropriate supporting evidence. Accordingly, this issue was restored to the jurisdictional Assessing Officer for verification and determination in accordance with law. The Tribunal also condoned a delay of 440 days in filing the appeal after considering the assessee’s serious health problems and open-heart surgery, relying on the Supreme Court decisions in Collector Land Acquisition, Anantnag Vs Mst. Katiji & Ors. and Inder Singh Vs State of Madhya Pradesh. The appeal was ultimately partly allowed for statistical purposes, with the Section 54 claim allowed and the Chapter VI-A issue remanded. Cases Discussed 1. Collector Land Acquisition, Anantnag Vs Mst. Katiji & Ors. (Supreme Court); Civil Appeal No. 460 of 1987; 19/02/1987; (1987) 2 SCC 107; (1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC). 2. Inder Singh Vs State of Madhya Pradesh (Supreme Court); Civil Appeal arising out of SLP (Civil) No. 6145 of 2024; 21/03/2025; 2025 INSC 382. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT NAGPUR This appeal by the assessee is directed against the order of Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)/NFAC, Delhi (for short, “CIT(A)”) dated 27.03.2025 passed u/sec. 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (for short, “Act”) which is arising out of assessment order dated 23.03.2023 passed u/sec. 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act for the Assessment Year (A.Y.) 2015-16. 2. Registry has informed that there is a delay of 440 days in filing the present appeal. Application for condonation of delay along with an affidavit is placed on record. The main reason for delay is that assessee is a senior citizen suffering from serious health issues and also undergone open-heart surgery. Owing to his ill-health and medical complications he was prevented from filing the present appeal within the prescribed time limit. Considering it to be a reasonable cause, I find that the delay is neither intentional nor deliberate and therefore taking guidance from the judgments of Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Collector Land Acquisition, Anantnag vs. Mst. Katiji & Ors [1987 (2) SCC 107] and also in the case of Inder Singh vs. State of Madhya Pradesh dated 21.03.2025 [(2025) INSC 382)], I hereby condone the delay and admit the appeal for adjudication. 3. Grievance of the assessee is two-fold, firstly, against the denial of deduction u/s. 54 of the Act against Long Term Capital Gain (LTCG) for the investment made in residential house property, secondly, disallowance of deduction under Chapter-VI A, even when supported by Form16. 4. I have heard the rival submissions and perused the material placed before me. I notice that assessee is an individual and is non-filer for A.Y. 2015-16. Based on the information about sale of immovable property at Rs.62,00,000/- Ld. AO issued valid notice u/s.148 of the Act and carried out re-assessment proceedings. In response, assessee filed return of income on 28.02.2023 declaring total income at Rs.9,38,280/- after claiming deduction under Chapter-VI A of the Act at Rs.1,61,599/-. The assessee, in the present case, is a retired government employee and the source of income is mainly from salary. During the course of assessment proceedings, assessee submitted that he owns 50% of the property and against the sale consideration of Rs.31,00,000/- claimed indexed cost of acquisition at Rs. 14,58,228/- and against the remaining amount i.e. LTCG at Rs. 16,41,772/- assessee claimed deduction u/s. 54 for the investment in residential house at Rs.22,50,000/- paid during the F.Y.2014-15. However, Ld.AO disallowed the assessee’s claim of deduction u/s. 54 of the Act observing that the property was purchased by the assessee during F.Y. 2020-21, which was almost five years after the date of transfer of the original property. According to the Ld. AO, the assessee was eligible to claim the benefit of deduction u/s. 54 only if the investment in the new residential property was made within one year before or two years after the date of transfer of the original residential property. Accordingly, Ld. AO denied the claim of deduction on the ground that the investment was made beyond the prescribed time limit. Further, Ld.AO also denied deduction under Chapter-VI A at Rs.1,61,599/- for lack of furnishing documents relating to such deduction. 5. Aggrieved, assessee preferred appeal before the Ld. CIT(A), but could not succeed as the Ld. CIT(A) confirmed the view taken by the Ld. AO. Now assessee is in appeal before this Tribunal. 6. Learned counsel for the assessee has stated that all the documents were filed before the lower authorities and that the genuine and correct claim has been made u/s. 54 of the Act as the delay in registering the sale deed was on account of builder/developer, who did not get the documents registered for certain other reasons and it was beyond the control of the assessee. On the other hand, Ld.DR submitted that assessee could not furnish these details before the authorities below. I find that the claim of deduction u/s. 54 has been made before the Ld. AO, who was very well aware about the said transaction and even the response given by the assessee on 16.03.2023 forms part of the assessment order. 7. Now assessee in order to get claim u/s. 54 of the Act is required to make investment in another residential house within one year prior or within two years from the date of transfer of house sold by the assessee. During the F.Y. 2014-15 sale consideration of Rs. 31,00,000/- has been received. Figure of LTCG of Rs.16,41,772/- is not in dispute. The purchase deed of the flat registration during the year 2020 is available on record. Perusal of the same indicates that assessee gave cheque No. 824967, dated 22.11.2014 of Rs. 2,00,000/- and at cheque No. 116182 dated 10.02.2015 of Rs. 22,25,000/- drawn on Corporation Bank, Nagpur. The remaining payment of Rs. 35,000/- was paid at the time of registration of document. These payment details indicate that the assessee has duly complied with the provisions of section 54 of the Act and has for purchased another residential house property within the stipulated time limit as provided in section 54 of the Act. Registration of the sale deed at a later stage will not come in the way of the assessee for claiming genuine deduction u/s. 54 of the Act. Since all these facts are very much placed before the lower authorities, the plea of the Ld.DR remitting the issue to the Ld. Jurisdictional Assessing Officer (JAO) is rejected. Further, in view of the above discussion, I am of the considered view that the deduction u/s. 54 of the Act at Rs. 16,41,772/- is allowable to the assessee as the necessary conditions prescribed u/s. 54 of the Act has been duly complied. Ground No.1 raised by the assessee is allowed. 8. Ground No. 2 relating to the denial of deduction under Chapter VI-A at Rs.1,61,599/-. Learned counsel for the assessee submitted that assessee is an employee of LIC which was issued Form No. 16 wherein the details of deductions claimed under Chapter VI-A of the Act were duly incorporated. I have, however, noticed that copy of Form No.16 is not furnished before this Tribunal and therefore it cannot be noted as to what type of deduction under Chapter VI A has been claimed. If they relate to PF/EPF then certainly based on Form 16, it can be allowed, but so far as payment made towards Life Insurance Premium or other type of deductions, then certainly the necessary evidence should be placed before the lower authorities. I, therefore, deem it appropriate to remit this issue of alleged disallowance of deduction under Chapter VI A to the file of Ld.JAO for necessary verification and decide in accordance with law after due examination of the documents to be furnished and in absence of filing any documents, the Ld. AO can proceed in accordance with law. Ground No.2 raised by the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes and other grounds of appeal raised, are general in nature, needs no adjudication. 9. In the result, appeal of the assessee is partly allowed for statistical purposes. Order pronounced on 16th November, 2026 under Rule 34(5) of the Income Tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963.
