The move could change the role of institutional investors in bank ownership by giving them greater flexibility to manage and rebuild their positions.Under the revised framework, prior RBI approval continues to be mandatory for an investor's initial acquisition of a major shareholding, defined as 5% or more of the paid-up share capital or voting rights of a bank. However, once an eligible investor obtains one-time approval, it can subsequently acquire shares taking its aggregate holding up to 10% without seeking fresh approval each time its holding has fallen below 5% and subsequently crosses the threshold again, subject to RBI conditions. Advt The revised directions, effective from October 1, cover mutual funds registered with Sebi, insurance companies registered with IRDAI and pension funds registered with PFRDA. The investor cannot belong to the promoter group or the group of the bank in which it is acquiring shares. The 10% ceiling is calculated on an aggregate basis under the applicable framework.The change addresses a regulatory issue that arose when an institutional investor with an approved stake subsequently reduced its holding below 5%. Under the earlier process, crossing the 5% major-shareholding threshold again required a fresh approval. The one-time approval route allows qualifying investors to rebuild their positions without restarting the approval process, subject to the conditions attached to the original clearance.The RBI oversightThe RBI has retained oversight over the process. The one-time approval remains subject to conditions and can be revoked if the investor fails to comply with them or if the investor or an associated person is subsequently found not to meet the fit-and-proper criteria. Investors with such approval must report movements in their aggregate holding across the 5% threshold to the RBI and the concerned bank within three working days.The framework also applies to small finance banks, payments banks and local area banks, in addition to commercial banks. Applications for approval are to be submitted through the RBI's PRAVAAH portal, with the concerned bank required to provide its comments as part of the process. Advt The RBI has also clarified the treatment of certain acquisitions made through portfolio managers. Where the client is the registered owner of the shares, has voting rights and the portfolio manager provides only non-binding investment or divestment advice, the acquisition will not be treated as an indirect acquisition by the portfolio manager.The immediate impact of the change is to give eligible institutional investors greater flexibility to manage bank shareholdings around the 5% threshold while retaining the RBI's approval and monitoring framework for major institutional ownership.
