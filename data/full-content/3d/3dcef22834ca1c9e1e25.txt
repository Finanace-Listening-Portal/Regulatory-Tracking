(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India dismissed the Revenue’s appeals against Heavy Engineering Corporation Ltd., declining to interfere with the impugned order. The Court condoned the delay, heard counsel for the appellant and dismissed the appeals without recording detailed findings on the underlying service tax dispute. The supplied material also reproduces the CESTAT proceedings concerning the classification and taxability of composite contracts involving machinery, materials, erection, installation, commissioning and associated civil and structural works. Heavy Engineering Corporation Ltd. had undertaken work for Northern Coalfields Limited involving machinery installation and related civil and structural activities. The contract included design, engineering, site preparation, geotechnical investigation and supply of fabricated structural steelwork. During October 2006 to September 2007, the company discharged service tax after claiming 67% abatement under Notification No. 1/2006-ST dated 1 March 2006. The Revenue disputed the benefit and issued show-cause notices dated 4 December 2008 and 17 April 2013, including demands relating to the period up to March 2010. Before the CESTAT, the assessee contended that its activities constituted a composite works contract involving both services and materials. Relying on Commissioner of Central Excise & Customs, Kerala Vs Larsen and Toubro Limited, 2015 (39) STR 913 (SC), and Total Environment Building Systems Pvt. Ltd. Vs Deputy Commissioner of Commercial Taxes, 2022 (63) GSTL 257 (SC), it argued that composite works contracts were not taxable under the earlier erection, installation and commissioning service category before 1 June 2007. For the subsequent period, it defended the 67% abatement claimed under Notification No. 1/2006-ST. The Revenue disputed the classification and abatement treatment. The CESTAT accepted that the contracts involved the supply of materials together with services and were appropriately classifiable as Works Contract Service. It held that the demand under Erection, Installation and Commissioning Service for the period before 1 June 2007 was unsustainable. For the subsequent period, the Tribunal accepted the assessee’s entitlement to 67% abatement, observing that service tax had been discharged on 33% of the gross value. It further held that the demands were barred by limitation because the taxability of the activities was disputed during the relevant period and the notices invoked the extended limitation period. Accordingly, the Tribunal set aside the impugned orders and allowed the appeals with consequential relief. The Supreme Court subsequently dismissed the Revenue’s appeals, stating that it was not inclined to interfere with the impugned order. The Supreme Court’s short dismissal is distinct from the CESTAT’s detailed findings: the supplied Supreme Court order does not independently discuss works contract classification, Notification No. 1/2006-ST or the extended limitation period. The practical outcome was that the Revenue’s challenge failed. The supplied Supreme Court text refers to an impugned High Court order, and the intervening procedural history between the Tribunal and Supreme Court has not been established from the material provided.
