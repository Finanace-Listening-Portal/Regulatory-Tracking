Tata has described any move to restructure or split Tata Sons to avoid RBI listing requirements as "deeply damaging", while making a case for greater transparency and accountability at the holding company.'Deeply Damaging': Jimmy Tata Opposes Any Move To Break Up Tata Sons To Avoid RBI Listingfollow usfollow usJimmy N. Tata has made a case for the listing of Tata Sons, while expressing reservations about any proposal to split the holding company into smaller entities amid the RBI's listing requirements. He has termed such a move "deeply damaging".Jimmy Tata stated any move to break up Tata Sons "into smaller entities to evade the RBI’s threshold requirements for listing" will be "deeply damaging and contrary to public and shareholder interests." In fact he stressed on the importance of timelines for the company's listing that the RBI must enforce and said, "the RBI should enforce strict timelines for listing without delay and ensure that breaking up the group is not permitted, while the government and the Charity Commissioner must step in to protect the original objects of the trusts for the people of India."Jimmy Tata also questioned the representation of multiple members of the present Chairman's family on the same Trust. He described such an arrangement as "entirely inappropriate". Jimmy Tata has also raised objections to changes made to Tata Sons' Articles of Association around 2013-14 and argued that the amendments increased the powers of the Tata Trusts Chairman, including in relation to veto provisions and the placement of Directors on the company's board. He questioned whether these provisions are consistent with the earlier governance framework and said, "a central driver of this issue is the drastic alteration of the Articles of Association around 2013-2014. Historically, under the pre-1989 framework, the Articles strictly separated the welfare trusts from the commercial business operations of Tata Sons, ensuring the trusts had no operational management role.
