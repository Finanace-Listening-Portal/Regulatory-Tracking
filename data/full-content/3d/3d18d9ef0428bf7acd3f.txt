He said that the Sensex and Nifty 50 may rise 5-8 per cent in a short period, adding that large caps have potential to deliver 12 per cent annualised returns over the next 3-5 years. Edited excerpts: Markets have been in a range over the past two years. For how long will this consolidation phase last, and what could lead to a meaningful recovery? Following a 24-month consolidation phase, we expect Indian equities to witness a gradual recovery, with the primary catalyst being a pullback in crude oil prices below $90/bbl. We remain optimistic about the forward outlook and believe domestic equities are likely to deliver better returns on the back of (a) double-digit earnings growth despite a vulnerable external environment; (b) likely conclusion of a trade deal between the US and India, alongside India’s ongoing export basket diversification; (c) reversal of global AI trade, which should redirect capital flows toward well-diversified and emerging markets like India; and (d) likelihood of continued reforms by the government to accelerate economic growth. Additionally, any relief in the taxation pertaining to STT/LTCG would serve as a big sentiment booster for equity markets. Amid heightened geopolitical uncertainties and elevated oil prices, where could Sensex and Nifty head by the end of 2026? If external headwinds recede, frontline indices can deliver returns of 5-8 per cent in a short period of time. This would be driven by short covering and the relative under-ownership of Indian equities within the broader Emerging Markets (EM) basket. We continue to maintain strong conviction in the long wealth creation potential of domestic equities and expect large caps to compound at 12 per cent CAGR over the next 3-5 years. Furthermore, smart investors can generate additional alpha by investing in mid/small cap segment, which has the potential to deliver 15-20 per cent compounded returns in the long run.
