Summary: XRP is the native digital asset of the XRP Ledger, a distributed ledger that validates transactions through a consensus process rather than proof-of-work mining or native proof-of-stake. Its account-reserve mechanism, transaction fees and escrow functionality are distinct from the business arrangements offered by custodians, token issuers or intermediaries. For Indian taxpayers, XRP transfers ordinarily require an examination of the virtual digital asset provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025: section 194(1), Table serial number 4, generally taxes income from transfer of a VDA at 30% while restricting deductions and loss set-off; section 393(1), Table serial number 8(vi), provides for 1% TDS on qualifying consideration subject to the statutory thresholds and payer rules. A resident individual or HUF using the applicable PAN-based mechanism may need Form 141 Schedule D, while exchange-level arrangements can engage Form 142. FIU-IND obligations principally attach to covered VDA service providers under the PMLA, not automatically to every private wallet holder. GST on a service fee must be analysed separately from the direct-tax treatment of the asset. Cross-border XRP purchases, transfers and receipts also raise fact-specific FEMA, remittance, accounting and evidence questions; income-tax treatment is not itself an RBI permission. This article explains the verified technical features and the Indian compliance boundaries without suggesting any investment or trading action. Key Takeaways Source Links Show all 21 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); What XRP and the XRP Ledger Actually Are XRP is the native unit recorded on the XRP Ledger (XRPL). The network records balances, payments, offers on its built-in decentralised exchange and other ledger objects. XRPL documentation describes a validator consensus process: servers exchange proposals and agree on a set of transactions for the next validated ledger. This is different from a proof-of-work chain where miners compete to solve a computational puzzle, and from native proof-of-stake systems that pay validators for staking the coin. The terms XRP, XRPL, Ripple and an exchange account should not be used interchangeably. XRP is the asset; XRPL is the ledger; companies may develop products or services using the network; and an exchange or custodian may maintain a customer claim rather than give the customer direct control of a private key. Tax records should distinguish the legal owner, wallet controller, custodian, trade counterparty and actual transfer event. Consensus, fees and reserve mechanics XRPL uses transaction fees and account reserves to discourage spam and excessive ledger growth. An account must satisfy the applicable reserve requirement, which may change through the protocol’s fee-voting process. Reserved XRP is not necessarily freely transferable while the account remains open, and transaction fees are consumed in network operation. It would be inaccurate to state a permanent reserve amount without checking the live protocol settings; the current requirement is not fixed by Indian tax law. XRPL also supports escrow objects subject to their protocol conditions. An escrow is not the same as an exchange deposit, a bank escrow, a regulated security, or a guaranteed income product. For tax analysis, the transfer of beneficial ownership and the consideration actually received must be established from documents and transaction history rather than inferred solely from a blockchain status code. The statute permits the cost of acquisition, if any, but bars deductions for other expenditure or allowances in computing that VDA transfer income. It also restricts set-off of loss and prohibits carry-forward of loss from transfer of VDA. Applicable surcharge and health and education cess are additional where relevant. A simple example illustrates the distinction between gross proceeds and taxable income. If a taxpayer acquired XRP for ₹1,80,000 and subsequently transferred that holding for ₹2,40,000, the difference of ₹60,000 is the starting transfer-income amount, assuming the full documented cost qualifies and there are no other statutory valuation adjustments. At the 30% special rate, base tax on that amount is ₹18,000 before applicable surcharge and cess. The 1% TDS mechanism is separate; it is not the final 30% tax. A transfer at a loss cannot be netted against a profitable transfer of another VDA merely because both appear in the same exchange statement. Likewise, network fees, custody charges and trading commissions should not automatically be deducted from taxable VDA income. The taxpayer should retain purchase confirmations, INR conversion support, fees, timestamps, wallet addresses, transfers between own wallets and evidence of any gift or non-sale receipt. For the statutory mapping and transition to the new Act, the TaxGuru guide to FY 2026-27 TDS/TCS restructuring explains the consolidated section-code framework. This does not substitute for checking the actual table entry and the identity of the person responsible for deduction. One Percent TDS: Section 393 and Form 141 Section 393(1), Table serial number 8(vi), addresses consideration for transfer of a VDA. The generally applicable deduction rate is 1% when the statutory conditions are met. The relevant threshold depends on whether the payer is a specified person and the aggregate consideration for the tax year; the familiar distinction between ₹50,000 for specified persons and ₹10,000 for other cases must be applied with the current-law definitions, not assumed from a single trade size. Consideration paid in kind or through another VDA needs special attention because tax must be ensured before release where the statute so requires. PAN-based and exchange-level reporting are different The Income Tax Department identifies Schedule D of Form 141 for eligible individual/HUF VDA transactions under section 393(1). The Department also describes Form 142 as a PAN-based quarterly statement for exchanges that have agreed, under applicable guidelines, to deposit tax on behalf of buyers or brokers. A retail investor should not file Form 142 simply because the investor uses an exchange. The applicable filer, challan and reporting route follow the actual transaction arrangement. An exchange statement showing “TDS deducted” is not enough by itself to prove credit in the correct PAN. The taxpayer should reconcile the amount, consideration, transaction date, counterparty, tax-year credit and available government statement. TaxGuru’s TDS challan nature-of-payment guide is relevant to the new codes, but the official form instructions govern the filing. FIU-IND, PMLA and the Service-Provider Boundary The Financial Intelligence Unit–India updated its AML/CFT guidelines for reporting entities providing services related to VDAs on 8 January 2026. Covered services can include exchange between VDAs and fiat, exchange between VDAs, transfer, safekeeping or administration, and participation in certain issuer-related financial services where the notified definitions apply. A covered service provider may have registration, customer due diligence, beneficial-ownership verification, transaction monitoring, record retention and suspicious-transaction reporting obligations. The existence of an XRP wallet does not by itself mean the holder is a PMLA reporting entity. Equally, a foreign platform’s incorporation outside India does not by itself remove Indian AML obligations when it carries on covered activity for Indian users. FIU registration is not a guarantee of solvency, consumer protection, legal permission for every product, or endorsement of the token. TaxGuru’s report on FIU-IND enforcement against a VDA service provider illustrates the separate compliance regime. GST: Distinguish the Token from a Taxable Service Income-tax classification as a VDA does not settle every GST question. Exchange commissions, platform services, custody services, facilitation fees or other identifiable supplies may require analysis under the CGST/IGST framework, including supplier location, recipient location, place of supply, consideration and registration status. A claim that all XRP transfers automatically attract GST at a single flat rate on gross token value would require a specific applicable legal basis and should not be made without one. Where a business receives XRP as consideration for taxable goods or services, the underlying supply can have independent GST consequences even though settlement is in a token. Invoicing, INR valuation, time of supply, accounting and any reverse-charge issue should be documented. The VDA transfer tax regime and the GST treatment of a service fee are distinct taxes on different legal events. FEMA, Cross-border Receipts and Foreign Platforms FEMA analysis starts with the residential status of the person, the nature of the underlying current or capital account transaction, the route of remittance, counterparty and permitted use. The Income-tax Act’s VDA tax charge is not a blanket authorisation to remit funds to an offshore crypto platform, use a foreign-currency account or settle an export invoice in XRP. Nor does the absence of a specific investment ban establish that every overseas crypto arrangement is authorised. Where a resident receives XRP from a foreign person for goods, services, salary or investment, the transaction may need separate analysis of export proceeds, payment mechanisms, foreign asset reporting and the legality of settlement. For NRIs, the source and Indian nexus of income, residential status and applicable remittance rules differ from those for residents. Records should include the bank trail, counterparty documents, transaction hashes and INR valuation method. Accounting, Audit Trail and Disclosure For businesses, the accounting classification depends on why XRP is held and the applicable financial-reporting framework. An entity holding tokens for sale in its ordinary course of business may have inventory considerations; another holding for longer-term use may need to evaluate intangible-asset recognition under the applicable Ind AS or Accounting Standards. A digital asset should not automatically be classified as cash, a bank balance or a financial asset merely because its price is quoted continuously. The accounting file should reconcile opening quantity, purchases, sales, internal transfers, fees, closing quantity and custody arrangements. Management must determine the appropriate measurement basis, impairment or net-realizable-value assessment where relevant, fair-value disclosures if required and controls over private keys. A tax working paper may differ from book profit because section 194 limits deductions and loss treatment. Transaction Evidence Checklist Maintain exchange contract notes and ledger exports, not only screenshots of wallet balances. Map each wallet transfer to a beneficial owner and distinguish self-transfers from disposals. Retain acquisition-cost proof, INR conversion methodology and contemporaneous consideration values. Reconcile TDS deducted, PAN credit and quarterly reporting for the correct tax year. For business users, document FIU-IND applicability, AML procedures, GST treatment and FEMA route independently. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Does XRP use proof-of-stake and pay native staking rewards? No. XRPL documentation describes a validator consensus protocol rather than a native proof-of-stake reward system. A third-party “yield” or lending product is a separate contractual arrangement requiring its own risk, legal and tax analysis. 2. What is the Indian tax rate on gains from XRP transfers in Tax Year 2026-27? Section 194(1), Table serial number 4, generally applies a 30% special rate to income from transfer of a VDA, plus applicable surcharge and cess. Cost of acquisition may be considered; other deductions and loss set-off are restricted. 3. Is the 1% VDA TDS the final tax? No. Section 393 TDS is a withholding mechanism on qualifying consideration. Final income-tax computation is separately governed by the special VDA income provision and other applicable law. 4. Can a loss on one XRP trade be set off against another crypto profit? The VDA special provision prohibits the specified set-off and carry-forward of VDA transfer losses. Do not treat ordinary capital-loss rules as automatically applicable. 5. Must every XRP wallet holder register with FIU-IND? No. FIU reporting-entity obligations principally apply to persons providing the covered VDA services within the notified PMLA framework, not automatically to every individual holding a wallet. 6. Does XRP taxation mean offshore trading is permitted under FEMA? No. Taxability and foreign-exchange permission are different questions. The residence, remittance purpose, payment channel and transaction structure must be checked. 7. Is GST payable at 18% on every XRP sale? No universal conclusion should be drawn from the VDA income-tax provisions. GST depends on whether there is a taxable supply, the nature of any platform or other service, valuation and the applicable statutory treatment. 8. How should an Indian business account for XRP? Classification depends on the holding purpose and applicable accounting standards; inventory or intangible-asset analysis may be relevant. Maintain quantity and cost reconciliations and evaluate disclosure and impairment requirements.
