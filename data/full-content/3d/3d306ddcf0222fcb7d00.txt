But lower payouts to agents, brokers and banks will not automatically translate into cheaper policies, with the eventual impact depending on how insurers adjust premiums, operating costs and distribution strategies.IRDAI has proposed product-specific limits on commissions and a phased reduction in insurers’ expenses of management (EoM). The proposals seek to improve cost discipline, increase transparency and ensure that policyholders receive greater value from the premiums they pay. The deadline for feedback on the proposals has been extended to October 31, 2026. The proposed changes have not yet become final rules. Advt Under the proposed framework, commissions on first-time individual health insurance sales would be capped at 20% for agents and 15% for distribution entities. For renewals, the proposed limits are 10% for agents and associates and 5% for distribution entities. The limits vary by insurance segment, distribution channel and the nature of the product.The potential savings, however, do not translate directly into an equivalent reduction in premiums. An insurance premium covers expected claims, distribution expenses, administration, technology, regulatory costs and other operating requirements. Commissions are only one component of this cost structure. Even if an insurer pays less to an intermediary, the final premium will depend on how the insurer adjusts its overall expenses and pricing, as well as the risk profile of its customers and the claims experience of its portfolio.IRDAI has also proposed reducing EoM limits, which cover a broader range of operating expenses than commissions alone. For life insurers, the proposed limit would fall to 15% of gross direct premium income within two years and 12.5% within five years. Life insurers already operating below the proposed benchmark would face a lower target of 10% over five years. For general insurers, the proposed limit would decline from 30% of gross written premium to 20% of domestic gross direct premium income over five years. The change in the calculation base means the two percentages are not directly comparable. Advt Whether these savings reach customers will depend on insurers’ response. Companies could lower premiums, improve margins, invest in digital systems or use the savings to strengthen claims servicing and customer support. Greater competition could encourage insurers to pass on some savings through lower prices, but this is not guaranteed. Insurers with high distribution costs may have greater scope to reduce expenses, while those already operating efficiently may have less room to cut prices without affecting other functions.Distribution changesThe proposals could also change how insurance is sold. Banks, brokers and agents rely on commissions to acquire customers, explain products and support policyholders through renewals and claims. Lower payouts could prompt intermediaries to prioritise products or customer segments that remain commercially viable. If the economics of serving smaller towns or low-premium customers deteriorate, distribution networks could become less extensive, potentially working against the objective of expanding insurance coverage. The proposed framework also provides for additional rewards for business in underserved areas, which could help mitigate this risk. Advt The effect will differ by product. In health insurance, the scope for lower premiums will depend on claims costs, medical inflation and operating expenses in addition to commissions. In life insurance, the impact will vary between protection and savings products because their commission structures and cost profiles differ. For motor third-party insurance, where premiums are regulated, a reduction in commission does not automatically result in a lower premium.The proposals also seek to improve transparency around distribution payments and address incentives that can encourage unsuitable sales. Bringing certain indirect benefits and incentives within the definition of commission, alongside stronger documentation of customer needs and product suitability, could improve policyholder protection even if premiums do not fall immediately. Advt The test for the reforms will therefore extend beyond a reduction in commission rates. Lower distribution costs must translate into some combination of more competitive pricing, better service, appropriate product recommendations and wider access to insurance. Until insurers adjust their pricing and operating models under the final rules, the extent of any premium reduction will remain uncertain.
