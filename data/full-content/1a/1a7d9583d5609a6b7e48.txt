Articles 2 min read Non-life insurance premium rises 8.9% to Rs 1.8 lakh crore in H1 FY27, health segment leads growth Non-life insurers excluding specialised companies recorded 11.74% growth in gross direct premium during April-September, while standalone health insurers posted 30.69% growth in the period, according to data released by the General Insurance Council. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Oct 8, 2026 at 11:40 AM IST var news_msid = 134781628; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook India’s non-life insurance industry recorded 8.92% growth in gross direct premium to Rs 1.8 lakh crore during April-September 2026, even as a sharp contraction in premiums written by specialised insurers, particularly the agriculture insurance segment, weighed on the overall growth rate, according to provisional data released by the General Insurance Council.Excluding specialised insurers, the industry’s premium growth was significantly stronger at 11.74%, with gross direct premium rising to Rs 1.76 lakh crore from Rs 1.58 lakh crore in the corresponding period last year. In September alone, non-specialised insurers reported a 15.01% increase in premium to Rs 30,820 crore. Advt The overall industry, including specialised insurers, reported premium of Rs 32,951 crore in September, up 6.12% from Rs 31,052 crore a year earlier. For the six-month period, the total stood at Rs 1.8 lakh crore compared with Rs 1.65 lakh crore in the year-ago period.Health insurance emerges as key growth engineStandalone health insurers continued to significantly outperform the broader non-life insurance market. The segment recorded 30.69% growth in gross direct premium during April-September, taking premiums to Rs 25,586 crore from Rs 19,578 crore a year earlier.Care Health Insurance led growth among the larger standalone health insurers, with premium rising 42.23% to Rs 6,193 crore during the period. Aditya Birla Health Insurance grew 36.14% to Rs 3,703 crore, while Niva Bupa reported 33.35% growth to Rs 4,634 crore.Star Health & Allied Insurance, the largest standalone health insurer in the report, recorded 19.31% growth to Rs 9,562 crore.Among private general insurers, SBI General recorded 21.34% growth in cumulative premium to Rs 8,739 crore, while HDFC ERGO grew 26.61% to Rs 9,348 crore. Tata AIG reported 25.52% growth to Rs 12,187 crore, while IFFCO-Tokio grew 19.52% to Rs 5,224 crore.ICICI Lombard, the largest private general insurer by premium in the report, recorded 5.43% growth to Rs 15,110 crore, while Bajaj General Insurance grew 10.76% to Rs 12,798 crore.The public sector insurers also recorded growth, with New India Assurance reporting premium of Rs 22,667 crore during April-September, while Oriental Insurance and United India Insurance recorded growth of 2.81% and 9.01%, respectively. Advt The biggest drag came from specialised insurers. Their cumulative premium fell 51.11% to Rs 3,608 crore, from Rs 7,378 crore a year earlier. Agriculture Insurance Company of India reported a 57.24% decline to Rs 2,867 crore, compared with Rs 6,704 crore in the year-ago period.Among individual insurers, some of the fastest percentage growth came from smaller players. Raheja QBE's cumulative premium jumped 248.76%, while Navi General Insurance grew 133.45%. Zuno General Insurance recorded 51.29% growth and Acko General Insurance 38.42%.Overall, the September data points to a stronger underlying trajectory for the core non-life insurance market, with health insurance and private-sector general insurers driving growth, even as the sharp decline in specialised insurance premiums continues to suppress the headline industry growth rate.
