Articles 6 min read UPI MDR won't make payment fintechs profitable overnight: Industry leaders While the 0.4% MDR on select high-value UPI transactions creates a new revenue pool for the payments ecosystem, industry leaders say it will only partially offset years of zero-MDR economics and make profitability dependent on scale and adjacent businesses. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Updated On Sep 17, 2026 at 11:44 AM IST var news_msid = 134295982; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook New Delhi, Sep 16 (IANS) The introduction of a Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions could provide a sustainable revenue model for the digital payments ecosystem and support its expansion to one billion users over the next decade, fintech industry leaders said on Wednesday.The introduction of MDR on UPI transactions above ₹2,000 is unlikely to immediately turn payments into a profitable business for fintechs, even as the new framework gives banks, payment aggregators and payment apps a long-awaited revenue stream, industry leaders said.The bigger shift, they said, is that UPI is moving towards a sustainable commercial model, but the 0.4% MDR on eligible transactions will not retroactively recover the losses accumulated since the zero-MDR regime and may only reduce the cost burden going forward. Executives across fintech and payments companies said the new revenue stream should be viewed as a partial offset rather than a mechanism to recover the industry's historical losses. Advt Raj P Narayanam, Executive Chairman, Zaggle, said the losses accumulated over years of zero MDR cannot be recovered through a modest charge on only a subset of transactions.“The cumulative revenue foregone by payment infrastructure providers since zero MDR was mandated in 2020 runs into thousands of crores across the ecosystem. A tiered MDR on transactions above ₹2,000 will create a new revenue stream, but it will not retroactively compensate past losses, it will only slow the forward accumulation of them," he said.Kunal Jhunjhunwala, Founder, airpay, similarly said the significance of the move lies less in recovering past losses and more in creating an economic model that can support continued investment in payments.“The industry should not view this as a way to recover all past losses. The bigger issue is making the model work going forward. Payments require continuous investment, and without a revenue stream, it becomes difficult for companies to keep investing at the same pace in technology, security, and the overall payment infrastructure," Jhunjhunwala said.0.4% MDR creates revenue pool, but profitability still a long roadThe impact of the new MDR will be concentrated in higher-value merchant transactions, with everyday low-ticket UPI payments remaining outside the charge framework. This means the revenue opportunity for payment companies will depend heavily on their exposure to large-ticket merchant transactions and how the MDR is ultimately distributed across the ecosystem. Advt Siddharth Mehta, Co-founder & COO, Kiwi, said the introduction of MDR provides a potential sustainable revenue stream and could improve the underlying economics of the payments business.“MDR is an important step towards making the core economics of payments more sustainable. Until now, fintechs have largely focused on building scale, driving customer engagement and monetising through cross-selling. With a revenue stream coming from the payment layer, the economics can become more viable and improve investor confidence," he said.However, industry leaders cautioned against viewing MDR alone as the answer to payment fintech profitability. Advt Ridhi Doongursee, Co-founder, Lxme, said the new MDR would not by itself be sufficient to take high-volume payment businesses into profitability.“Realistically, it does not seem like MDR on high-value transactions will alone get anyone to profitability, it just slows the bleed. The actual path runs through what you build on top of the payment itself: credit, insurance, investments and lending-linked products," she highlighted.“The FinTechs that will reach payment profitability fastest are those that have built monetisation layers beyond the transaction itself like credit products, insurance distribution, SaaS-based merchant tools, working capital financing and data-driven services," Narayanam said. Advt He added that for well-capitalised players, a three-to-five-year timeline could be realistic for a credible payment profitability story, assuming commercially viable MDR and continued volume growth.High-value merchants to bear immediate cost; impact could vary by categoryWhile consumers remain insulated from the MDR under the framework, merchants processing a large proportion of higher-value UPI transactions will see a direct change in their payment acceptance economics.The impact is likely to be more pronounced in categories such as large retail, travel, electronics and other businesses where the average UPI ticket size is significantly above ₹2,000.Jhunjhunwala said merchants will increasingly start evaluating UPI acceptance as a direct cost of doing business.Narayanam, however, cautioned that the formal incidence of MDR being on the merchant does not necessarily mean the economic burden will always remain there.“In the formal structure, MDR on transactions above ₹2,000 would be borne by the merchant, paid to their acquiring bank and split across the payment chain. But in practice, merchants have multiple levers to respond, nudging customers toward lower-cost payment instruments, splitting transactions below the threshold, or negotiating harder with acquiring banks on commercial terms," he added.Doongursee also flagged the possibility of the cost eventually moving through the payment chain.“Eventually, usually the consumer does. Cost in payments doesn't vanish, it migrates, from banks to fintechs to merchants, and from merchants it finds its way to the customer, whether as a visible charge or quietly priced into something else," she said.However, the immediate customer impact is expected to remain limited because the notified framework does not permit MDR to be passed on to consumers.Small-value UPI protected, but industry economics remain splitThe framework attempts to preserve UPI's mass-market proposition by keeping transactions up to ₹2,000 outside MDR. The industry sees this as important because the vast majority of UPI transactions by volume are low-ticket payments.Girish Krishnan, Director, Payment Experience, Amazon Pay, said retaining zero MDR for small-value transactions protects the foundation on which UPI adoption was built.“UPI's success was built on zero-cost adoption by consumers, small shopkeepers, and micro-enterprises, and the notified MDR framework preserves that foundation. We are reassured that small-value transactions up to ₹2,000, comprising over 95% of UPI P2M volume, remain zero MDR," he said.At the same time, the new framework creates a divide in the economics of UPI: the vast volume of smaller transactions will continue to generate costs without a direct transaction-level revenue stream, while a smaller pool of higher-value transactions will now contribute MDR.For fintechs, this makes scale and customer monetisation outside payments critical.Jhunjhunwala said MDR is only one part of the profitability equation.“MDR is one part of the equation, but several other costs come with running a payments business. For fintechs, the focus will have to be on scale as well as building other services around payments. Simply processing transactions at very thin margins is unlikely to be enough to build a profitable business.”MDR seen as sustainability reset, not profitability windfallDespite concerns around the quantum of revenue that will ultimately accrue to individual players, industry executives broadly view the move as a positive shift for the sustainability of India's payments infrastructure.Doongursee said the framework could particularly benefit smaller and mid-sized payment players that have struggled to absorb the cost of UPI operations without a direct revenue stream.“Right now, staying in UPI payments is really only viable if you're well-capitalised enough to absorb losses for years, which has quietly narrowed the field to a handful of heavily funded players. Even a modest MDR on higher-value transactions reduces that burn and that matters more for mid-sized and smaller players than the giants," she said.For payment companies, the immediate takeaway is therefore not that UPI has suddenly become a high-margin business, but that the economics have begun to move away from a pure volume-and-burn model.Narayanam summed up the shift as a correction to the economics of an infrastructure that has scaled dramatically without a corresponding transaction-level revenue model.“Zero MDR was the right policy to build the habit. MDR above ₹2,000 is the right policy to sustain the infrastructure. The ecosystem needed both in that order," he added.
