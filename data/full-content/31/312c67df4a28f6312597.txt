Articles 4 min read Sammaan Capital joins gold loan push by large NBFCs, to launch in 16 branches in Q3 After Tata Capital, Aditya Birla Finance and Godrej Finance, IHC-backed upper-layer NBFC Sammaan Capital is also entering the gold loan market as it looks to diversify beyond mortgages. “We will soft launch the product across around 16 branches in Q3 FY27, followed by another 30 branches in Q4,” said its Chief Operating Officer Sachin Chaudhary. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Nov 16, 2026 at 12:27 AM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 135864465; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Abu Dhabi-based International Holding Company (IHC)-backed Sammaan Capital will soft launch its gold loan business across approximately 16 branches in the third quarter of FY27 and expand the rollout to another 30 branches in the fourth quarter, as it looks to build a multi-product retail lending franchise beyond its traditional mortgage business.Sammaan's product will be secured against gold jewellery, with an indicative average ticket size of around Rs 2 lakh and a yield range of 11–13 per cent, with opportunities in higher-yielding segments. The company plans to offer loans with a maximum tenure of three years and a loan-to-value ratio of up to 85 per cent for loans below Rs 2.5 lakh. Advt “Our gold loan product policies have been approved with a soft launch planned in this quarter of Q3 FY27 and a commercial rollout in the fourth quarter of FY27,” said Deputy CEO Himanshu Mody and building an independent technology and operating platform to manage gold valuation, collateral management, disbursements, repayments and loan closures, along with branch-level vault controls. It has also brought in dedicated product leadership with specialist experience in the gold loan business.Sachin Chaudhary, chief operating officer at Sammaan Capital, said the company would take a calibrated approach to building the business, given the time required for gold loan branches to become profitable.“Gold is typically a workout of around 12 to 18 months to break out. So we are making a calibrated approach,” Chaudhary said during the company's Q2 FY27 earnings call.Per Chaudhry gold loan branches could take 12–18 months to break even.Large diversified non bank lenders are expanding their presence in the gold loan segment. Tata Capital entered the business through its proposed acquisition of an 88.6 per cent stake in Kerala-based Yogloans, while Godrej Finance acquired the gold loan business of Kanakadurga Finance, adding a portfolio of around Rs 280 crore and 54 branches.Aditya Birla Capital's NBFC business announced its entry into the segment in August, with plans to open 200–300 dedicated branches by March 2027. Advt IHC-backed capital and funding supportThe gold loan launch is part of Sammaan's wider transformation following the entry of Abu Dhabi-based International Holding Company (IHC) as its promoter. IHC has described India as a strategic, long-term investment market and Sammaan as its first promoter-level investment in the country.“India remains a long-term investment country for us. It's very strategic. We are actually committed to our long-term investment in Sammaan,” Dalia Khurshid, CEO of Avalora Holdings, IHC's financial services holding company, said during the call.Avenir, the IHC entity, converted the first tranche of warrants on October 30 by investing Rs 906 crore, taking its promoter shareholding in Sammaan from 28.34 per cent to 33.32 per cent. The remaining warrants represent a further investment of Rs 2,291 crore, which is expected to be converted by September 2027 in one or multiple tranches, taking the promoter stake to around 43.28 per cent. The total potential capital infusion through the warrant tranches stands at Rs 3,197 crore. Advt IHC's involvement also extends to governance and institutional oversight. Khurshid said the group was working closely with Sammaan's board and management on its digital and AI policy, transformation, risk models, new products and governance procedures.Managing director and chief executive officer Gagan Banga said Sammaan was leveraging IHC's balance sheet and banking relationships to access global pools of capital, including banks, large global funds and sovereign funds.“We have an opportunity in front of us to leverage on IHC's large balance sheet size and the related banking relationships which come with that to get Sammaan efficient access to global pools of capital, cutting across banks, large global funds, and also sovereign funds,” Banga said. Advt One UAE-based bank within the wider IHC family has already extended support of more than USD 150 million at competitive rates and has become one of the company's principal banking partners, Banga said. Another large UAE-based bank is expected to undertake a similar-sized transaction over the next couple of months.The company expects its borrowing mix to comprise around 30 per cent rupee-denominated term loans and 30 per cent rupee bonds. Another 20 per cent is expected to come from assignments, pass-through certificates and co-lending, while the remaining 20 per cent would comprise 10–15 per cent dollar bonds and 5–10 per cent dollar loans.Sammaan is also taking a calibrated approach to branch expansion, given the time required for newer products to achieve profitability. Chief operating officer Sachin Chaudhary said mortgage branches typically take around six months to break even, while personal loan and business loan branches take around four to six months. Gold loan branches could take 12–18 months to break even.The company is looking to leverage its existing distribution network, which expanded from 217 to 245 branches across 20 states during the first half of FY27, to scale its newer retail lending products.
