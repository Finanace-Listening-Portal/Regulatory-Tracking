Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Sajindra Singh Vs ITO (ITAT Patna) Summary: The ITAT Patna set aside the CIT(A), NFAC order dated 27 January 2026 and restored Sajindra Singh’s appeal for fresh consideration of a long-term capital-gains assessment of ₹68,98,500 for AY 2011-12. The dispute arose from a Joint Development Agreement dated 11 February 2011 with M/s. Om Sai Builders & Developers. The assessee maintained that the land belonged to his son, Shri Chandra Prakash, and that he had signed the agreement solely as a constituted attorney. The Tribunal considered factual verification necessary and allowed the appeal for statistical purposes. Based on information received from the Registrar of Properties, Patna district, the Assessing Officer initiated reassessment proceedings on the ground that capital gains arising from the development agreement had not been disclosed. Treating the transaction as a transfer under section 2(47)(v), read with section 53A of the Transfer of Property Act, the officer completed the assessment on 21 December 2018 under section 144 read with section 147. The CIT(A) upheld the assessment because the assessee had failed to substantiate his assertion that he was merely an agent. The assessee challenged the reopening, the ex parte assessment, the existence and timing of a transfer, and the computation of consideration. His grounds also disputed Section 50C, interest under sections 234A and 234B of ₹12,64,771 and ₹13,21,615 respectively, and the disputed demand including interest of ₹40,07,477. He argued that capital gains could not be assessed in his hands because he neither owned the land nor beneficially received the constructed share. To support that position, he produced the registered sale deed dated 28 May 1992 in favour of Chandra Prakash, the registered power of attorney dated 22 January 2011, and the development agreement. He relied on its description of him as Chandra Prakash’s constituted attorney and on clauses identifying the landowner and allocating ownership of the completed project between the developer and landowner. He maintained that documents had been submitted to the Assessing Officer through a letter dated 6 December 2018 and to the appellate authority through submissions dated 22 January 2021. He also sought refund, with interest, of ₹8,01,500 stated to have been deposited. The assessee cited Suraj Lamp and Industries Private Limited vs. State of Haryana & Anr. and State of Rajasthan v. Basant Nahata to support the distinction between ownership and an attorney’s representative authority. He disputed the applicability of ITO v. N.S. Nagaraj, relied upon below, contending that it involved landowners dealing with a developer rather than an attorney acting for a separate owner. In its written submissions, the Revenue stated that gains from the transaction should be assessed in the hands of the real landowner rather than a power-of-attorney holder. It relied on Suraj Lamp for the proposition that a power of attorney creates an agency and does not transfer title. On timing, it cited N.A. Haris v. Additional CIT and submitted that chargeability should be determined with reference to the handing over of constructed flats or built-up area. To protect the Revenue’s interests, it requested a direction for assessment of the actual landowner in accordance with law. The Tribunal observed that the sale deed and power of attorney produced before it supported the claim that the assessee acted as Chandra Prakash’s agent. It stated that Sajindra Singh was not the real owner and that Chandra Prakash was the landowner, unless he was a benamidar. Nevertheless, the Tribunal recorded that documentary evidence had not been produced before the lower authorities and that factual verification remained necessary. It directed the CIT(A) to consider the assessee’s submissions and documents, examine the Revenue’s written submissions, and pass a fresh order. The addition was thus remitted for reconsideration; the Tribunal did not finally determine the taxable year, direct an assessment against Chandra Prakash, or order the requested refund. That the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Suraj Lamp and Industries Private Limited vs. State of Haryana & Anr. (2012) 1 SCC 656 held in paragraphs 20 and 21 as under: “20. A power of attorney is not an instrument of transfer in regard to any right, title or interest in an immovable property. The power of attorney is creation of an agency whereby the grantor authorises the grantee to do the acts specified therein, on behalf of grantor, which when executed will be binding on the grantor as if done by him (see Section 1-A and Section 2 of the Powers of Attorney Act, 1882). It is revocable or terminable at any time unless it is made irrevocable in a manner known to law. Even an irrevocable attorney does not have the effect of transferring title to the grantee. 8. Further, in the matter of State of Rajasthan v. Basant Nahata, (2005) 12 SCC 77, the Hon’ble Supreme Court held: “13. A grant of power of attorney is essentially governed by Chapter X of the Contract Act. By reason of a deed of power of attorney, an agent is formally appointed to act for the principal in one transaction or a series of transactions or to manage the affairs of the principal generally conferring necessary authority upon another person. A deed of power of attorney is executed by the principal in favour of the agent. The agent derives a right to use his name and all acts, deeds and things done by him and subject tomthe limitations contained in the said deed, the same shall be read as if done by the donor. A power of attorney is, as is well known, a document of convenience 52. Execution of a power of attorney in terms of the provisions of the Contract Act as also the Powers of Attorney Act is valid. A power of attorney, we have noticed hereinbefore, is executed by the donor so as to enable the donee to act on his behalf Except in cases where power of attorney is coupled with interest, it is revocable. The donee in exercise of his power under such power of attorney only acts in place of the donor subject of course to the powers granted to him by reason thereof He cannot use the power of attorney for his own benefit. He acts in a fiduciary capacity. Any act of infidelity or breach of trust is a matter between the donor and the done. An attorney-holder may however execute a deed of conveyance in exercise of the power granted under the power of attorney and convey title on behalf of the grantor.” 9. SUBMISSION NO. 1-Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG) WILL BE COMPUTED IN THE YEAR IN WHICH POSSESSION OF THE FLATS ARE HANDED OVER 1 It is respectfully submitted that, as a matter of law, the liability to Long Term Capital Gains arising out of a Joint/Land Development Agreement, where the consideration flowing to the landowner is in the form of constructed area/built-up space rather than cash, arises in the year in which possession of the constructed flats/built-up area is actually handed over to the landowner by the developer, and not necessarily in the year of mere execution of the Development Agreement, particularly in cases where construction had not commenced, or was not complete, in the year of execution. The rationale is that till the constructed area is actually handed over, the landowner does not receive any ascertainable consideration in his hands, and the “transfer” for the purposes of section 45 read with section 48 of the Act fructifies only when the reciprocal obligation of the developer, namely construction and handing over of possession, stands discharged. 2.2 In this regard, reliance is respectfully placed on the order of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Bangalore Bench, in N.A. Haris v. Additional CIT, ITA No. 988/Bang/2018, order dated 15.02.2021, wherein the Co-ordinate Bench, while examining an identical Joint Development Agreement structure, held that mere permission to enter upon the property for development, without delivery of possession within the meaning of section 53A of the Transfer of Property Act, does not result in transfer, and that the transferee must have “performed or be willing to perform” its obligations under the agreement for section 2(47)(v) to be attracted, the year of taxability was accordingly held to be the year in which the developer’s obligations were substantially performed and the constructed area was actually received by the assessee, and not the earlier year of execution of the agreement. 2.3 Further, reference is respectfully invited to Ground No. 12 of the Grounds of Appeal raised by the Appellant before this Hon’ble Tribunal, which reads as under “For that the Ld. CIT (A), NFAC has failed to consider that the liability of capital gain in case of Development Agreement will arise in the year of exchange of the constructed portion falling to the share of the landlord and not as on the date of entering into the Development Agreement.” 2.4 It is submitted that the said Ground No. 12, on its own showing, proceeds on the footing that the liability to capital gains arises only in the year in which the constructed portion falling to the share of the landlord is exchanged with, ie, handed over to, the landlord, and not on the date of entering into the Development Agreement. This position, as urged by the Appellant himself in Ground No. 12, is consistent with, and lends further support to, the submission made hereinabove at Submission No. 1 that the Long Term Capital Gain is exigible to tax in the year in which possession of the constructed flats/built-up area is handed over to the landowner, and not in the year of execution of the Development Agreement. 2.5 It is accordingly submitted that the year of chargeability of the impugned Long Term Capital Gain be determined with reference to the year in which possession of the constructed flats/built-up area is actually handed over to the landowner, in accordance with the principle laid down in the above decision. 3. SUBMISSION NO. 2- ASSESSMENT TO BE MADE IN THE HANDS OF THE LANDOWNER AND NOT THE POWER OF ATTORNEY HOLDER 3.1 It is submitted that the capital asset, namely the land which was the subject matter of the Development Agreement dated 11.02.2011, is owned by, and vests in, the real and beneficial landowner, and that any income, gain, or consideration arising from the transfer or development of the said asset is taxable only in the hands of the true owner thereof. A Power of Attorney holder, who executes documents and performs acts on behalf of, and for the benefit of, the principal’lowner, acts purely in a fiduciary and representative “capacity, and does not thereby acquire ownership of the asset, nor does he receive the consideration arising from its transfer beneficially in his own right. It would therefore not be in consonance with the settled position of law to fasten the liability to capital gains tas upon a Power of Attorney holder/constinated attorney, where the real owner of the asset is identifiable and is a person other than the attorney holder. 3.2 Reliance in this regard is respectfully placed on the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Suraj Lamp & Industries (P) Lad. Tr. Director v. State of Haryana & Anr., (2012) 340 ITR 1 (SC), judgment dated 11.10.2011, wherein it has been authoritatively held that a power of attorney is not an instrument of transfer in regard to any right, title, or interest in an immovable property, and is merely the creation of an agency whereby the grantor authorises the grantee/donee to act on his behalf, such acts, when done, binding the grantor as if done by him; it was further held that the donee acts in a fiduciary capacity and “cannot use the power of attorney for his own benefit,” and that even an irrevocable power of attorney does not have the effect of transferring title to the grantee. 3.3 The Hon’ble Supreme Court further clarified, at paragraph 19 of the said judgment, that bona fide development agreements coupled with powers of attorney executed in favour of developers for the limited purpose of development continue to be governed by their own terms; ownership of the asset, and the consequent tax incidence, however, remains that of the principal/landowner and not of the attorney holder. It is accordingly submitted that, applying the ratio of the above judgment, the Long Term Capital Gain arising from the transaction in question be computed and assessed in the hands of the real landowner, and not in the hands of the Power of Attorney holder/constituted attorney 4.
