Excluding the book transferred from KMIL, corporate banking advances grew 17% year on year and 7% quarter on quarter. Advt Trade and supply chain finance also grew strongly as the bank invested in automation to serve corporate clients and their wider ecosystems of vendors, dealers and supply chain partners.Corporate banking's contribution to fee income was 20% in both Q2 and Q1 FY27, according to Elara Capital. Non-interest income, excluding processing fees on advances against FCNR(B) deposits, grew 17% year on year, driven primarily by higher foreign exchange gains, debt capital market syndication fees and increased disbursements across businesses.NIMs under pressureThe growth in fee income came as net interest margins (NIMs) faced pressure. FCNR(B) deposit mobilisation accounted for 15 basis points of NIM moderation, while the remainder reflected quarter specific day count variation and repayments under inter bank participation certificates (IBPCs).The bank expects the full quarter impact of FCNR(B) mobilisation to be offset progressively as the deposits are deployed into customer assets and repo rate transmission affects yields on floating rate advances.The bank's SME loan book grew 27% year on year and 10% sequentially, primarily driven by the Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme, under which it sanctioned Rs11,200 crore. Personal and business loans grew 9% year on year, while credit card receivables increased 8.9% year on year and 8% sequentially.Kotak's institutional capital markets businesses also benefited from a recovery in primary markets, with investment banking and institutional equities performing strongly. Investment banking recorded strength across equity capital markets and advisory, supported by a healthy pipeline. Custody balances, however, remained under pressure from muted foreign institutional investor flows, although domestic business traction was strong.
