Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Santhanam Ramesh Babu Vs ITO (ITAT Bangalore) Exemption, Loss Set-Off and TDS Credit Claimed in the First Return Filed U/s 148 Must Be Examined on Merits First Return Filed in Response to Reassessment Notice The Bangalore ITAT held that the Assessing Officer must examine capital gains exemption, current-year house property loss and TDS credit claimed in the first return filed in response to a section 148 notice. The absence of an earlier return under section 139 was insufficient to reject those claims outright. The Tribunal directed verification of the statutory conditions. It separately upheld an addition of ₹12,18,000 under section 69A and rejected the assessee’s objection that the Assessing Officer should have issued a draft assessment order under section 144C. Cash Deposits and Property Purchase Triggered Reopening The assessee had filed no original return for AY 2015–16. The Assessing Officer received information about cash deposits of ₹46,50,000 and an immovable property purchase of ₹1,32,79,000. After receiving the notice under section 148, the assessee filed his first return for the year on 28 April 2022, declaring income of ₹1,14,470. The assessee explained that the cash deposits came from ₹34,32,000 in property sale proceeds and ₹12,18,000 in receipts from freelance telecommunications and networking services. The Assessing Officer added the entire amount under section 69A read with section 115BBE and rejected claims made in the return. The CIT(A) accepted the explanation for the sale proceeds after considering the sale deed and the remand report, but sustained the addition relating to the alleged freelance receipts. Why Sun Engineering Did Not Bar the Claims The lower authorities relied on CIT v. Sun Engineering Works (P.) Ltd. (1992) 198 ITR 297 (SC) to reject the claims. The Tribunal distinguished that decision because the assessee had filed no earlier return and there had been no prior assessment for AY 2015–16. There was consequently no concluded determination that the assessee was seeking to reopen for his benefit. The Tribunal relied on the language of section 148, under which the Act applies, “so far as may be,” as if the return filed in response to the notice were a return required to be furnished under section 139. It also observed that the capital gains exemption formed part of computing the gain itself. The claim was therefore connected to the income being assessed. The Assessing Officer had to examine whether the claim satisfied the applicable conditions. A related decision, Rahul Monal Chokshi Vs National Faceless Assessment Centre (NFAC) (ITAT Ahmedabad), also addressed examination of a Section 54F exemption claimed in a return filed in response to a Section 148 notice. Three Claims Restored for Verification The Tribunal directed the Assessing Officer to verify the claims for capital gains exemption of ₹31,86,495, house property loss of ₹2,00,000 and TDS credit of ₹1,40,670. For TDS, it referred to section 199 read with Rule 37BA, under which credit follows the year in which the corresponding income is assessable. It also held that current-year house property loss could be considered for set-off even though the assessee had filed no return under section 139(1). The Assessing Officer must give the assessee a reasonable opportunity of being heard and grant each claim if the applicable conditions are satisfied. The grounds and first appellate discussion refer to section 54F, while the Tribunal’s operative direction refers to section 54. That difference matters because the applicable exemption provision governs what the assessee must establish on remand. Passport Records Established Residence and Defeated the Section 144C Objection The assessee argued that the assessment order recorded his status as non-resident and that he should therefore have received a draft order and an opportunity to approach the Dispute Resolution Panel. The Revenue produced passport records and a day-wise computation showing that he had spent 200 days in India during FY 2014–15, against 180 days claimed by him. The Tribunal held that the assessee was resident for the relevant year and treated the assessment order’s reference to non-residence as inadvertent. It upheld the issuance of the final assessment order without a draft order in the circumstances of this case. Freelance Explanation Remained Unsupported The Tribunal sustained the ₹12.18 lakh addition because the assessee produced no documentary evidence identifying the persons served, the dates on which services were rendered or the dates on which the cash was received. It accordingly partly allowed the appeal. The importance of supporting documentary evidence in Section 69A disputes is also illustrated by Maradi Jagadeesha Vs ITO (ITAT Bangalore), where the addition was deleted on different facts. Author’s Comments The ruling distinguishes entertaining a claim from allowing it after verification. When the section 148 return is the first return and no assessment has previously been made, the Tribunal’s reasoning requires the Assessing Officer to examine claims that form part of determining the correct taxable income. One statutory reference deserves care. The order mentions the ₹2 lakh limit under section 71(3A), but that subsection was inserted by section 31 of the Finance Act, 2017 with effect from 1 April 2018. The house property loss must therefore be verified under the provisions applicable to AY 2015–16. Cases Discussed Commissioner of Income-tax Vs Sun Engineering Works (P.) Ltd. (Supreme Court); (1992) 198 ITR 297; 17/09/1992. Distinguished. The Tribunal held that the restrictions concerning reopening concluded matters in reassessment proceedings did not justify rejecting claims in the assessee’s first return when no previous assessment had been made. The brief facts of the case as stated by the ld. AR are that the assessee being a NRI had not filed his return of income for the A.Y.2015-16 u/s 139 of the Act. As per the information within the meaning of the provisions of explanation 1(i) of section 148 of the Act available with AO for the year under consideration, it was seen that the assessee had deposited cash amounting to Rs.46,50,000/- and also purchased immovable property for Rs.1,32,79,000/-. Since the assessee had not filed the ROI for the year under consideration, the case of the assessee was reopened u/s 147 of the Act by issuing notice u/s 148 of the Act. In compliance, the assessee filed the ROI for A.Y. 2015-16 on 28.04.2022 by declaring total income of Rs.1,14,470/-. Thereafter, notices u/s 143(2) of the Act and u/s 142 of the Act along with the intimation u/s 129 of the Act were issued to the assessee on various dates calling for certain details in connection with the ongoing assessment proceedings. In compliance, the assessee also submitted the details as called for. 3.1 During the course of assessment proceedings, the AO had categorically asked the assessee to explain the source of cash deposits amounting to Rs.46,50,000/- as in the opinion of the AO, the assessee failed to discharge the source for the cash deposits made and declared in the said income. The assessee contended that the sale price (sale date 10/12/2014) of Rs.34,32,000/- along with business receipts of Rs.12,18,000/- by providing telecommunication & networking services to some friends & relatives while his stay in India was only deposited in cash. The AO did not accept the contentions of the assessee for the following reasons: – (i) The submission of the assessee appears to be after thought. (ii) The Business receipts of Rs.12,18,000/- by providing some telecommunication & networking services to some friends & relatives while his stay in India was not believed as it is not supported by any documentary evidences. (iii) The assessee failed to correlate the cash deposits with the sale proceeds received and the assessee had also not submitted any documentary proof in support of the claim that the sale proceeds of the said property were received in cash only. (iv) The business receipts of Rs.12,18,000/- during the short period of stay appears to be incorrect and accordingly not acceptable as not substantiated with any supporting documents. As the assessee had categorically failed to discharge proving the source for the cash deposits made, the AO had no other alternative but to treat the entire cash deposits of Rs.46,50,000/- as unexplained money u/s 69A r.w.s 115BBE of the Act. 3.2 Further, it was noticed from the working of capital gains furnished by the assessee that the resulting capital gain of Rs.31,86,495/- had been claimed as exempt u/s 54 of the Act. However, in the opinion of AO, the assessee is not eligible for the said exemption as the assessee cannot make a fresh claim in the ROI filed in response to notice u/s 148 of the Act when he had not filed any original return u/s 139(1)/139(4)/139(5) of the Act. Similarly, in the opinion of AO, the assessee is not entitled for the current year loss of Rs.2,00,000/- and refund of Rs.1,40,670/- as claimed in the ROI filed in response to notice u/s 148 of the Act. In the opinion of AO, the reassessment proceedings are for the benefit of the Revenue and not for the benefit of the assessee. If the assessee had not staked any claim by filing return u/s 139, the assessee cannot make a claim for his benefit u/s 148 of the Act. Further, the AO by relying certain judicial decisions had also held that the ROI filed u/s 148 is not a ROI filed u/s 139 of the Act. In view of the above, the AO concluded that in response to notice u/s 148 of the Act, the assessee had claimed a deduction u/s 54 of the Act of Rs.31,86,495/-, current year loss of Rs.2,00,000/- and refund of Rs.1,40,670/- which was not made by him originally by filing ROI u/s 139 of the Act and accordingly by heavily relying on the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of CIT v. M/s Sun Engineering Works (P.) Ltd. dated 17.09.1992 reported in 1992 Supp 1 SCR 732 rejected the claim of the assessee. Thus, the AO completed the assessment proceeding on a total assessed income of Rs.83,00,968/- vide order dated 26.03.2023 passed u/s 143(3) r.w.s 147 of the Act. 4. Aggrieved by the assessment completed u/s 143(3) r.w.s 147 of the Act dated 26.03.2023, the assessee preferred an appeal before the ld. CIT(A). 5. The ld. CIT(A), Bengaluru-12 after considering the assessing officer’s findings in the remand report as well as decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Sun Engineering (Supra) as well as provisions of Section 239(1) of the Act was of the opinion that the deduction claimed u/s 54F of the Act of Rs.31,86,495/-, claim of current year loss of Rs.2,00,000/- and refund of Rs.1,46,670/- are not allowable and accordingly dismissed this ground of appeal. 5.1 Further, with regard to cash deposits of Rs.46,50,000/-, the ld. CIT (A) observed that as per the remand report submitted by the AO, the assessee had explained the source of cash deposits and also submitted a copy of sale deed executed on 10.12.2014 wherein it had been stated that Rs.34,32,000/- was received by the assessee in cash. In view of the above, the ld. CIT (A) was of the view that the unexplained money of Rs.34,32,000/- is explained and accordingly directed the AO to delete the same. However, with regard to balance of Rs.12,18,000/- in respect of freelance income, the ld. CIT(A) also held to be remained unexplained as the assessee had not submitted relevant documents in support of his claim and hence the addition made by the AO of Rs.12,18,000/- was sustained. Thus, the appeal filed by the assessee was partly allowed by the ld. CIT(A). 6. Again, aggrieved by the order of the ld. CIT(A), Bengaluru-12 dated 24.11.2025, the assessee has filed the present appeal before this tribunal. The assessee has also field a paper book comprising of 157 pages containing therein synopsis, copy of section 144C of the Act along with the case laws relied upon by the assessee. 7. Before us, at the outset the ld. AR of the assessee by raising the ground No.12 & ground No. 15 which are purely legal in nature vehemently submitted that the AO erred in law and in facts in not issuing a draft assessment order as contemplated u/s 144C of the Act in the case of the assessee being a non resident individual which renders the entire reassessment proceedings and the impugned assessment order non-est and bad in law and liable to be quashed. Further, the ld. AR of the assessee submitted that the AO while framing the assessment has undisputedly admitted that the assessee is a non resident individual and the same is evident from bare perusal of the assessment order. Lastly, the ld. AR submitted that the final assessment order passed without providing an opportunity to the assessee to file his objections before the ld. DRP is contrary to the provision of section 144C of the Act which contains the terms ‘shall’ and ‘not withstanding to the contrary contained in the act’. Further with regards to the merits of the case, the ld. AR submitted that if a return is filed u/s 148 of the Act, the provisions of this Act shall apply accordingly as if such return required to be furnished u/s 139 of the Act & therefore the assessee can validly claim exemption u/s 54 of the Act, Loss from the House Property as well as TDS credits as per the provisions of the Act. Further, the ld. AR also submitted that the case of Sun Engineering (Supra) heavily relied upon both the authorities are quite distinguishable from the facts & circumstances of the case. Lastly, the ld. AR submitted that the ld. CIT(A) grossly erred in upholding the addition of Rs. 12,18,000/- being cash deposits made out of the income earned from freelancing u/s 69A of the Act. 8. The ld. The ld. DR also filed a written submission dated 10.08.2026 along with the comment/report of the AO wherein the AO has clearly stated that the assessee had stayed in India for 200 days during the relevant financial year and accordingly was a resident for the relevant year. Therefore, there was no requirement to pass a Draft Assessment Order. Further, the ld. DR in his written submission has also submitted the copy of the passport of the assessee along with the date wise chart of number of days stay in India by the assessee during the F.Y.2014-15 which is reproduced below for ease of reference and convenience: – from to 01-04-2014 10-03-14 arrival chennai 26-04-14 departure bengaluru 26 01.04.2014 to 26.04.2014 26-04-14 arrival US 03-08-14 arrival delhi 13-09-14 departure bengaluru 42 03.08.2014 to 13.09.2014 01-10-14 arrival bengaluru 01-11-14 departure bengaluru 32 01.10.2014 to 01.11.2014 30-11-14 arrival bengaluru 06-02-15 departure delhi 69 30.11.2014 to 06.02.2015 07-02-15 arrival US 01-03-15 arrival bengaluru 04-04-15 departure bengaluru 31 01.03.2015 to 31.03.2025 20-05-15 arrival bengaluru 19-06-2015 departure bengaluru Total days of stay during FY 2014-15 200 9. We have heard the rival submissions and perused the material available on record. In the present case, the ld. AR of the assessee at the outset, by raising the ground no. 12 as well as the ground no. 15 which are purely legal in nature has submitted that the AO grossly erred in not issuing a Draft Assessment Order as contemplated u/s 144C of the Act especially when the assessee being a NRI is an eligible assessee as defined u/s 144C(15)(b) of the Act and passed the final assessment order without providing an opportunity to the assessee to file his objections before the ld. DRP. The ld. DR on the other hand, vehemently submitted that the assessee had stayed in India for 200 days during the relevant financial year and accordingly the assessee was a resident for A.Y.2015-16 and therefore there was no requirement to pass a draft assessment order in the present case. On perusal of the number of days stayed by the assessee in India as per the above tabular form submitted by the ld. DR along with the copy of the passport of the assessee, we observed that the assessee had stayed 200 days during F.Y.2014-15 relevant for A.Y.2015-16 and therefore we have no hesitation to hold that the assessee was resident during the F.Y.2014-15 relevant for A.Y.2015-16. We completely agree with the contentions of the ld. DR that the recording by the AO in the assessment order that the assessee was a non-resident during the relevant year was an inadvertent error. In our considered opinion, in reply to the notice issued u/s 142(1) of the Act dated 14.02.2023, the assessee himself misrepresented before the AO by stating that the he had settled in USA and that his residential status was NRI from F.Y.2013-14 and onwards. Further, in our view, the assessee had wrongly mentioned his number of days stay in India during the F.Y.2014-15 relevant for A.Y.2015-16 as only 180 days and claimed to be Non Resident whereas we find that the assessee had stayed 200 days during the relevant period and therefore in our opinion, the AO had rightly passed the assessment order without first issuing the draft assessment order and accordingly we dismiss this grounds of appeal raised by the assessee. 9.1 Now coming to another contention of the assessee that the ld. CIT(A) grossly erred in upholding the addition of Rs. 12,18,000/- being cash deposits made out of the income earned from freelancing u/s 69A of the Act. We observed that the assessee before the AO had contended that the cash deposits of Rs. 12,18,000/- were from the Business receipts by providing some telecommunication & networking services to some friends & relatives while his stay in India. The authorities below did not accept this contention of the assessee as it is not supported by any documentary evidences. Even before us, the assessee could not produce any documentary evidences such as name, address & PAN of his friends & relatives along with the dates of providing the service as well as dates of receipts of cash from such relatives & friends. Accordingly, we also find no merits in the arguments of the ld. AR of the assessee and dismiss this ground of appeal of the Assessee. 9.2 Lastly, with regard to the denial of the exemption claimed u/s 54 of the Act amounting to Rs. 31,86,495, Current year loss of Rs. 2,00,000/- as well as TDS credit of Rs. 1,40,670/-, we are of the considered opinion that section 148(1) of the Act as applicable for the A.Y. 2015-16 categorically states that the AO shall serve a notice requiring the assessee to furnish within such period as may be specified in the notice, a return of his income, in the prescribed form & verified in prescribed manner and setting forth such other particulars as may be prescribed and the provisions of this Act shall, so far as may be, apply accordingly as if such return were a return required to be furnished under section 139 of the Act. Undisputedly, the assessee had not filed any return u/s 139 of the Act. Further, it is also an undisputed fact that while filing the return u/s 148 of the Act, the assessee had claimed the exemption claimed u/s 54 of the Act amounting to Rs. 31,86,495, Current year loss of Rs. 2,00,000/- as well as TDS credit of Rs. 1,40,670/-. We observed that, both the authorities below by heavily relying upon the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Commissioner of Income-tax v. Sun Engineering Works (P.) Ltd. reported in (1992) 198 ITR 297 have held that the proceedings under section 148 of the Act can only be used for the benefit of the Revenue & accordingly denied the claim of exemption u/s 54 of the Act amounting to Rs. 31,86,495, Current year loss of Rs. 2,00,000/- as well as TDS credit of Rs. 1,40,670/-. We are of the opinion that considering the facts & ratio decidendi in the above case, the present facts of the case are quite distinguishable. In our considered opinion, the Apex court has principally held as under: – (1) On reassessment, original assessment is not wiped off but it remains. (2) Matters disallowed in the original assessment & which have since acquired finality (i.e. no appeal, revision or rectification filed by the assessee against disallowance) cannot be claimed in the reassessment proceedings. (3) Expenses not claimed in the original assessment can not be claimed in the reassessment. However, the expenses relating to the escaped income can be claimed as deduction against the escaped income. (4) Reassessment proceedings are for the benefit of Revenue & not for the benefit of the assessee. In reassessment, the income cannot be reduced below the original income assessed. Thus, in the present case, the assessee had filed his return only in response to notice u/s 148 of the Act for the first time. Even previously no assessment was made by the AO for the A.Y. 2015-16. The assessee filed his return & claimed the exemption/deduction/TDS Credits as per the provisions of the Act i.e. in accordance with law. Section 54 of the Act is part of computing the capital gain & therefore it isn’t a fresh claim outside the escaped income. Further, as per Section 199 and Rule 37BA, the credit follows the year in which the related income is offered and assessed. Since the assessee declared the income in his return, in our considered opinion, the credit cannot be denied. Similarly, the loss from the head “Income from House Property” can be set off against other heads u/s 71(3A) of the Act up to Rs.2 lakhs. Set off doesn’t depend upon the filing of return u/s 139(1) of the Act. Furthermore, the provisions as enumerated u/s 148 of the Act clearly prescribed that the provisions of this Act shall, so far as may be, apply accordingly as if such return filed u/s 148 of the Act were a return required to be furnished under section 139 of the Act. Therefore, the contention of the AO that the ROI filed u/s 148 of the Act is not a ROI filed u/s 139 of the Act is not at all tenable. We are of the considered opinion that while entertaining the exemption u/s 54 of the Act amounting to Rs.31,86,495/-, loss from the House Property amounting to Rs. 2,00,000/- & TDS credits of Rs.1,40,670/-, the AO should have verified each of the conditions stipulated under the respective provisions of the Act rather than rejected outrightly by stating that the assessee cannot claim the same in the return filed u/s 148 of the Act. Accordingly, we direct the AO to verify the claim of exemption u/s 54 of the Act, claim of loss from house property as well as TDS Credit as per the provisions of the Act and if the assessee satisfies the conditions, then the AO must grant the exemption/deduction/ TDS Credit after due verification in accordance with law as claimed by the assessee in his return of income filed in response to notice u/s 148 of the Act. Needless to say, a reasonable opportunity of being heard must be granted to the assessee. It is ordered accordingly. 10. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is partly allowed.
