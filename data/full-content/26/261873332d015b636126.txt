Summary: Section 74 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 prescribes the procedure for determining tax not paid, short paid, erroneously refunded or input tax credit wrongly availed or utilised because of fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression of facts to evade tax. For tax periods up to FY 2023-24, it provides different penalty consequences depending on the stage at which tax, interest and the prescribed penalty are discharged. Before service of a show-cause notice, payment under Section 74(5) attracts a penalty of 15% of tax. Payment within 30 days of issue of the show-cause notice under Section 74(8) attracts 25%. Following an adjudication order, Section 74(11) permits payment with a 50% penalty within 30 days of communication of that order, while the ordinary adjudicated penalty is equivalent to 100% of the tax. The process involves Form GST DRC-01A, DRC-01, DRC-03 and DRC-07, depending on the stage of proceedings. This article examines six penalty scenarios and illustrates their financial consequences with practical input tax credit disputes. From FY 2024-25 onwards, Section 74A governs tax-demand determinations, making identification of the relevant financial year, statutory conditions and payment deadline essential.
