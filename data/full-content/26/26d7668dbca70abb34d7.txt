(GSTAT Agra) Summary: The GST Appellate Tribunal, Agra State Bench, in Commissioner State Tax, U.P. (Through Shailendra Kumar, Assistant Commissioner) Vs Kamlesh Trading Company, dismissed the State Tax Department’s appeal and upheld the first appellate authority’s order setting aside a penalty and fine of ₹1,45,055 imposed under Section 130 read with Section 122 of the CGST/UPGST Acts. The dispute arose from an inspection conducted by the Special Investigation Branch, Etawah, on 30 November 2024 at the business premises of Kamlesh Trading Company, a firm engaged in trading tobacco leaves and processed tobacco products. During verification, the officers found 15,441 kg of tobacco stem powder allegedly unaccounted for in the firm’s records. The goods were seized, and the proper officer initiated proceedings under Section 130 read with Section 122. After rejecting the taxpayer’s explanation, the adjudicating authority valued the unaccounted goods at ₹3,39,442 and imposed penalty and fine of ₹1,45,055 by order dated 17 March 2025. The first appellate authority, by order dated 12 September 2025, allowed the taxpayer’s appeal and set aside the demand, prompting the Department to approach the GSTAT under Section 112(1). The Tribunal examined whether confiscation proceedings under Section 130 read with Section 122 could lawfully be initiated merely because excess or unaccounted stock was discovered during an inspection under Section 67. The Department argued that tax officers were empowered to inspect business premises, verify records and initiate proceedings under Section 130. The taxpayer supported the first appellate authority’s findings and sought dismissal of the Department’s appeal. Referring to Sections 35(1) and 35(6) of the CGST Act, the Tribunal observed that registered persons must maintain true and correct accounts of goods and services. Where goods or services are not accounted for, Section 35(6) empowers the proper officer to determine tax payable as if those goods or services had been supplied, applying the provisions of Sections 73 or 74, as applicable. The Tribunal relied on three Allahabad High Court decisions: Dinesh Kumar Pradeep Kumar Vs Additional Commissioner, Shri Om Steels Vs Additional Commissioner and V.K. Electricals Vs Additional Commissioner, which had held that stock discrepancies discovered during surveys should be addressed through the statutory tax-determination mechanism rather than direct confiscation proceedings. Applying these precedents, the Tribunal held that the proper officer had adopted an incorrect statutory procedure by directly invoking Section 130 instead of initiating proceedings under Sections 73/74. Consequently, the first appellate authority had correctly quashed the original adjudication order and cancelled the consequential penalty and fine. Since the proceedings themselves were legally unsustainable, the Tribunal considered it unnecessary to examine the factual dispute concerning the unaccounted tobacco stock. The Department’s appeal was accordingly dismissed as devoid of merit. The decision reinforces the requirement that GST authorities must follow the appropriate statutory procedure when dealing with stock discrepancies discovered during business-premises inspections. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF GSTAT AGRA 1. Under section 112(1) Central Goods and Services Tax, Act/ Uttar Pradesh Goods and Services Tax, Act (for short CGST/UPGST, Act), this appeal has been preferred by department of State tax, Uttar Pradesh, against order dated- 12.09.2025 passed by First appellate authority, Division- Etawah, in appeal no. AD090625027697K, whereby by allowing the appeal of respondent taxpayer first appellate authority has set aside the order in original and consequential demand of penalty and fine of Rs. 1,45,055/-. For convenience appellant and respondent will be addressed as department and taxpayer, respectively. 2. Facts giving rise to the present appeal are in brief that special investigation branch unit Etawah headed by Additional commissioner Grade-II had inspected the business premises of taxpayer on 30.11.2024, owner of taxpayer firm Sh. Akhilesh Agrawal, was present at that time who told that his firm is engaged in the trade of tobacco leaves and its processed items. On verifying the records and goods kept in business premises, it was found that 15,441 kg tobacco stem powder was found unaccounted searching officer issued I.N.S no. 0583 dated 30.11.2024, and seized the stock found so unaccounted. Thereafter, a SCN u/s 130 read with section 122 was issued by proper officer. Though, SCN was replied by taxpayer but not accepted by proper officer and by determination of value of goods found unaccounted of Rs. 3,39,442/-, penalty and fine of Rs. 1,45,055/- were imposed by order in original dated 17.03.2025. 3. Feeling aggrieved by that order in original, taxpayer firm had preferred appeal before first appellate authority u/s 107 of CGST/UPGST, Act which was allowed and amount of penalty and fine was set aside, hence this second appeal by department u/s 112(1) of CGST/UPGST, Act. 4. Learned departmental representative has assailed the order under appeal as not sustainable in law and submitted for allowing the appeal. 5. Per contra, learned counsel appeared for taxpayer has supported the findings recorded by first appellate authority and submitted to dismiss the appeal. We have heard both the learned representatives of parties and perused the record available digitally on GSTAT portal. The question for determination may be formulated as under- Whether in a survey/search carried out under section 67 of CGST/UPGST, Act, proceedings u/s 130 read with section 122 is according to law? FINDINGS WITH REASONS 6. Learned departmental representative Sh. Mukesh Kumar, has submitted that impugned order passed by first appellate authority is not sustainable in law because of not only a tax officer is empowered to inspect and verify the records of any business firm in his local jurisdiction but he can also initiates proceedings u/s 130 of CGST/UPGST, Act. On the above, learned DR, has submitted for allowing this second appeal. On the other hand, learned counsel appearing for respondent has supported the findings recorded by first appellate authority and submitted for dismissal of appeal. 7. In case of M/s Dinesh Kumar Pradeep Kumar V/s Additional commissioner, Gr-II and others writ tax no. 1082 of 2022 date of decision 25.07.2024, it was held by Allahabad High court that whereon survey of business premises excess stock of goods was found which triggered the initiation of the proceedings u/s 130 of CGST act against petitioner then proceedings u/s 73/74 of CGST/UPGST act, should be initiated and not proceedings u/s 130 of the UPGST/CGST, Act read with rule 120 of the rules framed under the act. 8. Further in a recent case M/s Shri Om steels vs Additional Commissioner Gr. II and others writ tax no. 1007 of 2022 dated 19.07.2024, It is also held that if excess stock is found during a survey, tax and penalty proceedings must be initiated under Section 73 or 74 of the CGST Act, rather than invoking the harsh confiscation provisions under Section 130. 9. Section 35(1) of the CGST, Act provides that all the registered persons are required to keep and maintain at the principal palace of business a true copy and correct account of things specified in clause (a) to (f). The second proviso to this section and rule 56 and 57 makes it further necessary to keep the said documents in electronic form. Further section 35(6) of the CGST, act enumerates the consequences if such person fails to keep his account for the goods or services, the proper officer is empowered to determine the amount of tax payable on the goods or the services which are unaccounted for as if such goods or services had been supplied by such person and the provisions of section 73 or 74 shall mutates mutandis apply for determination of the said tax. Thus, by bare perusal of the section 35(1) and 35(6) conjointly makes it clear that proper officer is empowered to determine the taxes payable and while determine the said tax he is bound to determine the same in-accordance with the provisions u/s 73 and 74 of CGST act/ UPGST act. 10. Apart from above, in case of V. K electricals vs Additional commissioner Gr-II and another writ tax no. 957/2024 date of decision 23.08.2024, Allahabad High court has held that in case of excess stock found at the time of survey proceeding u/s 73/74 of CGST/UPGST, Act should be initiated and in place or in addition to launching proceedings u/s 130 read with rule 120 unsustainable and liable to be quashed. 11. Thus, on going through the binding principle laid down in above quoted ratios established by Hon’ble Allahabad High court as well as on bare perusal of section 35 of CGST/UPGST, Act, it is crystal clear that where at the time of survey of business premises excess or unaccounted goods are found, the proper officer is only empowered to initiate proceedings u/s 73/74 of CGST/UPGST, Act, against business entity concerned, he is not authorised by law to initiate proceedings u/s 130 and levy the penalty and fine in lieu of confiscation of goods. In the present matter same is the position where on the survey of business premises which was being used by appellant for producing and selling of tobacco and its edible products excess/unaccounted stocks of such items are said to be found but instead of initiation proceedings u/s 73/74 of CGST/UPGST, Act. Learned proper officer initiated the proceedings u/s 130 of the CGST Act directly, which is not sustainable in law and by holding so and quashing the order passed by Adjudicating officer, Learned First Appellate Authority has rightly allowed and quashed the Order-in-Original passed by proper officer resulted set aside the demand of penalty and fine. 12. As proceedings initiated by proper officer against taxpayer u/s 130 read with section 122 is found to be not according to law, therefore, factual aspect of the case need not to be discussed by this Tribunal. 13. Therefore, in view of discussion of facts of the case in hand and law applicable thereto, by answering the question formulated in negative, we are of the considered opinion that this present appeal is liable to be dismissed as sans merit, which this bench directs. 14. There is no pending interim application in the appeals. 15. Order is pronounced in open court on this day of 19th day of November, 2026. 16. Copy of order be communicated to all concerned, as per rules.
