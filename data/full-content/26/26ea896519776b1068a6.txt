Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Vodafone Idea Limited Vs ACIT (ITAT Mumbai) Summary : The ITAT Mumbai “F” Bench, comprising Challa Nagendra Prasad (Judicial Member) and Prabhash Shankar (Accountant Member), heard Vodafone Idea Limited’s appeals in ITA Nos. 1029 and 1030/Mum/2025 for AYs 2016–17 and 2017–18 respectively, and the Revenue’s cross-appeal in ITA No. 3030/Mum/2025 for AY 2016–17. The appeals arose from assessments under Section 143(3) read with Section 144B of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Bench treated AY 2016–17 as the lead year and pronounced its common order on 26 October 2026. Vodafone Idea challenged the validity of the CIT(A)’s orders, including the opportunity for a virtual hearing, and alleged failure to follow binding precedents. The Bench dismissed these grounds in both years. The authorised representative had not argued them, and the Tribunal found prima facie that adequate opportunity had been given. For AY 2017–18, it also found that the CIT(A) had in effect condoned the alleged filing delay by deciding the appeal on merits. The COVID-period explanation appeared satisfactory, and the Revenue did not contest the issue, so no separate adjudication was required. The Assessing Officer (AO) disallowed ₹203.56 crore paid or payable to foreign telecom operators under Section 40(a)(i). The assessee argued that the automated facilities did not give rise to royalties or fees for technical services and that Section 195 applied only to sums chargeable in India after considering the applicable treaties. The CIT(A) relied on Dishnet Wireless. Following earlier rulings, including Vodafone Idea’s Karnataka High Court case, the Bench held that the charges were not taxable in India, no tax had to be withheld and the deduction had to be allowed. The lower authorities treated ₹31.70 crore debited under Vodafone Idea’s employee stock option scheme as contingent. The company said the charge represented employee compensation apportioned over the vesting period under the mercantile system. Following the Biocon Special Bench decision and the treatment in the assessee’s earlier cases, the Tribunal held that the discount on the shares constituted expenditure deductible under Section 37(1). The AO invoked Section 14A read with Rule 8D(2)(iii) to disallow ₹38.28 crore despite Vodafone Idea earning no exempt income during the relevant year. The AO relied on CBDT Circular No. 5/2014. The assessee disputed the disallowance and, in the alternative, sought to restrict it to its own computation. Following precedents including Chettinad Logistics, the Bench directed deletion of the entire disallowance because no exempt income was received or receivable for AY 2016–17. Vodafone Idea challenged the disallowance of ₹1,977.84 crore for AY 2016–17 and sought consequential amortisation based on the unexpired term of each licence under Section 35ABB, following the Supreme Court’s ruling in Bharti Hexacom. It submitted a revised licence-wise computation and disputed the approach adopted below. The Tribunal found that the CIT(A) had discarded that computation without substantive reasons. It remanded the whole issue to the AO to verify the revised working and allow the claim in accordance with law. It issued the same direction for the ₹1,835.21 crore dispute for AY 2017–18. The AO and CIT(A) treated the tower sale and leaseback involving Quipo Telecom Infrastructure Ltd. as a colourable device and disallowed ₹48.69 crore in rent. The company pointed to its earlier reduction of the sale proceeds from the relevant plant and machinery block, which reduced the depreciation claimed, and to favourable rulings on the same arrangement. The Revenue identified no material factual difference. Following the earlier Tribunal decision, the Bench directed the AO to allow the lease rent. The ₹396.14 crore claim was made in the tax computation. The record did not establish whether the arrangement involved an operating or finance lease, who owned the equipment, or how the payments were accounted for. The Tribunal referred to the Delhi High Court’s Bharti Hexacom ruling on IBM payments, which required examination of the transaction’s true nature independently of the book entries. It directed the assessee to furnish the agreement, invoices and supporting records, and remanded the issue for the AO to decide deductibility after verification. The AO treated disputed plant and machinery expenditure of ₹41.65 crore for AY 2016–17 and ₹78.20 crore for AY 2017–18 as capital expenditure. The assessee maintained that it had furnished sample bills. Finding the record insufficient to decide the character of the expenditure, the Tribunal remanded both years and directed the assessee to submit the required particulars to the AO. Vodafone Idea sought amortisation of ₹42.12 crore in fixed licence fees for AY 2016–17. The lower authorities were concerned about duplication with the amortisation of revenue-share licence fees. The Tribunal required fresh examination of actual payment, the validity period of each licence, the amount remaining unallowed and the year-wise computation under Section 35ABB. The directions also apply to the corresponding ₹25.63 crore ground for AY 2017–18. Both grounds were allowed for statistical purposes. The Tribunal upheld the ₹15 crore disallowance under Section 37(1). Shareholder approval did not establish the actual services rendered or the business purpose and reasonableness of the expenditure. The Bench also referred to the managerial remuneration limits in Section 197 of the Companies Act, 2013. It found the evidence of the nature and extent of the directors’ services insufficient to substantiate the deduction. The assessee challenged the disallowance of ₹6,282.43 crore claimed as depreciation and the substitution of a deduction based on one-twentieth of written-down value. The Bench followed its earlier decisions on capitalised spectrum rights, found no material change in facts and directed the AO to allow depreciation under Section 32(1)(ii) on the capitalised 3G spectrum charges. The ₹11.28 crore disallowance included different kinds of breaches. Following the earlier telecom ruling for contractual breaches and Supreme Court decisions on separating compensatory and penal components, the Bench directed the AO to obtain a bifurcation. The disallowance attributable to contractual non-compliance was to be deleted. The disallowance attributable to breaches of radiation norms was to be upheld under Explanation 1 to Section 37(1). This ground was partly allowed. The AO disallowed the ₹2.28 crore provision under Section 40(a)(ia) for non-deduction of tax. The company said the payees could not be identified at year-end and sought alternative relief for the year of subsequent withholding or reversal. The Bench remanded the matter to verify subsequent tax deduction, any write-back of the provision and the applicability of the statutory proviso. The Tribunal directed deletion of the Section 14A-related adjustment under clause (f) of Explanation 1 to Section 115JB(2), as no exempt income was credited to the profit and loss account. It also directed deletion of the ₹31.70 crore employee stock option adjustment because the liability had already been held to be accrued business expenditure. The AO was directed to recompute Section 234B interest while giving effect to the order. The Bench upheld deletion of the ₹1.60 crore disallowance for abandoned mobile tower projects, following the Bombay High Court in the assessee’s own case. It also upheld the CIT(A)’s relief on the disputed interest issues relating to capital work in progress and interest-free advances to subsidiaries. The company said deferred-payment interest on spectrum not yet put to use had been allocated to CWIP without being claimed as revenue expenditure. On subsidiary advances, the Tribunal followed earlier decisions that considered commercial expediency and the availability of sufficient interest-free funds. The Revenue’s grounds concerning Section 14A failed in view of the finding that no exempt income had been earned. The Bench also upheld deletion of the ₹42.69 lakh disallowance for club entrance and membership fees. Following the assessee’s earlier cases, it accepted that the memberships helped senior executives develop business contacts and did not confer an enduring benefit. The Revenue’s appeal was dismissed in full. The Tribunal distinguished the ₹8,574.67 crore depreciation claim on brought-forward written-down value from the ₹884.15 crore amortisation claim on payments for spectrum rights acquired during the year. The first related to spectrum costs already in the intangible asset block. The second was governed by Section 35ABA, effective from AY 2017–18, which provides for amortisation of eligible capital payments over the spectrum term. The AO and CIT(A) had conflated the two claims. The Bench found that the claims related to separate costs and directed the AO to allow both. For AY 2017–18, the AO was also directed to recompute Section 234B interest and examine the disputed additions in the Section 143(1) intimation after allowing the assessee an opportunity to explain its claim. Both Vodafone Idea appeals were partly allowed. The issues remanded for verification remain subject to the AO’s examination under the Tribunal’s directions.
