Articles 4 min read Banks may see short term NIM boost after RBI rate hike The Reserve Bank of India’s 25 basis point repo rate hike to 5.5 per cent could provide banks with near-term support to net interest margins as lending rates, particularly on externally benchmarked loans, reprice faster than deposit costs, although the benefit is likely to narrow as deposits mature and are repriced. While loans linked to an external benchmark are reset relatively quickly following a repo rate change, deposits are repriced largely when existing deposits mature or when banks raise rates on incremental deposits.“Banks might get some early margin support, because lending rates rise faster than deposit rates,” Saurabh Bhalerao, Director-Research at CareEdge, told ETBFSI. He added that there would be a window during which lending rates would be relatively higher than deposit rates before funding costs catch up. Advt The RBI’s latest lending and deposit rate data also points to the existing spread between loan and deposit pricing. In August 2026, the weighted average lending rate (WALR) on fresh rupee loans of scheduled commercial banks stood at 8.61 per cent, while the weighted average domestic term deposit rate on fresh rupee term deposits was 5.67 per cent.For private-sector banks, the WALR on fresh rupee loans stood at 9.19 per cent in August, compared with 7.99 per cent for public-sector banks. On fresh term deposits, the corresponding rates were 5.82 per cent for private banks and 6.20 per cent for public-sector banks.On the outstanding book, private-sector banks' WALR stood at 9.82 per cent in August, against 8.38 per cent for PSBs. The weighted average domestic term deposit rate on outstanding deposits was 6.66 per cent for private banks and 6.62 per cent for PSBs.The difference between lending rates and term deposit rates, however, should not be read as banks’ NIM, as banks also have CASA deposits, wholesale funding, borrowings and investment assets on their balance sheets.The immediate margin benefit is therefore primarily a function of how quickly the asset side reprices versus how quickly the liability side reprices. Advt A banking analyst also told ETBFSI that a substantial portion of retail loans linked to EBLR could get repriced within a quarter, while deposits would reprice as they mature or as banks accept fresh deposits. This could result in some marginal improvement in NIMs in the initial period following the rate hike. The benefit, however, is unlikely to be permanent.As deposit rates are revised upwards to retain or attract deposits, banks’ cost of funds will begin to rise. The ability of individual banks to hold existing deposit rates for longer will therefore be an important determinant of how much of the initial margin benefit they retain. Advt The latest RBI data shows that fresh deposit rates have already moved differently across bank groups. In August, fresh term deposit rates stood at 6.20 per cent for PSBs and 5.82 per cent for private banks, while the outstanding deposit rates were much closer at 6.62 per cent and 6.66 per cent, respectively.Jefferies, in a report released after the RBI policy, said banks with higher EBLR-linked loans and manageable loan-to-deposit ratios could see positive earnings over the next three to six months. It expects the change in the interest-rate cycle to be a potential catalyst for bank earnings and sees scope for margin expansion as rate hikes feed through into loan yields. Advt The global investment bank estimates that if 75 basis points of rate hikes eventually seep into yields based on banks’ EBLR-linked loan share, and banks retain 15 per cent of the resulting margin benefit, this could support earnings or offset some other pressures on bank profitability.The impact will nevertheless differ across banks depending on their loan pricing mix, deposit franchise, loan-to-deposit ratio and funding structure.The rate hike also comes at a time when banks are absorbing the impact of the RBI’s special FCNR(B) deposit mobilisation window. CareEdge’s Bhalerao stated that the deployment of these deposits could also influence margins in the near term, making the October-December quarter a mix of the rate-hike impact and the effect of FCNR(B) funds on banks’ margins.
