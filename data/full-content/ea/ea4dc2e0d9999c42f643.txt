While eligible small vendors may remain exempt from charges on their own receipts, nearly 70% make payments exceeding Rs 2,000 to suppliers, exposing them to potential indirect procurement costs if suppliers pass on the charges.A study by the Centre for Analytical Finance (CAF) at ISB, based on a survey of 722 street vendors across 14 States and Union Territories and 64 urban local bodies, found that 45-53% of respondents across city tiers would reduce UPI acceptance if the proposed MDR framework were implemented.Titled UPI Utilisation and MDR Awareness Among Street Vendors, the study examined digital payment adoption, awareness of the proposed charges and vendors' potential responses to additional transaction costs. The survey was conducted between September 24 and October 10, 2026.The proposed framework envisages a 0.4% MDR on specified person-to-merchant (P2M) UPI transactions exceeding Rs 2,000, subject to a cap of Rs 300 for transactions of Rs 75,000 and above. Certain categories, including specified essential services, attract different rates. Eligible small merchants receiving up to Rs 1 lakh a month through QR-based payments under the P2PM category are exempt from MDR.The framework was initially scheduled to take effect on October 15, but the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) deferred its implementation on October 13 until after Diwali. A revised implementation date has not been announced. Advt Procurement costs could affect small vendorsNearly 70% of surveyed vendors make supplier payments exceeding Rs 2,000 at a time, placing these transactions above the proposed MDR threshold. While eligible small vendors are exempt from MDR on their own receipts, payments they make to suppliers could attract charges if the suppliers fall within the categories covered by the framework.The MDR is payable by the merchant receiving the payment, rather than the customer making it. However, suppliers could indirectly pass on the additional cost to vendors purchasing goods from them, putting pressure on the latter's already thin margins.The survey found that 10.4% of vendors had been asked by suppliers to pay additional charges for UPI transactions in the 10 days preceding the survey. These demands were reported even before the proposed MDR framework came into effect.The findings also indicate that vendors could respond to additional procurement costs by shifting supplier payments to cash or increasing the prices of goods they sell.Among surveyed vendors, 30% in Tier 1 cities indicated that they could pass on the additional cost to customers. The corresponding proportions were 14% in Tier 2 and 19% in Tier 3 cities.However, merchants are not permitted to levy MDR directly on customers. Any increase in retail prices to recover higher procurement costs would be distinct from directly imposing the MDR on customers.UPI adoption and MDR awarenessThe survey found that 67% of respondents accepted UPI payments, but only 52% were aware of the proposed MDR framework.UPI adoption was higher in larger cities, with 82% of vendors in Tier 1 cities accepting digital payments, compared with 66% in Tier 2 and 64% in Tier 3 cities.Awareness of the proposed charges also varied across city tiers. While 66% of Tier 1 vendors had heard about the framework, the proportion fell to 55% in Tier 2 and 47% in Tier 3.Among vendors accepting UPI payments, 64% were aware of the proposed charges, compared with 26% of those who did not accept digital payments.However, awareness did not necessarily translate into an understanding of the framework's applicability. Among vendors who had heard about MDR, 48% in Tier 1 cities, 46% in Tier 2 and 33% in Tier 3 believed that the charges would apply to them.The proportion of vendors indicating that they would reduce UPI acceptance if the charges were introduced was 53% in Tier 1 cities and 45% each in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities. Advt Study calls for pilot implementationThe study cautioned that exemptions based on the size of a merchant's retail transactions may not fully account for costs incurred through wholesale procurement. Even if eligible small vendors are exempt from charges on their own receipts, additional costs imposed on their suppliers could influence their payment preferences.A shift towards cash could have wider implications for digital payment adoption, transaction transparency and small businesses' access to formal credit, which can be supported by digital transaction records.The study recommended introducing any proposed charges through limited pilot programmes before a wider rollout. It also called for monitoring supplier demands, changes in customer pricing and any shift towards cash following implementation.It further recommended an independently reviewed assessment of the actual costs of operating UPI to establish how these costs should be distributed among participants in the payments ecosystem.
