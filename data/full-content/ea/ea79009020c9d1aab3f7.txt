Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Oppo Mobiles India Private Limited Vs ACIT (ITAT Delhi) Section 143(1) Intimation Is Liable to Be Quashed if CPC Makes Adjustments Without Mandatory Prior Notice Tribunal Quashes Intimation for Failure to Give Prior Notice The Delhi ITAT held that the Centralised Processing Centre (CPC) must communicate proposed adjustments before making them under section 143(1)(a) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. No evidence established that Oppo Mobiles India had received the mandatory prior opportunity. The Tribunal quashed the intimation dated 27 May 2024. The CIT(A)’s direction for subsequent verification did not cure the original defect. Revised Return and Adjustments Made by CPC The company filed its original return on 30 November 2023 and revised it on 30 December 2023, claiming a refund of ₹98,80,30,430. It replied on 12 January 2024 to a defect notice under section 139(9). The CPC’s later intimation made disputed adjustments of ₹4,10,77,228 for foreign-exchange loss on fixed assets, ₹74,51,250 for CSR expenditure and ₹20,18,11,240 for fines or penalties. The company argued that the amounts had already been disallowed in its return, resulting in double disallowance, and challenged the TDS credit and reduction in loss available for carry-forward. CIT(A) Directed Verification Without Deciding the Notice Ground On 20 February 2026, the CIT(A) recognised the company’s computation, tax audit report and supporting documents as demonstrating that the additions were unwarranted, but directed AO verification. The company challenged this approach and maintained that the prior-notice ground had remained undecided. The Revenue argued that later verification afforded sufficient opportunity. First Proviso Requires Communication Before Adjustment The first proviso to section 143(1)(a) requires communication of proposed adjustments in writing or electronically. The second proviso requires consideration of any response and allows adjustments where no response is received within 30 days. The company relied on ITBA screenshots; the Revenue could not rebut its assertion that no prior adjustment notice had been issued. The Tribunal held that later proceedings could not substitute for the prescribed opportunity. Bombay High Court Decisions Supported Quashing The Tribunal referred to Rallis India Ltd. v. CPC, WP (L) No. 37314 of 2025 (19 January 2026), and Bax India Ventures Pvt. Ltd. v. CPC, (2026) 183 taxmann.com 395 (Bom.). It quashed the intimation and allowed the appeal without separately deciding the substantive adjustments. Author’s Comments The defect notice under section 139(9) and the mandatory communication of specific adjustments under section 143(1)(a) serve different purposes. The company answered the former, but no evidence established issuance of the latter. The decision also matters for loss returns: adjustments can reduce losses available for carry-forward even where a refund is determined. Cases Discussed Rallis India Ltd. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI This appeal by the assessee is directed against the order dated 20.02.2026 of the Commissioner of Income Tax,(Appeal)-30, Delhi [hereinafter referred to as the ‘Ld. CIT(A)] arising out of the order dated 27.05.2024 passed under section 143(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’) passed by the Assessing Officer, DDIT, CPC, Bengaluru, (AO) pertaining to Assessment Year (A.Y.) 2023-24. 2. The Assessee has raised the following grounds of appeal: “ 1. That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the intimation order dated May 27, 2074 issued under section 143(1) of the Act is bad in law, void ab initio and thus, liable to be quashed as the same has been issued without first issuing the mandatory proposed intimation as required as per the first proviso 10 section 143(1)(a) of the Act. 2. That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Centralized Processing Centre (CPC) has erred in making the adjustments while processing the return under section 143(1) of the Act, without appreciating that such adjustments are beyond the limited scope of permissible adjustments under section 143/1)(a) of the Act. The CIT(A) has further erred in upholding the said erroneous action of the CPC 3. That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law the CPC has erred in making adjustments on issues which are highly debatable and require examination of facts, interpretation of law and / or verification of accounting treatment, which is impermissible while processing returns under section 143(1) of the Act. The CIT(A) has further erred in upholding the said erroneous action of the CPC 4 That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the order passed by the CIT(A) is invalid as the same has been passed in contravention of the provisions of section 251(1)(a) of the Act. 5. That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the CIT(A) has erred in not adjudicating the issue conclusively and in remanding/restoring the matter to the file of the Assessing Officer (AO) for verification, despite the fact that all material facts and evidence were already available on record and no further verification was warranted. 6. That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the CIT(A) CPC have erred in granting short credit for taxes deducted at source (TDS) being INR 94,92,95,258, as against INR 38,80, 11,016 claimed by the Appellant in its revised return of income (“ROI). 7. That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the CIT(A)/ CPC have erred in disallowing an amount of INR 4,10,77 228 pertaining to foreign exchange loss on fixed assets without appreciating that the said amount has already been suo moto disallowed and offered to tax by the Appellant in its ROI, thereby resulting in impermissible double disallowance. 8. That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the CIT(A)/ CPC have erred in disallowing an amount of INR 74,51,250 pertaining to expenditure incurred on corporate social responsibility (“CSR”) without appreciating that the said amount has been suo moto disallowed and offered to tax by the Appellant in its revised ROI, thereby resulting in impermissible double disallowance 9. That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the CIT(A)/ CPC have erred in disallowing an amount of INR 20,18,11,240 pertaining to expenditure incurred by way of penalty or fine for violation of any law for the time being in force without appreciating that the said amount has been suo moto disallowed and offered to tax by the Appellant in its revised ROI, resulting in double taxation. 10. That on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law the CIT(A)/ CPC have erred in reducing the amount of current year losses carried forward from INR 21,80,16,32,433, as claimed by the Appellant in the revised ROI, to INR 21,35,13,51,715 in consequence of the aforementioned adjustments.” 3. For the captioned assessment Year (“AY”), the assessee filed its return of income (“ROI”) on November 30, 2023, under section 139(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (“Act”) which was subsequently revised on December 30, 2023, claiming a refund of INR 98,80,30,430/-. Post filing the revised ROI, the assessee received a defect notice u/s 139(9) of the Act, to which it duly filed its rebuttal vide reply dated January 12, 2024. 3.1 Thereafter, an intimation order under section 143(1) of the Act dated May 27, 2024, was issued by the Central Processing Centre (“CPC”) in respect of the revised ROI filed by the assessee making certain adjustments without issuing the mandatory prior opportunity notice as required section 143(1)(a) of the Act. The CPC made disallowances / additions which were debatable and outside the scope of permissible adjustments. Pursuant to the said intimation, a refund of INR 1,01,57,66,690 (comprising refund of INR 94,93,14,672 and interest under section 244A amounting to INR 6,64,52,022) was determined, as against the refund of INR 98,80,30,430 claimed in the revised return. Further, as a consequence of abovementioned adjustments, the losses for the current year eligible to be carried forward were reduced to INR 21,35,13,51,715 as compared to INR 21,60,16,32,433 claimed in the revised return. 3.3. Against the impugned intimation order, the assessee filed an appeal before the CIT(A). Vide order dated February 20, 2026, the CIT(A) allowed the grounds of appeal filed by the assessee by observing that the it had demonstrated with reference to the computation, tax audit report and other documents that the impugned additions were not called for but remanded the matter to the AO for the purpose of verification of documents. The assessee is aggrieved on the ground that there was no requirement to remand the matter and hence is in appeal before the Tribunal. 4. Before us, ld. AR has argued that since the intimation u/s 143(1) was passed without giving mandatory prior opportunity the same is liable to be quashed. Following written submissions have been made in this regard: – “ 1.1 At the outset, it is submitted that the impugned intimation dated May 27, 2024, passed under section 143(1) of the Act is without jurisdiction, contrary to the mandatory provisions of section 143(1)(a) and therefore liable to be quashed. 1.2 It is further submitted that the first proviso to Section 143(1)(a) explicitly mandates that if the CPC intends to make any adjustments to the income declared in the return, a prior notice must be issued to the taxpayer before effecting such adjustment. Relevant extract of section 143(1)(a) is reproduced herewith as under “143. (1) Where a return has been made under section 139, or in response to a notice under sub-section (1) of section 142, such return shall be processed in the following manner, namely – (a) the total income or loss shall be computed after making the following adjustments, namely:- Provided that no such adjustments shall be made unless an intimation is given to the assessee of such adjustments either in writing or in electronic mode: Provided further that the response received from the assessee, if any, shall be considered before making any adjustment, and in a case where no response is received within thirty days of the issue of such intimation, such adjustments shall be made: (emphasis supplied) 1.3 A perusal of the above provisions clearly establishes that no adjustment can be made without issuing prior intimation or notice to the Appellant. 1.4 The screenshots of the ITBA portal annexed at page nos. 88-92 of case law compilation clearly demonstrate that no such proposed intimation was ever issued to the Appellant. It is a settled principle of law that no adjustment prejudicial can be made without affording a reasonable opportunity of being heard. In this regard, it is respectfully submitted that the Appellant was denied any opportunity to rebut or explain its case at the intimation stage, as the mandatory notice proposing adjustment under section 143(1)(a) of the Act was never issued to the Appellant.” 4.1 In support of its contentions, the Ld. AR has cited several decisions of the coordinate benches as similar facts and circumstances. With regard to the specific additions made by the CPC, it has been submitted that some of the disallowances had already been made suo moto by the assessee in its return while remaining issues were debatable and thus beyond the scope of adjustment u/s 143(1) of the Act. 5. Ld. DR, on the other hand, has argued that the intimation u/s 143(1) has been set aside to the AO for fresh consideration after giving due opportunity to the assessee and therefore, no interference is called for in the order of CIT(A). 6. We have heard the rival submissions and perused the material available on record. We first take up the ground relating to non-issue of mandatory notice before making adjustments u/s 143(1) of the Act by the CPC. 6.1 It has been stated by the Ld. AR that no notice was issued to the assessee before making adjustments as mandated by the Act. This ground was raised before the CIT(A) also but he did not adjudicate it. Before us, Ld. DR has not been able to rebut the assertion of the Ld. AR that no prior notice was issued before making the adjustments. In view of clear provisions of the first proviso of section 143(1)(a), regarding issue of notice mandatorily making before adjustment, we are of the considered view that in the absence of any evidence regarding prior mandatory opportunity having been given to the assessee, the impugned order is liable to be quashed. Besides several orders of the coordinate benches relied upon by the Ld. AR, we note that this issue has also been decided in favour of the assessee by the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the following cases:- (i) Rallis India Ltd. vs. CPC & Others, order dated 19.01.2026 in WP (L) No. 37314 of 2025 (ii) Bax India Ventures Pvt. Ltd. vs. CPC & Others [2026] 183 taxmann.com 395 6.2 Thus, in the absence of mandatory notice giving opportunity to the assessee, the intimation dated 27.05.2024 u/s 143(1) is bad in law and is hereby quashed. 7. In the result, appeal of the assessee is allowed.
