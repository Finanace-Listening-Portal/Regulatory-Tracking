The bank’s decision will help borrowers whose loans are linked to MCLR and whose interest rate resets based on the relevant MCLR. MCLR-linked loans do not necessarily change every time a bank changes its benchmark. The reset frequency is governed by the terms of the loan agreement. Therefore, a borrower whose reset date falls after the revised MCLR comes into effect could see the interest rate on the loan reduced. For example, a borrower with a loan linked to the one-year MCLR will not necessarily get the benefit on October 7 simply because the one-year MCLR has fallen. The borrower must also check when the loan's interest rate is due for reset. The size of the benefit will depend on the outstanding loan, remaining tenure and the spread charged by the bank. What does a 10-basis-point cut mean for EMI? A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point. Therefore, a 10-basis-point reduction means the interest rate falls by 0.10 percentage point. The reduction may look small, but it can make a difference over a large outstanding home loan and a long repayment period. For instance, if the interest rate on a Rs 50 lakh outstanding loan falls from 8.50 per cent to 8.40 per cent, the EMI reduction will depend on the remaining tenure. A borrower with a longer repayment period will generally see a larger cumulative saving in interest, although the monthly EMI reduction may be modest. Borrowers should therefore look at the total interest saving, rather than only the change in EMI. MCLR cut does not mean all home loans will become cheaperThis is important because many newer retail loans are linked to an external benchmark rather than MCLR. The Reserve Bank of India requires banks to link floating-rate retail and MSME loans to external benchmarks. These can include the RBI repo rate and certain Treasury bill yields. Therefore, a borrower should first check the loan sanction letter or statement to identify the benchmark applicable to the loan. If the loan is repo-linked, a change in HDFC Bank's MCLR by itself will not automatically change the borrower's interest rate. The move comes a day after the RBI raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50 per cent, making the MCLR cut notable for borrowers. What borrowers should check HDFC Bank loan customers should check four things before assuming that their EMI will fall: Benchmark: Is the loan linked to MCLR, repo rate or another benchmark? Reset date: When will the revised benchmark be applied to the loan? Spread: What margin is the bank charging over the benchmark? Outstanding balance and tenure: How much of the loan remains and for how long? The MCLR system was introduced by the RBI in 2016 to make banks' lending rates more responsive to their funding costs. For an MCLR-linked borrower, the latest reduction is therefore positive, but the actual benefit will depend on the individual loan contract.
