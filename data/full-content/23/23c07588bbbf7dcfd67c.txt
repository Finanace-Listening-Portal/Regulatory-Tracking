(NCLT Mumbai) Summary : The National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai Bench-I, partly allowed the application filed by Savronik BCC JV Construction Private Limited against IL&FS Transportation Networks Limited (ITNL), concerning the verification and admission of outstanding construction claims arising from the Chenani-Nashri Tunnel project in Jammu and Kashmir. The dispute arose in the resolution process being conducted pursuant to proceedings initiated by the Union of India under Section 241(2) of the Companies Act, 2013, concerning alleged mismanagement in the IL&FS group. The applicant challenged the Claims Management Advisor’s (CMA) repeated downward revisions of its admitted claims, rejection of several contractual dues, adjustment of alleged counterclaims and computation of interest. Its total claim was approximately ₹153.91 crore, while the amounts recognised by the CMA had changed substantially during successive verification exercises. The applicant contended that ITNL could not unilaterally reduce legitimate dues through unadjudicated deductions and counterclaims. The Tribunal first examined whether the applicant could pursue the disputed components after withdrawing CA No. 1001 of 2020 with liberty to institute fresh proceedings. It held that such liberty did not automatically extend the limitation period. Claims concerning liquidated damages, undue deductions, reduction in the scope of work and delay in execution were barred by limitation because their rejection had not been challenged in the earlier application and no subsequent reconsideration had created a fresh cause of action. However, the Tribunal permitted the applicant to pursue arbitration concerning these claims if otherwise maintainable under the contract and applicable limitation law. Conversely, claims involving non-scheduled work, materials and services supplied to third parties, relocation of the batching plant and withheld payments were not barred, as subsequent verification and additional material had disclosed fresh grounds for contesting the rejected amounts. On the merits, the Tribunal found that the applicant had furnished comprehensive supporting details for its claims relating to non-scheduled work, third-party supplies and relocation of the batching plant. ITNL admitted that it could not verify the corresponding amounts charged to the project developer or determine the monetary benefits received from those activities because of the age of the records and the involvement of multiple subcontractors. The Tribunal held that the applicant had discharged its initial evidentiary burden, shifting the onus to ITNL. In the absence of cogent evidence disproving execution of the work, supply of materials or relocation on ITNL’s instructions, the claims deserved admission on equitable grounds. The Tribunal directed that quantities and measurements claimed by the applicant be valued using the rates finally approved by ITNL for the respective work or services. Regarding retention money, the Tribunal rejected the CMA’s assumption that completion occurred on the date the taking-over certificate was issued, 13 June 2017. The certificate expressly recorded that the applicant had fulfilled the taking-over requirements on 31 March 2017. Since the contractual defect-liability period was twelve months, interest on retention money became payable from 31 March 2018, rather than the later date adopted by the CMA. The Tribunal accordingly directed recomputation and admission of the interest claim. The Tribunal separately considered the claim arising from changes in law. Under Clause 12.19 of the construction contract, read with Article 41 of the concession agreement, an increase in the contract price arising from a change in law required approval from the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI). As such approval remained outstanding, the Tribunal upheld the CMA’s classification of the claim as contingent. Nevertheless, ITNL was directed to pursue the matter reasonably and diligently with NHAI, keep the applicant informed of follow-up communications and, where necessary, authorise the applicant’s representative to pursue the matter on its behalf. On interest for delayed payments, the Tribunal held that belated approval of contractual claims could not deprive the contractor of interest on amounts that had already become provisionally payable. The contractual provisions contemplated provisional payments pending finalisation of rates, and ITNL could not rely on its own delay in approval to postpone the accrual of interest. However, the applicant’s demand for interest at 18% per annum was rejected because the contract prescribed the State Bank of India’s prime lending rate plus 2%. The Tribunal directed computation of interest on admitted claims under the relevant heads from 16 December 2016 at SBI PLR plus 2%, while recognising that no interest was presently payable on the change-in-law claim without the requisite NHAI approval. Accordingly, CA No. 90 of 2026 was partly allowed and disposed of, granting substantial relief on admissibility and interest while rejecting the time-barred components. Cases Discussed 1. Abhiman Apartment Co-operative Housing Society Limited, Represented by Liquidator Sri G.S. Bomme Gowda and Others v. Kasim Sab Peersab Nadaf & Others, 2023 SCC OnLine Kar 1578 (Karnataka High Court). Cited by ITNL for the proposition that permission to withdraw proceedings and institute fresh proceedings does not create a new cause of action or confer immunity from limitation. 2. Arifa and Others v. Abhiman Apartment Co-operative Housing Society Limited and Others, 2025 SCC OnLine SC 1492 (Supreme Court). Relied upon by ITNL to establish that limitation continues to apply to fresh proceedings instituted with judicial permission and cannot be calculated merely from the date liberty was granted. The learned Counsel for ITNL submits that the Applicant cannot raise fresh claims in this Application under the liberty granted by this Tribunal when it permitted the withdrawal of CA 1001 of 2020. Such claims must be examined independently for limitation, as the liberty granted by the Tribunal cannot create a fresh cause of action that relates to the filing date of the original application. In support, the learned Counsel relied on Abhiman Apartment Co-operative Housing Society Limited, Represented by Liquidator Sri. G.S. Bomme Gowda and Others Vs. Kasim Sab Peersab Nadaf & Others, 2023 SCC OnLine Kar 1578, which held that “ In case liberty is granted to a party to file a fresh suit or a comprehensive suit, the order of granting liberty will never give rise to cause of action nor can it be construed so. Once a suit is finally adjudicated, any suit instituted subsequently by any of the parties or persons claiming title under the parties to the suit must plead independent cause of action, only when the court permits a plaintiff under sub rule (3) of rule 1 of Order 23 of CPC, to withdraw, the suit, he can bring a fresh suit in respect of same subject matter, but the fresh suit is also not immune to law of limitation”. 12. The learned Counsel for ITNL also raised an issue of limitation concerning a new component of the case and relied on the decision in Arifa and Others Versus Abhiman Apartment Co-operative Housing Society Limited and Others, 2025 SCC Online SC 1492, which held that “8………… The specific averment regarding the cause of action, as stated in the plaint was extracted, which was the date on which the High Court had dismissed the second appeal and reserved such liberty. The limitation would commence from the date of execution of the agreements, the first two of which were in the year 1998. Though the two sale deeds in favour of the Society were thus executed, when the first suit was pending, the plaintiff did nothing to challenge the said conveyance. In the earlier suit, the defendant, in the written statement, had specifically pleaded regarding the conveyances, and the plaintiff admitted his knowledge of all the conveyances while verifying the ‘record of rights’. No cause of action can be claimed on the liberty reserved, which is only on just exceptions including limitation, which in any event has to go by the period prescribed in the statute of limitation. The High Court has further found that there can also be no resort to Section 14 of the Limitation Act, since the suit already laid was not before a wrong forum but was not properly framed. Even when a suit is withdrawn with leave of the Court to file a fresh suit, under Order 23 Rule 1 of the CPC limitation applies with full force as per Rule 2 of Order 23.” 13. Order 23 Rule 1 read with Rule 2 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (“CPC”) expressly provides that where a plaintiff is permitted to withdraw a suit with liberty to institute a fresh suit in respect of the same subject-matter, the fresh suit remains subject to the law of limitation as if the first suit had not been instituted. Order 23 Rule 2 of the CPC is reproduced below: “In any fresh suit instituted on permission granted under the last preceding rule, the plaintiff shall be bound by the law of limitation in the same manner as if the first suit had not been instituted.” 14. Learned Counsel for the Applicant contends that CMA has never made a final determination of the claim. Instead, CMA has wrongfully permitted illegal set-offs from time to time without the adjudication required by law, as detailed in the following sub­paragraphs. Each instance has given the Applicant a fresh cause of action, making this a continuing wrong. Further, CMA has never rejected the balance of the claim; it has consistently maintained that the balance requires adjudication. The Applicant accepts that the prayer in Application CA 1001 sought a restraint order against the impermissible re-evaluation of the admitted claim of Rs. 67 Crores (which claim was allegedly admitted erroneously by the CMA, per its own admission, and was disclosed to the Applicant only after the Application CA 1001 was filed). The Applicant distinguishes that prayer from the prayer in Application (CA 90), stating that the present application seeks a final determination of the Applicant’s claims as required by law, without any illegal set-off in the absence of adjudication of ITNL’s set-off/counterclaim, as settled law requires. 15. In the present case, the Applicant sought only a direction that the admitted claim of Rs. 67,04,26,913.92/-, which included claims for Retention Money, Change in Law, and Interest, be treated as the final amount payable to the Applicant, and that CMA not carry out any further revaluation. CMA removed the interest claim, leaving only Retention Money and Change in Law. Accordingly, the dispute in CA 1001 of 2020 was limited to the admission of the Change in Law claim as a contingent claim and the interest claim. During arguments in CA 1001 of 2020, CMA admitted portions of the claims for Non-Scheduled Item, Payment of Supplied Material & Services, Relocation of Batching Plant, and Withheld Payments. It also readmitted the interest claim to the extent of Rs. 1,59,80,498/-, bringing the total admitted claim to Rs. 18,34,71,012/-. The present application was filed pursuant to the liberty granted by this Tribunal to the Applicant to file a fresh Application raising all the contentions in relation to their claim, after observing that CMA has already re-evaluated the Applicant’s claim during the proceedings in CA 1001 of 2020. 16. Admittedly, in CA 1001 of 2020, the Applicant did not challenge the rejection of claims under heads other than Retention Money, Change in Law, and Interest. The Applicant merely pleaded that “there are other claims under different heads which are also legally due and payable based on the documents in support thereof submitted by the Applicant including correspondence from ITNL admitting the liability”, referring to other components of the total claim of Rs. 153.91 crores. In other words, the Applicant did not challenge the earlier rejection of claims under “Liquidated damages, Undue deductions, Scope Reduction, and Delay of work”, and those claims have not since been re-evaluated or revised. Accordingly, the Applicant’s contention that the re-examination of its claims in April 2024 and October 2024 gives rise to a fresh cause of action has no merit insofar as these heads are concerned; hence, the Applicant’s claim under “Liquidated damages, Undue deductions, Scope Reduction, and Delay of work”, being barred by limitation as on the date of filing of CA 90 of 2026, cannot be adjudicated by this Tribunal. Nonetheless, the applicant shall be at liberty to initiate arbitration proceedings in relation to these heads of the claim, if such arbitration proceedings are otherwise permissible under the relevant contract and the law governing the limitation in arbitration. 17. Insofar as the claims under the heads Non-Scheduled Item, Payment of Supplied Material & Services, Relocation of Batching Plant, and Withheld Payments are concerned, it is relevant to note that these claims under these heads were initially rejected in whole. As regards these heads, it is noted that the re-examination of the applicant’s claims in April 2024 and October 2024 did not relate to them; instead, ITNL’s Counter Claim against the applicant was admitted by the CMA in those re-examination exercises. However, part of these claims were admitted pursuant to the direction of this Tribunal for re-verification during the pendency of CA 1001 of 2020 and additional material indicating the reasons for rejection of the remaining part of the claims under these heads led to the applicant’s request for filing a fresh application for admission of such portion of the rejected claim. Admittedly, the applicant has raised claims in relation to these heads afresh. They were not part of the prayers in CA 1001 of 2020; however, considering that additional material being placed on record by ITNL/CMA had led to the discovery of fresh grounds for claiming the amounts under these heads beyond what got admitted during the pendency of CA 1001 of 2020, we are of the considered view that the claims under these heads cannot be said to be barred by limitation, particularly when this Tribunal had allowed the applicant to raise all contentions in relation to their claim. 18. After hearing the parties, this Tribunal had required ITNL, via order dated 29.7.2026, to clarify as regards partial admission of claims under the heads namely “Non-Scheduled Item, Payment of Supplied Material & Services, Relocation of Batching Plant, Withheld Payments”, in their written submissions whether (i) the additional work done by applicant was charged by ITNL from their clients; (ii) whether the defects/short comings for which deductions were rectified by the Applicant and if any deduction was made by the client in relation to such defect/shortcoming from their bills; and (iii) whether the material supplied to the third parties and relocation of batches had resulted into any corresponding benefit in monetary terms to ITNL, and such material and relocation was required for execution of the work. 24. In relation to Non-Scheduled Items, Payment of Supplied Material & Services, and Relocation of Batching Plant, ITNL submits in their written submissions that “As per the letter dated January 12, 2017, addressed by ITNL to Savronik, it has been stated that the rates were finalized by both parties, and it has been further mentioned that Savronik failed to justify the excess quantity claimed by it in respect of nonscheduled items and payments for supply of materials and services to third parties. From the said letter, it is evident that claims for non-scheduled items, payments for supply of materials and services to third parties, and relocation of batching plant remained disputed between both parties and that there was no unilateral treatment by ITNL”. It further submits that “On the specific query of whether ITNL received any corresponding monetary benefit, and the amounts it charged its clients in this regard, it is respectfully submitted that ITNL has not been able to cross-verify the exact rates and quantities it charged Chenani Nashri Tunnelway Limited (“CNTL”) for these claims from its available records, as the matters in question are historical, dating back close to a decade, It is further relevant to note that Savronik was only one of several sub-contractors engaged by ITNL, which makes it particularly difficult to retrieve itemized, sub-contractor wise information on the charges levied upon CNTL. 25. In relation to withheld payment, ITNL submits in their written submissions that “It follows, then, that the issuance of the take-over certificate cannot by itself answer the question of whether the defects were in fact rectified. This is precisely the clarification this Hon’ble Tribunal sought by its order dated July 29, 2026, namely, whether the defects/shortcomings for which deductions were made were rectified by Savronik, and whether any deduction was made by the client in relation to such defect/shortcoming from their bills. In answer to that query, it is submitted that the letter dated March 29, 2017 addressed by ITNL to Savronik makes it evident that, on account of Savronik’s failure to rectify the defects, ITNL was compelled to engage third parties to carry out the rectification para 3”. 26. In relation to Non-Scheduled Items, Payment of Supplied Material & Services, and Relocation of Batching Plant, ITNL has expressed its inability to ascertain from its records whether any corresponding benefit was received from them or whether they had raised any corresponding claim upon the developer or a third party. It is noted that the Applicant has provided comprehensive details in relation to its claim under these heads; hence, it has discharged the initial onus in this regard, thereby shifting the onus upon ITNL. In view of ITNL’s failure to discharge its onus, we are of the considered view that the Applicant’s claim under these heads deserves to be admitted on equitable grounds, as ITNL cannot take advantage of its own wrong, i.e., failure to cross-verify the corresponding receipt of material/services by third parties provided by the Applicant to them on ITNL’s instructions. It is for ITNL to disprove the Applicant’s claim on the basis of cogent evidence(s), and it cannot do so in the absence of cogent evidence demonstrating that the Non-scheduled work was not executed, that material/services to third parties were not supplied on its instructions, or that the relocation of the batching plant was not at its instruction. Since the rate for such work or material/services was to be approved by ITNL, the rate as finally approved by ITNL for each work/service shall be applied to the quantities/measurement claimed by the Applicant for admission of the Applicant’s claim under these heads. 27. As regards admission of claim dispute in relation to interest payable on Retention Money, it is noted that CMA has admitted claim for interest on delay in release of retention money w.e.f. from June 12, 2018 as against from March 31, 2018 claim by the applicant. Applicant submits that, as per the Taking Over Certificate dated 13 June 2017, ITNL had stated that taking over requirements (i.e. achievement of completion certificate as per Clause 10.1 of the agreement was fulfilled by the applicant on 31 March 2017, therefore, as per Clause 12.7 read with Clause 1.1 and 10.1 of the agreement, the retention money was to be released upon completion of the defect liability period being 12 months (i.e. 31 March 2018) from the date of completion (i.e. 31 March 2017). Per contra, CMA submits that the Taking-Over Certificate for the Project was issued on June 13, 2017; and according to Appendix 6 of the contract, the Taking-Over Certificate can only be issued after receipt of inter alia the completion certificate; therefore, on the date of issuance of the Taking-Over Certificate, the Completion Certificate will already have been issued, hence, it can be said that the Completion Certificate was issued on the same date, i.e. 13.06.2017. It is noted that Take Over Certificate dated 13.06.2017 clearly states that “taking over requirement fulfilled by M/s Savronik-BCC Construction Pvt Ltd on 31st March 2017, in accordance with clause 10.1 of the contract”, hence, CMA’s assumption that completion would have be on date of issuance of Taking Over Certificate is contrary to the statement in the taking over certificate itself as well as CMA’s own assumption that the Completion Certificate will already have been issued on or before issuance of Take Over Certificate. Accordingly, we are of the considered view that the interest on Retention is payable from 31st March 2018, and not from 13.06.2018, as determined by the CMA. Accordingly, the Applicant’s claim in relation to the Interest on Retention Money shall be recomputed and admitted. 28. As regards admission of claim under the head Change in Law as contingent claim, it is noted that Clause 12.19 of the Construction Contract makes the addition or reduction in the contract price subject to provisions of Article 41 of the concession agreement dated June 28, 2010. Further, Article 41 requires NHAI’s approval for an increase in cost arising from a change in law, and ITNL is stated to have written to NHAI vide letter dated 19.01.2018 for increased costs due to Change in Law, which the NHAI has yet to acknowledge. Since the claim of the Applicant under this head is like a pass-through obligation on the part of the ITNL, the admission of the claim under these heads as contingent does not warrant any interference; however, ITNL shall reasonably and diligently follow up with NHAI and shall keep the Applicant in the loop of all such follow-up communications, and, if required, shall authorise the Applicant’s representative to follow up with NHAI on behalf of ITNL. 29. As regards downward revision of admitted interest claim on delayed payments under the contract, the Applicant submits that CMA has wrongly computed interest from the date of ITNL’s e-mail approval (18.08.2017) whereas the interest on claims other than retention money was required to be calculated from the date of claim made by APPLICANT (Final claim made on 16.12.2016). The Applicant had claimed, vide its letter dated 16.12.2016, interest @ 18% p.a. on its claim under various heads amounting to Rs 558,633,209.73, the details of which are reproduced hereinbelow, besides its claim for delay of work : 30. While the applicant has claimed interest from the date of its letter making additional claims, i.e., 16.12.2026, the CMA admitted interest on the admitted claims under these heads w.e.f. the date of approval received from ITNL, i.e., 18.08.2017. The CMA states that, pursuant to clause 22.9 of the Construction Contract, in case of a delay in payment by ITNL beyond the due date of such payment, the Applicant would have a right to claim interest on such unpaid amounts due from ITNL. Therefore, the CMA proceeded with the calculation of interest on the amounts admitted above on a best estimate basis, up to the Cut-off date. It is further stated by the CMA that the applicant had shared interest computations with the CMA via email dated 11.12.2019, wherein interest was calculated after considering (a) payments received against older outstanding invoices, (b) the rate and quantity used to compute the outstanding amount were not approved by ITNL, (c) the outstanding amount was a sum of individual claim components, part of which were not admitted, and (d) interest calculated @ 18% as against the rate mentioned in clause 29.2, i.e., SBI-LLR + 2%. On further seeking details of computation, the applicant sent the interest workings on a consolidated basis via email dated 8.5.2020 instead of providing the same separately for each claim component. Accordingly, the applicant was again asked via email dated 10.08.2020 to provide (a) detailed interest workings for each claim component on a standalone basis, specifically for the amounts accepted by ITNL in its approval letter, and (b) rationale for claiming 18% interest instead of the rate provided in clause 29.2. However, the CMA did not receive a response from the Applicant; it therefore calculated interest on the amounts admitted on a best-estimate basis. 31. Per contra, the Applicant states that interest on delayed payment cannot be denied merely because ITNL approved the additional claim amounts belatedly. 32. Clause 22.9 of the Contract provides for Interest on Delayed Payment and states that “If payment under the Contract is delayed beyond the due date, the non-defaulting Party is entitled to receive interest on the amount unpaid during the period of delay at the rate of [two] percent per annum above the base lending rate of [SBI-PLR] both before and after any judgment or award. The non-defaulting Party is entitled to the payment without formal notice and without prejudice to any other right”. The rate of interest is clearly provided in clause 29.2 of the contract; hence, the interest can only be claimed @ SBI PLR +2% and not 18%. 33. Further, clause 12.4 of the Contract provides that “In case mutually agreeable settlement of rates is not arrived at between Developer’s Representative and the Contractor, the Contractor shall be bound to carry out the Works at rates to be decided by Developer’s Representative. In the absence of a finalized rate for a new item, Developer’s Representative shall be entitled to certify payment to the Contractor based on a provisional rate fixed by Developer’s Representative for the work done under the new item. This shall be subject to upward or downward adjustment after the rate is finalized by Developer’s Representative for that item”. This Clause, therefore, requires ITNL to pay the amounts on a provisional basis. Accordingly, ITNL was obligated to make payment on a provisional basis pending approval, and the said payment was subject to adjustments upon final approval; hence, the delay in approval on the part of ITNL cannot disentitle the Applicant from claiming interest on amounts that had provisionally become due. 34. Further, interest on claims under ‘Non-Scheduled Items’, ‘Payment for supplied material and services’ and ‘Relocation of batching plant’, which were not part of the contract scope, should be computed from the date of the claim, which was found admissible in terms of final approval, and not from the date of final approval. As regards interest on withheld payments, interest should be paid from the date ITNL received the intimation for rectified work from the Applicant. As regards interest on amounts due under the “Change in Law’ head, since these amounts were payable on receipt of approval from NHAI, no interest can be claimed unless NHAI approval is on record, which has not yet been received. Further, interest on amounts not admitted as payable by CMA does not become payable unless the principal amount is found payable; accordingly, interest on amounts not admitted under various heads cannot be claimed, and there is no infirmity in CMA’s action in restricting interest on the admitted claims. Since ascertaining the exact dates for these components may require further inquiry, and the Applicant has made claims for these heads vide letter dated 16.12.2016, we consider it appropriate to direct computation of interest payable on the admitted amounts of claims under these heads w.e.f. 16.12.2016 @ SBI PLR +2%. 35. In terms of the above, CA 90 of 2026 is partly allowed and disposed of.
