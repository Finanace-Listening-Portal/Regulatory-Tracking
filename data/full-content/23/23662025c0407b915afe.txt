Articles 4 min read YES Bank keeps AT-1 coupon payment decision open, says it has coupon discretion YES Bank is yet to decide on the payment of coupons on its AT-1 bonds after the Supreme Court set aside the 2020 write-off of Rs 8,415 crore. The bank said it retains coupon discretion under the bond terms and is assessing the judgment and its implications and shall communicate the final decision in due course. Advt The lender said that since its evaluation and assessment had not been completed, it would communicate its decision, if any, on whether or not to exercise coupon discretion after completing the assessment.The disclosure comes after the scheduled October 18 coupon payment date for YES Bank's 9% AT-1 bonds issued under the October 17, 2017 Information Memorandum. The coupon date fell three days after the Supreme Court's October 15 judgment on the bonds.What YES Bank said on October 19The latest disclosure follows commentary from YES Bank's management during its October 19 analyst call, when the lender said it was still assessing the accounting, capital and other implications of the Supreme Court judgment.YES Bank MD & CEO Vinay Tonse had said the reinstatement of the AT-1 principal amount was expected to have an indicative pro forma impact of around 240 basis points on the bank's CET1 ratio, including profits, based on the position as of September 30, 2026.The bank's reported CET1 ratio stood at 13.7% at the end of September. On a pro forma basis, the ratio would therefore fall to about 11.4%, while the overall Tier-1 capital ratio was expected to remain around 13.7%.CFO Niranjan Banodkar had said the pro forma CET1 level was not ideal for the bank, which had earlier operated with a comfort level of around 13.5-14%, although he said the bank continued to have options to support its capital position and growth. Advt The bank had also clarified that the reinstatement had not been recognised in its reported Q2 FY27 results, pending assessment of the appropriate accounting and regulatory treatment. On a pro forma basis, the bank's reported Rs 1,080-crore Q2 net profit would have turned into a Rs 5,217-crore loss if the impact had been routed through the profit and loss account.That represents a Rs 6,297-crore swing in reported quarterly profit on the pro forma presentation disclosed by the bank.Coupon payment remains a separate decisionThe Supreme Court, while upholding the Bombay High Court's decision that set aside the March 2020 write-off, also addressed the treatment of annual coupon payments under the AT-1 bonds. Bondholders have argued that the coupon payments should follow the respective Information Memoranda. Advt YES Bank's management had said on the October 19 call that the manner and timing of reinstatement of the bonds as well as payment of the coupon remained under assessment.“The manner and the timing of restatement of the bonds as well as payment of the coupon is also under assessment,” Banodkar had said during the analyst call. He subsequently said future coupons would also form part of the bank's assessment and that decisions would be taken after it concluded its evaluation.The bank has not, however, said that the coupon will not be paid. Instead, it has said that a decision will follow once its assessment of the Supreme Court judgment and its consequential implications is complete. Advt Rs 8,415-crore AT-1 reinstatementThe issue dates back to March 2020, when YES Bank's AT-1 bonds worth Rs 8,415 crore were written down to zero as part of the bank's reconstruction. Bondholders challenged the action, and the Bombay High Court in January 2023 set aside the write-off.On October 15, 2026, the Supreme Court upheld the High Court's decision, effectively requiring YES Bank to deal with the consequences of the reinstatement of the AT-1 instruments.For the capital impact, the bank has already indicated that reinstatement of the principal could lower CET1 by about 240 basis points on a pro forma basis. For earnings, its Q2 disclosure showed the potential P&L impact at Rs 6,297 crore, taking the reported Rs 1,080-crore profit to a pro forma Rs 5,217-crore loss.
