(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India declined to interfere with the judgment of the Calcutta High Court in the reassessment dispute involving Haldia Petrochemicals Ltd. and dismissed the Revenue’s Special Leave Petitions. The underlying proceedings concerned Assessment Year 2008-09, in which the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Kolkata, had quashed a reassessment order dated 21 January 2016. The Revenue challenged the Tribunal’s decision before the Calcutta High Court under Section 260A of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The controversy included disallowance of Rs.6,92,90,000 claimed on account of provision for Value Added Tax (VAT), but the central legal question was whether the assessment had been validly reopened. Read HC Judgment in this case: Reassessment Invalid Without Failure to Disclose Material Facts: Calcutta HC Revenue’s Challenge Before the Calcutta High Court The Revenue contended that the Tribunal had erred in holding the reopening invalid and in deleting the addition arising from the VAT provision. It argued that the Tribunal had incorrectly concluded that the recorded reasons contained no allegation of failure by the assessee to disclose fully and truly material facts for Assessment Year 2008-09. According to the Revenue’s proposed questions of law, the relevant facts appeared in the notes to accounts for the subsequent Assessment Year 2009-10 rather than the year under consideration. The Revenue therefore questioned the Tribunal’s decision to invalidate the proceedings initiated under Section 148. Calcutta High Court’s Findings on Reassessment The Calcutta High Court examined the reasons recorded for reopening, as reproduced in the assessment order passed under Section 147 read with Sections 154 and 143(3). It found that the Assessing Officer had not alleged any failure by Haldia Petrochemicals Ltd. to disclose material facts necessary for the assessment. Instead, the recorded reasons showed that material already available in the books of account had been reappraised before issuing the reopening notice. The High Court held that, in the absence of the necessary allegation concerning disclosure failure, the Tribunal was justified in holding the reassessment invalid. Finding no substantial question of law arising for consideration, the High Court dismissed the Revenue’s appeal in ITAT/252/2022. It separately condoned a delay of 378 days in filing the appeal, granting the benefit of the Supreme Court’s order extending limitation periods, and closed GA/2/2022. Supreme Court’s Order and Final Outcome In the subsequent Special Leave Petition proceedings, the Supreme Court stated that it was not inclined to interfere with the impugned judgment and dismissed the petitions. It also disposed of any pending applications. Consequently, the Calcutta High Court’s decision upholding the Tribunal’s invalidation of the reassessment remained undisturbed. The supplied Supreme Court order contains only this brief disposition; it does not independently discuss the recorded reasons, the VAT provision, or the statutory requirements governing reassessment. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER We are not inclined to interfere with the impugned judgment and hence, the special leave petitions are dismissed. Pending application(s), if any, shall stand disposed of.
