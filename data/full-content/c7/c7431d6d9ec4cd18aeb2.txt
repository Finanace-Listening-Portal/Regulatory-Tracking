Articles 2 min read Insolvency framework should be responsive to dynamic needs of economy: Official PTI Published On Nov 23, 2026 at 05:14 PM IST var news_msid = 136101149; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook New Delhi, The country's insolvency framework should be responsive to the dynamic needs of the economy and public authorities need to promptly give effect to approved insolvency resolution plans, a senior government official said on Monday.Regarding the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), Corporate Affairs Secretary Pallavi Jain Govil also said that further reforms and processes are on the way.The IBC provides for a market-linked and time-bound resolution of stressed assets. The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) is a key institution in implementing the Code."Looking ahead, our collective objective should be clear. We must try to create an insolvency framework that is faster, more predictable... technologically enabled, globally benchmarked and responsive to the needs of a dynamic economy," Govil said. Advt She was virtually addressing the event to mark the 10th Foundation Day of the Indian Institute of Insolvency Professionals of ICAI (IIIPI) in the national capital."We shall be urging all public authorities to give effect to approved plans promptly so that the new owners can start operations immediately," Govil said.Her remarks also come against the backdrop of many resolution processes facing inordinate delays due to various factors, including prolonged litigations.According to her, approval is not the end of the resolution process and it is where revival begins."A plan approved on paper helps no one if the business is not handed over, the money is not paid, or the commitments are not kept," she said.Creditors have realised more than Rs 4.30 lakh crore through approved insolvency resolution plans."The Code is now the most effective channel for banks to recover stressed assets," the corporate affairs secretary said.IBBI Whole Time Member Sandip Garg said that generally a corporate insolvency resolution process that ends in an approved plan takes about 633 days and much of the delay lies in litigation."(Insolvency) professionals can also play a major role in minimising the litigation by treating the stakeholders fairly, giving them adequate opportunity, by redressing their grievances early, and by explaining their decisions to stakeholders," he said. Advt At the event, ICAI President Prasanna Kumar D said that along with the ease of doing business, the speed of doing business is important.The Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) is the apex body of chartered accountants.
