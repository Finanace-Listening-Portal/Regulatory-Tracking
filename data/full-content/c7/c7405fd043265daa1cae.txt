Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Vs Rajesh Jain (ITAT Dehradun) Section 54F Relief Cannot Be Denied by Counting a Property Used as a Dairy Farm as a Second Residential House: ITAT Dehradun The Dehradun Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held that a property let out and used as a commercial dairy farm could not be counted as a second residential house for denying Section 54F relief. The Tribunal also upheld the assessee’s claim under Section 54B for reinvestment in agricultural land and deleted the 20% ad hoc disallowance of land development expenditure. It remanded a separate addition relating to an alleged land sale for verification. Facts of the Case Rajesh Jain, an individual engaged in real estate and hotel businesses, declared income of ₹32,75,400 for AY 2017–18. He reported long-term capital gains of ₹3,58,94,559 from the sale of property at Mauza Adhoiwala for ₹3,95,94,465. The assessment involved claims under Section 54B for purchase of agricultural land and Section 54F for purchase of a residential flat. The Assessing Officer (AO) restricted the Section 54B deduction to ₹13,32,874, citing the failure to deposit unutilised funds in the specified account and questioning the agricultural character of part of the replacement land. The AO denied Section 54F relief on the ground that the assessee owned two residential properties. After making a further addition relating to allegedly undisclosed property sales, the AO assessed total income at ₹4,52,47,965. The Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) [CIT(A)] granted relief under both sections but sustained a 20% disallowance of land development expenditure and directed verification of the disputed sales. Both parties appealed to the Tribunal. Commercial Use of Dairy Premises Supported Section 54F Claim The assessee owned a house in Vasant Vihar, New Delhi, and a property at Jakhan, Rajpur Road, Dehradun. The latter had been let to Narendra Gauseva Society for running a dairy farm. The Revenue argued that the premises were in a residential area and that the assessee had disclosed the rent under the head Income from House Property. It therefore sought to count the dairy premises as another residential house under Section 54F. The CIT(A) examined photographs of the dairy farm, the tenant’s income-tax returns and audited financial statements evidencing rent payments. The AO did not offer comments on the Section 54F issue despite reminders during the remand proceedings. The Tribunal found that the Revenue had failed to rebut the finding that the premises were used commercially. On those facts, the actual use of the property supported its exclusion from the residential house count. The Tribunal accordingly upheld the Section 54F relief of ₹66,91,200. Section 54B Relief Upheld Following Assessee’s Own Earlier Case The Revenue argued that the assessee had failed to deposit unutilised funds in the Capital Gains Account Scheme by the return filing due date. It also challenged relief on the entire 73.24-acre parcel purchased in Tuticorin, relying on an inspection report indicating that roughly 27 acres were under cultivation. The assessee submitted that the report did not establish that the remaining land was unsuitable for agricultural use and argued that the purpose for which the land was purchased was relevant under Section 54B. The Tribunal followed the coordinate bench decision in the assessee’s own case for AY 2016–17, in ITA No. 1/DDN/2026. In that year, relief had been upheld where the assessee substantially reinvested in agricultural land within two years despite the absence of a deposit under the scheme. The Revenue failed to distinguish the facts of the year under appeal. The Tribunal therefore upheld the CIT(A)’s decision allowing relief on the agricultural land purchases. 20% Ad Hoc Disallowance of Development Expenses Deleted The Revenue questioned the cash payments and handwritten vouchers supporting the development expenditure. On examining the invoices, the Tribunal found that they contained addresses and mobile numbers. The authorities could have used those details to verify the service providers under Section 133(6). Neither the AO nor the CIT(A) identified any specific expenditure unrelated to development of the land. The fact that the land had been developed after purchase was undisputed. The Tribunal therefore deleted the 20% disallowance and allowed the Section 54B claim in full. Alleged ₹8.91 Lakh Land Sale Sent Back for Verification The assessee denied selling land to Habib Ahmad for ₹8,91,000. The Tribunal observed that the AO had failed to verify the transaction with the land revenue authorities, including while giving effect to the CIT(A)’s order. It restored the issue to the AO and directed that the addition be dropped if verification showed that the assessee had made no such sale. The Tribunal treated interest under Sections 234B and 234C as consequential and held that the challenge to initiation of penalty under Section 270A was premature. The Revenue’s appeal was dismissed, and the assessee’s appeal was partly allowed. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is partly allowed and appeal of the Revenue is dismissed. Order pronounced in the open Court 18.11.2026.
