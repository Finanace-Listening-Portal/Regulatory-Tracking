He said financial institutions could increasingly depend on a relatively small number of cloud providers, technology vendors and model providers, often using overlapping datasets and similar technological infrastructure. Advt The concern is that a problem at a common technology provider could extend beyond an individual financial institution. If several institutions depend on the same provider or infrastructure, a disruption or error could affect multiple institutions at the same time, creating a channel through which an operational problem could spread across the financial system.The issue is becoming more relevant as artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies become more deeply embedded in financial services. Jain said technology can amplify existing risks by allowing their effects to travel through the financial system faster, wider and sometimes in ways that are harder to detect.Common technology dependenciesTechnology concentration differs from a failure confined to an individual bank because the same provider can potentially serve several financial institutions. The extent of any disruption would depend on the nature of the dependency, the functions supported by the provider and the number of institutions exposed to it.The increasing use of AI models adds another dimension to the concentration risk. Financial institutions could increasingly rely on a relatively small group of model providers and similar technological infrastructure, creating common dependencies across institutions. Jain specifically highlighted the use of overlapping datasets and similar infrastructure as part of this risk. Advt Speed can further amplify the impact of such risks. Automated systems can analyse information and initiate actions much faster than humans can respond, raising the possibility that an error could spread before it is detected and contained. Jain identified speed as a separate but related concern alongside concentration and opacity.The issue therefore extends beyond conventional technology risk at an individual institution. A failure involving a common provider could potentially have wider implications where several financial institutions depend on the same technology infrastructure.A wider financial stability concernThe growing use of external technology also means that risks arising from common providers can no longer be viewed solely as an individual institution's technology problem. Where the same provider supports several financial institutions, the resilience of each institution could be affected by an event outside its own systems. Advt This makes the concentration of technology providers relevant to financial stability as well as operational resilience. The potential vulnerability lies not only in whether an individual bank can withstand a technology failure, but also in whether a common dependency can transmit disruption across several institutions.For financial institutions, the challenge is consequently becoming broader as technology becomes more deeply integrated into financial services. The ability to manage technology risk will increasingly have to account for dependencies that may be shared across institutions, particularly where a small number of providers occupy critical positions in the technology ecosystem. Advt The issue does not mean that common technology providers will necessarily cause a financial disruption. But as dependence on a limited group of providers grows, a failure that might once have remained an isolated technology incident could have the potential to affect a much wider part of the financial system.
