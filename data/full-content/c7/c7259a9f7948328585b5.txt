While the latest liquidity operation is unlikely to immediately pressure net interest margins, continued withdrawal of surplus liquidity could gradually revive competition for deposits and wholesale funds. The key for banks will be how quickly excess liquidity is deployed into loans and whether credit growth can offset any rise in marginal funding costs. Advt The RBI accepted Rs 2,22,629 crore against a notified amount of Rs 2.25 lakh crore in Friday's VRRR auction at a weighted average rate of 5.24 per cent. The banking system had an estimated liquidity surplus of Rs 6.94 lakh crore as of September 17.The operation followed the RBI's Rs 50,000 crore open market sale of government securities on Thursday. The central bank has scheduled two further OMO sales of Rs 25,000 crore each for September 21 and September 28.The liquidity surplus followed a sharp increase in FCNR(B) deposits under the RBI's special scheme. Banks had mobilised $127.23 billion through FCNR(B) deposits by August 31, while total inflows under the special swap facility stood at $136.38 billion.The additional liquidity reduced banks' immediate dependence on certificates of deposit and other wholesale funding. Banks raised Rs 68,130 crore through CDs in August, the lowest monthly issuance since April. The easier funding conditions were estimated to have the potential to reduce banks' funding costs by up to 50 basis points.The improvement in funding conditions has also begun to show up in lending economics. Private banks' spreads on fresh lending widened by 22 basis points to 3.13 per cent in July as the additional liquidity reduced their dependence on relatively expensive wholesale funding. Advt Outstanding certificates of deposit fell to Rs 6.36 lakh crore from Rs 6.90 lakh crore during the fortnight ended August 31, while fresh CD issuance almost halved to Rs 19,500 crore from Rs 36,800 crore.The immediate absorption of Rs 2.23 lakh crore does not by itself imply a squeeze on bank margins. The bigger issue is whether the RBI continues to withdraw the surplus quickly enough to reverse the funding-cost benefit created by the FCNR(B) inflows.If excess liquidity remains available, banks can continue reducing wholesale funding and deploy surplus cash into loans. But if system liquidity tightens materially, lenders could once again compete more aggressively for deposits and certificates of deposit, pushing up marginal funding costs. Advt Deposit growthScheduled commercial bank deposits grew 17.76 per cent year-on-year as of August 31, while the loan-to-deposit ratio fell to 80.3 per cent from 81.7 per cent during the fortnight.The sharp increase in deposits was concentrated in time deposits, which accounted for Rs 8.03 lakh crore of the Rs 9.40 lakh crore fortnightly increase. This gives banks some room to absorb liquidity withdrawal without immediately returning to aggressive wholesale funding.Money-market rates remained below the repo rate on September 17, with the weighted average overnight rate at 4.89 per cent and the call money rate at 5.05 per cent. The persistence of surplus liquidity means the RBI's absorption operations could continue to influence short-term funding conditions even as the banking system retains a substantial liquidity cushion. Advt For banks, the key monitorable over the next few months will therefore be the pace of RBI liquidity absorption, the deployment of surplus deposits into credit and the extent to which deposit competition returns as the exceptional FCNR(B)-driven liquidity boost fades.
