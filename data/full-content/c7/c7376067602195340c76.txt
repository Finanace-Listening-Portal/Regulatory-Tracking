Articles 2 min read Bombay HC records Bank of Baroda's assurance to preserve documents in BR Shetty's USD 15-billion suit The Bombay High Court has recorded Bank of Baroda's assurance that records contested in B.R. Shetty's USD 15-billion suit will remain in their existing state, with no question of destruction, interpolation or alteration until the next hearing on December 17. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Updated On Nov 19, 2026 at 03:22 PM IST var news_msid = 135981984; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Bombay High Court has recorded Bank of Baroda's (BoB) assurance that records contested in the USD 15 billion suit filed by NMC Healthcare founder B.R. Shetty against the bank and others will remain in their existing state, with no question of their destruction, interpolation or alteration until the next hearing on December 17.Shetty's counsel sought a statement from the bank that the documents in its possession would be maintained in their current state, while the proceedings relating to the dispute are also pending in Abu Dhabi. The court recorded the assurance without prejudice to the rights and contentions of the parties. Advt The hearing concerned Shetty's application seeking inspection of documents in the bank's possession, including records relating to his accounts and documents allegedly bearing his signatures, which he said the bank relied upon but which were not available to him.Shetty's counsel, senior advocate Zal Andhyarujina, argued that the cause of action in the Bombay High Court proceedings was different from that in Abu Dhabi and that he was entitled to inspect the records under the Bankers' Books Evidence Act.BoB raises objection over Rule 89 of ADGM Court RulesDuring the hearing, Andhyarujina told the court that BoB had filed an application before the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) Court under Rule 89, alleging that Shetty had breached restrictions governing the use of documents disclosed in those proceedings.He said the bank had raised the allegation because Shetty's side had referred to certain matters from the disclosed documents in its pleadings. Andhyarujina contested the allegation, arguing that the Abu Dhabi rule had no bearing on the disclosure sought in the Indian proceedings.BoB's counsel, senior advocate Birendra Saraf, opposed the disclosure application and also questioned the timeline of Shetty's claim, referring to paragraphs 65, 66 and 129 of the plaint, which contain references to when Shetty became aware of the alleged events and when his right to sue arose.The bank also sought additional time, citing the voluminous nature of the records and the need to obtain instructions from the other defendants. Advt Andhyarujina told the court that he would file a reply to the bank's application once it was served on him and suggested that the applications be heard together on the next occasion.The matter was adjourned to December 17.
