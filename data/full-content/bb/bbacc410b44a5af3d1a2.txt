Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Indian Institute of Technology, Roorkee (GST AAR Uttarakhand) Summary: The Uttarakhand Authority for Advance Ruling considered an application by Indian Institute of Technology, Roorkee, through its Sponsored Research and Industrial Consultancy Office, regarding input tax credit on capital goods used for taxable research and consultancy projects and the GST consequences of transfers to project funding agencies. The applicant described research projects of varying durations and equipment sometimes installed at the funding agency’s premises. The Authority ruled that ITC on capital goods used for taxable research and consultancy services is admissible subject to Section 16 and the restrictions and reversal provisions of Sections 17 and 18 of the CGST Act and corresponding State provisions. Capital goods need not invariably be installed at the applicant’s own premises, but documentary evidence of ownership, control, procurement and taxable use is important. For transfers after 60 months, mere expiry of the period does not itself require reversal; a taxable supply attracts applicable tax, while gifts or free disposals require examination under Section 17(5)(h). For transfers within 60 months, Section 18(6) read with Rule 40(2) governs applicable amounts where the transfer is a supply. The Authority did not accept proportionate ITC reversal as a universal method. ITC on eligible inputs, consumables, manpower-related input services and other project procurements forming part of a taxable composite supply may be available subject to statutory conditions. Equipment bought solely for onward transfer to the funding agency requires separate consideration of the actual supply and applicable blocked-credit provisions. The ruling further highlights the distinction between the eligibility of input tax credit at the time of procurement and the GST implications arising from subsequent disposal or transfer of capital goods. The Uttarakhand Authority for Advance Ruling clarified that the expiry of 60 months from the date of purchase does not, by itself, determine whether ITC reversal is required. Instead, the nature of the subsequent transaction must be examined under the applicable provisions of the CGST Act, 2017. Where capital goods are transferred as a taxable supply, the provisions of Section 18(6), read with Rule 40(2), become relevant, while transfers constituting gifts or free disposals require examination under Section 17(5)(h). The Authority also recognised that capital goods installed at a funding agency’s premises may qualify for ITC where they remain under the applicant’s ownership and control and are used for providing taxable research and consultancy services. The ruling therefore emphasises that ITC eligibility, subsequent transfer obligations and the classification of composite supplies must be evaluated separately, with due regard to contractual arrangements, actual utilisation, supporting documentation and statutory restrictions. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF AUTHORITY FOR ADVANCE RULING, UTTARAKHAND 1. This is an application under Sub-Section (1) of Section 97 of the Central Goods 86 Services Tax Act, 2017 and Uttarakhand State Goods 86 Services Tax Act, 2017 (hereinafter referred to as CGST/SGST Act) and the Rules made there under filed by Indian Institute of Technology (IIT), Sponsored Research and Industrial Consultancy Office (SRIC), Main Building, IIT, Roorkee, Uttarakhand (herein after referred to as the “applicant”) and registered with GSTIN 05AAALI0033R1Z5 under the CGST Act, 2017 read with the provisions of the UKGST Act, 2017. 2. At the outset, we would like to state that the provisions of both the CGST Act and the SGST Act are the same except for certain provisions; therefore, unless a mention is specifically made to such dissimilar provisions, a reference to the CGST Act would also mean a reference to the same provisions under the SGST Act. 3. The Advance Ruling under GST means a decision provided by the authority or the appellate authority to an applicant on matters or on questions specified in sub­section (2) of section 97 or sub section (1) of section 100 in relation to the supply of goods or services or both being undertaken or proposed to be undertaken by the applicant. 4.1 As per the said sub-section (2) of Section 97 of the Act advance ruling can be sought by an applicant in respect of: a. Classification of any goods or services or both b. Applicability of a notification issued under the provisions of this Act, c. Determination of time and value of supply of goods or services or both, d. Admissibility of input tax credit of tax paid or deemed to have been paid e. Determination of the liability to pay tax on any goods or services or both f. Whether the applicant is required to be registered g. Whether any particular thing done by the applicant with respect to any goods or services or both amounts to or results in a supply of goods or services or both within the meaning of that term. 4.2 In the present case applicant has sought advance ruling on: 1. Whether Institute is eligible to claim ITC of GST paid on purchase of capital goods when such goods are used in providing outward taxable research and consultancy services. 2. Whether reversal of GST ITC is required if capital goods on which GST ITC has been claimed is subsequently returned/ transferred to the funding agency (recipient of service), in agreement of a clause in MOU entered into before commencement into project, without any consideration after successful completion of project: a. after 60 months from the date of purchase of such capital goods, b. Within 60 months from the date of purchase of such capital goods. 3. Whether Institute is eligible to claim ITC of GST paid on purchase of capital goods if capital goods are directly installed at the funding agency (recipient of service) site but used for outward taxable Research and Consultancy services. 4. Whether ITC is admissible on all eligible inputs (capital goods, consumables, Manpower, contingencies) forming part of composite supply (consisting of Research & consultancy, as principal supply and transfer of equipment purchased solely for the purpose of transfer and not capitalized in books account of the Institute. BRIEF FACTS OF THE CASE 5.1 In their application dated 02.09.2026, the applicant submitted that: IIT Roorkee SRIC office provides Research and Consultancy services in form of projects. Period of each project may vary depending upon period agreed upon between IIT Roorkee and Funding agency (Recipient of Service). Period of Project could be less than 60 months or in some cases more than or equal to 60 months. At present, generally all services provided by SRIC office is subject to GST. IIT Roorkee generally keeps Capital Goods at its premises but in some case, it is the requirement of the project that capital goods to be installed at site of Funding agency and IIT Roorkee use such capital goods at such site and provide service at such site by analyzing the results observed at the site. IIT Roorkee SRIC office intends to claim GST ITC of Tax paid on input, input services and capital goods, without any adverse legal complication in future. QUESTIONS ON WHICH THE ADVANCE RULING IS SOUGHT 5.2 In view of above, the applicant has sought advance ruling as to 5.2.1 Whether Institute is eligible to claim ITC of GST paid on purchase of capital goods when such goods are used in providing outward taxable research and consultancy services. 5.2.2 Whether reversal of GST ITC is required if capital goods on which GST ITC has been claimed is subsequently returned/ transferred to the funding agency (recipient of service), in agreement of a clause in MOU entered into before commencement into project, without any consideration after successful completion of project: a. after 60 months from the date of purchase of such capital goods, b. Within 60 months from the date of purchase of such capital goods. 5.2.3 Whether Institute is eligible to claim ITC of GST paid on purchase of capital goods if capital goods are directly installed at the funding agency (recipient of service) site but used for outward taxable Research and Consultancy services. 5.2.4 Whether ITC is admissible on all eligible inputs (capital goods, consumables, Manpower, contingencies) forming part of composite supply (consisting of Research & consultancy, as principal supply) and transfer of equipment purchased solely for the purpose of transfer and not capitalized in books account of the Institute. 5.3 APPLICANT’S INTERPRETATION OF LAW AND FACTS: 5.3.1 IIT Roorkee is eligible to claim GST ITC on capital goods as such capital goods are being used to provide outward taxable Research and Consultancy Services. 5.3.2 (a) No, reversal is not required because assets were purchased in name of IIT, Roorkee and subsequently transferred to funding agency after 60 months without any consideration. (b) Yes, proportionate reversal of GST ITC claimed is required for remaining months out of 60 months from date of purchase, along with interest. 5.3.3 Yes, IIT Roorkee is eligible to claim GST ITC even if capital goods are installed at site of funding agency, as ownership remain in the name of IIT Roorkee and IIT Roorkee utilize such Capital goods in providing Taxable services to the funding agency. 5.3.4 Yes, IIT Roorkee is eligible to claim GST ITC of tax paid on Input and Input services procured to provide composite supply. In this scenario equipment is treated as input and therefore tax paid on such equipment is also eligible for GST ITC even if such equipment is transferred under composite supply, as it is considered as input and not capital goods. 5.4 SUBMISSIONS OF THE CONCERNED OFFICER: Detailed written submission on the issue were tendered by the Deputy Commissioner (State Tax), Concerned Officer wherein it has been stated as under: 1. ITC on capital goods, inputs and input services procured and used by IIT Roorkee – SRIC for providing taxable Sponsored Research/ Consultancy services is admissible in principle, subject to satisfaction of Section 16 and restrictions/ apportionment under Section 17 and Rules 42/43. 2. Capital goods used exclusively for exempt supplies/ non-business purposes would not qualify for ITC to that extent. Common capital goods would be governed by the attribution mechanism prescribed under Rule 43. 3. Where capital goods on which ITC has been availed are subsequently transferred/ supplied to the funding agency/ recipient, the transaction cannot be treated merely as a simple “ITC reversal” issue. Section 18(6) is specifically attracted in the case of supply of capital goods, and the amount payable has to be determined in accordance with that provision. 4. Where transfer occurs within five years/ 60 months, the prescribed reduction of ITC and tax on transaction value have to be compared in accordance with Section 18(6), and the statutory amount payable must be discharged. 5. Where transfer takes place after 60 months, expiry of the five-year useful life for ITC purposes does not by itself render the subsequent supply non-taxable. Taxability of the transfer as an outward supply must still be examined under the CGST Act. (6) If the capital goods are allegedly handed over ‘free of cost”, the actual MOU must be examined. If transfer is a contractual obligation forming part of the sponsored project arrangement, it should not automatically be regarded as a gift. Conversely, if it is genuinely a gratuitous disposal by way of gift, Section 17(5)(h) would require consideration. 6. PERSONAL HEARING: The personal hearing in the matter was fixed and conducted, in virtual mode, on 15.10.2026 and the applicant was represented by their Authorized Representative Dr. Sheeba Ramola, Joint Registrar and Shri Rajat Grover, Chartered Accountant, who reiterated the facts and submissions made in their application dated 02.09.2026. 7. DISCUSSION AND FINDINGS: 7.1 We have carefully examined the application, the submissions made during personal hearing by the applicant, the relevant statutory provisions as well as the submissions of the concerned officer. Now, we take up the issues raised by the applicant one by one for examination and discussion. 7.2 As the moot questions involved in the application relate to eligibility of ITC, the relevant provisions of Section 16 of the CGST Act are being reproduced hereunder: – 16. Eligibility and conditions for taking input tax credit. (1) Every registered person shall, subject to such conditions and restrictions as may be prescribed and in the manner specified in section 49, be entitled to take credit of input tax charged on any supply of goods or services or both to him which are used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of his business and the said amount shall be credited to the electronic credit ledger of such person. (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in this section, no registered person shall be entitled to the credit of any input tax in respect of any supply of goods or services or both to him unless,– (a) he is in possession of a tax invoice or debit note issued by a supplier registered under this Act, or such other tax paying documents as may be prescribed; (b) he has received the goods or services or both. Explanation. – For the purposes of this clause, it shall be deemed that the registered person has received the goods or, as the case may be, services- (i) where the goods are delivered by the supplier to a recipient or any other person on the direction of such registered person, whether acting as an agent or otherwise, before or during movement of goods, either by way of transfer of documents of title to goods or otherwise; (ii) where the services are provided by the supplier to any person on the direction of and on account of such registered person. (c) subject to the provisions of section 41 or section 43 , the tax charged in respect of such supply has been actually paid to the Government, either in cash or through utilisation of input tax credit admissible in respect of the said supply; and (d) he has furnished the return under section 39: Provided that where the goods against an invoice are received in lots or instalments, the registered person shall be entitled to take credit upon receipt of the last lot or instalment: Provided further that where a recipient fails to pay to the supplier of goods or services or both, other than the supplies on which tax is payable on reverse charge basis, the amount towards the value of supply along with tax payable thereon within a period of one hundred and eighty days from the date of issue of invoice by the supplier, an amount equal to the input tax credit availed by the recipient shall be added to his output tax liability, along with interest thereon, in such manner as may be prescribed: Provided also that the recipient shall be entitled to avail of the credit of input tax on payment made by him of the amount towards the value of supply of goods or services or both along with tax payable thereon. (3) Where the registered person has claimed depreciation on the tax component of the cost of capital goods and plant and machinery under the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961, the input tax credit on the said tax component shall not be allowed. 8.1. ITC on capital goods used for taxable research and consultancy services We find that entitlement to ITC is primarily governed by Section 16(1) of the CGST Act. The decisive consideration is whether the goods are used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of the Applicant’s business and whether the statutory conditions for availing credit are fulfilled. On the facts stated in the application, the Applicant procures capital goods for conducting research, testing, analysis and other activities required for the execution of research and consultancy projects. Where the resulting research and consultancy services constitute taxable outward supplies, the capital goods are used for the purpose of making such taxable supplies. Accordingly, ITC on such capital goods is admissible under Section 16(1), subject to fulfilment of Section 16(2), proper classification of the goods as capital goods, maintenance of prescribed records, and the restrictions contained in Sections 17 and 18 and the rules made thereunder. We further observe that the entitlement is not unconditional. ITC shall not be admissible to the extent that the goods are used exclusively for non-business purposes, exempt supplies, or any other purpose covered by the statutory restrictions. Where capital goods are used commonly for taxable and exempt supplies, the applicable apportionment and reversal provisions shall apply. It is, therefore, held that the Applicant is eligible to avail ITC on capital goods used in providing outward taxable research and consultancy services, subject to the conditions and restrictions prescribed under the GST law. 8.2. Transfer of capital goods to the funding agency without separate consideration We observe that the question requires examination of two distinct legal issues: first, the treatment of the transfer of capital goods under the provisions governing supply; and second, the tax or credit adjustment applicable when capital goods on which ITC has been availed are supplied. We find that the fact that the capital goods were purchased in the name of IIT Roorkee does not, by itself, determine the GST consequences of their subsequent transfer. The terms of the relevant MOU/Agreement, the nature of the transfer, the ownership and control of the goods, the consideration for the overall project, and the treatment of the transfer under the contract must be examined. We find it relevant to mention that where capital goods form part of the Applicant’s business assets and are permanently transferred or disposed of in circumstances covered by paragraph 1 of Schedule I, the transaction may constitute a supply even if no separate consideration is charged. The Applicant’s obligation to transfer the goods under the MOU does not, by itself, exclude the transaction from the scope of supply. Further, it is also necessary to distinguish a contractual transfer forming part of the agreed consideration or deliverables of a taxable research and consultancy engagement from a gratuitous disposal by way of gift. If the transfer is genuinely in the nature of a gift or free disposal, Section 17(5)(h) must be examined. The mere absence of a separately stated price does not automatically establish that a transfer is a gift. We further observe that where capital goods on which ITC has been availed are supplied, Section 18(6), read with Rule 40(2), requires payment of the higher of the reduced ITC amount calculated in accordance with the prescribed reduction and the tax payable on the transaction value determined under Section 15 read with applicable rules for valuation of such supplies. The five-year period used for determining the prescribed reduction is not a general exemption from GST on the subsequent supply of capital goods. Consequently, the expiry of sixty months does not, by itself, establish that no GST liability arises on transfer of the goods. On examination of the two scenarios illustrated by the applicant, we observe as follows:- 8.2.1 Transfer of capital goods after sixty months Although no ITC is required to be reversed (as the ITC involved now stands as NIL), however, GST may still be required to be paid on the value determined as per Section 15 of the CGST Act read with applicable rules for valuation of such transactions. 8.2.2 Transfer of capital goods within sixty months We also observe that where capital goods on which ITC has been availed are transferred within sixty months and the transaction constitutes a supply, the Applicant is required to determine the amount payable under Section 18(6), read with Rule 40(2). The prescribed reduction is calculated at five percentage points for every quarter or part thereof from the date of issue of the invoice. The amount payable is the higher of the reduced ITC amount and the tax on the transaction value. It is not, in every case, merely a proportionate reversal of ITC for the remaining months of the sixty-month period. The Applicant’s proposed method of proportionate reversal, therefore, cannot be accepted as a universal rule for the transfer of capital goods. Where the transfer is a taxable supply, the Applicant shall comply with the applicable invoicing, valuation, reporting and tax-payment requirements. The tax liability must be determined on the actual facts and in accordance with the relevant statutory provisions. If, on the other hand, the goods are disposed of by way of gift or free samples, the consequences under Section 17(5)(h) must be considered separately. Accordingly, we find that in both scenarios, the applicable treatment depends on the nature of the transaction and the statutory provisions governing the relevant supply and the applicant’s simplistic interpretation is not agreeable. 8.3. Capital goods installed at the funding agency’s site Now we take up the third issue. In this regard, we observe that Section 16(1) does not prescribe that goods must invariably be installed or retained at the registered person’s own premises in order for ITC to be admissible. The essential requirement is that the goods be used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of the registered person’s business, subject to the statutory conditions and restrictions. In the present case, we find that the Applicant has stated that certain capital goods are installed at the funding agency’s site because the project requires testing, observation, measurement or analysis at that location. The Applicant nevertheless uses the equipment to carry out the research and consultancy activities for which it supplies taxable services to the funding agency. Where the Applicant establishes that the capital goods are procured for and used in the execution of its taxable research and consultancy services, the mere fact that the goods are physically installed at the funding agency’s site does not, by itself, disentitle the Applicant from ITC. The Applicant must maintain documentary evidence establishing the procurement, project-wise use, contractual arrangement, location and control of the equipment, and the nexus between the equipment and the taxable outward supplies. The Applicant must also establish that the goods have not, in substance, been transferred to the funding agency in a manner inconsistent with the claim that they remain its capital goods. Accordingly, we are of the view that ITC on capital goods installed at the funding agency’s site is admissible to the Applicant where the above conditions are satisfied and the goods are used by the Applicant for providing its taxable research and consultancy services. However, it is also suggested that as these sites remain active for substantial period of time and taxable services are provided from there, they may be declared as Additional Place of Business. 8.4. ITC on inputs and equipment forming part of a composite supply Coming to the last issue raised in the application, we find that the admissibility of ITC on goods and services procured for a composite supply must be determined with reference to the nature of the outward supply, the actual use of the inward supplies, the statutory definitions and the applicable restrictions under the CGST Act. We observe that where the Applicant supplies research and consultancy services together with other goods or services, it is necessary to establish that the supplies are naturally bundled and supplied in conjunction with one another in the ordinary course of business, and that research and consultancy constitute the principal supply. The description of the transaction as a composite supply in the MOU or invoice is not, by itself, conclusive. Further, where the conditions of Section 2(30) are fulfilled, Section 8(a) provides that the composite supply shall be treated as a supply of the principal supply. This provision determines the tax treatment of the outward composite supply; it does not independently confer ITC on every inward supply. In the instant case, we find that ITC on eligible consumables, manpower-related input services, other inputs and input services, and project-related expenses described as contingencies is admissible to the extent such inward supplies are used in the course or furtherance of the Applicant’s taxable business and the conditions of Section 16 are fulfilled. The admissibility of any particular expense depends on its actual nature, the supporting tax documents and the restrictions contained in Section 17. We further observe that the treatment of equipment purchased solely for transfer to the funding agency requires separate consideration. Under Section 2(19), the classification of goods as capital goods depends, inter alia, on their capitalization in the books of account and their use or intended use in business. Goods that are not capitalized and are purchased for use or consumption in the course of supplying services may qualify as inputs under Section 2(59), provided the statutory definition is otherwise satisfied. However, non-capitalization in the books of account is not, by itself, sufficient to establish that the equipment qualifies as an input or that ITC is admissible. The actual purpose for which the equipment is procured, whether it is used in performing the research and consultancy services, whether it is supplied or transferred to the funding agency, and the legal character of that transfer must all be examined. Where equipment is procured solely for transfer to the funding agency, is not used by the Applicant in providing the research and consultancy services, and is transferred without consideration, the Applicant must establish that the transaction forms part of the taxable outward supply and is not a disposal by way of gift or free samples. If Section 17(5)(h) applies, ITC shall not be available notwithstanding the general provisions of Section 16. Conversely, where the equipment is an eligible input used in making a taxable composite supply and its transfer is an integral part of the contractual deliverables of that supply, ITC may be admissible subject to the conditions of Section 16 and the inapplicability of the blocked-credit provisions. The tax treatment of the outward supply and any separate consequences applicable to the transfer of capital goods must be determined in accordance with the relevant provisions. Accordingly, we find that ITC on eligible inputs, consumables, input services and other project-related procurements is admissible to the applicant subject to the statutory conditions and restrictions. A blanket ruling that all items described as capital goods, consumables, manpower, contingencies or equipment qualify for ITC merely because they are associated with a composite supply cannot be granted. The Applicant must establish the eligibility of each category by reference to its actual use and the applicable provisions of law. RULING In exercise of powers conferred under Section 98 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and the corresponding provisions of the State Goods and Services Tax Act, it is hereby ruled as follows: In respect of Question 1: The Applicant is eligible to avail ITC on GST paid on the purchase of capital goods used in providing outward taxable research and consultancy services, subject to fulfilment of the conditions under Section 16 and compliance with the restrictions and reversal provisions contained in Sections 17 and 18 of the CGST Act, 2017 and the corresponding provisions of the UKGST Act, 2017. In respect of Question 2(a): Where capital goods on which ITC has been availed are transferred to the funding agency after sixty months, no ITC is to be reversed. Where the transfer constitutes a taxable supply, the applicable outward tax liability must be determined under the relevant provisions, including Section 18(6), Section 15 and the applicable rule. In respect of Question 2(b): Where capital goods on which ITC has been availed are transferred within sixty months and the transfer constitutes a supply, the amount payable shall be determined in accordance with Section 18(6), read with Rule 40(2), being the higher of the prescribed reduced ITC amount and the tax on the transaction value. Where the transfer is in the nature of a gift or free disposal, Section 17(5)(h) shall also be examined. In respect of Question 3: The Applicant is eligible to avail ITC on capital goods installed at the funding agency’s site where such goods are used by the Applicant in the course or furtherance of its business for providing outward taxable research and consultancy services, subject to the conditions of Section 16 and the other applicable provisions of the GST law. In respect of Question 4: ITC on eligible inputs, consumables, manpower-related input services and other project-related procurements forming part of a taxable composite supply is admissible to the Applicant subject to Section 16 and the applicable restrictions under Section 17. Equipment purchased solely for transfer to the funding agency cannot be held eligible merely because it has not been capitalized in the Applicant’s books. Its eligibility depends on its actual classification, use, contractual treatment and the nature of the outward supply. Where the transfer is an integral part of a taxable composite supply and does not attract the blocked-credit provisions, ITC may be admissible. Where Section 17(5)(h) applies, ITC shall not be available.
