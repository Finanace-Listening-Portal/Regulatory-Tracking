Section 149(1)(b) Rs. 50 Lakh Threshold Is Jurisdictional: Fifth & Sixth Provisos Can’t Save Time-Barred Section 148 Notice: Telangana High Court Summary: The Telangana High Court, in Prashanthi Gangireddy Vs ITO, allowed a writ petition challenging reassessment proceedings for Assessment Year 2020-21, holding that the Revenue could not invoke the extended limitation period under Section 149(1)(b) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, when its own order under Section 148A(d) quantified the alleged escaped income at ₹47,29,000, below the statutory threshold of ₹50 lakh. The notice under Section 148, issued on 24 April 2024, was beyond the ordinary three-year period ending on 31 March 2024. The Court held that the fifth and sixth provisos to Section 149(1), dealing with exclusion of specified periods in computing limitation, could not enlarge the substantive jurisdictional threshold. It also found that the sanctioning authority had granted approval under Section 151 mechanically, without examining the assessee’s reply, the quantum involved or the limitation issue. The Revenue could not cure these defects by introducing fresh justifications before the High Court. Accordingly, the Court quashed the proceedings, including the reassessment order and consequential penalty order. The decision highlights the importance of verifying the quantum of escaped income, applying the correct limitation provisions and obtaining an independent, informed sanction before initiating reassessment proceedings. Whether the Fifth and Sixth Provisos Can Sustain a Belated Reassessment Notice Whether, where the income alleged to have escaped assessment — as quantified by the AO himself in the Section 148A(d) order — falls below the Rs. 50 lakh threshold of Section 149(1)(b), a Section 148 notice issued beyond the ordinary three year period under Section 149(1)(a) can be sustained by recourse to the time exclusion available under the fifth and sixth provisos to Section 149(1)? Whether Approval Under Section 151 Was Valid Whether the approval under Section 151 is valid when the sanctioning authority neither examined the quantum involved nor recorded any application of mind to the availability of such proviso based exclusion (or to the assessee’s reply)? Court’s Reasoning On the Limitation / Threshold Issue The provisos compute time; they do not expand jurisdiction. Section 149(1)(a) bars a Section 148 notice after three years except where clause (b) applies — the extended ten year window being available only where income escaping assessment, represented by an asset, expenditure or book entry, is Rs. 50 lakh or more. Paragraph 22 is categorical: the provisos “operate to exclude… two specific and narrowly defined categories of time” (time allowed under a 148A(b) notice, and any stay period), and “[n]either proviso purports to enlarge the substantive threshold.” The Rs. 50 lakh threshold is a jurisdictional gateway, not procedural detail. Absent satisfaction of that threshold, the case stays governed by clause (a) and the clause (b) machinery — with its proviso exclusions — “simply does not come into play.” The threshold question answers itself from the Revenue’s own record: the Section 148A(d) order dated 23.04.2024 itself quantified escaped income at Rs. 47,29,000 — admittedly below Rs. 50 lakh. This was not a disputed assessee computed figure. The three year period therefore expired on 31.03.2024, and the 24.04.2024 notice was beyond it. The provisos could not rescue the notice arithmetically either. There was no court stay; even granting the full 148A(b) period exclusion from the 31.03.2024 outer limit, the extended date could not travel beyond late April 2024 — and that exercise presupposed a clause (b) case, which this never was. “The provisos cannot be pressed into service to rescue a notice that was never within the extended period to begin with.” On the Mechanical Approval Under Section 151 Neither the Section 148A(d) order nor the Section 151 approval recorded any application of mind to the provisos, any exclusion of time, or whether the Rs. 47,29,000 quantum could sustain action beyond three years. The sanctioning authority was obliged to independently satisfy itself on the material before it — including the assessee’s detailed 29.03.2024 reply — both on quantum and on limitation. Instead, it gave a “mechanical endorsement” of a proposal that was factually wrong (property purchase belonged to AY 2021-22) and legally time barred. Validity is judged by reasons in the order: settled administrative law forbids supplementing an order with reasons surfaced for the first time in a counter affidavit or at the Bar. A mechanical sanction reduces the Section 151 safeguard — meant to be an “independent, informed check” — to an “empty formality.” The requirement of independent application of mind under Section 151 has also been considered in WSFX Global Pay Ltd. Vs ACIT (Bombay High Court), Bhavesh Maganlal Dharod Vs ITO (Bombay High Court), Saraswat Co-operative Bank Ltd. Vs ACIT (Bombay High Court) and Kamuni Mallesh Vs DCIT (ITAT Hyderabad). Distinguishing Revenue Precedents BKR Capital (P) Ltd. v. CIT (2026 SCC OnLine SC 1609, affirming Delhi HC) and Sunil Bhalla v. CIT (2026 SCC OnLine Del 4379) were distinguished: in each, the record showed a contemporaneous, reasoned invocation of the extended computation by the authority itself at the time of the impugned action — not a post facto justification constructed before the Writ Court to salvage a delayed notice. The decision in Sunil Bhalla was subsequently considered by the Supreme Court, which declined to interfere with the High Court’s judgment. See Sunil Bhalla Vs DCIT (Supreme Court of India). Final Order Writ Petition Allowed. The question of law answered in favour of the petitioner / against the Revenue. Key Takeaways for Practitioners 1. Quantum in the Section 148A(d) Order Determines Limitation Quantum in the 148A(d) order binds the Revenue on limitation: If the AO’s own recorded figure is below Rs. 50 lakh, the case stays within Section 149(1)(a)’s three year limit — the Revenue cannot later argue a higher quantum or invoke clause (b) for the first time in litigation. 2. Proviso Arithmetic Does Not Confer Jurisdiction Proviso arithmetic ≠ jurisdiction: The fifth/sixth provisos (exclusion of 148A(b) time and stay periods) only assist cases already within clause (b). Assessees facing belated notices should check the quantified figure against Rs. 50 lakh before engaging with proviso based limitation defences. 3. Contemporaneous Invocation of Limitation Exclusions Contemporaneous invocation or nothing: Revenue victories like BKR Capital and Sunil Bhalla rest on the authority having reasoned the time exclusion in the record at the relevant time. Post hoc counter affidavit justifications will not cure a facially time barred notice. 4. Sanctioning Authorities Must Independently Apply Their Mind Sanctioning authorities must apply their mind: The Section 151 approver must examine the assessee’s reply and record satisfaction on quantum and limitation; a mechanical endorsement invites quashing of the entire chain, including the assessment and 271AAC penalty. Related decisions include Essen Marketing Pvt. Ltd. Vs ITO (ITAT Kolkata), Kailash Bansal Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) and APC Air Systems Pvt. Ltd. Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi). 5. Verify AIS/SFT Information Before Initiating Reassessment Fact check AIS/SFT triggers: Here the Rs. 1 crore property trigger actually belonged to AY 2021-22 and was a 25% share of Rs. 1,31,55,000 — sloppy verification can vitiate jurisdiction at the threshold stage itself. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) Q1. Can a Section 148 Notice Be Issued After Three Years if Escaped Income Is Below Rs. 50 Lakh? Answer: No. In Prashanthi Gangireddy v. ITO (Telangana HC, 30.10.2026), the High Court held that the extended period under Section 149(1)(b) applies only where escaped income is Rs. 50 lakh or more. Since the AO’s own Section 148A(d) order quantified escaped income at Rs. 47,29,000, the ordinary three year bar under Section 149(1)(a) applied and the notice dated 24.04.2024 was void. Q2. Do the Fifth and Sixth Provisos to Section 149 Extend Limitation for Reassessment? Answer: The Telangana High Court held they do not expand jurisdiction. The provisos merely exclude specific time (the period allowed under a Section 148A(b) notice, and any court stay period) when computing limitation; they cannot convert a case that never crossed the Rs. 50 lakh threshold into an extended period case. Q3. Can the Revenue Invoke the Provisos to Section 149 for the First Time Before the High Court? Answer: No. Applying settled administrative law that an order’s validity is judged by the reasons recorded in it, the Court held that provisos “never considered, never invoked, and never applied” when the notice was issued cannot cure a facially time barred notice. Post facto justification in a counter affidavit is impermissible. Q4. Is a Section 151 Approval Valid Without Application of Mind? Answer: No. The Court held the sanctioning authority must independently examine the material, including the assessee’s reply, on both quantum and limitation. A “mechanical endorsement” reduces the Section 151 safeguard to an empty formality, rendering the approval and consequential Section 148 notice invalid. Q5. What Happened to the Assessment and Penalty Orders in This Case? Answer: The High Court quashed the entire chain as void ab initio: the Section 148A(d) order dated 23.04.2024, the Section 148 notice dated 24.04.2024, the assessment order dated 31.10.2025 under Section 147 r/w 144B for AY 2020-21, and the penalty order under Section 271AAC(1). Q6. How Were BKR Capital and Sunil Bhalla Distinguished? Answer: In both, the record disclosed a contemporaneous, reasoned invocation of the extended computation by the authority at the time of the impugned action. Here, by contrast, the justification was constructed for the first time before the Writ Court, which the Court refused to accept.
