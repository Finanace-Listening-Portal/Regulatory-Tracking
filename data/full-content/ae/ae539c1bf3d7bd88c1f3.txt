Summary: Vaibhav Vyapaar Limited is scheduled to open its NSE Emerge initial public offering on 13 October 2026 and close bidding on 15 October 2026. Its Red Herring Prospectus (RHP), dated 7 October 2026, describes a fresh issue of up to 77,96,000 equity shares of ₹5 face value with no offer for sale. Crucially, the issuer’s corrigendum to the 8 October price-band advertisement, published in Business Standard on 9 October 2026, REVISED the price band from ₹50–₹52 to ₹51–₹54 per equity share and specifies a minimum application of 4,000 shares, in multiples of 2,000 thereafter. At the revised upper band of ₹54, gross issue proceeds are approximately ₹42.10 crore; the minimum 4,000-share application costs ₹2.16 lakh. The company operates in digital lending and associated loan-origination and technology services under the LoanFront brand. Its restated FY 2025–26 revenue from operations was ₹36.80 crore and profit after tax ₹1.97 crore; its total assets under management at 31 March 2026 were approximately ₹70.68 crore. This article examines the corrected offer structure, capital-augmentation objective, financial trends, lending and technology risks, SME-market liquidity considerations and income-tax treatment from 1 April 2026. Issuer-filed documents, including the later corrigendum, take precedence over outdated IPO calendars. No investment recommendation or expected listing price is provided. Offer at a Glance: Official Price Band and Dates Vaibhav Vyapaar Limited’s Red Herring Prospectus (RHP), dated 7 October 2026, describes a 100% book-built SME public issue proposed for listing on NSE Emerge. Its initial price-band advertisement in Business Standard dated 8 October 2026 stated ₹50–₹52, but the issuer subsequently published a CORRIGENDUM dated 9 October 2026 revising the price band to ₹51–₹54 and stating that applications must be for a minimum of 4,000 equity shares and multiples of 2,000 thereafter. The later issuer corrigendum supersedes the earlier advertised price and minimum application figures. The RHP itself retains placeholders for the final issue price. Reliance on the original advertisement without reading the corrigendum would materially misstate the offer. Business Model: Digital Lending and LoanFront The issuer describes itself as a digital lending company serving salaried and self-employed individuals, including customers with limited formal credit history. It uses its LoanFront platform for customer onboarding, identity checks, loan evaluation, disbursement and collection-related processes. The RHP also describes application and technology services offered to other non-banking financial companies, making the revenue model broader than interest earned on loans held on the issuer’s own balance sheet. A lending company’s reported income can expand because its loan book grows, pricing changes or fee-based business increases. Each driver has different risks. Interest income is sensitive to the cost of borrowings and collection performance. Origination and technology fees depend on contracts, service delivery and the financial position of counterparties. Growth in disbursements should therefore be read alongside average loan outstanding, impairment, credit costs, collections, funding availability and cash flow rather than treated as proof of improved credit quality. The company’s registered office is in Kolkata and its corporate office is in Bengaluru, as stated in the RHP. Its named promoters are Itha Venkata Raghava Gowrinath and Capfront Technologies Private Limited. The investor-relations page carries the RHP, restated financial statements, corporate-governance documents and offer-related advertisements. Those disclosures are more reliable for this article than promotional summaries or unofficial grey-market channels. Objects of the Fresh Issue and Use of Proceeds The RHP states two principal uses of net proceeds: augmenting the company’s capital base and general corporate purposes. This is materially different from an offer for sale, where the selling shareholders receive the sale proceeds. It also differs from an issue intended primarily to purchase plant and machinery or repay a specified borrowing. Capital augmentation is a common funding objective for a growing NBFC because its ability to originate and hold loans depends partly on capital adequacy and liquidity. The RHP notes that the objects were not independently appraised by a bank, financial institution or other third-party organisation. Investors should distinguish the issuer’s funding assumptions from a binding outcome. After listing, utilisation reporting and any permitted variation in objects are subject to the Companies Act, 2013 and the applicable SEBI disclosure framework. The offer document states that unutilised proceeds are not to be routed to promoters or group companies outside the disclosed terms. Material Risks Identified in the RHP Interest-rate and funding risk The RHP identifies interest-rate volatility as a risk to net interest income and margins. A lender may not be able to reprice its loans as quickly as the cost of borrowed funds changes. A rise in funding costs or a decline in yield on new loans can therefore compress margins. Borrowing concentration, refinancing dates and liquidity buffers warrant attention in addition to the headline debt-to-equity ratio. Unsecured lending and collection risk The company’s lending activities include unsecured personal loans. Delinquency, fraud, borrower over-indebtedness and adverse economic conditions may affect collections and require higher expected credit losses or other provisioning. Loan disbursement growth is not equivalent to cash profit; the quality and vintage of the loan book, write-offs and recoveries matter. Readers should inspect the RHP’s detailed risk factors and asset-quality disclosures rather than relying solely on a profit-and-loss table. Technology, regulatory and operational risk Digital onboarding and credit assessment depend on data, technology vendors, cybersecurity and regulatory compliance. System outages, unauthorised access, data breaches, incorrect customer verification or changes in RBI digital-lending directions can affect operations, reputation and compliance costs. A borrower-facing application also creates conduct risks in customer communication, collection practices and grievance handling. SME-market liquidity and valuation risk NSE Emerge securities can have different lot sizes, liquidity conditions and trading dynamics from mainboard shares. The issuer’s 9 October corrigendum specifies a minimum application of 4,000 shares, costing ₹2.16 lakh at the revised ₹54 cap price. This is a material concentration for some investors. Neither the issuer nor SEBI guarantees a post-listing market price. Unofficial grey-market premium reports are not regulated valuation measures and are deliberately excluded. These are arithmetic comparisons using historical pre-issue data, not a fair-value assessment. Post-issue shares, proceeds, changes in capital structure and future earnings can affect comparability. The issuer’s original 8 October advertisement and subsequent 9 October corrigendum must be read together with the RHP’s Basis for Issue Price chapter. The later corrigendum controls the revised price band. Price-to-earnings comparisons with other NBFCs are particularly sensitive to differences in product mix, underwriting, credit cost, funding, asset quality and accounting policies. No listing gain or target price can be inferred from a historical P/E multiple. Applications are to follow the ASBA or applicable UPI process and the SEBI ICDR framework. Investors should verify the current exchange application page and their broker’s supported bidding mechanism, including the correct investor category and lot. The UPI mandate acceptance deadline stated in the RHP is 5 PM on the issue-closing date, subject to the applicable final schedule. An application is not an allotment. Funds may remain blocked pending allotment and release. The issuer’s registrar is KFin Technologies Limited, and the company proposes to list on NSE Emerge subject to completion of required steps. Actual allotment, credit and listing should be verified through the registrar and stock exchange; dates advertised as tentative should not be presented as completed events before they occur. Income-Tax and Transaction-Cost Considerations in 2026 Income arising from sale of IPO shares after 1 April 2026 falls under the Income-tax Act, 2025 for Tax Year 2026–27, not the repealed 1961 Act for the new period. The successor provisions for eligible STT-paid listed-equity gains are section 196 (short-term) and section 198 (long-term), subject to statutory conditions. The broad special rates carried forward are 20% for qualifying short-term listed-equity gains and 12.5% on qualifying long-term gains exceeding the ₹1.25 lakh annual threshold, with applicable surcharge and cess. These rules depend on the holding period, recognised-exchange transaction and STT conditions; off-market or otherwise non-qualifying transfers can be treated differently. IPO allotment itself does not ordinarily create a taxable capital gain for the successful applicant. Tax consequences generally arise when the allotted shares are transferred, while dividends, if any, have their own income-tax treatment. Brokerage, securities transaction tax, exchange charges and other costs should be checked with the broker and prevailing law. For an investor who later sells at a loss, capital-loss set-off and carry-forward conditions also matter. This article does not provide a personal tax computation or recommend a holding period. How to Read the Prospectus Before Making a Decision A complete reading should cover the RHP’s Risk Factors, Objects of the Issue, Restated Financial Statements, Management Discussion and Analysis, related-party transactions, outstanding litigation, capital structure and basis for issue price. For a lending company, special attention should go to the loan-book composition, provisioning policies, asset-quality data, liquidity, borrowing maturity profile and regulatory capital. The issuer’s own offer documents are the proper starting point for these checks.
