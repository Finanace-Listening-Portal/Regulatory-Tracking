The Ministry of Finance said all Public Sector Banks and Regional Rural Banks will operate normally on September 27, while the Reserve Bank of India has approved all bank branches, offices, ATM-linked branches and currency chests to remain fully operational that day. Advt Without the additional Sunday working day, customers would face the fourth Saturday holiday on September 26, Sunday on September 27 and the proposed three-day strike from September 28 to 30, effectively creating five consecutive days of disruption to branch-based banking services. The United Forum of Bank Unions has called the strike primarily over the demand for a five-day banking week.The timing is significant because September 30 is the half-yearly closing date for banks. A strike during the closing period could affect activities such as reconciliation, provisioning, treasury and market operations, making uninterrupted access to banking infrastructure more important than during a normal three-day strike.The preparationsThe Finance Ministry has also asked public sector banks to prepare for the disruption by maintaining adequate cash availability through ATMs and business correspondent outlets and ensuring that alternative digital channels remain available. Banks including SBI and other public sector lenders have advised customers to complete important branch-based transactions before the strike.Digital banking channels such as internet and mobile banking, UPI and ATMs are expected to remain available, but branch-dependent activities can still be affected. Financial Express reported that cheque clearing, settlements and some account-related services could face disruption during the strike. Advt The Sunday opening therefore provides one additional operating day but does not eliminate the impact of the proposed strike. Its main purpose is to allow customers to complete transactions that require physical branches before the three-day stoppage, while giving banks another working day immediately before the half-yearly closing period.
