Summary: A foreign exchange provider authorised to sell or purchase currency is not automatically permitted to handle every outward remittance. In India, the permitted activities of authorised dealer banks, AD Category-II entities and full-fledged money changers depend on their RBI authorisation and applicable FEMA directions. Customers should identify the precise purpose of the transaction—such as overseas travel, education, maintenance of relatives, medical treatment or a business payment—before choosing a provider. They should verify the legal name and authorisation category of the contracting entity, the availability of the intended service, the exchange rate, all charges, the final amount receivable by the beneficiary and the documents required. The Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) is relevant to eligible resident individuals for permitted transactions, but should not be assumed to apply to every business or other remittance. Applicable tax collection at source (TCS) provisions also depend on the purpose, amount, person remitting and law in force on the transaction date. This article provides a practical due-diligence framework for selecting an authorised forex service provider and retaining a reliable transaction trail. Key Takeaway Show all 25 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Why RBI Authorisation Matters for Forex Transactions Foreign exchange transactions in India are governed principally by the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA), regulations and applicable RBI directions. An entity’s permission to exchange foreign currency does not, by itself, establish permission to remit funds abroad for all purposes. The authorisation must match the actual transaction, and the customer should verify the provider’s legal entity rather than relying solely on an app, brand name or intermediary’s advertisement. Types of Authorised Foreign Exchange Providers Authorised Dealer Category-I Banks AD Category-I banks generally provide a broad range of permitted current-account and capital-account foreign exchange services, subject to the applicable regulations, reporting requirements and transaction-specific conditions. Customers should check whether the bank supports the particular remittance purpose and destination. Authorised Dealer Category-II Entities AD Category-II entities may undertake the activities specifically permitted under their authorisation and applicable RBI directions. Their scope is not identical to that of an AD Category-I bank. Confirm that the proposed outward remittance or currency service falls within the entity’s permissions. Full-Fledged Money Changers Full-fledged money changers (FFMCs) primarily provide permitted money-changing services, including purchase and sale of foreign exchange within the applicable framework. An FFMC licence should not be treated as blanket permission to process all overseas bank transfers. If an FFMC offers a remittance through another authorised entity, identify which entity actually executes the transfer. Match the Provider to the Purpose of the Transaction Overseas Travel and Foreign Currency Purchase Check permitted currency products, cash or card limits, identification requirements, buy-back terms and the complete rupee cost. Keep invoices and transaction acknowledgements. Education, Medical Treatment and Family Maintenance Identify the beneficiary, permitted purpose, supporting invoice or other evidence, and whether the remittance falls under LRS. Confirm any additional conditions applicable to the purpose and the receiving country. Business and Commercial Remittances Do not assume that the individual LRS framework applies to company, partnership or other business payments. Confirm the appropriate FEMA route, underlying invoice or contract, tax documentation and authorised bank’s requirements. Compare the Total Cost of a Forex Remittance A low advertised transfer fee does not necessarily mean the cheapest transaction. Obtain a written quote covering the exchange rate, exchange-rate margin, processing charges, GST on applicable services, intermediary-bank charges, beneficiary-bank deductions and the expected net amount credited. Compare quotes obtained at approximately the same time, because rates can change. Illustrative Comparison Suppose two authorised providers quote different exchange rates and fees for the same permitted remittance. The correct comparison is the total INR debited for the same foreign-currency amount delivered, or the foreign-currency amount ultimately received for the same INR outlay. This is a method of comparison, not an actual provider quotation. LRS, FEMA, KYC and Tax Collection Requirements Liberalised Remittance Scheme The LRS generally permits eligible resident individuals to remit funds for specified permissible transactions within the prevailing RBI framework. The commonly stated annual limit of USD 250,000 per resident individual is subject to the applicable rules, exclusions and approvals. Verify the limit and treatment on the transaction date rather than assuming all remittances qualify. Identity Verification and Supporting Documents The provider may require PAN, identity and address documents, Form A2 or equivalent declarations, purpose codes, invoices, admission letters or beneficiary information, depending on the transaction. Requirements differ by customer and transaction type. Income-Tax TCS and Other Tax Checks Tax collection at source on relevant overseas remittances is governed by the applicable income-tax legislation and the rules effective on the transaction date. The threshold, rate and exceptions may differ for education, medical treatment, overseas tour packages and other purposes. Do not use a single TCS rate for all forex transactions. Separately consider any withholding-tax obligation for payments to non-residents and GST on the provider’s taxable service charges. How to Verify a Forex Provider Before Payment Obtain the full legal name, registered details and RBI authorisation category of the entity providing the service. Check the current RBI information and, where applicable, the authorised bank or principal through which the transaction is executed. Confirm in writing that the precise currency exchange or remittance purpose is permitted. Request the complete exchange-rate and fee quotation, including possible intermediary deductions. Verify beneficiary details and use only the provider’s confirmed official payment channel. Retain the transaction reference, invoice, bank debit advice, exchange-rate record and beneficiary confirmation. Fraud, Operational and Regulatory Risks Beware of personal-bank-account payment requests, unverified agents, unrealistic exchange rates, pressure to bypass documentation, promises of guaranteed forex trading returns and claims that an RBI money-changing licence authorises unrelated investment products. Currency exchange and outward remittance services must also be distinguished from speculative online forex trading. An entity’s authority for one activity does not establish authority for the other. Records to Retain for Audit and Compliance Maintain the provider’s authorisation evidence as checked on the transaction date, signed forms, invoices, purpose declarations, PAN/KYC records, rate quotations, bank statements, remittance acknowledgements, applicable tax certificates and relevant correspondence. For business transactions, also retain contractual documents, accounting entries and supporting tax analysis. Frequently Asked Questions Can Every Money Changer Send Funds Abroad? No. The scope of the provider’s authorisation and applicable RBI directions determines the permitted services. A money-changing licence alone does not establish unrestricted outward remittance authority. Are AD Category-I Banks and AD Category-II Entities Interchangeable? No. Their permitted activities differ. Check whether the chosen entity may execute the specific transaction. Does LRS Apply to All Overseas Payments? No. LRS concerns eligible resident individuals and permitted transactions. Business payments and other transactions may follow different regulatory provisions. Is the Advertised Exchange Rate the Final Cost? Not necessarily. Fees, spreads, taxes and intermediary-bank deductions can change the total cost or net beneficiary amount. Does RBI Authorisation Guarantee That a Provider Is Risk-Free? No. Authorisation does not eliminate operational, fraud, pricing, settlement or counterparty risks. Independent verification remains necessary. Key Takeaway Choose a forex provider according to the exact service and permitted transaction purpose, not merely its advertised brand or exchange rate.
