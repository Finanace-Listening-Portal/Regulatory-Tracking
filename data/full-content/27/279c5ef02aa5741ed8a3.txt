Articles 2 min read Govt constitutes high-level banking committee for Viksit Bharat; Suresh N Patel named chairman The government has constituted a four-member High Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat, chaired by former Central Vigilance Commissioner Suresh N Patel and including RBI Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu, former Indian Bank MD & CEO Padmaja Chunduru and former RBI Deputy Governor N S Vishwanathan, to recommend reforms across banking regulation, capital, technology and financial inclusion. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Published On Oct 30, 2026 at 11:57 PM IST var news_msid = 135374545; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Ministry of Finance on Friday announced that it has constituted the High Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat, tasked with undertaking a comprehensive review of the banking sector and recommending structural, institutional, regulatory and legislative reforms to make Indian banks globally competitive, financially inclusive and resilient.The committee will be chaired by Suresh N Patel, former Central Vigilance Commissioner, and will have three members — Shirish Chandra Murmu, Deputy Governor, Reserve Bank of India; Padmaja Chunduru, former MD & CEO, Indian Bank; and N S Vishwanathan, former Deputy Governor, RBI. The Joint Secretary (Banking), Department of Financial Services, will be the member secretary. The committee will have a tenure of six months. Advt The panel has been given a broad mandate covering the regulatory framework for banks, including Regional Rural Banks and cooperative banks, long-term capital requirements of banks, Development Financial Institutions and NBFCs, cost of capital for businesses, AI and digital innovation, cybersecurity and talent development.It will also review the existing Priority Sector Lending framework and Lead Bank Scheme, including their relevance and effectiveness in supporting inclusive and balanced credit delivery in line with changing economic priorities and financial inclusion objectives.The committee will assess existing banking practices and prudential norms against global best practices and suggest a roadmap for creating globally competitive banks. It will also examine funding sources and enabling mechanisms to meet the long-term capital requirements of the banking sector and support credit growth.On technology, the panel will prepare a roadmap for AI and digital innovation in banks, alongside safeguards against cyber risks, and recommend measures for talent acquisition and capacity building to create a future-ready banking workforce.The government will also seek the committee's recommendations on policy options to address differences in the cost of capital for businesses and support private investment for sustainable growth.The high-level banking committee was first announced by Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman in her Union Budget 2026-27 speech on February 1, when she proposed a comprehensive review of the financial sector to align it with India's next phase of growth while safeguarding financial stability, inclusion and consumer protection. Advt The government has now formally notified the committee, almost nine months after its announcement in the Budget.
