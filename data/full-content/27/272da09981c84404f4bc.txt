Articles 4 min read NBFCs see up to 15 bps rise in cost of funds as RBI rate hike feeds through Aditya Birla Finance expects a further 5–10-basis-point rise in incremental borrowing costs in Q3, while Cholamandalam Investment and Finance has guided for a 10–15-basis-point increase in cost of funds for FY27. Bajaj Finance has also flagged an additional margin headwind following the RBI’s October repo rate hike. Cholamandalam Investment and Finance, meanwhile, has guided for a 10–15-basis-point increase in cost of funds for the full financial year, with the projection factoring in another 25-basis-point rate hike. Advt At Bajaj Finance, chief financial officer Sandeep Jain said the company expects the higher repo rate to have a cascading impact on borrowing costs in Q3 and Q4. The company has undertaken interest-rate hedging in the previous quarter, which helped bring down its effective cost of funds at both Bajaj Finance and Bajaj Housing Finance.Jain said the company had initially guided for a 10–15-basis-point compression in margins for FY27 and had already seen a 5-basis-point impact in the first half. He flagged a marginal additional headwind to the earlier guidance as the rate hike feeds through.Borrowing costs begin to riseThe increase in funding costs is visible in the numbers reported by lenders. HDB Financial Services’ cost of funds rose 15 basis points in the first half of FY27, which its CFO Jaykumar Shah attributed to a spike in money-market rates. The company reported a cost of funds of 7.04 per cent in Q2.Shah said the lender was working to limit the increase by managing the borrowing mix, choosing instruments and benchmarks, and negotiating rates with banks. HDB is looking to keep its net interest margin within a narrow range over the next couple of quarters.Tata Capital reported a 5-basis-point sequential increase in cost of funds to 7.33 per cent in Q2 from 7.28 per cent in Q1. The lender’s net interest margin declined 10 basis points sequentially, of which 5 basis points was attributed to higher funding costs and the remaining 5 basis points to competitive intensity in prime home loans. Advt The company has approved a 20–25-basis-point increase in prime lending rates on its floating-rate portfolio. The revised rates are expected to support its net interest margin and fee income trajectory in Q4.Muthoot Finance’s cost of borrowing stood at around 8.6 per cent as of September 30. CFO Oommen Mammen said market borrowing rates had risen, with other issuers raising funds through bonds at 8.3–8.4 per cent, compared with around 7.5 per cent earlier. The company had not undertaken any non-convertible debenture issuances in the two months preceding the earnings call.Mammen said the cost of borrowing was likely to increase, but the lender could consider raising lending rates as gold loans are short-tenure products and can be repriced relatively quickly. Advt Transmission to lending ratesThe extent of the impact on lenders will depend on the share of floating-rate borrowings and assets, the timing of liability refinancing and the ability to reprice loans.At Aditya Birla Finance, 57 per of borrowings are floating-rate, while 70 per cent of assets are floating-rate. Managing director Rakesh Singh said the lender expects to transmit higher borrowing costs through repricing. However, the increase in funding costs has already offset the margin benefit from a higher share of unsecured loans.Mahindra Finance said a 25-basis-point increase in rates on its floating-rate borrowing book would result in nearly 10 basis points of its existing stock getting repriced within a quarter, depending on reset frequency. CFO Pradeep Agrawal also flagged that refinancing would add to borrowing costs with a lag as the company replaces maturing liabilities at higher rates. Advt Cholamandalam said its businesses were incorporating higher funding benchmarks into marginal lending rates. The company is also working on repricing its floating-rate portfolio, including affordable housing, loan against property and SME loans, over the next one to two months.
