Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re L&T-MHI Power Boilers Private Limited (CAAR Mumbai) Summary: The Customs Authority for Advance Rulings (CAAR), Mumbai, held that the project-specific Boiler Support Steel Structures (BSSS) proposed to be imported by L&T-MHI Power Boilers Private Limited in completely knocked-down (CKD) condition are classifiable under Customs Tariff Item 84029090 as “Other” parts of boilers. Classification under tariff item 73089010 as steel structures and Heading 9406 as prefabricated buildings was rejected on the facts and technical material placed on record. The ruling expressly requires the goods to be established as identifiable parts of the boiler system through their design, function and intended use. The applicant, a joint venture of Larsen & Toubro Limited and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Ltd., manufactures, supplies and services supercritical and ultra-supercritical boilers from its Hazira facility. Its application, received on 24 July 2026, concerned fabricated H-beams, column beams, girders, bracings and associated primary and secondary steelwork intended for particular boiler installations. According to the applicant, a supercritical boiler assembly bears an aggregate vertical load of approximately 75,000 MT, with nearly 18,000 MT suspended from top girders. Primary steelwork provides the load-bearing and suspension framework while accommodating thermal movement. Secondary steelwork includes platforms, galleries, walkways, stairs, ladders, gratings, handrails and equipment-support arrangements for operation, inspection and maintenance. The applicant submitted that the members are engineered to boiler-specific drawings and load calculations and undergo cutting, welding, precision drilling, inspection, protective treatment and individual tagging. They are imported disassembled because of their dimensions and weight, with tags matching engineering drawings, packing lists and shipping documents. It relied on Section XVI Note 2(b), the HSN exclusion from Heading 7308 for constructions clearly identifiable as machinery parts, Circular No. 964/07/2012-CX dated 2 April 2012 and Swetha Engineering Ltd. The jurisdictional Commissionerate furnished no comments, and no departmental representative attended the personal hearing on 9 September 2026. CAAR applied the tariff headings and relevant Section and Chapter Notes, distinguishing objective product identity from a declaration of future use. Heading 7308 broadly covers fabricated steel structures, including disassembled members, but excludes constructions clearly identifiable as machinery parts. The absence of Heading 7308 from the statutory definition of “parts of general use” did not by itself establish boiler-parts classification: Section XVI Note 2(b) still had to be satisfied. Substantial fabrication, regulatory coordination under the Indian Boiler Regulations and inclusion in project drawings were relevant evidence rather than independent classification tests. On the cumulative evidence, the Authority found that the primary framework had a specific relationship with the boiler’s geometry, suspension, thermal movement and operating loads, beyond that of a machine resting on an ordinary floor or foundation. Secondary steelwork was described as part of the same identified engineering package, rather than stand-alone walkways or general access arrangements for the power plant. Together, the members were objectively identifiable with, and suitable for use solely or principally with, the particular boiler system. Swetha Engineering supported this distinction, but was not read as classifying every steel structure in a boiler installation under Heading 8402. The BHEL decision concerned when unassembled components have the essential character of a complete boiler; the BSSS themselves did not constitute a complete or incomplete steam-generating boiler. Heading 9406 was rejected because the imported goods did not constitute, or possess the essential character of, a complete or incomplete prefabricated building. CKD presentation and on-site assembly alone could not give them that character. Beehive Foundry Engineering Works was distinguished because classification there depended on building-specific drawings, contractual scope and components imparting the essential character of a building. The intended-use authorities were considered only insofar as use is objectively discernible from the goods. The United States ruling on fabricated boiler panels was a non-binding comparison involving a different product and nomenclature. CAAR accordingly classified the described BSSS under CTI 84029090, subject to their established boiler-specific identity. Cases Discussed M/s. ABB India Limited v. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CUSTOMS AUTHORITY OF ADVANCE RULING, MUMBAI M/s L&T-MHI Power Boilers Private Limited (IEC: 0308051831) (hereinafter referred to as “the applicant”) has filed an application in Form CAAR-1 before the Customs Authority for Advance Rulings, Mumbai (hereinafter referred to as “CAAR” or “the Authority”). The application, along with the prescribed fee and supporting documents, was received in the Secretariat of CAAR, Mumbai on 24.07.2026 in terms of Section 28H (1) of the Customs Act, 1962. The applicant has sought an advance ruling under Section 28H (2) of the Customs Act, 1962 on questions relating to the classification of the goods described as “Boiler Support Steel Structures (BSSS)”.2. Applicant’s eligibility for Advance Ruling: The applicant holds a valid IEC No. 0308051831 and therefore qualifies as an “applicant” under Section 28E(c) of the Customs Act’ 1962. The question on which the advance ruling is sought is in respect of classification of goods under the Customs ‘Tariff Act’ 1975 which is admissible under Section 28H(2)(a). The applicant has declared that no proceedings involving the same issue are pending before any Customs authority, Appellate Tribunal or any Court of Law. Accordingly, the application appears admissible under Section 28-I (2) of the Customs Act, 1962. 3. Question(s) on which Advance Ruling is required: Whether the boiler structural support steel parts intended to be imported for exclusive use in boiler systems should be classified under the tariff entry 73089010 as structures of iron/steel, . or 84029090 as ‘Parts’ of boilers, or under CTH 9406 as prefabricated buildings of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975. 4. Submission by Applicant Statement of relevant facts having a bearing on the questions on which advance ruling is required. Background 4.1 The applicant is a Joint Venture between Larsen & Toubro Limited (L&T), India, and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Ltd, Japan (MHI). The applicant has established a state of the art manufacturing facility for Pressure Parts and Pulverisers for super-critical boilers(boilers) at Hazira in Gujarat. The applicant specializes in the design, engineering, manufacturing, supply and servicing of supercritical and ultra-supercritical boilers for coal-fired thermal power plants after procuring such contracts from their customers. These high-capacity boilers (500 MW to 1000 MW+) efficiently convert water into steam to generate electricity with reduced emissions and fuel consumption. 4.2. For the purpose of designing and manufacturing such high capacity boilers classified under CTH 8402, various components are required which are procured by the Applicant either domestically or are imported. One such component is specialized support structural steel parts (“Boiler Support Steel Structures” or “BSSS” or Subject Goods”) which are intended to imported from Asian countries to be exclusively used as an integral element of the boiler systems. 4.3. The Government of India is currently in the process of executing a multi-year capex cycle . doubling the investments in thermal power projects by 2028 in view of country Energy Security in response to these, there has been increase in the number of projects and due to the limited manufacturing capacity of the applicant and qualifying vendors in India, they are intending to import these BSSS for the manufacturing of boilers. 4.4 To appropriately understand the Subject Goods to be imported, it is imperative to understand its technical specifications, the overall essentiality and the functional role such BSSS entail in the boiler system. These critical aspects have been explained in detail in the ensuing paras: Essentiality and Functional Role of the Structural Steel in Boiler Operations 4.5 The requirement for purpose-built structural steel is indispensable, considering that supercritical boilers constitute one of the most demanding categories of thermal power generation equipment. A single supercritical boiler assembly is subjected to an aggregate vertical load of approximately 75,000 Metric Tons (MT), comprising the furnace enclosure, piping systems, coal silos, air pre-heaters, and other auxiliary equipment. Out of the said load, nearly 18,000 MT is directly suspended from the Top Girders through hanger rods and sling assemblies. 4.6 Under such extreme operating conditions, the supporting steel structure functions not merely as a passive framework, but asa specially engineered load-bearing system. The geometry, stiffness, member sizing, and connection integrity of the structure directly govern the long-term stability and operational reliability of the boiler’s critical pressure parts. The structure is simultaneously required to withstand exceptionally high static loads, resist wind and seismic forces over a design life of 35-40 years, and maintain precise geometric alignment during operation. 4.7 Further, the structural system must transfer these loads without imposing unintended restraint or secondary stresses on the boiler pressure parts. Any deviation in fabrication tolerances or compromise in structural integrity would result in uneven load distribution, locked stresses, and progressive structural failure, thereby affecting the safety, functionality, and operational performance of the supercritical boiler system.Product description and technical specifications 4.8 These BSSS constitute an engineered, purpose-built steel framework designed exclusively to support, suspend, and stabilize a coal-fired steam generator (boiler) and its ancillary equipment within a thermal power plant. These steel frameworks are neither a generic civil steel structure nor a simple warehousing or industrial shed framework but are an integral, safety and structure-critical component of the power plant, designed in strict coordination with the boiler OEM, and is entirely specific to the boiler geometry, mass, thermal expansion behavior, and seismic/wind loading conditions at the project site. Furthermore, the entire structural system complies strictly with earthquake-resistant design standards (IS 1893 Parts 1 and 4), utilizing vertically braced frames to transfer lateral seismic loads safely to the foundation. 4.9 Additionally, these BSSS are highly specialized steel components comprising of the following two critical components: i. Fabricated H-Beams: These structural components are pre-designed fabricated parts meant to be assembled on-site using detachable nuts and bolts. ii. Fabricated Column Beams: Similar to the H-beams, these fabricated column beams come with precise manufacturing, holes, and additional attachments exclusively engineered for the structural assembly of the boiler system 4.10 Technically, these components are broadly categorized into Primary and Secondary steel work. 4.10.1 Primary (Main) Steel work: i. The primary steelwork comprises a moment-resisting structural frame integrated with vertical bracing systems, engineered to safely withstand and transfer dead loads, live loads, equipment loads, and external environmental loads, including wind and seismic forces, to the foundation system in accordance with the applicable design and engineering standards. ii. A distinguishing and essential characteristic of the supercritical boiler support structure, which fundamentally differentiates it from conventional structural systems, is its specialized suspended support arrangement. Under this configuration, the boiler is supported through hanger slings connected to the Suspension Girder (Top Girder) positioned near the roof elevation of the structure. iii. The aforesaid suspension-based support mechanism is specifically designed to accommodate both vertical and horizontal thermal expansion of the boiler during operation, without transmitting or imposing thermal stresses and associated loads onto the supporting steel structure. Accordingly, the primary steelwork performs an integral and indispensable function in ensuring the structural stability, operational efficiency, and thermal flexibility of the supercritical boiler system. The secondary steelwork comprises structural and ancillary steel components provided for operational accessibility, inspection, servicing, and maintenance of equipment and associated systems within the boiler structure. The said secondary steelwork consists of galleries, platforms, walkways, stairways, ladders, and allied access arrangements, supported through an interconnected structural framework of steel members. ii. The aforesaid secondary steelwork components are essential and incidental to the effective operation, accessibility, inspection, maintenance, and safe functioning of the boiler and associated equipment systems. Although the Boiler Support Steel Structure itself is not a pressure-retaining part requiring direct IBR stamping, its fundamental purpose is to support and safely bear the load of IBR-regulated pressure equipment. Consequently, the framework must be designed in strict conformance with accepted structural codes and forms a mandatory, integrated part of the overall Boiler General Arrangement (GA) drawings submitted and accepted under statutory IBR compliance protocols. Raw materials and fabrication process of structural steels 4.13 All raw materials used in the fabrication of the Boiler Support Steel Structure are high- quality structural steels that conform strictly to internationally recognized standards. For example, the primary load-bearing columns are manufactured using high-tensile steel grades such as IS 2062. We have attached herewith a complete list of component used in the fabrication along with its equivalent Indian standards. 4.14. The fabrication process is a highly complex and quality-controlled operation carried out in dedicated heavy structural steel workshops. It involves review of Approved for Construction drawings, procurement and third-party inspection of traceable steel, CNC cutting and edge preparation, precision assembly, and welding by certified personnel using approved procedures under strict quality controls. The fabricated members are then straightened, dimensionally checked, CNC-drilled for HSFG bolting, and subjected to extensive Non-Destructive Examination (NDE). Finally, the structures undergo protective surface treatment, final inspection, and tagging for CKD shipment. 4.15 In other words, the Subject Goods proposed to be imported are not standard, multi- purpose structural steel profiles (such as generic pipes or beams). They are bespoke, precision- engineered boiler components subjected to rigorous thermal, static, and dynamic load testing, fabricated to exceptionally tight dimensional tolerances that dictate the operational safety of the particular thermal power plant being constructed. There is a complex engineering process undertaken for fabrication of BSSS and not mere standard process of bending or curving pipes, tubes or beams. . 4.16 The Boiler Support Steel Structure components are proposed to be imported in Completely Knocked Down (CKD) condition due to the immense size, weight, and transportation constraints associated with large-scale power plant structures. The structural members, including columns, beams, girders, and bracings, are too large and heavy to be transported as fully assembled units, with certain columns measuring up to 24 meters in length and top suspension girders weighing approximately 332 Metric Tons. Accordingly, the fabricated members are dispatched separately, while secondary steelwork and fasteners are shipped in identified bundles or containers. 4.17 Further, each fabricated member and assembly is assigned a unique tag number to ensure ° traceability, proper erection sequence, and structural integrity. These tag numbers are linked to the engineering drawings and are reflected in the packing lists and shipping documents, thereby enabling effective customs verification and quality control at the port of import and project site.4.18 In view of the above, present application is being preferred before the Hon’ble Customs Authority for Advance Ruling to seck: “Whether the boiler structural support steel parts (explained above) intended to be imported for exclusive use in boiler systems should be classified under the tariff entry 73089010 as structures of iron/steel, or 84029090 as ‘Parts’ of boilers, or under 9406 as prefabricated buildings of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975.” Statement containing Applicant’s interpretation of law and/or facts in respect of the question on which advance ruling is required First proposition — whether the goods merit classification under Tariff entry 84029090 or 73089010? 4.19 The Applicant proposes to import boiler support steel structure components which are designed, manufactured and intended for exclusive use in the onward use for engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) based supply of boiler systems for thermal power plants. These support structures comprise a wide range of components classified as primary (main) steel work and secondary steel work, each of which undergoes an extensive and complex manufacturing process in accordance with specific designs created for the construction of the boilers that would be supplied by the Applicant. Conceptually, the structural steel components proposed to be imported by the Applicant are not merely bent or curved steel items such as pipes, tubes or beams, but are specially manufactured and engineered products that are ultimately intended for exclusive use in the construction and installation of boilers. 4.20 It is submitted that the classification of goods under Indian tax jurisprudence is strictly governed by the General Rules for the Interpretation of Import Tariff (GRI), as contained in the First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975. To ascertain the appropriate HSN code for the Subject Goods, it is imperative to apply these principles systematically. Under GRI Rule I, while titles of Sections and Chapters are provided solely for ease of reference, the legal classification is primarily determined according to the terms of the headings and any relative Section or Chapter Notes. 4.21 However, in the present factual matrix, the classification of the Subject Goods cannot be conclusively determined through Rule | alone, as the goods are not exclusively or exhaustively covered by any single heading or its corresponding notes. Consequently, a sequential application of the subsequent GRls is necessitated. Given that the Subject Goods are neither presented in an incomplete or unfinished state nor constitute a mere mixture of materials, GRI Rules 2(a) and 2(b) are legally inapplicable. Therefore, the determination of the correct classification naturally progresses to GRI Rule 3(a). 4.22 The said rule provides that the heading which provides the most specific description would be preferred over headings providing a more general description. The said principle has also been adopted by the Supreme Court in Dunlop India Ltd vs Union of India (1983 (13) ELT 1566). Therefore, it is imperative to apply GRI Rule 3(a) to determine the appropriate HSN classification.4.23 Before determination of the appropriate HSN classification, it is prudent to understand the scope and coverage of the relevant Chapter Tariff Headings. The relevant extract of the same is reproduced as under: Sr. No HSN Code Description 1. 73 Articles of Iron and Steel 7308 Structures (excluding prefabricated buildings of heading 9406) and parts of structures (for example, bridges and bridge-sections, lock-gates, towers. lattice masts, roofs, roofing frame-works, doors and windows and their frames and thresholds for doors, shutters, balustrades, pillars and columns), of iron or steel; plates, rods, angles, shapes, sections, tubes and the like, prepared for use in structures, of iron or steel 73089010 Beams, channels, pillars and girders prepared for use in structures 2 84 Nuclear reactors, boilers, machinery and mechanical appliances; parts thereof. 8402 Steam or other vapour generating boilers (other than central heating hot water boilers capable also of producing low pressure steam ); super-heated water boilers 84029090 Other 4.24 Chapter 73 covers structures (excluding prefabricated buildings classified under heading 9406) and parts of structures made of iron or steel. Examples include bridges and bridge sections, lock-gates, towers, lattice masts, roofs, roofing frameworks, doors and windows along with their frames and thresholds, shutters, balustrades, pillars, and columns. Additionally, this chapter includes plates, rods, angles, shapes, sections, tubes, and similar items that have been prepared for use in such structures. 4.25 According to the World Customs Organization (WCO) Explanatory Notes under CTH 7308, this heading encompasses both complete and incomplete metal structures, as well as parts thereof. These structures are generally characterized by their fixed nature once installed, meaning they arc typically not moved after assembly. They are usually constructed from various metal components such as bars, rods, tubes, angles, shapes, sections, sheets, plates, and castings, joined together by riveting, bolting, welding, or similar means. Moreover, these structures may include . materials classified under other headings, such as woven wire panels or expanded metal. The heading also covers parts such as flat-rolled products and wide flats, including so-called universal plates, strips, rods, angles, shapes, sections, and tubes that have been prepared through drilling, bending, notching, or other processing for use in structures. 4.26 In light of the above, Chapter 73, particularly CTH 7308, embraces a broad array of iron or steel structures and their parts. It includes both finished and unfinished structures, along with components specifically prepared for structural use, such as rods, plates, and sections that have undergone processing like drilling, bending, or notching. These structures are typically fixed in place after installation and are assembled using methods such as welding, bolting, or riveting. While primarily composed of iron or steel, these structures may also incorporate other materials like woven wire or expanded metal if integrated within the overall design. However, CTH 7308 explicitly excludes prefabricated buildings under CTH 9406 and constructions which are identifiable as machinery parts under Section XVI. We have reproduced below relevant extracts of CTH 7308 for reference: CTH 7308: Structures (excluding prefabricated buildings of heading 94.06) and parts of structures (for example, bridges and bridge-sections, lock-gates, towers, lattice masts, roofs, roofing frame-works, doors and windows and their frames and thresholds for doors, shutters, balustrades, pillars and columns), of iron or steel; plates, rods, angles, shapes, sections, tubes and the like, prepared for use in structures, of iron or steel (+) This heading covers complete or incomplete metal structures, as well as parts of structures. For the purpose of this heading, these structures are characterized by the fact that once they are put in position, they generally remain in that position. The heading does not cover: (a) Assembled sheet piling (heading 73.01) ) (b)…. (c) Constructions clearly identifiable as machinery parts (Section XVI) 4.27 Upon perusal of the above, it is prudent to note that CTH 7308 encompasses the following two exceptions/exclusions from its purview: i) Prefabricated buildings classifiable under CTH 9406 ii) Constructions clearly identified as machinery parts under Section XVI Therefore, it is important to evaluate whether the BSSS identifies as machinery parts under chapter 84, In this regard, we have discussed chapter 84 and precisely CTH 8402 in the ensuing paras. 4.28 In the present case, given the functionality, complex engineering and construction process specific to the design qua boiler, these BSSS would not be considered as generic civil or building structure such as those exemplified in heading 73.08 (bridges, towers, roofs, building frameworks, etc.) as there is no generic or alternative use in the civil structures mentioned (bridges, towers, lock-gates). The BSSS isa bespoke structural system designed solely to suspend skeleton integral to the boiler assembly, the BSSS falls squarely within the intended statutory . scope of “boiler bases” explicitly covered under CTH 8402. 4.29 Chapter 84 specifically addresses nuclear reactors, boilers, machinery, and mechanical appliances, including parts thereof. CTH 8402 specifically covers steam or other vapor-generating boilers, excluding central heating hot water boilers capable of producing low-pressure steam, as well as superheated water boilers. 4.30 Further, the WCO Explanatory Notes of CTH 8402 clarify that steam or vapor-generating boilers are specialized equipment designed to produce steam or vapors for heating and industrial applications. These boilers include fire tube, water tube, hybrid, and forced circulation designs that enhance efficiency. Superheated water boilers operate under high pressure to effectively supply heat. 4.31 Importantly, a clear distinction exists between boilers and their auxiliary devices, which affects their classification when sold separately or together. Parts of boilers include boiler bodies and bases, internal assemblies such as tubes, water tube caps, headers, boiler drums, steam domes, non-mechanical fireboxes, inspection covers, and fusible plugs. However, metal tubes or pipes that have only been bent or curved without additional processing and presented in an unassembled state, without clear identification as boiler parts, are excluded. Such tubes or pipes identifiable as boiler components during inspection are classified under Section XV rather than as boiler parts. This establishes a threshold at which items are deemed part of a boiler. We have reproduced below the Relevant extracts of CTH 8402 for reference: CTH 8402: Steam or other vapour generating boilers (other than central heating hot water boilers capable also of producing low pressure steam); super-heated water boilers. Parts: Subject to the general provisions regarding the classification of parts (see the General Explanatory Note to Section XVI), parts of the boilers of this heading are also covered, e.g., boiler bodies and bases, internal assemblies for boilers consisting of tubes, watertube caps, headers, boiler drums, steam domes, non-mechanical fire-boxes, inspection covers and fusible plugs. Metal tubes or pipes which have been bent or curved but not otherwise worked, presented unassembled, are not identifiable as parts of boilers and are therefore to be classified in Section XV 4.32 According to the Explanatory Notes to CTH 8402, parts of boilers explicitly include “boiler bodies and bases”. The Fabricated Column Beams and H-Beams act as indispensable structural base and frame, without which the boiler pressure parts cannot be erected, suspended, or held in their designed operational position. By serving as the foundational load-bearing skeleton integral to the boiler assembly, the BSSS falls squarely within the intended statutory scope of “boiler bases” explicitly covered under CTH 8402. 4.33 Further, General explanatory Note to Section XVI (Parts – Section Note 2) explicitly states that parts which are suitable for use solely or principally with particular machines or apparatus would be classified in the same heading as of the machinery subject to the exclusions mentioned in Part I. We have reproduced below the relevant extract of General explanatory note of Section XVI for reference: In general, parts which are suitable for use solely or principally with particular machines or apparatus including those of heading 84.79 or heading 85.43), or with a group of machines or apparatus falling in the same heading, are classified in the same heading as those machines or apparatus subject, of course, to the exclusions mentioned in Part (1) above.4.34 The factual matrix of the present case aligns perfectly with the afore-mentioned statutory test. The Subject Goods are uniquely tagged and dimensioned to match specific boiler engineering drawings, deriving their geometry, hole patterns, connection designs, and member sizing solely from the boiler’s General Arrangement (GA) and load calculations. These customized structural components would not fit any other boiler design, nor could they be repurposed for generic civil structures without undergoing substantial re-engineering and re- fabrication. Therefore, they exist exclusively as a direct consequence of the boiler’s specific design requirements, fully satisfying the legal threshold of being “solely or principally” used with the machinery of heading 84.02.4.35 Further, Section XVI excludes from its scope only “parts of general use” of base metal as defined in Note 2 to Section XV which confines articles of headings 73.07, 73.12, 73.15, 73.17, 73.18 and specified headings of Chapter 83. Crucially, heading 73.08 is not included within this definition. Accordingly, even if the BSSS were, arguendo, to be scen as a 73.08-type construction, it would not be a “part of general use”, and Section XVI does not bar its classification as a specific machinery part under heading 84.02 when it satisfies the “solely or principally with” test of Section XVI Note 2.4.36 While the primary framework acts as the load-bearing base, the integrated BSSS package also encompasses secondary steelwork such as intermediate platforms, stairways, and galleries. It is imperative to note that these secondary components are not generic structural items of heading 73.08; they are included in the identical engineering package and boiler General Arrangement (GA) accepted under the statutory Indian Boiler Regulations (IBR). Under Section XVI Note 2(b), machinery parts need not be in direct contact with pressure media; it is sufficient that they are suitable for use solely or principally with the machine in question. Because these secondary access systems are statutorily mandated and exclusively designed for safe operation, continuous maintenance, and statutory inspection of the boiler’s pressure parts, they are functionally inseparable from the boiler system and merit classification alongside the primary steelwork under CTH 84.02.4.37 Additionally, as per parts under CTH 8402 includes metal tubes or pipes which have not been merely bent or curved but have been otherwise worked upon. It is pertinent to note that “otherwise worked” has not been universally defined in WCO explanatory notes. Instead, the definition is contextual and is derived from Section Notes, Chapter Notes, and the General Explanatory Notes of the respective chapters. 4.38 It is submitted that “working” is generally understood as a hierarchy of manufacturing process wherein “otherwise worked” goods are considered as goods which are subjected to secondary operations (mechanical, thermal, or chemical) that alter their profile, surface, or shape, advancing them from a general-purpose material into an article identifiable for a specific use. Minor processing like cutting the edges, painting for anti-rust, process for evaluation of flaws in the goods etc. would not amount to “otherwise worked” and will be classified as parts of general use.4.39 Therefore, applying the General Explanatory Notes, it is evident that these components have been ‘otherwise worked’ and legally qualify as machinery parts when secondary operations such as precision drilling, fining, specific bending etc. are explicitly committed on the general pipes and tubes to become an identifiable part of a boiler, thereby preventing its use as a standard conduit pipe and attaining an essential character of the machinery or plant module it is designed for.4.40 Analogy from the above can be drawn to the Subject Goods also which are not mere generic metal tubes or pipes that have been bent or curved but are fully fabricated structural steel members (H-beams and column beams) which are created as per specific alloys with characteristics and are subjected to a multitude of secondary manufacturing operations. As mentioned above, these operations include CNC cutting, precision drilling for HSFG bolting, and the welding of specific stiffeners and plates tailored exclusively to the boiler design.4.41 This complex manufacturing process mark them as specialized parts intended for integration within the structural framework of a boiler (i.e. particular element of a boiler design which cannot be deployed elsewhere and identifiably marked with part number). Unlike unassembled tubes or pipes that remain generic and indistinguishable and capable of generic use, these fabricated beams are tailored and prepared to function as essential structural elements of the boiler. Therefore, they do not fall within the exclusion applicable to unprocessed tubes or pipes presented in an unassembled state, thereby mandating their classification as parts under CTH 8402 rather than generic structures under CTH 7308.4.42 Consequently, CTH 7308 pertains to general structural elements of iron or steel used in construction, such as buildings, bridges, and towers. Further it would not be legally appropriate to classify components uniquely designed and fabricated for machinery or mechanical appliances, such as boilers under a residuary entry when such items are optically and from utility perspective are infact ‘parts of boilers’ as recognized in the Explanatory Notes.4.43 This also aligns with GRI under Customs Tariff Act, 1975 which states that if an article being imported is specifically covered under a tariff entry explicitly then the same should be classified under the tariff entry rather than classifying it in a general or residuary entry i.e., rule of specific over general. Consequently, the BSSS intended to be imported corresponds to “parts of boilers” covered under CTH 8402 as explained above and thus would merit classification under CTI-1 8402 as compared to the general tariff heading of CTH 7308 encompassing “structures of iron and steel”.4.44 In other words, the items in question are not general construction materials; rather, they are customized components integral to the mechanical structure of the boiler. Therefore, since these components are excluded from classification as unprocessed structural elements, they should not be classifiable under CTH 7308 and should be classified under CTEI 8402 as parts of steam or other vapor-generating boilers.4.45 Further, considering the fact that constructions identified as machinery parts, are explicitly excluded under CTI I 7308, these BSSS would be excluded from CTH 7308 as they are bespoke, precision engineered components which are designed solely to support the specific geometry and load of the supercritical boiler meeting the threshold of being “solely or principally used” with the boiler which is a critical parameter to be classified as parts under Chapter 84.4.46 A direct reference can be made to Circular No. 964/07/2012-CX dated 2nd April 2012 issued by the Central Government providing clarification on the classification of structural components used in boilers and the admissibility of CENVAT credit on such components. It is a settled principle that WCO Explanatory notes are recognized guide for interpretation of tariff schedules. Therefore, these guidelines must be strictly applied to ascertain the scope of CTH 9406. 4.62 Chapter 94 covers furniture; bedding, mattresses, mattress supports, cushions and similar stuffed furnishing; luminaires and lighting fittings, not elsewhere specified or included; illuminated signs, illuminated name plates and the like and prefabricated buildings. As per Note 4 of chapter 94, the expression ‘prefabricated buildings’ means buildings which are finished in the factory or put up as elements, presented together, to be assembled on site, such as housing or worksite accommodation, offices, schools, shops, sheds, garages or similar buildings. ‘[he relevant statutory extract of Chapter 94 Note 4 is reproduced below: 4.- For the purposes of heading 94.06, the expression “prefabricated buildings” means buildings which are finished in the factory or put up as elements, presented together, to be assembled on site, such as housing or worksite accommodation, offices, schools, shops, sheds, garages or similar buildings.” “Prefabricated buildings include “modular building units” of steel, normally presented in the size and shape of a standard shipping container, but substantially or completely pre-fitted internally. Such modular building units are normally designed to be assembled together to form permanent buildings. This legislative intent clearly indicates that the structural elements meant for CTH 9406 must possess the inherent characteristics of an architectural or habitable enclosure, rather than functioning merely as an industrial framework. 4.64 According to WCO Explanatory notes under CTH 9406, prefabricated buildings can be either equipped or unequipped; however, only built-in equipment that is typically supplied with such structures is classified along with them. This includes integrated systems like electrical wiring, heating and air conditioning units, plumbing and sanitary fixtures, kitchen installations, and built-in furniture such as cupboards. A notable type within this category is the modular building unit, which resembles a shipping container in size and form but is internally pre-fitted with elements such as walls, flooring, plumbing, and electrical systems. These modules are structurally self-supporting and designed for horizontal or vertical assembly with other units to form permanent buildings like schools, hospitals, or residential complexes. 4.65 Accordingly, the statutory language and WCO Explanatory Notes unequivocally restrict CTH 9406 to structures possessing habitable characteristics that are either fully finished in the factory or presented as prefabricated elements, intended to be assembled on-site into complete buildings such as housing, worksite accommodation, offices, schools, shops, sheds, or garages. These buildings may include modular building units typically steel structures made in the size and shape of a standard shipping container that are substantially or completely pre-fitted internally with components such as internal walls, flooring, doors, windows, electrical wiring, plumbing, and insulation. Only built-in equipment that is normally supplied with such buildings as electrical fittings, sanitary fixtures, kitchen installations, and built-in furniture is classified with prefabricated buildings. 4.66 To further validate the scope of the term “building”, one must look at its common parlance and dictionary definitions, as customs classifications heavily rely on how terms are understood in general trade. As per various dictionary meanings, a general building can be said to be a structure with walls and a roof, such as a house or factory, constructed to provide shelter or serve a specific purpose. It may also refer to the act, process, or business of construction. Definitions according to various dictionary are as follows: i. Collins’s dictionary – anything that is built with walls and a roof, as a house or factory; structure or the act, process, work, or business of constructing houses, ships, etc. ii. Merriam webster – a usually roofed and walled structure built for permanent use (as for a dwelling) or the art or business of assembling materials into a structure iii. Cambridge dictionary – a structure with walls and a roof, such as a house or factory or a structure with walls and a roof, such as a house or factory, to give protection to people, animals, or things. 4.67 Therefore, in the absence of a restrictive statutory definition, reliance on common trade parlance and dictionary definitions reinforces that a ‘building’ inherently provides shelter, featuring walls and a roof, which the Subject Goods do not possess. Further these buildings are considered to be providing a suitable habitat and protection to people. Consequentially, under CTH 9406, building which possess these characteristics and are prefabricated in nature would merit classification. 4.68 However, in the present scenario, the BSSS intended to be imported are precision-engineered components, featuring pre-drilled holes and mounting provisions which are designed to enable bolted on-site assembly of the boiler. These components do not possess any characteristics of the building as mentioned above and thus not constitute a complete building or any part thereof. Instead, they are designed specifically for industrial use within a boiler system, functioning as critical load-bearing elements that support the boiler’s pressure parts, associated piping, platforms, and mechanical equipment. 4.69 Further, these structural components are integral to the mechanical design and operation of the boiler, ensuring its stability, safety, and structural integrity. Nature and Identity of the Subject Goods 7.2 From the material placed on record, I find that the BSSS comprise fabricated H-beams, column beams, girders, bracings and other associated primary and secondary structural steel members, specifically designed for supporting, suspending and stabilising the designated supercritical boiler and its associated equipment. The Applicant has submitted that these members are engineered with reference to the General Arrangement of the particular boiler, the prescribed design loads, geometry, thermal expansion requirements and applicable wind and seismic loading conditions. Thus, the subject goods are not merely standard or generic steel sections imported for subsequent fabrication into an unspecified structure. 7.3 The Applicant has submitted that the primary steelwork constitutes the principal load-bearing framework of the boiler support arrangement. It comprises a structural frame, including columns, beams, girders and vertical bracing systems, designed to withstand and transfer the relevant dead loads, live loads, equipment loads and environmental forces to the foundation. The Applicant has further explained that the boiler is supported and suspended through hanger rods or sling assemblies connected to designated suspension or top girders. The dimensions, strength, configuration and connection arrangements of these members are determined with reference to the weight, geometry, suspension arrangement and operational requirements of the particular boiler. 7.4 I find that the primary steelwork performs a specifically engineered load-bearing and load-transfer function in relation to the boiler installation. The structural arrangement is designed to accommodate the loads associated with the boiler and its components while maintaining the requisite stability and alignment. The Applicant has also submitted that the structure is designed to accommodate the thermal expansion and contraction of the boiler during operation, so that unintended restraint or additional stresses are not imposed on the boiler pressure parts. These features demonstrate a close engineering relationship between the primary steelwork and the designated boiler. However, the tariff significance of this relationship must be determined under the applicable classification provisions and cannot be inferred solely from the fact that the structure is necessary for the installation of the boiler. 7.5 The BSSS also comprises secondary steelwork, which, includes platforms, galleries, walkways, stairs, ladders, gratings, handrails, stanchions, toe plates, equipment-support structures and runway beams for operational and maintenance equipment. These members provide access, inspection, operation and maintenance facilities and may also support equipment associated with the boiler system, including PA Fans, FD Fans, ID Fans, Air Preheaters and Mills. Their dimensions, locations and configurations are stated to be determined with reference to the layout and operational requirements of the particular boiler installation. 7.6 I find that the primary and secondary steelwork perform different, though related, functions within the overall boiler installation. The primary steelwork principally provides the load-bearing and suspension framework, whereas the secondary steelwork provides access, maintenance and equipment-support arrangements. Their respective functions must be considered while determining the tariff classification of the imported goods. In particular, the functional relationship of the principal load-bearing members with the boiler may require a different assessment from that of platforms, walkways, stairs or other members whose immediate function is to facilitate access or support ancillary equipment. The relevant question is therefore whether they qualify as other parts under Section XVI Note 2(b). 7.25 Section XVI Note 1(g) excludes parts of general use, as defined in Note 2 to Section XV, of base metal. The relevant portion is reproduced below: I. This Section does not cover: (g) parts of general use, as defined in Note 2 to Section XV, of base metal (Section XV), or similar goods of plastic (Chapter 39); Accordingly, before classifying the subject goods as parts of a boiler under I leading 8402, it is necessary to ascertain whether they fall within the definition of “parts of general use” under Note 2 to Section XV. 7.26 Note 2 to Section XV defines the expression “parts of general use” as follows. The relevant portion is reproduced below: 2. Throughout this Schedule, the expression “parts of general use” means: (a) articles of heading 7307, 7312, 7315, 7317 or 7318 and similar articles of other base metal, other than articles specially designed for use exclusively in implants in medical, surgical, dental or veterinary sciences (heading 9021); (b) springs and leaves for springs, of base metal, other than clock or watch springs (heading 9114); and (c) articles of headings 8301, 8302, 8308, 8310 and frames and mirrors, of base metals, of heading 8306. (d) In Chapters 73 to 76 and 78 to 82 (but not in heading 7315) references to parts of goods do not include references to parts of general use as defined above. Subject to the preceding paragraph and to Note 1 to Chapter 83, the articles of Chapter 82 or 83 are excluded from Chapters 72 to 76 and 78 to 81. Heading 7308 is not included among the enumerated categories of parts of general use under Note 2 to Section XV. Accordingly, the exclusion under Section XVI Note 1(g) does not apply merely because the subject goods are fabricated steel structures. However, this does not independently establish that they are parts of a boiler; the requirements of Section XVI Note 2(b) must still be satisfied. 7.27 The HSN Explanatory Notes to Heading 8402 provide the following relevant description of parts: (A) Steam or other vapour generation boilers (B) Super-heated water boilers Parts Subject to the general provision regarding the classification of parts (see the General. Explanatory Note to Section XVI), parts of the boilers of this heading are .also covered, e.g., boiler bodies and bases, internal assemblies for boilers consisting of tubes, watertube caps, headers, boiler drums, steam domes, non-mechanical fire-boxes, inspection covers and fusible plugs. Metal tubes or pipes which have been bent or curved but not otherwise worked, presented unassembled, are not identifiable as parts of boilers and are therefore to be classified in Section XV. 7.28 The above Explanatory Note makes it clear that parts of boilers are covered by Heading 8402 subject to the general provisions governing classification of parts under Section XVI. The examples include boiler bodies and bases as well as internal and other components of the boiler. These examples demonstrate that the parts provision is not confined exclusively to components directly involved in the generation of steam or vapour. Nevertheless, the Note does not provide that every structure which supports, surrounds or facilitates access to a boiler necessarily constitutes a part thereof. The nature and functional relationship of the article with the boiler must be assessed under Section XVI Note 2. 7.29 The HSN Explanatory Note to Heading 8402 further states that metal tubes or pipes which have been bent or curved but not otherwise worked and are presented unassembled are not identifiable as parts of boilers and are to be classified in Section XV. This specific illustration concerns tubes or pipes which have undergone only limited processing and which, in their imported condition, do not have the characteristics needs to identify them as boiler parts. It cannot be read as a universal rule that every fabricated component must satisfy the same processing test, regardless of its nature. The proper inquiry is whether the imported article is objectively identifiable as a part of the boiler, applying the relevant tariff terms and Section XVI Note 2. 7.30 The Applicant has submitted that the subject goods are not merely standard beams or columns selected from commercial stock. They are fabricated according to the specific geometry, load calculations and connection requirements of the designated supercritical boiler. The members have predetermined positions and are tagged to correspond with the relevant engineering drawings; they are also subjected to operations such as CNC cutting, precision drilling, welding of stiffeners and plates, dimensional checking and inspection. I find these matters relevant because they establish the objective identity of the goods at the time of importation and the relationship between each member and the designed boiler support arrangement. 7.31 The Applicant’s submission that the members could not be put to another use without substantial re-engineering or re-fabrication is also relevant, when considered alongside the drawings, dimensions, hole patterns, connection arrangements and tagging. The question is not whether it is physically imaginable that a steel beam could be reused elsewhere; it is whether, as imported, the fabricated members are objectively suitable for use solely or principally with the particular boiler system. On the facts described in the application and the technical material relied upon, the primary steelwork is specifically configured to support and suspend the boiler and to transfer its loads through the designed girders, columns and bracing system. 7.32 The functional relationship is more specific than the general relationship between a machine and the floor or foundation on which it is installed. According to the Applicant’s technical explanation, the primary members are engineered around the boiler’s weight, geometry, suspension arrangement, thermal movement and operating loads, and their connection details are coordinated with the boiler design. They form the prescribed load-bearing and suspension framework through which the boiler is maintained in its designed position. On this basis, the primary steelwork is not merely a civil structure incidentally supporting a machine; it is a purpose-built structural component of the designated boiler arrangement. 7.33 I have also considered the secondary steelwork, which includes platforms, galleries, walkways, stairways, ladders, gratings, handrails, toe plates, equipment-support structures and runway beams. The Applicant submits that these are designed as part of the same engineering package and are necessary for safe operation, inspection and maintenance of the boiler and its associated equipment. I find that the fact that these items provide access or support ancillary equipment does not determine their classification either way. Their design, location, attachment, identification and functional relationship with the boiler installation must be considered as part of the imported BSSS package. 7.34 On the facts submitted, the secondary steelwork is not described as a stand-alone access platform or a general walkway for the thermal power plant. Rather, the Applicant states that it is designed to the particular boiler layout, included in the integrated structural drawings and supplied with identified members for the same boiler installation. When these characteristics are read together with the primary framework and the stated scope of supply, the secondary steelwork forms the associated access, inspection and maintenance arrangement of the specifically engineered boiler support structure. Its classification follows the nature and identity of the goods established by the record and the applicable parts provisions, rather than the mere fact that it is situated within a boiler house. 7.35 I therefore find, on a cumulative assessment of the stated technical characteristics, that the subject BSSS is a project-specific, fabricated structural system whose members are identified for designated positions in the support arrangement of the particular boiler. Its primary function is to support, suspend, stabilise and transfer the loads of the boiler and its associated equipment; the associated secondary steelwork provides the access and maintenance arrangements designed around that boiler installation. These characteristics distinguish the goods from generic beams, pillars or girders merely prepared for use in an unspecified structure. 7.36 The fact that the structure is erected at site and transfers loads ultimately to the foundation does not make it a general foundation or an independent civil structure. The classification must be based on the complete factual description. Here, the Applicant’s case is that the members are manufactured to the boiler-specific engineering drawings, incorporate predetermined connection details, are individually tagged and collectively perform the designated support and suspension functions within the boiler arrangement. I find these features sufficient, when considered together, to establish that the goods are clearly identifiable with the particular boiler system and are suitable for use solely or principally with that system for the purpose of Section XVI Note 2(b). 7.37 I have considered Circular No. 964/07/2012-CX dated 02.04.2012, relied upon by the Applicant, which addresses the classification of structural components intended to be used essentially as parts of a boiler system. The relevant portion is reproduced below: “Accordingly, it is clarified that those structural components which are to be used essentially as a part of Boiler System would be classifiable as parts of Boiler only under Heading 8402 of the Tariff. It is further clarified that since these structural components are nothing but the parts and accessories of the Boiler, they would be covered by the definition of inputs under Rule 2(k)(iii) of the CENVAT Credit rules, 2004 (i.e. all goods for generation of electricity & steam). Further these structural components shall not be hit by the exclusion clause to the said definition of inputs, as these are not used for laying of foundation or making of structures for support of capital goods, but are essentially the part of said Boilers.” In the present case, the subject goods are described as purpose-built members of the boiler support structure, engineered and fabricated for the specific boiler and performing the identified supporting and suspension functions within its designed arrangement. The distinction drawn in the Circular is therefore relevant. On the facts and technical description placed on record, the subject BSSS are not presented as general foundation steel or an independent structure on which unrelated capital goods may be installed. They are intended to form the designated support framework of the boiler system. The Circular accordingly supports consideration of the subject goods under Heading 8402, subject to the tariff provisions governing parts. 7.38 I have also considered the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in M/s. Swetha Engineering Ltd. v. Commissioner of Central Excise, Chennai (2016 (5) TMI 448 (SC)), relied upon by the Applicant. The dispute concerned fabricated steel components used as structural support to hold boilers, where the Department sought classification under Heading 7308 instead of the boiler-parts entry under Heading 8402. The Supreme Court referred to the departmental clarification and observed that the clarification that structural components used as parts of a boiler system are classifiable under Heading 8402 vindicated the assessee’s classification of the boiler parts under sub-heading 8402.90 of the Central Excise Tariff. Tribunal’s Order: “Ld. SDR submits that from the purchase order of the appellants’ customer, the appellants were required to supply steel structures for erection of boilers. The appellants had cleared items such as plates, racks, angles etc. for erecting structures of iron or steel as a structural support to hold boilers. CTI 73.08 covered such structures and parts of structures.” Hon’ble Supreme Court’s Judgment: “The Department itself has come out with the clarification that those structural components which are to be used as parts of Boiler System would be classifiable as parts of Boiler only under Heading 8402 of the Tariff This clarification, thus, vindicates the stand which was taken by the assessee throughout by rightly classifying the parts of the boilers under Chapter sub heading 8402.90 of the Central Excise Tarif f Act, 1985.” This decision is relevant because it concerns the very classification distinction at issue here—fabricated structural components used as support for boilers versus structures and parts of structures of iron or steel. I do not read the decision as laying down that every steel structure used anywhere in a boiler installation must invariably be classified under Heading 8402. Its significance is that structural form and material do not preclude Heading 8402 where the components are established to be used essentially as part of the boiler system. For the reasons set out above, the subject BSSS has the specific design, fabrication, identification and functional relationship which brings it within that principle. 7.39 I have further considered the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Commissioner of Central Excise v. BHEL (2018 (2) TMI 1112 (SC)). The Applicant relies on this decision in relation to the distinction between a complete or incomplete boiler presented in an unassembled condition and separately identifiable parts of a boiler. The principle emerging from the decision is that the condition in which goods are presented as unassembled or incomplete machines must be considered before deciding whether the goods are classifiable as a complete boiler or as parts. The relevant extract of the judgment is reproduced below: “5. That apart, relying on the HSN note Part V under Section XVI, as extracted below, the aforesaid two authorities concluded that such components which are essential to classify the boiler as a machine, even if transported as components, must be understood to have been transported as a complete machine. Therefore, levy of duty should be under sub-heading 840210 and not sub-heading 840290 as claimed by the Revenue……” In the present case, the I3SSS, as described, do not themselves constitute a complete or incomplete steam-generating boiler. The decision therefore does not make the subject goods classifiable as a boiler under subheading 8402.10; rather, their classification is to be considered under the parts provision and Section XVI Note 2. 4.40 The Applicant has referred to the United States Customs ruling NY 188421 dated 11.12.2002, concerning fabricated steel panels for Endurance central-heating boiler units classified under 8403.90.0000. The ruling noted that the panels were bent, curved, drilled or punched in accordance with the specifications of the boilers for which they were designed and were identifiable as boiler parts when presented unassembled. The relevant extract of the ruling is reproduced below: “The steel panels will be used in the assembly of Endurance central heating boiler units. The Endurance boilers come in three models — EBP, EDP and EDN. The EBP series is a modulating combination boiler which provides domestic hot water as well as hydronic space heating. The EDP series is a modulating hydronic boiler. The EDN series is a non­ferrous, modulating boiler The EDP series and EDN series provide heat for hydronic space heating. All use natural or propane gas. The boilers are for residential use only. The panels come in various sizes and shapes. Examination of the two samples submitted shows that each panel is bent, curved, drilled or punched in accordance with the specifications of the boiler for which the panel has been designed As such, the plates, presented unassembled, are identifiable as parts of boilers.” The said ruling is not binding for classification under the Indian Customs Tariff and concerns a different product and tariff nomenclature. Nevertheless, it provides a comparative illustration of the relevance of product-specific fabrication and objective identifiability. In the present case, similar considerations arise from the stated fabrication, tagging and boiler-specific connection arrangements of the BSSS, but the conclusion rests on the Indian tariff provisions and the material placed on record. 7.41 The Applicant has also referred to the decisions concerning intended use, including Commissioner of Customs (Import) v. Welkin Foods [2026 (1) TMI 348-SC] and M/s. ABB India Limited v. Joint Commissioner of Commercial Taxes [2026 (5) TMI 1225]. The relevance of the intended-use principle in the present matter is confined to the extent that intended or principal use must be discernible from the objective characteristics of the goods and the applicable statutory scheme. The conclusion here is not founded merely on the Applicant’s stated future use; it is supported by the claimed drawings, predetermined dimensions and connections, fabrication, tagging and the particular load-bearing and suspension functions of the members as described in the application. Whether the goods are classifiable under CTH 9406 7.42 I now examine the alternative possibility of classification under Heading 9406 which covers “Prefabricated Buildings”. The relevant portion of the said Heading is reproduced below: 9406 Prefabricated Buildings 940610 Of wood: 94062000 Modular building units, of steel 940690 – Other: 94069090 Other 7.43 Further Chapter Note 4 to Chapter 94 defines the expression, “prefabricated buildings” for the purposes of Heading 9406. The Note further encompasses modular building units of steel, normally presented in the size and shape of a standard shipping container but substantially or completely pre-fitted internally, which are designed to be assembled together to form permanent buildings. The relevant portion of the said Note is reproduced below: For the purposes of heading 9406, the expression “prefabricated buildings” means buildings which are finished in the factory or put up as elements, presented together, to be assembled on site, such as housing or worksite accommodation, offices, schools, shops, sheds, garages or similar buildings. “Prefabricated buildings include “modular building units” of steel, normally presented in the size and shape of a standard shipping container, but substantially or completely pre-fitted internally. Such modular building units are normally designed to be assembled together to form permanent buildings. 7.44 The HSN Explanatory Notes to Heading 9406 further elaborate upon the scope of the Heading. The relevant portion is reproduced below: This heading covers prefabricated buildings, also known as “industrialised buildings”, of all materials. These building, which can be designed for a variety of uses, such as housing, worksite accommodation, offices, schools, shops, sheds, garages and greenhouses, are generally presented in the form of Complete buildings, fully assembled, ready for use; Complete buildings, unassembled,. Incomplete buildings, whether or not assembled, having the essential character of prefabricated buildings. In the case of buildings presented unassembled, the necessary elements may be presented partially assembled (for example, walls, trusses) or cut to size (beams, joists, in particular) or, in some cases, in indeterminate or random lengths for cutting on the site (sills, insulation, etc.). The buildings of this heading may or may not be equipped. However, only built-in equipment normally supplied is to be classified with the buildings. This includes electrical fillings (wiring, radiators, air conditioner, etc.), sanitary equipment (baths, showers, water heater, etc.), kitchen equipment (sinks, hoods, cookers, etc.) and items of furniture which are built in or designed to be built in (cupboards, etc.). Prefabricated buildings, include “modular buildings units” with a steel structure, also referred to as modules. They are normally presented in the size and shape of a standard shipping container intended for multi-modal transport. However, they are substantially or completely pre fitted internally with internal walls, floorings, ceilings, doors, windows and electrical and plumbing facilities as appropriate to the type of buildings module. They may also be equipped with other fixtures and fittings such as staircases, built-in furniture, kitchen equipment, sanitary fixtures, external cladding and roofing. They are structurally self-supporting and designed for assembly with other modules horizontally or vertically to become permanent buildings, such as hospitals, hotels, residential, communal facilities, or schools. They may be presented with assembly components to link modules. However, modular building units do not include units with a permanent chassis (“mobile homes’) (Chapter 87). Material for the assembly or finishing of prefabricated buildings (e.g., nails, glues, plaster, mortar, electric wire and cables, tubes and pipes, paints, wallpaper, carpeting) is to be classified with the building, provided it is presented therewith in appropriate quantities. Presented separately, parts of building and equipment, whether or not identifiable as intended for these buildings, are excluded from the heading and are in all cases classified in their own appropriate headings. 7.45 The aforesaid Heading, Chapter Note and HSN Explanatory Notes, when read together, indicate that Heading 9406 covers complete or incomplete prefabricated buildings, whether assembled or unassembled, which are finished in the factory or presented as elements for assembly at the site and possess the essential character of a building. Such buildings may be made of different materials, including steel, and may include modular building units designed to be assembled together to form permanent buildings. The Heading also covers materials and bu i lt-in equipment presented with the building in appropriate quantities. However, parts of buildings and equipment presented separately are expressly excluded from Heading 9406 and are required to be classified under their respective appropriate headings. Therefore, classification under this Heading depends upon whether the goods, in the condition in which they are presented at the time of importation, constitute a complete or incomplete prefabricated building or elements thereof possessing the essential character of such a building, rather than merely fabricated structural components intended for a specific industrial application. 7.46 The subject goods are described as a boiler support steel structure consisting of beams, columns, girders, bracings and associated access and equipment-support members. Their stated function is to support and suspend the boiler and its associated equipment and to provide the structural and access arrangements required for operation and maintenance. They are not presented as elements for assembly into housing, offices, schools, shops, sheds, garages or similar buildings, nor do they possess the essential character of such a building. Accordingly, the fact that the BSSS is imported in CKD condition and assembled at the project site does not bring it within Heading 9406. 7.47 The decision of the CESTAT in CCE, Chennai v. Beehive Foundry Engineering Works (2013 (11) MTI876 — CESTAT Chennai), relied upon by the Applicant, is also required to be examined ‘in the context of the facts of the present case. In the said decision, the Tribunal considered whether the goods supplied under different purchase orders were classifiable as structural steel components under Heading 7308 or as prefabricated buildings under Heading 9406. The Tribunal held that the absence of certain components, such as roofing sheets or walls, would not preclude classification under Heading 9406 where the goods were manufactured in accordance with the drawings and specifications of a building and the components supplied, considered together with the relevant contractual documents and other elements, imparted the essential character of a prefabricated building. The Tribunal distinguished such supplies from contracts involving only structural steel work, which did not possess the essential characteristics of a building. The relevant extract of the decision is reproduced below: “11. In respect of other purchase orders, it appears that the respondent entered into contract for manufacturing pre fabricated building as per drawing and specification of the contract. On reading of Note 4 of Chapter 94 and HSN Explanatory Notes, it is clear that pre fabricated building whether in assembled form or unassembled form and to be assembled at site, it would cover within Heading No. 94.06. Regarding the submission of the learned AR that every pre­fabricated building should have wall as a component, we find from the diagrams for the factory at Viralimalai, that the walls are made of pre fabricated concrete slabs. In our considered view, the classification as pre fabricated building cannot be changed for the reasons that roofing sheets or walls were bought-out items or supplied by the buyer of the product. The classification has to be decided by the facts whether the goods manufactured are made to measurements of a building and that the components manufactured and supplied will give essential characteristics of a building as may be seen from purchase orders and bills of materials. In the present cases, it is seen from letter dated 10.1.2000 of the respondent that they supplied pre fabricated cut to size parts as per drawing of pre fabricated building together with other bought out elements, such as roofing sheets, side walls etc. and as per HSN Explanatory Notes, it would be covered within the Heading 94.06 as pre fabricated building. So, the goods supplied in respect of the other purchase orders are rightly classifiable under sub-heading NO.9406.00. We have noted that the Revenue in the grounds of appeal had mentioned only the contract of M/s. Godrej & Boyce Manufacturing Co. Ltd.” 7.48 The aforesaid decision establishes that the classification of fabricated steel components under Heading 9406 depends upon whether the goods supplied, having regard to the relevant drawings, specifications, purchase orders and bills of materials, constitute or impart the essential character of a prefabricated building. The decision does not support the proposition that every fabricated steel structure intended for assembly at a project site is classifiable under Heading 9406. In the present case, the subject goods, namely, Boiler Support Steel Structure (BSSS), are designed to perform a specific industrial function as part of the boiler installation. They serve as a framework for supporting and suspending the boiler and associated equipment and for providing the requisite access and support arrangements for operation and maintenance. They do not constitute a building, nor do the goods, as presented for importation, possess the essential character of a prefabricated building. Accordingly, the decision in Beehive Foundry Engineering Works is distinguishable on facts and does not support classification of the subject goods under CTH 9406. 7.49 In view of the foregoing discussion, I find that the subject Boiler Support Steel Structure (BSSS) does not merit classification under Heading 9406. The mere fact that the fabricated structural members are imported in completely knocked-down (CKD) form and assembled at the project site does not impart to them the character of prefabricated buildings. Having regard to their nature, design and intended function, the subject goods are identifiable as specialised structural components designed to support and form an integral part of the particular boiler installation, rather than as complete or incomplete prefabricated buildings or elements possessing, the essential character of such buildings. Accordingly, classification of the subject goods under CTH 9406 is ruled out. Conclusion 7.50 On a cumulative consideration of the nature and technical description of the BSSS, the project-specific GA drawings, predetermined dimensions and connection arrangements, degree of fabrication, unique tagging, CKD condition of importation and the functions performed within the designated boiler support arrangement, I find that the subject goods are specially engineered structural components identifiable with the particular boiler system. Their primary steelwork provides the load-bearing and suspension framework for the boiler and its associated equipment, while the associated secondary steelwork is designed as part of the same boiler-specific arrangement for access, inspection, maintenance and equipment support. I also observe that since the Applicant is purely into manufacturing, supply & servicing of super critical & ultra-super critical Boilers and the imported goods presented in CKD condition shall be used in boiler unit only for specified purpose. These observations are based on the Submission & additional Submissions made by the applicant viz: – – iv. Detailed List of components used in the fabrication process 7.51 I further find that the subject goods are suitable for use solely or principally with the boilers of Heading 8402 and qualify as parts for the purposes of Section XVI Note 2(b). This conclusion is supported by the specific exclusion in the HSN Explanatory Notes to Heading 7308 for constructions clearly identifiable as machinery parts, the description of parts in the Explanatory Notes to Heading 8402, Circular No. 964/07/2012-CX dated 02.04.2012 and the principle recognised by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Swetha Engineering Ltd. The conclusion is based on the cumulative objective characteristics and functional relationship of the subject goods with the boiler, and not solely on the Applicant’s declaration of intended use. 7.52 Accordingly, I find that the subject goods, namely, “Boiler Support Steel Structures”, as described in the application and supported by the material placed on record, merit classification as parts of boilers under Heading 8402 of the First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975. The relevant portion of Heading 8402 and its corresponding tariff entries are reproduced below: 8402 Steam or other vapour generating boilers (other than central heating hot water boilers capable also of producing low pressure steam) super-heated water boilers 840290 Parts: 84029010 Parts of fire tube boilers 84029020 Parts of watertube boilers 84029090 Other In view of the above, the subject goods are appropriately classifiable under CTI 84029090, as “Other” parts of boilers, provided that the goods are established to be identifiable as parts of the boiler system on the basis of their design, function and intended use, as evidenced by the material on record. 8. I rule accordingly.
