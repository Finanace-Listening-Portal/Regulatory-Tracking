Summary: Ind AS 34 does not ordinarily measure each quarter’s income-tax expense by multiplying that quarter’s accounting profit by a headline statutory rate. It requires the best estimate of the weighted-average annual effective income-tax rate expected for the full financial year, applied to interim pre-tax income on a year-to-date basis. The estimate must reflect enacted or substantively enacted law, permanent differences, credits, differently taxed income, jurisdictions and supportable recognition of tax losses. When the annual estimate changes, the cumulative tax requirement is recomputed and the current quarter records the difference after subtracting tax already recognised. This guide develops the control logic, illustrates a year-to-date true-up, separates accounting expense from advance-tax cash payments and addresses difficult loss-year and near-zero-profit cases without inventing a universal rate. Appendix B reinforces that interim tax expense is accrued using the rate applicable to expected total annual earnings. The interim period is therefore part of a larger annual measurement, not an isolated tax year compressed into three months. The annual-rate method should be read with paragraph 28’s year-to-date principle. At each reporting date, management updates expected annual accounting profit and expected annual tax expense, derives an appropriate effective rate where that ratio is meaningful, applies the revised estimate to the relevant year-to-date profit pool, and compares the cumulative requirement with expense already recognised. The current quarter is the balancing amount. Prior interim statements are not reissued merely because a later estimate changes, although significant changes require disclosure under the standard. Separate income-tax expense from advance-tax and withholding cash flows Ind AS 34 determines the interim accounting expense. Advance-tax instalments, self-assessment tax, refunds and tax deducted at source are cash or receivable/payable movements governed by tax law and payment dates. They do not become the income-tax expense simply because cash was paid during the quarter. Conversely, an accounting tax charge can arise without an equal current-quarter cash payment. Maintain a three-part reconciliation: current tax for the reporting period, deferred-tax movement and cash-tax or withholding balances. Reconcile opening tax receivable or payable, payments, refunds, withholding credits, current-tax provision and closing balance. The annual effective-rate model should connect to current and deferred tax, while the cash bridge connects ledger balances to challans, statements and tax records. Combining both into one percentage conceals timing differences and can misstate working capital. Ind AS 34’s application guidance contemplates separate estimated annual effective rates, to the extent practicable, for each tax jurisdiction and for categories of income taxed differently. An entity with ordinary business profit, capital gains, exempt income or foreign operations may need separate pools. Losses in one jurisdiction cannot automatically offset profit in another, and credits may have legal limits. The calculation should respect those boundaries before any weighted average is considered. Interim loss benefits need support under Ind AS 12 A tax benefit arising from an interim loss is recognised only when the underlying tax asset meets the relevant recognition requirements. Forecast taxable profits, reversal of taxable temporary differences, tax-planning opportunities, expiry periods and recent loss history are relevant. A benefit should not be booked merely to maintain a smooth annual rate, nor should a supportable benefit be omitted only because the quarter is loss-making. The evidence must be refreshed at each interim reporting date. Frequently Asked Questions on Ind AS 34 Interim Tax Expense Should quarterly profit simply be multiplied by the company’s headline tax rate? Generally no. Ind AS 34 uses the best estimate of the weighted-average annual effective income-tax rate expected for the full year, applied on a year-to-date basis. Why can the current quarter’s apparent tax rate differ from the annual effective rate? The current-quarter entry includes the true-up of the revised annual estimate on profit recognised in earlier quarters of the same year. Are advance-tax payments the same as interim tax expense? No. Payments affect tax receivables or payables. Expense is measured under Ind AS 34 and Ind AS 12 and includes current and deferred components. Must the prior quarter be restated when the annual rate estimate changes? A genuine later change in estimate is reflected through the current year-to-date calculation; prior interim statements are not ordinarily retrospectively restated merely for that change. Can one rate be used for all countries and income categories? Only if that weighted average reasonably approximates the result. Separate rates should be determined, to the extent practicable, for jurisdictions and differently taxed categories. Can an interim loss automatically create a deferred-tax benefit? No. Recognition depends on Ind AS 12 criteria and supportable evidence of recovery, including forecast taxable profit, reversals, expiry and recent-loss history. Which Indian income-tax law enters a 2026-27 annual forecast? For tax year 2026-27, the authenticated Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended and other applicable law should be used. Earlier assessment-year proceedings may continue under the 1961 Act and must not be mixed into the current-year forecast without analysis. Key Takeaways Ind AS 34 measures interim tax from the best estimated annual effective rate, not a mechanical quarter-only rate. Keep the annual tax numerator and accounting-profit denominator on the same forecast scope. Recompute cumulative year-to-date expense and record the current quarter as the difference from expense already recognised. Separate accounting expense from advance-tax, TDS, refund and other cash-tax movements. Use separate pools for jurisdictions, special-rate income and losses when one blended rate would mislead.
