(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India considered a Special Leave Petition filed by TTK Prestige Limited challenging an interlocutory order arising from a pending commercial suit concerning alleged infringement of the registered trademark ‘PRESTIGE’. The underlying proceedings involved an application under Order XI Rule 1(5) of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, as amended by the Commercial Courts Act, 2015, seeking permission to introduce additional documents relating to the plaintiff’s historical use of the trademark. The documents included promotional material from 1968 to 1986, Chartered Accountant certificates containing sales and promotional figures for earlier periods, and an unaudited statement of sales and promotional expenses. Read Delhi HC Judment in this case: Belated Trademark Evidence Rejected for Lack of Reasonable Cause: Delhi High Court The underlying High Court order had rejected the plaintiff’s application, finding that the requirements governing belated production of documents in commercial suits had not been satisfied. The High Court considered the chronology of the proceedings, including the filing of the suit in June 2021, the defendants’ written statement, the plaintiff’s replication and the subsequent applications for additional documents. It found that the plaintiff had multiple opportunities to produce the material and had failed to establish reasonable cause for the delay. The High Court distinguished the procedural flexibility applicable to ordinary civil suits from the stricter disclosure requirements applicable to commercial litigation. Before the Supreme Court, the petitioner challenged the interlocutory order passed in the pending commercial suit. After hearing learned Senior Counsel for the petitioner at considerable length and examining the material placed on record, the Supreme Court declined to interfere with the impugned order. The Court did not undertake a detailed examination of the underlying dispute concerning the admissibility of additional documents or pronounce a final ruling on the interpretation of Order XI Rule 1(5) CPC. Accordingly, the Supreme Court dismissed the Special Leave Petition and disposed of the pending interlocutory application. Significantly, it expressly kept the question of law open for determination in an appropriate case. Therefore, the dismissal leaves the impugned interlocutory order undisturbed without converting the High Court’s detailed reasoning into a binding pronouncement of the Supreme Court on the legal question. Cases Discussed Sugandhi & Anr. Vs P. Rajkumar — (2020) 10 SCC 706 (Supreme Court). Relied upon by the plaintiff to support a liberal approach towards admitting additional documents where procedural lapses do not cause serious prejudice. The High Court distinguished this decision because it concerned an ordinary civil suit rather than a commercial suit. Vijay Kumar Varshney Vs Longlast Power Products Ltd. & Anr. — 2023 SCC OnLine Del 3775 (Delhi High Court). Cited by the plaintiff concerning permission to introduce documents rebutting a defendant’s claim of prior trademark use. The High Court distinguished the decision on its facts and the timing of the application. CEC-CICI JV & Ors. Vs Oriental Insurance Company Limited — CS(COMM) 7/2020, decided on 15 May 2023 (Delhi High Court). Relied upon by the defendants regarding strict compliance with disclosure requirements under Order XI Rule 1(5) CPC. The High Court considered its reasoning relevant to the plaintiff’s delayed application. Ambalal Sarabhai Enterprises Ltd. Vs K.S. Infraspace LLP — (2020) 15 SCC 585 (Supreme Court). Referred to regarding the legislative objective of expeditious disposal of commercial disputes and the need to interpret the Commercial Courts Act consistently with that objective. Sudhir Kumar alias S. Baliyan Vs Vinay Kumar G.B. — 2021 SCC OnLine SC 734 (Supreme Court). Discussed through the reasoning reproduced from CEC-CICI JV, particularly the requirements of Order XI Rules 1(4) and 1(5) CPC and the circumstances in which reasonable cause for non-disclosure must be established. Bela Creation Private Limited Vs Anuj Textiles — 2022 SCC OnLine Del 1366 (Delhi High Court). Cited through the underlying High Court precedent concerning the meaning of reasonable cause and why negligence in filing documents does not ordinarily satisfy the statutory requirement. Nitin Gupta Vs Texmaco Infrastructure & Holding Limited — 2019 SCC OnLine Del 8367 (Delhi High Court). Referred to regarding enforcement of the stricter procedural requirements applicable to commercial suits and the need to establish good cause for delayed document production. Rishi Raj Vs Saregama India Ltd. — 2021 SCC OnLine Del 4897 (Delhi High Court). Cited concerning rejection of belated additional documents where the explanation of inadvertent error did not establish reasonable cause. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER 1. We have heard learned Senior Counsel for the petitioner at a considerable length and carefully perused the material placed on record. 2. Having regard to the fact that the impugned interlocutory order is arising out of a pending Commercial Suit, we are not inclined to interfere with the same. 3. The Special Leave Petition is, accordingly, dismissed. 4. However, the question of law is kept open to be decided in an appropriate case. 5. As a result, the pending interlocutory application also stands disposed of.
