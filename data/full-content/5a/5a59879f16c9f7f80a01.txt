Summary: Compliance with the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) requires Indian entities to use different Reserve Bank of India reporting and approval systems for foreign investment, regulatory permissions and annual financial information. The FIRMS portal facilitates reporting through the Single Master Form, including Form FC-GPR for eligible equity issuances and Form FC-TRS for reportable transfers. Registrations through Entity Master and Business User accounts, proper AD-bank mapping, valuation documents and compliance with applicable reporting deadlines are essential. The PRAVAAH portal provides a centralised mechanism for applications seeking RBI regulatory authorisations, licences and approvals. The FLAIR portal is used for the annual Foreign Liabilities and Assets return, ordinarily due by 15 July where the reporting conditions are satisfied. Delayed reporting can attract Late Submission Fees or other FEMA consequences, subject to the applicable regulations. Companies should maintain reliable transaction records, reconcile foreign investment and banking information, document valuations and preserve acknowledgements. Monitoring cross-border transactions alongside EDPMS and IDPMS records helps identify discrepancies affecting export and import compliance. Errors or minor discrepancies often result in immediate rejections by Authorized Dealer (AD) banks, compounding late submission fees (LSF), or formal contraventions. 1. FIRMS Portal (Foreign Investment Reporting and Management System) The FIRMS Portal hosts the Single Master Form (SMF) used to report inbound and outbound equity investments (such as Form FC-GPR, Form FC-TRS, and Form LLP-I). The Two-Tier Registration Framework: The entity must first register its corporate identity via Entity Master (EM) managed by an Entity User (EU). Separately, a Business User (BU) registration is required for the individual executing the actual filings, which must be mapped to and manually approved by a specific AD Category-I Bank. Strict Calendar Deadlines & LSF Triggers: Timelines are strictly calculated from the date of the underlying corporate action. Form FC-GPR (Inbound FDI Allotment) must be submitted within 30 days of issuing shares, and Form FC-TRS (Inbound FDI Transfer) must be submitted within 60 days of transfer execution or receipt/remittance of funds, whichever occurs earlier. Missing these triggers requires regularisation via Late Submission Fees (LSF). Underlying Technical and Pricing Violations: Filings can still be rejected during manual processing if they fail substantive benchmarks. Ensure filings contain certified valuation reports adhering to FEMA pricing guidelines (e.g., DCF method), definitive Foreign Inward Remittance Certificates (FIRC), and KYC reports from the remitting bank, Board resolutions, MoA and AoA, Company secretary certificates. Failure in filing and obtaining UIN :Fully restrict outward remittances of future sale proceeds, fractional amounts, future issue or further issue of shares etc. 2. PRAVAAH Portal (Platform for Regulatory Application, Validation and Authorisation) The PRAVAAH Portal serves as a centralized platform for processing complex regulatory permissions, regulatory interpretations, and special approvals rather than routine reporting. Pre-Filing Readiness: Required to register on it before any anticipated application requirements arise. Centralization of Approval Applications: It handles complex cross-border structures that fall outside standard automatic limits, such as revised External Commercial Borrowings (ECB) approval requests, structural corporate guarantees, and unique trade-credit structures. Precision in Documentary Evidence: Applications are reviewed directly by central RBI regulatory cells rather than front-line AD banks. KYCs, Board resolutions, MoA and AoA, Company secretary certificates. Explanatory narratives, financial projections, end-use undertakings, and structural diagrams must align exactly with the specific FEMA notification. 3. FLAIR Portal (Foreign Liabilities and Assets Information Reporting) The FLAIR Portal tracks the annual status of cross-border investment positions. The Cutoff dates: The annual FLA Return must be filed electronically on or before 15 July every year based on audited or provisionally approved financial statements. Filing Blockades: Failing to meet the July 15 deadline carries severe downstream consequences, automatically blocking the entity from receiving additional FDI or executing outbound direct investments (ODI) until the default is rectified. Reporting Basis: The FLA return captures aggregate balance sheet positions—including direct foreign equity holdings, retained earnings allocated to foreign shareholders, market valuations, and cross-border debt liabilities. Operational Note for Compliance Tracking Always ensure a tight internal reconciliation loop between your actual cross-border transaction data and the corresponding entries in the EDPMS (Export Data Processing and Monitoring System) and IDPMS (Import Data Processing and Monitoring System) maintained by your AD Bank. All transaction histories, valuation certificates, and portal acknowledgements must be archived in a dedicated compliance register for at least 5 years. ***** Disclaimer: This is an explanatory summary.
