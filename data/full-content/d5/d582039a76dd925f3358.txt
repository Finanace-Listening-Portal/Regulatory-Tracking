ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Sep 1, 2026 at 07:51 AM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 133666137; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook HDFC Bank’s incoming managing director and CEO is likely to face a difficult balancing act between restoring the bank’s balance-sheet structure and maintaining growth, with succession uncertainty, funding pressures and potential senior-management churn emerging as key challenges.The new CEO may have to prioritise fixing the bank’s balance-sheet structure even at the cost of near-term growth, as the lender has seen a sharp decline in its CASA ratio as a proportion of external funding following the merger with HDFC Ltd. At the same time, the cost of funds has risen while lending yields have moderated, creating pressure on medium-term profitable growth, according to a report by ICICI Securities. Advt The brokerage expects HDFC Bank’s loan growth to remain at 13-14% annually through FY26-28, below systemic growth, while estimating around 14% return on equity. It has cut its target price on the bank to Rs 920 from Rs 1,020, while retaining a BUY rating.Succession presents no easy optionThe immediate challenge for the board will be finding a successor, with neither the internal nor external route offering an easy solution.Deputy Managing Director Kaizad Bharucha is considered a strong internal candidate, but his residual tenure is less than three years under existing RBI norms. Another executive director, V Srinivasa Rangan, has limited experience in commercial banking.An external candidate could bring a different set of challenges. While HDFC Bank should not have difficulty attracting outside talent given its scale and franchise, an external appointment could involve cultural-integration issues, management exits and a potentially longer strategic overhaul.The board could also promote a senior executive who is not currently on the board, although this route could have its own challenges. An appointment from a public sector bank, while unlikely, also cannot be ruled out.Balance-sheet repair could take priority over growthThe bigger strategic challenge for the incoming CEO could be rebuilding HDFC Bank’s funding profile. Advt The bank has lagged in FCNR(B) mobilisation, which otherwise could have helped address its loan-to-deposit ratio and liquidity concerns.At the same time, its CASA ratio, measured as a proportion of external funding, has fallen sharply since the HDFC Ltd merger. The resulting increase in cost of funds, alongside moderation in lending yields, has created pressure on medium-term profitable growth.This could force the incoming CEO to make a strategic trade-off between growth and balance-sheet repair.The new management may therefore prioritise fixing the balance-sheet structure over near-term loan expansion. Loan growth and the strategy to improve the loan-to-deposit ratio have also oscillated in recent periods. Advt Management stability another key monitorableThe succession decision could have implications for the broader leadership team, particularly if the board opts for an external candidate.An outside appointment could result in cultural-integration issues, management exits and a longer period of strategic change. Senior-management stability could therefore become an important monitorable during the transition.The leadership change also comes after a period in which HDFC Bank faced several regulatory and governance issues.These included the Credit Suisse AT1 bond matter involving the bank’s overseas operations and NRI clients, the MSRDC bulk-deposit pricing issue and a subsequent internal review that termed the actions “business overreach”. The board later issued warning letters and imposed monetary penalties on Jagdishan and other officials. Advt The abrupt resignation of former chairman Atanu Chakraborty in March 2026 also brought renewed attention to board oversight and governance. Chakraborty had cited a lack of congruence between recent developments at the bank and his personal values and ethics.New CEO will inherit a bank seeking a resetJagdishan’s tenure included some of HDFC Bank’s most consequential strategic changes, including the merger with HDFC Ltd and the listing of HDB Financial Services. At the same time, the bank did not achieve exemplary outcomes on CASA and deposit growth despite significant branch expansion.The bank has also oscillated on its loan-growth and loan-to-deposit-ratio improvement strategy, leaving the incoming CEO with the task of establishing a clearer medium-term direction.The leadership change could therefore become more than a routine succession exercise. The new MD&CEO may need to undertake a strategic pivot focused on funding, liquidity and balance-sheet structure, even if that means accepting slower growth in the near term.For investors, the extent of this course correction could determine the scope for a re-rating. The bank’s stock has underperformed the banking index by around 40% over the past three years, suggesting that a significant portion of the weakness may already be priced in.The key risks remain a difficult CEO succession and further churn in senior management.
