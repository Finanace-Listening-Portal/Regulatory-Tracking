Hayakriva Associates and Builders Vs Deputy Commissioner of CGST and Central Excise & Another, quashed the order dated 23 December 2025 revoking a waiver earlier granted under Section 128A of the CGST Act, 2017. Justice C. Saravanan remitted the matter to the respondents for a fresh order in accordance with law. The dispute related to FY 2018–19 and arose from belated payment of tax declared in monthly returns and GSTR-3B, together with belated filing of GSTR-3B. The petitioner challenged the Form GST VOID ORDER bearing Reference No. ZD331225353428F dated 23 December 2025 and sought implementation of Form GST SPL-05 dated 7 August 2025, through which its settlement application had initially been accepted. The judgment records that the impugned order confirmed the proposal in SCN No. 05/2024 (O.C. No. 63/2024) dated 22 January 2024. It was undisputed that proceedings had been initiated under Section 73, rather than Section 74, and that the petitioner had discharged its tax liability before the waiver application was initially allowed. The Court followed Incompressible Fluid Control System v. Assistant Commissioner, Chennai, (2026) 45 Centax 261 (Mad.). The Revenue’s counsel fairly conceded that the issue was covered against the Department by that decision. The reproduced precedent held that proceedings under Section 73 are essential for a Section 128A application. Direct recovery under Section 79 read with Section 75(12), without invoking Section 73, does not permit such an application; once the Revenue determines liability under Section 73, however, self-assessed tax is not impliedly excluded from the waiver provision. The reproduced principles further stated that an approval in Form GST SPL-05 cannot be declared void by invoking Section 161 or otherwise, unless the matter falls within Rule 164(16) or (17). Failure to issue an order within Rule 164(13) attracts deemed approval under Rule 164(14), provided the substantive requirements of Section 128A are met. The scope of the statutory waiver cannot be curtailed by Circular No. 238/32/2024-GST, while the time limits prescribed in Rule 164 remain mandatory. The quoted batch decision distinguished eligible applications rejected merely because they concerned self-assessed tax, including applications initially approved and subsequently declared void, from cases involving late applications, belated payments or no waiver application. It set aside the former category of rejection and void orders but dismissed the identified delayed or unfiled-application matters. Those tables and batch dispositions form part of the precedent reproduced in this order; they are distinct from the present petitioner’s operative relief. Applying the precedent, the Court quashed the impugned order, directed fresh consideration in accordance with law, disposed of the writ petition without costs and closed the connected miscellaneous petition. The conclusions in paragraphs 38 to 40 are reproduced below: “38. The conclusions that follow from the above discussion and analysis of the relevant provisions may be summarised as under: (i) The initiation of proceedings under Section 73 of applicable GST enactments is a sine qua non for filing an application for waiver. (ii) If recovery measures were to be initiated under Section 79 of applicable GST enactments, without invoking Section 73, by relying on Section 75(12), the taxable person cannot file an application under Section 128A. On the other hand, once the revenue decides to determine liability under Section 73, whether in relation to self-assessed tax or otherwise, it is open to the taxable person to file an application under Section 128A. (iii) Once an order accepting the waiver application is issued in Form GST SPL 05, unless the matter falls within the scope of sub-rule (16) or (17) of Rule 164, it is not open to the GST authorities to issue an order declaring that the earlier approval order is void by reference to Section 161 or otherwise. (iv) If the order is not issued within the time limit prescribed in sub-rule (13), there shall be deemed approval of the application and conclusion of proceedings in terms of sub-rule (14), provided the substantive conditions stipulated in Section 128A are complied with. (v) The implied exclusion of cases relating to self- assessed tax cannot be read into Section 128A. The meaning, as gleaned from the text and context, does not warrant such implied exclusion. (vi) The scope and ambit of Section 128A cannot be curtailed by the issuance of Circular No.238. (vii) The time limits prescribed in Rule 164 are mandatory. Adjudication of individual cases 39. This is merely another way of framing rejection of waiver applications in relation to self-assessed tax.
