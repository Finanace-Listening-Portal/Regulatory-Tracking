Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Sep 30, 2026 at 04:49 PM IST var news_msid = 134592380; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook India’s economy is expected to grow 7% in 2026-27 after expanding 7.8% in 2025-26, but sustaining that pace will require the country to convert its recent macroeconomic resilience into a broader and more durable growth cycle, according to S&P Global and Crisil’s latest report.The report said the next phase of India’s development will increasingly depend on private investment, deeper capital markets, stronger financial intermediation and greater integration with global markets, rather than relying predominantly on public infrastructure spending and domestic demand.S&P Global said India’s strong growth performance despite global uncertainty and high US tariffs highlights the resilience of domestic growth drivers. However, the report argues that the challenge now is to translate that resilience into sustained momentum through the next phase of infrastructure development, competitive federalism and stronger channels for mobilising and allocating capital. Advt Private investment needs to become a stronger growth engineThe report’s assessment of India’s medium-term growth prospects puts greater emphasis on private investment and FDI, alongside trade agreements, as engines of the next growth phase.S&P Global and Crisil’s analysis said India has outperformed major economies following the pandemic, supported by public infrastructure spending, digital infrastructure, targeted manufacturing incentives, welfare measures and economic reforms. It also noted that India’s estimated growth potential has been raised to around 7% from 6.5% earlier.The report, however, points to the need to activate these medium-term drivers as global shocks become more frequent and the global economic environment becomes more protectionist. Greater engagement through trade agreements is identified as an important avenue for India to expand its external growth opportunities.The implication for the financial sector is significant, a sustained investment cycle would require banks, NBFCs, capital markets and other financial intermediaries to increasingly channel savings into productive investment.Deeper capital markets, financial intermediation in focusThe report noted that India’s capital market has expanded substantially over the past decade. As of June 30, 2026, the Indian equity market had $2.03 trillion in investable market capitalisation, alongside a significantly broader listed-company universe.But the report also highlighted a paradox in the equity market, despite strong market growth and significant dispersion across sectors and stocks, more than 70% of actively managed Indian funds underperformed their relevant benchmarks across every category examined in the SPIVA India Scorecard over the 10 years ended June 30, 2026. Advt That raises a broader question for India’s financial ecosystem, whether deeper capital markets are translating into more efficient capital allocation, particularly as the economy attempts to shift towards a larger private investment cycle.Digital rupee could add another layer to India's financial infrastructureS&P Global’s report also identified the digital rupee as a potential fourth layer of India’s digital public infrastructure, alongside digital identity, payments and account aggregators.Rather than measuring the CBDC primarily through retail adoption, the report argues that its larger economic potential could lie in programmable money, wholesale applications, tokenised securities settlement and cross-border payments. Advt In a tokenised global economy, the digital rupee could also help keep cross-border B2B trade and remittances anchored to the sovereign currency rather than shifting towards foreign-currency stablecoins, according to the report.For India’s BFSI sector, this puts the focus beyond the headline 7% GDP number. The next phase of growth will increasingly depend on how effectively the financial system mobilises capital, supports private investment and uses digital infrastructure to improve the movement and settlement of money and assets.
