Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); State Bank of India Vs DCIT (ITAT Mumbai) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Mumbai, disposed of cross-appeals involving State Bank of India and the Income Tax Department for assessment years 2001-02 and 2002-03 by a consolidated order pronounced on 12 July 2021. The appeals concerned ITA Nos. 4409, 4656, 4408 and 4655/Mum/2011, challenging appellate orders dated 29 March 2011. At the hearing, both sides acknowledged that many questions had already been adjudicated in the bank’s own appeals for earlier years. The Tribunal therefore applied those precedents issue by issue rather than repeating extensive factual discussion. The assessee’s appeals were partly allowed for statistical purposes, while the Revenue’s appeals were dismissed. For assessment year 2001-02, the Tribunal allowed the bank’s claim relating to broken-period interest of Rs.274,04,19,099, following its earlier orders and noting that the Revenue’s appeal for assessment year 1996-97 had been dismissed by the jurisdictional High Court. It also allowed the deduction for deferred-payment guarantee commission of Rs.64,82,28,164 and depreciation on securities of Rs.35,99,26,182, both on the basis of consistent earlier-year decisions. However, it rejected the bank’s ground concerning scientific research payments of Rs.3,36,420, despite its argument that the payments had been made from funds created out of taxed profits. A significant dispute concerned disallowance under Rule 8D analysis” target=”_blank” rel=”noopener”>Section 14A for exempt income. The bank contested disallowance of Rs.337,18,21,921 for assessment year 2001-02, relying on its own non-interest-bearing funds and arguing that Rule 8D could not apply retrospectively. The Tribunal accepted that Rule 8D was inapplicable to that assessment year and followed its earlier practice of estimating expenditure at one percent of exempt income. It issued the same direction for assessment year 2002-03 in relation to the disputed disallowance of Rs.199,64,30,232. These directions were based on consistency with the Tribunal’s decisions in SBI’s preceding assessments. The Tribunal rejected depreciation on leased assets for both years, following decisions against the bank on similar sale-and-leaseback issues. It also upheld the treatment of a one-time Rs.24 crore payment received from Cardif S.A. in connection with formation of SBI Life Insurance Company as revenue income. Although SBI described the receipt as a non-compete payment or capital receipt, the Tribunal found that it represented consideration for the commercial advantage of access to the bank’s established branch network and customer base in developing the insurance business. It therefore sustained the addition for assessment year 2001-02. Other issues for assessment year 2001-02 produced mixed results. The Tribunal allowed expenses connected with the India Millennium Deposits issue, including road-show, legal and advertising expenditure, following an earlier order in the bank’s favour. It rejected the original grounds concerning bad debts written off on non-rural advances and provisions for doubtful debts under Sections 36(1)(vii) and 36(1)(viia), respectively, based on the decisions then followed in the bank’s own cases. It restored the foreign-office provision claim for fresh examination and upheld interest under Section 234D. Additional grounds on bad-debt write-offs, recovery of previously written-off debts and foreign-branch income were restored to the Assessing Officer for verification and fresh adjudication. For assessment year 2002-03, the Tribunal allowed deferred-payment guarantee commission and broken-period expenses, but rejected depreciation on matured investments and scientific research payments. It accepted the bank’s revised valuation of Held for Trading securities under Reserve Bank of India guidelines and deleted the addition of Rs.106.46 crore for unrealised appreciation. The Tribunal reasoned that banks were required to follow RBI valuation directions and should not recognise unrealised gains merely because the valuation policy had changed. It also allowed the bank’s claim concerning Rs.36.60 crore paid to US banking regulators, characterising the payments in the circumstances as compensatory rather than punitive and therefore deductible business expenditure. The Tribunal’s remaining findings for assessment year 2002-03 included rejection of the original doubtful-debt provision claim and the ground against interest under Section 234D, while additional grounds concerning bad-debt write-offs, recovery of bad debts and foreign-branch income were restored for fresh adjudication. The Revenue’s appeals for both years were dismissed. For assessment year 2001-02, relief for payments to schools for employees’ children and taxation of interest on securities was sustained. For assessment year 2002-03, the Tribunal upheld relief relating to staff-welfare expenditure, amortisation of securities in the Held to Maturity category and taxation of interest on securities. The final result was that SBI’s appeals were partly allowed for statistical purposes and the Revenue’s appeals dismissed. Cases Discussed State Bank of India v. The learned Departmental Representative on the other hand submitted that the amount received by the assessee is credited in Profit & Loss Account and the assessee claimed the above income as exempt only in computation of income for the purpose of Income Tax which is placed at paper book Page-103 to 109. He submitted that the payment received by the assessee as a part of premium as per the communication submitted by the assessee in paper book at Page-100­103. He also brought to our notice the joint venture agreement. Further, he brought to our notice Para-11.3.1 of the order passed by the learned CIT(A) and he brought to our notice the findings given by the learned CIT(A) that the payment was made for utilization of assessee’s brand as Cardif S.A. did not receive any asset in exchange of the consideration paid. There is nothing on record to indicate that there is a case of sale of brand or extinguishment of assessee’s right over its brand and the assessee allowed its brand to be used by the Insurance Company, therefore, it is revenue in nature. For that proposition, he heavily relied upon the findings of the learned CIT(A). 27. Considered the rival submissions and perused the material on record. We notice that the assessee entered into Joirt Venture with Cardif S.A. to form an entity SBI Life Insurance Co. Ltd., with the ratio of 74:26 between them. The Assessing Officer notice that the assessee received an amount of Z 24 crore for accepting Cardif S.A. as Joint Venture partner. It was paid as premium for the above purpose. The assessee submitted consistently that this payment is towards non-compete fee and not received in the ordinary course of business. Since it is onetime payment, it is only towards commencing insurance business. Therefore, it can only be capital in nature and, hence not taxable. It was argued that the non-compete fee is not taxable in this assessment year. We also noticed that the Revenue authorities treated the above payments towards utilization of brand of the assessee for the insurance business. The authorities applied the purpose test and the value exchange test to bring this receipt in revenue in nature. 28. We also notice that the assessee agreed to enter into insurance business with the collaboration of Cardif S.A. to form a new venture. The assessee will facilitate to establish the insurance business along with its regular business of banking. The existing and prospective customers are targeted and encouraged to take insurance policy. The concept itself “bancasurrance”. It means Insurance company in this case is Joint Venture, ties up with the assessee branches, the branches targets existing and prospective customers not only for banking facilities but also for insurance. These activities are carried out simultaneously with the banking facilities. The Joint Venture gets added advantage to expand their business with the existing facilities of vast network of the assessee. The Joint Venture i.e., Cardif S.A. paid the premium not only for selecting it for Joint Venture partner but for getting ready made platform for establishing the business and huge advantage to the Joint Venture partners and Joint Venture to establish its business. The payment is more towards the facilitation than the brand value. In fact, there is no doubt that the assessee carries the huge brand value since it is in the interest of Joint Venture not to compete with each other. Therefore, we cannot agree that it will fall under non-compete fee or restrictive trade practice. Rather it is towards offering the branch network for the establishment of insurance business. In the changing business scenario, the banking business has huge shift from the traditional banking business. The RBI has taken note of it and we noticed that so many banks has established its own insurance business along with the banking business. Some of them has utilised their own brand name. But some of the banks have entered Joint Venture with the international big players in the insurance business. In this case, the assessee has selected Cardif S.A. The international players will look for readymade establishment to establish their business. The assessee is providing its huge network facility to establish this new line of business along with its regular banking business. The assessee received the onetime premium towards this additional facilities and advantage which it was willing to offer to the other partner. Therefore, in the changing business module in the banking sector, it can only be treated as charges for facilitation offer or advantage for establishing the insurance business in India. Therefore, it cannot be said that it is for non-compete but it is for the advantage offered for the Joint Venture business. Therefore, we are inclined to sustain the addition made by the Assessing Officer. Thus, ground no.7, raised by the assessee is dismissed. 29. The board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, USA, proposed the above civil penalty on the non-compliance of accounting and reporting requirements. The Assessing Officer held that penalty imposed for breach of law during the course of trade be treated as commercial loss. This view was supported by the decision of various Courts that infraction of law is not a normal incident of business, therefore, no expenses which are claimed by way of penalty for breach of law can be an amount wholly and exclusively for the purpose of business. The above view of the Assessing Officer was upheld by the learned CIT(A).
