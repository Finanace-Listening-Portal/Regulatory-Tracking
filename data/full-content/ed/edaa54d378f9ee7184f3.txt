Before the learned CIT(A), the assessee had challenged the reopening of the assessment and non-application of procedure relating to the assessment. The said grounds of appeal were dismissed by the learned CIT(A) and the relevant extracts of the order of the ld. CIT(A) in para no. 5 & 6 are reproduced as under: “5. Ground of appeal no. 1: I have carefully examined the assessment order and the written submission filed by the assessee, the legal position laid down by various judicial pronouncements clearly establishes that so long as the Assessing Officer has reason to believe, based on tangible material, that income has escaped assessment, the proceedings can not be rendered invalid on basis of hyper-technical defects. The following judicial pronouncements, each in favour of the Revenue, support the validity of reassessment action. Ankit Financial Services Ltd. v. DCIT(Gujarat High Court) This decision reiterates that reassessment can not be invalidated merely because the Assessing Officer relied upon information from investigation wing. The court held such information constitutes tangible material and when AO forms his own belief, jurisdiction is correctly assumed. Thus, objections of borrowed satisfaction are unfounded. Ankit Agrochem (P.) Ltd. v. JCIT (Rajasthan High Court) The Hon’ble Rajasthan High court ruled that borrowed satisfaction argument can not invalidate reopening where AO has applied his mind to investigation wing report. The Court observed that AO’s independent application of mind while recording reason was evident, hence jurisdiction is proper. From the perusal of the assessment order, it is clearly evident that the AO has done has done application of mind with regard to information received and thereafter reopened the case of the assessee. Therefore, In view of the above judicial pronouncements, the reopening of the case is valid. Hence, grounds no.1 isdismissed. 6. Ground Nos. 2 to 5: It is evident from the assessment order that due procedure has been followed during re-assessment proceedings. Hence, ground nos.2 to 5 are dismissed.” 2.5 On the merits, the learned CIT(A) agreed with the findings of the AO and dismissed the appeal of the assessee. In this regard, the relevant extract of the order of the learned CIT(A) in para 7.3 is reproduced as under: “7.3 Decision: I have carefully examined the assessment order and the written submission filed by the assessee. The AO has mentioned in the Assessment Order as follows: “7. To verify the genuiness of transaction proof of identity of the creditors and creditworthiness of the sundry creditor a notice u/s 133(6) was issued to M/s Pawansut Holdings Limited and Focus Industrial Resources Limited on 15/12/2021 and fixing the case for reply by 30/12/2021 to the both above companies with supporting documents vis-a-vis, copy of income-tax return, bank statements, copy of ledger account, Mode of payments made, statements of affair’s as on 31/03/2014 and 31/03/2015. But till date no reply filed by the both companies nor filed any adjournment.” Thus, it is evident from the Assessment Order that as per the report of investigation wing, the appellant has been provided accommodation entries in lieu of cash through the various companies handled by the entry provided Shri Pradeep Kumar Jindal. Further, when the AO issued notice under section 133(6) to M/s Pawansut Holdings Limited and Focus Industrial Resources Limited then they did not respond. It may be pertinent to refer to the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of CIT vs. Durga Prasad More 1973 CTR (SC) 500: (1971) 82 ITR 540 (SC)at p.545. In this case, their Lordships of the Supreme Court in regard to the appreciation of evidence laid down the following principles of law:- “it is true that an apparent must be considered real until it is shown that there are reasons to believe that the apparent is not the real. In a case of the present kind a party who relies on a recital in a deed has to establish the truth of those recitals, otherwise it will be very easy to make self-serving statements in documents either executed or taken by a party and rely on those recitals. If all that an assessee who wants to evade tax is to have some recitals made in a document either executed by him or executed in his favour then the door will be left wide open to evade tax. A little probing was sufficient in the present case to show that the apparent was not the real. The taxing authorities were not required to put on blinkers while looking at the documents produced before them. They were entitled to look into the surrounding circumstances to find out the reality of the recitals made in those documents. Now, coming to the question of onus, the law does not prescribe any quantitative test to find out whether the onus in a particular case has been discharged or not. It all depends on the facts and circumstances of each case. In some cases, the onus may be heavy whereas, in others, it may be nominal. There is nothing rigid about it.” It was further laid down by their Lordships: “Science has not yet invented any instrument to test the reliability of the evidence placed before a Court or Tribunal. Therefore, the Courts and Tribunals have to judge the evidence before them by applying the test of human probabilities, Human minds may differ as to the reliability of a piece of evidence. But in that sphere the decision of the final fact finding authority is made conclusive by law.” The above principles of law have been reiterated by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Sumati Dayal vs. CIT (1995) 125 CTR (SC) 124: (1995) 214 ITR 801 (SC). Thus, by applying the principles of law mentioned in above judicial pronouncements, the genuineness of transaction, proof of identity of the creditors and creditworthiness of the sundry creditor could not be established during the assessment proceedings. Hence, the AO has rightly done the addition. In view of the above, the addition amounting to Rs. 85,00,000/- & 2,12,500/-made by the A.O. is being upheld. Hence, ground nos. 6 to 14 & 16 of appeal are dismissed.” 3. Aggrieved with the said order, the assessee is in appeal before us on the following grounds of appeal: “1. Ground No. 1 of the appeal is a legal ground challenging the legal validity of the issue of notice dated 31.03.2021 u/s. 148 of the Act on the ground that the same was issued on ‘borrowed satisfaction by the AO. This ground of appeal goes to the root of the matter and therefore, it is taken up for adjudication first. 5. The learned AR in this regard filed a written submission and the relevant extract of the same is reproduced as under: “1.1 In the cases relied on by Ld.CIT(A) the Hon’ble Court observed that AO forms his own belief on information report received from Investigation Wing. However, in the present case AO acted on the belief of ADIT who incorrectly reported that cash introduced in the bank account of accommodation entry provider before providing accommodation entries whereas there was no cash deposit in the bank account of the alleged accommodation entry provider. Further information report lacking the mode i.e. instrument number, bank details and nature of transactions which is evident that in the reasons all these details are missing which shows that the belief is not of AO. 1.2 In this regard the assessee relied on the following decisions:- a) 476 ITR 236(Guj) Nimesh Maheshbhai Shah (HUF) vs ITO b) 474 ITR 131 (Delhi) Well Trans Logistics India Pvt. On the other hand, the learned Sr. DR supported the orders of the authorities below. 7. We have heard both the parties and perused the material on record. In this regard, the reasons recorded by the AO for the issuance of notice u/s 148 of the Act dated 31.03.2021 in this case is reproduced as under: Annexure-A Name of the Assessee S V D PROMOTERS Address of the Assessee 24/25 PATEL MARKET LOHAMANDI AGRA 282002 PAN of the Assessee ACQFS3161E Assessment Year 2015-16 Details of the Assessing Officer having Jurisdiction over the Assessee Income Tax Officer-1(1)(1), Agra Reason for reopening of the Assessment in case of S V D PROMOTERS for A.Y. 2015-16 u/s 147 of the Act. 1. Accordingly, in this case, the only requirement to initiate proceeding u/s 147 is reason to believe which has been recorded above. 7. It is pertinent to mention here that in this case the assessee has filed return of income for the year under consideration but no assessment as stipulated u/s 2(40) of the Act was made and the return of income was only processed u/s 143(1) of the Act. In view of the above, provisions of clause (b) of explanation 2 to section 147 are applicable to facts of this case and the assessment year under consideration is deemed to be a case where income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment. 8. In this case more than four years have lapsed from the end of assessment year under consideration. Hence necessary sanction to issue notice u/s 148 has been obtained separately from Principal Commissioner of Income Tax as per the provisions of section 151 of the Act. (Balbir Singh) ITO-1(1)(1), Agra 7.1 On perusal of the above reasons, it is noticed that the AO recorded the reasons that the transaction of Rs.85,00,000/- received from M/s. Pawansut Holdings Ltd. and Focus Industrial Resources Ltd. are in the nature of accommodation entries. This was on the basis of an information received from ADIT (Inv) – 2(1), New Delhi that a search and seizure and survey operations was conducted in the case of Shri Pradeep Kumar Jindal in 2015/2016 wherein, it was informed that Shri Pradeep Kumar Jindal introduced/deposited cash in banking channel through some of its companies and after layering provided accommodation entries to the beneficiaries including the assessee. Further, there is also a reference in the reasons recorded that during the search and survey operations conducted by the department in the case of Shri Praveen Kumar Jindal and his various allies during the year 2015 and 2016, whereinthe statements of the entry provider Shri Pradeep Kumar Jindal, his allies and beneficiaries were recorded wherein they all accepted that Shri Praveen Kumar Jindal provided accommodation entries in lieu of cash to various beneficiaries including the present assessee and also the entire modus operandi was explained. However, on the perusal of the above reasons recorded, we notice that there isno extract of any suchstatement recorded wherein, Shri Pradeep Kumar Jindal or his allies or the beneficiaries had accepted that Shri Pradeep Kumar Jindal provided accommodation entries in lieu of cash to various beneficiaries including the present assessee. Similarly, in the reasons recorded, no extract of any bank statement in support of the reasons recorded that cash was being introduced in the bank accounts in the companies controlled by Shri Praveen Kumar Jindal facilitating such accommodation entries including the alleged accommodation to the assessee is stated. In absence of the same, the contention of the assessee that the AO did not apply his mind to the information received, but acted only on the ‘borrowed satisfaction’ in respect of the information received by the AO from the ADIT (Inv.)- 2(1), New Delhi is found to be acceptable. Non- mentioning of the above details in the reasons recorded show that the AO has not verified such statements or the bank accounts, on the basis of which he has recorded the reasons. Therefore, we do not agree with the findings of the Ld. CIT(A) that the AO had applied his mind in respect of the information received from the investigation wing before issuing the impugned notice u/s 148 of the Act dated 31.03.2021. On similar facts, the Hon’ble Delhi High Court in the case of PCIT vs. Meenakshi Overseas Pvt. Ltd., 395 ITR 677 (Delhi) held that initiation of proceedings u/s. 147/148 of the Act to reopen the assessment for the year in question did not satisfy the requirement of law. In this regard, the relevant extract of the order of the Hon’ble Court is reproduced as under: “36. In the present case, as already noticed, the reasons to believe contain not the reasons but the conclusions of the AO one after the other. There is no independent application of mind by the AO to the tangible material which forms the basis of the reasons to believe that income has escaped assessment. The conclusions of the AO are at best a reproduction of the conclusion in the investigation report. Indeed it is a ‘borrowed satisfaction’. The reasons fail to demonstrate the link between the tangible material and the formation of the reason to believe that income has escaped assessment. 37. For the aforementioned reasons, the Court is satisfied that in the facts and circumstances of the case, no error has been committed by the ITAT in the impugned order in concluding that the initiation of the proceedings under Section 147/148 of the Act to reopen the assessments for the AYs in question does not satisfy the requirement of law. 38. The question framed is answered in the negative, i.e., in favour of the Assessee and against the Revenue. The appeal is, accordingly, dismissed but with no orders as to costs.” 7.2 Therefore, in view of the above discussion and respectfully following the order of the Hon’ble Delhi High Court in the case of Meenakshi Overseas Pvt. Ltd. (supra), we hold that the reasons recorded by the AO are based on ‘borrowed satisfaction’ and there is no independent application of mind by the AO to the tangible material, which formed the basis of reason to believe that income to the extent of Rs.85,00,000/- and Rs.2,12,500/- had escaped assessment in this case. Accordingly, we hold that the notice issued u/s. 148 of the Act dated 31.03.2021 in this case is not a valid notice and the impugned assessment order dated 11.02.2022 passed u/s. 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act in pursuant to the said invalid notice is not a valid assessment order in the eyes of law. Accordingly, the notice u/s. 148 of the Act dated 31.03.2021 and the assessment order u/s. 147 dated 11.02.2022 are set aside and quashed. Ground No. 1 of the appeal is allowed. 7.3 In view of ground No. 1 of the appeal being allowed, the other grounds of appeal become academic and are left open in this case. 8. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 18/11/2026.
