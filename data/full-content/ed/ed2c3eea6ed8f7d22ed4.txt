Articles 3 min read NBFCs must prioritise quality lending and MSME growth to drive India’s credit economy: SEBI’s G Ram Mohan Rao NBFCs must move beyond disbursement targets and focus on productive credit, asset quality and underserved borrowers to sustain growth, G Ram Mohan Rao, Executive Director, SEBI, said at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026. He also flagged opportunities in agricultural financing and cautioned against risks associated with tokenisation of loan assets. Advt Quality lending must drive credit growthRao said NBFCs have a critical role in serving smaller borrowers and businesses that may not be adequately catered to by traditional banks. Banks account for around 78% of total credit, while NBFCs contribute approximately 22%, according to figures cited in his address.However, he cautioned lenders against pursuing aggressive growth without assessing how borrowers use credit. Instead, NBFCs should help businesses expand progressively, allowing access to larger loans as their financial performance improves.Using the example of a small entrepreneur borrowing ₹1 lakh, Rao said the objective should be to help the business grow to qualify for a ₹2 lakh facility through stronger performance, rather than simply increasing borrowing to meet funding needs.He also stressed that asset quality and customer trust would be critical to sustaining growth. NBFCs must focus on preventing loans from turning into non-performing assets (NPAs), with sound underwriting and continued borrower engagement taking precedence over recovery after defaults.Technology can widen credit accessRao identified technology as a key enabler of credit expansion, particularly in customer acquisition, onboarding, recoveries and credit assessment. Better data and technology-led insights can help lenders track business performance and extend additional credit as borrowers grow.India’s credit-to-GDP ratio stands at around 68%, compared with approximately 150–200% in developed economies, he said, pointing to the headroom for credit expansion as the country works towards its Viksit Bharat 2047 ambitions. Advt He also cautioned that emerging financial innovations, including tokenisation of loan assets, must not compromise the quality of underlying credit.“The quality of the asset where you are tokenising becomes super important,” Rao said, drawing parallels with risks exposed during the 2008 global financial crisis.He called for banks, NBFCs, industry associations and other market participants to work together to identify emerging risks and share concerns before they spread across the financial system.MSMEs and agricultural businesses offer growth opportunitiesRao identified MSME financing as a key priority for NBFCs, arguing that lenders must support businesses capable of expanding operations and generating economic value. He said the focus should be on developing borrowers over time, rather than merely meeting immediate credit demand. Advt Agriculture and allied businesses also offer significant untapped potential. Rao noted that agriculture contributes around 18% of India’s economic output, while a substantial share of the workforce depends on the sector.He said NBFCs should pay greater attention to agricultural businesses over the next five to ten years, including by developing lending products suited to their financing needs and business cycles. Loans with tenures of one to two years could be an area of opportunity, he added.Rao’s message was that India’s expanding credit economy offers substantial opportunities for NBFCs, but sustained growth will depend on lending that strengthens businesses, preserves asset quality and directs capital towards productive activity.
