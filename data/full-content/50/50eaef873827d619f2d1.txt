(Delhi High Court) Summary: The Delhi High Court dismissed the Customs Department’s appeal in CUSAA 79/2019 concerning the applicability of limitation to refund claims for Special Additional Duty of Customs (SAD) under Notification No. 102/2007-CUS. The respondent-importer’s refund claim had initially been rejected by the Adjudicating Authority. However, the Commissioner of Customs (Appeals), by order dated 18 October 2017, allowed the importer’s appeal by relying on Sony India Pvt. Ltd. v. Commissioner of Customs, 2014 (304) ELT 660 (Delhi). The Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT) subsequently dismissed Revenue’s appeal on 20 February 2018, following the same precedent. The Department challenged that decision before the High Court, contending that the limitation applicable to SAD refunds remained disputed. The proceedings also involved an application seeking recall of an earlier High Court order dated 21 November 2022. That order had been passed after counsel for the Department submitted that the controversy was similar to the dispute decided in Principal Commissioner of Customs v. Kunal Lalani, CUSAA 20/2021, on 28 March 2022. Upon reconsideration, the High Court found that the issues were materially different and recalled its earlier order, restoring the appeal to its previous position. Addressing the substantive dispute, Revenue pointed out that the Supreme Court had dismissed the special leave petition against Sony India on 26 February 2016 because of delay while leaving the question of law open. Revenue also relied on the contrary view expressed by the Bombay High Court in CMS Infosystem Ltd. v. Union of India, 2017 (349) ELT 236 (Bom.). The Delhi High Court observed that the controversy regarding the applicability of the limitation period under Section 27 of the Customs Act, 1962, to SAD refund claims was covered by its decision in Sony India. It referred to subsequent decisions, including Commissioner of Customs v. S.R. Traders, CUSAA 36/2021, decided on 18 April 2022, and Commissioner of Customs v. Tanvir Trading Import, CUSAA 69/2019, which had followed that precedent. The Court reiterated that the earlier decision remained binding on coordinate benches notwithstanding the contrary position taken by another High Court. Accordingly, the Customs Department’s appeal was dismissed, and all pending applications were disposed of. The decision applied the existing Delhi High Court precedent without independently resolving the broader inter-High-Court disagreement. It is apparent from the order impugned in the present appeal that the issues involved are not similar to those involved in the case of Commissioner of Customs v. Kunal Lalani. 5. The principal issue involved in the present appeal relates to the applicability of time period to claim refund of Special Additional Duty of Customs (SAD) on goods imported in terms of the Notification No.102/2007-CUS. 8.
