Articles 3 min read Shriram Finance sees gold loan portfolio at Rs 11,000 crore by FY27 end: Umesh Revankar Shriram Finance expects its gold-loan portfolio to reach around Rs 11,000 crore by FY27-end from nearly Rs 9,000 crore currently. Executive Vice Chairman Umesh Revankar also spoke to ETBFSI about funding costs, deposits, securitisation, RBI rate transmission, asset quality and drought exposure. So, we are at Rs 9,000 crore now. We should end up with around Rs 11,000 crore by the year-end. The gold-loan portfolio is still a relatively small part of our overall book. We are monitoring the asset quality closely and will continue to watch the portfolio as it grows.Q: What is your assessment of the impact of drought on the tractor-loan portfolio?Revankar: Tractor loans are around 2% of our overall book. Our exposure to the three drought-affected states is insignificant. Advt Most of our tractor portfolio is in Uttar Pradesh, Punjab and Madhya Pradesh, where there is no significant drought issue. So, the impact of drought on our tractor portfolio should be very, very minimum. Having said that, we will be a little careful in drought-prone geographies.Q: How are you looking at the recent RBI rate hike from a funding-cost perspective?Revankar: The rate hike may have an impact, but so far we have not seen much impact on our funding cost. We did not borrow anything in the previous quarter. In the current quarter, we are mainly looking at priority-sector securitisation.Priority-sector securitisation is one of the cheapest sources of funding for us and we got it at a very good rate.Q: What are the other funding sources you are looking at?Revankar: Deposits are an important source for us. Around 31% of our liabilities come from deposits and the average tenor is around 42 months.ECB is also a reasonable source of funding. We use four-five different funding segments and we will always look at the cheaper sources of funding.Q: Are you looking at the bond market currently?Revankar: We are not raising through bonds at present because the rates in the bond market are high. Bank term loans are available depending on the appetite of the banks.Q: Do you need to raise equity capital? Advt Revankar: No, we don't need to raise equity at this point. Our CRAR is around 32%, so we have sufficient capital. Our focus is on deposits and priority-sector securitisation.Q: How much transmission are you seeing from the RBI's rate hike?Revankar: There is not much transmission at this point. Only around 15% of our portfolio is floating-rate. Most of our borrowing is fixed-rate, so the immediate impact is limited.Q: Are you looking to expand your co-lending business?Revankar: Our co-lending book is very small. We don't have any plan to enter co-lending in a significant way because we have our own capital. Advt Q: Are you seeing any early signs of stress or deterioration in delinquencies?Revankar: We don't see any specific early signs of delinquencies at this point. We will continue to watch the drought-prone geographies and monitor the portfolio closely.
