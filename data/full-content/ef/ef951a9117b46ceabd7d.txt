Articles 3 min read Two RBI MPC members oppose calibrated tightening stance, warn of hit to growth, capex While all six members of the Reserve Bank of India’s Monetary Policy Committee voted in favour of the 25 basis point repo rate hike announced on October 5, two members - Dr Nagesh Kumar and Professor Ram Singh, did not support the shift to calibrated tightening, warning that a tighter policy signal could hurt demand, interest-rate sensitive sectors, private investment and capex. The other four — Saugata Bhattacharya, Indranil Bhattacharyya, Poonam Gupta and Governor Sanjay Malhotra — backed the shift to calibrated tightening Advt Singh cautioned that a shift to a tightening stance could signal an extended cycle of rate hikes, potentially neutralising the benefits of growth-supportive monetary and fiscal policies in recent months.He said the current inflation pressures were primarily supply-side, driven by rising fuel, food and commodity prices, while classic signs of economic overheating such as a wage-price spiral, demand-driven capacity overstretch or abnormal growth in private consumption and capex were largely absent."Expectations of significant rate increases can undermine demand" in interest-rate sensitive sectors such as vehicles, real estate and discretionary spending, Singh said, adding that they could also drag on the recent momentum in private investment.He also warned that significant rate hikes, coming alongside stress on the farm sector due to below-normal rains and El Niño, rising input costs and global uncertainty, could hurt profit margins and the sustainability of capex plans of both large and small firms.Singh nevertheless voted for the 25-basis-point hike, taking the repo rate to 5.50 per cent, while opposing the change in stance.Kumar also backed the rate increase but said a neutral stance remained appropriate in view of continued geopolitical and trade policy uncertainties. He pointed to the economy's resilience, with Q1 FY27 GDP growth at 7.8 per cent and signs that growth momentum was carrying into Q2. Advt Kumar said the expansion of industrial credit, double-digit growth in the capital goods sector and higher gross fixed capital formation suggested that the private investment cycle may finally be taking off.He noted that gross fixed capital formation stood at 34.3 per cent in Q1 FY27 compared with 31.4 per cent a year earlier, while industrial credit had expanded and capital goods had recorded double-digit growth for four consecutive months.The disagreement comes even as the MPC unanimously voted to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points and explicitly changed its stance to calibrated tightening. Advt The RBI said the new stance signals that rate cuts are off the table in the near term, with policy action ahead limited to either a rate hike or a pause depending on the evolving growth-inflation outlook. The duration and extent of any hiking cycle would depend on underlying inflation, the broadening of price pressures, second-round effects of supply shocks and demand impulses.The majority's decision reflected concerns that inflation pressures were becoming broader despite the economy remaining resilient. The MPC projected CPI inflation at 5.2 per cent for 2026-27, with inflation expected to rise to 6 per cent in Q3 before moderating to 5.7 per cent in Q4. Advt Governor Sanjay Malhotra said the share of CPI commodities recording inflation above 4 per cent had risen to 37 per cent in August from 20 per cent in March, while core inflation had increased to 4.2 per cent. He said underlying inflation was also expected to move above target in the coming months.The RBI governor said the extent and duration of the rate hiking cycle would remain contingent on the actual growth-inflation developments, particularly underlying inflation, the broadening of price pressures and second-round effects of supply shocks.
