Summary : CBIC Instruction No. 02/2026-GST dated 30 October 2026 gives Central GST field formations immediate administrative directions while legislative amendments recommended by the 57th GST Council remain pending. The instruction directs that no further arrests be carried out under GST, narrows interception of goods in transit to specific-intelligence cases authorised in writing by an officer not below Joint Commissioner, limits detention or seizure under section 129 to the circumstances stated in the instruction, and directs that show-cause notices under sections 73, 74 and 74A should not be issued where the combined amount involved is below ₹10,000. These are interim field instructions, not a completed repeal of the statutory provisions. Their institutional reach, cut-off conditions and documentation requirements therefore matter. This guide explains what Central officers must do, what taxpayers should retain during transit or notice review, why State GST treatment must be checked separately, and how to distinguish an administrative restraint from a substantive amendment expected to operate from 1 April 2027. The Council indicated that the proposed legislative amendments should come into force from 1 April 2027. CBIC’s 30 October instruction implements selected reforms as interim trade-facilitation measures for its field formations until the amendments are enacted and brought into force. The instruction should therefore be read with the existing CGST Act and Rules, not as if the statutory text has already disappeared. This distinction affects legal character and scope. A statutory amendment changes the law for all persons and authorities within its operation. An administrative instruction directs the officers to whom it is addressed and may restrain the manner in which existing powers are exercised. Taxpayers should rely on the exact instruction, relevant jurisdiction and later amendments rather than generalising a press headline into a permanent immunity. It directs CGST field formations not to make further arrests under GST pending the recommended repeal of section 69. The document should not be described as a judicial declaration that every earlier arrest, investigation or prosecution is automatically invalid. Pending proceedings, bail orders, summons, evidence collection and prosecution questions require case-specific legal analysis. State GST Jurisdiction Requires Separate Verification GST is administered by Central and State authorities. A CBIC instruction governs its own formations; the instruction’s circulation to the GST Council Secretariat does not by itself prove that every State has issued an identical binding direction. A taxpayer dealing with a State officer should check the relevant State instruction or adoption. The formation named on the summons, authorisation, interception record or notice is therefore a critical first fact. Interception Must Begin with Specific Intelligence and Written Approval Under the interim direction, routine or random stopping of conveyances is replaced by a specific-intelligence model. Interception should be authorised by an officer not below the rank of Joint Commissioner, who records in writing the reasons to believe that goods are being transported in contravention of the Act or Rules. This control is designed to create accountability before a vehicle is stopped. It does not permit the transporter to obstruct an officer; the practical response is to cooperate, identify the formation, request the documents legally available, and preserve a contemporaneous record. Supplier or Recipient Jurisdiction Matters The instruction links detention or seizure under section 129 to the supplier or recipient being registered or, if unregistered, located within the State or Union territory of the intercepting officer. The taxpayer should therefore keep the supplier GSTIN, recipient GSTIN, invoice addresses, delivery address and place-of-supply analysis accessible. A mismatch between the commercial destination and GST registration data may require explanation, but it should not be converted into an unrelated confiscation proceeding without the conditions stated in the instruction. The No-Document Exception Remains Important Where neither an e-way bill has been generated nor any document showing the origin or destination of the goods is produced, the instruction permits inspection, detention or seizure regardless of the normal supplier-or-recipient jurisdiction condition. Drivers and logistics providers should receive the correct e-way bill, tax invoice or delivery challan before dispatch and a reliable method to retrieve electronic records. A system outage should be logged with time-stamped evidence rather than asserted later without support. The amount is not tested separately for each tax component. It is the sum of amounts under the CGST Act, the relevant SGST Act, the IGST Act or UTGST Act and compensation cess. A working paper should show the proposed tax or other covered amount by statute and the combined total. Splitting one issue into several small line items should not distort the threshold test. The phrase “below ₹10,000” is different from “₹10,000 or below.” A combined amount of exactly ₹10,000 is not below the stated threshold. The direction also concerns issuance of show-cause notices under the named demand provisions; it should not be extended without analysis to every communication, return discrepancy, recovery action, penalty, late fee or proceeding under another provision. Existing notices already issued and proceedings already adjudicated require separate treatment based on the instruction’s wording and applicable law. Taxpayer Workflow for a New Notice or Transit Interception Identify whether the officer belongs to a Central or State GST formation. Record the statutory section, tax period, amount and date of initiation. For transit action, preserve the written interception record, e-way bill, invoice, authorisation details and vehicle documents. For a proposed notice, aggregate CGST, SGST, IGST, UTGST and compensation cess amounts covered by the same issue. Cite the instruction factually without claiming that the underlying statute has already been repealed. Check for a later amendment, court order, CBIC clarification or State adoption before filing a representation. Maintain evidence of submission, acknowledgement and any oral hearing or field interaction. Governance Controls for Businesses and Advisers Businesses with frequent goods movement should update logistics SOPs and escalation matrices. Drivers should know whom to call, but should not be asked to argue legal points roadside. Finance and legal teams should maintain a central interception register and compare patterns by route, formation and issue. For notices, the tax-litigation register should include a threshold-computation field and identify whether the matter arises under section 73, 74 or 74A. Advisers should avoid recycling an instruction-based response where the jurisdiction or statutory section is different. The instruction is temporary by design. Compliance teams should monitor the legislative bill, enactment, commencement notification and consequential rule or form changes. When the amendments commence, the operative legal text may differ from the Council recommendation or interim administration. Files should state the law and direction applicable on the actual action date. Frequently Asked Questions Has section 69 already been repealed? No. The instruction implements an interim administrative restraint pending legislative amendment. The operative statute and later commencement must still be checked. Can a Central GST officer make a further arrest after the instruction? The instruction directs CGST field formations that no further arrests may be carried out under GST during the interim period. Does the instruction automatically bind every State GST officer? Not merely because CBIC issued it. State adoption or an equivalent instruction should be verified for action by a State formation. Can officers still intercept a goods vehicle? The interim model requires specific intelligence and written authorisation by an officer not below Joint Commissioner, subject to the instruction’s conditions. What if there is no e-way bill and no document showing origin or destination? The instruction preserves a specific exception allowing inspection, detention or seizure irrespective of the normal supplier-or-recipient territorial condition. How is the ₹10,000 notice threshold calculated? The covered amounts under CGST, SGST, IGST or UTGST and compensation cess are aggregated. The test is not applied separately to each component. Does exactly ₹10,000 fall below the threshold? No. The wording refers to an amount below ₹10,000, so an exact ₹10,000 amount is not below it. When will the permanent reforms apply? The Council recommended 1 April 2027, but the enacted amendment and commencement instrument must be checked before treating that date as final law. Key Takeaways Instruction 02/2026-GST is an interim administrative measure, not a completed statutory repeal. The no-arrest direction applies to CGST field formations; State jurisdiction must be checked separately. Transit interception requires specific intelligence, written senior authorisation and the stated detention conditions. The ₹10,000 threshold aggregates covered GST and cess amounts and applies to the named demand provisions.
