(Delhi High Court) Summary: The Delhi High Court dismissed nine departmental appeals concerning Riya Travel and Tours India Pvt. Ltd. and Akbar Travels of India Pvt. Ltd. as not maintainable because the disputed demands were below the ₹2 crore monetary limit and did not fall within the prescribed exceptions. The appeals challenged the CESTAT order dated 25.09.2024. Taking the facts from SERTA 21/2025, the Court recorded that a show cause notice dated 19.10.2015 sought service tax on air travel agent services; the demand was confirmed on 30.05.2018 but set aside by the Tribunal. The proposed questions concerned recovery of retained service tax under Section 73A(3) of the Finance Act, 1994, commission received from airlines and shared with sub-agents, the identity of the service provider and recipient, and whether the commission included service tax. The respondents raised a preliminary objection under the instructions dated 06.08.2024. The Revenue admitted that the demands were below ₹2 crore but argued that the issues had cascading effects, relying on Commissioner of Income Tax v. Surya Herbal Limited and decisions of the Gauhati and Patna High Courts. Examining the successive Board instructions, the Court held that the instructions dated 17.08.2011 provided two exceptions: a challenge to the constitutional validity of an Act or Rule, and a notification, instruction, order or circular being held illegal or ultra vires. The clarification dated 26.12.2014 expressly applied monetary limits to recurring cases, including subsequent cases involving the same or other parties. Although the instructions dated 17.12.2015 added an exception for classification and refund issues of a legal or recurring nature, that exception was withdrawn on 03.04.2018. The instructions dated 06.08.2024 enhanced the limits while retaining the original two exceptions. The Court distinguished Surya Herbal because it concerned a CBDT circular under the Income-tax Act and merely granted liberty to approach the High Court concerning cascading effects. It disagreed with the Gauhati High Court’s view in Pan Parag India Limited, explaining that the reference to paragraph “1.3” in the instructions dated 22.08.2019 was actually to paragraph 3 of the 2011 instructions, which contained no cascading-effect or bunch-matter exception. Regarding the Patna High Court’s Indian Oil Corporation Limited decision, it noted that the Department had failed to disclose the withdrawal of the recurring-issue exception. Recording the applicable limits as ₹60 lakh before CESTAT, ₹2 crore before High Courts and ₹5 crore before the Supreme Court, the Court dismissed the appeals, kept all proposed substantial questions of law open and disposed of pending applications. It also suggested that the Department take up uniform implementation with the Board/Secretary, observing that Commissionerates continued filing appeals despite binding litigation-reduction instructions. Cases Discussed Commissioner of Central Excise and Service Tax v. Indian Oil Corporation Limited — Miscellaneous Appeal No. 376 of 2019, decided on 14.05.2026 (Patna High Court). The Revenue relied on the decision concerning recurring issues. The Delhi High Court noted that the Department had not brought the withdrawal of the relevant exception on 03.04.2018 to the Patna High Court’s notice. Central Goods and Service Tax v. M/s Pan Parag India Limited — 2021 SCC OnLine Gau 2737 (Gauhati High Court). Cited by the Revenue to support a cascading-effect exception. The Delhi High Court expressly disagreed, finding no such exception in the governing instructions. Commissioner of Income Tax v. Surya Herbal Limited — (2011) 15 SCC 482 (Supreme Court). The Court distinguished the order as relating to a CBDT circular and granting liberty to approach the High Court, without deciding the applicability of the circular. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF DELHI HIGH COURT 1. These appeals are filed under Section 35G of the Central Excise Act, 1944 read with Section 83 of the Finance Act,1994 against the order dated 25.09.2024 of the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal, New Delhi (hereinafter ‘the tribunal’). 2. For convenience, the facts are being taken from SERTA 21/2025. 3. On 19.10.2015, a show cause notice (for short ‘SCN’) was issued to the respondent for recovery of service tax on the services rendered as an air travel agent. The order-in-original (OIO) dated 30.05.2018 was passed confirming the demand raised in SCN. The respondent was successful before the tribunal and vide order dated 25.09.2024, the impugned demand was set aside. Hence, the present appeal was filed and the substantial questions of law proposed are reproduced below:- “a. Whether the amount of service tax retained by the ‘agent’ i.e. the Respondent, is recoverable under Section 73A(3) of Finance Act, 1994? b. When the commission received from the airlines by the ‘agents’ and some part of it is paused on to ‘sub-agents’ whether in this case the service is provided by sub-agents to agents in the head ‘air travel agent services’? c. Whether the Ld. CESTAT is correct in holding that the Respondent has not provided any services to the Airlines? d. Whether the Ld. CESTAT is correct in holding that commission received by the Respondent from the airlines was not inclusive of service tax? e. Whether the Ld. CESTAT is correct in holding that the Respondent had provided services to the sub-agents and charging of Service Tax from the sub-agents was justified when the Respondent had paid/shared the commission received from the Airlines to the sub-agents? f. Whether the Respondent rendered air travel agent services to the airlines as contended by the department or the Respondent rendered this service to the sub-agents or customers as contended by the Respondent? g. Whether it is the Respondent that is providing service to the sub-agents are providing service to the Respondent? h. Whether the Respondent rendered any service to the airlines or services were rendered by the Respondent only to the subagents?” 4. Learned counsel for the respondent raises a preliminary objection that the department could not have preferred the appeals in view of the instructions dated 06.08.2024 as the amount involved is less than two crores. 5. Learned counsel for the appellant contends that the demand in dispute is less than two crores. The submission is that there are cascading effects of the issue involved in the appeals and hence the circular should not apply. Reliance is on the decision of the Supreme Court in the case of Commissioner of Income Tax v. Surya Herbal Limited (2011) 15 SCC 482 to contend that in case of a cascading effect, the circular ipso facto shall not apply. Reliance is upon the decision of the Gauhati High Court in the case of Central Goods and Service Tax v. M/s Pan Parag India Limited 2021 SCC OnLine Gau 2737 and the decision of Patna High Court in the case of Commissioner of Central Excise and Service Tax v. Indian Oil Corporation Limited Miscellaneous Appeal No. 376 of 2019 decided on 14.05.2026 to contend that the decision of the Supreme Court in Commissioner of Income Tax v. Surya Herbal Limited (supra) was followed by the High Courts in cases of Central Excise as well as under the Goods and Service Tax. 6. Before proceeding further, it would be relevant to reproduce the instructions issued by the Central Board of Excise and Customs (CBEC) dated 17.08.2011, 26.12.2014, 17.12.2015, 03.04.2018, 22.08.2019 & 06.08.2024. In exercise of the powers conferred by Section 35R of the Central Excise Act,1944 made applicable to Service Tax vide Section 83 of the Finance Act,1994 and Section 131BA of the Customs Act, 1962 the Central Board of Excise & Customs (hereinafter referred to as the Board) fixes the following monetary limits below which appeal shall not be filed in the Tribunal, High Court and the Supreme Court: Sl.No. Appellate Forum Monetary limit 1. CESTAT Rs.5,00,000/- 2. HIGH COURTS Rs.10,00,000/- 3. SUPREME COURT Rs.25,00,000/- 2. For ascertaining whether a matter would be covered within or without the aforementioned limits, the determinative element would be duty/tax under dispute. To illustrate it further in a case involving duty of ₹ 5 lakhs or below with equal penalty and interest, as the case may be, no appeal shall be filed in the Tribunal. Similarly, no appeal shall be filed in the High Courts if the duty involved does not exceed ₹ 10 lakhs with or without penalty and interest. Further, the Commissionerates shall not send proposal to the Board for filing Civil Appeal or Special Leave Petition in the Supreme Court in a case involving duty up to ₹ 25 lakhs, whether with penalty and interest or otherwise. However, where the imposition of penalty is the subject matter of dispute and the said penalty exceeds the limit prescribed, then the matter could be litigated further. Similarly, where the subject matter of dispute is the demand of interest and the amount of interest exceeds the prescribed limit, then the matter may require further litigation. 3. Adverse judgments relating to the following should be contested irrespective of the amount involved: a) Where the constitutional validity of the provisions of an Act or Rule is under challenge. b) Where Notification/ Instruction/ Order or Circular has been held illegal or ultra vires 4. Several queries connected with application of monetary limits have been raised by the field formations which were considered by the Board and are being clarified as below:- Issues Clarifications a) Whether duty involved mentioned in the Instruction dated 20.10.2010 refers to duty outstanding to be collected or the total duty demanded for deciding the threshold limit prescribed therein. In a case where a part of the duty demanded is not disputed and is paid and the outstanding duty under dispute is less than the monetary limit prescribed by the Board, no appeal shall be filed. In other words, monetary limit shall apply on the disputed duty and not on the total duty demanded in a case. b) Whether monetary limits would apply to cases of refund. It is clarified that the monetary limits being prescribed by the Board would apply to cases of refund as well. c) Whether applications being filed by the Department before office of Joint Secretary (Revision Application) would also be covered under the stipulation of monetary limits. The limit specified herein will not be applicable to application filed before the Joint Secretary (Revision Application). d) Whether exclusion of audit objections mentioned in para 6(c) of Instruction dated 20.10.2010 would cover internal audit objection cases also or whether they would be limited to cases of revenue audit alone. The intention was to apply the exclusion clause mentioned at para 6(c) only to disputes arising out of revenue audit objections accepted by the Department. It has now been decided to delete the said exclusion clause (refer para 3 of this Instruction). Therefore, in all cases of audit objections accepted by the Department, while protective demands may continue to be issued but the same would be subjected to the monetary limits for filing appeal in the Tribunal, High Courts and the Supreme Court. 5. The revised monetary limits shall come into force from 1.9.2011. 6. This Instruction is in continuation of earlier Instruction of even number dated 20.10.2010 and seeks to revise the monetary limits, exclusion clauses and clarifies the doubts raised by the field formations on this issue. As stated in the Instruction dated 17.8.2011, the present monetary limits are Rs 5 lakhs/Rs 10 lakhs/ Rs 25 lakhs respectively for appeal to be filed in the Tribunal/High Courts and the Supreme Court. Appeal is not required to be filed in cases below these monetary limits unless the dispute falls in the two exclusion category mentioned in para 3 of Instruction dated 17.8.2011. 2. The Board has been receiving letters from the field seeking clarifications on various aspects relating to implementation of the Instructions governing monetary limits for filing appeal in the Tribunal and Courts. Mostly, the clarifications sought is whether cases of recurring nature, whether involving the same party or even other parties, need to be pursued in litigation irrespective of the amount involved in such cases. The second issue relates to applicability of the threshold limits in various situations, mostly where the adjudicating/appellate authority disposes of more than one appeal in a common order which is sought to be challenged. Such order, generally involve cases of more than one parties, some of which fall below the monetary limit fixed for filing appeal in the forum of appeal. 3. It is hereby clarified that the existing Instruction regarding applicability of monetary limits to cases of recurring nature would continue. Therefore, all cases, including cases of recurring nature, are covered under the Instruction on monetary limits and appeal is not to be filed in such cases except those falling in the two exclusion clauses mentioned above. Even if an appeal is pending in the higher appellate forum, subsequent case of the same party or other party shall not be pursued further in litigation if the case falls below the monetary limit prescribed by the Board. 4. The Instructions mentioned above used the word “case”. However, the same was not defined. The term “case” needs to be interpreted in the context of National Litigation Policy which aims at reduction of litigation. In respect of a composite order which disposes of more than one appeal/SCN and the Department contemplates filing of appeal, every appeal would be a “case” and should be subjected to the threshold limit prescribed. To illustrate, if the Tribunal passes one composite order disposing of more than one appeal filed before it, and if the Department being aggrieved is required to file more than one appeal against the said Tribunal order, then each appeal shall be subject to the monetary limit prescribed. 5. There is no change in the monetary limits prescribed by the Board. 6. The above clarification may be taken note of while processing appeals before the Tribunal and Courts. The first where the validity of the provisions of an Act or Rule is under challenge and second where notification/instruction/order or circular has been held illegal or ultra vires. 8. On 26.12.2014, CBEC issued a clarification stating that the monetary limits for filing appeals would apply to cases of a recurring nature and appeals in such cases would not be filed, except in two cases specified in the circular dated 17.08.2011. It was further clarified that despite pending appeals in the higher appellate forums subsequent case of the same party or other party shall not be pursued if it falls below the prescribed monetary limit. 9. The instructions dated 17.12.2015 partially modified the instructions dated 17.08.2011 by adding one more exception in paragraph 3 relating to classification and refunds issues of a legal and/or recurring nature. 10. By the instructions dated 03.04.2018, sub-clause ‘c’ of paragraph 3 of the instructions dated 17.08.2011 which operated as an exception to the general monetary limits was withdrawn. 11. By the instructions dated 06.08.2024, the monetary limits were enhanced. The two exceptions provided in the instructions dated 17.08.2011 were retained and it was clarified that except for the modification to the monetary limits, all other terms and conditions of the instructions dated 17.08.2011 stand. 12. From the instructions quoted above, it is clear that the vires or validity of the provisions of the Act or Rule or notification/instruction/order or circular are the only exception for filing the appeal below the prescribed monetary limit. It is an admitted fact that the dispute in these appeals involves neither the validity of any provision nor of circular/notification/instruction. 13. The reliance placed by learned counsel for the appellant on the decision of the Supreme Court in Commissioner of Income Tax v. Surya Herbal Limited (supra) is of no avail. The case was with regard to circular issued by the CBDT for filing appeals under the Income Tax Act, 1961. The issue of applicability of circular was not decided, only liberty was granted to the Income Tax Department to approach the High Court to point out that there is a cascading effect and that the circular ipso facto would not apply. 14. In the case of Central Goods and Service Tax v. M/s Pan Parag India Limited (supra), the department relied upon the decision of the Supreme Court in Commissioner of Income Tax v. Surya Herbal Limited (supra) to contend that the instructions would not apply where there is a cascading effect and a common principle may be involved in a subsequent group of matters or a large number of matters. Reliance was placed by the department on instructions dated 22.08.2019, it is by this instruction the monetary limits were increased. Clause 4 of the instructions dated 22.08.2019 stated that the substantial questions of law as described in paragraph 1.3 of instructions dated 17.08.2011 would be contested irrespective of the monetary limit. On perusal of the instructions dated 17.08.2011, it is evident that there is no paragraph 1.3. The reference in clause 4 of the instructions dated 22.08.2019 is in fact to paragraph 3 of the instructions dated 17.08.2011 which provides only two exceptions as discussed above and no exception for cascading effects or bunch matters is there. 15. With due respect we are not in concurrence with the view taken by the Gauhati High Court. 16. The department made another attempt to rigour out of the instructions issued before the Patna High Court in Commissioner of Central Excise and Service Tax v. Indian Oil Corporation Limited (supra) contending that the issue of a recurring nature had to be entertained irrespective of the amount involved as mentioned in the instructions. The same argument is made before this Court. 17. The department failed to bring to the notice of the Patna High Court that the instructions dated 17.12.2015 were withdrawn by the instructions dated 03.04.2018, with effect from 03.04.2018. 18. The monetary limits for filing an appeal prevailing as on date are Rs. 60 lakhs before the CESTAT, Rs.2 crores before the High Court and Rs.5 crores before the Supreme Court. The two exceptions being: (i) Challenge to the constitutional validity of the provisions of an Act or Rule. (ii) Challenge to the notification/instructions/order or circular held to be illegal or ultra vires. 19. Before concluding we would be failing if we do not appreciate the efforts put by learned counsel for the appellant to assist the court on instructions and circular. 20. The department, if it deems appropriate, should take up this matter with the Board/Secretary to ensure uniform implementation of the instructions and circular. It is noticed regularly, that Commissionerate’s are filing appeals despite the binding instructions issued with an object to reduce litigation. 21. The present case does not fall in any of the exceptions. The appeals are dismissed as not maintainable. The substantial questions of law proposed are kept open. All pending applications are also disposed of.
