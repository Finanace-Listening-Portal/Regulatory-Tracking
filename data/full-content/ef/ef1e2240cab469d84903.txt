Exclusive Articles 3 min read Exclusive: Insurers seek more time to absorb EoM cuts, due process for mis-selling flags as IRDAI weighs 650-plus comments Industry feedback is understood to have sought a more gradual transition to tighter expense limits, citing rising compliance and technology costs, while also pressing for an adjudication process before alleged mis-selling is recorded against distributors. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Oct 29, 2026 at 07:00 AM IST var news_msid = 135279459; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook With more than 650 comments received on its proposed insurance distribution overhaul, the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) is weighing competing industry concerns over commission caps, expense-of-management (EoM) limits and accountability for mis-selling. The regulator has extended the deadline for feedback to October 31 from October 25, after releasing its two-part consultation paper, Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution, on September 23.A senior life insurance executive familiar with the industry’s feedback said the broad direction of the reforms was not the primary point of contention among insurers, but the speed at which the proposed changes would be implemented. The industry has sought a more workable transition to tighter EoM limits, arguing that a significant portion of insurers could find it difficult to meet the proposed levels immediately, even with further cost-cutting. Advt The executive said insurers were also dealing with a series of one-time and recurring investments linked to regulatory and technology requirements, including Ind AS, data protection, cybersecurity and the proposed risk-based capital framework. These costs could weigh on expense ratios in the near term, making the pace of convergence a key issue in the industry’s feedback to the regulator.EoM transition and compliance costs at the centre of industry feedbackIRDAI’s consultation paper proposes a phased reduction in EoM limits, with the limits for life insurers moving towards 12.5% and those for general insurers towards 20% over five years. It also proposes product- and channel-specific commission caps as part of a broader overhaul of distribution economics.The industry’s concern, according to the executive, is not necessarily about the end objective of improving cost efficiency, but whether insurers can reach the proposed limits at the prescribed pace. Companies are incurring costs to comply with multiple regulatory and technology changes, some of which are one-time expenses that may ease over the next two to three years.The executive said the industry had been discussing a more gradual transition with the regulator, with the aim of allowing insurers to adjust their operating models without compromising business continuity. The final impact will also depend on the changes IRDAI makes after reviewing stakeholder feedback. Advt The proposals have different implications for insurers depending on their distribution mix, operating structures and product portfolios. Insurers relying heavily on intermediaries may face a different cost equation from those with larger proprietary or direct-sales channels, while the ability to reduce expenses may vary across companies.Insurers seek adjudication before alleged mis-selling is recordedIndustry feedback has also raised concerns over the proposed treatment of mis-selling complaints and the possibility of putting an agent’s record in the public domain without a sufficiently clear adjudication process, the executive said. Advt The concern is that a complaint or allegation should not automatically be treated as a proven instance of mis-selling. Insurance companies can receive complaints arising from misunderstandings, disputes over product features or policyholder dissatisfaction, and not all such cases necessarily establish deliberate misconduct by the distributor.The executive said the industry had sought a more deliberative mechanism to determine whether a case constituted mis-selling, with safeguards to distinguish proven and repeated violations from isolated complaints or cases where the facts remained unclear. The principle of natural justice, including giving the person concerned an opportunity to respond, was a key consideration in the feedback, the executive said. Advt The industry is not seeking to dilute accountability for deliberate or repeated misconduct, the executive said, but wants a process that establishes the facts before a distributor is publicly flagged. The design of the proposed mechanism and the threshold for recording a violation are therefore likely to remain important points as IRDAI examines the responses.IRDAI has said the feedback received will inform further examination and refinement of the proposed reforms. Chairman Ajay Seth has indicated that the regulator is engaging with stakeholders and will consider the business and economic rationale behind their suggestions, including the impact on different entities and the transition to the proposed framework.
