DPSS/2026-27/477, CO.DPSS.POLC.No.S910/02-14-001/2026-2027 dated 27 November 2026, has issued the Reserve Bank of India (Digital Payments – Card-based transaction) Directions, 2026, with immediate effect. Issued under Section 18 read with Section 10(2) of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, the Directions consolidate 25 earlier circulars and apply to payment system providers and participants, including banks and non-banks. They prescribe EMV chip card standards, credit card network selection, customer-controlled transaction limits and security requirements. The relaxation from Additional Factor of Authentication (AFA) for eligible contactless card-present transactions remains available up to ₹5,000 per transaction, excluding ATM and card-not-present transactions. The Directions regulate device tokenisation and Card-on-File Tokenisation (CoFT), requiring explicit customer consent, authentication, secure data storage, periodic system audits and monthly reporting. Actual card data storage for card-not-present transactions is restricted to card issuers and card networks, subject to the prescribed limited-data exception for reconciliation and tracking. Debit card Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) ceilings remain differentiated according to merchant turnover and payment acceptance infrastructure. The Directions also provide for customer awareness, free tokenisation services, dispute resolution and reporting of lost or stolen cards. All 25 specified earlier circulars stand repealed, while existing authorisations, approvals, actions and acknowledgements remain valid under the savings provisions. Yours faithfully, (Sara Rajendra Kumar Neti) Chief General Manager-in-Charge Encl.: As above In exercise of the powers conferred by Sections 18 read with Section 10(2) of the Payment and Settlement Systems (PSS) Act, 2007, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), being satisfied that it is necessary and expedient in the public interest so to do, hereby consolidates all circulars (issued till date) pertaining to card payments and issues the Directions hereinafter specified. Chapter I – Preliminary A. Short Title and Commencement (a) These Directions shall be called the “Reserve Bank of India (Digital Payments – Card-based transaction) Directions, 2026”. (b) These Directions shall be effective immediately from the date of issuance. B. Applicability (a) The provisions of these Directions shall be applicable to payment system providers and payment system participants (including banks and non-banks) under the PSS Act. C. Definitions (a) The terms ‘credit card’, ‘corporate credit card’, ‘commercial credit card’, ‘debit card’ shall have the same meaning as defined in Reserve Bank of India (Credit Cards and Debit Cards: Issuance and Conduct) Directions, 2025 as applicable to the respective regulated entity and as amended from time to time. (b) The term ‘Prepaid Payment Instrument (PPI) card’ shall have the same meaning as defined in Master Directions on Prepaid Payment Instruments (PPIs), as amended from time to time. (c) Card-on-File Token: A token unique for the combination of card, token requestor and merchant. (d) De-Tokenisation: Conversion of the token back to actual card details. (e) Device Token: A token unique for the combination of card, token requestor and device (hereafter referred to as “identified device”). (f) Guest Checkout Transaction: A card-not-present transaction wherein cardholders decide to enter the card details manually every time while undertaking the transaction. (g) Token: A unique code that replaces actual card details for transaction processing. It may refer to a Card-on-File Token or Device Token. (h) Token Service Provider (TSP): The entity which tokenises the actual card credential and de-tokenises it, whenever required. TSP may be Card Issuers, Card Networks or both. (i) Tokenisation: The process of replacing actual card details with a unique alternate code called the “token”. (j) The terms ‘Additional Factor of Authentication (AFA)’, ‘authentication’, ‘Card Present transaction’, ‘Card Not Present (CNP) transaction’, ‘cross-border CNP transaction’, ’factor of authentication’, ‘issuer’, ‘merchant’ shall have the same meaning as defined in Reserve Bank of India (Authentication mechanisms for digital payment transactions) Directions, 2025 dated September 25, 2025 and Master Direction on Regulation of Payment Aggregator (PA) dated September 15, 2025, both as amended from time to time. (k) All other expressions unless defined herein shall have the same meaning as have been assigned to them under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, or any statutory modification or re-enactment thereto or as used in commercial parlance, as the case may be. Chapter II – Standards and arrangements D. Card related standards (a) All credit, debit, and PPI cards issued by banks and non-banks shall necessarily be chip cards adhering to EMV payment standard, to be acceptable across the card acceptance infrastructure [Automated Teller Machine (ATM) / Point of Sale (PoS)] which shall be EMV compliant as well. (b) All card present acceptance (ATMs / PoS) infrastructure shall also be enabled for PIN and biometric validation as Additional Factor of Authentication (AFA). (c) Ab-initio processing of EMV Chip and PIN cards based on magnetic stripe shall not be permitted. Fall back to magnetic stripe, if any, shall be indicated to the card issuer as part of the transaction message. Liability for any loss to customers due to non-adherence of the above, shall be responsibility of the acquirers. (d) The terminals shall adhere to requirements as specified under Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank Payment System Operators dated November 05, 2026, as amended from time to time. (e) For all cards issued in India and used for making payments towards purchase of goods and services provided within the country, the acquisition shall be through a bank in India, and the transaction should necessarily settle only in Indian currency. E. Arrangement with Card Networks for issue of Credit Cards (a) Credit card issuers shall not enter into any arrangement or agreement with card networks1 that restrain them from availing the services of other card networks. (b) Card issuers shall provide an option to their eligible customers to choose from multiple card networks at the time of issue. For existing cardholders, this option may be provided at the time of the next renewal. (c) Card issuers and card networks shall ensure adherence to the above requirements in existing agreements at the time of amendment or renewal thereof, and in fresh agreements executed. (d) The provisions in paragraph-E(b) shall not be applicable to credit card issuers with number of active cards issued by them being 10 lakh or less in number. The provisions in paragraph-E shall not be applicable to the card issuers who issue credit cards on their own authorised card network. Chapter III – Security of card transactions F. Relaxation in requirement of Additional Factor of Authentication (a) Card transactions (including tokenised card transactions) shall adhere to provisions in Reserve Bank of India (Authentication mechanisms for digital payment transactions) Directions, 2025 dated September 25, 2025, as amended from time to time. (b) Exemption from AFA for commercial / corporate cards which are used for all travel bookings including Global Distribution System of International Air Transport Association (IATA) is permitted on the condition that card issuers issuing such cards should take an undertaking from such entities that “We accept that the AFA is mandated by RBI vide Reserve Bank of India (Authentication mechanisms for digital payment transactions) Directions, 2025 dated September 25, 2025 and use of such cards for travel CNP transactions without such AFA carries security risk and such cards are vulnerable to frauds”. (c) In case of Mail Order Telephone Order (MOTO) transactions, AFA mandate has to be complied with. Any MOTO transaction effected without AFA should carry additional information in the transaction alerts sent to cardholders that this transaction is effected by the card issuer without following the AFA mandated by RBI. (d) In case of small value card present transactions: i. The AFA requirement is relaxed for small value card present transactions using contactless technology subject to adherence to EMV standards for transactions up to ₹ 5,000/- per transaction. ii. The limit of ₹ 5,000/- per transaction will be the limit set across all categories of merchants in the country where such contactless payments will be accepted. iii. Beyond this transaction limit, the card has to be processed as a contact payment and AFA will be mandatory. iv. Even for transaction values below this limit, the customer may choose to make payment as a contact payment, which has to be facilitated by both issuing and acquiring banks. In other words, customers cannot be compelled to do a contactless payment. v. Issuer banks are free to facilitate customers to set lower per-transaction limits. The responsibility for authorising the contactless payment based on such card-based limits will lie with issuer banks. vi. Suitable velocity checks (i.e., how many such small value transactions will be allowed in a day / week / month) may be put in place by banks as considered appropriate. vii. The contactless cards should necessarily be chip cards adhering to EMV payment standard, so as to be acceptable across the existing card acceptance infrastructure which are EMV compliant based on the earlier mandate in this regard. viii. The above relaxations do not apply to ATM transactions and CNP transactions irrespective of the transaction value. G. Card controls by customers (a) At the time of issue / re-issue, all cards (physical and virtual) shall be enabled for use only at contact-based points of usage [viz. ATMs and PoS devices] within India. Issuers shall provide cardholders a facility for enabling card not present (domestic and international) transactions, card present (international) transactions and contactless transactions, as per the process outlined below. i. facility to switch on / off and set / modify transaction limits (within the overall card limit, if any, set by the issuer) for all types of transactions – domestic and international, at PoS / ATMs / online transactions / contactless transactions, etc.; ii. the above facility on a 24×7 basis through one or more of the channels -mobile application / internet banking / ATMs / Interactive Voice Response (IVR). Additionally, it may be offered at branches / offices. iii. alerts / information / status, etc., through SMS / e-mail, as and when there is any change in status of the card. (b) The provisions in paragraph G (a) are not mandatory for prepaid gift cards and those used at mass transit systems. (c) Issuers and card networks may give wide publicity to the provisions in paragraph G. Chapter IV – Tokenisation H. Card Tokenisation Service (a) Card Issuers, Card Networks or both may act as Token Service Provider (TSP) for the provision of card tokenisation services only for cards issued by/ affiliated to them. The ability to tokenise and de-tokenise card data shall be with the same TSP. (b) Tokenisation and de-tokenisation requests should be logged by the TSP and available for retrieval, if required. (c) Actual card data, token and other relevant details shall be stored in a secure mode. Token requestors shall not store PAN or any other card detail. (d) Secure storage of tokens and associated keys by token requestor on successful registration of card shall be ensured. (e) Recovery of original Primary Account Number (PAN) shall be feasible for the TSPs only. Adequate safeguards shall be put in place to ensure that PAN cannot be found out from the token and vice versa, by anyone except the TSP. Integrity of token generation process shall be ensured at all times. (f) Registration of card on token requestor’s app shall be done only with explicit consent of the customer. Such consent shall be authenticated through an Additional Factor of Authentication (AFA) by the card issuer and shall not be obtained through pre-selected, default /automatic / forced options, including checkboxes, radio buttons or similar mechanisms. (g) The permission for card tokenisation services shall extend to all payment use cases / channels (e.g., Near Field Communication (NFC) / Magnetic Secure Transmission (MST) based contactless transactions, in-app payments, QR code-based payments, etc.) or token storage mechanisms (cloud, secure element, trusted execution environment, etc.). (h) Cardholders shall be provided the option to register or de-register their cards for specific use cases, i.e., contactless, QR code based, in-app payments, etc. (i) Customer shall be given option to set and modify per transaction and daily transaction limits for tokenised card transactions. I. Tokenisation Certification requirements (a) TSP shall get the Token Requestors (TR), card issuers, acquirers, their service providers and any other entity involved in payment transaction chain certified in respect of changes done for processing of tokenised card transactions. All certification / security testing by the TSP shall conform to international best practices / globally accepted standards. (b) TSPs shall put in place a mechanism for periodic system (including security) audit at frequent intervals (at least annually), of all entities involved in providing card tokenisation services. This system audit shall be undertaken by empanelled auditors of Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) and all related instructions of Reserve Bank in respect of system audits shall also be adhered to. A copy of this audit report shall be furnished to the Reserve Bank, with comments of auditors on deviations, if any, from the conditions listed in this Chapter, along with the compliance thereto. Data on tokenised transactions shall be submitted at monthly intervals as provided in Annexure. (c) The TSP shall get the token requestor certified for (a) token requestor’s systems, including hardware deployed for this purpose, (b) security of token requestor’s application, (c) features for ensuring authorised access to token requestor’s app on the identified device, and, (d) other functions performed by the token requestor, including customer on-boarding, token provisioning and storage, data storage, transaction processing, etc. J. Device Tokenisation (a) For device tokenisation, the token shall be unique to a combination of the card, token requestor, and device. The cardholder shall have the flexibility to use any cards registered with Token Requestor for undertaking transactions. (b) The facility shall be offered through consumer devices – mobile phones, tablets, laptops, desktops, wearables (wrist watches, bands, etc.), Internet of Things (IoT) devices, etc. (referred to as “identified device”). (c) The TSP shall put in place a mechanism to ensure that the transaction request originate only from the “identified” device. (d) Card issuers shall ensure easy access to customers for reporting loss of “identified device” or any other such event which may expose tokens to unauthorised usage. Card network, along with card issuers and token requestors, shall put in place a system to immediately de-activate such tokens and associated keys. (e) For performing any transaction, the customer shall be free to use any of the cards registered with the token requestor app. K. Card-on-File Tokenisation through merchant interface (a) The TSP shall put in place a mechanism to ensure that the transaction request has originated from the merchant and the token requestor with whom the token is associated. (b) If purchase transaction at a merchant is being performed along with the token provisioning request, then AFA validation may be combined. (c) The merchant shall give an option to the cardholder to de-register the token. Further, a token requestor having direct relationship with the cardholder shall list the merchants in respect of whom the CoFT has been opted through it by the cardholder; and provide an option to de-register any such token. (d) A facility shall also be given by the card issuer to the cardholder to view the list of merchants in respect of whom the CoFT has been opted by her / him, and to de-register any such token. This facility shall be provided through one or more of the following channels – mobile application, internet banking, Interactive Voice Response (IVR) or at branches / offices. (e) Whenever a card is renewed or replaced, the card issuer shall seek explicit consent of the cardholder for linking it with the merchants with whom (s)he had earlier registered the card. L. Card on File Tokenisation (CoFT) through card issuers (a) CoFT can be enabled directly through card issuers also to provide cardholders with an additional choice to tokenise their cards for multiple merchant sites through a single process. (b) Generation of CoF Tokens for a card, through the card issuer, can be enabled through mobile banking and internet banking channels. (c) CoFT generation shall be done only on explicit customer consent, and with AFA validation. If the cardholder selects multiple merchants for which to tokenise his/her card, AFA validation may be combined for all these merchants. (d) The tokens thus generated shall be made available on the merchant’s payment page, in the cardholder’s account with the merchant. (e) The cardholder may tokenise the card at any time of his convenience, either on receipt of the new card or later. (f) The card issuer shall provide a complete list of merchants for whom it can provide tokenisation services. The cardholders shall select the merchants with whom he/she wishes to maintain tokens. M. Other provisions on tokenisation (a) Based on risk perception, etc., card issuers may decide whether to allow cards issued by them to be registered by a token requestor. (b) Complete and ongoing compliance with the directions shall be the responsibility of the respective TSPs. (c) TSP shall ensure monitoring to detect any malfunction, anomaly, suspicious behaviour or the presence of unauthorized activity within the tokenisation process, and implement a process to alert all stakeholders. (d) Suitable velocity checks (i.e., how many such small value transactions will be allowed in a day / week / month) may be put in place by banks as considered appropriate. Chapter V – Other Provisions N. Restriction on storage of actual card data (a) No entity in the card transaction / payment chain, other than the card issuers and / or card networks, shall store the actual card data for card not present transactions. Any such data stored previously shall be purged. (b) For transaction tracking and / or reconciliation purposes, entities can store limited data – last four digits of actual card number and card issuer’s name – in compliance with the applicable standards. (c) Complete and ongoing compliance with the above by all entities involved shall be the responsibility of the card networks. (d) System participants may devise alternate mechanism(s) to card-on-file (CoF) tokenisation to handle any use-case (including guest checkout transactions, recurring e-mandates, EMI option, etc.) or post-transaction activity (including chargeback handling, dispute resolution, reward / loyalty programme, etc.) that currently involves / requires storage of CoF data by entities other than card issuers and card networks. O. Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) for Debit Card Transactions MDR for debit cards has been rationalised based on Categorisation of merchants on the basis of turnover, adoption of a differentiated MDR for QR-code based transactions and specifying a ceiling on the maximum permissible MDR for both ‘card present’ and ‘card not present’ transactions. The maximum MDR for debit card transactions shall be as under: Sr. No Merchant Category Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) for debit card transactions (as a % of transaction value) Physical POS infrastructure including online card transactions QR code-based card acceptance infrastructure 1. Small merchants (with turnover upto ₹ 20 lakh during the previous financial year) Not exceeding 0.40% (MDR cap of ₹ 200 per transaction) Not exceeding 0.30% (MDR cap of ₹ 200 per transaction) 2. Other merchants (with turnover above ₹ 20 lakh during the previous financial year) Not exceeding 0.90% (MDR cap of ₹ 1000 per transaction) Not exceeding 0.80% (MDR cap of ₹ 1000 per transaction) (a) Acquirers shall ensure that MDR levied on the merchant shall not exceed the cap rates as prescribed above. Further, banks shall ensure that the MDR levied on the merchant shall not exceed the cap rates as prescribed above, irrespective of the entity which is deploying the card acceptance infrastructure at the merchant location. (b) System participants are advised to ensure that merchants on-boarded by them do not pass on MDR charges to customers while accepting payments through debit cards. (c) System participants shall ensure that MDR is clearly unbundled for different categories of cards. (d) System participants shall enter into separate agreements or annexes within the same agreement for debit cards, credit cards and prepaid cards so as to bring in more clarity and transparency; and (e) System participants shall educate the merchants regarding the charges associated with different categories of cards at the time of acquisition. P. Customer Awareness and Protection (a) Dispute resolution process shall be put in place by TSP for tokenised card transactions. (b) No charges shall be recovered from the cardholder for availing tokenisation service. (c) In respect of contactless cards, card Issuers shall: i. clearly explain to customers about the technology, its use, and risks while issuing contactless cards ii. create awareness among customers to look for / identify the “contactless” logo on the card (to distinguish them from other cards) as well as the merchant location / POS terminal (to identify that contactless payments are accepted at that location); iii. to clearly indicate to the customers that they can use the card in contactless mode (without PIN authentication) for transactions upto ₹ 5,000/- in locations where contactless payments are accepted and to make customers aware that they are free to use the same card as a regular chip card (with PIN authentication) at any location irrespective of transaction value; (d) Card Issuers shall clearly indicate the maximum liability devolving on the customer, if any, at the time of issuance of cards along with the responsibility of the customer to report the loss of cards to the issuer. (e) Card Issuers shall put in place a robust mechanism for seamless reporting of lost/stolen cards, which can be accessed through multiple channels (website, phone banking, SMS, IVR etc.). Q.
