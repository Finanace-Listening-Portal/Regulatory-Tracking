The RBI is expected to retain its “neutral” stance alongside the October hike, with the terminal repo rate seen at 5.75% after a further 25 basis point increase in December.The case for advancing the first rate hike to October has strengthened as inflation is set to breach 5% in September and potentially peak in October-December, while global crude oil prices have remained above USD100 a barrel. Stronger-than-expected April-June GDP growth and resilient high-frequency indicators for July-August could also prompt the RBI to revise up both its inflation and growth forecasts. The assessment is from Barclays. Advt The RBI was earlier expected to delay the first rate hike to December, as inflation generalisation was considered far from feared, the festive season was approaching and a large liquidity surplus could delay transmission of an immediate hike. However, the report said the case for an October hike has now solidified.August CPI inflation rose to 4.8% year-on-year from 4.45% in July, driven by higher food and fuel inflation. While inflation generalisation remains limited, 38 of 198 items in the core CPI basket recorded inflation of 4% or more in August, up from 36 in July. The report said the MPC may respond pre-emptively as higher inflation broadens further.The RBI may also revise its FY27 CPI inflation forecast from the current 5%, with Barclays forecasting 4.8% and highlighting upside risks. In the August MPC meeting, one member said it would be reasonable to assume oil prices close to USD90 a barrel. With crude now elevated above USD100, the report sees a possibility of further pass-through to retail prices.If global oil prices remain above USD100 a barrel, Barclays expects another round of retail fuel price increases, possibly after Diwali, creating further risks to retail inflation. WPI and PPI inflation in August were also trending above 9% year-on-year. Advt Growth strongGrowth has meanwhile remained stronger than the RBI had expected. GDP growth in the April-June quarter was 7.8% year-on-year, 80 basis points above the RBI's 7% forecast. While high-frequency indicators for July-August showed some moderation, the report said the narrative of robust growth continues. Barclays expects the RBI to revise its FY27 real GDP growth forecast up from 6.7%, with its own growth forecast at 7.1%.The report expects the October hike to be accompanied by a “neutral” stance, with the RBI retaining optionality rather than indicating a series of rate increases. A second and final 25 basis point hike is expected in December, taking the terminal repo rate to 5.75%, alongside a possible shift in stance to “calibrated tightening”. Advt Financial conditions have also tightened since the August MPC meeting. Barclays' financial conditions indicator rose to 0.90 on September 28 from 0.66 on August 5, with most of the tightening coming through the government securities and equity markets.Benchmark government bond yields have risen above 7%, alongside higher US Treasury yields and energy prices above USD105 a barrel. Continued FPI outflows and the unresolved Middle East conflict have also weighed on financial conditions.At the same time, liquidity conditions remain relatively easy. System liquidity was around 1.8% of NDTL in surplus and the weighted average call rate was 5.1% as of September 28, below the repo rate. Barclays expects the RBI to continue liquidity absorption through overnight and term VRRR operations, OMO sales and swaps. Advt FCNR, ECB and OFCB inflows of USD143.6 billion between June 8 and August 31 had added substantially to domestic rupee liquidity. The report expects liquidity tightening to continue alongside monetary tightening to ensure effective transmission, while higher currency demand during the festive season and continued intervention in the spot foreign exchange market could also drain liquidity.The expected October move is therefore being viewed as a “monetary policy calibration” in response to the normalisation of inflation, with Barclays expecting the RBI to keep its neutral stance initially and retain flexibility on the pace of further tightening.
