Summary: This article examines GST registration requirements following Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax and Circular No. 257/03/2026-GST, both dated 26 October 2026, distinguishing amendments to the CGST Rules from CBIC’s administrative guidance. It compares the earlier and revised position on applications, verification, amendments, voluntary cancellation, departmental cancellation and suspension. The revised FORM GST REG-01 provides category-specific documentary requirements, while the circular explains identity particulars, proof of business premises, Aadhaar authentication, processing timelines, limits on officer queries and grievance redressal. It also addresses the existing simplified registration option under Rule 14A and the new Rule 14B facility for eligible sellers operating through e-commerce warehouses in other States. From 1 November 2026, substituted Rule 19 changes the approval process for amendments, Rule 20 introduces specified electronic cancellation procedures and Rule 21 revises grounds for cancellation. Further changes take effect on 1 December 2026, including certain registration-form and amendment provisions. From 1 April 2027, revised Rules 21A and 22 and new Rule 23A introduce further system-driven suspension, cancellation and revocation mechanisms. The article reproduces detailed checklists for business constitutions and premises, specimen declarations and supporting documents, processing and compliance tables, and clarifications on the interaction between the circular and statutory rules. It emphasises that circulars guide administration but cannot override the rules, and that businesses must identify the correct effective date before applying any new requirement. GST Registration after Circular No. 257/03/2026-GST and Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax: The Position Today Abstract: This article reads the CGST (Amendment) Rules, 2026 and CBIC’s registration guidelines of 26.10.2026 together, and sets out the position on application, amendment, surrender and cancellation of GST registration as it stands today, with the checklists and formats prescribed in the circular. Introduction On 26.10.2026, two documents on GST registration were issued on the same day, both following the 57th GST Council meeting of 08.10.2026: Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax, which notifies the CGST (Amendment) Rules, 2026. This is the law. It substitutes FORM GST REG-01 and rules 19, 20 and 21, and inserts rule 14B, from 01.11.2026. It also substitutes rules 21A and 22 and inserts rule 23A, from 01.04.2027. Circular No. 257/03/2026-GST, issued by CBIC under Sec. 168(1). This is guidance. It explains to applicants how to fill REG-01, which documents to upload, how an application is processed, and what an officer may ask. It also contains 51 FAQs and six annexures. Neither document gives the complete picture by itself. The notification changes what happens after registration: amendment, surrender and cancellation. The circular explains how to apply. A reader needs both, read with the CGST Act and the rules that were not amended, such as rules 8, 9, 10A and 14A. This article puts the two together. For each stage of registration, it sets out the earlier position, what has changed, and the position today. One rule of reading applies throughout: a circular explains the law but cannot override it. Where a sentence in the circular and a rule appear to point in different directions, the rule governs. 2. Applying for registration What the law says. The procedure for grant of registration is in rules 8 and 9, which Notification 05/2026 does not amend. What it changes is the form. From 01.11.2026, REG-01 is substituted. For the constitution of business and the nature of premises, the applicant selects a category and the form lists the documents for it. The new list of premises includes “E-Commerce Operator’s Warehouse under rule 14B“. An authorised signatory declaration in a set format is appended to the form. What the circular adds. Circular 257 is a field-by-field guide to the new form: Part A gives the Temporary Reference Number (TRN). The legal name must match the PAN, and for companies the MCA certificate. Part B covers the constitution, promoters and partners (DIN is mandatory for directors and designated partners), up to ten authorised signatories, the principal and additional places of business, goods and services, bank account and State-specific details. Annexure II is a constitution-wise checklist of documents and of who signs digitally. Annexure III lists the proof of premises for each type: owned, inherited, rented, rent-free, consent-based, co-working and SEZ. Annexures IV, V and VI give the formats of the affidavit for inherited property, the undertaking where there is no rent agreement, and the owner’s consent letter. Processing: the position today. Rule 9 is unchanged, and the circular restates it. Step Time limit Form Application with Aadhaar authentication Processed within 7 working days REG-02 (acknowledgement) Application without Aadhaar, or flagged for physical verification Processed within 30 days, after verification REG-30 (verification report) Query by the officer Within 7 working days; within 30 days in physical-verification cases REG-03 Reply by the applicant Within 7 working days REG-04 Decision on the reply Within 7 working days REG-06 or REG-05 No action by the officer in time Deemed approved REG-06 Limits on queries. Officers are asked not to raise queries about the residence of the applicant or directors, the Udyam certificate, or whether the activity can be carried on from the premises. Under para 6, utility bills are checked only for name and address, and no subjective view is to be taken on the type of electricity connection. Under para 8, a grievance about delay or unwarranted queries can be sent to the jurisdictional Chief Commissioner or Commissioner on a designated e-mail address. Bank account. Under rule 10A, bank details may be furnished within 30 days of registration, or before the first GSTR-1, whichever is earlier. Special routes: rule 14A and rule 14B 4.1 Rule 14A: simplified registration (in force since 01.11.2025) Rule 14A is not amended. The circular explains it (para 5.3, FAQs 8 to 10): It is optional, for applicants whose tax on supplies to registered persons will not exceed ₹2.5 lakh a month. Aadhaar authentication is required, and only one such registration is allowed per PAN in a State. Registration is granted by the system within three working days, without officer intervention. To exit, for example to pass on more credit, the registrant files REG-32. All pending returns must be filed first, and at least one return under rule 14A. REG-32 is then processed like a normal application (circular para 6.1). What Notification 05/2026 adds for rule 14A registrants: From 01.12.2026, a change in the principal place of business is also automatic (new rule 19(3)). A rule 14A registrant who wishes to use rule 14B must first exit through REG-32 (rule 14B(15)). 4.2 Rule 14B: sellers using an e-commerce operator’s warehouse (new, from 01.11.2026) Rule 14B gives effect to the Council’s decision on small sellers on e-commerce platforms. The circular does not discuss it. Its scheme is as follows: Who can use it: a person already registered in one State (the “home State”) who wants to sell goods through an e-commerce operator that collects tax at source, from the operator’s warehouse in another State where he has no physical presence. Limit: tax on supplies to registered persons, other than those on the same PAN, must not exceed ₹2.5 lakh a month. Principal place of business: the operator’s warehouse in that State, with the operator’s consent within 7 working days. Other warehouses may be added as additional places. Verification: biometric Aadhaar authentication and document verification by the home-State officer, and physical verification of the principal place of business in the home State, in each case if not done earlier. Deemed approval: if physical verification is not done within 30 days, the application is deemed approved. If it shows no business in the home State, the application is deemed rejected (REG-05A). Continuing conditions: books are kept at the home-State premises, and the GSTIN need not be displayed at the warehouse. Only one rule 14B registration is allowed per PAN per State. Deemed cancellation (REG-38): if the operator withdraws consent and the address is not amended within 30 days, or if the home-State registration is cancelled. The new REG-01 carries detailed instructions for rule 14B applications (instruction 10), including the operator’s consent process and auto-rejection where a home-State field visit is negative. 5. Amendment of registration Earlier position. Under the earlier rule 19, changes in core fields needed the proper officer’s approval. Core fields were the legal name, the address of the principal or any additional place of business, and the addition, deletion or retirement of partners, directors, Karta, trustees or the CEO. Other changes took effect on filing. Position from 01.11.2026 (new rule 19). Any change must be applied for in REG-14 within 15 days of the change, with the supporting documents. Then: Change Treatment Rule Principal place of business (normal registrants) Sent to the officer. He approves (REG-15) or raises a query (REG-03) within 15 days. Reply in 7 working days (REG-04); decision within 7 working days of the reply. No action in time: deemed amended. No reply: deemed rejected (REG-05A) 19(2) Principal place of business (rule 14A registrants) Automatic on filing and online validation, from 01.12.2026 19(3) All other particulars, including additional places of business, legal name, partners and directors Automatic on filing and online validation 19(4) Legal name, partners, directors, Karta, trustees, CEO The change applies to all registrations on the same PAN across States 19(5) Mobile number or e-mail of an authorised signatory Effective only after online verification 19(6) Change in constitution that changes the PAN Fresh registration needed 19(7) GST practitioners amend their enrolment in the same way (new rule 83C). How circular para 3.9 fits in. Para 3.9 says the documents for amending a principal or additional place of business “remain same as those specified for the Principal Place of Business”. Read with rule 19, this is about the documents to be uploaded with REG-14, which rule 19(1) requires for every change. It does not change the treatment. An amendment of an additional place of business still needs the same proof of premises, but under rule 19(4) it takes effect automatically on filing and validation, without officer approval. The effect in practice. A business opening a new godown or branch in the same State, inducting a partner or changing its legal name no longer waits for an officer. Only a shift of the principal place of business goes to the officer, and even then within a 15-day deemed-approval window. 6. Cancellation on the taxpayer’s request Earlier position. The application was made in REG-16 within 30 days of the event. The officer examined it and passed an order in REG-19. The time limits for the officer were in rule 22. Position from 01.11.2026 (new rule 20). Who may apply and when. A person who closes the business, transfers it, ceases to be liable, or opts out of voluntary registration applies in REG-16 within 30 days of the event. The 30-day limit does not apply on the death of a sole proprietor. His legal heirs may apply as if the proprietor had (rule 20(13)). Pre-conditions. All returns up to the date from which cancellation is sought must be filed. Where GSTR-1 for a period has been filed, GSTR-3B for that period must also be filed. The rule gives an illustration: if GSTR-1 for June 2026 was filed on 11.07.2026, GSTR-3B for June must be filed before applying. On filing. The registration stands suspended until the proceedings are completed (rule 20(4)). Returns for the remaining period must be filed by their due dates. Automatic cancellation. The portal cancels the registration (REG-38), from the date sought, where: returns are filed in time and all dues paid; and either the tax on supplies to registered persons did not exceed ₹2.5 lakh in any month since registration, or the final return in GSTR-10 has been filed. Otherwise, the officer decides. The officer cancels (REG-19) or raises a query (REG-03) within 15 days. The applicant replies within 15 days (REG-04), and the officer decides within 15 days of the reply. If the officer does not act in time, the application is deemed approved. If the applicant does not reply, it is deemed rejected (REG-05A). Changing the effective date later. The officer may propose an earlier effective date of cancellation by a notice in REG-34. The reply is due in REG-35 within 30 days. The officer must pass an order (REG-36 dropping, or REG-37 changing the date) within the time set. If no order is passed within 60 days of the notice, the proceedings are deemed dropped. An effective date once changed cannot be changed again. Form. From 01.12.2026, REG-16 is revised. It carries a detailed table of stock, semi-finished and finished goods and capital goods, with the input tax credit to be reversed, and an option to file a nil return for the period up to cancellation. 7. Cancellation and suspension by the department The position changes in two steps: the grounds from 01.11.2026, and the procedure from 01.04.2027. 7.1 Grounds: new rule 21 (from 01.11.2026) Sec. 29(2) lets the proper officer cancel a registration on grounds in the Act, and for contravention of provisions prescribed in the rules. Rule 21 is where those provisions are prescribed. Earlier rule 21. It listed several grounds, including not conducting business from the declared place of business, issuing invoices without supply, and mismatches between returns. New rule 21. It lists only five: a composition taxpayer not filing the annual return for more than three months after the due date; a monthly filer not filing returns for a continuous period of six months; a quarterly filer not filing returns for two continuous tax periods; contravention of the third or fourth proviso to rule 23(1); failure to furnish bank account details under rule 10A. The grounds in Sec. 29(2) itself, such as registration obtained by fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression of facts, continue to apply. 7.2 Procedure: until 31.03.2027 The existing rules 21A (suspension), 22 (cancellation by notice in REG-17 and order in REG-19) and 23 (revocation) continue to govern the procedure. They now operate on the narrower grounds of the new rule 21 and the grounds in Sec. 29(2). 7.3 Procedure: from 01.04.2027 (new rules 21A, 22 and 23A) Situation Procedure Officer has reason to believe the registration is liable to be cancelled He may suspend the registration on starting proceedings (21A(1)). Show cause notice in REG-17, with 7 working days to reply in REG-18. Proceedings dropped (REG-20) or registration cancelled (REG-19) within 30 days of the reply (rule 22) Returns not filed (as in rule 21), or bank account not furnished The portal suspends the registration and intimates it in REG-31, allowing 30 days to comply. On compliance, the suspension is deemed revoked from its start. Without compliance, the registration is deemed cancelled by the portal (REG-38) (rules 21A(2), 21A(4), 22(5)) Revocation of a deemed cancellation Apply in REG-21 within 180 days after filing all returns with tax, interest and late fee, or furnishing bank details. The portal revokes the cancellation (REG-22). Returns for the intervening period must be filed within 30 days (rule 23A) During suspension No tax invoice may be issued and no tax charged; returns are not required for that period. If the suspension is revoked, invoices may be issued under Sec. 31(3)(a) and the supplies declared under Sec. 40 (rule 21A(3), (7)) 7.4 Suspension for any other reason In several States, and in some Central GST formations, welcome letters are sent to new registrants by post, and registrations have been suspended when the letters are returned undelivered. Neither the circular nor the notification provides for suspension on that basis. Under the rules as they stand, suspension is linked to cancellation proceedings. It must rest on a reason to believe that the registration is liable to be cancelled, under a ground in Sec. 29(2) or the new rule 21. From 01.04.2027, suspension by the system is confined to non-filing and the bank account requirement. Para 7 of the circular also states that the registration process is online and faceless, and that all communication is through the common portal. A taxpayer facing such a suspension can therefore ask for the specific ground and for a notice and hearing before any cancellation. 8. Reading the circular and the rules together: points of clarification The circular was prepared as a guide to applying for registration. The notification amends the rules on what follows registration. The following points show how they fit together. Question Clarification Does an additional place of business still need officer approval to be added? No. Under new rule 19(4), it is amended automatically on filing and validation. Circular para 3.9 is about the documents to be uploaded, which remain the same as for the principal place Is physical verification still possible, and of which premises? Yes, under rules 9 and 25 where the applicant does not opt for Aadhaar, is flagged on risk parameters, or the officer obtains approval. The report under circular para 5.6(a) is on the existence of the principal place of business. For rule 14B, verification is of the principal place in the home State (rule 14B(7)) How long can an officer take to raise a query where physical verification applies? Up to 30 days from the application (rule 9; circular para 5.4.2). The verification report is to be uploaded at least 5 days before the 30 days end (para 5.6). This timeline is not changed by the notification Is the circular binding? It binds the officers of the department (Sec. 168). Para 6 asks officers to stick to the prescribed documents. Where the circular and a rule differ, the rule prevails Is rule 14B automatic? Registration is granted electronically, but only after the operator’s consent, biometric Aadhaar and, where not done earlier, physical verification in the home State. It is deemed approved if verification is not done in 30 days Is cancellation on request now always automatic? Only where returns and dues are clear and either the ₹2.5 lakh monthly limit was never exceeded or GSTR-10 is filed. Other cases go to the officer, with deemed approval after 15 days Can a registrant be suspended for not adding a bank account? Yes. Under the new rule 21 (from 01.11.2026) non-compliance with rule 10A is a ground for cancellation. From 01.04.2027 it leads to system suspension after notice in REG-31 Which version of rules 21A and 22 applies today? The existing versions, until 31.03.2027. The new versions apply from 01.04.2027 9. Key dates and conclusion Date What takes effect 26.10.2026 Circular No. 257/03/2026-GST: guidelines, 51 FAQs, checklist and formats 01.11.2026 New REG-01; rule 14B; new rule 19 (except 19(3)); new rule 20; new rule 21; rule 83C 01.12.2026 Rule 19(3): automatic PPoB amendment for rule 14A registrants; revised REG-16 01.04.2027 New rules 21A, 22 and 23A; new REG-17, REG-22 and REG-31 Read together, the two documents of 26.10.2026 cover the whole registration life cycle. Applying: the rules on grant (rules 8, 9, 14A) are unchanged. Circular 257 now explains them in detail, with a fixed list of documents and formats, limits on queries, and a grievance channel. Amending: changes other than the principal place of business are automatic, and a principal-place change is deemed approved if the officer does not act in 15 days. Closing: a compliant small business can surrender its registration without officer intervention. Cancellation by the department: this rests mainly on non-filing of returns and the bank account requirement, with notice and 30 days to comply from 01.04.2027.
