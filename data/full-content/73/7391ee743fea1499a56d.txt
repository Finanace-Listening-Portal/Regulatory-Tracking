Articles 4 min read FCNR inflows to dilute bank margins, but could add Rs 10,000 crore to bank earnings The record USD 127.2 billion FCNR-B mobilisation is set to reshape banks’ funding mix, with Jefferies expecting near term NIM compression but an annual Rs 10,000-11,000 crore addition to the banking sector’s profit pool. CareEdge, meanwhile, estimates core banking-system liquidity could swell to Rs 13-14 lakh crore by December, potentially accelerating the decline in wholesale funding costs. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Updated On Sep 4, 2026 at 06:19 PM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 133763281; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The record mobilisation of FCNR(B) deposits is likely to create a near term squeeze on banks’ net interest margins (NIMs), the scale of inflows which happened to be four times of 2013, could ultimately add around Rs 10,000-11,000 crore annually to the banking sector’s earnings, according to Jefferies.The brokerage estimates that FCNR-B deposits could create an additional profit pool of Rs 100-110 billion a year, equivalent to around 2 per cent of the banking sector’s profit before tax. While the deposits are structurally a lower-NIM business and are likely to dilute both NIM and return on assets (ROA), they are expected to be accretive to net interest income (NII) and return on equity (ROE). Advt The concessional swap window for FCNR-B deposits closed on August 31, with FCNR-B inflows reaching USD 127.2 billion. Including USD 5.3 billion raised through overseas foreign currency borrowings and USD 3.9 billion through ECBs, total inflows under the broader RBI scheme stood at USD 136.4 billion by August 31.Why NIMs could come under pressureThe first impact on bank margins is expected to be visible in the second quarter, according to Jefferies.Banks had raised bonds and loans ahead of receiving and deploying the FCNR-B deposits, creating a timing mismatch between the cost of funds and their deployment. As a result, the brokerage expects banks to report lower NIMs in Q2 and potentially quantify the short-term impact as a non-recurring effect.There is also a structural reason for the lower margin profile. FCNR-B deposits involve what Jefferies describes as a “double-counting” of deposits and assets, with the first leg of the transaction generating only a 10-15 basis point spread.This means that a sharp increase in FCNR-B balances can mechanically dilute the average margin even when the transaction adds to absolute interest income.“While FCNR-B deposits dilute NIMs & ROA, they are accretive to NII & ROE,” the brokerage said, estimating an annual Rs 100-110 billion addition to the sector’s earnings.Funding costThe larger opportunity for banks could come from how they use the new foreign-currency funding to change their existing liability and asset mix. Advt Jefferies expects banks to gradually normalise margins over the next two to four quarters by reducing their dependence on high-cost wholesale deposits, lowering the proportion of government securities held for liquidity coverage requirements and reducing exposure to low-margin overseas trade financing.This could create a second-order benefit for banks. If FCNR-B funds allow lenders to replace relatively expensive domestic wholesale deposits, the initial NIM dilution could be offset over time through a lower overall cost of funds.The early signs of this funding-cost reset are already visible in the money markets. Three-month certificate of deposit rates fell by 90 basis points in the last month to 5.9%, while six-month CD rates declined by 50 basis points to 6.46 per cent, according to Jefferies. Advt The brokerage expects banks to lower wholesale funding rates further as the liquidity impact of the FCNR-B inflows works through the system.Liquidity glut could amplify pressure on domestic ratesCareEdge's assessment suggests that the liquidity impact could be substantially larger than the immediate deposit numbers indicate.The rating agency estimates that core liquidity in the banking system, which stood at Rs 8.1 lakh crore in mid-August, could rise to around Rs 13-14 lakh crore by December-end in the absence of RBI liquidity-management operations. That would amount to around 4.5-5 per cent of net demand and time liabilities. Advt The calculation takes into account several offsets. CareEdge expects currency in circulation to rise by around Rs 1.1 lakh crore during the festive season, while the maturity of the RBI's short dollar-forward position could create another Rs 3 lakh crore drag on core liquidity. CRR accretion from deposit growth could absorb a further Rs 70,000 crore. Even after these adjustments, however, liquidity could still rise sharply.This creates the possibility of a broader funding-cost reset across the banking system.Jefferies estimates that system surplus liquidity has already risen to around Rs 10 lakh crore and expects better liquidity conditions ahead of the festive season to keep rates under check. The brokerage expects the RBI to absorb excessive short-term liquidity and re-inject it over time, with a preference for open market operations over a CRR increase.CareEdge, however, sees a wider menu of possible liquidity-absorption measures. It expects the RBI could initially use longer-tenor VRRR operations and OMO sales, followed by an incremental CRR if the surplus proves persistent.
