Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Contray Liquar Shop Vs ITO (ITAT Nagpur) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Nagpur SMC Bench, in M/s. Contray Liquar Shop Vs ITO, restored three disputed additions aggregating ₹34,88,601 to the jurisdictional Assessing Officer for fresh adjudication in relation to Assessment Year 2018-19. The additions concerned disallowance of salary expenditure of ₹6,03,840 under Section 40(a)(ia) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, unexplained sundry creditors of ₹7,75,554 and unexplained licence-fee-related balances of ₹21,09,207. The assessee, a partnership firm engaged in the liquor business, had filed its return on 9 October 2018 declaring income of ₹2,06,140. The additions made during scrutiny assessment were sustained by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)/National Faceless Appeal Centre despite written submissions and supporting records furnished by the assessee. Before examining the substantive disputes, the Tribunal considered a delay of four days in filing the appeal. The assessee had submitted an application for condonation supported by an affidavit. After considering the explanation and hearing both parties, the Tribunal found that the delay was neither intentional nor deliberate. Relying upon the Supreme Court decisions in Collector Land Acquisition, Anantnag Vs Mst. Katiji & Ors., (1987) 2 SCC 107, and Inder Singh Vs State of Madhya Pradesh, 2025 INSC 382, the Tribunal condoned the delay in the interest of justice and admitted the appeal for adjudication. Regarding the salary expenditure, the Assessing Officer had disallowed 30% of total salary expenses of ₹20,12,800, resulting in an addition of ₹6,03,840 for alleged non-deduction of tax at source. The assessee argued that the salary paid to each employee was below ₹2,50,000, being the minimum taxable limit applicable to the relevant assessment year. The Tribunal examined the employee-wise salary details appearing at page 128 of the paper book and observed that the annual payment to each employee was below that threshold. It further recorded that tax was not deductible on salary payments to resident individuals up to the applicable minimum taxable limit. These factual observations supported the assessee’s contention that the disallowance required reconsideration. On the addition of ₹7,75,554 towards sundry creditors, the assessee explained that the outstanding balances related to two regular suppliers, namely M/s. Agrawal Associates, having a balance of ₹5,59,919, and M/s. Vijay Agencies, having a balance of ₹2,15,635. The assessee submitted that regular purchases were made from these suppliers and payments were generally settled through banking channels on a bill-to-bill basis. The Tribunal examined Schedule C of the audited balance sheet and the ledger accounts placed at pages 47 to 49 of the paper book. It observed that the records prima facie supported the existence of regular purchase transactions and banking payments. Importantly, the Assessing Officer had neither rejected the book results nor disputed the purchases made from the two parties. Although confirmation letters from the creditors had not been furnished, the Tribunal considered the available accounting records relevant to the examination of the addition. The third dispute concerned ₹21,09,207 treated as unexplained licence fee. The assessee explained that this amount represented different balances disclosed in Schedule B of the audited financial statements rather than a single unexplained expenditure. The Tribunal noted tax collected at source relating to financial years 2015-16, 2016-17 and 2017-18 amounting to ₹1,50,884, ₹1,50,771 and ₹2,90,277 respectively. It also identified a Tijori account balance of ₹13,51,750, representing a separately maintained cash account, and prepaid licence fee of ₹1,65,525, being an advance payment required for obtaining a liquor licence. These components were reflected in the audited records and supporting documents furnished by the assessee. Cases Discussed Inder Singh Vs State of Madhya Pradesh, 2025 INSC 382; 21/03/2025 (Supreme Court of India). The Tribunal relied upon this judgment while condoning the four-day delay in filing the assessee’s appeal. The decision was cited in support of a justice-oriented approach to the consideration of delay. Collector Land Acquisition, Anantnag Vs Mst. Katiji & Ors., (1987) 2 SCC 107; [1987] 167 ITR 471 (SC); Civil Appeal No. 460 of 1987; 19/02/1987 (Supreme Court of India). The Tribunal drew guidance from this decision for adopting a liberal and substantial-justice-oriented approach to condonation of a short, non-deliberate delay. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT NAGPUR This appeal by the assessee is directed against the order of Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)/NFAC, Delhi (for short, “CIT(A)”) dated 15.01.2026 passed u/sec. 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (for short, “Act”) which is arising out of assessment order dated 12.03.2026 passed u/sec. 143(3) r.w.s. 143(3A) & 143(3B) of the Act for the Assessment Year (A.Y.) 2018-19. 2. Registry has informed that there is a delay of 04 days in filing the present appeal. Application for condonation of delay along with an affidavit is placed on record. Perusal of the same and after hearing both the sides and considering that the delay is minimal, I find that the delay is neither intentional nor deliberate and therefore, in the interest of justice and taking guidance from the judgments of Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Collector Land Acquisition, Anantnag vs. Mst. Katiji & Ors [1987 (2) SCC 107] and also in the case of Inder Singh vs. State of Madhya Pradesh dated 21.03.2025 [(2025) INSC 382)], I hereby condone the delay and admit the appeal for adjudication. 3. The grievance of the assessee is against the disallowance of salary expenses u/s. 40(a)(ia) of the Act at Rs. 6,03,840/-, unexplained sundry creditors at Rs. 7,75,554/- and unexplained license fee at Rs. 21,09,207/- 4. At the outset, learned counsel for the assessee submitted that complete details were filed before both the lower authorities except confirmation letters from sundry creditors, no disallowance u/s. 40(a)(ia) is called for as the salary paid to each of the employees is below the monetary taxable limit of Rs.2,50,000/-, sundry creditors are only two parties from whom regular purchases are made and that the purchases have been accepted and books of accounts are not rejected. As regards license fee, he submitted that it contains tax collected at source for F.Ys. 2015-16, 2016-17 & 2017-18 in Tijori account and also prepaid license fee and that all these investments/deposits form part of the audit report. Copies of relevant documents are placed in paper book running into 237 pages. On the other hand, Ld.DR supported the orders of the Ld. CIT(A) and Ld. AO. 5. I have heard the rival submissions and perused the material placed before me. I find that assessee is a partnership firm engaged in liquor business and income of Rs. 2,06,140/- declared in the return of income furnished for AY 2018-19 on 09.10.2018. In the course of scrutiny carried out after validly serving statutory notices u/s. 143(2) & 142(1) of the Act, Ld. AO made impugned disallowances, against which, assessee preferred appeal before the Ld. CIT(A). In spite of filing written submissions assessee could not find any favour. 6. I observe that first addition is towards disallowance u/s. 40(a)(ia) of the Act for non-deduction of tax at source on salary payments and Ld. AO made disallowance of 30% on the total salary expenses of Rs. 20,12,800/-. In the paper book at page No.128, list of employees is appearing in the ledger, as per which, the year payment to each of the employee is less than Rs.2,50,000/- which is minimum taxable limit at Rs.2,50,000/- applicable for the year under consideration and upto this amount TDS not deductible on salary payments to Resident Individual. 7. As regards sundry creditors is concerned, I note that as per Schedule-C of the audited balance sheet, the total amount consists of two parties namely, M/s. Agrawal associates at Rs. 5,59,919/- and M/s. Vijay Agencies at Rs. 2,15,635/-. Ledger accounts of both these parties are placed at page Nos. 47-49 which prima-facie indicates that assessee is making regular purchases from both these parties and regular payments through banking channels are made which are normally bill to bill. I also note that Ld. AO has not rejected the book results and accepted the purchases made by the assessee from both these parties. 8. Third issue relates to the addition for unexplained license fee. I note that as per Schedule-B, the alleged sum of Rs. 21,09,207/- consists of tax collected at source for F.Ys. 2015-16, 2016-17 & 2017-18 at Rs.1,50,884/-, Rs.1,50,771/- & Rs.2,90,277/- respectively which are in the nature of license fee. Schedule-B also indicates Tijori account showing balance of Rs.13,51,750/- which is basically a cash account separately kept by the assessee. Schedule B “Deposit” also includes prepaid license fee of Rs. 1,65,525/- being the amount required to be paid in advance for obtaining a liquor license. 9. On due consideration of the facts discussed hereinabove, I find that in spite of the fact that assessee has furnished these details before the lower authorities which are self-speaking, but still they have not been given due cognizance and therefore, in the larger interest of justice and being fair to both the parties, I deem it appropriate to remit the issues raised in the instant appeal to the file of Ld. Jurisdictional Assessing Officer (JAO) for denovo adjudication in light of observations of facts discussed above and after verification of the records shall decide in accordance with law. Needless to mention that JAO shall afford a reasonable opportunity of being heard to the assessee and the assessee is also directed to remain vigilant and not to take unnecessary adjournments unless otherwise required for reasonable cause. Effective grounds of appeal raised by the assessee are allowed for statistical purposes. 10. In the result, appeal of the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes. Order pronounced on 16th November, 2026 under Rule 34(5) of the Income Tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963.
