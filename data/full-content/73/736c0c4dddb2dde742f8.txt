Since financial institutions handle public deposits, investment, and insurance protection, those working in the sector must stay conscious of their responsibility even when they head large hierarchies Bagchi, who began his career in 1992 at the erstwhile ICICI, said the rapid pace of technological and economic change made continuous learning essential for young professionals entering the workforce. “Future does not belong to people who cling to the past, without context. The context changes and we have to change with the context,” he said. He likened employees to pixels in a photograph, saying individuals must understand both their specific roles and how they contribute to the larger institution. While young professionals often focus on individual achievements, they must recognise that an institution’s success depends on the contribution of its constituent parts. The order of priority, he said, must be society first, then institution, then team, then self. Drawing on cricket, he noted that the “player of the match” award almost always went to someone in the winning team. “You invert it, (because) you are not going to win for long,” he said. He also cautioned against treating competence and technical skills as sufficient for success. Professionals must build networks of goodwill and trust, he said, by keeping their promises and communicating honestly when they cannot fulfil them. Bagchi said trust and goodwill often took time to yield results but compounded over the long term. Careers, he argued, are more like marathons than sprints, requiring stamina, resilience and the ability to recover from setbacks. “It’s not a straight line,” he said, adding that professionals would encounter both good and bad periods, but what mattered was continuing to make progress over time. Luck and blessings also played a role, he said, but individuals could improve their chances by strengthening their competence and building goodwill. On decision-making, Bagchi said much of finance, outside operations, was about judgment and assessing uncertain outcomes. Since these depended on variables beyond one’s control, professionals should think in probabilities rather than assume that results were predetermined. “There is no perfection in judgment,” he said, urging young professionals to keep refining their understanding as circumstances changed. With the external environment changing rapidly, Bagchi said, opportunities and obstacles would both multiply. Professionals should embrace change with optimism while remaining mindful of risks and managing them. He also stressed the importance of family and close relationships as sources of unconditional support through difficult periods. “Add value, add value, add value,” he said, urging students to focus on contributing to society, their institutions and their teams over the long term.
