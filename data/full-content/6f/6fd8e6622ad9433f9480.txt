(GSTAT Bengaluru) Summary: The GST Appellate Tribunal, Bengaluru, in Cauvery Constructions Vs Commissioner of Commercial Taxes & Ors., set aside a penalty of ₹10,28,380 imposed under Section 129 of the CGST Act, 2017, holding that a mismatch between the vehicle registration number recorded in Part B of an e-way bill and the vehicle actually transporting the goods did not, in the circumstances of the case, establish an intention to evade tax. The appellant was engaged in renting, leasing, subcontracting and undertaking job work involving road-laying machinery. It dispatched a cold milling machine from Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu, to Mangaluru, Karnataka, for job work. On 19 February 2023, the consignment was intercepted at the Attibele check post because the actual vehicle bore registration number MH06AQ5952, whereas Part B of the e-way bill mentioned MH46BB2138. The appellant explained that the discrepancy arose from incorrect communication by the transporter and that the goods were accompanied by a valid delivery challan. The proper officer nevertheless imposed a penalty under Section 129(1)(a), which the appellant paid to secure release of the consignment. The first appellate authority upheld the penalty, treating the incorrect vehicle particulars as a substantive statutory contravention. The Tribunal examined Section 129 of the CGST Act, Rule 138 governing e-way bills and Rule 55 governing delivery challans. It found that the e-way bill expressly recorded the movement as “outward – Job Work” and referred to delivery challan No. PL/343/22-23 issued in favour of Vishal Infrastructure Limited, Mangaluru. The delivery challan clearly stated that the machine was being sent for milling job work and not for sale. Except for the discrepancy in the vehicle number, the authorities had identified no defect in the accompanying documents or physical verification of the consignment. The Tribunal emphasised that correctly furnishing vehicle particulars is a statutory requirement and cannot be dismissed as an empty formality. However, the nature and extent of a documentary discrepancy must be assessed together with the taxpayer’s explanation and surrounding circumstances before concluding that penalty proceedings are justified. In considering judicial precedents, the Tribunal distinguished the Supreme Court decision in Commercial Tax Officer Vs Bajaj Electricals Limited and the Madhya Pradesh High Court decisions in Gati Kinetsu Express Private Limited and Manish Packaging Private Limited. It relied particularly on Assistant Commissioner (ST) Vs Satyam Shivam Papers Private Limited and the jurisdictional Karnataka High Court decision in BVM Trans Solutions Private Limited Vs Commercial Tax Officer. The Tribunal also considered Podaran Foods India Limited and the Allahabad High Court rulings in BMR Enterprises Vs State of UP and Varun Beverages Limited Vs State of UP. These authorities supported examining whether a documentary irregularity was accompanied by circumstances indicating tax evasion rather than treating every discrepancy as automatically warranting the penalty imposed. The Tribunal concluded that the appellant’s explanation was acceptable because physical verification revealed no defect, the accompanying documents established the job-work movement, and the authorities had produced no other material showing an intention to evade tax. The vehicle-number mismatch was the sole basis for imposing the penalty. Accordingly, the Tribunal allowed the appeal, set aside the appellate order dated 31 March 2025 and directed the respondent to give effect to its order within eight weeks. The demand summary recorded disputed penalties of ₹5,14,190 each under central tax and state tax, aggregating ₹10,28,380, with the amount determined by the Tribunal reduced to nil. The decision illustrates the importance of examining the actual transportation documents, taxpayer explanation and evidence of evasion while applying Section 129. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF GST APPELLATE TRIBUNAL, BENGALURU (1) The appellant, M/s. Cauvery Constructions, has filed this appeal assailing the order of the Joint Commissioner of Commercial Taxes (Appeals)-8, Koramangala, Bengaluru (“the Appellate Authority”), passed in order-in-appeal GST AP. No.396/24-25 dated 31.03.2025. The Appellate Authority confirmed the penalty imposed under Section 129 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (“the Act”), by the Commercial Tax Officer, Enforcement-46, Viveknagar, Bangalore – 47 (the proper officer), passed in Order No. AdCOM/ENF/SZ/CTO(ENF)-46/DTN-15/2022-23 dated 21.2.2023. (2) The appellant rents, leases, subcontracts, and does job work for road-laying machinery and equipment across India, primarily for contractors, based on customers’ needs. The appellant sent a cold milling machine for subcontract work from Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu, to Mangaluru, Karnataka. The consignment was accompanied by an e-way bill and delivery challan. The consignment was intercepted and detained on 19.02.2023 @ 2.20 AM at Attibele check post on the ground that the consignment was transported in vehicle no. MH06AQ5952, whereas the e-way bill was generated for vehicle no. MH46BB2138 in Part B of the e-way bill. The appellant represented that the error was minor and requested release of the consignment. The proper officer rejected the objections, holding that the error was not minor; the consignment moved with defective documents, and the officer imposed a penalty of Rs. 10,28,380/- under Section 129(1)(a) of the Act. The appellant paid the penalty amount to obtain release of the consignment. (3) The Appellate Authority dismissed the appeal, holding that Part B of the e-way was updated with a different vehicle number, which was a contravention, and that valid e-way bills were not produced at the time of interception. The appellate authority relied on the Supreme Court decision reported in (2008) 18 VST 436 (Commercial Tax Officer Vs. Bajaj Electricals Limited), on the Madhya Pradesh High Court decision reported in 2018 (15) GSTL 310 (Gati Kintetsu Express private limited Vs. Commissioner of Commercial Taxes, M.P.) and of the Kerala High Court in the decision reported in 2021 (50) GSTL 412 (Podaran Foods India Limited Vs. State of Kerala) to dismiss the appeal. Hence, the present appeal before the Tribunal. (4) Mr Nimit Jain, counsel for the appellant, reiterated the contentions raised in the grounds of appeal. The counsel submitted that the e-way bill mentioned the vehicle number as MH46BB2138 instead of MH06AQ5952 due to miscommunication by the transporter. They were unaware that the transporter had loaded the goods in a different vehicle number, as the appellant was not informed of this change. This was a minor error, and there was no intention to evade tax payment. The consignment was also accompanied by a delivery challan as required under Rule 55 of the CGST Rules. The counsel relied on the decision of the Allahabad High Court in 2024 (19) Centax 57 (BMR Enterprises v. State of UP) and (2023 (71) GSTL 4 (Varun Beverages Limited Vs. State Vs. UP) in support of his contention that penalty cannot be levied for minor errors and in the absence of intention to evade payment of taxes. (5) Mr Prakash, Assistant Commissioner for the respondent, supported and relied on the Appellate Authority’s findings and also filed written submissions. It was submitted that an E-Way Bill bearing the registration number of another vehicle cannot, without further statutory compliance, be treated as the valid E-Way Bill for the actual vehicle merely because the goods, invoice and taxpayer may otherwise be genuine. In addition to the decisions relied upon by the Appellate Authority, the learned Assistant Commissioner also relied on the decision of the Madhya Pradesh High Court in (2025) 33 Centax 109 (Manish Packaging Private Limited Vs. State of Madhya Pradesh). It was submitted that the absence of an allegation of clandestine supply cannot, by itself, invalidate the Section 129 action where the specific transportation is established. It was necessary to distinguish between cases where the statutory defect was trivial and cases where the statutory documentary movement does not correspond to the actual conveyance. (6) Heard the rival submissions and perused the connected records. The following issue arises for our consideration: Whether invoking Section 129 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 is just and proper on the facts and circumstances of the case? (7) It is undisputed that the consignment was accompanied by an e-way bill and delivery challan. The e-way bill stated the reason for transportation as “outward – Job Work”. The e-way bill referred to document no. PL/343/22-23, which was the delivery challan issued by the appellant in the name of Vishal Infrastructure Limited, Mangaluru – 574142. The appellant issued the delivery challan under Rule 55 of the CGST Rules. The delivery challan contained an endorsement “The above machine is sent for milling job work purpose and it is not for sale”. The proper officer did not notice any other defect in the transportation except the discrepancy in the transport vehicle number. The appellant explained that the e-way bill mentioned the vehicle number as MH46BB2138 instead of MH06AQ5952 due to incorrect communication from the transporter. Neither the proper officer nor the appellate authority had considered the explanation while confirming the penalty. (8) The decisions relied upon by the appellate authority to dismiss the appeal are not applicable to the present case. In (2008) 18 VST 436 (Commercial Tax Officer Vs. Bajaj Electricals Limited), the Supreme Court interpreted Section 78(2) and 78(5) of the Rajasthan Sales Tax Act and considered a situation where the documents accompanying the consignment were left blank except for a signature. The authorities could not verify the correctness of the declaration forms. Section 78(5) of the Act empowered the authorities to impose a penalty equal to 30% of the value of the goods if the movement was in violation of Section 78(2)(a) or for submission of false or forged documents or declarations. Section 78(2)(a) required the driver or person in charge of the vehicle to carry with him a goods vehicle record, including “challans” and “bilties”, bill of sale or despatch memo. The Court therefore held that mens rea was not a necessary ingredient for imposing the penalty. It was further held that, even if mens rea was a necessary ingredient, the modus operandi adopted by the dealers in not giving particulars in the declaration Form disclosed that the dealers had the intention to evade payment of taxes. The relevant principle from the above case is that the need to prove mens rea depends on the statutory provision and the nature of the contravention. (9) The Supreme Court, in the decision reported in 2022 (57) GSTL 97 (Assistant Commissioner (ST) Vs. Satyam Shivam Papers Private Limited), upheld the requirement of mens rea before invoking Section 129 of the Act. The Court approved the Telangana High Court’s reasoning that the proper officer had no material to conclude an intention to evade tax merely because the time mentioned in the e-way bill had lapsed. (10) The issue before the Madhya Pradesh High Court in Gati Kinetsu Express Private Limited Vs. Commissioner of Commercial Taxes [2018 (15) GSTL 310] related to non-updating of Part B of the e-way bill. The Court held that the distance was more than 1200-1300 kilometres and it was mandatory for the taxpayer to provide all the details in the e-way bill before loading the goods on the vehicle. The Court upheld the penalty imposed under Section 129 of the Act. The Court did not consider the issue from the perspective of an intention to evade tax payment. The decision cannot be applied for three reasons. First, the Madhya High Court’s decision predates the Supreme Court’s decision in 2022 (57) GSTL 97 (Satyam Shivam Papers Private Limited). Second, the Karnataka High Court in BVM Trans Solutions Private Limited v. Commercial Tax Officer [(2025) 37 Centax 391] did not subscribe to the Madhya Pradesh High Court’s view, as seen in para 12 of the report. Third, the Karnataka High Court held that failing to fill in Part B of the e-way bill does not warrant invoking Section 129 of the Act when there was no intention to evade tax. Applying the decision of the Supreme Court in Satyam Shivam Papers Private Limited case (cited supra), the Madhya Pradesh High Court in 2022 (55) GSTL 12 (Daya Shankar Singh Vs. State of Madhya Pradesh) had cancelled the penalty imposed under Section 129 of the Act, holding that the authorities did not prove that the taxpayer had the intention to evade payment of tax. (11) The Madhya Pradesh High Court in (2025) 33 Centax 109 (Manish Packaging Private Limited Vs. State of Madhya Pradesh) was concerned with transport of goods, for a distance of over 2100 kms, without mentioning the vehicle number in Part B of the e-way bill. The Court noted that the e-way bill contained an endorsement that it was not valid because Part B was not entered. Without considering whether an intention to evade tax was present, the Court upheld the penalty imposition in light of the endorsement in the e-way bill. We find that the Supreme Court’s decision in Satyam Shivam Papers Private Limited (cited supra) was not brought to the Court’s notice. In any view, we are bound by the decision of the jurisdictional High Court, namely, the Karnataka High Court. (12) The Kerala High Court in Podaran Foods India Limited Vs. The State of Kerala [2021 (50) GSTL 412] was considering the invocation of Section 129 of the Act in cases of misdescription and misclassification of goods. While considering the issue, the Court held that Section 129 was merely a machinery provision that is intended to check evasion of tax and which must be read along with the substantive provisions of the statute that provide for the levy and collection of tax. The Court further held that it has to be borne in mind that Section 129 forms part of the machinery provisions under the Act to check evasion of tax and a detention can be justified only if there is a contravention of the provisions of the Act in relation to transportation of goods or their storage while in transit. The decision supports the appellant’s case. (13) The decisions of the Allahabad High Court in 2024 (19) Centax 57 (BMR Enterprises v. State of UP) and (2023 (71) GSTL 4 (Varun Beverages Limited Vs. State Vs. UP) supports the appellant’s case. In BMR Enterprises (supra), the goods were transported by vehicle bearing number UP 83 CT 2724, whereas the e-way bill specified vehicle number UP 80 CT 7024. The Court held that the dealer had no intention to evade any tax; the minor discrepancy in the vehicle registration in the e-way bill would not attract penalty proceedings under Section 129. The department also did not place before the Court any other material to bring on record that the dealer intended to evade tax, except the incorrect mention of part of the vehicle registration number in the e-way bill. The Court therefore set-aside the order imposing penalty under Section 129. The Allahabad High Court laid down identical principles in 2023 (71) GSTL 4 (Varun Beverages Vs. State of UP). (14) The reasoning of the appellate authority that mens rea is not a relevant factor for invoking Section 129 of the Act is rejected in view of the decision of the Supreme Court in the case of Satyam Shivam Papers Private Limited (cited supra), of the Kerala High Court in the case of Podaran Foods India Limited (cited supra), of the Karnataka High Court in the case of BVM Trans Solutions Private Limited v. Commercial Tax Officer (cited supra) and of the Allahabad High Court in the case of BMR Enterprises v. State of UP (cited supra) and Varun Beverages Limited Vs. State Vs. UP (cited supra). (15) We have verified the e-way bill and the delivery challan. The e-way bill and the delivery challan contain all the information as required under Rule 138 and Rule 55 of the CGST Rules. Rule 138 requires the e-way bill to include the vehicle number used to transport the goods. The only discrepancy noticed was the vehicle registration number mentioned in Part B of the e-way bill. The delivery challan otherwise conformed to Rule 55 of the CGST Rules. Furnishing correct conveyance particulars under Rule 138 of the CGST Rules is a statutory requirement and cannot be regarded as an empty formality. Nevertheless, the nature and extent of the discrepancy cannot, by themselves, establish an intention to evade tax. The appellant’s explanation, the accompanying documents, and the other circumstances surrounding the transportation must be examined cumulatively. (16) We are inclined to accept the appellant’s explanation because physical verification of the consignment revealed no defects, and also the authorities had not relied on any other material to establish that the appellant intended to evade tax payment. The appellant had no intention to evade tax payment. The authorities have relied only on a mismatch in the vehicle number to justify imposing a penalty under Section 129 of the Act. Applying the principles laid down in the above-said cases, we are of the view that levy of penalty under Section 129 of the Act is unwarranted in the facts and circumstances of the case. The impugned order is accordingly set-aside. Order (i) The appeal is allowed. (ii) The order passed by the Appellate Authority in GST AP. No.396/24-25 dated 31.03.2025, is set-aside. (iii) The respondent is directed to give effect to this order within a period of 8 weeks from the date of this order.
