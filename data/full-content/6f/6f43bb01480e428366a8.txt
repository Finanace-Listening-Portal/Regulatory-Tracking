Summary: Filing an income tax return requires careful selection of the applicable ITR form, accurate reporting of income, proper disclosure of financial transactions and timely verification. For financial year 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27), taxpayers must follow the applicable provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961, even though the Income-tax Act, 2025 commenced on 1 April 2026. The Income Tax Department has made ITR-1 to ITR-7 available for AY 2026-27, with revised eligibility conditions for certain forms. Taxpayers should verify their residential status, select the appropriate tax regime, reconcile Form 26AS with the Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS), and ensure that all income, deductions, taxes paid and other reportable particulars are correctly disclosed. Income tax returns are generally filed electronically without attaching supporting documents, although records must be retained and specified audit reports or other forms may require separate electronic submission. Refunds are ordinarily credited to validated bank accounts nominated on the e-Filing portal. Returns must also be verified within the prescribed period, generally 30 days, to avoid invalidity or late filing consequences. Special verification requirements apply to companies, firms and other entities under Section 140. Taxpayers must additionally consider the deadlines for original, belated, revised and updated returns. This article provides practical guidance on selecting the correct form, preparing information, filing and verifying returns, avoiding common errors and ensuring compliance with the applicable income tax provisions. Select the Correct Income Tax Return Form Before filing an income tax return, every taxpayer must identify the appropriate ITR form based on residential status, sources of income, taxpayer category and other eligibility conditions. ITR form Broad applicability ITR-1 (Sahaj) Eligible resident individuals with total income up to ₹50 lakh and specified income sources ITR-2 Individuals and HUFs without income from profits and gains of business or profession ITR-3 Individuals and HUFs having business or professional income ITR-4 (Sugam) Eligible resident individuals, HUFs and firms other than LLPs having qualifying presumptive income ITR-5 Firms, LLPs, AOPs, BOIs and other specified persons ITR-6 Companies other than those claiming exemption under Section 11 ITR-7 Specified persons required to furnish returns under Sections 139(4A) to 139(4D) Changes in ITR Forms for AY 2026-27 The eligibility for ITR-1 and ITR-4 has been expanded in specified circumstances. For example, eligible taxpayers may report income from up to two house properties and specified long-term capital gains under Section 112A up to ₹1.25 lakh, subject to the conditions of the respective forms. The availability of these features does not mean that all taxpayers having capital gains can use ITR-1 or ITR-4. Taxpayers should examine the relevant form instructions before filing. No Supporting Documents Are Generally Attached to ITR Under Rule 12(2) of the Income-tax Rules, 1962, returns are generally furnished without attaching supporting documents. Accordingly, taxpayers ordinarily need not attach Form 16, investment proofs, TDS certificates, bank statements, tax payment challans or computation statements to their income tax returns. However, the absence of an attachment requirement does not remove the obligation to maintain supporting records. Separate Filing of Audit Reports and Other Forms Where applicable, tax audit reports under Section 44AB, transfer pricing reports under Section 92E and other prescribed forms must be furnished electronically in accordance with their respective provisions. Taxpayers should not assume that filing the income tax return automatically satisfies separate reporting obligations. 3. Verify Personal Information and Bank Account Details Incorrect personal or banking information can result in processing difficulties, refund failures and communication problems. Taxpayers should check their PAN, name, date of birth, contact information, address, residential status and bank account details before submitting the return. Pre-Validate Bank Account for Income Tax Refund Income tax refunds are generally credited electronically to a bank account that has been pre-validated and nominated for refund on the e-Filing portal. Taxpayers should ensure that the bank account is active, correctly linked to PAN and successfully validated. The earlier system of refund bankers sending physical refund instruments to taxpayers’ communication addresses should not be treated as the normal current procedure. Reconcile Form 26AS, AIS and TIS Before Filing Taxpayers should not rely exclusively on Form 16 or automatically pre-filled information. They should reconcile the return with Form 26AS, the Annual Information Statement and the Taxpayer Information Summary. Particular attention should be given to salary, bank interest, fixed deposit interest, dividends, capital gains, TDS, TCS, advance tax and self-assessment tax. What if AIS Contains Incorrect Information? Where AIS contains an incorrect or duplicate transaction, taxpayers may submit feedback through the prescribed facility. However, income must be reported according to the applicable law and actual records, rather than simply copying or excluding entries based on AIS. 5. Choose the Appropriate Tax Regime For AY 2026-27, the new tax regime under Section 115BAC is the default regime for eligible taxpayers. However, the old regime may be more beneficial in certain circumstances, particularly where substantial deductions or exemptions are available. Taxpayers should compare the liability under both regimes before filing. Special Rules for Business and Professional Income Taxpayers having business or professional income are subject to specific conditions for opting out of the default regime, including the requirement to furnish Form 10-IEA where applicable. The timing and consequences of exercising the option differ from those applicable to taxpayers without business or professional income. 6. File the Income Tax Return Electronically Income tax returns are generally furnished electronically through the official Income Tax Department portal. The principal filing methods include submission using a digital signature certificate, submission followed by electronic verification, and electronic submission followed by verification through ITR-V. When Is Paper Filing Permitted? Paper filing is permitted only in limited circumstances under Rule 12. An individual aged 80 years or more during the relevant previous year may be eligible to furnish a paper return in ITR-1 or ITR-4, subject to the prescribed conditions. This exception should not be confused with the earlier, broader paper-filing rules that applied to certain taxpayers having income up to ₹5 lakh. Companies and other taxpayers for whom digital signature filing is mandatory must comply with the prescribed electronic filing requirements. 7. Verify the Income Tax Return Within 30 Days Uploading an income tax return does not, by itself, complete the filing process. The return must be verified through an authorised method within the prescribed period. Available Verification Methods Aadhaar OTP, where applicable. Electronic Verification Code through an eligible validated bank or demat account. Net banking or other supported electronic methods. Digital Signature Certificate. Submission of a duly signed ITR-V to the Centralised Processing Centre, where permitted. The general time limit for verification is 30 days from the date of uploading the return. Consequences of Delayed Verification Where verification is completed after the prescribed 30-day period, the date of verification may be treated as the date of furnishing the return, with applicable consequences for late filing. A return remaining unverified may be treated as invalid, subject to the prescribed condonation facility. Taxpayers should preferably verify the return immediately after submission. 8. Who Can Sign and Verify an Income Tax Return? Section 140 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 specifies the persons authorised to verify returns for different categories of taxpayers. Taxpayer Person ordinarily authorised to verify Individual Individual taxpayer, subject to statutory exceptions HUF Karta or another eligible adult member in specified circumstances Company Managing Director or another authorised person under the statutory conditions Firm Managing Partner or eligible partner where permitted LLP Designated Partner or another eligible partner in specified circumstances Local authority Principal Officer AOP Member or Principal Officer, as applicable Verification by an Authorised Representative Where the law permits verification by another person, the prescribed authorisation and statutory conditions must be satisfied. Merely holding a general power of attorney does not automatically establish eligibility to verify every category of return. The person verifying the return must ensure that the declarations are true and complete. Knowingly making false statements in verification may attract consequences under Section 277, subject to the applicable legal requirements. 9. Income Tax Return Filing Deadlines for AY 2026-27 The applicable filing deadline depends on the taxpayer’s category. Category Statutory due date Specified individuals and HUFs not requiring audit 31 July 2026 Specified non-audit business and professional taxpayers 31 August 2026 Companies and other specified audit cases 31 October 2026 Specified transfer pricing cases requiring a report under Section 92E 30 November 2026 Extension update: CBDT Circular No. 07/2026 extended the filing deadline for specified audit cases from 31 October 2026 to 21 November 2026, and the corresponding audit-report deadline from 30 September 2026 to 21 October 2026. Taxpayers must consider applicable extensions before determining whether a return has been filed late. 10. Belated, Revised and Updated Income Tax Returns Belated Return Under Section 139(4) Taxpayers who miss their applicable due date may generally file a belated return up to 31 December 2026 or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier. Late filing fees under Section 234F and interest under Section 234A may apply, subject to the statutory conditions. Revised Return Under Section 139(5) Taxpayers who discover omissions or incorrect particulars in an original or belated return may generally revise it up to 31 March 2027 or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier. For AY 2026-27, Section 234-I prescribes a fee for specified revised returns filed after 31 December 2026, subject to its conditions. Updated Return Under Section 139(8A) Eligible taxpayers may consider filing an updated return within the prescribed 48-month period from the end of the relevant assessment year. However, updated returns are subject to restrictions, including limitations relating to loss returns, refunds, reduction of tax liability and specified proceedings. Additional tax under Section 140B may also be payable. 11. Understand Processing Under Section 143(1) After a valid return is furnished, the Income Tax Department may process it under Section 143(1). The processing may result in acceptance of the returned income, adjustments permitted by law, determination of a refund or identification of tax payable. Acknowledgement Is Not the Same as Intimation An ITR acknowledgement confirms submission of the return, but it should not be treated as proof that the return has been processed or that all claims have been accepted. Taxpayers should monitor the e-Filing portal for processing status, intimations, refund information and any outstanding demand. 12. Filing Through a Tax Return Preparer or Professional Taxpayers may obtain assistance from an eligible Tax Return Preparer, Chartered Accountant or other authorised professional, depending on the nature of the services and applicable legal requirements. The earlier references to a fixed ₹250 Tax Return Preparer fee and government incentive structure should not be presented as universally applicable current requirements. Responsibilities of the Taxpayer Even where a professional prepares the return, the taxpayer should review the income reported, deductions claimed, tax credits, bank details and verification declarations. Professional assistance does not remove the taxpayer’s statutory responsibility for accurate reporting. 13. Common Mistakes to Avoid While Filing ITR for AY 2026-27 Selecting an incorrect ITR form. Reporting an incorrect residential status. Failing to disclose interest income or capital gains. Ignoring differences between Form 26AS, AIS, TIS and actual records. Selecting a tax regime without comparing the alternatives. Claiming deductions or rebates without satisfying eligibility conditions. Failing to disclose applicable foreign assets or foreign income. Entering an incorrect or unvalidated bank account. Omitting advance tax or self-assessment tax credits. Failing to furnish applicable audit reports or prescribed forms. Not verifying the return within the prescribed period. Ignoring notices, defective return communications or processing intimations. Related TaxGuru reading: 25 Practical Errors Found During ITR Filing That Every Taxpayer Should Avoid. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Which Income Tax Act applies to FY 2025-26? Income earned during FY 2025-26, corresponding to AY 2026-27, continues to be governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961 under the applicable transitional framework. 2. Is it mandatory to attach Form 16 to an income tax return? No. Income tax returns are generally filed without attaching Form 16 or other supporting documents. However, relevant records should be retained. 3. Can I file ITR without Form 16? Yes. Taxpayers can prepare returns using salary records, Form 26AS, AIS and other relevant information, provided the income and tax particulars are correctly reported. 4. Is bank account pre-validation necessary for receiving refunds? Yes. Taxpayers should pre-validate and nominate an eligible bank account for receiving income tax refunds electronically. 5. Can a taxpayer file an income tax return on paper? Only in limited circumstances permitted under Rule 12, including specified cases involving individuals aged 80 years or more. 6. What is the time limit for e-verifying an ITR? The general time limit is 30 days from uploading the return, subject to the applicable procedural rules. 7. What happens if the return is not verified? An unverified return may be treated as invalid. Delayed verification may also affect the date of filing and attract applicable consequences. 8. Can a belated return be revised? Yes. A belated return can generally be revised within the prescribed time under Section 139(5). 9. What is the revised return deadline for AY 2026-27? The ordinary deadline is 31 March 2027 or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier. 10. Can an income tax return be filed after the belated return deadline? An eligible taxpayer may consider the updated return facility under Section 139(8A), subject to statutory restrictions and additional tax. 11. Is ITR acknowledgement proof that the return has been processed? No. Acknowledgement of filing and processing under Section 143(1) are separate matters. 12. Who is responsible for errors where a professional files the return? The taxpayer remains responsible for ensuring that the return and its verification contain accurate information, without prejudice to any separate professional responsibilities. Key Takeaways Select the correct ITR form according to income sources and eligibility. FY 2025-26 returns are governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961. Compare the old and new tax regimes before filing. Reconcile income and tax credits with Form 26AS, AIS and TIS. Income tax returns are generally filed without supporting attachments. Pre-validate and nominate the bank account for refunds. Verify the return within the prescribed 30-day period. Ensure that the return is verified by a person authorised under Section 140. Check applicable CBDT extensions and filing deadlines. Monitor processing status and respond to departmental communications. Retain records supporting income, deductions, exemptions and tax payments.
