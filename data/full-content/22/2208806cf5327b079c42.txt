Articles 3 min read ‘Only premium, no payout’: Despite drought, scores of farmers in Pune district avoid crop insurance Farmers in Pune district face challenges in enrolling for crop insurance after experiencing a difficult monsoon season. Limited enrollment under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana has raised concerns among officials and farmers alike. Many farmers distrust the insurance process due to past experiences with claim settlements and compensation. District agriculture authorities are aiming to increase insurance coverage significantly but face obstacles. Despite the anxieties, scores of farmers in Pune district, remain reluctant to enrol under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), with distrust over insurance companies and claim settlement processes emerging as major hurdles.Officials received only 599 applications covering 392 hectares between Nov 2 and Nov 11, a worryingly low number considering the scale of cultivation in the district. Officials fear tens of thousands of farmers may find themselves without monetary support if the next season’s sowing runs into trouble. Against an average rabi cultivation area of 1.87 lakh hectares last year, only around 2,000 hectares were insured. Advt The district agriculture office has set a target of bringing at least 25,000 hectares under insurance cover this season and has asked field officials to reach out to farmers in drought-affected tehsils in the district.But farmers said their reluctance comes from past experience of how claims were assessed and settled. Many said paying premiums did not necessarily guarantee adequate compensation.“Farmers are repeatedly told to insure their crops, but when losses do occur, getting compensation becomes a difficult exercise. There is no clarity on how losses are assessed or how much money will be credited. Why should we spend money on insurance if there is no assurance of settling a claim?” said Sagar Holgunde, a farmer from a drought-affected village in Shirur tehsil.Another cultivator said farmers were already struggling to recover their investments following kharif crop losses. “We spent a lot of money on seeds, fertilisers, pesticides and labour, but deficient rainfall affected yields. Now govt wants us to take insurance, but the experience of many farmers, who didn’t get satisfactory compensation, has made us sceptical. Insurance should provide security and not become another formality,” said Rahul Thorat from Daund tehsil.District agriculture officer Vaibhav Tambe said efforts were underway to increase coverage. “The main reason for low enrollment is poor understanding of how crop insurance works. Farmers feel the companies don’t hand out compensations,” he told TOI, adding that the premium was affordable, costing farmers less than Rs 100 per acre. Advt Agriculture experts TOI spoke to said crop insurance is particularly important during periods of rainfall uncertainty as it provides financial support after losses and helps stop heavy borrowing. But they stressed that the credibility of insurance policies depends on transparent loss assessment, timely claim settlement and effective grievance redressal.“Awareness campaigns alone will not resolve the problem,” one expert said. “Farmers need to see that claims are being assessed fairly with compensation reaching eligible beneficiaries without delays. Some farmers’ organisations have been fighting legal battles with crop insurance companies for years. So many wish to avoid these legal hassles. Their confidence will only improve when insurance mechanisms become transparent and accountable,” the expert added. Advt Between Nov 2 and Nov 11 in the district, onion drew the highest number of insurance applications, followed by jowar, chickpea, maize and wheat.Relief under PMFBY is also available to farmers who have not taken loans. Farmers must insure only the crops actually sown in their fields. Loanee farmers who do not wish to participate must submit a written declaration to their respective banks at least seven days before the enrolment deadline to prevent deduction of the insurance premium.A senior official from the agriculture department said with officials now targeting a more than 12-fold increase over last year’s insured area, the challenge was not merely to increase registrations but to also rebuild farmers’ confidence in crop insurance. “Unless concerns over claim settlements are addressed, the scheme could continue to see limited participation, even when farmers face the greatest need for financial protection,” the official said.
