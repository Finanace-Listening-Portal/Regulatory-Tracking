investment causing the currency to be far more dependent on volatile foreign portfolio inflows than in the past, according to MUFG Bank Ltd. “Higher rates are one way to resolve the fundamental imbalance and is one key reason why we are forecasting RBI to hike rates further,” said Michael Wan, senior currency analyst at the lender.Hiking MSF RateThe RBI can raise its emergency lending rate for banks, making funds more expensive and tightening liquidity to support the rupee. The so-called Marginal Standing Facility currently stands 25 basis points above the key policy rate. The RBI last raised the MSF rate independently of the repo rate to defend the rupee in 2013. During the taper tantrum, the rate was raised by 200 basis points, widening the spread over the policy rate. The measure briefly slowed the rupee’s slide. “Enhanced corridor on the upper side by increasing MSF rate will result in higher price of the rupee,” said Soumya Kanti Ghosh, chief economic adviser at State Bank of India.Long-term Capital Gains Tax CutA cut in the long-term capital gains on stocks could bring inflows and support the rupee, according to IIFL Capital. Foreigners have been relentless sellers of Indian stocks with net outflows exceeding $30 billion so far this year as investors shifted funds toward markets offering greater exposure to the artificial-intelligence trade. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said at the Bloomberg New Economy Forum on Tuesday that “the long-term capital gains tax is something on which a lot of people have been commenting,” but she would “tread cautiously” on the question of taxation very close to the budget. Greater Exchange Rate FlexibilityThe central bank should also look at allowing a more flexible exchange rate, according to Nomura Holdings Inc. and Emkay Global Financial Services. “A durable solution requires greater exchange-rate flexibility, allowing the rupee to adjust gradually rather than through episodic corrections,” said Madhavi Arora, chief economist at Emkay. The central bank has been intervening in the currency market in recent weeks to check rupee volatility. The RBI has repeatedly said that it doesn’t target a specific level for the currency. Lower Exporter Proceed Realisation TimelineAuthorities could further shorten the deadline for exporters to bring their overseas earnings back to India to six months, in line with the typical credit period for export payments, according to a State Bank of India note. The RBI had cut the timeline to nine months in June after extending it to 15 months last year when the US imposed punitive tariffs on India.Raising Fuel PricesRaising domestic fuel prices to reduce fiscal pressure and consumer demand for oil is an option, Nomura economists including Sonal Varma wrote in a note. Such as move can curb fuel demand over time, potentially reducing India’s oil-import bill and demand for dollars, thereby supporting the rupee. India’s state run refiners that command over 90% of the market have kept diesel and gasoline prices unchanged since May 25. Private retailers, however, have raised prices. State refiners are losing ₹530 crore daily on discounted fuel sales, according to Icra Ltd. Lowering LRS LimitsIndividuals in India can send foreign exchange for travel and education among other things under the so-called Liberalized Remittance Scheme. Currently, the annual limit for such transfers is $250,000. Tightening the LRS, as was done in 2013, is an option, according to an Ambit Capital note by analysts Swayamsiddha Panda and Eashaan Nair.
