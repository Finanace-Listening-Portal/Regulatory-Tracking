Articles 6 min read Indian Bank plans premium repositioning of credit card business, targets Q3 launch: MD Binod Kumar Indian Bank plans to launch a premium segment credit card in the third quarter of FY27 as it looks to reposition its credit card business, Managing Director & CEO Binod Kumar told ETBFSI. Kumar also spoke about FCNR(B) deployment, rate hikes and margins, growth guidance, IRDAI’s proposed distribution norms, the bank’s insurance plans, RAM growth, and impact of drought on agri loans. How has Indian Bank deployed the FCNR(B) funds raised, and how much of the deposits have effectively replaced existing funding versus going towards credit?Binod Kumar: By September, all the deployment of FCNR(B) was over. I had said that Rs 10,000 crore of bulk deposits came down in September itself.Normally, we raise around Rs 10,000–11,000 crore through retail term deposits, but this time it was only around Rs 5,000–6,000 crore. So, around Rs 5,000 crore of that growth did not happen. That makes it around Rs 15,000 crore of deposit replacement, while another Rs 6,000 crore has gone towards credit. At present, I do not have surplus liquidity; rather, I am on the borrowing side. Advt Q. With the rate cycle changing, liquidity tightening and other factors such as FERR coming into play, how does your strategy on NIM, deposits and credit growth change for FY27?Binod Kumar: First of all, because of the rate hike, there will be some expansion in NIM. A 25 basis point rate hike will have an annual impact of around 5–6 basis points. For Q3, it will be around 7–8 basis points. If there is another 25 basis point hike, you can add another 4–5 basis points.However, before a second rate hike, once the surplus liquidity position is squeezed, bulk deposit rates will start moving up first, followed by retail term deposit rates. So, the expansion in NIM will also moderate.Despite the rate going up, I do not see any respite in growth. Growth will continue. That is why I have revised the deposit growth guidance from 9–11 per cent earlier to 11–12 per cent, and advance growth from 11–13 per cent to 13–14 per cent.For the first half, year-on-year credit growth is already around 16 per cent. So, I think I will be able to do more than even 13–14 per cent.Q. What impact do you expect from IRDAI’s proposed distribution norms on Indian Bank’s bancassurance income?Binod Kumar: We approximately have Rs 200 crore of bancassurance income, so the bank is not going to be impacted much. There will be some impact on life as well as non-life, but overall, I think the combined impact may be around Rs 30–40 crore, which will not be very substantial. Advt Q. With MDR charges becoming applicable, how do you see the opportunity for Indian Bank on the payments side?Binod Kumar: I would rather like to use this opportunity to increase my base. If you see UPI usage, not only at my bank but across banks, usage of the banks’ own UPI apps is very low.The customer may be a bank customer, but they are using the UPI app of some other third party. I will try to use this opportunity to increase my own base. If customers use my app, whatever we are getting out of that, we may pass on some concession to them.So, I will use this opportunity to increase my base. Even if, by passing on some benefit, I can increase my base, I will be happier. Advt Q. What is the progress on Indian Bank’s proposed life insurance venture, and do you remain committed to it despite IRDAI’s proposed distribution norms?Binod Kumar: We have taken board approval and will now move for various regulatory approvals. But it will take time. I do not see it happening before one-and-a-half to two years. There will be due diligence and valuation issues as well.I still stand firm on the insurance plan. What I believe in is value creation. It may be temporary that income does not come or income may be lower, but if I can create an insurance company that ultimately becomes a good company, value creation will also happen through valuation. It is not only about income. Advt My intention is to have a minimum 26 per cent stake. Income is one consideration, but value creation is the other. Also, in any case, my customers are using somebody else’s products. So why not have my own product? That is the intention.Q. Are you open to a domestic or foreign partner for the insurance venture, and have you started evaluating potential partners?Binod Kumar: I have an open mind—domestic or foreign—wherever I get good value. But not now. I have not started engaging because unless I have at least an in-principle nod, there is no point in talking.RBI, DFS and IRDAI will all have to be kept in the loop. There may also be some other regulator involved. Since I am a listed entity, I will have to see whether SEBI comes into the picture.Q. How are you looking at the retail and corporate loan mix going forward?Binod Kumar: RAM growth will be in the range of 15–17 per cent, while corporate growth will be in the lower double digit and its share will increase. I will endeavour to maintain it in the range of around 65–67 per cent.Q. What impact are you seeing from drought in states such as Karnataka and Maharashtra, particularly on agriculture and vehicle loans?Binod Kumar: If there is any impact, the only thing that will happen is payment deferment. Whatever has to be paid because of drought will be deferred, as decided by SLBC. So, there will be tenor deferment.Fortunately, I do not have very high exposure to agriculture in these states. The only impact will be that repayment will be delayed. Since it will be coming through RBI, we will also not be required to make additional provisions.Q. You have spoken about scaling the credit card business. What is your strategy for the segment, what kind of product are you looking to launch, and will this eventually be housed in a separate subsidiary?Binod Kumar: Credit card will be scaled up. First of all, what we are doing is that I will completely try to reposition myself, would like to place it in the upper bracket, the premium segment. Bank has already made a product, but I was not satisfied, so I have asked them to rework it.My endeavour will be to launch it in Q3. If we can really scale to a good number, only then can we think of a subsidiary. That will depend on the numbers and whether it makes commercial sense.
