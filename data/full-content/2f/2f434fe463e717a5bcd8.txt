Summary: The 57th GST Council Meeting held on 8 October 2026 proposed removing the existing restriction on input tax credit relating to free samples under Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017. Presently, Section 17(5)(h) generally blocks ITC attributable to goods distributed without consideration as free samples, even when the distribution serves genuine business-promotion purposes. The proposed relaxation may benefit pharmaceutical manufacturers, FMCG businesses, cosmetic companies, food manufacturers, chemical industries and other businesses distributing product samples for testing, demonstration and promotion. CBIC Circular No. 92/11/2019-GST clarifies that ordinary free samples generally do not constitute taxable outward supplies unless Schedule I applies, while the corresponding ITC is restricted under the existing law. The proposed change may permit businesses to retain qualifying ITC on such samples, subject to Section 16 conditions and the final statutory wording. However, independent free samples must be distinguished from gifts, genuine buy-one-get-one schemes, deemed supplies under Schedule I and goods consumed during product testing. The proposal also raises questions concerning outward GST liability where credit is availed on goods subsequently distributed without consideration. Illustrative calculations demonstrate the potential benefit of retaining previously blocked credit, but eligibility will depend on proper invoices, business purpose, stock records, credit attribution and other statutory requirements. The Council has not expressly recommended retrospective relief for earlier financial years. As of 20 October 2026, the proposed amendment had not become operative, and the final legislation will determine its scope, commencement and transitional treatment. Table of Contents▸ Introduction 1. The proposal is particularly relevant for pharmaceutical, FMCG, cosmetic, food, chemical, textile and other businesses that regularly distribute genuine product samples for promotion, testing or demonstration. However, the proposed relaxation should not be treated as an automatic entitlement in every free-distribution case, because the final statutory wording, Section 16 conditions, Schedule I deemed-supply rules, distinction between free samples and gifts, treatment of BOGO schemes, exempt-supply implications and outward GST liability may still affect the final tax position. This proposed relaxation is particularly important for pharmaceutical companies, FMCG manufacturers, cosmetic companies, food-product manufacturers, chemical industries, textile businesses and other companies regularly distributing free samples to promote their products. Under the existing GST law, free samples distributed without consideration are generally not treated as taxable outward supplies. However, ITC attributable to these samples is specifically blocked under Section 17(5)(h), even where they are distributed for genuine business promotion. The proposed amendment seeks to remove this restriction, potentially allowing businesses to retain ITC on goods distributed as promotional samples. Important: The GST Council has recommended removal of the ITC restriction relating to free samples under Section 17(5). Since free samples are presently covered by Section 17(5)(h), the proposed reform is expected to affect that provision. However, the exact statutory wording and effective date will be known only when the amendment is enacted and brought into force. 1. Existing Legal Position under Section 17(5)(h) Section 17(5)(h) presently provides that ITC shall not be available in respect of goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off or disposed of by way of gift or free samples. Consequently, where a manufacturer distributes products free of cost for promotional purposes, the ITC attributable to those products is generally blocked. This restriction applies even where samples are distributed exclusively for increasing turnover, introducing products or promoting future taxable sales. It should not automatically be interpreted as removing every restriction contained in Section 17(5)(h). 2. CBIC Circular No. 92/11/2019-GST – Existing Departmental Clarification CBIC Circular No. 92/11/2019-GST, dated 7 March 2019, explains the existing GST treatment of free samples, gifts and sales-promotion schemes. It establishes two important principles. First – Outward GST Liability Goods supplied free of cost, without consideration, are generally not considered a supply under Section 7 of the CGST Act, unless the transaction falls within Schedule I. Thus, ordinary free samples distributed to unrelated customers or medical practitioners generally do not attract outward GST. Second – Input Tax Credit Under the existing Section 17(5)(h), ITC attributable to inputs, input services and capital goods used in relation to free samples distributed without consideration is generally unavailable. The Circular further recognises that where free distribution itself constitutes a deemed taxable supply under Schedule I, the corresponding ITC may be available. Two separate questions therefore arise: whether GST is payable on outward distribution of samples; and whether ITC relating to those samples can be claimed or retained. The proposed amendment primarily addresses the second question; it does not automatically change Section 7. Consequential effect on the Circular: Once the proposed statutory amendment becomes effective, the existing clarification in Circular No. 92/11/2019-GST regarding denial of ITC on free samples will have to be read subject to the amended statutory provision and any consequential clarification or revised circular issued by CBIC. 3. How Can ITC Be Allowed When Free Samples Are Not Treated as Sales? Section 16(1) does not require every inward purchase to result directly in a taxable outward sale. It permits ITC on qualifying goods or services used or intended for use in the course or furtherance of business, subject to other statutory provisions. Businesses incur expenditure on advertising, promotional campaigns, marketing and product development without receiving separate consideration for each activity. Similarly, free samples may be distributed to promote future taxable sales. Example – Pharmaceutical Samples Particulars GST treatment Medicines distributed to doctors Free of cost Consideration received Nil Whether treated as an ordinary taxable sale Generally no Whether outward GST is payable Generally no, unless Schedule I applies Business purpose Promotion of taxable products Section 16(1) business-use requirement May be satisfied ITC under existing Section 17(5)(h) Generally unavailable ITC after proposed amendment Potentially eligible, subject to final statutory wording and applicable conditions The essential distinction is between a taxable sale and expenditure incurred for business purposes. A sample may not itself constitute an outward taxable supply, but the expenditure to manufacture and distribute it can still be connected with taxable business. The proposal seeks to remove the specific block that currently prevents ITC on these promotional goods. 4. FMCG and Consumer-Products Sector The proposed relaxation may similarly benefit makers of soaps, shampoos, detergents, cosmetics, packaged foods and other consumer products. After the proposed amendment, the company may become eligible to retain this ITC, subject to the final statutory wording and other applicable conditions. Difference Between Product Samples and Advertising Services Where a company pays an advertising agency ₹2,00,000 plus GST to conduct a promotional campaign, the GST on ordinary qualifying advertising services may already be available under Section 16. Advertising services are not generally blocked merely because they promote products. However, goods actually disposed of as free samples, together with attributable inputs and services, are specifically affected by Section 17(5)(h). Businesses should distinguish the two categories. 8. Buy One Get One Free – ITC Already Allowed under Existing Law A genuine buy-one-get-one-free scheme is generally treated differently from independent free distribution. CBIC Circular No. 92/11/2019-GST explains that where two or more goods are provided for a single consideration, the transaction is not necessarily an independently free supply of the second item. Example A soap manufacturer offers two soaps for ₹100. The customer pays one price for the combined package. Although advertised as one soap being free, the package is supplied for consideration. ITC relating to the goods is generally already available, subject to ordinary requirements. Free Samples versus Gifts The Council has specifically recommended removing the restriction on free samples; its announcement has not expressly confirmed general relief for gifts. Putting a company logo on an item does not automatically turn a gift into a product sample. 10. Does a Free Sample Become an Exempt Supply? Section 2(47) defines exempt supply by reference to nil-rated supplies, notified exemptions and non-taxable supplies. But where free goods are distributed without consideration and Schedule I does not apply, distribution is generally not a supply under Section 7. Therefore, an ordinary free sample should not automatically be treated as an exempt outward supply merely because no GST is payable. There is a substantial legal basis for arguing that it should not automatically cause an exempt-supply reversal under Section 17(2). Instead, sampling may be an activity supporting taxable business. Currently, however, Section 17(5)(h) expressly blocks the attributable ITC despite the business-use argument. Once amended, the interaction of Sections 16 and 17(2) will become more significant. The final legal wording will govern. 11. Important Legal Issue – Schedule I and Deemed Supply Schedule I treats certain transactions as supplies without consideration. Paragraph 1 covers permanent transfer or disposal of business assets where ITC has been availed on those assets. A question may arise once the free-sample ITC restriction is removed: If a business purchases finished products, claims ITC and later distributes them freely as samples, could that transfer constitute a deemed taxable supply under Schedule I? Facts may differ between: purchased finished goods with directly availed ITC; and manufactured samples using raw materials and input services. Existing Circular No. 92/11/2019-GST treats ordinary free samples as generally outside supply unless Schedule I applies, but the interaction with any new credit entitlement will have to be considered. Therefore, it would be premature to conclude that every free-sample transaction will automatically enjoy both full ITC and no outward GST liability. The final amendment or a consequential CBIC clarification should address this interaction. 12. Will Outward GST Be Payable on Free Samples after the Amendment? The proposed relaxation relates to Section 17(5), presently affecting free samples through Section 17(5)(h). It does not automatically amend Section 7 or Schedule I. Existing principles for determining outward supply therefore remain relevant. ITC should be based on actual eligible input tax attributable to samples, not merely a GST rate applied to their MRP or notional selling price. 14. Whether ITC of Earlier Financial Years Can Be Reclaimed The Council has not expressly recommended retrospective application of the free-sample relaxation. Companies therefore cannot presently reclaim historically blocked ITC solely because the Council has recommended removing the restriction. The final amendment must be reviewed to determine: whether relief is prospective or retrospective; whether transitional provisions apply; how Section 16(4) time limits apply; and whether credit previously availed and reversed is treated differently from credit never availed. Until the change becomes effective, taxpayers should follow the existing Section 17(5)(h). Conclusion The proposed ITC relaxation relating to free samples, recommended by the GST Council on 8 October 2026, represents meaningful potential relief for genuine product sampling and promotion. Under existing law, ITC attributable to free samples is generally blocked despite their use in promoting taxable business. The proposed change seeks to remove that restriction. Section 16(1) permits credit for qualifying business inputs without requiring each purchase to result directly in a separate taxable outward sale. Nevertheless, the treatment of free samples must be distinguished from genuine single-price buy-one-get-one schemes, gifts and deemed supplies under Schedule I. The interaction between Sections 7, 16, 17(2), 17(5)(h) and Schedule I remains relevant in assessing whether both ITC and absence of separate output GST are supportable. The exact legislative drafting is still awaited. Consequently, businesses should not assume that every item currently falling within Section 17(5)(h), particularly gifts, will receive the same treatment merely because the Council has recommended relief for free samples. As of 20 October 2026, the proposed amendment has not been brought into force.
