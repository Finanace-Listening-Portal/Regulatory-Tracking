Articles 3 min read Debit cards may get UPI MDR boost in higher value payments, but not reverse decline The introduction of MDR on higher value UPI merchant payments could give debit cards some breathing room in larger transactions, but a broad based comeback is unlikely as UPI remains free for payments up to Rs 2,000 and debit card usage is projected to continue declining. It will be capped at Rs 300 for transactions of Rs 75,000 and above, while consumers cannot be charged for the MDR. UPI payments up to Rs 2,000 will remain outside the levy.The opportunity for debit cards is concentrated in higher value payments. Only about 4% of P2M UPI transactions in FY26 were above Rs 2,000, but these transactions accounted for around two thirds of the total transaction value, according to data reported by The Indian Express. This makes the higher value segment particularly relevant for card issuers and networks as merchants begin to factor the cost of UPI payments into their payment choices. Advt The introduction of MDR does not, however, mean merchants will automatically shift from UPI to debit cards. The 0.4% UPI MDR is still lower than the charges generally associated with card payments, while UPI retains a significant convenience advantage through QR based payments and direct bank account transfers.More attractiveThe new pricing structure could nevertheless make debit cards relatively more attractive for some higher ticket purchases, particularly where banks offer cashback, reward points or merchant discounts. For consumers, the choice could increasingly depend on the relative value of these benefits rather than simply on the cost of the payment instrument.Debit cards are already becoming more concentrated in higher value transactions. PwC's Payments Handbook 2026 estimates that the average debit card transaction size rose to Rs 3,480 in FY26 from Rs 1,647 in FY21 and could reach Rs 4,000 by FY31.At the same time, debit card usage is expected to continue falling. PwC projects debit card transactions to decline to 851 million by FY31 from 1.28 billion in FY26. Average annual usage per debit card is projected to fall to 0.8 transactions from 1.2 during the same period.This suggests that the new UPI MDR may reinforce an existing trend rather than reverse it. Debit cards could increasingly be used for higher value purchases while UPI dominates everyday, low value transactions. Advt RuPay debit cards could have an additional advantage because the government has explicitly protected RuPay powered debit card payments from charges, alongside UPI transactions up to Rs 2,000. This could improve the relative attractiveness of RuPay for some transactions, although actual usage will depend on merchant acceptance and the rewards offered by banks.The distinction between transaction volume and value will therefore become more important. UPI is likely to retain its dominance in the large number of small payments, while debit cards could retain a role in larger transactions where card rewards, offers and established acceptance infrastructure provide an incentive to use them. Advt The new MDR could consequently give debit cards a shot at higher value payments, but not a comeback. The structural advantages that drove the shift towards UPI remain intact, while the latest PwC projections suggest debit card usage will continue to decline even as the average value of each transaction rises.
