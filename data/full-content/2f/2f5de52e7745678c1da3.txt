Articles 2 min read Over 30% midsize firms have no risk-management solutions for trade, climate risks; 77% use insurance for cyber risks: Report More than 30% of midsize enterprises in India have no risk-management solutions in place to deal with risks arising from global trade realignment and climate change, even as insurance remains a prominent tool for managing cyber risks, according to a recent report by ICICI Lombard & IRM India. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Nov 23, 2026 at 05:46 PM IST var news_msid = 136102070; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook A latest report by ICICI Lombard and IRM India found that 33% of midsize organisations had no solutions to manage trade war and global trade realignment risks, while 31.8% had no solutions for climate change risks. Macroeconomic risks were another area of concern, with 30.7% of respondents reporting no measures to address them.In contrast, 77.3% of midsize enterprises reported using insurance as a risk-management measure for cyber risks, compared with 55.7% that had adopted non-insurance measures. Only 8% said they had no solution in place to manage cyber risks.The findings highlighted a gap in the preparedness of midsize businesses, with organisations appearing better equipped to manage established risks than emerging threats linked to climate change, geopolitical developments and shifts in global trade. Advt Cyber, fire risks see greater use of insuranceCyber risks ranked among the key concerns for businesses, with the report identifying them as the top short-term risk for Indian industry. Despite this, the adoption of risk-management measures varies across categories.Apart from cyber risks, 84.1% of midsize enterprises reported using insurance to manage fire and explosion risks, while 72.7% used it for natural catastrophes. For business interruption, including supply-chain disruption, 60.2% reported using insurance as a risk-management measure.However, the reliance on insurance was relatively lower for emerging and broader business risks. Only 30.7% of midsize organisations reported using insurance to manage trade war and global trade realignment risks. The corresponding figure was 51.1% for climate change, 42% for technology and AI risks, and 26.1% for macroeconomic risks.The report noted that about a third of midsize organisations had no solutions for trade realignment, macroeconomic and climate change risks. Technology/AI and civil strife or geopolitical risks followed, with around 28% reporting no solutions.Climate change and AI expose preparedness gapsThe preparedness findings further underlined the challenges faced by midsize enterprises. Around 33% of respondents had neither a strategy nor capabilities in place to address climate change risks. The corresponding share was 29.5% for trade war and global trade realignment risks.Technology and AI risks also showed gaps, with 10.2% reporting no preparedness strategy or capabilities and 19.3% having a strategy but lacking the capabilities to implement it. Advt Climate change and technology/AI recorded the widest exposure gaps among midsize enterprises, indicating that preparedness is lagging behind the importance attached to these risks.The report is based on the ICICI Lombard-IRM Enterprise Risk Perception Survey 2026, which received 253 responses from organisations across sectors. The midsize enterprise analysis covers a segment of 88 respondents, defined as organisations with annual revenues of up to ₹2,000 crore.
