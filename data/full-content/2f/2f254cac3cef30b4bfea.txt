Advt "Things have changed slightly over the last couple of days, particularly after the US midterm elections. Geopolitical tensions have eased somewhat as negotiations have started, leading to a correction in oil prices," he said."Considering domestic and international conditions, I believe a 0.25 per cent hike is likely. In the previous policy, the RBI also changed its stance to calibrated tightening. Therefore, a gradual approach would be more appropriate," Pimple told PTI.The RBI's monetary policy committee is scheduled to meet between December 2 and December 4 to decide on the policy interest rate.The central bank increased rates by 0.25 per cent in the October monetary policy.The October hike was the first such move in nearly four years, and the RBI had also signaled that further rate hikes could follow amid rising inflation, a weak rupee and high crude oil prices.RBI also changed policy stance to calibrated tightening from neutral at the last policy review, and Governor Sanjay Malhotra had also made it clear that a rate cut is not in the offing.Expectations of aggressive monetary tightening have moderated following a correction in crude oil prices, which have settled below USD 100 per barrel, compared with levels of USD 115-118 seen earlier, he said. Advt Earlier, some market participants had expected rate hikes of 0.25-0.50 per cent, with cumulative increases of 0.75-1 per cent over the next couple of policies.However, Pimple said domestic inflation remains a concern, with food prices facing pressure from deficient monsoons and other supply-side constraints.He expects the apex bank to remain slightly hawkish on inflation, with its projections moving towards the upper end of the 4-6 per cent tolerance band.On the bond market, Pimple said a hawkish inflation projection alone may not push the 10-year benchmark government bond yield convincingly above 7.50 per cent, as expectations of elevated inflation have already been factored in. Advt The benchmark yield had earlier risen to around 7.40-7.48 per cent before correcting following the decline in crude oil prices.However, a fresh rise in crude oil prices above USD 100 per barrel could exert upward pressure on yields through its impact on inflation expectations.
