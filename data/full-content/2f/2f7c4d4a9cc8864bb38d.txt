An index fund does not promise the exact index return. Its net asset value reflects expenses, transaction costs, cash balances and timing differences, while the published index may use a different return convention. Equity-index exposure also retains market risk: diversification across constituent companies reduces single-stock concentration but does not eliminate the possibility of substantial broad-market losses. Tracking Difference Versus Tracking Error Tracking difference is the gap between a fund’s realised return and its benchmark return over a chosen period. Tracking error examines the variability of that gap across observations. SEBI’s investor education material explains tracking error as a measure of how closely a portfolio follows the index. The two measures answer different questions: one focuses on the return shortfall or excess, and the other on consistency of replication. When comparing funds, use the same benchmark return variant, period and observation frequency. A fund with a low stated expense ratio can still experience tracking slippage because of rebalancing, corporate actions, cash drag or execution costs. A single month’s apparent outperformance may reflect timing rather than manager skill. Investors should examine several periods, not select a fund from one isolated ranking. Direct and Regular Plans: The Cost Difference AMFI explains that direct and regular plans of the same scheme share the underlying portfolio but have different expense ratios because the regular plan includes distribution-related costs. This generally makes the direct plan’s expense ratio lower, although the actual rates must be checked in the latest disclosures. Investors should compare like-for-like growth options and not infer that a higher NAV means a fund is inherently more expensive. For example, if two otherwise identical plan options differ in annual recurring expenses, the effect can compound over many years. But it would be misleading to project a fixed future advantage without allowing for changes in expenses, index returns and cash flows. An investor who needs advice may separately evaluate the value and cost of a distributor’s services. The correct choice depends on both service requirements and total cost. SIP Execution and Portfolio Allocation A systematic investment plan automates purchases at periodic NAVs; it does not guarantee a profit or eliminate market risk. Investors should decide the allocation to Indian large-cap equities within their broader portfolio, taking account of emergency reserves, debt obligations, retirement horizon and existing equity exposure. A Nifty 50 index fund can overlap materially with other large-cap or diversified equity holdings. The portfolio-level question is not how many mutual fund schemes an investor owns but what underlying risks are duplicated. A combination of multiple Nifty 50 trackers may add administrative complexity without creating meaningful diversification. Review the aggregate exposure to sectors and large index constituents. If money may be needed within a short horizon, an equity index fund can be unsuitable irrespective of its low expense ratio. Redemption, Exit Load and Tax Records The applicable exit load, minimum investment, cut-off time and redemption processing rules must be confirmed from the current UTI scheme documents. A transaction submitted near a cut-off may receive a different NAV from what an investor expected. Capital gains tax treatment depends on the law effective at disposal, holding period and classification of the units; past rates should not be applied automatically to the 2026 tax year. Maintain investment dates, units, purchase values, redemptions and any distributions or switches. A switch between schemes or plans can have tax consequences and should not be treated as a purely administrative transfer without analysis. Where an investor has a large unrealised gain, staggered redemptions might affect cash-flow and tax timing, but decisions should be grounded in personal circumstances rather than assumed tax savings. Frequently Asked Questions Does an index fund guarantee Nifty 50 returns? No. Costs, cash balances and replication effects can make fund returns differ from the index. Are tracking error and tracking difference identical? No. Tracking difference measures return gap, while tracking error describes variability in the gap. Why do direct and regular plans have different NAVs? The portfolio is common but distribution-related expenses differ; AMFI notes that direct plans generally cost less. Does a SIP remove the risk of loss? No. A SIP changes purchase timing, not the underlying equity market exposure. Can switching plans create a tax event? Potentially. A switch can be a redemption and purchase for tax purposes; verify the actual transaction and law. Which disclosures should I compare before investing? Compare the current SID, factsheet, benchmark, expenses, tracking metrics, exit load and portfolio overlap.
