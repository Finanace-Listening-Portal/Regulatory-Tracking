Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Transcorp International Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Jaipur) CIT(A) Cannot Condone Delay and Then Dismiss Same Appeal as Time-Barred: ITAT Introduction The ITAT, Jaipur Bench, has set aside an appellate order containing contradictory findings on condonation of delay. The CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, initially accepted the assessee’s explanation and condoned a 25-day delay, but subsequently refused condonation and dismissed the same appeal as time-barred. The Tribunal held that these inconsistent conclusions within a single order were legally unsustainable. Since the CIT(A) had also failed to adjudicate the substantive grounds, the matter was restored for a fresh decision on merits after providing a proper opportunity of hearing. The assessee’s appeal was consequently allowed for statistical purposes. Facts of the Case The assessee, Transcorp International Limited, was engaged in the business of foreign exchange and inward money remittance. For Assessment Year 2017-18, it filed its return of income on 30 October 2017, declaring total income of ₹2,71,45,940. The return was selected for scrutiny. The assessment resulted in a ₹5 lakh disallowance of repair and maintenance expenses, a ₹32,51,200 disallowance under Section 14A, and a further disallowance of ₹58,577. Aggrieved by the assessment, the company filed an appeal before the CIT(A). However, the appeal was filed with a delay of 25 days, for which the assessee furnished detailed reasons and supporting material. Delay Initially Condoned The CIT(A) reproduced the assessee’s explanation for the delay and expressly accepted it in paragraph 1.3.3 of the appellate order. The appellate authority recorded that it had considered the submissions, found force in the explanation and the supporting evidence, and therefore condoned the delay. This was an affirmative finding accepting the assessee’s request. It formed the basis of the company’s subsequent objection that the appeal could not consistently be dismissed on the opposite conclusion in the same order. Contradictory Finding in the Concluding Portion Despite initially granting condonation, the CIT(A) later reached a different conclusion. In the findings recorded in paragraph 4.1, the appellate authority discussed judicial decisions and observed that the assessee was required to explain each day’s delay with credible proof. It then declined to condone the 25-day delay and dismissed the appeal. The resulting order thus both condoned and refused to condone the same delay. Although the assessee’s written submissions had been reproduced, the CIT(A) did not decide the underlying assessment issues on merits. The company challenged this contradictory disposal before the Tribunal. Grounds Raised by the Assessee The assessee argued that dismissal of the appeal as time-barred was inconsistent with the express condonation already recorded in the order. It also contended that failure to adjudicate the substantive grounds violated Section 250(6) and the principles of natural justice. On the assessment issues, the company challenged the lump-sum disallowance of repair and maintenance expenses, arguing that the supporting details and evidence had been ignored. It disputed the Section 14A read with Rule 8D disallowance, contending that the requisite satisfaction had not been recorded and that no exempt income had been earned during the year. The company further argued that the ₹58,577 expenditure was compensatory rather than penal and claimed denial of credit for ₹8,28,202 of Dividend Distribution Tax. These remained the assessee’s contentions; the Tribunal did not determine their correctness. Submissions Before the Tribunal The assessee’s representative furnished an 88-page paper book and advanced arguments seeking deletion of the assessment additions. It was emphasised that the first appellate authority had dismissed the appeal only on limitation, without examining its merits. The Revenue, on the other hand, supported the orders of the lower authorities. The Tribunal examined the appellate order and the material placed before it, focusing on the conflicting findings concerning the delay. Tribunal’s Findings and Directions The Tribunal found that the CIT(A) had clearly recorded condonation of delay after considering the assessee’s explanation, but had refused condonation in the concluding portion. It held that taking these opposing positions in the same order was legally unsustainable. The Tribunal also noted that merely reproducing the assessee’s written submissions did not amount to adjudicating the grounds on merits. Accordingly, it set aside the CIT(A)’s order and directed the appellate authority to provide a proper opportunity of hearing and pass a fresh order on merits in accordance with law. The Tribunal expressly refrained from expressing any opinion on the substantive disputes. Conclusion The ruling underscores the need for consistent findings and effective adjudication in appellate orders. An express acceptance of a delay-condonation request cannot coexist with dismissal of the same appeal on the contradictory premise that the delay remains uncondoned. The relief granted was a fresh appellate consideration, rather than deletion of the assessment disallowances. The company’s claims concerning expenditure, Section 14A and tax credit remain open for determination by the CIT(A).
