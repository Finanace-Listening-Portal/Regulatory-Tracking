Merchant associations, fintech companies and payment firms have sought more time to prepare for the new regime ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Oct 9, 2026 at 07:50 AM IST var news_msid = 134803820; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook UPI MDR rollout may be deferred from October 15 to January 1, 2027, with a final decision by NPCI still pending.The proposed 0.4 per cent MDR will apply to eligible P2M transactions above Rs2,000, with a Rs300 cap for transactions of Rs75,000 and above.The exemption for small merchants could be widened from monthly turnover of Rs1 lakh to annual turnover of Rs40 lakh.Paytm, MobiKwik and Pine Labs shares fell after reports of the possible delay, as investors reassessed the timing of the new revenue stream.The rollout of the new merchant discount rate (MDR) on Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions is likely to be deferred from October 15 to January 1, 2027, providing merchants and the payments ecosystem more time before the new fee structure takes effect.The proposed deferment is being considered ahead of the festive season, when UPI transactions typically surge. Merchant associations, fintech companies and payment firms have sought more time to prepare for the new regime, with discussions taking place between the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) and the Finance Ministry, according to reports. The UPI Steering Committee has also discussed the timing of the rollout. Advt A final decision is yet to be formally announced, but the proposed January 1 date has emerged as the likely new implementation date. The deferment would effectively keep the existing zero-MDR regime in place through the main festive shopping period before the new charges are introduced next year.Under the proposed framework, a 0.4 per cent MDR will apply to eligible person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs2,000, subject to a cap of Rs300 for transactions of Rs75,000 and above. Consumers will not pay the MDR. Person-to-person transactions and merchant transactions of up to Rs2,000 will remain outside the levy. Certain categories, including bill payments, utilities, education and fuel, are proposed to attract a flat Rs5 fee on transactions above Rs2,000.The possible postponement follows resistance from retail traders and concerns over introducing a new cost on digital payments during the festive season. The timing is significant because UPI has become deeply embedded in retail payments, with September recording 24.07 billion transactions worth Rs29.37 trillion. Average daily transactions stood at about 802 million during the month.Likely changesThe discussions are also looking at changes to the exemption available to smaller merchants. The existing proposal exempts merchants with monthly turnover of up to Rs1 lakh from MDR. The threshold under consideration could instead be linked to annual turnover of up to Rs40 lakh, which would substantially widen the number of businesses protected from the fee. Advt The possible delay has already affected listed payment companies. Shares of Paytm parent One 97 Communications, One MobiKwik Systems and Pine Labs fell sharply on Thursday after reports of the proposed postponement, reflecting investor expectations that the MDR framework would provide an additional revenue stream for payment companies and banks.The MDR framework was expected to create a new revenue pool for the digital payments and banking ecosystem after several years in which UPI transactions remained free for merchants. A delay to January would therefore not alter the proposed fee structure itself, but would postpone the beginning of the revenue flow by roughly two and a half months. Advt The move would also give the payments industry additional time to make operational changes and allow merchants to adjust to the new economics of UPI transactions.
