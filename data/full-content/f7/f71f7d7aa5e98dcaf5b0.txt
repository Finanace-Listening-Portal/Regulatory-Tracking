Articles 4 min read NPCI CEO Asbe says 10% of UPI value linked to non card merchants may face consumer pass through risk NPCI CEO Dilip Asbe said the 10% figure he cited in the context of UPI charges refers to the value associated with merchants that do not accept credit cards, where there could be a risk of charges being passed on to customers. The majority of MDR, he said, will come from existing credit-card accepting merchants who already pay MDR. So, that is where we will have to ensure, the ecosystem will have to ensure that the charges are not passed back to the customers,” Asbe said on Thursday while speaking at the 13th SBI Banking and Economic Conclave in Mumbai. Advt Asbe said the actual credit-card accepting merchants are already paying charges for credit cards and NPCI believes the price is already embedded with the charge.“So, the merchants will not pass back the charges to the customer. At least for the majority part of the MDR payment. MDR will come from the existing merchants who are paying MDR for credit cards,” he said.NPCI Chief said any change would create some pain but NPCI does not foresee any major impact.“Any change will create some pain. But we believe that as of now, we don't foresee any major impact on the volumes or value,” he said.“In fact, it will grow manifold because the ecosystem will push back. Ecosystem will invest back into the ecosystem.”Asbe said there is a huge need for investment in merchant infrastructure, particularly soundboxes.“I spoke about 20 million soundbox. The remaining 40 million merchants are having a QR code. Why not we ensure that they get a soundbox? So, I think there is a huge uptake required,” he said.He also pointed to the need for investment on the consumer side, including AI-based solutions.“Similarly, on the consumer side, right? You know, we need AI. We need some of the solutions to kind of drive that investments,” he said. Advt NPCI open to feedbackPer Asbe NPCI would remain open to feedback and would prefer to wait for data before making changes.“We will be open for the feedback. So, I don't believe that, you know, once the policy is made,” he said.“But we would like to avoid any sudden change. We would like to wait for the data. If there are some clarifications required,NPCI is open to provide.”On possible changes to merchant category codes, Asbe said there are around 900 merchant category codes and that there could be minor additions or eliminations.“There might be one or two additions, one or two eliminations. Those kind of minor changes will happen. But I don't foresee any major change in the pricing policy, which has been put up,” he said. Advt ‘Huge miscommunication and misinformation’Asbe said there was “huge miscommunication and misinformation” around the policy.“As I spoke inside, you know, about 75% UPI merchants out of 60 million has never ever seen transaction above 2000,” he said.“You look at MDR collectively, 80% of the value is going to come back from the rail card accepting merchants.”“So, if you look at this kind of boundary, auto pay has been exempted,” he said.Asbe also said there had been an issue in the market around the Rs 2,000 threshold and whether the limit applied per transaction.“There was one issue in the market that whether this 2000, up to 2000, after 2000, the charges apply and they thought it's not per transaction. So, that kind of, this level of misinformation back in the system,” he said. Advt He urged media professionals to communicate the policy nuances to merchants.NPCI to continue outreachAsked whether NPCI was planning outreach to merchants, Asbe said the organisation had already done some work and would continue.“We have done, we have done some of the part. But is there, considering what she said. No, we will continue to do that. To whatever our limited capacities are,” he said.He cited voice-based payments as an example of the investment required in the ecosystem.“So, for example, the voice-based payments, right? You need a large AI stack to be deployed. How do you, how do you invest into that, right? So, we launched voice-based payments. But again, the market will have to invest back in the standard,” he said.“We are kind of working with the ecosystem to deal with the investments and I believe that players will come up with AI.”Asbe said some large players had started investing in voice-based payments and feature-phone-based payments.
