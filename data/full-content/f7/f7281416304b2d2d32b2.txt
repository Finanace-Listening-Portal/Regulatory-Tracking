Articles 3 min read Rupee climbs past 93/USD as investors seek shelter in Indian stocks, shorts unwind The Indian rupee has strengthened past the 93-per-dollar mark for the first time since April. This recovery is attributed to over $12 billion of foreign investments in Indian equities in November. Hedge funds have unwound bearish positions on the rupee, contributing to its recent gains. The Reserve Bank of India has taken measures to support the local currency amidst stabilization efforts. It has since recovered sharply, cutting year-to-date losses to about 3% compared to nearly 8% at its weakest point in May and closing the week at 92.67, following its strongest session since October 12.Friday's gains accompanied gains in Indian stocks in the face of a worsening global rout in AI-linked stocks. Asian shares logged their worst week since 2020. India's benchmark Nifty 50, on the other hand, notched a fourth straight weekly gain. Advt Foreigners have poured $12 billion into Indian shares so far in November, the largest-ever monthly inflow, compared with more than $60 billion of selling from September 2024 to October 2026."More balanced global investor positioning and moderation in the oil price could see a sharp reversal in investor flows, to the benefit of the Indian stock market," said Edward Evans, portfolio manager at Ashmore Group.The surge in inflows has sharply increased the supply of dollars, boosting the rupee at a time when measures taken by the Reserve Bank of India have curbed demand.Earlier this year, policymakers rolled out steps to alleviate sustained pressure on the local currency, including clamping down on speculative trades, attracting capital inflows from overseas Indians and shifting crude oil import-related dollar demand out of the spot market.As the rupee struggled near record low levels of 97 last month, RBI chief Sanjay Malhotra had hinted that monetary tightening could also be used to support the unit.A sustained recovery may help reverse capital outflow-induced weakness in the currency, which has eroded returns for foreign investors and could deter further investments, analysts say.OFFSHORE SHORTS UNWIND, EXPORTERS EYEDThe rupee has also benefited from a rush to unwind bearish wagers on the currency this week, with the flows concentrated in offshore markets, bankers said.The reversal, which four bankers said was led by overseas hedge funds, has been aggressive enough to lift the rupee to a rare premium in the non-deliverable forward markets. Advt The sentiment shift is also apparent in the options market, with a key gauge showing that demand for options betting on rupee weakness has eased relative to those betting on its strength."Importantly, the RBI is choosing to let the INR appreciate and not draw a floor by buying reserves to elicit a behavioural or expectation shift among exporters and offshore participants," said Dhiraj Nim, an economist and FX Strategist at ANZ.Persistently elevated demand for forward dollars -- typically from importers -- has been a sore spot for the rupee throughout this year.A reduction in speculative dollar demand from companies and an unwinding of offshore wagers are both helping the rupee, according to an FX salesperson at a foreign bank.
