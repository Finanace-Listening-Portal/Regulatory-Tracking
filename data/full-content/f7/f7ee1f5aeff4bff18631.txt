Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); PCIT Vs SAP India Private Limited (Karnataka High Court) Software Developers Cannot Be Compared Mechanically With Distributors: Karnataka High Court Upholds Exclusion of Three Comparables Introduction The Karnataka High Court, in Pr. Commissioner of Income-tax-1 v. SAP India Private Limited, upheld the exclusion of Quick Heal Technologies Limited, Innovana Thinks Labs Limited and Tally Solutions Private Limited from the comparables selected for benchmarking the assessee’s software distribution segment. The Court found that the Tribunal’s conclusions rested on undisputed functional differences. Since the Revenue neither formulated a question alleging perversity nor explained why the findings were perverse, no substantial question of law arose under Section 260A. The judgment highlights the importance of examining what an enterprise actually does before treating it as comparable merely because it operates in the software industry. Background of the Dispute The assessee was engaged in selling, licensing, installing and providing maintenance and consultancy services relating to SAP products in India. It also provided training services. Under arrangements with its associated enterprise, it marketed and sub-licensed software products and provided support services to independent customers. For Assessment Year 2020-21, the assessee benchmarked its international transactions using the Transactional Net Margin Method, adopting the operating profit-to-sales ratio for its distribution segment. The Transfer Pricing Officer rejected the study on the ground that current-year data had not been used and conducted a fresh comparability analysis. Eleven companies were selected, including the three companies subsequently disputed before the High Court. Following the transfer pricing proceedings and directions of the Dispute Resolution Panel, the Assessing Officer made a transfer pricing adjustment of approximately ₹174.62 crore in the final assessment. The assessee challenged the assessment before the Bengaluru Tribunal. Why the Three Companies Were Excluded The Tribunal examined the functional profiles of the disputed companies and found that they differed materially from the assessee’s relevant operations. Quick Heal Technologies Limited was engaged in software development, research and development, and other activities beyond software distribution. Its accounts reflected substantial research expenditure, and it had a research and development team of approximately 340 persons. The Tribunal also noted its training and educational activities. Innovana Thinks Labs Limited owned intellectual property rights and undertook software development and research activities. Its business profile therefore differed from that of the assessee’s software distribution segment. Tally Solutions Private Limited carried on diverse activities, including developing packaged software and trading. It was consequently a developer of products as well as a trader, rather than merely a distributor. The exclusions thus rested on identified business characteristics and supporting material. Revenue’s Challenge Before the High Court The Revenue questioned the exclusions and argued that the Tribunal had applied a narrower functionality filter without testing the remaining comparables against the same standard. It also alleged that the Tribunal had not undertaken the necessary enquiry into the functions performed by the companies or the markets in which they operated. The High Court found that these questions essentially challenged the Tribunal’s conclusion on functional dissimilarity. The Court specifically observed that the Tribunal had not adopted any separate filter; it had merely found certain companies selected by the TPO to be functionally different. Undisputed Functional Findings Were Decisive The Court emphasised that there was no dispute regarding the functional profiles recorded by the Tribunal. Once those profiles were accepted, the conclusion that the three companies were functionally dissimilar could not be faulted. The Revenue had neither projected a question alleging perversity nor assigned reasons demonstrating that the Tribunal’s findings were perverse. Its objections regarding inadequate enquiry likewise failed because the underlying functional characteristics remained undisputed. Accordingly, the High Court held that none of the proposed questions raised a substantial question of law and dismissed the appeal. Does This Mean Comparability Findings Can Never Be Challenged? The judgment should not be read as granting blanket immunity to transfer pricing findings. In SAP Labs India Private Limited v. Income Tax Officer, Civil Appeal No.8463 of 2022, decided on 19 April 2023, the Supreme Court rejected the absolute proposition that the Tribunal’s determination of arm’s length price is invariably beyond scrutiny under Section 260A. The High Court can examine compliance with the governing statutory provisions and Rules, the evidentiary basis of comparability and filter selection, and whether the findings are perverse. api.sci.gov.in The present judgment does not discuss that Supreme Court decision. Its outcome rests on the particular record: the functional profiles were undisputed, and no perversity was demonstrated. Author’s Comments The practical lesson is that industry similarity does not establish functional comparability. A software distributor and an enterprise developing proprietary software may both earn software-related revenue, but their functions, assets and risks can differ substantially. For taxpayers, objections should be supported by annual reports, research expenditure, intellectual property ownership and evidence of development activities. For the Revenue, an appeal must identify the actual legal error, evidentiary defect or perversity; repeating disagreement with an exclusion may be insufficient. Equally, this decision does not permanently disqualify these companies as comparables for every taxpayer or year. Comparability remains dependent on the tested transaction and the relevant evidence. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT 1. The Revenue has filed the present appeal under Section 260A of the Income Tax Act, 1961 [the Act], impugning the order dated 17.11.2025 passed by the learned Income Tax Appellate Tribunal [Tribunal] in ITA No.1519/Bang/2024. 2. The respondent [Assessee] had filed the said appeal against the Assessment Order dated 18.06.2024 passed by the Assessing Officer [AO] under Section 143(3) read with Sections 144C(13) and 144B of the Act for the Assessment Year [AY] 2020-21. 3. The Assessee is engaged in the business of selling, licensing, installing and providing maintenance and consultancy services in regard to products of SAP SE, Germany and its subsidiaries in India. The Assessee also provides training services on the said products. 4. The Assessee had entered into a distribution agreement with its Associated Enterprise [AE], SAP AG, SAP India, to market a range of SAP products and provide support services to independent customers in India. The AEs had also granted a non-exclusive licence to the Assessee to use, market and sub-licence the software products of SAP AG. In consideration for the same, the Assessee pays royalty to SAP AG for sub-licensing the software and maintenance services provided in respect of the said software. 5. The Assessee had disclosed the international transactions entered into with its AE and the AO made a reference to the Transfer Pricing Officer [TPO] for determination of the Arm’s Length Price [ALP] of those international transactions. 6. The Assessee had furnished its transfer pricing study to establish that its international transactions were at ALP. It had used the Transactional Net Margin Method [TNMM] with the operating profit to sales ratio [OP/OR] in the distribution segment as the Profit Level Indicator [PLI] in TNMM analysis. 7. The learned TPO rejected the transfer pricing study submitted by the Assessee on the ground that the Assessee had not used the current year’s data. The learned TPO thereafter undertook an analysis to identify companies that, according to the TPO, were similar to the Assessee’s profile, based on the FAR analysis. 8. The entities selected by the appellant also included the following three companies: i. Quick Heal Technologies Limited; ii. Innovana Thinks Labs Limited; and iii. Tally Solutions Private Limited. 9. The Assessee objected to some other entities selected for conducting the transfer pricing study, including the aforementioned three companies. However, the learned TPO rejected the objections raised by the Assessee and selected 11 companies for conducting the transfer pricing studies. On the basis of the aforesaid directions, the AO passed the final assessment order. The AO made a transfer pricing adjustment of ₹174,61,81,776/- and assessed the total income at ₹11,71,68,43,086/-. The Assessee impugned the order before the learned Tribunal. 13. The learned Tribunal considered the Assessee’s objections, inter alia, regarding inclusion of the following three entities as comparables for the purposes of transfer pricing analysis: i. Quick Heal Technologies Limited; ii. Innovana Thinks Labs Limited; and iii. Tally Solutions Private Limited. 14. The learned Tribunal examined the said objections and found that the functional profile of the aforementioned three entities was not similar to that of the Assessee. 15. In regard to the Quick Heal Technologies Limited, the Tribunal accepted the Assessee’s contention that the said company was functionally different, as it was engaged in providing diverse services apart from the distribution of software. It was also engaged in software development, research and development, and other software development activities. The accounts of the said company indicated that it had incurred substantial expenditure on research and development. It had a research and development team comprising approximately 340 people. Additionally, it was also engaged in providing training and running education institutions. 16. Similarly, the learned Tribunal also found that Innovana Thinks Labs Limited was not functionally similar to the Assessee, as it owned intellectual property rights and was engaged in the software development. It was also involved in research and development activities. 17. The learned Tribunal found that Tally Solutions Private Limited was also involved in diverse business activities. It was involved in developing packaged software as well as trading activities. Thus, Tally Solutions Private Limited was not merely distributing the products but had also developed them. 18. From the aforesaid context, the Revenue has projected the following questions for consideration before this Court: 1. “Whether in the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Tribunal is right in excluding companies namely Quick Heal Technologies Ltd., Tally Solutions Pvt. Ltd. and Innovana Thinks Lab Ltd. that are functionally similar to the taxpayer’s business in the set of comparables for determining the arm’s length price? 2. Whether in the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Tribunal is right in excluding some comparables adopted by the TPO by applying a narrower functionality filter than the TPO, and not testing other comparables using this filter applied by it which is in violation of sections 92 to 92C of Income tax Act, 1961 and Rule 10B of Income Tax Rules, 1962? 3. Whether in the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Tribunal is right in excluding the comparable without carrying out necessary enquiry pertaining to functions vis-à-vis tested party? 4. Whether in the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Tribunal is right in excluding the comparable without carrying out necessary enquiry pertaining to markets it is operating in vis-à-vis tested party?” 19. Insofar as question No.1 is concerned, the same relates to the learned Tribunal’s finding that the three companies in question (Quick Heal Technologies Limited; Innovana Thinks Labs Limited; and Tally Solutions Private Limited) are functionally dissimilar. This finding is a finding of fact and does not give rise to any substantial question of law. The Revenue has neither projected any question nor assigned any reasons for this Court to hold that the aforesaid finding as perverse. 20. There is no dispute as to the functional profiles of the three entities as noted by the learned Tribunal. If the said functional profiles are accepted, the finding that the companies are functionally dissimilar to the Assessee cannot be faulted. 21. Question No.2 proceeds on the basis that the learned Tribunal narrowed the functionality filter but it had not tested the other comparables against the said filters. The learned counsel for the Revenue submitted that the said question also relates to the exclusion of the aforementioned three companies on the ground of functional dissimilarity. 22. The learned Tribunal has not adopted any separate filter. Therefore, in our view, the learned Tribunal has merely found that some of the entities selected by the TPO are functionally dissimilar. In our view, question No.2 is, in substance, a question relating to the functional dissimilarity of the companies. 23. Question No.3 also does not arise, as there is no dispute as to the functional profile of the companies as noted by the learned Tribunal. In this regard, question No.4 also does not raise any substantial issue. 24. In our view, no substantial questions of law arise in the present appeal. The appeal is accordingly dismissed. 25. The pending interlocutory application also stands disposed of.
