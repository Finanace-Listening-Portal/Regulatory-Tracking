Summary: Input Tax Credit (ITC) is an essential component of GST compliance and business cash-flow management. It enables registered taxpayers to offset eligible GST paid on business purchases against their output tax liability, thereby reducing the cascading effect of taxation. However, claiming ITC requires compliance with Sections 16 and 17 of the CGST Act, 2017, the applicable rules, and return-reporting requirements. Businesses must distinguish between eligible operational expenditure, blocked credits under Section 17(5), and common credits requiring proportionate reversal under Rules 42 and 43. Expenses relating to raw materials, machinery, commercial rent, professional services and advertising may qualify, subject to statutory conditions, while specified passenger vehicles, employee welfare expenditure, immovable-property construction and personal consumption may be restricted or blocked. The availability of credit also depends on invoice documentation, receipt of supplies, supplier reporting, prescribed tax-payment conditions, return filing and statutory time limits. Proper reconciliation of purchase records with GSTR-2B and accurate reporting in Table 4 of GSTR-3B are particularly important. This guide explains common expense categories, statutory exceptions, practical reversal calculations, reporting requirements and risks arising from incorrect claims. It also discusses how digital tax-compliance tools, including Apkireturn, can support invoice reconciliation and ITC review, subject to the functionality available in the selected software. Fortunately, the Goods and Services Tax (GST) framework provides a mechanism to prevent the cascading effect of taxes: Input Tax Credit (ITC). However, misinterpreting GST input tax credit eligible and ineligible expenses can quickly turn this tax-saving mechanism into a legal and financial liability. Claiming ITC on non-eligible business expenses may trigger audit notices, interest, penalties and unnecessary scrutiny from tax authorities. Consequently, every business owner, accountant and finance manager must understand the fine line between what can be legally claimed and what is explicitly blocked under Indian tax law. In this comprehensive guide, we unpack eligible and ineligible ITC under GST, explain the critical rules governing Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, and demonstrate how businesses can streamline return filings with modern fintech tools like Apkireturn. What is Input Tax Credit (ITC) and How Does It Impact Business Cash Flow? At its core, Input Tax Credit allows a business to reduce the GST payable on outward supplies by utilising eligible GST paid on inward supplies. Consequently, the business generally pays only the balance tax liability after applying available credit in accordance with the statutory utilisation rules. For instance, if a manufacturing business has an output GST liability of ₹1,00,000 and eligible input tax credit of ₹60,000, its net GST liability payable through cash, assuming the credit can be fully utilised against that liability, is: ₹1,00,000 − ₹60,000 = ₹40,000. However, to claim this credit legally, the underlying purchase must satisfy the fundamental criterion under Section 16(1) of the CGST Act: the goods or services must be used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business. Business use alone does not establish eligibility. The claim must also satisfy the remaining requirements of Section 16, the restrictions under Section 17 and the applicable CGST Rules. When businesses fail to distinguish between valid operational purchases and personal or blocked expenses, their risk of audit increases significantly. Therefore, knowing the strict eligibility criteria established by tax law is the first line of defence against costly tax demands. Statutory Conditions to Claim ITC under GST Even if an expense appears directly linked to business operations, ITC cannot be claimed automatically. Section 16(2) of the CGST Act prescribes cumulative eligibility conditions. Businesses must also consider Section 16(4), Rule 37 and other applicable restrictions. Essential Conditions under Section 16(2) 1. Possession of a Tax Invoice: The recipient must possess a valid tax invoice, debit note or other prescribed tax-paying document issued in accordance with GST law. 2. Receipt of Goods or Services: The recipient must have received the underlying goods or services. In qualifying bill-to-ship-to arrangements, delivery to another person on the recipient’s direction is treated as receipt by the recipient. 3. Supplier Reporting and Communication: The supplier must furnish the relevant invoice or debit-note details in the prescribed outward-supply statement, and those details must be communicated to the recipient under Section 16(2)(aa). Applicable restrictions communicated under Section 38 must also be considered. 4. Tax Payment to the Government: Subject to the statutory provisions and applicable mechanisms, the tax charged on the supply must have been paid to the Government, either in cash or through admissible ITC. 5. Filing of Returns: The recipient must furnish the return required under Section 39. 6. Payment to Supplier: Where the 180-day payment condition applies, failure to pay the supplier the value of supply together with tax within the prescribed period triggers the applicable reversal or payment consequences, with re-availment permitted upon subsequent payment. Furthermore, invoice reporting through GSTR-1 or GSTR-1A, as applicable, and communication through GSTR-2B are critical to ITC reconciliation. A missing invoice should be investigated and corrected through the appropriate supplier-reporting process before credit is availed, subject to statutory exceptions and applicable rules. Businesses must also observe the time limit under Section 16(4), generally linked to 30 November following the relevant financial year or furnishing of the relevant annual return, whichever is earlier, subject to applicable statutory exceptions and amendments. Detailed Categorisation: Eligible ITC Expenses Understanding eligible and ineligible ITC under GST requires a clear analysis of everyday business expenses. Eligible expenses generally support business operations, expansion, manufacturing and commercial service delivery, provided the statutory conditions are satisfied. Key Categories of Eligible Expenses Raw Materials and Consumables: GST on materials used in manufacturing or supplying taxable goods or services is generally eligible, subject to business use, exempt-supply apportionment and other restrictions. Capital Goods: Machinery, equipment, office laptops, server hardware and office furniture used for business purposes may qualify. Under Section 16(3), ITC is unavailable on the tax component of capital goods and plant and machinery where depreciation is claimed on that tax component under the Income-tax Act. Operational Overheads: GST on taxable commercial rent, software subscriptions, cloud hosting, eligible utilities and legal or accounting services may qualify when incurred for business purposes. Marketing and Distribution: GST on digital advertising, agency services, promotional materials and eligible freight services may qualify, subject to applicable restrictions, including those concerning gifts and free samples. Important: An expense does not automatically qualify for 100% ITC merely because it is recorded in the business accounts. Eligibility depends on taxable business use, the nature of supply, statutory exclusions and the proportion attributable to taxable or zero-rated outward supplies. Ineligible ITC and Blocked Credits Under Section 17(5) While many operational expenses qualify for credit, the CGST Act explicitly blocks certain purchases from receiving tax offsets. Section 17(5) prescribes the blocked credit under GST provisions. Claiming credit on these items without satisfying the applicable exceptions may violate GST law. Motor Vehicles and Conveyances GST paid on motor vehicles for transportation of persons having an approved seating capacity of not more than 13 persons, including the driver, is generally blocked under Section 17(5)(a). Exceptions: ITC may be available where such vehicles are used for making the following taxable supplies: Further supply of such motor vehicles, such as by vehicle dealers. Transportation of passengers. Imparting training on driving such motor vehicles. Motor vehicles designed for transportation of goods are not covered by this particular passenger-vehicle restriction. However, other eligibility conditions continue to apply. Separate provisions govern specified vessels, aircraft and related insurance, repair and maintenance services. Food, Beverages, Catering and Employee Welfare ITC is generally blocked on specified goods and services, including: Food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, and cosmetic and plastic surgery. Membership of clubs and health or fitness centres. Travel benefits extended to employees on vacation, including leave or home travel concession. Specified life insurance, health insurance and vehicle-related leasing or hiring services, subject to statutory exceptions. Crucial exceptions: Credit may be available where the inward supply is used for making an outward taxable supply of the same category, or as an element of a taxable composite or mixed supply, where the relevant statutory exception applies. Credit may also be available where provision of the specified goods or services by the employer to employees is obligatory under a law in force. The legal obligation must be established from the applicable law. Merely providing a benefit under company policy does not automatically make ITC eligible. Works Contract Services and Construction of Immovable Property ITC on specified works contract services and goods or services used for construction of immovable property is restricted under Sections 17(5)(c) and 17(5)(d), subject to their respective exceptions and the applicable statutory wording. For works contract services, credit may be available where the service is an input for further supply of works contract services. The treatment of construction-related credit also requires consideration of the statutory distinction involving plant and machinery, the nature of the property, capitalisation and the applicable period. Key distinction: Routine repairs, painting and maintenance that are not capitalised as construction costs may qualify for ITC if the general conditions are satisfied. However, accounting treatment alone does not conclusively determine eligibility, and the statutory meaning of construction must be considered. Personal Consumption and Lost or Stolen Goods Goods or services used for personal consumption are not eligible for ITC. Furthermore, Section 17(5)(h) blocks credit on: Goods lost, stolen, destroyed or written off. Goods disposed of by way of gift or free samples. The treatment of promotional schemes, discounts and other commercial arrangements depends on their actual structure and applicable GST provisions. Tax Paid Under Specified Demand and Enforcement Provisions Section 17(5)(i) has undergone legislative changes. Accordingly, businesses should not apply the earlier reference to Sections 74, 129 and 130 indiscriminately to every tax period or every demand. The applicable provision, period, nature of demand and effective date of the relevant amendment must be examined before determining whether tax paid pursuant to enforcement proceedings is eligible for ITC. Sections 17(1) and 17(2) restrict credit to the amount attributable to eligible business purposes and taxable or zero-rated supplies. Rule 42: Inputs and Input Services Rule 42 prescribes the calculation and reversal mechanism for common inputs and input services used partly for exempt supplies or non-business purposes. The calculation generally involves identifying exclusively eligible and ineligible credits, determining common credit and calculating the proportion attributable to exempt or non-business use in accordance with the prescribed formula. Rule 43: Capital Goods Rule 43 governs common capital goods used for taxable and exempt supplies. It generally operates using a prescribed useful life of 60 months, with the applicable monthly attribution and reversal calculations. Failing to calculate these reversals accurately can result in tax demands and interest exposure. Tax-compliance platforms such as Apkireturn may assist with these calculations where the relevant reconciliation and reversal functionality is available. How to Separate Eligible and Ineligible ITC in GSTR-3B Accurately reporting ineligible ITC under Section 17(5) in the monthly GSTR-3B return is essential to maintaining compliance. The reporting framework is explained in New Form GSTR-3B amendments as per Circular No.170/02/2022-GST. Table 4(A): ITC Available Table 4(A) contains ITC available under its prescribed categories, including relevant auto-populated details from GSTR-2B. The taxpayer must verify the figures and make appropriate corrections in accordance with the return instructions. Table 4(B)(1): Permanent ITC Reversals Report permanent reversals, including applicable blocked credit under Section 17(5) and reversals under Rules 38, 42 and 43, in Table 4(B)(1). Table 4(B)(2): Temporary ITC Reversals Report reversals that may subsequently be reclaimed when the applicable statutory conditions are fulfilled, such as certain reversals arising from non-payment to suppliers within 180 days. Table 4(C): Net ITC Available The net ITC available is calculated as: Table 4(C) = Table 4(A) − [Table 4(B)(1) + Table 4(B)(2)]. Only the resulting eligible net credit should be reflected in the electronic credit ledger. Table 4(D): Additional ITC Disclosures Table 4(D)(1) captures eligible ITC reclaimed after earlier reversal under Table 4(B)(2), while Table 4(D)(2) covers specified ineligible ITC, including time-barred credit under Section 16(4) and certain place-of-supply restrictions. The distinction between permanent and temporary reversal is particularly important. For a detailed discussion, see Everything you must know about new disclosures in GSTR-3B and Changes in GSTR-3B in simplified manner – Circular No. 170/02/2022-GST. Common Penalties and Risks of Incorrect ITC Claims Claiming ineligible ITC can carry significant financial consequences. The nature of the liability depends on the period, whether credit was merely availed or also utilised, the circumstances of the claim and the applicable demand provisions. Interest: Where ITC has been wrongly availed and utilised, interest may apply under Section 50(3), read with Rule 88B, generally at the notified rate of 18% per annum for the relevant period. Tax Recovery: Wrongly availed ITC may be recoverable under the applicable demand provisions, subject to the relevant financial year and statutory framework. Monetary Penalties: Penalties depend on the nature of the contravention, the applicable provision and whether fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression is established. A penalty equal to the tax amount is not automatic for every incorrect claim. Audit and Litigation Risk: Unsupported credit claims may result in notices, reconciliation disputes, additional documentation requirements and litigation costs. Taxpayers who identify an incorrect claim should examine whether reversal, payment through the appropriate statutory mechanism or correction in a subsequent return is required. Where credit was wrongly availed but not utilised, the interest consequences must be examined under Section 50(3) and Rule 88B rather than assumed merely from the act of availment. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) Which Expenses Are Ineligible for Input Tax Credit under GST? Common blocked categories include specified passenger motor vehicles, food and beverages, outdoor catering, certain employee benefits, specified construction-related expenditure, personal consumption, goods disposed of by way of gift or free samples, and goods lost, stolen, destroyed or written off. Eligibility must be assessed with reference to Section 17(5) and its exceptions. How to Separate Eligible and Ineligible ITC in GSTR-3B? Reconcile purchase records with GSTR-2B and verify the statutory eligibility of each credit. Report the relevant ITC in Table 4(A), permanent reversals in Table 4(B)(1), temporary reversals in Table 4(B)(2), and additional disclosures in Table 4(D), as applicable. Table 4(C) reflects net ITC available. Can GST ITC Be Claimed on Office Repair and Maintenance? Yes, ITC may be available on qualifying office repairs and maintenance used for taxable business activities. However, where expenditure constitutes construction of immovable property within the applicable statutory provisions, the restrictions under Section 17(5) must be examined. Merely recording expenditure in the Profit and Loss account does not independently establish eligibility. Is Input Tax Credit Available on Health Insurance for Staff? Health insurance is generally covered by the restrictions under Section 17(5)(b). However, ITC may be available where the employer is legally obligated to provide the insurance under an applicable law or where another statutory exception is satisfied. The legal obligation and supporting documents should be maintained.
