Summary: Standard on Auditing (SA) 570 (Revised) requires auditors to distinguish between the appropriateness of the going-concern basis of accounting and the existence of material uncertainty regarding an entity’s ability to continue operations. The article explains six going-concern reporting situations, including unmodified opinions, separate Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern sections, qualified opinions, adverse opinions and Emphasis of Matter paragraphs where an acceptable alternative accounting basis is adopted. Through a hypothetical example involving financial losses, negative net worth, working capital deficiencies and loan defaults, it illustrates how different levels of supporting audit evidence influence the auditor’s reporting conclusions. The article also examines NFRA Order No. 29/2023 concerning Burnpur Cement Limited, highlighting deficiencies in evaluating management’s going-concern assumptions, obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence and maintaining adequate documentation. It further distinguishes the requirements of SA 570 from clause 3(xix) of CARO 2020, which addresses the company’s ability to meet existing liabilities falling due within one year from the balance sheet date. The article concludes with a practical checklist covering management assessments, cash-flow forecasts, promoter support, financial statement disclosures, audit documentation and reconciliation of reporting conclusions under SA 570 and CARO 2020. It emphasises that an Emphasis of Matter paragraph cannot substitute for mandatory reporting of material uncertainty or an appropriately modified audit opinion. File checklist before you sign References Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Going Concern Under SA 570 (Revised): Audit Reporting, NFRA Findings and CARO 2020 When a company is in financial trouble, the going-concern paragraph in the audit report is where auditors most often go wrong. The usual mistake is to put the matter in an Emphasis of Matter (EOM) paragraph and leave the opinion unmodified, because the note in the financial statements “already discloses it”. SA 570 (Revised) does not work that way. It gives six situations, and only two of them end in an unmodified opinion with any going-concern wording at all. This note sets out the six, shows how one set of facts moves between them, and explains why NFRA penalised an engagement partner and firm in Order No. 29/2023 where the going-concern matter went into an EOM. It also covers how CARO 2020 clause 3(xix) can give a different answer from SA 570, which is a source of regular confusion. 1. Two questions, not one Most of the confusion goes away once you separate two questions that SA 570 asks in order. 1. Is the going-concern basis itself appropriate? The auditor concludes on this at para 17. If the answer is no, the audit is over before disclosure is even looked at. 2. If it is appropriate, does a material uncertainty exist, and is it adequately disclosed? Para 18 deals with the uncertainty, and paras 19 to 23 deal with disclosure and the resulting opinion. Ask the second question before the first and you end up in an EOM paragraph. That is the shortcut NFRA objected to. 2. The six situations # Position Opinion SA 570 (Revised) 1 No events or conditions casting significant doubt Unmodified. No going-concern reporting. Para 17 2 Events or conditions exist, but no material uncertainty after audit work Unmodified. No separate section. Disclosures must still meet the framework. Paras 16, 20 3 Basis appropriate, material uncertainty exists, disclosure adequate Unmodified, with a separate section headed “Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern“ Paras 19, 22 4 Basis appropriate, material uncertainty exists, disclosure inadequate Qualified if material but not pervasive; adverse if pervasive Para 23, with SA 705 (Revised) 5 Going-concern basis used, but inappropriate Adverse, whether or not the statements disclose it Para 21 6 Basis inappropriate, and an acceptable alternative basis (for example liquidation) is used and disclosed Unmodified, with an EOM drawing attention to the alternative basis Para A27 Look at where the EOM appears: only in situation 6. In situation 3 the right tool is the separate going-concern section, and in situations 4 and 5 the opinion itself changes. An EOM is never the response to an unresolved going-concern doubt. 3. One set of facts, three outcomes Take a hypothetical private company, Alpha Textiles Pvt Ltd, for FY 2025-26. It has a loss of ₹4.2 crore, negative net worth of ₹6.8 crore, current liabilities exceeding current assets by ₹9.5 crore, and a term loan of ₹12 crore on which two instalments are overdue. The promoters have given a letter saying they will “support the company”. The figures are invented for illustration. What the audit file shows Situation Report The bank has issued a sanction letter restructuring the loan. Promoters have brought in ₹5 crore, evidenced by bank credits. A cash-flow forecast has been tested and the doubt is resolved. 2 Unmodified, no going-concern section. Disclosure of the events must still be adequate. The restructuring is only under discussion. The forecast depends on it. The note to the accounts clearly states that a material uncertainty exists and sets out management’s plans. 3 Unmodified, with a separate “Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern” section. Same facts, but the note says only that “management is confident of continuing operations”. 4 Qualified (or adverse). Basis for Opinion must say a material uncertainty exists and is not adequately disclosed. The promoter letter is the only support and the promoters have no means to honour it. 5 Adverse. Good disclosure does not save the opinion. The fourth row is where the file needs the most care. A promoter letter without evidence of the promoters’ ability to pay is not audit evidence of the plan, and it is not a reason to move to situation 3. 4. What NFRA held in Burnpur Cement NFRA Order No. 29/2023 dated 21/08/2023 was passed under section 132(4) of the Companies Act, 2013 in the matter of the statutory audit of Burnpur Cement Limited for FY 2018-19, the firm’s first year as auditor. The company had a loss of ₹12.67 crore, accumulated losses of ₹115.64 crore, negative net worth, negative working capital, high debt and loan defaults. The auditors reported the going-concern matter only in an EOM paragraph. NFRA’s findings on this point were: The auditors had not questioned management’s going-concern assumption or analysed whether the opinion had to be modified. There was no working paper in the audit file supporting the EOM. The management representation produced later with the reply was not in the audit file and was not on company letterhead, so NFRA treated it as an afterthought. Going concern was one of several charges in that order, along with interest on NPA borrowings, the income-tax order, PPE impairment, trade receivables, the engagement quality control reviewer and audit documentation. The firm, M/s K. Pandeya & Co., was fined ₹25 lakh. The engagement partner was fined ₹5 lakh and debarred for five years. The lesson for the going-concern part is narrow. The EOM was not wrong because it was an EOM. It was wrong because the file did not show the work that would justify an unmodified opinion. 5. CARO 2020 clause 3(xix) can say something different Clause 3(xix) of the Companies (Auditor’s Report) Order, 2020 asks whether the company can meet its existing liabilities as they fall due within one year from the balance sheet date, based on the auditor’s examination of ratios, ageing, expected realisation and payment dates and other information. SA 570 asks whether the going-concern basis is appropriate, over management’s assessment period of at least twelve months from the end of the reporting period. NFRA’s September 2026 publication in its Auditor-Audit Committee series (Series 6, on going concern under SA 570 (Revised)) points out that the two can differ: A company may meet its liabilities in the next year, so CARO 3(xix) is clean, while SA 570 concerns remain, for example the loss of a key customer. A short-term timing mismatch may be reported under CARO, yet resolved under SA 570 by a committed facility that is properly evidenced and disclosed. If the two answers differ, the auditor should be ready to explain why to the audit committee. Writing the reconciliation in the file before the report is signed costs an hour and saves a long reply later. 6. File checklist before you sign 1. List the events and conditions you found, with reference to SA 570 para 10 and the examples in A3. Do this even if management has identified none. 2. Record your conclusion on appropriateness of the basis (para 17) before you look at disclosure. 3. Where a material uncertainty exists, document why (para 18) and test the cash-flow forecast, past forecast accuracy and each assumption (para 16). 4. Obtain evidence of the plan, not just a letter. For promoter or group support, evidence of the ability to give it. 5. Match the disclosure note to para 19: principal events and conditions, management’s plans, and a clear statement that a material uncertainty exists. 6. Pick the situation from the table above and write the reason in the file in one paragraph. 7. Reconcile your SA 570 conclusion to clause 3(xix) of CARO 2020. 8. If you use an EOM, note why it is situation 6 and which working paper supports it.
