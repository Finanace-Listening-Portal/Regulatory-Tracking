Minister Narendra Modi, approved the sale of a 60.72 per cent stake in IDBI Bank to Canada-based Fairfax Financial Holdings Ltd for Rs 52,878 crore post market hours. The process will see the government sell 30.48 per cent of its stake in IDBI Bank, while Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) will divest 30.24 per cent of its shareholding. This will leave the government and LIC with residual stake 15 per cent and 19 per cent, respectively. The transaction, once completed, will be the largest foreign direct investment (FDI) in India’s financial sector. At the bourses, meanwhile, IDBI Bank stock has been an underperformer and has slipped 13.4 per cent year-till-date (YTD) till October 14 as compared to the NSE 500 index that lost 7.8 per cent during this period, NSE data shows. On the BSE, stocks such as Rail Vikas Nigam Ltd (RVNL), Gujarat Energy, Ircon International, NBCC (India), Indian Railway Finance Corporation, Hindustan Petroleum Corporation (HPCL) and Rashtriya Chemicals and Fertilizers have slipped up to 45 per cent YTD as compared to 14 per cent dip in the BSE Sensex. PSU banks such as Bank of Baroda (BoB), Canara Bank, UCO Bank, Central Bank of India and State Bank of India (SBI) have slipped up to 19 per cent YTD during this period, ACE Equity data shows. The BSE PSU Bank index has lost 3.3 per cent, data shows. While the price comes as a surprise, said G Chokkalingam, founder and head of research at Equinomics Research, the road ahead for PSU bank stocks is not smooth. Stiff inflation and a possible rise in interest rates, he believes, will continue to dent the financial performance in the months ahead.
