Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Vs Kamdhenu Cement (ITAT Delhi) Business and Sales Undisputed: Delhi ITAT Restricts Alleged Bogus Purchase Addition to 5% on Case-Specific Facts Summary: The Delhi Income Tax Appellate Tribunal has restricted the addition relating to alleged bogus purchases of ₹1,21,78,359 to 5%, noting that the assessee’s business activity and sales were not disputed, although the supplier had been found to be an entry provider. In DCIT v. Kamdhenu Cement, the Tribunal considered a lump-sum addition of 5% appropriate in the interests of justice. It expressly stipulated that the decision should not be treated as a precedent. The Revenue’s appeal was consequently partly allowed. Facts of the Case The dispute arose from reassessment proceedings under Section 147 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 for Assessment Year 2019-20. The Assessing Officer disallowed purchases of ₹1,21,78,359 under Section 69C, treating purchases sourced from Mr. Sanjay Jain as bogus. The Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)/National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi, reversed the Assessing Officer’s action through its order dated 20 April 2026. The Revenue challenged that relief before the Tribunal. When the appeal was called twice, nobody appeared on behalf of the assessee. The Tribunal therefore proceeded ex parte after hearing the departmental representative. Revenue’s Contention The departmental representative argued that the CIT(A)/NFAC had erred in reversing the disallowance of the entire disputed purchase amount. According to the Revenue, supporting documents relating to the purchases had not been brought on record during the assessment proceedings. It therefore sought restoration of the Assessing Officer’s action. Tribunal’s Findings The Tribunal considered two material circumstances. First, there was no dispute regarding the assessee’s business activity and sales in principle. Second, the supplier concerned had been found to be an entry provider. The Bench referred to several judicial decisions concerning bogus purchases and noted that they reflected divergent opinions. The cited authorities included Ravjibhai Becharbhai Dhamelia Vs ACIT, PCIT Vs Hitesh Mody (HUF), PCIT Vs Forum Sales (P) Ltd., PCIT Vs Kanak Impex (India) Ltd., DCIT Vs Kohinoor Foods Ltd. and DCIT Vs Tirupati Matsup (P.) Ltd. The order did not undertake a detailed comparison of the factual circumstances or legal reasoning in those decisions. Instead, the Tribunal adopted a fact-specific estimation approach. Addition Restricted to 5% of Disputed Purchases Considering the accepted business activity and sales alongside the adverse finding concerning the supplier, the Tribunal held that a lump-sum disallowance/addition of 5% of the alleged bogus purchases would be just and proper in the larger interest of justice. The Tribunal also rejected the assessee’s book entries in the terms recorded in its order and directed that the necessary computation follow in accordance with law. Particulars Amount Alleged bogus purchases ₹1,21,78,359 Addition directed by ITAT 5% of disputed purchases Calculated addition ₹6,08,917.95 Approximate addition ₹6,08,918 The Tribunal thus declined to restore the full purchase disallowance while also declining to leave the disputed purchases entirely without an addition. This approach may be compared with other fact-dependent decisions concerning taxation of the profit element in alleged bogus purchases where sales are accepted. Such decisions do not establish a universally applicable estimation rate. Express Restriction on Precedential Use The Tribunal expressly stipulated that its 5% disallowance/addition shall not be treated as a precedent. Accordingly, the order does not establish a uniform 5% rate for all alleged bogus purchase cases. Its conclusion was confined to the circumstances before the Bench. The distinction is important because other judicial decisions have taken different approaches to full additions under Section 69C and partial estimation of disputed purchases, depending on the evidence and findings in each case. Outcome of the Appeal The Tribunal directed an addition of 5% of ₹1,21,78,359 and partly allowed the Revenue’s appeal. The short order does not provide a detailed account of the purchase documents, supplier investigation or CIT(A)’s reasoning. Its operative relief must therefore be understood within the limited factual findings expressly recorded. Practical Takeaway The decision illustrates a fact-specific approach to additions concerning alleged bogus purchases, particularly where sales and business activity remain undisputed but the supplier is found to be an entry provider. For taxpayers and tax authorities, the important considerations remain the evidence supporting purchases and the source of expenditure, the treatment of corresponding sales and the particular factual findings. The Tribunal’s express restriction on precedential use must be respected. The 5% estimate cannot be presented as a generally applicable legal rule. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI This Revenue’s appeal for assessment year 2019-20 arises against the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)/National Faceless Appeal Centre [in short, the “CIT(A)/NFAC”], Delhi’s DIN and order no. ITBA/NFAC/S/250/2026-27/1088523471(1), dated 20.04.2026 involving proceedings under section 147 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’). Case called twice. None appears at the assessee/respondent’s behest. It is accordingly proceeded ex-parte. 2. Learned departmental representative vehemently argues during the course of hearing that the CIT(A)/NFAC has erred in law and on facts in reversing the Assessing Officer’s action disallowing the assessee’s entire bogus purchases of Rs.1,21,78,359/- u/s 69C sourced from Mr. Sanjay Jain as no supportive documents had been brought on record regarding the same in scrutiny. 3. That being the clinching factual position, we note that various judicial precedents (2025) 173 taxmann.com 592 (Guj.) Ravjibhai Becharbhai Dhamelia vs. ACIT; (2024) 160 taxmann.com 110 (Bom) PCIT Vs. Hitesh Mody (HUF), (2024) 160 taxmann.com 93 (Del) PCIT Vs. Forum Sales (P) Ltd.; (2025) 172 taxmann.com 283 (Bom) PCIT Vs. Kanak Impex (India) Ltd; (2025) 178 taxmann.com 424 (Del. – Trib.) DCIT Vs. Kohinoor Foods Ltd.; and (2025) 177 taxmann.com 836 (Delhi-trib.) DCIT Vs. Tirupati Matsup (P.) Ltd. have recently decided the instant issue of bogus purchases with divergent opinions as well. We wish to emphasize here that there is no quarrel qua the assessee’s business activity and sales in principle; as the case may be, although its above supplier has been found as an entry provider only. It is thus deemed appropriate in the larger interest of justice that lumpsum disallowance/addition @ 5% of the assessee’s alleged bogus purchases of Rs.1,21,78,359/-, would be just and proper with a rider that the same shall not be treated as a precedent. The assessee’s book entries are hereby rejected in very terms. Necessary computation shall follow as per law. 4. This Revenue’s appeal is partly allowed.
