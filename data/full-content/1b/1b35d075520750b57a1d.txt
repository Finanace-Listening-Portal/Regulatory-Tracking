Vs ACIT (ITAT Agra) Earlier-Year VAT Deduction Allowed Subject to Verification; Enhanced Audit Fee Deductible: ITAT Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Agra Bench, partly allowed the appeal of Mahim Patran Pvt. Ltd. for Assessment Year 2011-12. The dispute involved four principal adjustments: disallowance of ₹55,39,361 relating to input VAT of earlier financial years, disallowance of ₹1,59,533 towards enhanced auditors’ remuneration, disallowance under Section 14A read with Rule 8D, and disallowance of interest under Section 36(1)(iii). The Tribunal granted conditional relief on the input VAT claim, deleted the enhanced audit fee addition, restricted the Section 14A disallowance to the exempt dividend income of ₹51,151, and sustained the remaining interest disallowance of ₹1,47,980. The assessee carried on the businesses of security printing and ice cream manufacturing. Its original return, filed on 30 September 2011, declared income of ₹3,32,58,158. A revised return filed on 21 March 2013 reduced the declared income to ₹2,75,59,264, principally by claiming enhanced audit fees and input VAT expenditure relating to earlier years. The Assessing Officer rejected those deductions and disallowed the entire interest expenditure of ₹23,70,667 under Sections 14A and 36(1)(iii). The CIT(A) upheld the VAT and audit fee disallowances but substantially reduced the interest-related additions. The assessee challenged the surviving adjustments before the Tribunal. The input VAT dispute arose from the assessee’s position that its security printing receipts were not liable to VAT. Input taxes paid on materials, including paper and ink, had initially been treated as recoverable because the assessee anticipated adjusting them against output VAT. Meanwhile, the underlying sales tax litigation remained unresolved. Following the Allahabad High Court’s interim order dated 24 March 2011 and professional advice, the assessee considered that the accumulated input credit might not be recoverable and claimed ₹55,39,361 as expenditure in AY 2011-12. The Revenue opposed the claim because the expenditure related to earlier years, the High Court had not finally decided the taxability issue, and the relevant order was available before the original return was filed. The Tribunal considered the genuineness of the input VAT expenditure to be undisputed and accepted that the circumstances of the pending litigation reasonably explained the timing of the deduction. Referring to CIT v. Excel Industries Ltd., it considered the year-of-deduction controversy in its factual setting. It also rejected the objection that the claim had been raised through a revised return, noting the absence of established mala fides. Nevertheless, the assessee’s own submissions suggested that certain amounts had already been charged to the profit and loss account for Financial Year 2009-10. To prevent a double deduction, the Tribunal directed the Assessing Officer to verify whether any part of the ₹55,39,361 had already been allowed for AY 2010-11 and grant the deduction accordingly. Thus, the VAT issue was decided in principle in the assessee’s favour, subject to verification. The enhanced audit fee claim concerned ₹1,59,533 relating to Financial Year 2009-10. Although the company had provided for the additional remuneration in its accounts, the increase remained subject to approval by its Board of Directors. The assessee had therefore not claimed the amount for income-tax purposes in the earlier assessment year. Once the Board approved the enhanced remuneration during Financial Year 2010-11, the company claimed the deduction in AY 2011-12. The Tribunal disagreed with the CIT(A)’s conclusion that Board approval was merely a formality. Since the genuineness of the expenditure was not disputed and the issue concerned crystallisation of liability, the Tribunal accepted the claim and deleted the ₹1,59,533 addition. On the Section 14A issue, the assessee had earned exempt dividend income of ₹51,151. The Assessing Officer originally made a disallowance of ₹2,58,462 under Rule 8D, which the CIT(A) reduced to ₹79,909. Before the Tribunal, the assessee relied on judicial authorities supporting the proposition that, for the relevant assessment year, the disallowance could not exceed exempt income actually earned. The Tribunal accepted this contention and directed the Assessing Officer to restrict the disallowance to ₹51,151, granting further relief of ₹28,758. The finding was confined to the applicable law and facts of AY 2011-12. The assessee did not succeed on the remaining ₹1,47,980 interest disallowance under Section 36(1)(iii). It argued that substantial interest-free owned funds were available and relied on decisions concerning the presumption that investments and advances were made from such funds. However, the Tribunal noted that the assessee had not displaced the CIT(A)’s finding that approximately ₹56.39 lakh had been advanced for non-business purposes. Applying the jurisdictional Allahabad High Court’s ruling in CIT v. Sahu Enterprises (P.) Ltd., and in the absence of a factual demonstration connecting the advances to interest-free funds, the Tribunal sustained the disallowance. Introduction The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Agra Bench, accepted a company’s eligibility to claim ₹55,39,361 of earlier-year input VAT expenditure, subject to verification that the deduction had not already been allowed. It also deleted the disallowance of ₹1,59,533 towards enhanced audit fees, accepting the claim following approval by the Board of Directors. The Tribunal separately restricted the Section 14A disallowance to exempt dividend income of ₹51,151, while sustaining interest disallowance of ₹1,47,980 under Section 36(1)(iii). The assessee’s appeal was partly allowed. Background of the Case Mahim Patran Private Limited carried on security printing and ice cream manufacturing. It filed its original return on 30 September 2011, declaring total income of ₹3,32,58,158. A revised return filed on 21 March 2013 declared income of ₹2,75,59,264 after claiming deductions for enhanced audit fees and input VAT relating to earlier years. The Assessing Officer disallowed both claims. He also disallowed the entire interest expenditure of ₹23,70,667 through adjustments under Sections 14A and 36(1)(iii). The CIT(A) upheld the VAT and audit fee disallowances but substantially reduced the interest-related additions. The assessee challenged the remaining disallowances before the Tribunal. Input VAT Claim and Pending Litigation The VAT claim concerned input taxes relating to Financial Years 2007-08 to 2009-10. The assessee disputed whether its security printing receipts were liable to VAT. It explained that input VAT had initially been retained as a recoverable asset because it could potentially be adjusted against output VAT liability. Following the Allahabad High Court’s order dated 24 March 2011 and professional advice, the assessee considered that the input credit might not be capable of adjustment. It therefore claimed deduction in Assessment Year 2011-12. The lower authorities rejected the claim principally because the expenditure related to earlier years and the High Court had granted a stay rather than finally deciding the VAT dispute. VAT Deduction Accepted with Verification The Tribunal noted that the genuineness of the ₹55,39,361 expenditure was undisputed. It accepted that the assessee had reasonable justification for not claiming the amount earlier, given its position in the VAT litigation. Referring to CIT Vs. Excel Industries Ltd., it considered the dispute concerning the year of deduction in its factual setting. The Tribunal also rejected the objection to raising the claim through a revised return. It noted that no mala fides had been established and that deliberations between management and advisers could take time. However, the assessee’s submissions indicated that some amounts had been charged to the profit and loss account in Financial Year 2009-10. Consequently, the Tribunal directed the Assessing Officer to verify whether any part had already been allowed for Assessment Year 2010-11 and allow the deduction accordingly. The relief was therefore subject to preventing a duplicate deduction. Enhanced Audit Fee Allowed The assessee claimed ₹1,59,533 of enhanced auditors’ remuneration relating to Financial Year 2009-10. Although provision had been made in that year’s accounts, the company explained that the increase remained subject to Board approval. It had therefore excluded the amount from the earlier year’s income-tax claim. The Board approved the increase during Financial Year 2010-11, and the assessee claimed deduction for Assessment Year 2011-12. The Tribunal rejected the CIT(A)’s view that Board approval was merely a formality. Since genuineness was undisputed and the controversy concerned timing, it accepted the deduction following approval and deleted the addition. Section 14A Restricted to Exempt Income The assessee earned exempt dividend income of ₹51,151. The Assessing Officer initially computed a Section 14A disallowance of ₹2,58,462. The CIT(A) reduced it to ₹79,909, comprising interest and other expenditure under Rule 8D. Before the Tribunal, the assessee argued that the disallowance could not exceed the exempt income earned during the relevant year. The Tribunal accepted this submission and directed restriction to ₹51,151, granting further relief of ₹28,758. Interest Disallowance Sustained The Tribunal upheld the remaining ₹1,47,980 disallowance under Section 36(1)(iii). The assessee relied on the availability of substantial interest-free own funds. However, the Tribunal noted that it had not displaced the finding that advances of approximately ₹56.39 lakh were for non-business purposes. Following the jurisdictional Allahabad High Court’s decision in CIT Vs. Sahu Enterprises (P.) Ltd., and noting the absence of a factual basis establishing that the advances came from own interest-free funds, it sustained the disallowance. Final Outcome The appeal was partly allowed. The company secured conditional relief on the VAT claim, deletion of the enhanced audit fee addition and reduction of the Section 14A disallowance. The interest disallowance under Section 36(1)(iii) remained sustained. Cases Discussed CIT Vs Excel Industries Ltd. (Supreme Court); (2013) 358 ITR 295 — Expressly considered by the Tribunal in deciding the timing of the input VAT deduction. The Tribunal examined the Supreme Court’s observations concerning the year of taxability and the undesirability of unnecessary disputes over timing where the Revenue suffers no corresponding loss. The decision was also relied upon in the assessee’s submissions regarding enhanced audit fees. CIT Vs Sahu Enterprises (P.) Ltd. (Allahabad High Court); (2013) 31 taxmann.com 270 — Expressly followed by the Tribunal while sustaining the disallowance of ₹1,47,980 under Section 36(1)(iii). The Tribunal reproduced the jurisdictional High Court’s reasoning concerning interest-bearing borrowings, non-business advances and commercial expediency. It found that the assessee had not displaced the finding concerning advances of approximately ₹56.39 lakh for non-business purposes. CIT Vs Corrtech Energy (P.) Ltd. The Tribunal accepted the assessee’s contention that the disallowance for the relevant assessment year should be restricted to the exempt dividend income actually earned.
