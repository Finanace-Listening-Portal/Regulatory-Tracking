Michael Kennadi Vs Revision Authority (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madurai Bench of the Madras High Court dismissed S. Michael Kennadi’s challenge to the rejection of his customs revision application, holding that the dispute was appealable before CESTAT under Section 129A of the Customs Act, 1962, rather than revisable by the Central Government under Section 129DD. The petitioner challenged the confiscation of his domestically registered Maruti Suzuki Wagon R, a redemption fine of ₹1,00,000, confiscation of Indian currency of ₹5,35,000, a penalty of ₹1,50,000 and handling and warehousing charges of ₹6,160 relating to mobile phones. He did not challenge the confiscation of the gold chains. The adjudicating authority had ordered vehicle confiscation under Section 115(2), currency confiscation under Section 121 and a penalty under Section 112(b). The appellate authority reduced his penalty from ₹5,96,000 to ₹1,50,000 while otherwise upholding the adjudication order. The Court examined the first proviso to Section 129A, which excludes specified disputes concerning baggage, goods not unloaded at their destination and drawback from CESTAT’s jurisdiction. It found that the seized cash, mobile phone and car were neither imported nor exported. The car was allegedly intended for transporting smuggled gold, but the gold had not been loaded into it when it was seized. Consequently, the statutory exclusion did not prevent CESTAT from entertaining the appeal, and the Revision Authority lacked jurisdiction under Section 129DD. The Court therefore declined to interfere with the rejection of the revision application. However, it granted liberty to file an appeal before CESTAT within 30 days from receipt of a copy of the judgment and directed the Tribunal, if such an appeal was filed, to decide it on merits and in accordance with law after hearing the petitioner, without further reference to limitation. The confiscation, fine, penalty and charges were not adjudicated on their merits in the writ proceedings. Cases Discussed Earlier proceedings concerning S. Operative portion of the impugned order holding that no revision lies under Section 129DD of the Customs Act, 1962, reads as under: “7.4 Examined on this touchstone, the Government observes that the Applicant has not assailed the confiscation of the gold chains. The reliefs claimed in the instant Revision Application are confined to: (i) the confiscation of the Applicant’s conveyance – Maruti Suzuki Wagon R bearing Registration No. TN 63 AT 1076- ordered under Section 115(2) of the Act, and the redemption fine of Rs. 1,00,000/- relatable thereto; (ii) the absolute confiscation of the Indian currency of Rs. 5,35,000/- ordered under Section 121 of the Act; (iii) the penalty of Rs. 1,50,000/-confirmed upon the Applicant under Section 112(b) of the Act; and (iv) the handling and warehousing charges of Rs. 6,160/- recovered towards release of the mobile phones. 7.5 None of the aforesaid reliefs relates to “any goods imported or exported as baggage” within the meaning of clause (a) of the first proviso to Section 129A(1) of the Act. The Applicant was admittedly not a passenger; he did not import or export any goods as baggage; and he never crossed the Customs barrier. The vehicle in question is an indigenous, domestically registered conveyance, which was seized from the parking area of the airport, outside the Customs area, and its confiscation has been ordered under Section 115(2) of the Act on the finding that it was to be used as a carriage of the smuggled gold. The currency in question is Indian legal tender, recovered from the glove box of the said vehicle, and its confiscation has been ordered under Section 121 of the Act on the finding that the amount was meant for paying the persons involved in the smuggling. Confiscation of a conveyance under Section 115(2) and confiscation of monies under Section 121 are independent measures provided under Chapter XIV of the Act, directed against the instrumentalities and the financing of smuggling; such orders do not determine the classification, dutiability, declaration, confiscation or redemption of any baggage of any passenger. The expression “relates to any goods imported or exported as baggage” occurring in the first proviso takes its colour from disputes arising out of the clearance of baggage under Chapter XI of the Act read with the Baggage Rules, 2016; it cannot be stretched to encompass the confiscation of a domestically registered motor vehicle, or of Indian currency seized outside the Customs area from a person who undertook no travel and carried no baggage. 7.6 In view of the foregoing, the Government holds that the impugned Order-in-Appeal, in so far as it is assailed by the Applicant in the instant Revision Application, is not an order “of the nature referred to in the first proviso to sub-section (1) of Section 129A” of the Act. Consequently, the instant Revision Application does not lie before the Central Government under Section 129DD of the Act and is not maintainable”. 5. The short point that arises for consideration in the facts of this case is as to whether against the order of the Lower Authority as affirmed/modified by the Appellate Authority under Section 128 of the Customs Act, 1962, whether an appeal under Section 129A of the Customs Act, 1962 has to be filed before the Customs and Excise Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT) or a revision has to be filed under Section 129DD of the Customs Act, 1962, where the issue involves imposition of penalty under Section 112(b) of Customs Act, 1962 and redemption of fine under Section 115(2) of the Customs Act, 1962 ordering redemption and penalty. 6. As per Section 129A of the Customs Act, 1962, an Appeal against the order of the Appellate Authority passed under Section 128 of the Customs Act, 1962, lies before the Customs and Excise Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT). (1)Any person aggrieved by any of the following orders may appeal to the Appellate Tribunal against such order- (a) a decision or order passed by the [Commissioner of Customs] [[ Substituted by Act 22 of 1995, Section 50, for ” Collector of Customs” and ” Collector(Appeals)” , respectively (w.e.f. 26-5-1995).]] as an adjudicating authority; As per the first proviso to Section 129A of the Customs Act, 1962, no appeal lie to the Appellate Tribunal and the Appellate Tribunal shall not have jurisdiction to decide any appeal in respect of any order referred to in clause (b) if such order relates to, any goods imported or exported as baggage, any goods loaded in a conveyance for importation into India, but which are not unloaded at their place of destination in India, or so much of the quantity of such goods as has not been unloaded at any such destination if goods unloaded at such destination are short of the quantity required to be unloaded at that destination and payment of drawback as provided in Chapter X, and the rules made thereunder. 17. Reading of the first proviso to Section 129A of the Customs Act 1962, thus makes it clear that the Tribunal is not empowered to dispose the Appeal in respect of (a), (b), (c) of the first proviso to Section 129A. 18. The order of the Lower Authority and the First Appellate Authority, as also the Revision Authority under Section 129DD of the Customs Act, 1962, makes it clear that the goods that were seized were cash, mobile phone and car. They are neither exported nor imported. The car that was seized was allegedly meant for smuggling gold item had not been loaded on its seizure. Thus, there is no bar on the Tribunal to entertain the Appeal against the First Appellate Authority order under section 129A of the Customs Act, 1962. Therefore, by implication, the jurisdiction of the first respondent under Section 129DD of the Customs Act, 1962, is barred. 19. Therefore, the impugned order passed by the first respondent/Revision Authority under Section 129DD of the Customs Act, 1962, does not merit any interference. This Writ Petition is therefore liable to be dismissed. However, liberty is given to the petitioner to approach the Appellate Tribunal under Section 129A of the Customs Act, 1962, within a period of thirty (30) days from the date of receipt of a copy of this order. In case such an appeal is filed before the Appellate Tribunal, the Appellate Tribunal shall dispose of the Appeal on merits and in accordance with law, after hearing the petitioner, without further reference to the limitation. 20. This Writ Petition is dismissed, with the above liberty. No costs. Consequently, connected miscellaneous petition is closed.
