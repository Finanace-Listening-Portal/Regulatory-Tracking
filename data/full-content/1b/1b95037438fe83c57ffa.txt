Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Commissioner State Tax (Through Neeraj Shukla) Vs Acevector Limited (GSTAT Agra) Summary: The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), State Bench Agra, held that penalty under Section 129(3) of the CGST/UPGST Act, 2017, could not be sustained merely on account of non-updating of Part-B of an e-way bill in the absence of evidence establishing an intention to evade tax. The Tribunal also examined the territorial jurisdiction of officers of a transit State in respect of interstate movement of goods. Accordingly, it dismissed the Department’s appeal and upheld the First Appellate Authority’s order setting aside the penalty of Rs.5,12,676 imposed against Acevector Limited. The taxpayer was transporting goods from Gurugram, Haryana, to Hosur, Tamil Nadu, when the vehicle was intercepted by the Mobile Squad Officer at Khadera, Lalitpur, Uttar Pradesh, on 18 September 2024. At the time of inspection, the person in charge of the vehicle produced two e-tax invoices dated 13 September 2024 and 14 September 2024, together with the corresponding Part-A slips of the e-way bills. However, the conveyance particulars in Part-B of the e-way bills had not been updated. Treating non-updating of Part-B as a violation of the GST provisions, the proper officer detained the goods and vehicle under Section 129(1) and issued an order in Form GST MOV-06. A notice under Section 129(3) was subsequently issued on 20 September 2024. Although the taxpayer furnished an updated e-way bill along with a written explanation, the proper officer rejected the explanation and imposed a penalty through an order dated 21 September 2024. The goods and vehicle were released upon payment of the penalty. The taxpayer challenged the penalty before the First Appellate Authority under Section 107 of the CGST/UPGST Act. By an order dated 7 January 2025, the First Appellate Authority allowed the appeal and set aside the penalty. Aggrieved by this decision, the State Tax Department preferred a second appeal before the Tribunal under Section 112. The Department contended that furnishing conveyance details in Part-B of an e-way bill was a mandatory requirement under Rule 138 of the CGST/SGST Rules and that transportation of goods without completing Part-B rendered the e-way bill invalid. It argued that the requirement was substantive rather than merely procedural, as it enabled tracking of goods and prevention of tax evasion. According to the Department, updating Part-B after interception could not cure the original statutory violation, and the First Appellate Authority had erroneously treated the omission as a minor technical mistake. The taxpayer raised a preliminary objection regarding maintainability of the departmental appeal. It submitted that the disputed amount was Rs.4,18,717, which was below the monetary threshold prescribed by the Commissioner of State Tax through Circular No.2425008 dated 5 July 2024. On merits, the taxpayer submitted that both the buyer and seller were bona fide registered dealers, the e-invoices contained the e-way bill numbers, and the vehicle registration number was duly mentioned in the transport bilty. Since the e-invoices had been generated through the GST portal and auto-populated in GSTR-1, complete transaction information was available to the Department even before commencement of transportation. The taxpayer further pointed out that neither the show cause notice nor the penalty order contained any allegation of undervaluation, misdescription, non-payment of tax, diversion of goods or reuse of e-way bills. The sole ground for initiating proceedings was non-updating of Part-B. Relying upon the Allahabad High Court judgment in Citykart Retail Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner Commercial Tax, the taxpayer contended that penalty under Section 129(3) could not be sustained without establishing mens rea or intention to evade tax. On the preliminary objection concerning monetary limits, the Tribunal examined Clause 4 of the circular dated 5 July 2024, which prescribed exceptions permitting departmental appeals irrespective of the monetary threshold. These exceptions included matters involving constitutional validity, validity of statutory rules or notifications, recurring issues concerning interpretation of law, valuation, classification, refunds, place of supply and cases which, in the Commissioner’s opinion, required contesting in the interest of justice or revenue. The Tribunal relied particularly on Clause 4(vi), which permitted filing of an appeal where the Commissioner considered it necessary in the interest of justice or revenue. It observed that where an appeal was filed on the basis of such an opinion, the Commissioner was not required to state the grounds or reasons for forming that opinion. Accordingly, the taxpayer’s preliminary objection regarding maintainability of the departmental appeal was rejected, and the Tribunal proceeded to examine the substantive dispute. On merits, the Tribunal found that the Mobile Squad Officer had imposed the penalty solely on account of violation of the e-way bill provisions without recording any finding regarding intention to evade tax. Even in its grounds of appeal before the Tribunal, the Department had not alleged any underlying intention to evade tax and had instead sought to justify the penalty exclusively on the basis of statutory non-compliance. The Tribunal referred to the Allahabad High Court decisions in Satyam Traders Vs State of U.P. and Hindustan Herbal Cosmetics Vs State of U.P., which recognised the importance of establishing mens rea in proceedings under Section 129 involving technical deficiencies in e-way bills. The Tribunal identified three principles emerging from these judgments: mens rea was a necessary ingredient for Section 129 proceedings; genuine commercial documentation negated the presumption of tax evasion; and the authorities were required to record reasoned findings regarding intention to evade tax rather than merely reproduce the statutory contravention. Applying these principles, the Tribunal observed that the transaction involved two bona fide registered dealers and that the e-invoices had been generated before movement of goods, making the transaction particulars available on the GST portal and in the taxpayer’s GSTR-1. It found the facts comparable to those considered by the Allahabad High Court in Citykart Retail Pvt. Ltd., where penalty proceedings had been set aside because non-filling of Part-B was the sole allegation and there was no allegation of transportation without payment of tax. Consequently, the Tribunal held that the First Appellate Authority was justified in quashing the penalty. The Tribunal additionally examined the territorial jurisdiction of the Uttar Pradesh GST authorities. It noted that the goods originated in Haryana and were destined for Tamil Nadu, while Uttar Pradesh was merely a transit State. Referring to the Allahabad High Court judgment dated 14 May 2026 in Maruti Enterprises Vs State of U.P., the Tribunal observed that inspection and verification of goods in transit were distinct from exercise of penal jurisdiction under Section 129. Following that judgment, it noted that where an interstate consignment merely passed through a State without any tax implication arising therein, the transit State’s officers could communicate identified documentary deficiencies to the jurisdictional authorities of the originating or destination State rather than exercise penal jurisdiction in the circumstances described in that judgment. The Tribunal concluded that the departmental appeal disclosed no material factual or legal infirmity in the order of the First Appellate Authority. Although the preliminary objection regarding the monetary threshold was rejected, the Department failed to establish its case on merits. The appellate order setting aside the penalty of Rs.5,12,676 was accordingly affirmed, and the departmental appeal was dismissed without any order as to costs. Cases Discussed: Citykart Retail Pvt. Present appeal has been preferred u/s 112 of CGST/UPGST, Act, 2017 by the Revenue/State of U.P, against the order dated 07-01-2025 passed by Additional Commissioner Gr. II (Appeal-II), State Tax, Jhansi in Appeal no.-AD0912240275090, M/s Acevector Limited GSTIN: 06AABCJ8820B1ZJ Temporary ID: 092400034870TMP Vs Assistant Commissioner, Mobile Squad, Masaura, Lalitpur, Jhansi, whereby penalty of Rs. 5,12,676/- imposed by Adjudicating officer u/s 129(3) of CGST/UPGST, Act has been set aside. For shake of convenience here-in-after in the order, appellant and respondent shall be addressed as “Department” and “Taxpayer”, respectively. 2. Factual matrix of the case is delineated below: The goods being transported from Gurugram (Haryana) to Hosur (Tamil Nadu) by vehicle number HR39D2201 which was intercepted by Mobile Squad Officer on 18.09.2024 at 13:43 hrs, at a place called Khadera, Lalitpur (U.P). 3. At the time of inspection, the person in charge of the vehicle produced before the proper officer the documents relating to the goods loaded in the vehicle, namely, e-Tax Invoice No. SD/M/2425/2431 dated 14-09-2024 issued by the taxpayer M/S Acevector Limited, Gurugram (GSTIN-06AABCJ8820B1ZY) and the corresponding e-Way Bill Part-A Slip Unique No. 3118 4575 6204 and e-Tax Invoice No. SD/M/2425/4181 dated 13-09-2024 and the corresponding e-Way Bill Part-A Slip Unique No. 3618 4576 0312. 4. Considering the non-updating of Part B of the e-way bill submitted in respect of the goods being transported as a violation of the provisions of the CGST/UPGST Act and the relevant Rules, the Proper Officer detained the goods and vehicle under Sub-section (1) Section 129 of the CGST Act, by an order in FORM GST MOV-06. 5. A notice was issued under sub-section (3) of Section 129, dated 20-09-2024. The taxpayer submitted an updated e-way bill with a written explanation, but the Proper Officer rejected the explanation and passed an order under sub-section (3) of Section 129, dated 21-09-2024. Upon payment of the penalty imposed, by the taxpayer, the goods and vehicle were released by the Proper Officer. 6. Aggrieved by the order passed by the proper officer, the taxpayer filed an appeal before Additional Commissioner Appeals, u/s 107 of CGST/UPGST, Act. Such appeal filed by the taxpayer was accepted by the First Appellate Authority vide the impugned order dated 07-01-2025, setting aside the penalty imposed. 7. Being aggrieved with the order of first appellate authority, this appeal/application has been filed by the department before the Tribunal. 8. Shri Mukesh Kumar Deputy Commissioner and departmental authorised representative, appeared on behalf of the Revenue and reiterating the grounds of appeal made following submission. 9. Stating that the impugned order passed by the Appellate Authority is erroneous in law and on facts and is liable to be set aside, he argued that the respondent has contravened the provisions of Rule 138 of the CGST/SGST Rules by not furnishing the details of conveyance in Part-B of the E-way Bill, which is mandatory for movement of goods, hence this mandatory requirement is violated. 10. He further submitted that the movement of goods without valid completion of E-way Bill (i.e., without Part-B details) renders the E-way Bill invalid, and therefore the goods were being transported in contravention of the provisions of the Act and Rules. 11. He also submitted that the requirement of filling Part-B is not a mere procedural formality but a substantive requirement to track movement of goods and prevent tax evasion. Post-interception compliance invalid Generation of Part-B after interception is an afterthought and does not cure violation. 12. On the basis of above submission, he argued that the Appellate Authority has erred in treating the lapse as a minor/technical mistake, ignoring the statutory mandate under the GST law and the order passed has resulted in undue relief to the respondent and is prejudicial to the interest of revenue. 13. Submitting these arguments, he prayed that the impugned order be set aside and the order passed by the proper officer be restored in the interest of justice and revenue. 14. Shri Akash Singhal, learned counsel appearing for the taxpayer, submitted that the first appellate order was supported by facts and in accordance with the legal principles laid down by the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in series of cases. 15. Submitting a written submission he further argued that the amount in dispute is only Rs. 4,18,717 which is much below the monetary threshold limit prescribed by Commissioner State Tax vide his own circular No. 2425008 dated 05.07.2024 to file an appeal before the Tribunal. In view of the above circular this appeal filed by the department is not maintainable and is liable to rejection in limine. 16. He further stated that in the case in question, both the buyer and seller are bonafide dealers and that e-invoices were issued for the transaction, which contained the e-way bill number and the vehicle number was duly mentioned on the transport bilty. 17. The e-invoices were issued through the GST portal and auto-populated in the return GSTR-1. Therefore, there is no possibility of any tax evasion, as complete transaction information was available on the portal prior to the movement of goods. 18. He Further stated that the the Notice and Order contain no allegation of under valuation, mis-description, tax non-payment, diversion of goods or reuse of e-way bills. As recorded by the First Appellate Authority, the only ground throughout was non-updating of Part-B of e-way bill. 19. He also submitted that in the case of Citykart Retail Pvt. Ltd. v. Commissioner Commercial Tax, [2022] taxmann.com 155 (All): where the only allegation leading to seizure was that Part-B was not filled and there was no allegation that the goods were transported without payment of tax, Hon’ble Allahabad High Cort set aside the order imposing penalty. Learned first Appellate Authority has passed the impugned order on the basis of the ratio decided Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in this case and in several other cases. 20. Citing some other cases he further argued that without establishing mens rea and intention to evade tax, penalty under section 129(3) cannot be sustained. 21. We have heard the learned authorized representatives of respective parties, perused the materials on record and evaluated the relevant provisions of the law applicable to the facts of the case in hand. 22. As far as the question of admissibility of appeal on the basis of monetary limit mentioned in the circular dated 05-07-2024 is concerned, the exceptional circumstances in which this monetary limit will not be applicable are mentioned in Paragraph 4 of this circular itself. Clause 4 of the said circular is as follows 4- EXCLUSIONS Monetary limits specified above for filing appeal or application by the department before GSTAT or High Court and for filing Special Leave Petition or appeal before the Supreme Court shall be applicable in all cases, except in the following circumstances where the decision to file appeal shall be taken on merits irrespective of the said monetary limits: i) Where any provision of the CGST Act or UPGST Act or IGST Act or GST (Compensation to States) Act has been held to be ultra vires to the Constitution of India; or ii) Where any Rules or regulations made under CGST Act or UPGST Act or IGST Act or GST (Compensation to States) Act have been held to be ultra vires the parent Act; or iii) Where any order, notification, instruction, or circular issued by the Government or the Board has been held to be ultra vires of the CGST Act or UPGST Act or IGST Act or GST (Compensation to States) Act or the Rules made thereunder;or iv) Where the matter is related to – a) Valuation of goods or services; or b) Classification of goods or services; or c) Refunds; or d) Place of Supply; or e)Any other issue, which is recurring in nature and/or involves interpretation of the provision of the Act /the Rules/ notification/circular/order/instruction etc; or v) Where structures/adverse comments have been passed and/or cost has been imposed against the Government/Department or their officers; or vi) Any other case or class of cases, where in the opinion of the Commissioner, it is necessary to contest in the interest of justice or revenue. 23. So, the subclause vi of clause 4 of the said circular permits to file the appeal in the cases where in the opinion of the Commissioner, it is necessary to contest in the interest of justice or revenue. In cases where the appeal is filed on the basis of the opinion of the Commissioner, the opinion of the Commissioner cannot be challenged and the Commissioner is not required to state the grounds or reasons for his opinion. Hence the objection raised by the respondent on the maintainability of the present appeal is not sustainable and rejected accordingly. 24. Now we return to the merit of the case. In the present case, the only objection raised by the mobile squad officer is that the part B of the E- Way Bill was not updated and due to this non updating he inferred that goods were being transported in contravention of provisions of act and rules. 25. Learned mobile squad officer has imposed a penalty solely for the violation of provisions. No finding of intention to evade tax is mentioned in his order. 26. It is also pertinent to mention that the department has nowhere in the grounds of appeal has taken stand that in this case there is any underlying intention to evade tax; rather the attempt is to justify the imposition of penalty based on the violation of provisions. 27. The question for consideration now is whether penalty under Section 129(3) can be imposed merely on the basis of technical errors or whether intention to evade tax is sine qua non for invoking section 129. 28. Hon’ble Allahabad High Court has repeatedly held that mens rea / intention to evade tax is an essential requirement for imposing penalty under section 129 of the CGST/UPGST Act, particularly where alleged violation is merely technical and the transaction is otherwise genuine. 29. In Writ Tax No. 1095 of 2025 decided on 19 March 2025 M/S Satyam Traders Vs. State of U.P. and others Hon. Allahabad High Court held: “Since no mens rea to evade tax was there, we are of the view that the detention proceedings along with order under Section 129(3) of the Act are arbitrary and invalid in law” 30. Similarly in WRIT TAX No. – 1400 of 2019 M/S. HINDUSTAN HERBAL COSMETICS VS STATE OF U.P. AND 2 OTHERS decided on 2-1-2024 Court held: “That the presence of mens rea for evasion of tax is a sine qua non for imposition of penalty. An error in the e-way bill without any further material to substantiate the intention to evade tax should not and cannot lead to imposition of penalty”. 31. Taking together, the core operative principles emerging from these decisions can be summarized as: 1- Mens rea is a necessary ingredient for section 129 proceedings 2- Presence of genuine commercial documentation negates presumption of evasion 3-There should be reasoned findings on intent; mere recitation of contravention is insufficient The facts of the present case are required to be analysed in the light of the above principles. 32. In the present case, the transaction in question is between two bonafide registered dealers and the e-Invoices were generated on the portal before the movement of goods, making all transaction information available on the portal and auto-populating the taxpayer’s return GSTR-1, making it impossible to avoid paying the tax due on it. Therefore, no intention of tax evasion is established in the case in question. 33. Facts of the case in question are identical to the facts of the case M/s Citykart Retail Pvt. Ltd. v. Commissioner Commercial Tax, Writ C No. 22285 of 2019, decided on 06.09.2022, where the only allegation leading to the seizure was that Part-B was not filled and there was no allegation that the goods were transported without payment of tax, the orders were set aside. In this case Hon’ble Allahabad High Court observed “In the present case, prima-facie no intent to evade the duty can be ascertained, only on the allegation that Part-B of the e-way bill was not filled” The same view has been taken by the Hon’ble Court in many other cases also. 34. In light of the foregoing discussion in our view the taxpayer has rebutted that e-way bill part-B was left blank with intent to evade tax/ mens rea, hence in our opinion, the decision of the first appellate authority to quash the penalty order is entirely justified and supported by the materials available. 35. Before parting, it is necessary to mention another important fact of this case, it is not in dispute that origin state of the said transaction of goods was Haryana and destination state was Tamil Nadu. Uttar Pradesh is a transit state simpliciter. No tax event is about to happen in transit state of U.P where movement was duly covered with papers required in law. Recently, in a landmark judgement dated MAY 14, 2026, rendered by Allahabad High Court, M/s Maruti enterprises vs State of U.P, in WRIT TAX NO. 1423, 1573, 2001, 2445, AND 2451 OF 2026, It is held that under GST frame work a transit state has a limited power of inspection and verification of goods but cannot exercise penal jurisdiction u/s 129 of CGST/SGST Act. When the supply originates outside the transit state and is destined outside it. No tax incidence either under CGST/SGST or IGST accrues to or is payable in U.P for an interstate consignment. It is further held that verification of goods in transit and imposition of tax/penalty are two distinct powers. Where goods are merely passing through a state with no tax implication arising therein, authorities of such state cannot invoke powers conferred on them by virtue of section 129. It is further held that if transit state officer notices a potential document deficiency or cross unit anomaly such officer may point out the same and intimate to jurisdictional GST authorities either to origin state or destination state. Hon’ble High court has also held in that judgement that seizing of goods and imposing penal demands by an officer of transit state violates the constitutional guarantee for trade and commerce enshrined in article 301 of constitution of India. 36. In the light of the above discussion, the departmental appeal does not contain any material fact or legal aspect that would suggest any infirmity in the impugned order. Therefore, the order passed by the first appellate authority is affirmed, and the departmental appeal is dismissed. 37. No order as to cost. 38. No interim application is pending in present appeal. 39. Order Pronounced today on 19-11-2026 in open court. 40. Order be communicated to all concerned, as per rules.
