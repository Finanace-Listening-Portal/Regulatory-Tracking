Articles 5 min read Silver prices are tarnished but not forgotten. Why HSBC thinks gains in white metal may be limited? Silver prices have plunged nearly 60% from their January record high, but HSBC expects limited gains ahead. The bank sees prices consolidating and edging higher in 2027, supported by gold, while rising mine output, recycling supply, weaker industrial demand, a firm US dollar and elevated yields could cap rallies. The sharp reversal followed a record-setting rally, with surging oil prices amid the Iran war stoking inflation concerns and shifting US Federal Reserve rate expectations, weighing heavily on precious metals.Global investment bank HSBC says silver had tumbled to nearly $54 an ounce before prices stabilised. While there is room for further gains, the upside is likely to be limited. Gold prices will remain a key driver for silver, with HSBC maintaining a moderately positive outlook on bullion, which could help support the white metal. Advt HSBC believes silver prices are close to fundamentally justified levels and remains mildly positive on the metal. Industrial and jewellery demand is expected to weaken further, while institutional purchases, particularly from China and India, should support demand for large bars. Demand for coins and small bars may also recover, though at a moderate pace as high prices continue to weigh on buyers. Silver ETFs, which saw outflows earlier this year, are rebuilding their holdings.However, a firm US dollar, as forecast by HSBC FX Research, and elevated yields could cap gains. If the anticipated interest rate hikes fail to materialise, silver prices could move higher. Fiscal profligacy and geopolitical risks are also expected to support prices.Also read: Nifty's PEG ratio near 10-year low but why don't stocks look cheap yet? Valuation paradox explainedSupply to weigh on pricesSilver supply is expected to rise despite challenges to increasing mine output at elevated prices. Most mined silver is produced as a by-product of gold and base metals, limiting the scope for a rapid supply response to higher prices. Lower ore grades and mine closures this year are expected to be largely offset by new projects and capacity expansions. However, mine output is likely to increase significantly in 2027, HSBC said.Mining costs remain well below prevailing silver prices, providing an incentive to raise production. Recycling is also expected to increase as bullion holders sell their holdings amid concerns over further price declines. Hedge supply is rising as well. Here’s how to check statusDeficit to narrowHSBC expects the silver market deficit to narrow sharply from 140 million ounces (moz) in 2025 to 5 moz in 2026, based on its supply-demand model and data from the Silver Institute’s 2026 Survey. The deficit is projected to widen slightly to 12 moz in 2027, with the narrowing this year partly attributed to investor liquidation in the first half of 2026.The moderating deficits support HSBC’s view that silver prices are close to equilibrium levels, limiting the potential for strong rallies. However, persistent shortages of high-purity .9999 silver Advt could provide price support. Over the longer term, above-ground stocks and rising recycling supply could cap sharp rallies and weigh on prices.Silver back to $100 levels?The Middle East conflict triggered a chain of events that pushed silver prices lower. Rising oil prices intensified inflationary pressures, shifting monetary policy expectations and weighing on silver. Developments in Iran and expectations surrounding new Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh’s anti-inflationary policies have shifted market expectations from interest rate cuts at the start of the year to a more neutral stance, followed by the possibility of rate hikes. Advt These changing expectations, along with a stronger US dollar, weakened the case for holding silver and other precious metals. Declining investor and industrial demand added to the pressure on prices. Meanwhile, significant silver stocks held on COMEX began flowing back to London in the second quarter of 2026, easing physical market tightness and further weighing on prices.The silver market may now be entering a consolidation phase, with prices likely to trend slightly higher. HSBC’s mildly positive outlook on gold is expected to support silver, helping limit downside. However, the brokerage does not expect a sharp rally in the near term and instead anticipates more moderate gains in 2027.A strong US dollar, elevated yields in the US and other markets, weaker industrial and jewellery demand, and rising mine and recycling supply are likely to weigh on prices. Unless tariff concerns flare up again, HSBC does not expect silver to reach $100 an ounce in the foreseeable future.Not a lot of downside eitherHSBC said the sharp correction in silver prices has cleared out weaker long positions and brought prices closer to levels justified by fundamentals. This could help establish a floor for prices and encourage bullish investors to re-enter the market. While tighter monetary policies in the US and elsewhere remain a headwind, these expectations are already largely priced in.
