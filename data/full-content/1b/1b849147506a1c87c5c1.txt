Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Punjab National Bank Vs Rabin Das (NCLT Kolkata) Summary: The National Company Law Tribunal, Kolkata Bench-II, in Punjab National Bank Vs Rabin Das, admitted an application filed by Punjab National Bank under Section 95(1) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, seeking commencement of insolvency resolution proceedings against Rabin Das, personal guarantor of Purulia Metal Casting Pvt. Ltd. The proceedings arose from credit facilities extended to the corporate borrower, secured by personal guarantees executed on 18 February 2021 and 10 September 2021. The bank stated that the outstanding debt amounted to ₹75,32,16,880.52 as on 31 January 2024. The Tribunal examined the guarantee documents, the bank’s demand notices, the Resolution Professional’s report and the absence of objections from the guarantor before admitting the petition. The financial creditor had invoked the personal guarantee through a recall notice dated 5 April 2023, demanding payment of ₹75,16,58,814.64 outstanding as on 31 March 2023. The Tribunal identified 12 April 2023 as the date of default, following expiry of the seven-day payment period. Subsequently, the bank issued a demand notice dated 24 October 2024 in Form B under Rule 7(1) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority for Insolvency Resolution Process for Personal Guarantors to Corporate Debtors) Rules, 2019. The Tribunal recorded that this notice was served on 2 November 2024. It further found that the Section 95 petition, filed in March 2025, was within the prescribed limitation period. This conclusion is significant when compared with other decisions examining the consequences of delayed invocation of personal guarantees, including State Bank of India Vs Suranjan Kumar Santosh Chatterjee (NCLT Mumbai) and Canara Bank Limited Vs Sanjana Uday Desai (NCLT Mumbai). The corporate debtor had separately undergone corporate insolvency resolution proceedings initiated on an application by Subhadra Commercial Private Limited under Section 9 of the IBC. Those proceedings were admitted on 18 October 2023 and disposed of on 7 January 2025. Punjab National Bank received ₹51,40,66,591 under the approved resolution plan. The Resolution Professional recorded a remaining amount of ₹28,38,95,743.52 as payable by the personal guarantor, subject to the further interest mentioned in his report. The report proceeded on the basis that the liability of the corporate debtor and guarantor was co-extensive. The Tribunal considered the earlier recovery and the outstanding liability while deciding whether to admit the personal insolvency application. The Resolution Professional, Rajnandan Kumar, appointed under Section 97, examined the creditor’s application, supporting documents and communications issued to the guarantor. A request for information and proof of repayment was sent to Rabin Das on 21 August 2025, but no reply was received. The Resolution Professional recommended admission under Section 99, observing that the application was complete, the prescribed demand notice had been issued and the guarantor had failed to establish repayment. The Tribunal also recorded that, despite being granted ten days on 9 September 2025 to object to the Resolution Professional’s report, the personal guarantor did not file any objection. The decision may be read alongside Iqbal Jumabhoy Vs Manoj Kumar Anand (NCLAT Chennai), which addresses procedural opportunities in personal guarantor insolvency proceedings. On examination of the documents and the Resolution Professional’s recommendation, the NCLT concluded that the requirements of Section 95 read with Rule 7 of the Personal Guarantor Rules, 2019 had been satisfied. It accordingly admitted the application under Section 100 of the IBC. The Tribunal directed that a moratorium under Section 101 would commence in relation to the guarantor’s debts and continue for 180 days from admission or until an order on the repayment plan under Section 114, whichever occurred earlier. During the moratorium, pending proceedings concerning debts would stand stayed, creditors would be restrained from initiating proceedings concerning debts, and the debtor would be prohibited from transferring, alienating, encumbering or disposing of assets or interests covered by the statutory restrictions. The Tribunal retained Rajnandan Kumar as Resolution Professional and directed compliance with Sections 102 to 106 of the IBC, including publication of notice inviting creditors’ claims, preparation of the list of creditors, assistance in preparing a repayment plan and submission of the required report. The order required public notice within seven days and invited claims within twenty-one days of its issue. It also prescribed further procedural steps for considering the repayment plan and, if necessary, convening a creditors’ meeting. The matter was listed for further consideration on 10 December 2026. The ruling illustrates the operation of the personal guarantor insolvency framework where a bank establishes invocation of guarantee, timely filing and compliance with statutory procedure, and the guarantor does not contest the Resolution Professional’s findings. ANALYSIS AND FINDINGS I. We have Heard Learned Counsels appearing for the Financial Creditor and perused the report of the RP and documents placed on record. II. This Adjudicating Authority notes that, vide its daily order dated 09.09.2025, the Personal Guarantor was granted ten (10) days’ time to file objections to the RP’s Report. However, no such reply or objection has been filed to date. Accordingly, this Adjudicating Authority proceeds to decide the matter on merits, based on the material and information available on record. III. We observe that owing to the continuing defaults made by the Principal Borrower, the Applicant Bank on 05.04.20237 issued recall notice to the personal guarantor invoking the guarantee and demanding to pay jointly and severally with other Borrower and guarantors an aggregate outstanding amount of Rs. 75,16,58,814.64 as on 31.03.2023 and the same was payable within seven (7) days from the receipt of the notice. Since no payment was received by the personal guarantor within the due date hence the date of default is 12.04.2023. IV. Subsequently, the Principal Borrower was admitted into Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (‘CIRP’) vide order dated 18.10.20238 on an application filed under Section 9 of the IBC by one M/s Subhadra Commercial Private Limited. V. Thereafter, the Applicant Bank issued demand notice dated 24.10.20249 in Form-B under Rule 7(1) of the PG Rules, 2019 and the said notice has been served upon the guarantor on 02.11.2024 according to track report attached with the Form-B. VI. Regarding limitation period we find that the applicant issued a recall notice dated 05.04.2023 in their address being S/o Dhananjay Das, Bally Jagachha, Harora, West Bengal- 711204 wherein requesting the Guarantor to pay the outstanding debt in default being a sum of Rs. 75,16,58,814.64 within seven days from the receipt of the notice. The present Company petition being no- 157/KB/2025 was filed on 24.03.2025 so it’s well within the prescribed period of limitation. VII. The present application is complete as required under Section 95 of the IBC read with Rule 7 of the PG Rules, 2019. VIII. We have considered the report and perused the details of claim indicated therein and we are satisfied with the recommendation of the RP to admit the application. 10. In absence of any dispute or controversies raised by the Personal Guarantor, this application is admitted with the following directions. I. This application is admitted under Section 100; II. In terms of Section 101: A moratorium shall commence in relation to all the debts and shall cease to have effect at the end of the period of one hundred and eighty days beginning with the date of admission of the application or on the date this Adjudicating Authority passes an order on the repayment plan under section 114, whichever is earlier. During the moratorium period— a) Any pending legal action or proceeding in respect of any debt shall be deemed to have been stayed; b) The creditors shall not initiate any legal action or legal proceedings in respect of any debt; and c) The debtor shall not transfer, alienate, encumber or dispose of any of his assets or his legal rights or beneficial interest therein; 4) In relation to a firm, the moratorium under sub-section (1) shall operate against all the partners of the firm. 5) The provisions of this section shall not apply to such transactions as may be notified by the Central Government in consultation with any financial sector regulator. 6) In absence of any prayers from any quarters against the nominated Resolution Professional, the IRP i.e., Mr. Rajnandan Kumar will act as the Resolution Professional. 7) The said RP shall act in terms of Section 102, 103, 104 of the Code, to cause public notice, invite claims from creditors, prepare list of creditors, and hold regular meeting as directed hereunder: III. In terms of Section 102: 1) The Resolution Professional shall cause a public notice within seven days of passing the order under section 100 inviting claims from all creditors within twenty-one days of such issue. 2) The notice under sub-section (1) shall include— a) Details of the order admitting the application; b) Particulars of the resolution professional with whom the claims are to be registered; and c) The last date for submission of claims. 3) The notice shall be— a) Published in at least one English and one vernacular newspaper which is in circulation in the state where the debtor resides; b) Placed on the website of the Adjudicating Authority. IV. In terms of Section 103: a) The creditors shall register claims with the resolution professional by sending details of the claims by way of electronic communications or through courier, speed post or registered letter. b) In addition to the claims referred to in sub-section (1), the creditor shall provide to the resolution professional, personal information and such particulars as may be prescribed. V. In terms of Section 104: The resolution professional shall, within 30 days from the date of notice prepare a list of creditors on the basis of— a) the information disclosed in the application filed by the debtor under section 94 or 95, as the case may be; and; b) claims received by the resolution professional under section 102. VI. In terms of Section 105: The Resolution Professional shall assist the debtor in preparing repayment plan containing a proposal to the creditors for restricting of his debts or affairs. VII. In terms of Section 106: The Resolution Professional shall submit the repayment plan under Section 105 along with his report, within 21 days from the last date of submission of claims under Section 102 specifying. a) That the repayment plan is in compliance with the provisions of any law for the time being in force; b) That the repayment plan has a reasonable prospect of being approved and implemented; and c) Whether there is a necessity of summoning a meeting of the creditors, if required, to consider the repayment plan: Meeting of creditors shall be held if necessary, specifying the- a) Date, Time and Place of meeting after consulting the creditors; b) Within 14 to 28 days from submission of its report; c) After issuance notice for meeting at least 14 days in advance, to all the creditors mentioned in the list of creditors. VIII. The Resolution Professional shall prepare a report of the meeting in accordance with Section 112 and furnish a report to this Adjudicating Authority. 11.
