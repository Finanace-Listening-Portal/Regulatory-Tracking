Articles 2 min read Blackstone Ropes in Another HDFC Ergo Ex-exec for Insurance Venture A Fresh Bet Ritesh Kumar joins Anuj Tyagi as a partner with 10 % stake in proposed entity shilpy sinha ET Bureau Published On Nov 25, 2026 at 07:28 AM IST var news_msid = 136145701; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Mumbai: Blackstone has brought in former HDFC Ergo MD and CEO Ritesh Kumar as a 10% shareholder in its proposed general insurance venture in India, reducing its stake to 80% from 90%. Kumar joins former HDFC Ergo chief Anuj Tyagi, who had earlier partnered with Blackstone with a 10% stake in a new venture targeting the country's nearly ₹3 lakh crore non-life insurance industry.The proposed joint venture will see Blackstone hold an 80% stake, with Tyagi and Kumar owning 10% each. The venture has filed its R1 application with the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (Irdai) and is awaiting regulatory approvals to advance its licensing process. Advt With an initial capital commitment of ₹150-200 crore, the company plans to build a nationwide general insurance business, with additional capital to be infused as the business expands.The venture aims to differentiate itself through an AI-native operating model, building its technology infrastructure from the ground up rather than relying on conventional legacy platforms. Its proposed policy administration system will incorporate generative AI and agentic AI capabilities across underwriting, claims management, customer servicing and distribution.India's general insurance market, estimated at over ₹3 lakh crore, is growing at 9-10% annually, supported by increasing demand for health, motor and commercial insurance. The government's decision to raise the FDI limit in insurance to 100% earlier this year has further opened the sector to global investors.The proposed venture will be among the new insurance applicants following the liberalisation of foreign ownership norms. Manulife, in a joint venture with Mahindra, is setting up a life insurance company, while Prudential Plc has set up a new health insurance venture in India, with a 70% stake under a joint venture structure.Recently, Blackstone, through its Singapore-based affiliate Ingot SG, announced a primary equity investment of ₹5,000 crore in IIFL Finance for an approximately 16% stake.Blackstone has around $1.4 trillion in assets under management across global investment strategies focused on real estate, private equity, credit, infrastructure, life sciences, growth equity, secondaries and hedge funds. In India, Blackstone operates real estate and private equity franchises, alongside growing credit, insurance and infrastructure businesses.
