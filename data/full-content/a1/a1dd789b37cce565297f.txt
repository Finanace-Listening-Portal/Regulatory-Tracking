Summary: Ind AS 20 does not permit a grant to be recognised merely because cash has arrived or an approval letter exists. Recognition starts only when there is reasonable assurance that the entity will comply with the attached conditions and that the grant will be received. Thereafter, the grant is recognised in profit or loss systematically over the periods in which the related costs are recognised. This guide builds an accounting control file around those principles, explains the two permitted balance-sheet presentations for asset-related grants, the presentation choices for income grants, the treatment of non-monetary grants, forgivable and below-market government loans, repayment accounting, impairment review and the disclosures required by paragraph 39. Key Takeaways Show all 24 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Start with scope before choosing an accounting entry Ind AS 20 applies to accounting for and disclosure of government grants and to disclosure of other forms of government assistance by entities that prepare financial statements under Indian Accounting Standards. “Government” includes government, government agencies and similar bodies at local, national or international level. A government grant is a transfer of resources to an entity in return for past or future compliance with conditions relating to its operating activities. The label is not decisive: subsidies, subventions and premiums can fall within the definition when the substance meets it. The Standard excludes benefits available in determining taxable profit or tax loss, or determined or limited by income-tax liability, such as income-tax holidays, investment tax credits and accelerated depreciation. It also excludes government participation in ownership, grants covered by Ind AS 41 and certain issues relating to changing prices. A preparer should document why the arrangement is inside Ind AS 20 before applying its measurement and presentation choices. Transactions with government that cannot be distinguished from normal trading transactions are not grants merely because the customer is a government body. The reasonable-assurance gate is a two-part test Condition compliance and receipt probability must both be supported Paragraph 7 requires reasonable assurance that the entity will comply with the conditions and that the grant will be received. Receipt by itself is not conclusive evidence that conditions have been or will be fulfilled. The accounting paper should identify each substantive condition, the evidence available at the reporting date, any milestones not yet achieved, management’s ability and intention to comply, and the basis for concluding that collection is reasonably assured. An approval that remains subject to investment, employment, location, production or retention conditions needs a condition-by-condition assessment. The manner of receipt does not change the method. A cash payment and a reduction of a liability to government are accounted for using the same recognition principles. This prevents form-driven accounting in which a net settlement is omitted from grant analysis or a cash receipt is immediately credited to income regardless of the related costs. Recognition follows the related costs, not the cash timetable Once recognised, a grant is taken to profit or loss on a systematic basis over the periods in which the entity recognises the expenses that the grant is intended to compensate. A grant related to a depreciable asset is usually recognised over the periods and in the proportions in which depreciation is recognised. A grant attached to a non-depreciable asset may still require recognition over the periods that bear the costs of satisfying an obligation. Where one assistance package compensates several cost streams, allocation may require more than one recognition basis. The deferred amount is recognised in profit or loss systematically over the useful life of the related asset. The asset remains at its gross carrying amount, while the grant release offsets the economic burden over the same periods. The entity should reconcile opening deferred income, additions, releases, repayments and closing balance. Deduction from the asset’s carrying amount Alternatively, the grant may be deducted in arriving at the asset’s carrying amount. The benefit reaches profit or loss through lower depreciation over the asset’s life. Both methods are acceptable under the Standard, but the policy must be applied consistently to similar grants and disclosed. The cash-flow statement often presents the purchase of the asset and receipt of the related grant as separate items to show gross investment, regardless of the balance-sheet presentation selected. A policy choice is not an opportunity to switch methods to manage a period’s result. The accounting manual should define the selected approach, the population to which it applies and the approval required for any change under Ind AS 8. The fixed-asset register and grant subledger must remain reconcilable under either method. Non-monetary grants and government loans require separate measurement analysis Land or other non-cash resources A non-monetary grant such as land or another resource may be accounted for by measuring both the asset and the grant at fair value. Ind AS 20 also permits an alternative of recording both at a nominal amount. The entity should not mix the alternatives within a transaction. Valuation evidence, restrictions on use, useful life, any obligation to construct or operate, and the related recognition pattern should be documented before posting entries. Forgivable loans and below-market government loans A forgivable government loan is treated as a grant when there is reasonable assurance that the entity will meet the terms for forgiveness. Until that threshold is met, the entity must not assume that repayment has disappeared. A government loan bearing a below-market rate is initially recognised and measured under Ind AS 109. The grant benefit is the difference between the loan’s initial carrying value under Ind AS 109 and the proceeds received. That benefit is then accounted for under Ind AS 20 after considering the conditions and obligations and the costs it is intended to compensate. Both methods are acceptable. Separate presentation can make the gross expense and assistance visible; net presentation can reflect that the expense might not have been incurred without the grant. Whichever method is chosen, the effect on any income or expense item that must be separately disclosed should be explained when necessary for users to understand the financial statements. The presentation decision does not alter the recognition date or total systematic allocation. Preparers should avoid recording an income grant as equity simply because it is non-refundable or comes from a shareholder-controlled government. Ind AS 20’s income approach recognises qualifying grants from sources other than shareholders in profit or loss over the appropriate periods. When a grant becomes repayable Repayment is a change in accounting estimate A government grant that becomes repayable is accounted for as a change in accounting estimate under Ind AS 8. For an income-related grant, repayment is first applied against any unamortised deferred credit for that grant. Any excess, or the full amount when no deferred credit exists, is recognised immediately in profit or loss. A repayment is therefore not silently netted against a new grant or taken directly to equity. Asset grant repayment may also trigger impairment analysis For an asset-related grant, the entity increases the asset’s carrying amount or reduces the deferred-income balance by the repayable amount. Cumulative additional depreciation that would have been recognised to date without the grant is charged immediately to profit or loss. The circumstances that caused repayment may also indicate impairment of the revised carrying amount. Legal correspondence, breach notices, repayment computations, revised depreciation and the impairment assessment should form one controlled file. It should flag expired milestones, probable clawbacks, unspent balances, assets disposed of before required holding periods and changes to legislation or scheme terms. Reviewers should challenge whether “reasonable assurance” rests on evidence available at the reporting date rather than a post-close hope that the entity will cure non-compliance. Frequently Asked Questions on Ind AS 20 Can a grant be recognised as soon as the money is received? Not automatically. The entity needs reasonable assurance both that it will comply with the attached conditions and that the grant will be received; cash receipt alone is not conclusive evidence of compliance. Must an asset-related grant always be shown as deferred income? No. It may be shown as deferred income or deducted in arriving at the asset’s carrying amount. The selected accounting policy and presentation method must be disclosed. How is a grant for past losses recognised? When it compensates expenses or losses already incurred, or provides immediate support without future related costs, it is recognised in profit or loss when it becomes receivable, with disclosure sufficient to explain its effect. Is a zero-interest government loan recorded entirely as a grant? No. The loan is initially measured under Ind AS 109. The difference between that initial carrying value and the proceeds is the grant benefit accounted for under Ind AS 20. When does a forgivable government loan become a grant? When there is reasonable assurance that the entity will meet the conditions for forgiveness. How is repayment of an asset grant recorded? Increase the asset’s carrying amount or reduce deferred income, recognise cumulative additional depreciation immediately, and consider whether the revised carrying amount is impaired. What disclosures are mandatory? The accounting policy and presentation methods, nature and extent of recognised grants and directly beneficial assistance, and unfulfilled conditions or contingencies attached to recognised assistance. Key Takeaways Apply Ind AS 20 only after confirming the arrangement is a government grant or relevant assistance within its scope. Recognition requires reasonable assurance of both condition compliance and receipt; cash collection alone is insufficient. Match grant income systematically with the related costs rather than using a cash-based recognition rule. Asset grants may be presented as deferred income or deducted from the asset; income grants may be shown separately or netted against related expense. Measure below-market government loans under Ind AS 109 first, then account for the identified grant benefit under Ind AS 20.
