Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Vikas Tyagi Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Commission Deduction Fails When the Assessee Cannot Prove That Services Were Actually Rendered Delhi ITAT Upholds ₹8 Lakh Commission Disallowance The Delhi ITAT upheld the disallowance of ₹8 lakh claimed as commission because the assessee failed to substantiate the actual rendition of services. The expenditure was originally claimed as commission paid to his wife, Nicky Tyagi, while the explanation during verification referred to a payment to Abhishek Kumar Dubey. The Tribunal found that the services of either person had yet to be established through documentary evidence. It also rejected the alternative request to compute income under section 44AD because the assessee had furnished no details to support that plea. Replies Under Section 133(6) Raised Questions About the Payee The assessee, described in the order as a professional, filed his return for AY 2020–21 on 3 February 2021, declaring income of ₹4,84,650. He had claimed total commission expenditure of ₹30,12,301 involving three individuals and HDFC Bank. The Assessing Officer issued notices under section 133(6) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 to the three individuals, all of whom responded directly. According to the assessee, two of the individuals, including Nicky Tyagi, had received salary as well as commission. Nicky Tyagi received salary of ₹60,000 and stated that she had provided customer leads and assisted with documentation. She also stated that the commission she had earned was paid directly to Dubey. This raised a question about whose services generated the commission and who received the payment. TDS Was Deducted in the Wife’s Name The Assessing Officer asked why TDS had been deducted in Nicky Tyagi’s name if the ₹8 lakh had been paid to Dubey and why the expenditure should be allowed. In response, the assessee stated that he had originally claimed payment of commission to Nicky Tyagi but found on verification that no such commission had been paid to her. She had also not offered the commission as income in her return. The Assessing Officer disallowed the expenditure because the assessee had furnished no proof of the services for which the commission was claimed. The assessment was completed on 12 September 2022 under section 143(3) read with section 144B. The Assessing Officer also rejected the alternative request to determine income under section 44AD. Commission Agreement and Customer List Before CIT(A) Before the CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, the assessee produced a commission agreement dated 1 April 2019 with Nicky Tyagi. The agreement specified commission at 22.50% on sales routed through her. The assessee also furnished a list of customers said to have been routed through her, along with the corresponding value of business. The CIT(A) examined Form 26AS, which showed that Nicky Tyagi was employed with IFFCO E-Bazar Ltd. The CIT(A) treated that information as contradicting the assessee’s earlier account of her employment and confirmed the disallowance by order dated 24 July 2026. The assessee carried the matter to the Tribunal. Tribunal Focused on Evidence of Work Performed The Tribunal examined whether the assessee had proved that Nicky Tyagi or Abhishek Kumar Dubey had actually rendered the services underlying the claim. The agreement set out the rate at which commission would be payable, and the customer list identified transactions attributed to Nicky Tyagi. On the record before it, however, the Tribunal found the evidence insufficient to establish rendition of services by either individual. That finding was decisive. The Tribunal upheld the entire disallowance of ₹8 lakh. Its reasoning focused on the evidentiary basis for the deduction, particularly given the differing accounts of who earned the commission and to whom it was paid. Alternative Claim Under Section 44AD Also Failed The assessee alternatively requested that his income be computed under the presumptive taxation provisions of section 44AD. The Tribunal recorded that he had furnished no details in support of this claim before it or before the lower authorities. It therefore rejected the alternative plea and dismissed the appeal in full. The finding on section 44AD turned on the absence of supporting particulars for the claim made in this case. Author’s Comments A commission agreement explains the terms on which a payment becomes due. Proof that those terms were met comes from the work itself, such as records of referrals, correspondence with customers or documentation showing who handled the transactions. Where an amount is said to have been paid to one person on behalf of another, the payment trail and TDS records also need to be reconciled with that explanation. The disputed deduction failed at that factual stage. The agreement and customer list did not resolve whether the wife or the other individual had performed the work for which the ₹8 lakh was claimed. The decision accordingly turns on proof of actual services, a question that arises independently of the relationship between the assessee and the person named in the agreement. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. The appeal in ITA No. 8344/Del/2026 for AY 2020-21, arises out of the order of the ld. National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. CIT(A)’, in short] dated 24.07.2026 against the order of assessment passed u/s 143(3) r.w.s. 144B of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’) dated 12.09.2022 by the Assessing Officer, Assessment Unit, Income Tax Department (hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. AO’). 2. The first issue to be decided in this appeal he says to whether the learned CITA was justified in confirming the disallowance of Commission paid in the sum of ₹8,00,000 by the assessee to his wife in the facts and circumstances of the instant case. 3. I have heard the rival submissions and perused the materials available on record. The assessee is a professional and had filed his return of income for assessment year 2020-21 on 3-2-2021 declaring total income of ₹4,84,650. The assessee paid ₹30,12,301 as commission to 3 individuals and HDFC Bank. Notices were issued under section 133(6) of the Act by the learned AO to 3 individuals. The learned AO noted that all the three individuals directly replied before the learned AO to the notice issued under section 133(6) of the Act. Out of them, Mr Sumit Gupta and Ms Nicky Tyagi claimed to have been employee of the assessee. Both of them earned salary as well as commission according to assessee. Ms Nicky Tyagi was given salary of Rs 60,000 and she claimed to have provided leads to target customers and assistance in ensuring complete documentation with such target customers. She had even claimed that commission she had earned from the assessee was actually paid to one Mr Abhishek Kumar Dubey directly. The assessee was showcaused during the course of assessment proceedings regarding the claim made by Smt Nicky Tyagi that ₹8,00,000 Commission was paid to Abhishek Kumar Dubey and in such a scenario, why TDS was not done by the assessee in the name of Mr Abhishek Kumar Dubey and also the reason as to why the same was done in the name of Smt Nicky Tyagi. Accordingly, the assessee was also showcaused as to why the Commission expenditure of ₹8,00,000 be not treated as bogus expenditure and added to the total income of the assessee. The assessee submitted his reply stating that he had claimed to have paid ₹8,00,000 towards Commission to Smt Nicky Tyagi, but on verification, it was found that no such commission was paid to her. Accordingly, she had not offered the commission income in her income tax returns. The learned AO proceeded to disallow the commission expenditure on the ground that no proof was given for rendition of services. The assessee also made alternative plea before the learned AO to determine the income in accordance with the provisions of section 44AD of the Act. The learned AO dismissed this alternative plea. 4. Before the learned CITA, the assessee furnished the Commission agreement between him and his wife Smt Nicky Tyagi dated 1-4-2019. The said agreement stated that Commission would be paid at the rate of 22.50% on sales routed through Smt Nicky Tyagi. The list of customers routed through Smt Nicky Tyagi was also tabulated together with the value of business thereon. The said table is reproduced in Page 9 of the order of the learned CITA. The learned CITA noted that from Form 26AS of Smt Nicky Tyagi, it revealed that she was employed with IFFCO E-Bazar Limited and accordingly the claim made by the assessee during the course of assessment proceedings that Smt Nicky Tyagi was employed with assessee was proved to be false. The learned CITA noted that the entire claim of expenditure made by the assessee in the sum of ₹8,00,000 on account of Commission clearly proved to be bogus and hence confirmed the action of the learned AO. 5. I find that the assessee had not proved with any documentary evidence to prove the rendition of services either by Smt Nicky Tyagi or by Mr Abhishek Kumar Dubey. Hence the entire claim of expenditure of ₹8,00,000 on account of Commission does not deserve to be allowed as an as a deduction in the hands of the assessee. I find that the learned CITA had rightly confirmed the disallowance made by the learned AO and hence I do not find any infirmity in the said order of the learned CITA. The ground raised by the assessee is hereby dismissed. 6. With regard to the alternative ground raised by the assessee to determine the income by applying the provisions of section 44AD of the Act, I find that no details in this regard were filed by the assessee before me. No details were even filed before the lower authorities in this regard. Hence this plea of the assessee that income is to be determined by applying the provisions of section 44AD of the Act does not deserve any merit. Accordingly, the ground raised in that regard is also dismissed. 7. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is dismissed. Order pronounced in the open court on 18-Nov-2026.
