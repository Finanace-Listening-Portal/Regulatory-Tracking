(Supreme Court of India) The Supreme Court granted bail to Arvind Walia, a promoter-director of Ramprastha Promoters & Developers Pvt. Ltd., arrested on 21 July 2025 in a money-laundering investigation concerning alleged diversion of homebuyers’ funds. The Punjab and Haryana High Court had dismissed his challenge to arrest and remand on 29 January 2026. The Supreme Court limited its consideration to bail. The High Court examined objections under Section 19 PMLA concerning predicate FIRs, the later addition of FIRs to the ECIR, grounds of arrest and forwarding documents to the Adjudicating Authority. It rejected these objections. The Supreme Court did not reverse the High Court’s findings on the legality of arrest. The Supreme Court noted that Walia was 65, had been detained for more than eight months, had attended five ED appearances before arrest, and that the prosecution complaint was filed. It held that further detention was unnecessary, and that Section 45’s twin bail conditions cannot override the constitutional protection of liberty under Article 21. The Court granted bail subject to bonds, witness-protection and trial-attendance conditions, with cancellation possible for breach. The arrest ruling was left undisturbed, and the Court expressly stated that its observations were not findings on the merits. The Supreme Court of India, in Arvind Walia Vs Directorate of Enforcement & Anr., granted bail to Arvind Walia, a promoter-director of M/s Ramprastha Promoters & Developers Private Limited (RPDPL), in a money laundering case arising under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA). The appellant was arrested by the Directorate of Enforcement (ED) on 21 July 2025 in connection with allegations that RPDPL and its promoters had collected substantial amounts from homebuyers for residential projects in Gurugram but failed to deliver the promised flats and plots. The ED alleged that funds collected from purchasers were diverted to group companies and other entities instead of being utilised for completing the projects. According to the investigation material reproduced in the Punjab and Haryana High Court judgment, approximately ₹1,100 crore had been collected from homebuyers awaiting possession of about 2,600 flats and plots. The ED registered ECIR No. ECIR/GNZO/14/2021 on 9 August 2021 and subsequently investigated allegations of offences under Sections 3 and 4 of the PMLA. Related High Court Judgment: Before the Supreme Court granted bail, the Punjab and Haryana High Court had dismissed Arvind Walia’s challenge to his arrest and remand under the PMLA. The High Court examined objections concerning Section 19, the addition of predicate FIRs to the ECIR, communication of grounds of arrest and statutory procedural compliance. We have taken note of the fact that prosecution complaint has already been filed by the ED and that the appellant (aged 65 3 years) has been in custody for over 8 months. We have also taken note of the fact that the appellant, prior to his arrest, physically appeared before the ED on five occasions. Presently, there seems to be no necessity to continue him in custody. What remains for consideration is the restrictions imposed by Section 45 of the PMLA, 2002. 8. Section 45 of the PMLA, 2002 cannot be interpreted to justify indefinite detention. In appropriate cases, the constitutional courts must intervene and have, in fact, intervened in the past to safeguard the right to personal liberty of the accused under Article 21. The twin conditions under Section 45 cannot override the constitutional safeguards under Article 21 of the Constitution (see: Manish Sisodia v Enforcement Directorate, 2024 SCC OnLine SC 1920). Applying this principle, this Court has granted bail in appropriate cases irrespective of the period of custody4. 9. In view of the above, we are of the considered opinion that further detention of the appellant pending trial is not necessary.
