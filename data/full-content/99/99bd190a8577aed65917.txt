Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Venkata Padmavathi Dalapathirao Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) Summary: The ITAT Hyderabad allowed the appeal of Venkata Padmavathi Dalapathirao for AY 2015-16 and quashed the reassessment order dated 27 December 2018 because the notice issued under Section 148 on 3 December 2018 did not bear either the manual signature or the digital signature of the Assessing Officer. The appeal arose from the order of the Addl./JCIT(A), Panaji, dated 28 August 2025, which had upheld the assessment. The assessment narrative records an addition of ₹63,29,191 on account of long-term capital gains from the sale of immovable property. The original grounds challenged reopening without fresh tangible material, adoption of Sub-Registrar guideline rates instead of the fair market value as on 1 April 1981, rejection of the values claimed for land and built-up area, and failure to consider the registered valuer’s report reproduced in the written submissions dated 4 May 2025. They also challenged computation of long-term capital gains at ₹95,29,171 and disallowance of ₹3,00,000 claimed as transfer expenditure. The assessee additionally questioned reopening on the allegedly incorrect assumption that no return had been filed and separately challenged the unsigned notice dated 3 December 2018. Relying on National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. Vs CIT, (1998) 229 ITR 383 (SC), the Tribunal admitted the additional grounds under Rule 11 of the Income Tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963, since they involved legal questions arising from the existing record; the Department did not object to their admission. On the signature issue, the assessee referred to the notice at page 22 of the paper book and contended that an unsigned Section 148 notice could not confer jurisdiction to reassess. Reliance was placed on Prakash Krishnavtar Bhardwaj Vs ITO, 451 ITR 27 (Bombay High Court). The Department relied on the orders of the lower authorities. After examining and reproducing the notice, the Tribunal found that it contained neither a manual nor a digital signature of the Assessing Officer. It followed the Bombay High Court’s ruling that absence of either form of signature invalidates the notice and prevents the Assessing Officer from assuming reassessment jurisdiction. The Tribunal held that a valid notice under Section 148 was a prerequisite for reassessment. Consequently, it declared the unsigned notice invalid and quashed the assessment order passed under Section 143(3) read with Section 147 as being without jurisdiction. The remaining grounds became academic and were not adjudicated separately. Accordingly, the appeal was allowed; the order does not decide the merits of the valuation, capital-gains computation or transfer-expenditure claims. Cases Discussed Prakash Krishnavtar Bhardwaj Vs ITO — (2023) 451 ITR 27 (Bombay High Court) — Followed to hold that a Section 148 notice lacking both manual and digital signatures is invalid and cannot confer reassessment jurisdiction. National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. Vs CIT — (1998) 229 ITR 383 (Supreme Court) — Relied upon for admission of additional legal grounds arising from facts already on record. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT HYDERABAD This appeal is filed by Shri Venkata Padmavathi Dalapathirao (“the assessee”), feeling aggrieved by the order passed by the Learned Addl./JCIT(A), Panaji (“Ld. First Appellate Authority ”) dated 28.08.2025 for the A.Y. 2015-16. 2. The assessee has raised the following grounds of appeal: 1. The Learned First Appellate Authority has erred in law and on facts in upholding the reopening of assessment under Section 147 without fresh tangible material, contrary to settled judicial precedents. 2. The Learned First Appellate Authority has erred in law and on facts in confirming the Assessing Officer’s adoption of Sub-Registrar (SRO) guideline rates as against the Fair Market Value (FMV) of the property as on 01.04.1981 3a. The Learned First Appellate Authority has erred in ignoring the values adopted by the appellant as fair market value as on 01.04.1981, both for the land and built-up area. 3b. The Learned First Appellate Authority has failed to appreciate and consider the Registered Valuer’s Report submitted during appellate proceedings and reproduced in the written submissions dated 04.05.2025. 4. The Learned First Appellate Authority has erred in confirming the computation of Long- Term Capital Gains at ₹95,29,171/- without appreciating the appellant’s computation based on legally admissible FMV and indexed costs. 5. The Learned First Appellate Authority has erred in law and on facts in confirming the disallowance of ₹3,00,000/- claimed as expenditure incurred in connection with the transfer of property. 6. The appellant craves leave to add, amend or alter any of the grounds at the time of hearing of appeal. [Signature] APPELLANT Represented by Legal Heir 3. The assessee has also raised the following additional grounds before us: “ 1. The initiation of proceedings under section 147 through the notice issued under section 148 dated 06th February, 2018, based on the assumption of wrong facts that the Income Tax Return of the relevant A.Y was not filed, is bad in law and void ab-initio, and the consequent re-assessment proceedings and the order passed pursuant thereto are without jurisdiction and 2. The initiation of the proceedings which is led by the issuance of notice under section 148 dated 03rd December 2018 is unsigned and hence, the said notice is improper and all the further proceedings initiated are bad in law”. 4. The Learned Authorized Representative (“Ld. AR”) submitted that additional grounds so filed are admissible in view of judgment rendered by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. Vs. CIT (1998) 229 ITR 383 (SC). The Learned Departmental Representative (“Ld. DR”) also did not raise any objection for admission of the additional grounds. The prayer for admission of additional grounds noted above which are not in memorandum of appeal are being admitted for adjudication in terms of Rule 11 of the Income Tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963 owing to the fact that objections raised in additional grounds are legal in nature for which relevant facts are stated to be emanating from the existing records. 5. The brief facts of the case are that the Assessing Officer (“AO”) completed the assessment for the Assessment Year 2015-16 under section 143(3) read with section 147 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (“the Act”), vide order dated 27.12.2018, making an addition of Rs.63,29,191/- on account of long-term capital gains arising from the sale of immovable property. 6. Aggrieved by the assessment order, the assessee preferred an appeal before the Ld. CIT(A), who confirmed the addition made by the AO and dismissed the appeal of the assessee. 7. Aggrieved by the order of the Ld. CIT(A), the assessee is in appeal before this Tribunal. At the outset, the Learned Authorized Representative (“Ld. AR”) submitted that in Additional Ground No. 2, the assessee has challenged the validity of the notice issued under section 148 of the Act on the ground that it does not bear the signature of the AO. In this regard, the Ld. AR invited our attention to the copy of the notice issued under section 148 of the Act, placed at page no. 22 of the paper book, and submitted that the said notice does not bear either the manual signature or the digital signature of the AO. He contended that an unsigned notice under section 148 of the Act is invalid and cannot confer jurisdiction upon the AO to initiate reassessment proceedings. Consequently, the assessment order passed pursuant to such notice is also without jurisdiction and liable to be quashed. In support of this contention, the Ld. AR relied upon the decision of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of Prakash Krishnavtar Bhardwaj Vs. ITO, reported in 451 ITR 27. 8. Per contra, the Learned Departmental Representative (“Ld. DR”) relied upon the orders of the lower authorities. 9. We have heard the rival submissions and perused the material available on record including the case law relied upon. We have examined the copy of the notice issued under section 148 of the Act, placed at page no. 22 of the paper book, which is to the following effect: GOVERNMENT OF INDIA MINISTRY OF FINANCE INCOME TAX DEPARTMENT OFFICE OF THE INCOME TAX OFFICER WARD 4(1),HYDERABAD To, VENKATA PADMAVATHI DALAPATHIRAO FLAT NO 401, BLOCK NO A GLODEN HABITAT APARTMENTS . ADJ. RATNADEEP STORES KONDAPUR HYDERABAD 500084 , Telangana India PAN: AFRPD3978E AY:2015-16 Dated: 03/12/2018 Notice No :ITBA/AST/S/148/2018-19/1013977526(1) Notice Under Section 148 Of The Income Tax Act, 1961 Sir/ Madam/ M/s, Whereas I have reasons to believe that your Income chargeable to Tax for the Assessment Year 2015-16 has escaped Assessment within the meaning of section 147 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. I, therefore, propose to assess/ re-assess the income/ loss for the said Assessment Year and I hereby require you to deliver to me within 15 days from the service of this notice, a return in the prescribed form for the said Assessment Year. This notice is being issued after obtaining the necessary satisfaction of the RANGE-4,HYDERABAD SURESH PALLAKONDA WARD 4(1),HYDERABAD Certified True Copy[Signature]P Yashodhan Chartered Accountant 10. On perusal of the above, we find that the said notice does not bear any signature of the AO, either manual or digital. The issue, therefore, is whether such an unsigned notice can constitute a valid notice under section 148 of the Act and confer jurisdiction upon the AO to proceed with the reassessment. In this regard, we have also gone through para no. 21 of the judgment of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of Prakash Krishnavtar Bhardwaj Vs. ITO (supra), “21. We are, therefore, of the considered opinion that in the present case, the notice u/s.148 dated 02.04.2022 having no signature affixed on it, digitally or manually, the same is invalid and would not vest the Assessing Officer with any further jurisdiction to proceed to reassess the income of the petitioner. Consequently, the notice dated 02.04.2022 u/s.148 of the Act issued to the petitioner being invalid and sought to be issued after three years from the end of the relevant assessment year 2015-16 with which we are concerned in this petition, any steps taken by the respondents in furtherance of notice dated 21.03.2022 issued under clause (b) of section 148A of the Act and order dated 02.04.2022 issued under clause (d) of section 148A of the Act, would be without jurisdiction, and therefore, arbitrary and contrary to Article 14 of the Constitution of India. Consequently, we quash and set aside the notice dated 02.04.2022 issued by the respondents u/s.148 of the Act, order dated 02.04.2022 under clause (b) of section 148A of the Act and notice dated 21.03.2022 issued under clause (b) of section 148A of the Act.” 11. On perusal of the above, we find that the Hon’ble High Court has categorically held that a notice issued under section 148 of the Act, having no signature affixed to it, either digitally or manually, is invalid and would not vest the AO with jurisdiction to proceed to assess the income of the assessee. In the present case, the notice issued under section 148 of the Act likewise does not bear either the manual signature or the digital signature of the AO. Thus, the facts before us are covered by the aforesaid decision. The issuance of a valid notice under section 148 of the Act is a prerequisite for assuming jurisdiction to undertake reassessment proceedings. In the absence of a valid notice, the assessment proceedings undertaken pursuant thereto cannot be sustained. Accordingly, respectfully following the decision of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of Prakash Krishnavtar Bhardwaj Vs. ITO (supra), we hold that the unsigned notice issued under section 148 of the Act is invalid. Consequently, the assessment order dated 27.12.2018, passed under section 143(3) read with section 147 of the Act pursuant to such notice, is without jurisdiction and is hereby quashed. 12. Since the assessment order has been quashed on the aforesaid legal ground, the remaining grounds raised by the assessee have become academic and do not require separate adjudication. 13. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the Open Court on 16th October, 2026.
