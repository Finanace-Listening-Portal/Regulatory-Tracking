Summary : Akash Network is a decentralised marketplace for cloud computing capacity. Its on-chain marketplace coordinates deployment orders, provider bids, leases and settlement, while actual workloads run on infrastructure operated by independent providers. This guide separates the network’s technical workflow from the market value of AKT, explains recent protocol changes including burn-mint equilibrium, and outlines the Indian tax and compliance questions that can arise when AKT is acquired, transferred, staked or used. It is an educational profile, not a price forecast or a recommendation. A tenant expresses a deployment requirement, providers compete to supply the requested resources, and a lease records the commercial relationship. The network is built for workloads that may need CPU, GPU, memory, storage and network capacity. The blockchain is the coordination and settlement layer; it is not itself the machine executing a container. This distinction matters because availability, hardware quality and data-handling controls ultimately depend on the selected provider and the deployment configuration. From deployment order to active lease A deployment is represented through an on-chain order. Providers evaluate the order and may submit bids. The tenant selects a bid and creates a lease, after which the provider supplies a manifest endpoint and starts the workload if its operational checks succeed. Funds are held and released through the network’s settlement process. Providers lock AKT while bids or leases are active, but official documentation does not present this as permanent collateral guaranteeing every service outcome. Users therefore need to monitor balances, lease status, provider responsiveness and workload health rather than treating the smart-contract state as a substitute for operational supervision. Stage What occurs Practical control Deployment specification Tenant declares resources and service configuration. Avoid embedding secrets; validate image, ports, storage and region requirements. Order and bidding Eligible providers submit commercial bids. Compare price with provider attributes, capacity and operational history. Lease creation Tenant accepts a bid and the lease becomes active. Confirm funding, provider endpoint and expected service state. Workload operation Provider runs containers and reports service details. Monitor logs, backups, keys, uptime and resource consumption. Closure or exhaustion Lease ends through closure or lack of funds. Export data and retain an independent recovery path before termination. AKT Utility, Staking and Governance AKT is the network’s native asset. It is used in protocol settlement, staking and governance. Validators and delegators participate in proof-of-stake security, while governance can alter network parameters and approve software or economic changes. Holding AKT does not confer ownership of a cloud provider, a legal claim on network revenue or a guaranteed right to a particular service level. A delegator also depends on validator performance and may face protocol-level consequences, including reduced rewards or slashing where the applicable rules impose it. Burn-mint equilibrium changed the settlement design The 2026 roadmap records implementation of the burn-mint equilibrium proposal on 23 March 2026. Under this design, marketplace spending and protocol issuance interact through defined mechanisms intended to align settlement with network activity. It should not be simplified into a claim that compute demand must make AKT appreciate. Token supply rules, market liquidity, staking participation, governance changes and external demand remain separate variables. The roadmap also records CosmWasm support and later tooling work, but roadmap items marked planned or estimated should not be reported as completed until official status confirms implementation. Workload Security Is a Shared Operational Responsibility A decentralised marketplace changes who operates the infrastructure, but it does not remove ordinary cloud-security duties. Container images must be trusted, ports should be limited, credentials should be injected securely, and stateful data should have tested backups outside a single provider. Tenants should assume a lease can end and design for migration. A provider can also experience hardware failure, network interruption, misconfiguration or capacity constraints. Sensitive or regulated workloads may require contractual, geographic, audit and data-processing assurances that a permissionless marketplace entry does not automatically supply. Keys, wallets and transaction finality Wallet credentials authorize deployments, funding and token transfers. A lost seed phrase can make assets inaccessible, while a compromised key can allow irreversible transactions. Hardware wallets, role-separated operational accounts, tested recovery procedures and careful verification of addresses can reduce risk. Users should also distinguish a wallet interface from the underlying network and verify chain identifiers, fee settings and transaction status before assuming an action succeeded. Provider selection and service continuity Price alone is not a complete provider-selection criterion. Workload owners should examine advertised attributes, available resources, geographic needs, reputation evidence, response procedures and the possibility that a provider may leave the market. Stateless services are generally easier to redeploy than databases or services that depend on local persistent volumes. A continuity plan should include container definitions, configuration records, encrypted backups, DNS controls and a rehearsed migration method. Indian Tax Treatment When AKT Is a Virtual Digital Asset For an Indian taxpayer, AKT will ordinarily fall within the broad virtual digital asset framework when it is acquired or transferred as a crypto-asset. Income from transfer is generally taxed under the special VDA regime at the prescribed rate, subject to surcharge and cess, and the statutory restriction on deductions and loss set-off must be considered. Section 194S withholding can apply to consideration for transfer when statutory conditions and thresholds are met. The person responsible for withholding, the timing and the treatment of an exchange or peer-to-peer transaction depend on the actual payment flow. Using AKT for compute can create more than one recordable event If AKT is acquired and later spent to obtain compute, the disposal of AKT may be a transfer for tax purposes even though no rupees are received. The taxpayer needs a defensible rupee value, acquisition-cost record, timestamp, wallet evidence and transaction identifier. A business user must separately consider whether the compute expense is allowable under ordinary business rules and whether indirect-tax or withholding obligations arise from the service relationship. The special restriction for computing VDA transfer income should not be confused with the accounting or tax treatment of the separately purchased cloud service. Staking and rewards require fact-specific classification Indian law does not make every token receipt identical. Validator or delegation rewards may require recognition based on the legal character of the receipt, control over the asset and the taxpayer’s activity. A later sale can create a separate transfer event. Taxpayers should preserve validator statements, wallet histories, fair-value methodology, fees and any slashing records rather than relying only on an exchange’s annual summary. Cross-border service facts, business status and GST registration can materially change the analysis. Decision Checks Before Deploying or Acquiring AKT Confirm that the intended workload can tolerate provider and lease migration risk. Test backup restoration and do not keep the only copy of stateful data on one provider. Separate operational keys from long-term holdings and document wallet custody. Read completed protocol proposals separately from planned roadmap items. Map every acquisition, reward, spend and transfer to Indian tax records in rupees. Evaluate regulatory, data-location, contractual and sector-specific requirements for the workload. Frequently Asked Questions on the Subject Is Akash Network itself a conventional cloud company? No. It is a decentralised marketplace and protocol coordinating independent providers. The provider selected under a lease operates the actual infrastructure. Does an Akash lease guarantee uninterrupted service? No. The on-chain lease records the marketplace relationship, but hardware, connectivity, configuration, funding and provider operations can still interrupt a workload. What is AKT used for? AKT supports settlement, staking, governance and provider marketplace activity. Those functions do not create a guaranteed return or a claim on a provider. Did Akash implement burn-mint equilibrium in 2026? The official roadmap and first-quarter report record the change as live from 23 March 2026. Its economic effect should not be treated as a price prediction. Can an Indian user owe tax when AKT is spent rather than sold for rupees? Potentially yes. Spending a VDA can constitute a transfer, so the rupee value and cost records may be needed even where consideration is a service. Are staking rewards taxed only when sold? The timing and character of staking receipts are fact-specific. Receipt and later transfer may be separate tax events, so professional advice and contemporaneous records are important. Is provider pricing the only factor to compare? No. Capacity, location, attributes, security controls, reputation evidence, persistence and migration readiness are also material. Key Takeaways Akash coordinates compute orders, bids, leases and settlement; independent providers run the workloads. AKT has protocol utility, but neither utility nor burn-mint equilibrium guarantees investment performance. Backups, key security, provider due diligence and migration planning remain essential. Indian VDA tax and section 194S questions can arise on sale, exchange or spending of AKT.
