Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Axis Bank Limited Vs Dadha Pharma LLP (Madras High Court) Bank Fraud in 2011: Madras High Court Applies 2017 RBI Circular Retrospectively, Upholds ₹16.15 Lakh Refund Introduction Can a bank resist repayment of money fraudulently siphoned from a customer’s account by arguing that the protective RBI circular was issued several years after the transaction? The Madras High Court, in Axis Bank Limited v. M/s. Dadha Pharma LLP and Others, answered this question in favour of the customer in the circumstances before it. Dismissing the bank’s review application, Justice N. Anand Venkatesh held that the beneficial RBI circular dated 6 July 2017 deserved retrospective effect to ensure fair and uniform treatment of customers who lose their money through unauthorised transactions. The Court consequently upheld relief concerning ₹16,15,000, siphoned from the customer’s current account in 2011. Background of the Dispute Dadha Pharma LLP, formerly Dadha Pharma Private Limited, approached the High Court through W.P. No.413 of 2024, seeking a direction to Axis Bank to restore ₹16.15 lakh illegally withdrawn from its current account. Although notice was served, the bank did not appear either through its representative or counsel. By an order dated 12 June 2025, subsequently clarified on 3 July 2025, the Court directed the bank to act upon the RBI circular and restore the amount within four weeks. The customer thereafter initiated contempt proceedings for non-compliance. During those proceedings, the bank expressed willingness to deposit the amount. The contempt petition was closed on 10 April 2026, with a direction to make the deposit, which the bank subsequently complied with. Meanwhile, the bank sought review of the original order. Its 272-day delay was condoned, and the review application was considered on merits. Bank’s Three Grounds for Review The bank principally raised three objections. First, it contended that the RBI circular did not apply to private sector banks. Secondly, it alleged that it had not received sufficient opportunity to contest the writ petition. Thirdly, it argued that the disputed transaction occurred in 2011, when the RBI notification dated 8 April 2002 governed the matter. According to the bank, its compensation policy framed pursuant to that notification restricted compensation under Clause 11.3 to ₹25,000. The bank therefore maintained that applying the subsequent 2017 circular to a 2011 transaction constituted an error apparent on the face of the record, warranting review. Private Banks Cannot Claim Exemption The Court rejected the argument that the RBI’s directions were inapplicable to private banks. It observed that banks deal with depositors’ money and that the RBI safeguards depositors’ interests. The bank’s private ownership therefore provided no basis for avoiding the applicable regulatory protection. The objection concerning lack of opportunity was also rejected. Since the bank had been served with notice but chose not to contest the writ proceedings, it could not subsequently contend that an opportunity had been denied. The 2002 Notification and ₹25,000 Policy Limit The judgment reproduced the relevant portion of the 2002 RBI notification. It required banks to compensate customers without objection where the bank was at fault. Where neither the bank nor the customer was at fault, but the fault lay elsewhere in the system, compensation up to a limit was contemplated under a Board-approved customer relations policy. An important distinction emerges here: the ₹25,000 figure was relied upon as the limit under Axis Bank’s compensation policy. The passage of the RBI notification reproduced in the judgment did not itself prescribe that numerical ceiling. The bank nevertheless relied on this policy restriction to resist repayment of the substantially larger amount lost by the customer. Why the Court Applied the 2017 Circular The customer had pursued criminal proceedings to expose the fraud. However, the investigation did not resolve the case, and a closure report was filed on 4 October 2023. Thereafter, the customer commenced proceedings to recover the siphoned amount. The Court emphasised that the 2017 circular was already in force when those recovery proceedings commenced. It regarded the circular as beneficial protection intended for customers whose money had been siphoned from their bank accounts. In paragraph 17, the Court expressly reasoned that the beneficial circular should receive retrospective effect so that affected customers were treated fairly and uniformly. Accordingly, applying it did not disclose any error apparent warranting review. Full Refund Is Not Automatic in Every Fraud Case The decision should be understood alongside the structure of the 2017 RBI circular. That circular differentiates between bank negligence, customer negligence and third-party breaches. Customer liability also depends, in specified situations, on the time taken to report the unauthorised transaction. Consequently, this judgment should not be presented as granting unconditional reimbursement for every banking fraud. rbi.org.in Author’s Comments The ruling provides significant support to customers facing rejection merely because the fraudulent transaction predates the protective circular. Its central contribution is the Court’s express recognition of retrospective operation of beneficial customer protection in the facts before it. Equally, the chronology requires care. The Court considered the customer’s prolonged criminal proceedings and the subsequent closure report; it did not prescribe a general requirement to await completion of a police investigation before claiming reimbursement. The review application was dismissed, and the customer was permitted to withdraw ₹16.15 lakh together with accrued interest, if any, on the deposited amount. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF MADRAS HIGH COURT 1. The third respondent bank in the said writ petition filed the above review application seeking to review the order passed by this Court dated 12.6.2025 as clarified on 03.7.2025 in W.P.No.413 of 2024. 2. Heard the learned counsel for the applicant bank and the learned counsel appearing on behalf of the first respondent/writ petitioner. 3. This Court has carefully considered the submissions of the learned counsel on either side and perused the materials available on record and more particularly the order under review. 4. The said writ petition was filed by the first respondent in this review application seeking for the relief of a Writ of Mandamus directing the applicant bank to re-credit the amount of Rs.16,15,000/-, which was illegally siphoned from the current account of the writ petitioner. 5. It is relevant to point out that the applicant bank was served with notice in the said writ petition. However, there was no appearance on behalf of the applicant bank either through a representative or through counsel. Therefore, this Court heard the learned counsel for the writ petitioner and the learned Government Advocate (Crl.Side) appearing for the second respondent in the writ petition and by order dated 12.6.2025, directed the applicant bank to act upon the circular issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and restore/re-credit the amount of Rs.16,15,000/-, which was siphoned from the current account of the writ petitioner to the account of the writ petitioner within a period of four weeks from the date of receipt of a copy of the said order. 6. The applicant bank filed the above review application with a delay of 272 days. In the meantime, a contempt petition in Cont.P. No.586 of 2026 was filed by the writ petitioner for non compliance of the order dated 12.6.2025 as corrected on 03.7.2025 passed in the said writ petition. After notice was issued to the applicant bank in the said contempt petition, they expressed their willingness to deposit the said sum of Rs.16,15,000/-. In view of the same, the said contempt petition was closed on 10.4.2026 and the applicant bank was directed to deposit the said amount within two weeks from the date of receipt of a copy of the said order 10.4.2026. Further, the said amount was also deposited. 7. Later, the delay was condoned by this Court vide order dated 22.7.2026. Thereafter, the review application was listed for final hearing. 8. The main ground that was urged on the side of the applicant bank is that the RBI circular did not apply to the private sector banks, that the applicant bank was not given sufficient opportunity to contest the said writ petition and that at the best, the writ petitioner would only be entitled to the relief under the Notification of the RBI dated 08.4.2002 and not under the subsequent circular issued by the RBI on 06.7.2017 since the alleged siphoning of the amount was said to have taken place in the year 2011. 9. In so far as the first ground that was urged on the side of the applicant bank is concerned, the same is liable to be rejected for the simple reason that the RBI Notification will equally apply to all the banks since they are dealing with the deposits made by depositors, whose interest is safeguarded by the RBI. Hence, the first ground is rejected. 10. In so far as the second ground that has been raised is concerned, a notice was issued to the applicant bank in the said writ petition and in spite of it, they had chosen not to contest the said writ petition and therefore, the applicant bank cannot turn around and take a stand that no opportunity was given to them to contest the said writ petition. Thus, the second ground raised is also rejected. 11. The last ground raised by the learned counsel for the applicant bank is the only material ground that has to be considered by this Court and test if the order dated 12.6.2025 as clarified on 03.7.2025 passed in the said writ petition would suffer from an error apparent on the face of the records. 12. The alleged incident of siphoning had taken place in the year 2011 and at that point of time, the RBI Notification dated 08.4.2002 was in force. The relevant portion in the said RBI Notification dated 08.4.2002 reads as follows: “With a view to redressing the grievances of the customers in this regard, we have reviewed the position and advise that (i) in cases where banks are at fault, the banks should compensate customers without demur, and (ii) in cases where neither the bank is at fault nor the customer at fault, but the fault lies elsewhere in the system, then also the banks should compensate the customers (upto a limit) as part of a Board approved customer relations policy. ” 13. It was contended on the side of the applicant bank that pursuant to the said Notification, the revised compensation policy was issued and Clause 11.3 provided that the applicant bank would be liable to compensate the customer subject to the upper limit of Rs.25,000/-; that the writ petitioner would only be entitled to this amount since the transaction pertained to the year 2011 when the circular dated 08.4.2002 was in force; that this Court had issued directions to the applicant bank to pay the said sum of Rs.16,15,000/- based on the RBI circular dated 06.7.2017; that this circular does not apply to the transaction of the year 2011; that it only has a prospective effect; and that to that extent, the relief granted suffers from an error apparent on the face of the records. 14. Per contra, it was submitted on the side of the writ petitioner that the Notification of the year 2002 issued by the RBI was withdrawn in the year 2017; that the writ petitioner was prosecuting the criminal complaint filed before the concerned Police Station, which reached the fruition only on 04.10.2023 when the final closure report was filed; and that only thereafter, the writ petitioner would be entitled to make a claim and at that point of time, the policy of the year 2017 came into force. 15. The circular of the year 2002 had fixed an upper cap and accordingly, the applicant bank issued a compensation policy restricting the upper limit of Rs.25,000/-. Subsequently, the RBI issued the circular of the year 2017 withdrawing the earlier circular. This circular rectified the patent error in the earlier circular, which restricted the total amount payable by the applicant bank at Rs.25,000/- irrespective of the amount that has been siphoned from the accounts of the depositors. 16. In the case in hand, the writ petitioner was running from pillar to post to somehow expose the fraud committed by initiating the criminal proceedings. However, the Investigation Officer was not able to solve the case and a closure report was filed on 04.10.2023. Only thereafter, the writ petitioner commenced the proceedings for getting back the said amount that was siphoned from his current account. At that point of time, the RBI circular dated 06.7.2017 was in force. It was a circular, which was issued for the benefit of customers, who lose their hard earned money by means of siphoning of funds from the bank accounts of the customers. 17. Such beneficial circular issued in the year 2017 was taken into consideration by this Court while granting the relief to the writ petitioner. This was due to the fact that a beneficial circular like the one which was issued in the year 2017 has to be given a retrospective effect to ensure that the customers, who lose their hard earned money, are treated with fairness uniformly. 18. In the light of the above discussions, this Court does not find any error apparent on the face of the records warranting the review of the order passed in the said writ petition. 19. Accordingly, the above review application is dismissed. The writ petitioner is permitted to withdraw the said sum of Rs.16,15,000/-, which has already been deposited by the applicant bank together with accrued interest, if any. Consequently, the connected WMP is closed.
