UPI is now active in 11 countries, while its linkage with Singapore’s PayNow has provided an example of how domestic payment systems can be connected for cross-border transactions.Different payment systems need to work together Advt UPI’s international expansion is not simply a matter of making Indian users' apps available overseas. Each new market can require integration between UPI and the local payment infrastructure.NPCI International has facilitated UPI and RuPay acceptance in countries including Singapore, Nepal, the UAE, France, Bhutan, Mauritius, Qatar, Cambodia and Sri Lanka. The arrangements differ by market, ranging from merchant acceptance to linkages with local payment systems.The Singapore PayNow connection illustrates the more complex form of integration, allowing users of the linked systems to make cross-border transfers using mobile numbers or virtual addresses.Regulatory requirements can differA broader international footprint would also expose UPI-linked transactions to the regulatory and compliance requirements of each participating jurisdiction. Issues such as customer-data handling, financial-crime controls, transaction monitoring and dispute resolution can require agreement between regulators and payment operators before a linkage can go live.The Alipay+ proposal demonstrates how these considerations can become particularly sensitive when a foreign payment platform is involved. Reuters reported that Indian authorities had concerns over transaction-data processing, storage and management as well as money laundering and cyber fraud.Cybersecurity becomes more important Advt The security challenge will grow as UPI connects more payment systems and users across borders. The government has separately identified cybersecurity and ethical data protection as priorities for the fintech sector, reflecting the importance of protecting a payments infrastructure that is increasingly being used beyond India.Cross-border connectivity also creates a larger number of points at which payment systems, banks, merchants and technology providers have to maintain compatible security standards.Data security emerges as key hurdleThe proposed linkage between UPI and Alipay+, operated by Singapore-based Ant International, shows how data and security concerns can hold up cross-border payment partnerships. Advt India has stalled the proposal over national-security and data-privacy concerns, with authorities also flagging potential risks involving customer-data misuse, money laundering, cyber fraud and the handling of transaction data. The proposal, submitted in January, was intended to facilitate payments for Indian travellers in China, Hong Kong and other parts of Asia, with a later phase envisaging payments by international visitors in India.The episode could make security assessments an important part of future UPI partnerships, particularly where payment platforms have links to foreign entities or where transaction data would cross national borders. Advt Scale is not the problemUPI's domestic scale gives it a strong base for international expansion. The platform processed a record 24.51 billion transactions worth Rs 29.82 lakh crore in August, with monthly volume rising 22% from a year earlier.The challenge for UPI's global expansion is less about proving the capacity of the underlying payment rail and more about replicating its domestic simplicity across different payment systems, regulatory regimes and security environments.As more countries consider linking their instant-payment networks with UPI, the pace of expansion will depend on how these issues are resolved alongside the commercial and policy case for cross-border connectivity.
