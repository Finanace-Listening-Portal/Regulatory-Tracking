Articles 5 min read Private lenders may turn to other fee pools as IRDAI proposes insurance commission cuts Private lenders are engaging with the regulator on IRDAI’s proposed insurance distribution norms, but are also preparing for the possibility of lower commissions. While the impact will vary across lenders, banks say they could see pressure on insurance fee income and are looking to offset it through higher volumes, other fee pools, product-mix changes and efficiency gains. JM Financial estimates insurance distribution income across the banks in its coverage rose from 3.5 per cent of profit before tax (PBT) in FY23 to 5.1 per cent in FY26, while increasing from 4 basis points to 7 basis points of average assets. Advt Third largest private sector lender Axis Bank executive director Subrat Mohanty said the lender is looking at the potential impact over a medium-term horizon.He said Axis Bank continues to focus on building fee income from multiple sources across both its retail and corporate businesses, while cautioning against assessing the contribution of insurance fees purely as a percentage of PBT.“When you take a ratio with respect to PBT, that is not entirely the accurate way of looking at it because in several some years, the PBT is impacted by one off and things of that kind, which is why it is better to take a bit of a medium term view on how to see the fee income impact on a franchise,” Mohanty said.According to JM Financial, Axis had the highest insurance-income exposure among large private banks, with insurance income accounting for 12.5 per cent of FY26 PBT and 18 basis points of average assets.Mohanty said the bank expects to have some time before the final regulatory provisions apply, allowing it to work on alternative sources of fee income and mitigate the potential impact through higher volumes and changes in product mix.The bank will also engage with insurance partners to increase the overall market, he said, pointing to the possibility that lower insurance prices could encourage greater customer participation. Advt “The customer will get the benefit of lower prices. Whenever this has happened in the past, including when the GST rates have gone down to zero, the volumes went up quite a bit,” Mohanty said.RBL Bank expects a 50–60 per cent hit to its roughly Rs 300 crore annual bancassurance income if the proposed norms are implemented in their current form. Its bancassurance income stood at Rs 296.49 crore in FY26, compared with Rs 226.23 crore in FY25.Other lenders are taking a less definitive view because the IRDAI proposals are still under consultation.At AU Small Finance Bank, management said the proposed changes would be a medium-term headwind, although the final impact would depend on the shape of the regulations. The bank is looking to growing forex business, a recovery in credit-card issuance, trade finance and other income streams to offset pressure. Advt Largest private lender HDFC Bank CFO Srinivasan Vaidyanathan said that the proposed “cut down in the commission rates is quite high”. The bank will assess the impact once the final rates are known, including whether lower insurance costs could increase customer participation and volumes, improve persistency and alter the cost structure.IDFC First Bank, meanwhile expects three factors to help manage the impact: savings in cost of funds from FCNR(B) deposits raised under the RBI window, potential rate hikes that could support income on its floating-rate book, and growth in its product agency business and other income. Advt Ujjivan SFB management said the guidelines remain a draft and that it does not immediately see them being implemented in Q4. However, if implementation happens in Q4, “this will be some stress”, management said, while adding that the bank would be able to deliver its committed number if implementation is later.IndusInd Bank MD & CEO Rajiv Anand said the bank is part of industry-level discussions involving insurers, manufacturers, distributors and agents.The proposed changes are likely to have a greater impact on lenders with higher dependence on insurance-linked fees. JM Financial estimates insurance income at 12.5 per cent of Axis Bank’s FY26 PBT, compared with 7.3 per cent for HDFC Bank, 5.8 per cent for Kotak Mahindra Bank and just 0.6 per cent for ICICI Bank.ICICI Bank has said third-party fee income contributes less than 1 per cent of total income, while bancassurance fees account for less than 1 per cent of total income and around 5 per cent of total fee income. It therefore expects the P&L impact to be minimal and does not plan to alter its insurance strategy solely because of the proposed commission changes.For lenders, the eventual impact will therefore depend not only on the final commission caps but also on how customers respond to lower insurance prices, whether volumes rise, and how quickly banks can build alternative fee pools.JM Financial estimates insurance income across the private-bank universe at 7.2 per cent of FY26 PBT, while the figure is considerably higher for several mid-sized lenders and small finance banks.
