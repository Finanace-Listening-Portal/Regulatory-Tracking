Articles 3 min read FinMin asks public sector banks to stock ATMs, ensure cash at BC outlets ahead of 3 day bank strike The proposed September 28-30 bank strike could effectively stretch to a five-day disruption with the preceding weekend, while September 30 also marks half-year closing. DFS has asked banks to ensure cash and essential services, as unions head into conciliation talks on September 22. Advt As part of the contingency measures, banks have been asked to ensure adequate cash availability across alternative delivery channels and keep essential services running during the strike period.Banks are also expected to ensure adequate cash loading at ATMs and automated deposit and withdrawal machines, while monitoring cash availability during the strike period.“We try to minimise the impact by alternate delivery channels and that ATMs remain functional,” a senior public sector banker told ETBFSI.The proposed strike is over the implementation of a five-day banking week and other pending demands of bank unions. The government has already kept the Performance Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme in abeyance, while the five-day banking issue remains unresolved.The timing of the strike has added to the operational concerns for banks. September 26 is the fourth Saturday and September 27 is Sunday, meaning the proposed three day strike could result in five consecutive days of disruption to normal branch operations, with September 30 also marking the half-year closing for banks.“Normally, ATMs can sustain cash requirements for one or two days. But if people are on strike for five days, cash will have to be replenished,” the banker said.The banker also said the timing was particularly significant because of the half-year closing.“Five-day and that too of the last days of the quarter, half-year. It's crucial timing, that's why,” the other banker added. Advt Finance Ministry urges employees to call off strikeThe Finance Ministry on Monday urged employees of public sector banks and regional rural banks to refrain from the proposed strikes and resolve the remaining issues through dialogue.In a statement, the ministry said the government had already kept the PLI scheme in abeyance, addressing one of the two principal demands raised by the unions, while the remaining demand for a five day workweek continues to be examined.12 PSBs issue customer advisoriesAll 12 public sector banks have issued customer advisories asking customers to complete important branch-related transactions before the strike and use alternative channels during the period. Advt State Bank of India, Punjab National Bank, Bank of Baroda, Union Bank of India, Bank of India, Canara Bank, Indian Bank, Indian Overseas Bank, Bank of Maharashtra, UCO Bank, Punjab & Sind Bank and Central Bank of India are among the lenders that have issued advisories.The advisories largely ask customers to use internet and mobile banking, UPI, ATMs, business correspondent/customer service points and other digital channels for routine banking requirements.Some banks have also specifically advised customers to plan cash requirements in advance and complete urgent branch transactions before September 28. Advt The advisories come as banks prepare for possible employee participation in the strike while attempting to keep essential banking channels operational.The next development to be watched is the conciliation meeting scheduled for September 22 between the unions and the authorities. The meeting will be held as part of the labour conciliation process ahead of the proposed strike with the Chief Labour Commissioner (CLC).
