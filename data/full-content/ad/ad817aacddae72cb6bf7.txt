(Patna High Court) Summary: The Patna High Court, in Manoj Kumar Das Vs State of Bihar & Anr., granted conditional anticipatory bail to a petitioner accused of involvement in the transportation of coal using allegedly forged GST documents. Justice Rajiv Roy, by an oral order dated 15 October 2026, considered the allegations arising from Kishanganj Police Station Case No. 454 of 2026, registered on 28 April 2026 under Sections 338, 336(3), 340(2), 318(4), 111(3), 111(4) and 3(5) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS). The FIR was lodged by the Assistant Commissioner, Taxes, Kishanganj Circle, following the interception of a coal-laden truck allegedly carrying forged GST documentation. The petitioner approached the High Court seeking protection against arrest in connection with the criminal proceedings. The petitioner’s counsel submitted that the documents accompanying the truck were correct. Nevertheless, in view of the registration of the FIR, the petitioner expressed his willingness to pay the applicable GST amount or penalty. Counsel further submitted that the petitioner had no criminal antecedents. The State, represented by the learned Additional Public Prosecutor, opposed the application, maintaining that forged documents had been used for the movement of the truck. The competing submissions therefore concerned the alleged use of forged GST documents, the petitioner’s willingness to discharge the tax or penalty liability and the appropriateness of granting anticipatory bail. The High Court considered the allegations, the petitioner’s undertaking regarding payment and his absence of criminal antecedents. While acknowledging that the petitioner would have to face the criminal proceedings, the Court found it appropriate to extend anticipatory bail subject to conditions. Significantly, the Court required the petitioner to submit documentary proof showing payment of the penalty to the concerned office at the time of surrender. The order did not determine whether the GST documents were actually forged or finally adjudicate the petitioner’s criminal liability. Its determination was confined to granting conditional protection against arrest. Accordingly, the Court directed that, in the event of arrest or surrender within four weeks from receipt of the order, the petitioner should be released on anticipatory bail upon furnishing a bail bond of ₹10,000 with two sureties of the like amount each, to the satisfaction of the Chief Judicial Magistrate, Kishanganj, or the successor court. The relief was made subject to the conditions prescribed under Section 438(2) of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, as expressly referred to in the order, along with additional safeguards imposed by the Court. The conditions required one surety to be a family member or relative furnishing official identity documentation, the petitioner’s attendance on every trial date, cooperation with the police investigation, abstention from influencing witnesses or tampering with evidence and avoidance of further criminal offences. Absence from the trial court on two consecutive dates without a plausible explanation would entail cancellation of the bail bond by the trial court itself. The State was also permitted to seek cancellation for witness interference, evidence tampering or further criminal conduct. Thus, the High Court granted anticipatory bail in the alleged GST document forgery case while expressly preserving the criminal proceedings and imposing payment-related and procedural conditions.
