Introduction The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC), through Circular No. 266/12/2026-GST dated 28 October 2026, has clarified an important issue concerning the GST treatment of statutory regulatory authorities, particularly the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI). The clarification addresses whether an authority established under an Act of Parliament, performing statutory regulatory functions and receiving financial assistance from the Government, can be treated as the Central Government for GST purposes. CBIC has clarified that TRAI is a separate statutory authority and does not fall within the definition of Government under Section 2(53) of the CGST Act, 2017. This distinction has significant implications for determining the applicability of the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) on payments made to statutory regulators. However, the clarification does not automatically establish whether every fee charged by such authorities is taxable or exempt. The nature of the transaction, existence of a taxable supply, applicable exemptions and relevant charging provisions must be examined independently. 2. Background of the Issue The GST treatment of fees collected by statutory regulatory authorities has raised questions regarding the applicability of the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM). Several statutory authorities are established through Acts of Parliament or State Legislatures, perform functions of public importance and receive Government funding. TRAI, established under the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India Act, 1997, performs regulatory functions in the telecommunications sector. However, its statutory origin and Government funding do not automatically make it part of the Central Government. The controversy concerns whether services provided by TRAI to business entities fall within the Government-services RCM provisions. CBIC has clarified that TRAI’s separate statutory identity must be recognised when determining GST liability. 3. Relevant Legal Provisions Section 2(53) of the CGST Act, 2017 defines “Government” as the Central Government, while Section 9(3) provides for GST liability under the Reverse Charge Mechanism on notified supplies. Entry 5 of Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate) covers specified services provided by the Central Government, State Government, Union Territory or local authority to business entities. Since TRAI is a separate statutory authority, the Government-services RCM entry cannot apply merely because TRAI performs regulatory functions. The taxability of its fees must be independently examined under Section 7, Schedule III and applicable exemption notifications. 4. Key Clarifications by CBIC Through Circular No. 266/12/2026-GST, CBIC has clarified that TRAI is not the Central Government under Section 2(53) of the CGST Act. Merely being established under legislation, performing regulatory functions or receiving Government grants does not confer Government status. Consequently, Government-services RCM under Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate) cannot apply solely on this basis. However, the GST treatment of fees collected by TRAI must be determined separately, considering the nature of the supply and applicable exemptions. 5. Legal Interpretation and Analysis CBIC’s clarification reinforces the distinction between statutory status and Government status under GST law. Although TRAI performs public regulatory functions, its establishment by Parliament does not make it the Central Government. Therefore, Government-services RCM under Section 9(3), read with Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate), cannot be applied merely on account of its statutory character. Importantly, non-applicability of RCM does not automatically mean exemption from GST. The liability to GST must be examined independently based on whether the activity constitutes a taxable supply, the nature of consideration and any applicable exemption. 6. Relevant Judicial Precedents CERC & DERC v. GST Authorities – Delhi High Court (15 January 2025) The Delhi High Court quashed GST demands concerning statutory regulatory functions performed by the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission and Delhi Electricity Regulatory Commission. The decision highlights that the taxability of statutory regulatory activities requires examination of their legal nature rather than automatically treating every regulatory fee as consideration for a taxable supply. This principle is relevant to CBIC’s clarification concerning TRAI because the legal classification of an authority and the taxability of its activities are separate questions. 7. Impact on Taxpayers and Businesses CBIC’s clarification has important implications for businesses making payments to TRAI and other statutory regulatory authorities. Taxpayers should not automatically discharge GST under the Government-services Reverse Charge Mechanism merely because a payment is made to a statutory authority performing public functions. Instead, the legal status of the supplier, nature of the services, relevant GST provisions and applicable exemptions must be examined independently. The clarification also provides an opportunity for businesses to review their existing treatment of regulatory fees and RCM compliance. 8. Practical Implications and Action Points Businesses and GST professionals should review payments made to TRAI and other statutory regulatory authorities to ensure the correct GST treatment. The legal status of the authority, nature of the regulatory fee, applicable exemptions and relevant RCM provisions must be examined separately. Taxpayers should also reassess past RCM payments, evaluate the applicability of forward charge and maintain proper supporting documentation to minimise compliance risks, incorrect tax payments and potential GST litigation. 9. Conclusion / Key Takeaway CBIC’s clarification establishes that statutory authorities cannot automatically be treated as Government under GST merely because they perform regulatory functions or receive Government funding. Consequently, payments to TRAI do not attract Government-services RCM solely on account of its statutory status. However, non-applicability of RCM does not automatically mean exemption from GST. Businesses must independently examine the nature of regulatory fees, applicable exemptions and relevant charging provisions to determine the correct tax treatment. 10. Conclusion / Key Takeaway CBIC’s clarification reinforces that statutory authorities cannot automatically be treated as Government under GST merely because they perform regulatory functions or receive Government funding. Consequently, payments to TRAI do not attract Government-services RCM solely on account of its statutory status. However, non-applicability of RCM does not automatically mean exemption from GST. Businesses must independently examine the nature of regulatory fees, applicable exemptions and relevant charging provisions to determine the correct tax treatment.
