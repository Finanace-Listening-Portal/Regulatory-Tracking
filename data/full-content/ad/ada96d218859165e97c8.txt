The sharp decline in gold prices over the past two weeks has pushed loan-to-value (LTV) ratios beyond permissible limits.Gold prices have dropped 35% from their January peak and 11% so far in November, as markets factor in the possibility of higher US policy rates. The prospect of rising rates has reduced the appeal of gold, which does not generate any yield. According to lenders, the steep correction has triggered margin calls for a substantial number of customers for the first time in around four years. Advt Gold loan noticesNearly 40% of the gold-loan portfolios of these lenders have reportedly breached the thresholds, with the ratio exceeding 95% in some cases.The notices ask borrowers to either repay a portion of their outstanding dues or furnish additional collateral to bring their LTV ratios back within the prescribed limits. Failure to do so could result in the pledged gold jewellery being auctioned, according to an ET report.Banks and gold-loan financiers, including Muthoot and Manappuram, have told the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) that they have lowered the LTV ratio for new loans as a precautionary measure.The average has been reduced to 60%-65% from around 75%, providing lenders with a greater buffer against any further decline in gold prices, the people cited above said.In gold lending, a breach of the LTV limit is treated as a borrower default, even when the borrower has not missed a scheduled repayment.Executives at lending companies, who requested anonymity, said they are contacting affected borrowers and giving them the option of pledging additional gold or making an upfront payment to cover the margin shortfall before proceeding with an auction. Gold auctions are conducted 30 days after a legal notice has been served. Advt Gold loans are generally short-term facilities, with borrowers often repaying the principal in a lump sum at the end of the tenure. Given this repayment structure, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has instructed lenders to ensure that the value of pledged gold remains adequate to cover the outstanding principal, accumulated interest and auction-related expenses throughout the loan period.Senior executives at major gold-loan companies said they are keeping a close watch on their portfolios, moderating fresh disbursements and stepping up collection efforts. They also warned that auctions could increase if borrowers fail to meet margin calls. Advt Lenders generally maintain an effective loan-to-value (LTV) ratio of around 85% when customers pay interest every month. Where interest is settled at the end of the loan tenure, the ratio is typically kept lower, at 70%-72%.Additional collateralThe fall in gold prices has pushed up LTV ratios as the value of the pledged jewellery has declined. Consider a lender that provides a loan of Rs 75,000 against gold worth Rs 1 lakh. The initial LTV in this case would be 75%. If the collateral loses 15% of its value, the ratio would rise to approximately 88%. A 25% fall would push it to 100%, even if the borrower’s outstanding loan amount remained unchanged. Advt Data from credit information company CRIF show that the gold-loan portfolio had grown to around Rs 23 lakh crore as of September 30, representing approximately 12% of the country's Rs 185 lakh crore retail-loan market.The RBI's concern stems from the fact that a significant part of this expansion has been driven by existing customers borrowing larger amounts, renewing their loans and taking additional credit through top-ups. This growth has not been matched by a comparable rise in the number of borrowers or the volume of gold pledged, according to the ET report.Figures published by the Indian Bullion and Jewellers’ Association (IBJA) show that the price of 999-purity gold in India fell to Rs 1,21,038 per 10 grams on a rolling one-month basis, from the evening closing rate of Rs 1,41,710 per 10 grams on October 26.International gold prices have also declined sharply. The metal's price in US dollars per troy ounce fell 13% over one month and more than 25% over three months to $3,481, according to the latest data from investing.com.Meanwhile, the dollar index, which measures the US currency's strength against a basket of major global currencies, is hovering near its highest level in about two years. It traded close to 104 late Wednesday. A reading above 100 indicates relative strength in the dollar, which can reduce the attractiveness of assets denominated in other currencies.
