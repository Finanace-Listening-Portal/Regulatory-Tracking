Summary : Motilal Oswal Financial Services Limited, or MOFSL, is a SEBI-registered stock broker with registration number INZ000158836 and National Stock Exchange member code 10412. Its current customer materials state that retail clients can access currencies through web, mobile, desktop and call-and-trade channels, subject to account activation and segment approval. This is an Indian exchange-traded derivatives service. It is not permission to remit money to an offshore broker, trade leveraged foreign-exchange contracts on an unauthorised portal or use the Liberalised Remittance Scheme for margin. RBI permits resident users to transact electronically through authorised electronic trading platforms or recognised Indian stock exchanges under the prescribed product and purpose rules. MOFSL’s published client screen shows currency-futures brokerage of ₹25 per lot on both sides for the illustrated tariff, but an individual client’s executed tariff, exchange charges, clearing charges, GST, SEBI turnover fee, stamp duty and other levies must be checked before trading. This profile distinguishes the contracting broker from group companies and explains onboarding, margins, settlement, grievances and tax without promising profit or recommending derivatives. It does not make the broker an Authorised Dealer bank for outward remittances and does not validate a separate overseas platform. A client should reject solicitations that ask for funds outside the registered broker account, promise fixed currency returns or route trading through remote-access software or personal bank accounts. Permitted Currency Contracts and Resident User Conditions RBI’s current exchange-traded foreign-exchange direction allows recognised stock exchanges to offer futures, European call options and European put options in specified pairs. The permitted pairs include USD-INR, EUR-INR, GBP-INR, JPY-INR, EUR-USD, GBP-USD and USD-JPY. Exchange availability can be narrower than the regulatory list, so the live MOFSL contract master and exchange circulars remain decisive. Tenor and contract specifications are determined under RBI and exchange rules. For contracts involving INR, the permitted purpose is hedging a valid contracted exposure. RBI’s direction permits positions without producing underlying documents up to the stated aggregate threshold, but the user must still have a valid underlying contracted exposure that is not hedged elsewhere and must be able to establish it if required. Non-INR pairs have a different purpose rule. A simplified trading screen does not remove these regulatory obligations. Recognised Exchange Trading Is Different From Offshore CFDs An exchange-traded USD-INR future has a standard contract, exchange risk controls, clearing and rupee settlement. A retail CFD offered by an overseas website is a bilateral leveraged product outside that recognised Indian exchange framework. Foreign regulation, marketing in India or absence from the RBI Alert List does not establish Indian-resident eligibility. LRS funds must not be remitted for overseas margin or margin calls. KYC Segment Activation and Order Process The customer needs a valid trading account with completed KYC, PAN, bank verification, contact details and required declarations. Currency derivatives normally require separate segment activation and acceptance of the risk disclosure documents. The broker may seek income or financial proof for derivatives and can apply enhanced checks. A client should activate only through the official logged-in process and should verify the segment status before placing an order. Brokerage Charges Settlement and Withdrawals MOFSL’s current additional-segment consent screen displays currency-futures brokerage of ₹25 per lot on both sides for the tariff shown. This is not a universal quote for every account and does not cover every contract. Options brokerage, negotiated plans and promotional tariffs can differ. The total cost also includes exchange transaction charges, clearing charges where applicable, SEBI turnover fees, GST on taxable services, stamp duty and other statutory levies. Bid-ask spread and slippage are economic costs even though they are not line-item brokerage. Exchange-traded contracts are settled under the exchange and clearing timetable, generally in Indian rupees. Profit shown on the screen is not necessarily withdrawable immediately because realised funds, margin release, settlement credits and bank processing occur on different timelines. A rejected, expired or cancelled order does not create a trade, but charges or margin blocks should be reconciled. Refunds of mistaken deposits and withdrawal requests follow account, bank and compliance checks; the broker cannot reverse a valid market loss merely because the client misunderstood the product. Tax GST and Books for Currency Derivatives For an Indian taxpayer, recognised-exchange currency-derivative results are generally examined under the business-income provisions rather than treated as speculative merely because they are derivatives, subject to satisfaction of statutory conditions and the taxpayer’s facts. Turnover computation, audit thresholds, expense deduction, loss set-off and carry-forward require professional review. Contract notes, ledger, bank statements and exchange-wise trade files should be retained. GST applies to broker and related taxable service charges, not to the entire notional contract value as if it were a service fee. Grievance Escalation and Fraud Controls A client should first raise a written ticket with MOFSL and retain the reference number, contract note and evidence. If unresolved, the matter can be escalated to the broker’s compliance officer, then through the exchange investor grievance mechanism and SEBI’s SCORES platform as applicable. Exchange arbitration may be available for eligible disputes. Credentials, OTPs and remote-access control should never be shared. Payments should go only to verified broker-designated accounts and UPI handles approved for the securities market workflow. Frequently Asked Questions Is MOFSL an RBI authorised dealer bank? No. The service covered here is its role as a SEBI-registered stock broker on a recognised exchange, not an AD bank remittance service. Can a resident use the account for offshore forex CFDs? No. The verified service is Indian exchange-traded currency derivatives. It does not establish permission for overseas CFDs or leveraged portals. Does SEBI registration permit every currency pair? No. The available product must also be permitted by RBI, admitted by the exchange and enabled by the broker. Is an underlying exposure unnecessary below the RBI limit? Documents may not be demanded upfront within the threshold, but the resident must still have a valid unhedged contracted exposure for INR-involving contracts and be able to establish it. Is brokerage the only trading cost? No. Statutory charges, exchange and clearing fees, GST, stamp duty, spread, slippage and funding effects also matter. Can the broker close a position for a margin shortfall? Yes, subject to its risk policy and exchange requirements. Margin needs can rise rapidly. How should an unauthorised trade be reported? Notify the broker immediately in writing, preserve exchange alerts and evidence, and escalate through the compliance, exchange and SEBI routes if unresolved. Can LRS be used to send margin to an overseas broker? No. RBI rules do not permit LRS remittances for overseas margin or margin calls.
