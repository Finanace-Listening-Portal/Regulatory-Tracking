Here's what changed in its reserves and why it matters.Reserve Bank of India (File Photo)Photo : PTIfollow usfollow usThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has increased the proportion of its gold reserves held within the country after transferring more than 60 tonnes of the precious metal from overseas storage facilities to domestic vaults during the six months ended September 2026. The shift changes how India's gold reserves are geographically distributed, even though the central bank's overall gold holdings remained unchanged during the period. By the end of September, nearly 84 per cent of the RBI's gold was stored in India, compared with around 77 per cent at the end of March.Gold is an important component of India's foreign exchange reserves. It helps the central bank diversify its reserve portfolio and provides an additional layer of protection against uncertainties in international financial markets.The RBI's latest half-yearly report also highlights how movements in global bullion prices influenced the value of India's gold reserves.RBI Holds 880.52 Tonnes Of Gold, Domestic Share Rises To 84%According to the RBI's Half-Yearly Report on Management of Foreign Exchange Reserves: April–September 2026, released on 25 November, the central bank held 880.52 metric tonnes of gold as of 30 September 2026.However, the location of these holdings changed considerably over the six-month period.Of the total gold stock:740.13 tonnes were held in domestic vaults in India.140.39 tonnes were maintained in safe custody with the Bank of England and the Bank for International Settlements (BIS).At the end of March 2026, the RBI had 680.05 tonnes stored domestically and 197.67 tonnes held overseas. Another 2.80 tonnes were maintained as gold deposits.Between March and September, the central bank transferred 60.08 tonnes from overseas storage to domestic facilities. Consequently, the domestic share of its gold holdings increased from 77.2 per cent to 84.05 per cent.The overall quantity of gold, however, remained unchanged at 880.52 tonnes.Gold Prices Fall, RBI's Reserve Valuation DeclinesWhile the RBI retained its overall gold holdings, a decline in international bullion prices reduced their valuation.The central bank's report noted that international gold prices fell from $4,721 per troy ounce in April to $4,319 in September 2026, registering a decline of approximately 9 per cent.Several factors contributed to the weakness in prices, including higher US Treasury yields, a stronger US dollar and expectations of tighter monetary policy. These developments weighed on bullion despite continued demand from investors seeking a safe-haven asset and portfolio diversification.As a result, the value of the RBI's gold reserves declined from $115.40 billion at the end of March to $106.08 billion by September.The figures highlight the difference between the quantity of gold held by the central bank and its market value, which can change with international price movements.The RBI's total foreign exchange reserves increased during the reporting period, rising from $691.11 billion at the end of March to $737.78 billion at the end of September.However, gold's contribution to the overall reserves declined in value terms.At the end of March 2026, gold represented 16.70 per cent of India's foreign exchange reserves. By September, this proportion had fallen to 14.38 per cent.This decline came even as the RBI maintained the same quantity of gold throughout the six-month period.The change reflects the combined influence of lower international gold prices and an increase in the country's overall foreign exchange reserves.In other words, the central bank did not reduce its gold holdings, but the metal accounted for a smaller portion of the total reserve portfolio by September.Does RBI's Gold Strategy Affect Retail Buyers And Investors?The movement of gold from overseas vaults to domestic storage does not directly determine the prices consumers pay for jewellery, coins or gold exchange-traded funds (ETFs).Retail gold prices are influenced by several broader market factors, including international bullion rates, currency fluctuations, interest rate movements and geopolitical developments.Nevertheless, the RBI's gold management strategy is relevant to the wider economy. Gold provides diversification within India's foreign exchange reserves and serves as an asset that can help the central bank navigate periods of international financial volatility.For individual investors, the RBI's latest data offers an insight into the country's reserve management priorities.
