Articles 4 min read India InsurTech funding stagnates at $120 million in 2025; 69% flows into distribution: Report India's InsurTech sector is witnessing a shift towards artificial intelligence (AI)-led insurance solutions, but funding continues to remain concentrated in distribution-focused businesses, highlighting a gap in investments across underwriting, claims management and other parts of the insurance value chain, according to a recent report by Boston Consulting Group (BCG) and the India InsurTech Association (IIA). Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Updated On Nov 25, 2026 at 03:23 PM IST var news_msid = 136155997; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook A latest report by the India InsurTech Association (IIA) and BCG found that funding for Indian InsurTech companies remained largely flat at around $120 million in 2025, compared with $118 million in 2024. This is nearly 80% below the 2021 peak of $683 million. The number of funding deals also declined from 48 in 2021 to just 13 in 2025.Distribution-focused InsurTechs accounted for 69% of India's total InsurTech funding in 2025, significantly higher than the global share of 22%. In comparison, risk assessment and underwriting accounted for 12% of domestic funding against 26% globally, while full-stack digital insurance players attracted 17% in India compared with 33% globally. Advt The report said claims management, policy administration, servicing and product development also remain underfunded in India, accounting for just 3% of funding, compared with 19% globally.The report noted that India's InsurTech ecosystem has expanded to over 150 companies, with a cumulative valuation of more than $14.7 billion and cumulative funding exceeding $3 billion. The sector generated over $1.4 billion in revenue in FY25, nearly five times the level recorded in FY21. Eleven companies have crossed the $100 million valuation mark, including two unicorns and two soonicorns.Global InsurTech funding rebounds, AI-led companies attract investor interestGlobally, InsurTech funding increased 9% to $4.9 billion in 2025, marking its first annual rise since 2021, after three consecutive years of decline. AI-led InsurTech companies accounted for nearly 50% of global funding and 44% of deals during the year, attracting around $1.4 billion.Seven of the 10 largest global InsurTech funding rounds in 2025 went to AI-led companies. These companies also accounted for 15 of the 18 largest funding rounds across stages, from seed to Series E and beyond.The report highlighted that global investors are increasingly backing businesses focused on underwriting, claims automation and risk assessment, indicating a shift away from distribution-led models towards technology embedded in core insurance operations. Advt AI adoption gathers pace among insurers; leaders generate 2.5 times more valueAI adoption among insurers is also gaining momentum. According to the report, 53% of insurers are now classified as AI-mature on BCG's AI maturity index, compared with 32% a year ago. Insurers are spending an average of 3.4% of their revenue on AI, while industry leaders are realising 2.5 times more value from their investments than their peers.The report identified four areas where AI is delivering measurable results across the insurance value chain.In health insurance claims, AI agents that assess medical necessity and billing against treatment protocols and tariffs can help reduce claim payouts by 3-7% and turnaround time by 25-30%. In motor insurance, AI-powered renewal journeys have improved renewal rates by five percentage points and conversion rates by 15%. Advt AI-enabled sales workflows are also helping improve advisor productivity by 20%, while automating managers' administrative tasks and freeing up 30% of their time for engagement and coaching.Customer trust remains critical despite growing AI adoptionDespite the increasing use of AI, human intervention continues to play a significant role in insurance purchases and servicing. BCG's survey of over 1,200 insured consumers across 16 cities found that 27% of buyers in 2025 said AI influenced their insurance purchase. However, 79% still bought their policies with a human advisor involved.The survey also found that 53% of consumers were willing to share personal and health data in exchange for more relevant insurance offers, while 62% wanted premiums that reflected their health. Advt Claims experience emerged as a key determinant of customer retention. As many as 92% of dissatisfied claimants said they did not understand their policy, including its terms, coverage limits, inclusions and exclusions.Meanwhile, 82% of customers who had an excellent claims experience said they would consider buying more products from the same insurer, compared with just 12% among those who had a poor claims experience.The report also highlighted that 53% of policy lapses were linked to failed reminders, calls, emails or payment journeys, rather than customers deliberately choosing not to renew.It said insurers would need to move beyond fragmented AI pilots, redesign end-to-end customer journeys and strengthen data foundations to capture the benefits of AI. For InsurTechs, the next growth opportunity lies in expanding beyond distribution into claims, underwriting, renewals and servicing, with a greater focus on demonstrating measurable value for insurers.
