Articles 5 min read ARCIL seeing fastest growth in NBFC originated retail bad loans: CEO Phanindranath Kakarla Asset Reconstruction Company India Ltd (ARCIL) is seeing the fastest growth in retail assets originated by NBFCs, even as bank originated, corporate and other retail portfolios continue to grow, with CEO Phanindranath Kakarla and CFO Pramod Kumar Gupta saying retail and SME assets are likely to gain a larger share of the portfolio as they grow faster than corporate assets. The company, which is set to become the first standalone asset reconstruction company to list in India, is also open to acquisitions of smaller ARCs. What is changing in the stressed-asset market, particularly between corporate and retail assets?Phanindranath Kakarla and Pramod Gupta: The banking system's NPAs are at decadal lows, but that does not mean the opportunity for ARCs has disappeared. The overall credit base is now more than Rs 200 lakh crore, and even if around 2 per cent of that gets stressed in a year, that still creates a very large pool of assets.There is also incremental lending of around Rs 30 lakh crore every year. So, while the percentage of stressed assets may be lower, the absolute opportunity remains significant.We are seeing a shift in the composition of the assets available to us. Retail and SME are growing faster than corporate, and we expect their share of our portfolio to increase going forward. Advt Q. Within retail, where are you seeing the strongest growth?Phanindranath Kakarla: The fastest growth has come from retail assets originated by NBFCs. That does not mean that bank-originated assets are not growing. They are also growing, as are corporate assets and other retail portfolios.It is just that NBFC-originated retail assets are growing faster compared with the other asset classes.Q. How significant are retail and SME assets in ARCIL’s portfolio now?Phanindranath Kakarla: In FY26, corporate assets accounted for around 69 per cent of our portfolio, while SME was around 8 per cent and the balance was retail. So retail and SME together were a little over 31 per cent.Over the last couple of years, the retail and SME portfolio has grown at a CAGR of close to 56 per cent. Given the availability of assets and the growth opportunity, we expect retail and SME to continue gaining share.Q. Are you seeing greater opportunity in secured or unsecured retail bad loans?Phanindranath Kakarla: We are seeing opportunities in both secured and unsecured assets. We are not seeing a sharp divergence between the two at this point. What is changing is that smaller-ticket secured and unsecured assets are increasing as a share of the opportunity. That requires us to strengthen our systems, processes and operating capabilities because the volumes are much higher. Advt Q. How large is ARCIL’s retail resolution operation today?Phanindranath Kakarla: Today, we handle around 32 lakh NPA accounts on the retail side, which makes us one of the larger handlers of NPA accounts in the country.We have a pan-India presence and cover around 24 states. Retail resolution is much more technology-led compared with corporate resolution, and we have invested significantly in building those capabilities.Q. How much are you investing in technology and AI for retail recovery?Phanindranath Kakarla and Pramod Gupta: The platform today handles millions of transactions, and the scale of our retail business has increased substantially. Five years ago, we had fewer than one lakh retail borrowers or accounts; today, that number is around 32 lakh. Advt AI is already being used in our operations. It is not merely at the pilot stage. We will continue to invest in and deploy AI wherever we see a clear return on investment and where it can improve the efficiency of our processes.Q. How do you see NARCL’s role in the market?Phanindranath Kakarla and Pramod Gupta: We see NARCL as both a competitor and a complement. There are very large-ticket stressed assets where private ARCs may not have the capacity or appetite to participate, and that is an area where NARCL can play a role.There is some overlap at the lower end, but the overall stressed-asset pool is large enough for multiple players. The ARC industry is still handling only a relatively small proportion of the total stressed-asset opportunity. Advt Q. Could ARCIL look at acquisitions of smaller ARCs?Phanindranath Kakarla and Pramod Gupta: We are open to acquisitions. If there is a willing seller, the right price and a strategic fit, we would certainly evaluate it.Any such transaction would, of course, have to go through the Board and meet our strategic and financial criteria. We believe the sector needs larger ARC players with greater access to capital and stronger operating capabilities.Q. What is the strategy for ARCIL after the IPO?Phanindranath Kakarla and Pramod Gupta: The IPO does not change our strategy. We will continue to remain a standalone, pure-play ARC.The listing will bring greater visibility and will also come with higher disclosure and governance requirements, but our core strategy will remain focused on asset reconstruction. We are not looking to diversify beyond this business.Q. What is the outlook for ARCIL’s growth across asset classes?Phanindranath Kakarla and Pramod Kumar Gupta: Retail will remain an important focus because of the scale, predictability of cash flows and the technology capabilities we have built. At the same time, we will continue to evaluate corporate and SME opportunities based on the quality of the assets and their resolution potential.
