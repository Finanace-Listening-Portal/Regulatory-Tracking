Articles 4 min read RBI reviews priority sector lending framework, retains broad targets in draft norms The Reserve Bank of India has proposed replacing the common priority sector lending framework with five separate directions for different categories of banks, while retaining the broad lending targets and most existing eligibility criteria. Feedback has been invited until November 26. The existing framework is contained in the RBI's Priority Sector Lending – Targets and Classification Directions, 2025, which has been amended subsequently. Advt While the drafts retain the broad PSL targets and several existing eligibility thresholds, RBI has proposed a separate structure for each category of regulated entity. The central bank has also said that instructions not incorporated into the consolidated draft directions will be repealed once the final directions are issued. These will be notified separately.Under the proposed daft framework, commercial banks and local area banks will continue to have an overall PSL target of 40 per cent of adjusted net bank credit (ANBC) or the credit equivalent of off-balance sheet exposures, whichever is higher.Small finance banks and urban cooperative banks will retain an overall target of 60 per cent, while regional rural banks will continue to have a higher target of 75 per cent.For commercial banks, SFBs and LABs, the agriculture sub-target remains at 18 per cent, the micro-enterprise target at 7.5 per cent and the weaker-sections target at 12 per cent. RRBs will also have an agriculture target of 18 per cent and a micro-enterprise target of 7.5 per cent, while their weaker-sections target stands at 15 per cent.UCBs will continue to have a 7.5 per cent target for micro enterprises and 12 per cent for weaker sections, without a separate agriculture sub-target. Advt For RRBs, lending to medium enterprises, social infrastructure and renewable energy will be reckoned towards PSL achievement only up to 15 per cent of ANBC.Existing eligibility limits largely carried forwardThe drafts retain several of the existing eligibility thresholds across agriculture, education, housing, social infrastructure and renewable energy.These include the aggregate sanctioned limit of Rs 100 crore per borrower from the banking system for agriculture infrastructure and food and agro-processing loans. The framework also retains the Rs 25 lakh limit for individual education loans, including vocational courses. Advt For housing, loans for purchase or construction of dwelling units will continue to qualify within limits of Rs 50 lakh in centres with a population of 50 lakh and above, Rs 45 lakh in centres with a population of 10 lakh to below 50 lakh, and Rs 35 lakh in centres with a population below 10 lakh, subject to the prescribed dwelling-unit cost limits.The drafts also retain the provision allowing loans of up to Rs 2.5 lakh to individuals exclusively engaged in allied agricultural activities to be counted under the small and marginal farmers category, without requiring any accompanying landholding criteria. Advt In renewable energy, loans of up to Rs 35 crore for eligible projects and up to Rs 10 lakh for individual households continue to qualify for priority sector classification.The directions provide for the treatment of eligible FCNR(B) and NRE-funded advances in the computation of ANBC. Such advances, funded from deposits qualifying for CRR and SLR exemptions under RBI's August 2026 directions, are excluded from the calculation, subject to the prescribed limits. This provision was already introduced through an earlier amendment and is not a fresh proposal in the November drafts.The drafts further provide that loans already classified as priority sector under the erstwhile directions will continue to retain that classification until maturity.
