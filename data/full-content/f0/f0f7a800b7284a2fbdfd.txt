Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Coforge Smartserve Limited Vs Commissioner of Service Tax (CESTAT Chandigarh) Summary: The Chandigarh Bench of the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT) set aside a service tax demand of Rs. 52,34,008 confirmed against Coforge Smartserve Limited, formerly known as NIIT Smartserve Ltd., together with an equal penalty under Section 78 and a penalty of Rs. 5,000 under Section 77 of the Finance Act, 1994. The Tribunal held that a demand under Business Auxiliary Services cannot be sustained without identifying the applicable sub-clause of Section 65(19). It also held that commercial coaching and training, convention centre services and software maintenance services performed outside India were not liable to service tax under the applicable import of services provisions in the circumstances of the case. The appellant was engaged in providing call centre services exported outside India and procured various services from overseas service providers. Following an audit, a show cause notice dated 17 April 2009 proposed recovery of service tax for the period from 1 October 2003 to 31 March 2008 under different taxable service categories. These included marketing support services, commission paid to foreign service providers, convention centre services, subscriptions paid to overseas institutions for commercial training or coaching, and software upgradation and maintenance services. The adjudicating authority dropped the demands relating to the period prior to 1 January 2005 but confirmed the remaining demands. The confirmed amount comprised Rs. 41,12,109 under Business Auxiliary Services, Rs. 1,06,652 under Convention Centre Services, Rs. 4,41,455 under Commercial Coaching or Training Services and Rs. 5,73,792 under Management, Maintenance or Repair Services. Before the Tribunal, the appellant argued that service tax on imported services under the reverse charge mechanism could not be levied prior to 18 April 2006, when Section 66A of the Finance Act, 1994 came into effect. Reliance was placed upon the Bombay High Court judgment in Indian National Ship Owners Association Vs Union of India, subsequently affirmed by the Supreme Court, and the departmental circular dated 26 September 2011. The appellant further contended that neither the show cause notice nor the adjudication order identified the specific sub-clause of Section 65(19) under which the marketing support services were sought to be taxed. It also submitted that the disputed payments after 18 April 2006 concerned advertisements in foreign magazines and journals and that sale of advertising space in print media was specifically excluded from the taxable category introduced under Section 65(105)(zzzm) with effect from 1 May 2006. Examining the demand under Business Auxiliary Services, the Tribunal reiterated that the definition under Section 65(19) encompassed seven distinct categories of activities. Following Joshi Auto Zone Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner of Service Tax, Chandigarh, Syniverse Mobile Solutions Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner of Customs, Central Excise & Service Tax, Hyderabad-IV, CCE, Goa Vs Swapnil Asnodkar, United Telecoms Ltd. Vs CST, Hyderabad and Balaji Enterprises Vs Commissioner of Central Excise and Service Tax, Jaipur-I, it held that the Department was required to identify the particular statutory sub-clause applicable to the alleged services. Merely referring to the general category of Business Auxiliary Services without identifying the precise taxable activity was insufficient. Accordingly, the Tribunal set aside the demand of Rs. 41,12,109 on this ground. Regarding the demand of Rs. 4,41,455 under Commercial Coaching or Training Services, the Tribunal noted that the disputed subscription fees had been paid to institutions situated outside India and that the services were performed entirely abroad. Under Rule 3(ii) of the Taxation of Services (Provided from Outside India and Received in India) Rules, 2006, services falling within the relevant performance-based category could qualify as imported taxable services only when the prescribed territorial performance conditions were satisfied. Following the decisions in Firmenich Aromatics India Pvt. Ltd. and ABB Ltd., the Tribunal held that no service tax was payable under the reverse charge mechanism on the disputed overseas training services. On the demand of Rs. 1,06,652 under Convention Centre Services, the Tribunal found that the entire disputed demand related to the period prior to 18 April 2006, before the introduction of Section 66A. It additionally observed that convention services were covered by the performance-based provisions of Rule 3(ii) and that the services in question had been performed outside India. Consequently, the demand was unsustainable on both grounds. Similarly, regarding the demand of Rs. 5,73,792 under Management, Maintenance or Repair Services, the Tribunal found that although the software was installed in systems located in India, the entire software upgradation and maintenance activity had been undertaken from outside India through the internet. Applying Rule 3(ii) in the circumstances of the case, it set aside this demand as well. The appellant also challenged invocation of the extended period of limitation, contending that the Department had failed to establish fraud, collusion, wilful misstatement, suppression of facts or contravention of statutory provisions with intent to evade payment of tax. The Tribunal accepted this contention and further observed that the transactions were revenue neutral because the appellant would have been entitled to avail CENVAT credit of the service tax paid under reverse charge. In the absence of evidence establishing mala fide intention to evade payment of tax, it held that the entire demand was barred by limitation. Since the substantive tax demands were set aside, the consequential interest and penalties were also held unsustainable. Accordingly, CESTAT set aside the impugned adjudication order dated 30 September 2010 and allowed the appeal with consequential relief, if any, in accordance with law. The decision reinforces the requirement of precise statutory classification in service tax proceedings, the significance of territorial performance conditions applicable to specified imported services, and the necessity of establishing the statutory ingredients before invoking the extended period of limitation. The findings relate to the erstwhile service tax regime and the statutory provisions applicable to the disputed period. In this regard, we note that it is not in dispute that the services in question are performed outside India as evident from Para 34.6 of the Impugned order whereas in terms of Rule 3(ii) of the Taxation of Services (Provided from outside India and Received in India) Rules, 2006, receipt of such services shall qualify as import only if the services are performed in India. In the present case, we find that the subscription fee paid to institutes i.e. foreign service providers, were for coaching and training performed completely outside India and therefore, no tax is payable under reverse charge mechanism as held in the case of M/s Firmenich Aromatics India Pvt. Ltd. cited (supra), the relevant findings contained in Para 6 which are reproduced herein as under:- “6. We have gone through the rival submission, we find that liability of the tax of the question of law can be raised at any time. It is not in dispute that the training on which the tax has been demanded was under taken abroad. The services under Section 65 (105) (zzc) fall under Rule 3(2) of the Taxation of Service (provided from outside India and received in India) Rules, 2006. The services covered under the said sub-Rule is taxable only when the same are performed in India. In the instance case, it is not in dispute that the said service was performed outside of India. Therefore, there cannot be any liability of tax. The appeal on this count is allowed.” 7.1 Similarly, in the case of ABB Ltd. cited (supra), the Tribunal has considered this issue as held as under:- “Further as far as commercial training or coaching services are concerned, the Learned Counsel submitted that the appellant had made the payment to M/s. ABB Technology Ltd. Zurich, Switzerland in connection with the participation of the employees of the appellants in a seminar conducted by the foreign company at a place outside India. Appellant has also submitted that 4 of their employees attended IMD Finance & Business Control Seminar from 4-3-2007 to 16-3-2007 at Lusanne conducted by M/s. ABB Ltd., Zurich and the appellant has paid in aggregate Rs. 15,69,846/- to the foreign company. The seminar was exclusively conducted outside India. Further, we find that appellant has also attached the copies of the certificates showing the payment for the seminar which were conducted outside the country. In view of these facts, we are of the opinion that since training and coaching service happened outside India, the appellants were not liable to pay Service Tax as the same is excluded from the ambit of Rule 3(1)(ii) of the Rules.” 7.2 By following the ratio of the above said decision, we set-aside the demand on this service also. 8. As regards, the demand of service tax on Convention Centre Service, we find that the entire demand in respect of Convention Centre Services pertains to the period prior to 18.04.2006 hence no service tax can be demanded for the period prior to 18.04.2006 as Section 66A was introduced only w.e.f. 18.04.2006. We also find that as per Rule 3(ii) of the Import Rules, receipt of convention services shall qualify as import only if the services are performed in India and therefore, it cannot be taxed in India. Hence, we set-aside this demand also. 9. As regards, the demand under Management, Maintenance or Repair Service, we find that the demand has been confirmed with respect to the services of software upgradation and maintenance in respect of software installed in systems located in India but the entire activity has been undertaken from outside India through Internet. 9.1 Further, as per Rule 3(ii) of Taxation of Services (Provided from outside India and received in India) Rules, 2000, the receipt of such services shall qualify as import only if the services are performed in India whereas in the present case, it is not disputed that the services are provided outside India and therefore no service tax can be demanded under this head also and we set-aside the demand under the head of Management, Maintenance or Repair Services also. 10. As far as the invoking the extended period of limitation is concerned, we find that the department in order to invoke the extended period has to establish fraud, collusion, wilful misstatement or suppression of facts or contravention of any provisions of this Act or Rules with an intent to evade the payment of tax which has not been done by the department in this case and moreover, the entire transaction was revenue neutral as the appellant would have taken the credit of the service tax paid under reverse charge and there was no malafide intention to evade the tax. Therefore, we hold that the entire demand is barred by limitation. Once the demand of tax has been set-aside then there is no question of interest and penalty. 11. In view of our discussion above, we are of the considered view that the impugned order is not sustainable in law, therefore, we set-aside the same by allowing the appeal of the appellant with consequential relief, if any, as per law.
