Meetings of the RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) have consistently faced challenging global and domestic macroeconomic backdrops since the outbreak of the US-Iran conflict in February this year. The MPC's latest review (concluded on October 7) proved to be no different. Externally, Brent crude is hovering around the $100 mark, while the US 10-year bond yield, trading near 5.3 per cent, continues to attract massive capital flows to the US, strengthening the greenback. Consequently, FIIs maintain their selling spree in India, compounding pressure on the rupee. Domestically, the El Niño phenomenon and the deficient monsoon have emerged as major threats to rural consumption, even as headline inflation threatens to breach comfortable thresholds. In this challenging context, reining in potential inflation without upsetting the economy's growth momentum is a delicate balancing act.
