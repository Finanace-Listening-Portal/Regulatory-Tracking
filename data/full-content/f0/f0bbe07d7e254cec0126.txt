(Bombay High Court) Summary: The Bombay High Court allowed the writ petition filed by Laxmi Organic Industries Ltd. against the appellate order dated 24 February 2022 denying transitional credit of ₹6,29,02,146. The company manufactured ethyl acetate through two units sharing a common GST registration, with superior denatured spirit stored in one unit and transferred to the other. It carried forward credit into GST through a timely Form TRAN-1, reporting the amount under Section 140(5) of the CGST Act. The Revenue alleged that the credit should instead have been reported under Section 140(1), and confirmed reversal following a show-cause notice dated 30 September 2020. The Court observed that neither the quantum nor the underlying eligibility of the CENVAT/input tax credit was disputed. The issue was the provision or column used in the declaration. It held that a wrong category in TRAN-1, which remains subject to verification by the authorities, could not defeat an otherwise admissible vested credit. Relying on Heritage Lifestyles and Developers Private Limited v. Union of India, the Bench held that technical defects could not justify rejection of admitted credit. It found the impugned order without jurisdiction, allowed the petition in the terms recorded in the operative order and made the rule absolute. The entire dispute revolves around Section 140(1) and Section 140(5) of the CGST Act, 2017. Under Section 140 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 117 of the Rules, 2017, a migrated registered supplier is permitted to file a declaration in Form TRAN-1 intimating details of CENVAT credit of tax paid on inputs, capital goods, and input services in order to carry forward the same to the electronic credit ledger / input tax credit account under the CGST Act, 2017. 13. In the present case, the total quantum of transitional credit as is contended by the petitioner was Rs. 6,29,02,146/-. The quantum of credit is not disputed by the respondents. The petitioner filed TRAN-1 form within the prescribed time limit. Under bonafide belief, the petitioner transitioned the CENVAT credit into GST regime under Section 140(5) of the CGST Act, 2017. 14. On 30/09/2020, a show-cause notice was issued by the respondent No.6 alleging that the petitioner has wrongly transitioned the credit to the tune of Rs. 6,29,02,146/- under Section 140(5) of the CGST Act, 2017 and thus proposed to disallow the input tax credit transferred to GST regime. The show-cause notice proposed to recover the amount. By an order dated 26/02/2021, the show-cause notice was confirmed, and ultimately the respondent No.6 upheld the demand of reversal of input tax credit of Rs. 6,29,02,146/- on 24/02/2022. The respondent No. 7 dismissed the appeal. 15. Section 140(1) and Section 140(5) of the CGST Act, 2017 reads as under: 140. Transitional arrangements for input tax credit.— (1) A registered person, other than a person opting to pay tax under section 10, shall be entitled to take, in his electronic credit ledger, the amount of CENVAT credit of eligible duties carried forward in the return relating to the period ending with the day immediately preceding the appointed day, furnished by him under the existing law [within such time and]in such manner as may be prescribed: Provided that the registered person shall not be allowed to take credit in the following circumstances, namely:— i. where the said amount of credit is not admissible as input tax credit under his Act; or ii. where he has not furnished all the returns required under the existing law for the period of six months immediately preceding the appointed date; or iii. where the said amount of credit relates to goods manufactured and cleared under such exemption notifications as are notified by the Government. ….. ….. ….. (5) A registered person shall be entitled to take, in his electronic credit ledger, credit of eligible duties and taxes in respect of inputs or input services received on or after the appointed day but the duty or tax in respect of which has been paid by the supplier under the [existing law, within such time and in such manner as may be prescribed, subject to the condition that the invoice or any other duty or tax paying document of the same was recorded in the books of subject to the condition that the invoice or any other duty or tax paying document of the same was recorded in the books of account of such person within a period of thirty days from the appointed day: Provided that the period of thirty days may, on sufficient cause being shown, be extended by the Commissioner for a further period not exceeding thirty days: Provided further that said registered person shall furnish a statement, in such manner as may be prescribed, in respect of credit that has been taken under this sub­section.” 16. The eligibility of CENVAT credit / input tax credit was never under dispute. The returns were filed under the erstwhile regime. With introduction of GST, the petitioner has filed declaration in the nature of form TRAN-1. The said form was accepted. Once this is the case, the question of denial of input credit for mere inadvertent disclosure in the form TRAN-1 would not arise. The petitioner had taken transitional credit under Section 140(5) of the GST Act. The petitioner was entitled to transitioned credit pertaining to inputs in terms of Section 140(5) of the CGST Act. The petitioner had applied for extension beyond 30 days to the jurisdictional Commissioner in accordance with provisions of Section 140(5) of the CGST Act. Even the impugned order does not dispute the eligibility of transitional input credit. The impugned order notes that the petitioner is entitled to transitional credit under Section 140(1) of the CGST Act. 17. The whole case of the Revenue is that the petitioner has wrongly taken credit in terms of Section 140(5) instead of Section 140(1) of the CGST Act. TRAN-1 is a declaration which would be subject to verification and scrutiny by the Revenue. Only on the ground that the credit was transitioned under a wrong category/column could not be a ground to disallow the same. The unutilized credit arising on account of duty/tax paid is a vested right which cannot be taken away on a technical grounds. Right to input credit is an indefeasible right. Only because incorrect provision/column was mentioned was not sufficient to disallow the same. 18. This Court in the matter of Heritage Lifestyles and Developers Private Limited (supra) has held in paragraph Nos. 22 and 23 as under: “22. Be that as it may, it is true that the above circular has been issued keeping in mind cases where difficulties have been faced by a section of tax payers owing to technical glitches on the GST Portal. However, the facts of this case are peculiar in as much as the respondents themselves admitted that the Petitioner is eligible for input tax credit but have rejected the claim because the ITGRC has not approved it saying that the tax payer has neither tried for saving/submitting or filing TRAN-1. There is no further explanation or clarification on this issue by the Respondents except to state the ITGRC description viz. “The tax payer has neither tried for saving/submitting or filing TRAN-1”. Therefore it would be not necessary for us to even deal with the Circular under which the application for manual TRAN-1 has been made. When there is no dispute to the fact that the Petitioner is otherwise eligible for credit of Rs. 78,62,466/- then to deny the benefit of such Input credit merely on technical grounds cannot be justified. Merely on technical ground an admitted input credit is sought to be denied to the Petitioner. That according to us would be wholly unfair and a travesty of justice. It is in these facts and circumstances that we are compelled to invoke our writ jurisdiction in this case. 23. In this context, we would like to refer to the Supreme Court decision in the case of Mangalore Chemicals and Fertilizers Ltd. v. Deputy Commissioner (supra). That was a case where there was no dispute that the appellant was entitled to the benefit of an exemption under notification dated 30.06.1969 nor there was a dispute that the refunds were eligible to the adjusted against sales tax payable for respective years, but the only controversy was whether the appellant not having actually secured “prior permission” would be entitled to adjustment having regard to the words of notification of 11th August, 1975, that until permission of renewal is granted by the Deputy Commissioner of Commercial Taxes, the new industry should not be allowed to adjust the refunds. Hon’ble Supreme Court aptly summarized the contention as under :- “The contention virtually means this: “No doubt you were eligible and entitled to make the adjustments. There was also no impediment in law to grant you such permission. But see language of Clause 5. Since we did not give you the permission you cannot be permitted to adjust” Is this the effect of the law?” 19. In the present case also, admitted input credit is sought to be denied to the petitioner on technical grounds. In absence of any dispute that the petitioner was otherwise eligible for the input credit of Rs. 6,29,02,146/- the impugned order is totally without jurisdiction and hence deserve to be quashed and set aside. 20.
