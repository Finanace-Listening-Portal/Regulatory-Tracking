#AD Payment gateway reconciliation is the process of matching every online payment with the settlement that reaches your bank account. It confirms that each order, fee, refund and deduction adds up, so your books show the true picture of your revenue. For a business with a few payments a day, a spreadsheet is enough. Once volumes grow, it turns into a monthly struggle. Settlements arrive in batches, charges are deducted at the source, and refunds sit in a different report. The tools you choose decide how much of this work your team still does by hand. These are the usual causes. One bank credit covers hundreds of transactions, so it cannot be matched line by line. Gateway fees and GST on those fees are deducted before payout, so the bank amount never equals the sales figure. Refunds and chargebacks are adjusted in later settlements. Each payment mode and channel has its own report format. Failed or pending transactions are sometimes marked as paid in the order system. Bank transfers often arrive without an order number or customer reference. Every unmatched entry needs someone to investigate it. This slows down reporting, delays GST filing and makes audits harder. How the Reconciliation Process Works The steps stay the same whether you use a tool or not. Good software makes each step faster. 1. Pull the transaction report for the period. 2. Pull the settlement report showing what was paid out and when. 3. Match each payout with the credit in your bank statement. 4. Check that fees, taxes and refunds explain the gap between sales and payouts. 5. Match every successful payment with its order or invoice. 6. Flag and resolve anything that does not match. Steps three to five take the most time when done manually. This is where your choice of the best payment gateway makes a real difference, because the gateway controls how clean your data is from the start.
