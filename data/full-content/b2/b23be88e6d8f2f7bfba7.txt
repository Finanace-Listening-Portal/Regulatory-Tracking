Vs ACIT (ITAT Delhi) Adding Sundry Creditors and Disallowing the Corresponding Expenses Results in Double Addition Tribunal Deletes the Addition for Sundry Creditors The Delhi ITAT deleted an addition of ₹1,28,85,956 under section 68 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 because the Assessing Officer had added a portion of the outstanding sundry creditors while also disallowing the corresponding expenditure. The Tribunal found that this resulted in a double addition. It separately examined the evidence supporting the expenditure and reduced the estimated disallowance from 10% to 5%. The appeal also involved share application money, a difference between Form 26AS and the accounts, and the year in which TDS credit could be allowed. Same Parties Appeared in the Creditor and Expense Schedules The assessee provided management services to the construction sector. In scrutiny, the Assessing Officer questioned the increase in sundry creditors from ₹42.66 lakh to ₹6.87 crore. The company furnished the names of the creditors, the nature of services and the outstanding balances. The Assessing Officer considered the material insufficient in the absence of confirmations and other supporting records and made an estimated addition at 20% of outstanding creditors. He also disallowed ₹2,55,95,986, representing 10% of the expenditure examined. The Tribunal compared the trade payable and expense schedules and found that parties appearing as creditors also appeared as service providers in the expense schedule. The Assessing Officer had questioned their outstanding balances and disallowed the corresponding service expenses for want of substantiation. The Tribunal therefore directed deletion of the creditor addition. It found, however, that the company had failed to establish that the entire expenditure was incurred wholly and exclusively for business under section 37(1). On the facts, it considered a 5% disallowance sufficient. Share Application Money From an Individual Investor The company disputed the treatment of amounts received from Anant Jain. The Tribunal found that ₹1,31,47,650 received in FY 2011–12 could be traced to the maturity proceeds of five fixed deposits in his State Bank of India account. The assessment for AY 2012–13 had already been completed under section 143(3), and the share capital had not been questioned in that assessment. The Tribunal held that the CIT(A)’s direction to reopen that year was incorrect. For the ₹1.35 crore received in the year under appeal, the Tribunal noted a credit of ₹10 crore in the investor’s Citibank account and his confirmation of the investment. It directed the Assessing Officer to obtain the bank records and verify whether sufficient funds were available. If verified, no addition was to be made. The Tribunal also expressly directed the Assessing Officer not to examine the source of the source in Jain’s hands. That direction forms part of the remand in this case. Foreign Subscription Established Through Documentary Evidence The Tribunal deleted a separate addition of ₹52,13,696 relating to share application money received from Turner International Pte. Ltd. The company produced the foreign inward remittance certificate, the subscriber’s authorisation, RBI approval, the share certificate, the subscriber’s financial statements and the return of allotment filed with the Registrar of Companies. Taken together, these documents established the subscriber’s identity, creditworthiness and the genuineness of the transaction. The Tribunal accepted that the investment was absent from the subscriber’s financial statements because the subscriber had treated it as a loss. In the circumstances, that accounting treatment did not justify treating the remittance as unexplained in the assessee’s hands. Form 26AS Difference and TDS Credit Required Different Inquiries The CIT(A) had sustained ₹46,56,280 out of the addition made for the difference between Form 26AS and the receipts recorded in the accounts. The assessee submitted that it had already reduced its expenditure by the disputed amount. Since the details on record were insufficient to verify that claim, the Tribunal restored the issue to the Assessing Officer. It directed that no further addition be made if the adjustment against expenditure was verified. The Tribunal separately considered TDS credit of ₹35,28,350 on customer advances. The corresponding income had been offered to tax in subsequent years. The Tribunal directed the Assessing Officer to allow the credit in the year in which the corresponding income was offered to tax, consistent with the matching principle under section 199 read with Rule 37BA. The appeal was partly allowed. Author’s Comments The creditor issue turned on matching the outstanding liability with the underlying expense. A party-wise reconciliation showed why taxing the payable and disallowing the corresponding expense duplicated the adjustment. The Form 26AS issue called for a different reconciliation. The receipts had to be traced to their treatment in the profit and loss account, while the TDS on advances had to be matched with the year in which the related income was offered. Each inquiry depended on how the same transaction appeared in the relevant records. Cases Discussed CIT Vs. Lovely Exports P Ltd (2008) 216 CTR 195 (SC) — cited in the assessee’s grounds of appeal. CIT Vs. Gagandeep Infrastructure (P) Ltd. (2012) 394 ITR 680 (Bom.) — relied on in paragraph 10 regarding the source-of-source requirement. M/s J.R. Fiber Glass Industries Pvt. Ltd. Vs. NFAC, ITA No. 2848/Mum/2023 — cited in paragraph 10. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI The present appeal is filed by assessee against the order dated 04.09.2018 passed by Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax (A)-28, New Delhi [“Ld. CIT(A)”] in Appeal No. 348/17-18 u/s 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 [“the Act”] arising out of assessment order dated 23.03.2016 passed u/s 143(3) of the Act pertaining to Assessment Year 2013-14. 2. Brief facts of the case are that during the year under appeal assessee company was engaged in the business of providing management services in relation to all types of constructions sectors. The return of income was e-filed on 31.10.2013, declaring total loss of INR 15,69,82,968/- and the same was revised on 10.07.2014 at a loss of INR 15,68,98,089/-. The case was selected under CASS for following 04 reasons:- (i) High ratio or refund of TDS; (ii) Large other expenses claimed in Profit & Loss Account; (iii) Unsecured loans from persons who had not filed return of income; and (iv) Depreciation claimed at high rate. 3. Notice u/s 143(2) of the Act was issued on 03.09.2014 followed by various notices issued u/s 142(1) alongwith questionnaire from time to time which were replied by the assessee. The assessment order was passed by making addition of INR 6,52,55,696/- towards the share application money received during the year by holding the same as unexplained credit u/s 68 of the Act. Besides this, a sum of INR 1,28,85,956/- being 20% of the total amount of sundry creditors was added and a sum of INR 4,89,20,490/- was added as difference in 26AS statement and Profit & Loss Account as undisclosed income. Further, disallowance of INR 2,55,95,986/- was made being 10% of the expenses claimed. The AO further observed that assessee has made delayed payment of employee’s contribution towards PF & ESI of INR 48,04,492/- thus the same was added back and accordingly total income of the assessee was assessed at an income of INR 5,64,531/-. 4. Against the said order, an appeal was filed by the assessee before the ld. CIT(A) who in terms of order dated 04.09.2018, partly allowed the appeal of the assessee. wherein out of the addition of INR 6,52,55,696/- made towards share application money, deleted the addition of INR 3,33,90,000/- received from Sahara Prime City Ltd. and confirmed the share application money of Rs. 2,66,52,000/- received from Anant Jain and Rs. 52,13,696/- received from Turner International Pte. Ltd. Ld. CIT Further held that out of the total sum of INR 2,66,52,000/- received from Anant Jain, a sum of INR 131,47,650/- was received in preceding assessment year i.e. in AY 2012-13 and thus directed to reopen the same in view of provisions of section 150(1) of the Act. The addition of INR 1,28,85,196/- made as unexplained cash credit u/s 68 of the Act being 20% of Sundry creditors was also confirmed. With respect to the addition of INR 4,89,20,490/- made towards the difference between the income declared and as per 26AS statement, ld. CIT(A) has confirmed the addition of INR 46,56,280/- and deleted the balance amount. Further, ld. CIT(A) has confirmed 10% of the adhoc disallowance made out of the expenditure made in the Profit & Loss Account. Assessee’s claim of TDS credit of INR 35,28,350/- was also denied. Accordingly, the appeal was partly allowed. 5. Aggrieved by the order of ld. CIT(A), the assessee is in appeal before the Tribunal by taking following Grounds of appeal as mentioned in the appeal memo:- 1. “On the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld. CIT(A) erred in confirming the addition of Rs 1,35,00,000/- under Section 68 representing share application money received by the Appellant Company. 2. On the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld. CIT(A) erred in directing the AO to issue notice under Section 148 for Assessment Year [“AY”] 2012-13 to make re- assessment of assessee company for share application money received from Annat Jain during the course of appellate proceedings of assessee company for AY 2013-14. 2.1 Without prejudice to the above, on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld. CIT(A) erred in directing the Assessing Officer to make addition in the hands of assessee company (Investee) in AY 2012-13 for the failure of Annat Jain (Investor) to satisfactorily explain the source of investment in assessee company, in complete disregard of binding Supreme Court ruling in the case of CIT Vs Lovely Exports P Ltd (2008) 216 CTR 195 (SC), to the effect that in case the Investor fails to explain the source of investment in his hands, the Assessing Officer has to investigate/assess/re- assess the Investor, not the Investee. 3. After having recorded the finding that an amount of Rs 1,31,47,650/- pertained to AY 2012-13 and not to AY 2013- 14, the. Ld. CIT(A) erred in confirming the addition of the said amount of Rs 1,31,47,650/- in AY 2013-14. 4. On the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld. CIT(A) erred in confirming the addition of Rs 52,13,696/- under Section 68 representing share application money received from Turner International Pte Ltd. by misinterpreting the law and also misreading and misinterpreting the financial statements of the non-resident share applicant. 5. On the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld. CIT(A) erred in confirming the addition of Rs 1,28,85,956/- under Section 68 on account of sundry creditors, which addition was made by the AO on a purely arbitrary, conjectural and ad-hoc basis without pointing out any specific defects in the books of accounts of the Appellant Company. 6. On the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld. CIT(A) erred in confirming the addition of Rs 2,55,95,986/- being 10% of the total expenses debited to Profit & Loss account of the Appellant Company, which addition was made by the AO on a purely arbitrary, conjectural and ad-hoc basis and without pointing out any specific defect in the books of accounts of the Appellant Company. 7. Without prejudice to the merits of Ground No 5 and 6, the Ld. CIT(A) erred in confirming the double addition of same amounts, as some amounts included in Ground No 5 and Ground No 6 are common. 8. On the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld. CIT(A) erred in confirming the addition of Rs 46,56,280/- on account of reconciliation between entries in Form 26AS and the Profit & Loss account. 9. On the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Ld CIT(A) erred in directing the AO to deny TDS credit of Rs 35,28,350/- on account of reconciliation of entries in Form 26 AS and Profit &Loss account, instead of directing the AO to allow TDS credit in the Assessment Years in which corresponding income is assessable as per Section 199 read with Rule 37BA. 10. That the appellant craves leave to add, alter, amend or withdraw any or all the grounds of appeal on or before the date of hearing.” 6. With respect to Ground of appeal Nos. 1 to 3 raised by the assessee, ld.AR submits that the assessee has discharged the burden casted upon it u/s 68 of the Act by filing the confirmation of accounts, relevant extract of bank statements of the share applicants and the copy of share certificates issued. He further submits that the share applicant Shri Anant Jain had sufficient funds and for this he drew our attention to pages 22 and 23 of the Paper Book which are the copies of bank accounts of Anant for the period from 14.03.2008 to 01.06.2015 funds were transferred to the assessee. Ld.AR drew our attention to pages 24 to 25 which are the copies of share certificates issued the assessee. Ld. AR further submits that confirmation is placed at pages 26 to 28 of the Paper Book. It is further submitted by ld.AR that in preceding year i.e. in AY 2012-13, a sum of INR 1,31,47,650/- was received from Shri Anant Jain and as per his bank statement placed at page 22 of PB, it could be seen that same was made out of the liquidation of Five FDR of INR 26,96,833/- each received immediately before the transfer of funds tot eh assessee thus the directions of ld. CIT(A) of reopening the assessment for AY 2012-13 is devoid of any merits. Ld.AR further filed copy of Form No. 2 i.e. the return of allotment filed before the ROC and requested to delete the addition made u/s 68 of the Act towards the share application money received during the year. He further requested that the direction of ld. CIT(A) to re-open the assessment of AY 2012-13 is also bad in law as the assessee has duly explained the source of investment. He prayed accordingly. 7. On the other hand, ld. CIT DR for the Revenue vehemently supported the orders of the lower authorities and submits that ld. CIT(A) had dealt with this issue in detailed and after examining the facts of the case, has confirmed the additions. He submits that addition made deserves to be uphold. 8. Heard the contentions of both the parties at length and perused the material available on record. With respect to the investment made by Anant, it is observed that Shri Anant Jain is having 02 bank accounts, one is maintained with State Bank of India placed at page 22 and other is with CITI Bank whose Account No. 5-905045-228 placed at page 23 of Paper Book. It is observed that investment of INR 1,31,47,650/- made in FY 2011-12 was out of the SBI where maturity sum of Five FDRs of INR 26,96,833/- each were credited and out of the said funds, investment was made in the shares of assessee company. Further the assessment for AY 2012-13 has already been completed u/s 143(3) vide order dt. 27.03.20215 wherein no doubts were raised about the share capital received from Shri Anant Jain. Thus, the directions of ld. CIT(A) in reopening the assessment for AY 2012- 13 are not correct. 9. Similarly, from the perusal of statement of CITI Bank, it is observed that there was a credit entry of INR 10.00 crores out of which INR 1.35 crores were transferred to the assessee’s account. Further page 21 of the paper book is the copy of the confirmation by Shri Anant Jain dated 20.10.2017 affirming the investments made in the shares of the assessee company. These facts have not been appreciated by the lower authorities nor controverted by bringing on record any contrary material. 10. Considering the entirety of facts, we set aside the orders of the lower authorities and remand the issue back to the file of AO with the direction to examine the bank accounts of Shri Anant by obtaining the same from the bank and if it is found that sufficient funds were available in the bank account of Shri Anant Jain, no addition is required to be made. Here it is pertinent to state that the amendment in section 68 of the Act was made w.e.f. 01.04.2013 regarding examine the source of source in case of share capital and since the same is prospective in nature and applicable from 01.04.2013, thus, has no retrospective application. This view is supported by the judgment of Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of CIT Vs. Gagandeep Infrastructure (P) Ltd. reported in (2012) 394 ITR 680 (Bom.) and order of the Mumbai Bench of Tribunal in the case of M/s J.R. Fiber Glass Industries Pvt. Ltd. Vs. NFAC, in ITA No.2848/Mum/2023.therefore, the AO is directed not to examine the source of source in the hands of Shri Anant. With these directions and for making verifications of the facts, Ground of appeal Nos. 1 to 3 raised by the assessee are allowed for statistical purposes. 11. Ground of appeal No.4 raised by the assessee is with respect to the addition of INR 52,13,696/- u/s 68 towards the share application money received from Turner International Pte. Ltd. 12. Heard the contentions of both parties at length and perused the material available on record. It is observed that the AO has made the addition as the assessee was failed to justify the credits. Whereas ld. CIT(A) observed that name of the assessee was not appearing in the list of investment in the books of accounts of Turner International Pte. Ltd. and therefore, it is observed by ld. CIT(A) that this amount has already been liquidated therefore, when the investor company has not shown this amount in its balance sheet, there is no reason for treating the same as share capital and thus the addition made was confirmed. It was the claim of the assessee that the subscriber company M/s Turner International Pte limited had sufficient funds and filed copy of ‘Certificate of Foreign Inward Remittance’ issued by CITI Bank, permission of RBI for issue of shares and also the NOC of the board of directors for making investments which are placed at pages 42 to 44 of the paper book. Assessee further claimed that since the funds were received through banking channel from outside India therefore, provisions of section 68 of the Act are not applicable. 13. From the material placed before us in the shape of paper book, it is observed that during the course of assessment proceedings, assessee has filed following documents to prove the genuineness of the transactions:- [i] Certificate of Foreign Inward Remittance issued by CITI bank placed at page 43 of the Paper Book; [ii] Copy of NOC letter of the Turner International Pte. Ltd. wherein management authorized to transfer a sum of US$ 99975 to the assessee company as share application money which is equivalent to INR 52,13,696/- placed at page 44 of paper book; [iii] Page 45 of the Paper Book is the copy of approval given by Reserve Bank of India for issue of share to Turner International Pte. Ltd.; [iv] Copy of the share certificate issued to the Turner International Pte. Ltd. placed at page 46 of Paper Book; [v] Financial statements of Turner International Pte. Ltd. placed at pages 47 to 88; and [vi] Copy of Form No. 2 i.e. Return of Allotment of shares filed by the assessee company before ROC placed at pages 92 to 95 of the Paper Book. 14. All these documents duly support the contention of the assessee that it had discharged the burden casted upon it u/s 68 of the Act to prove the genuineness of the transactions and identity and creditworthiness of the share applicant. It is also matter of fact that independent Agencies CITI Bank and RBI had approved the investment made by Turner International Pte. Ltd. Once the subscriber company, Turner International Pte. Ltd. has accepted the investment made, the same cannot be added as unexplained credit merely because the said amount was not appearing in its financial statements since the same was treated as loss by the investor company. Considering the entirety of the facts, we direct the AO to delete the addition, more particularly looking to the fact that the funds were received from non-resident entity through banking channel and after due permission of the RBI. Thus, Ground of appeal No. 4 raised by the assessee is allowed. 15. Ground of appeal No. 5 raised by the assessee is with respect to the addition of INR 1,28,85,956/- made u/s 68 of the Act being 20% of the outstanding creditors and Ground of appeal No.6 of the assessee is with respect to the disallowance of 10% of the expenses claimed at INR 2,55,95,986/- on adhoc basis. 16. Heard the contentions of both the parties at length and perused the material available on record. It is observed that AO has made the addition of INR 1,28,85,956/- u/s 68 of the Act being 20% of the total Sundry creditors appearing in the Balance Sheet amounting to INR 6,86,95,615/- by observing that the creditors had increased from INR 42,65,822/- to INR 6,86,95,615/- which is abnormally high and the assessee has failed to confirm the same and also failed to file other evidences to substantiate its claim of having receipts of such credits during the year. As per the lower authorities, the assessee has only filed the name of the creditors, nature of expenses and amounts outstanding. The AO has also doubted the expenses incurred of INR 25,59,59,858/- incurred on programme management of INR 2,37,29,223/-, employees benefit expenses of INR 1,46,55,644/-, depreciation and amortization of INR 35,00,449/- and other expenses of INR 8,21,74,542/-. The AO has made the disallowance @ 10% out of such expenses by holding that these expenses were not verifiable as the assessee had not filed complete details of such expenses. 17. With respect to the sundry creditors, it is an admitted fact that the assessee has filed the name of the parties, party-wise nature of services taken and amount outstanding which fact has been accepted by ld. CIT(A) also in its order vide para 6.2 of the order. The sole basis for making the additions was that the assessee had not filed any confirmations or ledger accounts, bank statements etc. to prove the genuineness of transactions. At the same time, the corresponding expenses incurred which were part of the outstanding balances were also disallowed. Necessary copies of the trade payable outstanding at the end of the previous year are placed at pages 96 to 97 of the Paper Book and the list of other expenses is placed at pages 104 to 110 of the Paper Book. It is further observed that the parties whose name are appearing in the list of creditors for goods and others are also appearing in the list of expenses claimed as service provider. The AO as well as ld. CIT(A) has failed to appreciate the facts that the parties from whom services were received, their outstanding balances were doubted and 20% of the same was added. At the same time, corresponding expenses claimed by the assessee towards services rendered by them were also disallowed by holding that assessee has failed to substantiate these expenses, which is nothing but double addition. Accordingly, we direct the AO to delete the addition of INR 1,28,85,956/- being 20% of the outstanding Sundry Creditors. 18. With respect to the disallowance of INR 2,55,95,986/- made @ 10% out of the expenses claimed, it is observed that the assessee has not been able to justify such expenses being incurred wholly and exclusively for the purpose of business and under business expediency and thus the same cannot be allowed as claimed u/s 37(1) of the Act. It is settled law that the burden is on the assessee to prove the genuineness of expenses incurred and to further prove that the same were incurred wholly and exclusively for the purpose and for the furtherance of business which the assessee has failed to establish. Thus, considering the entirety of facts, we are of the considered opinion that expenses claimed could not be fully allowed however, the disallowance made by the AO seems to be on higher side thus the same is reduced to 5% which will cover the possible leakage of revenue and further the doubts raised by the AO with respect to the outstanding liability of corresponding payable. Thus, Grounds of appeal No. 5 and 6 of the assessee are partly allowed. 19. Ground of appeal No.7 is already answered while deciding Ground of appeal Nos. 5 & 6 in above-mentioned paras thus, required no separate adjudication. 20. Ground of appeal No.8 of the assessee is with respect to the confirmation of 46,56,280/- out of the total addition of INR 4,89,20,490/- being the mis-match between receipts as per 26AS statement and receipt recorded in the books of accounts. 21. Heard the contentions of both the parties and perused the material available on record. The assessee claimed that this amount represents the advance receipts from the customers which were duly recorded in the books of accounts and offered for tax in subsequent years when the services were provided. Since the TDS was deducted at the time of payment, the same appears in 26AS statements. However, ld. CIT(A) has partly accepted the contention and partly deleted the addition. The assessee was providing management services where TDS is to be made on every payment irrespective of the fact whether the same is advance payment or payment against the regular bill. It is further observed that assessee claimed that the amount of INR 46,56,280/- was reduced from the expenses claimed in Profit & Loss Account thus the same cannot be added again to the total income. However, this fact has not been accepted by the AO. 22. Once the assessee has claimed that it has reduced the amount of INR 46,56,280/- from the expenses claimed in the Profit & Loss Account and therefore, income by the assessee includes such amount however in absence of the precise details of such deduction, verification of claim of the assessee is not possible at this stage. Therefore, for the purpose of verification of the claim of the assessee, we restored this issue to the file of AO for making verification whether the assessee has reduced the expenses with receipts to the extent of INR 46,56,280/- and if it is found correct, no further addition is required to be made. With these directions, Ground of appeal No.8 raised by the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes. 23. Ground of appeal No.9 of the assessee is with respect to the denial of TDS credit of INR 35,28,350/- on account of reconciliation of entries in Form 26AS and as per Profit & Loss Account. 24. Heard the contentions of both the parties at length and perused the material available on record. It is observed that this amount was deducted from the advances received during the year under appeal and corresponding income was offered for tax in subsequent years. It is further observed that Ld. CIT(A) had denied the credit of such TDS even in subsequent years. Once the TDS was deducted out of the payments made to the assessee and the corresponding income was assessed in subsequent years int eh hands of the assessee, credit of the TDS should be allowed int eh year when the income was offered for tax. Accordingly, this issue is restored to the file of AO with the directions to allow the credit of TDS in the year when the corresponding income was offered for tax. With these directions, Ground of appeal No.9 raised by the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes. 25. Ground of appeal No.10 of the assessee is general in nature hence, not adjudicated. 26. In the result, appeal of the assessee is partly allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 18.11.2026.
