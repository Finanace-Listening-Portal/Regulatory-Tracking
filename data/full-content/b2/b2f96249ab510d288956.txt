Summary: ICICI Securities Limited is the legal stock-broking entity behind the ICICIdirect currency-derivatives service covered here. The current NSE member record identifies member code 07730 and SEBI registration INZ000183631, and shows the Currency Futures trading-member segment enabled as of 21 October 2026. ICICIdirect’s current product page states that resident Indians eligible under FEMA can access exchange-traded futures and options in USD/INR, EUR/INR, GBP/INR and JPY/INR, with brokerage of ₹20 per executed order. These contracts are cash-settled exchange products; they are not delivery of travel currency, an overseas leveraged spot account or a contract for difference. RBI’s framework requires an INR-involving exchange-traded currency derivative to hedge foreign-exchange exposure. The documentary dispensation up to the combined USD 100 million notional limit does not remove the need for a valid underlying contracted exposure. Trading also requires KYC, a broker account, margin and compliance with exchange position limits and contract specifications. Total cost includes brokerage, exchange and regulatory charges, GST, stamp duty and margin-funding consequences where applicable. This profile explains eligibility, onboarding, execution, settlement, complaints and Indian tax treatment without recommending ICICI Securities or any currency position. A customer does not choose an arbitrary notional, strike or maturity outside the listed contracts. Contract availability can change, so the live exchange contract file and broker order window control. Cash Settlement Is Not Foreign-Currency Delivery RBI’s directions state that exchange-traded foreign-exchange derivative contracts are cash-settled in Indian rupees. Profit or loss is determined from contract and settlement prices; the customer does not receive dollars, euros, pounds or yen for travel or remittance. A person needing actual foreign currency must use an authorised dealer or money changer for a permitted underlying transaction, with separate KYC, purpose and remittance rules. A Future and an Option Create Different Obligations A futures position is marked to market and can create daily cash debits or credits. An option buyer pays premium and holds a contractual right under the listed specification; an option seller receives premium but faces margin and potentially substantial adverse settlement exposure. Broker risk controls can require extra margin, reject orders or close positions. The exchange clearing framework reduces but does not remove market, liquidity, operational and broker-client risks. Resident Eligibility Does Not Remove the RBI Exposure Requirement ICICIdirect states that resident Indians eligible under FEMA may participate. RBI’s April 2024 clarification reiterates that INR-involving exchange-traded currency derivatives are permitted only for hedging exposure to foreign-exchange risk. The January 2024 directions allow positions up to USD 100 million equivalent in notional value, outstanding across all exchanges, without producing underlying documents at entry. However, the user must still have a valid underlying contracted exposure not hedged by another derivative and must be able to establish it if required. Documentation Relief Is Not Permission to Speculate The combined limit is an operational documentation dispensation, not a licence for a resident to take a directional INR position without exposure. An importer, exporter, borrower, traveller or other eligible user should identify the contracted cash flow, currency, amount and timing, then ensure the derivative does not exceed or duplicate the hedge. The broker or exchange can call for evidence. False declarations can have FEMA, contractual and account consequences. Non-INR Contracts and Overseas Platforms Need Separate Analysis This profile establishes only the current domestic exchange service described by ICICIdirect. It does not establish that ICICI Securities offers cross-currency contracts not shown on the page or that an Indian resident may trade any overseas spot, margin, CFD or derivative venue. Foreign FCA, ASIC or CySEC regulation and absence from RBI’s Alert List do not establish Indian eligibility. LRS should not be represented as permitting overseas margin or margin-call remittances. KYC, Segment Activation and Margin Precede the First Order A prospective client must complete the broker’s KYC and account-opening process, supply PAN, identity and address evidence, bank and contact details, beneficial-owner information where relevant, and complete current risk and nomination disclosures. Existing equity access does not necessarily mean that the currency segment is active. The client may need to accept derivative risk disclosures, provide income or financial evidence under current policy, and complete segment activation before placing an order. Order Entry Requires Contract-Level Checks The customer selects exchange, pair, futures or option contract, expiry, strike where applicable, buy or sell side, quantity, order type and price. The screen should be checked for lot size, tick size, available margin, price bands, market depth and estimated charges. A market order can fill at multiple prices in a thin contract. A rejected order creates no hedge. A partial fill can leave the exposure under-hedged or differently sized from the original intention. Margins Can Rise Before Expiry Initial, exposure, extreme-loss and additional margins can change with exchange risk parameters and volatility. Futures are marked to market. Short options can attract substantial margin. A broker can impose margins above exchange minimums and can square off positions under its risk policy, but it does not guarantee that square-off will occur before losses exceed available funds. Pledged securities used as collateral introduce valuation haircuts, pledge mechanics and potential interest or funding charges. That is not the all-in cost. The contract note can also include exchange transaction charges, SEBI turnover fees, GST on taxable brokerage and specified services, stamp duty and other permitted levies. Currency derivatives are not ordinarily subject to Securities Transaction Tax, but a client should verify the current contract note and law rather than reuse an equity-derivative cost template. Collateral and Delayed Funding Can Add Charges The broker’s current page discloses interest of 0.027% per day plus GST for a specified shares-as-margin facility. Whether it applies depends on the selected facility, collateral composition and ledger. That rate should not be treated as a universal currency-account charge. A client should obtain the current tariff, brokerage plan, risk-policy schedule and sample contract note, and distinguish brokerage from exchange levies, taxes, interest and bank costs. A Hedge Can Still Lose Money A derivative loss can be economically offset by a favourable move in the underlying exposure, but the timing, amount and basis may not match. Futures create daily cash flows while an invoice may settle later. Options require premium and may expire unused. Closing a hedge early, choosing a different maturity or hedging only part of the exposure creates basis and rollover risk. Transaction cost should be measured across both the derivative and the underlying commercial cash flow. Settlement, Withdrawal and Position Closure Follow Exchange Rules Exchange-traded contracts use clearing-corporation settlement. Futures positions are marked to market daily; option premium and exercise or expiry consequences follow the contract specification. The final settlement price and expiry process are not selected by the broker. A customer can close a position by entering an opposite trade in the same contract before expiry, subject to liquidity and risk controls, but a cancellation of an order is different from reversal of an already executed trade. Available cash after settlement can be withdrawn under the broker’s payout process, subject to outstanding obligations, margins, bank validation and cut-off times. A profitable screen position is not necessarily withdrawable cash before settlement. Incorrect bank details, an account freeze, pledge obligations, surveillance, compliance review or a debit in another segment can delay payout under the applicable agreement. Refund language is generally relevant to excess ledger balance or failed payment, not to reversal of a valid market loss. Grievances Escalate from the Broker to Exchange and SEBI ICICIdirect’s current customer-care page lists its helpdesk channels. A currency complaint should identify ICICI Securities Limited, the UCC, exchange, contract, order and trade numbers, date and time, disputed price or margin entry, contract note and requested remedy. The customer should first use the broker’s grievance and compliance escalation route. A phone conversation should be followed by written evidence. If unresolved, the investor can use the exchange investor-service mechanism and SEBI’s SCORES platform, subject to their current procedures and limitation rules. Disputes concerning exchange trades can also proceed through the prescribed online dispute resolution or arbitration framework. A grievance process can examine execution, disclosure or ledger issues; it does not compensate a normal market loss merely because the trade was unprofitable. Indian Tax Treatment Depends on Facts and Record Quality Exchange-traded currency derivative profit or loss is generally analysed under business-income provisions because eligible exchange-traded derivatives are excluded from the speculative-transaction definition subject to statutory conditions. That label does not answer every case. Frequency, intent, books, other activity and the relationship to a business hedge can affect classification, expense deductibility, audit requirements and loss treatment. An individual hedging a personal exposure can present different facts from an active derivatives business. Turnover Is Not the Contract Notional For tax-audit analysis, derivative turnover is computed under applicable tax guidance from realised differences and option premium or reverse-trade components, not by adding the full currency notional. The correct method should be applied consistently to broker statements and books. Gross contract value, margin deposited and tax turnover are different figures. GST charged by the broker is a service tax item and not the customer’s income-tax rate. A Commercial Hedge Must Link to the Underlying Exposure A business claiming hedge treatment should preserve the exposure, board or risk policy where relevant, hedge designation, contract size, maturity, accounting entries and settlement link. Over-hedging, duplicate hedges or a derivative retained after the exposure disappears can weaken the connection. Companies subject to applicable accounting standards may also need fair-value, hedge-accounting and disclosure analysis separate from the income-tax return. Limits That Must Remain Visible The verified service is domestic exchange-traded currency futures and options, not offshore leveraged spot forex or CFDs. Resident access under FEMA does not displace the requirement for a valid underlying contracted foreign-exchange exposure in INR contracts. The USD 100 million combined notional threshold is documentary relief, not permission to trade without exposure. ₹20 per executed order is brokerage, not the entire cost shown on the contract note or ledger. Margins, exchange limits, broker risk controls and contract availability can change before a position is closed. SEBI registration and NSE membership do not guarantee profit, liquidity, broker solvency or the outcome of a complaint. Frequently Asked Questions 1.
