Articles 1 min read India’s forex reserves fall $1.2 billion to $719.74 billion as of October 24 India’s foreign exchange reserves fell by $1.2 billion to $719.74 billion in the week ended October 24, according to the RBI. The decline followed a sharper $5 billion drop in the previous week, when reserves stood at $721.02 billion, indicating continued pressure on the country's foreign exchange kitty. However, the country's forex reserves have been on a declining trajectory since then. Advt Market experts have attributed the sustained drawdown partly to the deployment of funds raised through Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) or FCNR(B) deposits, as well as the RBI's interventions in the foreign exchange market to support the rupee through dollar sales.For the week ended October 24, foreign currency assets, which account for a major share of India's forex reserves, increased by $1.3 billion to $593.20 billion, according to RBI data.Foreign currency assets are expressed in dollar terms and reflect not only movements in major currencies but also changes in the value of other currencies, including the euro, pound and yen, held in the reserves.The value of gold reserves, meanwhile, declined by $2.6 billion to $103.15 billion during the week.Special Drawing Rights (SDRs), the reserve assets created by the International Monetary Fund (IMF), increased by $27 million to $18.605 billion, the RBI data showed.India's reserve position with the IMF, another component of its foreign exchange reserves, fell by $4 million to $4.787 billion during the reporting week.
