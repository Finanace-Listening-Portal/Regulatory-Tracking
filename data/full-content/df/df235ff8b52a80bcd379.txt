Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Dhaval Muktibhai Shah Vs ITO (ITAT Rajkot) ₹4 Lakh Political Donation, Section 148 Notice After Three Years — Rajkot ITAT Holds Reopening Time-Barred Summary: The Rajkot Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal has allowed an assessee’s appeal against reassessment arising from an alleged bogus political donation of ₹4 lakh. The notice under Section 148 for AY 2019–20 was issued after the three-year period had expired. Since the alleged escaped income was only ₹4 lakh, the Revenue could not invoke the extended period under Section 149(1)(b), which required escaped income of ₹50 lakh or more under the law applicable to the notice. How the ₹4 Lakh Political Donation Came Under Scrutiny The assessee filed his original return on 21 June 2019, declaring taxable income of ₹11,46,960. His Chapter VI-A deductions totalled ₹5,75,000, including ₹4 lakh contributed to Apna Desh Party. The grounds of appeal identify the disputed deduction as one claimed under Section 80GGC of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Department conducted searches in February 2021 and September 2022 covering several registered unrecognised political parties and associated entities. According to the Department, donations received through banking channels were routed through entities under the guise of purchases and returned to donors in cash after retaining a commission of 3% to 5%. The Department relied on material gathered during the investigation and statements said to identify the assessee as a beneficiary. The Assessing Officer passed an order under Section 148A(d) and issued a notice under Section 148 on 11 April 2023. The assessee maintained his claim in the return filed in response. The Assessing Officer disallowed the entire ₹4 lakh in the reassessment, and the CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, upheld the disallowance. Why the Date of the Section 148 Notice Was Decisive Before the Tribunal, the assessee pointed out that AY 2019–20 ended on 31 March 2020. The ordinary three-year period under Section 149(1)(a) therefore expired on 31 March 2023. The notice dated 11 April 2023 fell outside that period. Under the version of Section 149 applicable to the notice, reopening after three years depended on the Assessing Officer possessing books of account, documents or evidence revealing escaped income, represented in one of the prescribed forms, which amounted to or was likely to amount to ₹50 lakh or more. In this case, the Tribunal recorded that the amount stated in the reasons for reopening was ₹4 lakh. The Revenue reiterated the Assessing Officer’s stand, and the Tribunal held that the amount fell below the threshold for invoking the extended period. Gujarat High Court Precedent Applied The Tribunal followed the jurisdictional Gujarat High Court’s judgment in Bipinkumar Girdharlal Parekh v. Office of the Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax, Special Civil Application No. 15105 of 2025, decided on 15 June 2026. The High Court considered Section 152(3) and held that the transitional provision could also apply to a person who was not searched where the reassessment arose from incriminating material found in a qualifying third-party search. It quashed the notice in that case because it was issued beyond three years and the alleged escapement was below ₹50 lakh. Applying the limitation principle, the Rajkot ITAT held that the Assessing Officer could not initiate reassessment for the stated escapement of ₹4 lakh after the three-year period. The Tribunal allowed the appeal on limitation, leaving the genuineness of the political contribution undecided. Author’s Comments The significant fact is that ₹4 lakh was the amount recorded for reopening itself. This was a case where the jurisdictional shortfall was apparent from the reasons for reopening. The Tribunal therefore had no occasion to consider a different situation where the evidence available at the initiation stage indicated likely escapement of ₹50 lakh or more, but the amount ultimately assessed was lower. In cases arising from a common investigation, the scale of the alleged arrangement may explain why the Department examined several donors. For limitation, however, the relevant exercise is to identify the escapement attributed to the assessee, read the notice and the underlying reasons, and apply the version of Section 149 governing that notice, including any applicable statutory exclusion. Here, the Tribunal found that the notice was outside the ordinary period and the recorded escapement fell short of the statutory threshold. Cases Discussed Bipinkumar Girdharlal Parekh Vs Office of the Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax (Gujarat High Court); Special Civil Application No. 15105 of 2025; date recorded in the ITAT order: 15/06/2025; AY: 2021-22. The jurisdictional precedent relied upon and extensively reproduced by the Tribunal. The High Court addressed Section 152(3), third-party search material and the Section 149 limitation framework. The original order records 15/06/2025, while the supplied editorial text states 15/06/2026; the correct High Court judgment date requires independent verification. 5. I have heard both the parties and carefully gone through the submission put forth on behalf of the assessee along with the documents furnished and the case laws relied upon, and perused the fact of the case including the findings of the ld CIT(A) and other materials brought on record. Learned Counsel for the assessee submitted the reassessment u/s 147/148 is bad in law. The Ld. Counsel took me through paper-book page no. 12 where the notice u/s. 147/148 of the Act is placed and in the assessee’s case under consideration, the assessment year involved, is the assessment year 2019-20, and three years elapsed on dated 31.03.2023, however, notice u/s 148 was issued after the period of three years on dated 11.04.2023, wherein the income was escaped form assessment only Rs.4,00,000/-, which is below Rs.50,00,000/-. If the reassessment is to be reopened after three years, then the income escaped from the assessment should be Rs.50,00,000/- or more. However, in the assessee’s case income escaped from the assessment is only Rs.4,00,000/- , therefore, the Ld.Counsel submitted that the reassessment proceedings initiated against the assessee is not in accordance with the provision of the Act. Hence, the Ld. Counsel relied on the judgement of the Jurisdictional High Court in the case of Bipinkumar Girdharlal Parekh, Civil Application No.15105/2025 order dated 15.06.2025, and stated that on similar facts, the reassessment proceedings was quashed by the Hon’ble High Court. 6. On the other hand, the Ld. DR for the Revenue has primarily reiterated the stand taken by the Assessing Officer, which I have already noted in my earlier para and is not being repeated for the sake of brevity. 7. I have heard both the parties and perused the material available on record. I find that the issue under consideration is covered in favour of the assessee, by the judgement of the Jurisdictional High Court of Gujarat in the case of Bipinkumar Girdharlal Parekh, vide Civil Application No.15105/2025 order dated 15.06.2025. I note that in the assessee’s case under consideration the assessment year is 2019-20 where three years elapsed on 31.03.2023, however, in the assessee’s case, the reassessment proceedings were initiated by issuing notice u/s 148 on 11.04.2023, which is clearly after three years, and income escaped from assessment is only Rs.4,00,000/-. Hence, the issue is covered in favour of the assessee by the judgement of of the Jurisdictional High Court of Gujarat in the case of Bipinkumar Girdharlal Parekh(supra), wherein it was held as follows: “9. Keeping in mind the aforesaid dates, we may now incorporate the provision of Section 152(3) of the Act, which reads as under: “Section 152(3) Where a search has been initiated under section 132 or requisition is made under section 132A or a survey is conducted under section 133A [other than under sub-section (2A)] on or after the 1st day of April, 2021 but before the 1st day of September, 2024, the provisions of section 147 to 151 shall apply as they stood immediately before the commencement of the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024.” Thus, since the date of search falls within the period from the 01.04.2021 to the 01.09.2024, the provision of Sections 147 to 151 of the Act, as they stood prior to the Finance Act (No. 2), 2024, shall apply. 10. Section 149 (Old regime) of the Act reads thus: “Time limit for notice. 149 . (1) No notice under section 148 shall be issued for the relevant assessment year, – (a) if three years have elapsed from the end of the relevant assessment year, unless the case falls under clause (b); (b) if three years, but not more than ten years, have elapsed from the end of the relevant assessment year unless the Assessing Officer has in his possession books of account or other documents or evidence which reveal that the income chargeable to tax, represented in the form of- (i) an asset, (ii) expenditure in respect of a transaction or in relation to an event or occasion, or (iii) an entry or entries in the books of account, which has escaped assessment amounts to or is likely to amount to fifty lakh rupees or more:] Provided that no notice under section 148 shall be issued at any time in a case for the relevant assessment year beginning on or before 1st day of April, 2021, if a notice under section 148 or section 153A or section 153C could not have been issued at that time on account of being beyond the time limit specified under the provisions of clause (b) of sub-section (1) of this section or section 153A or section 153C, as the case may be, as they stood immediately before the commencement of the Finance Act, 2021: Provided further that the provisions of this sub-section shall not apply in a case, where a notice under Section 153-A, or Section 153-C read with Section 153-A, is required to be issued in relation to a search initiated under Section 132 or books of account, other documents or any assets requisitioned under Section 132-A, on or before the 31st day of March,2021.” 11. In the present case, the provision of Section 149(1)(a) of the Act, which prescribes a time limit of three years, will get attracted, as the alleged escapement of income is below the amount of Rs.50 lakh, which finds place in the provision of Section 149(1)(b) of the Act. The relevant assessment year in the present case is A.Y. 2021-22, and hence, as per the provision of Section 149(1)(a) of the Act, the end of the relevant assessment year would be 31.03.2022. However, the impugned notice under Section 148 of the Act has been issued on 23.05.2025 for A.Y. 2021-22, which is beyond the limitation of three years, and hence, the reopening of the assessment runs contrary to the provision of Section 149 of the Finance (No. 1) Act, 2024, and hence is liable to be interfered with. 12. The contention raised before us by the Revenue, to the extent that the Finance (No. 2) Act of 2024 will apply in the present case, as there is no search conducted against the petitioner, is misconceived, since the reopening of the assessment against the petitioner is exclusively premised upon the incriminating materials found during the search under Section 132 of the Act. The provisions of Section 152(3) of the Act are not limited for undertaking the reassessment against searched person only, as projected before us by the Revenue, and not against other person, who is not subjected to search. The provision of Section 152(3) of the Act is applicable to all the assessee, where the reassessment proceedings are initiated, ‘on the basis’ or ‘as a consequence’ of a search conducted between 01.04.2021 and 01.09.2025, and against whom incriminating material is found. However, the re-opening of the assessment is subject to limitations as provided under Section 149 of the Act. As mentioned herein-above the provision of Section 132 of the Act finds place in the provision of Section 149 of the Act and hence, the reassessment, since it emanates on the incriminating material found during the search at M/s. DCW Ltd. group, connecting the present petitioner with such material, the provision of Section 149 of the Finance (No.1) Act prescribing limitation gets attracted. Thus, on this sole ground, the writ petition succeeds, and the impugned reopening of the assessment order dated 19.05.2025 and the impugned notice dated 23.05.2025 are hereby quashed and set aside. The writ petition stands allowed.” 8. Therefore, I note that notice u/s 148 of the Act issued by the assessing officer is dated 11.04.2023, which is beyond three years. As in the assessee’s case under consideration, the assessment year involved, is the assessment year 2019-20, and three years elapsed on 31.03.2023 and to reopen assessment after three years, the income escaped from the assessment should be Rs.50,00,000/- or more, however, in the assessee’s case under consideration the income escaped from the assessment, which is mentioned in the reasons recorded, is Rs.4,00,000/-, therefore reassessment proceedings cannot be initiated by the assessing officer, after period of three years and hence respectfully following the binding precedent by the Jurisdictional Hon’ble High Court of Gujarat in the case of Bipinkumar Girdharlal Parekh, Civil Application No.15105/2025 order dated 15.06.2025 (supra), wherein it has been clearly held that the provision of Section 149(1)(a) of the Act, which prescribes a time limit of three years, will get attracted, as the alleged escapement of income is below the amount of Rs.50,00,000/-, however, if the income is above Rs.50,00,000/- then beyond the period of three years, the re-assessment proceedings may be initiated, therefore, respectfully following binding precedent of the Jurisdictional High Court Gujarat in the case of Bipinkumar Girdharlal Parekh (supra), I allow the appeal of the assessee. 9. In the result, appeal filed by the assessee is allowed. Order is pronounced in the open Court on 17/11/2026.
