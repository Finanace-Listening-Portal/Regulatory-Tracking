The central bank issued an advisory on November 16 focusing on API security and governance, particularly API-based fund transfers. The regulator has also been communicating its expectations to lenders through regular advisories and discussions, extending its engagement to NBFCs. "There have been discussions, and banks have been advised to remain cautious. These advisories and discussions are happening regularly, almost on a weekly basis. The RBI has been communicating what banks should be doing and what it expects regulated entities to implement. The regulator is also reaching out to NBFCs,” said a senior banker at a private-sector bank. A key concern is the exploitation of vulnerabilities in third-party systems connected to banks and NBFCs. In some instances, fraudsters have compromised the systems of financial entities to divert money through banking channels. In other cases, beneficiary account details in loan disbursement systems have been manipulated, resulting in funds being transferred to accounts controlled by fraudsters. These are essentially supply-chain attacks, in which a weakness in one participant’s systems can expose other entities connected to it. The safeguards being emphasised include transaction velocity checks, rate limits on API requests, whitelisting and encryption, pre-validation of beneficiary accounts, and more frequent reconciliation of transactions. Lenders also need to periodically rotate API authentication keys and introduce additional checks when transactions appear abnormal. Banks have been asked to review the security arrangements of their counterparties as well. The RBI’s advisories have covered different aspects of the problem, including loan disbursements and direct APIs used for fund transfers. Banks had initially been asked to re-examine existing controls, including limits on the number of fund transfer requests that can be initiated through APIs within a specified period, regular monitoring of transaction volumes, and the use of throttling mechanisms wherever necessary. Beyond API security, the regulator is also focusing on third-party risk management, data protection and incident response. Lenders are expected to proactively assess their exposure to cyber risks rather than limit their response to reporting incidents after they occur. “The focus is on the preparedness of institutions, particularly banks, to ensure that they are not merely responding to a cyber incident after it occurs. They should proactively assess their own exposure, protect the interests of customers and other stakeholders, and maintain the resilience of the banking ecosystem,” said another senior banker. Regulated entities are currently required to report certain cyber incidents to the RBI within six hours. However, lenders are also expected to strengthen internal response mechanisms, including senior-level monitoring, root cause analysis, corrective action, and forensic investigations, wherever necessary.
