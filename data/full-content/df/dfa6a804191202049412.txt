SBI Research says banter to use the rupee as a shock absorber were unnecessary, as depreciation beyond a point amplified rather than absorbed shocks. From May 2014 to February 2025, the movements were nearly equivalent.From February 2025 to September 2026, both currencies depreciated, but the rupee fell much more sharply. SBI Research says allowing continuous depreciation as a shock absorber was unnecessary and destabilising.Since September 2026, both currencies have appreciated, but the rupee has gained much more than the dollar.This difference can become significant when two candidates with comparable capabilities approach the same roleThe banter that the rupee should be allowed to depreciate continuously to act as a shock absorber, even past 100 against the dollar, was entirely unnecessary and created financial instability during February 2025-September 2026, according to an SBI Research note. The report says currency depreciation in the Indian context acts as a shock amplifier beyond a point.The rupee's movement against the dollar has gone through four distinct phases since 2008, the note said. The latest phase, which began in September 2026, has seen both the rupee and the dollar appreciate. The rupee's gains have been much stronger, making this a welcome development, it said. Advt The earlier phase, from February 2025 to September 2026, saw both currencies depreciate. But the rupee fell much more sharply than the dollar. SBI Research says the banter that the rupee should be allowed to weaken continuously to absorb external shocks was unnecessary and contributed to financial instability.The note also points to a debate recently revived by the Bank for International Settlements (BIS): whether exchange-rate movements help absorb shocks or amplify them through inflation, balance-sheet risks and overall risk sentiment. SBI Research's view is that, in India's case, rupee depreciation amplifies shocks beyond a point.The rupee phasesThe report traces the first phase of rupee movement from January 2008 to May 2014. During this period, the rupee depreciated more than the dollar appreciated, indicating weak macroeconomic fundamentals.In the second phase, from May 2014 to February 2025, rupee depreciation was nearly equivalent to dollar appreciation, amid strong macroeconomic fundamentals.The third phase, from February 2025 to September 2026, saw both currencies depreciate, but the rupee's decline far outpaced the dollar's.The fourth phase began in September 2026. Both currencies have appreciated since then, but the rupee's appreciation has been considerably stronger than the dollar's.
