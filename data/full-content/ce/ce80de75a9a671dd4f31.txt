The review comes at a sensitive moment for HDFC Bank, which has a market value of about $115 billion and has spent three decades cultivating a reputation for conservative management, strong internal controls and consistent profitability. The lender is preparing for its first outsider chief executive following boardroom disputes and management departures. Its share price has declined about 28% this year. The probe and its findings were reported to senior management, including some members of the board, earlier this year, the people said. Both executives have since been formally informed of the findings, and it wasn’t immediately clear what disciplinary action will be taken, the people said. The findings add to the challenges confronting Anup Bagchi, a veteran of rival ICICI Group, who is set to become HDFC Bank’s chief executive on Oct. 27. In recent months, internal divisions in HDFC have played out publicly. In March, then-chairman Atanu Chakraborty resigned abruptly, citing differences between his personal values and certain practices at the bank. An independent legal review commissioned by the lender subsequently said his concerns were not substantiated. Bagchi’s appointment follows the surprise decision of Sashidhar Jagdishan, the incumbent chief executive, not to seek another term. Jagdishan is stepping down after six years at the helm. Kaizad Bharucha, deputy managing director and one of the bank’s longest-serving executives, has also asked to leave at the end of December.
