Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Sunland Metal Recycling Industries Vs Commissioner of Customs, Kandla (CESTAT Ahmedabad) Summary: The Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), Ahmedabad, in Sunland Metal Recycling Industries Vs Commissioner of Customs, Kandla, set aside customs duty demands, confiscation of imported goods and penalties arising from allegations of misdeclaration and undervaluation of zinc ash, zinc dross, zinc scrap and aluminium scrap imported during 2002–2006. The appeals were filed by Sunland Metal Recycling Industries (SMRI), engaged in manufacturing aluminium, zinc, brass and copper ingots, and its partners Sushil Kumar Agarwal, Surendra P. Kachhara and Sanjeev Kumar Agarwal. The dispute arose from a show-cause notice dated 7 May 2007, followed by an adjudication order dated 26 March 2019. The Customs Department alleged that imported metal scrap had been declared at artificially reduced prices and that its assessable value should be enhanced by reference to London Metal Exchange (LME) prices of virgin metals, adjusted according to prescribed discount bands. The Tribunal examined whether such valuation methodology was permissible under the Customs Valuation Rules, whether the allegations were supported by reliable evidence, and whether the Department could reopen or demand differential duty on consignments already provisionally assessed or finally assessed. The Department’s principal allegation concerning zinc imports was that 32 consignments supplied by U.S. Zinc, USA, had been misdeclared as zinc ash although the goods were allegedly zinc skimmings containing a higher percentage of recoverable zinc. The Department relied on correspondence obtained through the Indian Consulate in the United States, emails exchanged between suppliers and an indenting agent, and statements recorded during investigation. It alleged that the declared invoices did not reflect the actual transaction prices and that differential payments had been made through cash advances, hawala channels or overseas transfers. In respect of other zinc imports, the Department proposed valuation by reference to estimated recoverable metal content, including 35% of LME prices for zinc ash and 75% for zinc dross. Similar allegations were made concerning aluminium scrap imports, where the Department relied on Alert Circular No. 14/2005 dated 16 December 2005 issued by the Directorate General of Valuation. The circular prescribed discount bands for different grades of aluminium scrap relative to LME prices. On that basis, the Department proposed differential duty across multiple consignments imported through Nhava Sheva, Kandla, Mundra and Tuglaqabad, together with confiscation and penalties. The appellants challenged the allegations of misdeclaration by relying on chemical examination reports prepared at the time of importation. They submitted that the imported zinc ash had been tested by the Customs Chemical Examiner at Nhava Sheva and found to conform to the declared description. The Department, however, relied on a subsequent test report relating to material sampled from the appellant’s factory, which described the material as other than zinc ash without positively identifying it as zinc skimmings. The appellants argued that an inconclusive examination of material subsequently found at the factory could not override the contemporaneous examination of imported consignments. They also disputed the reliance placed on third-party correspondence and requested cross-examination of the chemical examiner. The Tribunal accepted the significance of the original import-stage test reports and observed that the subsequent report did not establish that the goods were zinc skimmings. Since the imported goods had been tested and cleared as zinc ash, and the contrary allegation was not satisfactorily established, the Tribunal held that the charges of misdeclaration and consequential undervaluation were unsustainable. A separate issue concerned the legality of demands raised under Section 28 of the Customs Act, 1962, in respect of provisionally assessed consignments. The appellants submitted that the zinc ash and zinc dross consignments covered by the relevant annexures to the show-cause notice had been provisionally assessed pending laboratory testing. They produced Bills of Entry containing references to test bonds and electronic assessment records demonstrating the provisional nature of the assessments. Although the adjudicating authority acknowledged the legal distinction between provisional assessment and recovery proceedings under Section 28, it rejected the appellants’ contention on the ground that the relevant provisional assessments had not been adequately identified or supported by evidence of executed bonds. The Tribunal found this reasoning factually incorrect. It noted that the appellants had specifically identified the relevant consignments in their reply and produced documents indicating provisional assessment. Consequently, the Tribunal held that the demands raised in the manner adopted by the Department could not be sustained against those provisionally assessed imports. On the central issue of customs valuation, the Tribunal rejected the Department’s practice of determining the value of imported aluminium scrap by taking LME prices of virgin metal and deducting standard discount percentages. It observed that the Customs Valuation Rules required the assessing authority to follow the prescribed valuation methods sequentially after establishing legally sustainable grounds for rejecting the declared transaction value. The Department could not directly resort to a residual valuation method merely because LME prices suggested a higher value. The appellants had produced details of contemporaneous imports of identical or comparable scrap at prices similar to their declared values. However, the adjudicating authority had not adequately considered those transactions and had instead relied on LME-based calculations. The Tribunal considered this approach contrary to the statutory valuation framework. It emphasised that contemporaneous import values, where available and relevant, constituted an important basis for valuation and could not be disregarded in favour of a generalised formula derived from the price of virgin metal. The Tribunal relied on several judicial precedents rejecting the use of LME quotations as the sole basis for enhancing the assessable value of imported scrap. In Commissioner of Customs Vs Prabhu Dayal Prem Chand, the Supreme Court had upheld rejection of an enhancement based on LME prices where the Department had not produced supporting evidence of contemporaneous imports. The Tribunal also considered GKN Sinter Metals Limited Vs Commissioner of Central Excise, Bharati Rubber Lining & Allied Services Pvt. Ltd. and Pushpak Metal Corporation, which supported the proposition that indicative international metal prices could not automatically replace the transaction value of scrap. It further referred to the Department’s own communications recognising that aluminium scrap prices did not necessarily move proportionately with virgin aluminium prices. The Institute of Scrap Recycling Industries had also explained that scrap prices depended on commercial negotiations, market information, quality and other factors, rather than a direct quotation on a metal exchange. The Tribunal therefore concluded that LME prices, even after applying discount bands, could not independently justify rejection of the declared import values. The Tribunal also examined the effect of earlier assessment proceedings involving the same imports. Certain aluminium scrap consignments imported through Nhava Sheva had initially been assessed provisionally and subsequently subjected to enhancement based on LME prices. That enhancement had been set aside in appellate proceedings, and the Department’s further challenge had been rejected by the Tribunal. Similarly, provisional assessments concerning imports through Kandla and Mundra had been finalised by orders accepting the declared transaction values and rejecting LME-based enhancement. Those orders had attained finality. The Tribunal held that the Department could not disregard these completed proceedings and once again enhance the assessable values through the impugned adjudication. It also noted that values relating to 550 Bills of Entry covered by another annexure had already been enhanced at the original assessment stage and that the resulting assessments had not been challenged through the prescribed appellate or review process. The Tribunal considered a further attempt to reassess those finalised values legally unsustainable. Another important aspect of the dispute was the evidentiary value of statements recorded from the firm’s partners, indenting agents and other persons during investigation. The Department relied heavily on statements attributed to Sushil Agarwal concerning the relationship between scrap prices and LME quotations. The Tribunal noticed material inconsistencies between statements recorded on different dates. One statement suggested that aluminium scrap prices were generally negotiated at approximately 80% of LME prices, while another referred to different discount bands ranging from 5% to 35%, corresponding to those mentioned in the Directorate General of Valuation’s circular. The appellants disputed the voluntariness and reliability of these statements and sought cross-examination. They also questioned the authenticity of documents allegedly recovered from an indenting agent’s laptop and requested examination of the officers and witnesses involved in the recovery proceedings. The Tribunal found that the adjudicating authority had improperly denied several requests for cross-examination, while the indenting agents whose examination had been permitted were not produced. Relying on Andaman Timber Industries Vs Commissioner of Central Excise, Basudev Garg Vs Commissioner of Customs and Commissioner of Customs Vs Motabhai Iron & Steel Industries, it held that disputed statements could not be relied upon against the appellants without providing a meaningful opportunity to test their credibility. The Tribunal further considered the Department’s allegation that the difference between declared import prices and allegedly higher actual prices had been settled through hawala transactions or overseas transfers. It found that the investigation had not identified the persons to whom the alleged differential payments were made, established how the appellants generated or possessed the corresponding cash, or produced reliable evidence demonstrating that additional consideration had actually reached the overseas suppliers. Mere suspicion arising from price comparisons, third-party statements or correspondence was insufficient to establish clandestine payments. The Tribunal also examined certain export declarations relating to consignments shipped from New Zealand and Spain. It noted that these declarations had not been authenticated by the relevant foreign customs authorities and that several concerned goods or consignees unrelated to the appellants’ imports. Moreover, the Department had not actually determined the disputed assessable values on the basis of those declarations. The Tribunal therefore declined to treat them as reliable evidence supporting the demands. The appellants additionally argued that countervailing duty, described in the proceedings as additional customs duty, was not payable on imported zinc ash, zinc dross, skimmings and scrap where the corresponding goods did not arise from a manufacturing process and were consequently not liable to central excise duty. They relied on the principles laid down in Hyderabad Industries Ltd. and subsequent judicial decisions and departmental circulars concerning the excisability of waste and scrap. The adjudicating authority had rejected this contention partly because the appellants had not raised it during the original assessments. The Tribunal disagreed, observing that the nature of the imported scrap and the applicable judicial precedents supported the appellants’ position. It also held that an assessee defending recovery proceedings under Section 28 could raise relevant grounds demonstrating that duty was not legally payable, even if those grounds had not been advanced during the original assessment. The Tribunal accordingly rejected the adjudicating authority’s reasoning for denying consideration of the additional-duty objection. Finally, the Tribunal held that the Department had failed to establish the essential factual and legal foundation for the disputed duty demands. The alleged misdeclaration of zinc ash was contradicted by the import-stage chemical examination reports; the LME-based valuation methodology disregarded the prescribed valuation rules and available contemporaneous imports; several assessments had already attained finality; and the evidence relied upon to establish undervaluation and differential payments was insufficient or procedurally unreliable. Since the underlying allegations of undervaluation and misdeclaration were not sustained, the confiscation of goods, including consignments seized at ports and the appellant’s factory, also lacked justification. The penalties imposed on Sunland Metal Recycling Industries and its partners consequently could not survive. By Final Order Nos. A/11871–11874/2019 dated 1 October 2019, the CESTAT Ahmedabad set aside the Commissioner’s order dated 26 March 2019 and allowed all four appeals with consequential relief to the appellants. The decision reinforces the requirement that customs valuation must rest on legally admissible evidence and the prescribed valuation methodology, rather than uncorroborated international commodity prices or unsupported allegations of additional payments. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CESTAT AHMEDABAD The present appeals have been filed by M/s Sunland Metal Recycling Industries (SMRI) and its partners Shri Sushil Kumar Agrawal, Shri Surendra P. Kachhara and Shri Sanjeev Kumar Agrawal against OIO No. KMD-COSTM-000-COM-18-18-19 dated 26.03.2019 passed by the Commissioner of Customs, Kandla. The brief facts of the case are that M/s SMRI are engaged in the manufacture of Aluminum Ingots, Zinc Ingots, Brass ingots and Copper Ingots. The Appellants M/s Sunland during the impugned period of 2002 – 2006, had imported consignments of Zinc Ash, Zinc dross, Zinc scrap and Aluminum scrap of different grades through Ports of Nhava Sheva, Mumbai, Kandla, Mundra and Tuglaqabad. 1.1 M/s SMRI after investigation on the allegation of mis-declaration and under valuation of Zinc and Aluminum scrap were issued Show Cause Notice dt. 07.05.2007. It was contended that M/s SMRI had imported consignments of Zinc Ash and Aluminium Scrap from many parties. In case of 32 consignments of Zinc Ash imported from U.S. Zinc, U.S.A and detailed in Annexure – ‘A-II’ of show cause notice it was alleged that the documents received from the Consulate General of India, USA shows that M/s US Zinc Ltd entered into the agreement for payment of higher rate and the formal contracts were prepared at under-valued rates through indentor, Shri Tarun Jhingon of M/s Trendene India Pvt Ltd, New Delhi. The email correspondence dated 5.4.2003 between Shri Tarun Jhingon and the officials of M/s US Zinc Ltd, USA and emails between the persons of M/s U.S. Zinc were relied upon to allege that the goods were Zinc skimming and not zinc ash containing higher zinc content. Also the test report dated 31.07.2006 of the Chemical Examiner, Vadodara was relied upon that the sample drawn from the factory of M/s SMRI showed the sample to be other than Zinc Ash. It was alleged that the invoice issued by exporter were indicating lower value and the balance amount was paid as cash advance to M/s U.S. Zinc through third parties by Hawala or Telegraphic Transfer outside India. Reliance was placed upon statement of Shri Tarun Jhingon, Indenter of Trendene India Pvt. Ltd. and Shri Sushil Agarwal, Partner of M/s SMRI. 1.2 In case of other varieties of Zinc Scrap namely Zinc Die cast scrap “Saves” and Zinc Dross, the Show Cause Notice relied upon the report dt. 10.01.2007 of Second Secretary (Trade), High Commission of India, U.K wherein it was reported that 11 consignments of Aluminium and Zinc scrap loaded at Barcelona Port, Spain and imported by SMRI consigned by M/s Sunberg Ltd., U.K were undervalued. It also relied upon three emails between M/s Aboura Trading and Commission and Shri Tarun Jhingon to allege undervaluation. Reliance was placed upon Report dated 3.11.2006 of the High Commission of India, Singapore wherein it was reported that in respect of 2 consignments the value declared by the exporter at the port of exports in case of supply from M/s P.W.T. Australia were on higher side as compared to that declared by the Appellant. Reliance was also placed upon report dt. 17.04.2007 of High Commission of India that two consignments loaded from ports in Greece were undervalued. The show cause notice alleged that Shri Sushil Agarwal in his statement has stated that the prevailing LME prices formed the basis of negotiations and normally the price negotiated at 80% of the LME as the Aluminium scrap imported by them. Shri Sushil Agarwal was shown abstract of LME for the period 2006 to April’ 2006 and 80% of the High and low price and on showing comparison of the same with the import price, he stated that the aspect of undervaluation was apparent and almost in all the cases the price declared in respective invoice was on lower side than 80% of the LME which is normally negotiated. Statement of Indentor Shri Tarun Jhingon was also recorded on 25.04.2006 wherein he stated that M/s Trendene India Pvt. Ltd is indenting agent for the firms namely M/s FBM Metals U.K & Germany, M/s Mount Star Metal UK, M/s Aboura Trading, Jordan, M/s Euro Gulf, Jordan, M/s Arsh Metal, UAE, M/s N. Raydan Comm. Org., Saudi Arabia, M/s U.S. Zinc, USA & M/s Indra Recycling, Germany. Statement of Shri Rajeshwar Prasad Dubey, CHA was also recorded on 22.06.2006 wherein he stated that the sample of Aluminium scrap were not drawn but in case of Zinc Dross/Ash, the samples were drawn and sent to Deputy Chemist at Nhava Sheva/Mumbai and the test reports were received of the same were directly received by the Customs Authorities and based on test reports the consignments assessed provisionally were finalised. During investigation against M/s SMRI the imported scrap of Zinc and Aluminium lying at different ports and factory of M/s SMRI were seized on grounds of undervaluation. Statement dt. 16.01.2007 of Shri Mihir Bhatt, Prop. of M/s Metal Resources, Mumbai, who was dealing in import & export of non-ferrous metal & scrap was relied upon wherein he stated that the minimum metal content in the Aluminium Scrap was 70% to 80% and considering impurities content in the respective lot, the prices of the metal content therein could be the purchase price. He also stated that his business with M/s SMRI was only of around 300 MT, which is negligible quantity. Statement of Shri Haji Amin Gadawala, Chairman of Bibi Group of companies was recorded 17.1.2006 wherein he stated that he was acting as a follow-up agent of M/s Ala International Trading, Sharjah. The Indian buyers were importing Aluminium Scrap from M/s Shar Metals, Sharjah, M/s Aboura Trading, Jordan, M/s Namma Trading, Saudi Arabia, M/s Al-Sharif Metals, UAE and M/s Eta, Dubai. All the Indian buyers negotiated with M/s Ala International Trading, who in turn negotiated and arranged the consignments from the above-said entities and accordingly invoices were raised by the respective exporters and payment was directly made to them. He also submitted that the invoices raised against the respective contracts were not the actual prices/values negotiated. He was shown the comparison of imported invoices and LME prices, to which he stated, there has been under-invoicing. The show cause notice also placed reliance upon the search made in another case against M/s Agravanshi Aluminium Ltd and where the CPU was seized. It was alleged that said M/s Agravanshi Aluminium Ltd resorted to under-valuation in imports of Aluminum Scrap during the period 01.01.2005 to 30.04.2005 through indentors, M/s Nehan Ispat, Mumbai in imports made from M/s Al-Sharief, Dubai and same modus operandi was adopted by the Appellant unit for import from the same supplier in six consignment. Statements of Shri Surendra Kachhara, Partner of M/s SMRI, was also recorded wherein he agreed with the statement of Shri Sushil Agrawal. He also stated that they were purchasing Aluminium scrap in various forms, which were classified on the basis of their form, source and quality. He also stated that the import price of Aluminium scrap before Customs authority was much lower than the corresponding LME prices of Aluminium Scrap while effecting imports. Statement of Shri Sanjeev Kumar Agrawal, another partner, was also recorded, who had agreed with the statements given by other Partners. 1.3 Based on above, it was also alleged that M/s SMRI had imported 1699 consignments of Aluminium scrap of various grades as defined in ISRI. That on scrutiny of declared value of imported goods vis-a-vis the prevailing LME of the respective imports, the imports were found to be undervalued. The show cause notice relied upon the LME Prices for Aluminium metal for the impugned period and Circular No. 14/2005 issued from F. No. VAL/TECH/37/2005 dt. 16.12.2005 by Director of Valuation wherein the directorate had allowed discounts in the LME Prices considering the metal content of Aluminium in the various grades of Aluminium Scrap. U.K, the value declared by the said exporter are on the higher side as compared to that declared by SMRI at the time of clearance in India. The said consignments including 3 consignments from Aboura Trading, M/s Metal Interest Ltd. and M/s Sunberg, UK were proposed to be valued on the basis that it contained 65% metal content. (v) Demand of Rs. 1,91,22,810/- was also proposed in respect of seized Aluminium and Zinc Scrap and the goods contained in finished goods seized at factory of M/s SMRI as detailed in Annexure – RM-I. (vi) Duty was also proposed in Annexure – RM-II on Aluminium and Zinc scrap seized from Nhava Sheva/JNPT on the basis that the imported price when compared with LME price of virgin material in case of Zinc Scrap as per ISRI Norms and LME minus discount band as per DGOV Circular No. 14/2005 dt. 16.12.2005 in case of Aluminium Scrap shows difference. Aggrieved the present Appellants has filed the present appeals. 2. Shri Jaideep. C. Patel, Ld. Counsel appearing for the Appellants submits that the impugned order has been passed without any reasoning. The confirmation of demand on the ground of mis declaration and on the basis of LME price does not have any base. In case of Zinc Ash imported from U.S. Zinc the goods at the time of import were tested by the Chemical Examiner, Nhava-Sheva and were found to be of declared description. He takes us through the sample test reports to support his contention. He submits that when the examination of goods showed them as Zinc Ash, in such case the same cannot be alleged to be Zinc Skimming on the basis of correspondence between supplier and indenter or the supplier documents. The allegation is not supported by any evidence but only on third party documents. The test report of chemical examiner in case of sample drawn from factory cannot be relied upon to support allegation of misdeclaration of zinc skimming as zinc ash as the report only states that the goods are other than Zinc Ash. The cross examination of chemical examiner was not allowed and hence in view of above fact the report given by him cannot be relied upon. He submit that on the basis of documents and report of the Consulate of India, U.S.A or the correspondence between the employees of supplier or the email between indentor and supplier it cannot be alleged that the goods were Zinc Skimming as the chemical examination at the time of import clearly shows that the goods are Zinc Ash. 2.1 He also submits that during impugned period the imports of Zinc Ash and Zinc Dross were provisionally assessed and hence the demand under section 28 of the Customs Act is not tenable. In respect of Zinc Ash and Zinc dross, covered by Annexures A-II to A-IV to the SCN, the assessments were provisional and therefore the demands could not have been made under Section-28 of the Customs Act. The adjudicating authority though accepted the proposition that in respect of provisionally assessed Bills of entries the demand under section 28 is not maintainable, but confirmed the demand by holding that the Appellants had not specifically mentioned which bills of entry are provisional and that there was no evidence of provisional assessment in the form of execution of bond. This contention of the adjudicating authority is erroneous and he has overlooked the material facts. The Hon’ble Apex Court in the said case held as follows: “The valuation of the imported goods where the transaction value in the opinion of Assessing Authority is liable to be rejected because of invoice manipulation or under-invoicing or un-realistic price or misdeclaration in respect of valuation of goods or description or where transaction value of the goods declared is ridiculously low, which of course the Assessing Authority has to justify, he must proceed to determine valuation of goods by following Customs Valuation Rules. The availability of evidence of contemporaneous import of the same goods obviously provides the best guide for determination of value of the import of goods but in the absence of evidence of contemporaneous import, reference to foreign journal for finding out correct international price of imported goods may not be irrelevant.” 5.5 In the instant case, the proposition in the show cause notice is that the value of the contemporaneous imports indicated a higher price. If that be so, that should have been the starting point for determination of value of the imported goods and not some other basis. Further even when we take the values of the contemporaneous imports, the lowest of such value has to be adopted as provided for in Rule 6 and not the highest. In the instant case no such thing has been done by the assessing officer. 5.6 In view of the above, we do not find any infirmity in the observation of the Commissioner (Appeals) that the DGOV Circular cannot override the provisions of Valuation Rules. That the cross examination of Shri Sushil Agarwal was also not allowed. Further that even though the cross examination of indentors were allowed but they did not appear for the cross examination. The Appellant has pleaded that in such circumstances, the statement of Shri Sushil Agarwal and indentors cannot be relied upon. We find that the adjudicating authority ought to have allowed cross examination of Shri Sushil Agarwal as the same was necessarily required in view of his statements dated 29.5.2006 and 23.04.2007 which were contradictory in respect of value of imported goods. In his statement dated 29.5.2006 on being shown an alleged abstract of LME prices of Aluminium Scrap, which showed price of Aluminium scrap as 80% of the LME price he had stated that the prevailing LME prices formed the basis of negotiation and that normally, the prices of Aluminium scrap were negotiated at 80% of the LME. However in his statement dated 23.04.2007, he has stated that the import price of Aluminium Scrap was negotiated by applying different discount bands ranging from 5% to 35% to the LME price of various grades, viz. Trump, Tense, Taldon, Trob etc., which corresponded to the discount band provided in Alert Circular No. 14/2005 dated 16.12.2005 of the Director General of Valuation. In such a case, we are inclined to accept the submission of the Appellant that initially the investigating officers were attempting to apply 80% of the LME, but later on realizing that even the Director General of Valuation was not recommending valuation of 80% of the LME, the statement of Shri Sushil Agarwal was recorded to match the discount band, as per DGOV Circular. In such view of the facts, we do not find any reason to rely upon the statement of Shri Agarwal to support the allegation of under-valuation on the part of the Appellant. The Appellant has pleaded that it is incomprehensible that Shri Sushil Agarwal, who was in business of imports of Aluminium Scraps and Zinc Scrap since last many years would state that the prices of Scrap would be equal to the prices of metal content. It is a known fact that re-cycling of scrap would not result into recovery of entire metal content as there would be a process loss, cost to be incurred for conversion of scrap to metal. Such metal produced from Scrap cannot command same price as that of virgin metal. We find that the Appellant had sought cross examination of Shri Sushil Agarwal under Rule 138B of the Customs Act. The allegation of undervaluation are based upon the letters of Indian Consulates and statements of indentors namely Shri Anil Parolia of Nihon Ispat, Shri Tarun Jhingon, Shri Ehsan Amin Gadawala and Shri Mihir Bhat as well as letters from Indian Consulate at U.S.A in reference to import of 32 consignments of Zinc Ash, Indian Consulate at UK in respect of 11 Consignment imported from Sunberg and Indian Consulate at Singapore in respect of 2 consignments imported from PWT Australia. The Appellant had sought cross examination of Indentors. The Appellant has also sought cross-examination of the officials and panchas, who were present during drawal of Panchnama on 26.04.2006 for examination of seized laptop of Shri Tarun Jhingon on the ground that it was not possible to complete the proceedings in 2 hours, as stated in the show cause notice. The cross examination of the officials, who had recorded the statement of Shri Sushil Agarwal on 9.4.2007 was also sought since it was recorded in his statement that he had submitted in tabular form running into 120 pages the particulars of all imports made in 5 years by the Appellant, which are involved in the impugned appeal. The Appellant in their reply had contended that the cross examination of officials was sought as all the import related documents of the Appellant were lying seized with the investigating officers and there was no occasion for Shri Sushil Agarwal to compile such information in absence of any import documents. However except indentors no cross examination of any of the above persons was allowed. The cross examination of Shri Sushil Agarwal was denied by the adjudicating authority on the ground that no new facts are likely to come out at such examination and cross-examination. This reasoning of the adjudicating authority is highly erroneous since it cannot be assumed that the cross examination of a person would not bring any material not already available. Our views are based upon the judgment of the Hon’ble apex court in case of Andaman Timber Industries – 2015 (324) ELT 641 (SC), wherein the apex court held as under: “6. According to us, not allowing the assessee to cross-examine the witnesses by the Adjudicating Authority though the statements of those witnesses were made the basis of the impugned order is a serious flaw which makes the order nullity inasmuch as it amounted to violation of principles of natural justice because of which the assessee was adversely affected. It is to be borne in mind that the order of the Commissioner was based upon the statements given by the aforesaid two witnesses. Even when the assessee disputed the correctness of the statements and wanted to cross-examine, the Adjudicating Authority did not grant this opportunity to the assessee. It would be pertinent to note that in the impugned order passed by the Adjudicating Authority he has specifically mentioned that such an opportunity was sought by the assessee. However, no such opportunity was granted and the aforesaid plea is not even dealt with by the Adjudicating Authority. As far as the Tribunal is concerned, we find that rejection of this plea is totally untenable. The Tribunal has simply stated that cross-examination of the said dealers could not have brought out any material which would not be in possession of the appellant themselves to explain as to why their ex-factory prices remain static. It was not for the Tribunal to have guess work as to for what purposes the appellant wanted to cross-examine those dealers and what extraction the appellant wanted from them. 7. As mentioned above, the appellant had contested the truthfulness of the statements of these two witnesses and wanted to discredit their testimony for which purpose it wanted to avail the opportunity of cross-examination. That apart, the Adjudicating Authority simply relied upon the price list as maintained at the depot to determine the price for the purpose of levy of excise duty. Whether the goods were, in fact, sold to the said dealers/witnesses at the price which is mentioned in the price list itself could be the subject matter of cross-examination. Therefore, it was not for the Adjudicating Authority to presuppose as to what could be the subject matter of the cross-examination and make the remarks as mentioned above. We may also point out that on an earlier occasion when the matter came before this Court in Civil Appeal No. 2216 of 2000, order dated 17-3-2005 [2005 (187) E.L.T. A33 (S.C.)] was passed remitting the case back to the Tribunal with the directions to decide the appeal on merits giving its reasons for accepting or rejecting the submissions. 8. In view the above, we are of the opinion that if the testimony of these two witnesses is discredited, there was no material with the Department on the basis of which it could justify its action, as the statement of the aforesaid two witnesses was the only basis of issuing the show cause notice. 9. We, thus, set aside the impugned order as passed by the Tribunal and allow this appeal.” 11. Similarly, in case of Vasudev Garg – 2013 (294) ELT 353 (Dl), it was held that it was mandatory to give cross examination. It was held that the statement against the assessee cannot be used without giving them opportunity of cross examining the witness as it is valuable right of accused/ noticee in quasi judicial proceedings which can have adverse consequence for them. The Adjudicating authority had allowed cross examination of indentors and even they were also issued notice, none of the indentor was made available for cross examination. In such circumstances when these persons could not be produced for cross examination, their statements could not have been relied upon. Especially in case of Mihir Bhatt, Ehsan Amin Gadawala where the only evidence was their own statements stating undervaluation. In case of other persons also in the light of above facts, the adjudicating authority should have allowed the cross examination. In absence of the opportunity to cross examine the above persons, we are of the view that no reliance can be placed upon their statements. Our views are supported by the judgment of Hon’ble High Court of Gujarat in case of CC Vs. Motabhai Iron & Steel Industries 2015 (316) ELT 374 (GUJ) wherein the Hon’ble High Court has held that “no reliance can be placed on the statement of such witness who has not subjected himself to cross-examination by the affected party”. Similarly we are of the view that no reliance can be placed on the alleged recovery of email from indentor Shri Tarun Jhingon as no opportunity of cross examination of panch witness and officers was given to Appellant. We further find that during the visit to the factory of M/s SMRI, the officers had questioned the employee of M/s SMRI namely Shri Rajesh Kumar Trivedi, C. Haridas and Nikhil Jain on the basis of documents called “Abstract of LME Price of Aluminium Scrap and Zinc Scrap’ to which said employees had stated that the Appellant’s declared price was less. This document was handed over to the Appellant on 20.02.2018 and it contained Pages 1 to 8 lowest & Highest Zinc prices of a day and the next 10 pages tiled MP Prices & Archive containing low price of the day alongwith 80% of said price. However we find that nowhere the said papers contain prices of Aluminium or Zinc Scrap. Also no prices of Aluminium Scrap is issued by the LME as it is not concerned with scrap pricing. Hence the allegation that the Appellant’s declared price was less than the mentioned in said document does not hold any substance. We also find that the Appellant had sought cross examination of the officers for the source and authenticity of such document and 80% formula derived by officers. They had also sought cross examination of panch witness who had witnessed such proceedings. However the same was not allowed. In absence of authenticity of such document and refusal of cross examination we find that the charges of undervaluation are not sustainable. 12. Coming to the issue raised by the Appellants that no additional duty of Customs is payable in respect of Zinc Ash, skimmings and scrap as these are not manufactured product, we observe that adjudicating authority has denied relief to Appellant on ground that they have not produced evidence to show that goods are not manufactured products. We find from the definition of various scrap imported as per ISRI and the photographs annexed to appeal papers that it clearly shows that the scrap was not arising as a result of process of manufacture. The ratio of law on such scarp being non excisable is absolutely settled by the judgments and Circulars viz. Hindalco Industries Ltd. 2015 (315) ELT 10 (Bom), Circular No. 904/24/2009 – CX dated 28.10.2009, Circular No. 1027/15/2016-CX dated 25.04.2016, Slugs India Ltd Vs. CCE – 202 (278) ELT 611, CC vs Tata Iron & Steel Co. Ltd – 2004 (165) ELT 386 (SC), Bhushan Steel Ltd vs CCE – 2012 (284) ELT 713, Shri Ram Agro Chemicals (P) Ltd vs UOI – 2009 (234) ELT 218 (P & H), CC vs L. Madanlal (Aluminium) Ltd – 2010 (258) ELT 107, Karnataka Chemical IndusCorpn. Ltd vs CC – 2005 (183) ELT 207. The adjudicating authority also held that since the issue of non-payment of Additional duty was not raised at the time of assessment, hence cannot be raised in reply to show cause notice under section 28. The contention of adjudicating authority is not sustainable as when notice under section 28 is issued to an assessee, he can contest the whole assessment to say that duty is not payable for a reason not taken up at the time of original assessment. He is entitled for all reliefs/ exemption associated with the assessment. The issues is well settled by the Tribunal’s order in case of Decora Ceramics P. Ltd. 1998 (100) ELT 297, Lili Foam Indus. P. Ltd. 1990 (46) ELT 462, Bakeman Home Products P. Ltd. 1997 (95) ELT 278. 13. Further, we also find that in respect of 550 Bills of entries covered under Annexure – C, value was already enhanced at the time of assessment and hence further proposal to re-enhance the value when the earlier assessment order has attained finality since no appeal/review was filed against such order, is not sustainable. There cannot be any reassessment of the said values, which had become final for want of appeal against the same. Our views are supported by judgments in case CC vs Lord Shiva Overseas – 2005 (181) ELT 213, Malhotra Impex vs CC – 2006 (203) ELT 561 and CC vs Paras Electronics – 2009 (246) ELT 231. 14. In respect of export declaration in respect of one consignment shipped from New Zealand and 11 consignments shipped from Spain, we are in agreement with the submission of the Appellant that such declarations have no relevance as the same were not authenticated by the News Zealand Customs and Spanish Customs. Even otherwise also most of these declarations are in respect of goods viz. Iron and Steel i.e other than those imported by the Appellants and the Appellant are not consignees. Even in some declarations, the goods are Iron & Steel scrap which were not imported by Appellant. Even the values has not been re-determined on the basis of such declarations and hence the same are not relevant in the instant case. 15. In respect of demands made in Annexure – RM-I and II, we find that the demands was raised on same ground as in case of demand made in other annexures. We have elaborately discussed the grounds for non sustainability of demand and the same ratio would apply in respect of seized goods also. Also since the charges of undervaluation of declared price is not sustainable, hence the confiscation of goods is also not sustainable. 16. In view of our above observations and findings, we are of the view that the demands confirmed against M/s SMRI, confiscation of goods and penalties imposed upon M/s SMRI is not sustainable. For the same reason the penalty imposed upon co-appellants namely Shri Sushil Kumar Agarwal, Shri Surendra P. Kachhara and Shri Sanjeev Kumar Agarwal is also not sustainable. We thus set aside the impugned order and allow all the appeals before us with consequential reliefs to the Appellants.
