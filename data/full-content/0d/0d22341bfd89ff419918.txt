Summary: The ledger candidate Axis Bluechip Fund is now Axis Large Cap Fund. Axis Mutual Fund’s current material states that the name changed with effect from 2 June 2025; this is a rename of the existing scheme, not a new fund and not a basis for duplicate direct-versus-regular coverage. The current Scheme Information Document describes an open-ended equity scheme that normally invests 80% to 100% of total assets in equity and equity-related instruments of large-cap companies. Its AMFI Tier 1 benchmark is the BSE 100 Total Return Index, and both scheme and benchmark currently carry the Very High Riskometer label. The Direct Growth page displayed AUM of ₹29,231.05 crore as at 30 September 2026 and a direct-plan expense ratio of 0.92% as at 21 October 2026; these are dated disclosures that can change. The scheme is market-linked, carries concentration, valuation, liquidity, derivative and active-management risk, and does not guarantee returns. Redemptions can attract exit load and equity-oriented-fund capital-gains tax. The scheme’s stated objective is to generate long-term capital appreciation by investing in a diversified portfolio predominantly consisting of equity and equity-related instruments of large-cap companies. An objective is a portfolio mandate, not a promise. The SID expressly states that there is no assurance the objective will be achieved. Large-cap classification follows the applicable AMFI/SEBI market-cap framework and can change as the ranked universe is periodically updated. Active security selection can cause the scheme to perform differently from its benchmark in either direction. Plans, Options, Minimums and Expense Ratio Direct and Regular are cost routes, not separate schemes The Direct Plan excludes distributor commission and must have a lower expense ratio than the Regular Plan to the extent required by regulation; both invest in the same scheme portfolio. Growth and IDCW options change how distributable surplus is handled. IDCW is not interest and can include a return of capital; the NAV falls to the extent of the distribution and applicable statutory adjustment. A switch between plans can have tax consequences even when the AMC does not levy an exit load for that internal switch, because tax law can treat the switch as a transfer. The current SID states a minimum initial and additional application amount of ₹100 and multiples of ₹1 thereafter, with no minimum redemption criterion, subject to folio balance, lien, pledge and other operational conditions. The AMC product page displayed a Direct Plan expense ratio of 0.92% as at 21 October 2026. TER accrues in the NAV rather than appearing as a separately debited invoice. The regular-plan TER is normally higher because it includes distribution expenses. Investors should verify the live daily TER and plan name immediately before transacting. Exit Load and Redemption Timing The SID states that if units are redeemed or switched out within 12 months from allotment, up to 10% of the investment can be redeemed without exit load and the remaining amount attracts 1%. Redemption or switch-out after 12 months attracts nil exit load. The treatment applies to each allotment, so SIP instalments have separate holding periods. Switches between Direct and Regular Plans, and between Growth and IDCW options within the same plan, have special load treatment in the SID; tax treatment must still be assessed independently. The SID says redemption proceeds are to be dispatched within three working days after receipt of a valid request, subject to AMFI/SEBI exceptions. Bank holidays, rejected or incomplete requests, frozen or lien-marked folios and exceptional market circumstances can affect credit. Exit load is deducted from the applicable NAV-based redemption value, and applicable GST on the exit-load component is paid from the load before the net load is credited back to the scheme. An investor should preserve the allotment date, units, NAV, load computation and bank credit. Tax Treatment for an Indian Resident Investor Axis Large Cap Fund is designed to maintain predominantly domestic equity exposure and is ordinarily an equity-oriented fund for tax purposes, but the applicable status and transaction conditions should be verified for the period concerned. For STT-paid redemptions or eligible transfers on or after 23 July 2024, section 111A generally taxes short-term capital gains on equity-oriented-fund units at 20%, plus applicable surcharge and 4% health and education cess. Units generally become long-term after a holding period exceeding 12 months for this purpose. Section 112A generally taxes aggregate long-term capital gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year at 12.5%, plus applicable surcharge and cess, where statutory conditions including STT are met. No indexation is available under section 112A. IDCW receipts are generally taxable at the investor’s applicable slab rate, and the mutual fund may deduct tax subject to section 194K, thresholds, residential status and treaty or certificate relief. Securities transaction tax and stamp duty can also apply as prescribed. Tax rates, set-off rules and return schedules depend on the investor and financial year, so the AMC tax reckoner is explanatory rather than a substitute for advice. No. The current name is Axis Large Cap Fund, effective 2 June 2025. References to Axis Bluechip Fund ordinarily concern the historical name of the same underlying scheme. Does the 80% large-cap rule mean the portfolio is fully invested in shares? No. The SID permits other equity, debt and money-market instruments and REIT/InvIT units within ranges. Cash and derivatives can also affect effective exposure under applicable rules. Are Direct Growth and Regular Growth separate portfolios? They are plans of the same scheme and share the portfolio, but have different expense structures. Direct excludes distributor commission; NAVs and returns differ because expenses differ. How does the 10% exit-load-free allowance work? For units redeemed within 12 months, the SID allows up to 10% of the investment without load and charges 1% on the remaining early-redemption amount. Each allotment date matters, including each SIP instalment. Is the 0.92% expense ratio fixed? No. That was the Direct Plan figure displayed for 21 October 2026. TER can change within regulatory limits; verify the AMC’s daily TER for the exact plan. Does the Very High Riskometer mean a loss is certain? No. It indicates the prescribed risk level, not a forecast. Actual outcomes depend on market prices, portfolio decisions, valuations, costs and the investor’s entry and exit dates. What tax applies when units are switched from Regular to Direct? A switch can be treated as a redemption and fresh purchase for tax even where the AMC levies no exit load.
