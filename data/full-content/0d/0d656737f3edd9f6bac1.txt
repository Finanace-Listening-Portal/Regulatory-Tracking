The trend is raising questions about the sustainability of growth in a segment that has become an important source of secured credit for households and small businessesShriram Finance has reported an increase in loans overdue by more than 90 days, while Bajaj Finance has seen a rise in Stage 2 assets linked to short-tenure gold loan schemes. Muthoot Finance has also recorded a sharp increase in Stage 2 and Stage 3 assets, which it attributes largely to changes in repayment structures. The developments raise questions about whether gold loans are increasingly being used by borrowers already struggling to service existing debt. Advt Gold loans have become one of the fastest-growing segments of retail credit. Bank gold loans increased 83% year on year to Rs 5.6 lakh crore in August 2026, while NBFC gold loans rose 69% to Rs 3.6 lakh crore, according to data reported by Reuters. The Reserve Bank of India’s June 2026 Financial Stability Report identified gold loans as the fastest-growing retail credit segment, with the portfolio recording a compound annual growth rate of 42.4% since March 2024.The expansion has been accompanied by concerns about multiple borrowing. Piramal Finance estimates that lenders disbursed around Rs 4.7 lakh crore in gold loans during FY26 to borrowers who were already 90 days or more overdue on at least one other loan. The estimate points to the possibility that some households are using gold-backed borrowing to meet obligations elsewhere rather than financing fresh consumption or productive activity.Piramal’s management has also indicated that a significant proportion of its gold loan customers had loans with other lenders. Such overlaps do not automatically imply default, but they make it harder for lenders to assess a borrower's overall repayment capacity when underwriting is based primarily on the value of pledged jewellery.Delinquencies warrant attentionShriram Finance’s results offer a more direct indication of stress. Its gold loan portfolio grew 68% year on year to Rs 8,974 crore in September 2026. However, the Gross Stage 3 ratio increased to 3.12% from 2.57% in the June quarter and 2.28% a year earlier. The Gross Stage 2 ratio also edged up to 9.88% from 9.71% a year earlier. Advt The company has partly attributed the increase in delinquencies to borrowers adjusting to EMI-based and periodic interest-servicing products. These structures require more regular payments than bullet repayment loans, under which borrowers repay principal and interest at maturity. The shift may expose repayment weaknesses that were less visible when borrowers could settle interest later.Bajaj Finance has reported a different pattern. Its combined Stage 2 and Stage 3 assets increased by Rs 1,601 crore during the September quarter. Of this, Rs 1,047 crore came from higher Stage 2 assets in its gold loan business, linked to short-tenure schemes introduced after the revised RBI guidelines. The company said Rs 550 crore of these assets had subsequently returned to Stage 1 following repayments. It discontinued the short-tenure offering in August. Advt Stage 2 classification indicates a significant increase in credit risk; it does not mean that every such loan is overdue by a specified number of days. The movement therefore needs to be distinguished from a straightforward increase in loans that have stopped receiving payments.Muthoot Finance, India's largest gold loan NBFC, has also reported a sharp increase in assets in higher-risk accounting categories. Its gold loan assets under management rose 43% year on year to Rs 1.79 lakh crore in September. Stage 3 assets increased to Rs 7,268 crore from Rs 3,926.5 crore in June, while Stage 2 assets rose to Rs 23,467 crore from Rs 1,876 crore. Advt The company has attributed much of this movement to borrowers adjusting to its shift towards monthly interest servicing from bullet repayment structures. Management does not regard the increase as evidence of a comparable deterioration in underlying borrower quality and expects the position to ease as customers adapt. The change should not, however, be interpreted as a universal RBI requirement for all gold loans. The revised directions continue to permit bullet repayment loans for consumption, subject to a maximum tenure of 12 months.What happens if gold prices fall?The rapid rise in gold prices has supported the value of collateral and given lenders a larger buffer against defaults. But this protection is not unlimited. A sustained fall in prices could reduce the value of pledged jewellery, particularly for loans originated at higher loan-to-value ratios.The RBI's revised directions cap the LTV ratio for consumption loans at 85% for amounts up to Rs 2.5 lakh, 80% for loans above Rs 2.5 lakh and up to Rs 5 lakh, and 75% for loans above Rs 5 lakh. Lenders must also maintain the prescribed LTV throughout the loan tenure. For bullet repayment loans, the calculation must include the total amount payable at maturity, including interest.Most gold-focused NBFCs have historically maintained LTV ratios below the regulatory ceiling. Crisil Ratings has noted that such lenders generally operate at 65-75%. Its analysis also found that around 90% of 12-month bullet loans were repaid by maturity, while less than 3% of gold loans disbursed were typically recovered through auctions.These safeguards reduce the likelihood of large losses, but do not eliminate the risk. If gold prices fall sharply while borrowers are unable to repay, lenders may have to auction collateral in a weaker market. A concentration of borrowers with multiple loans could compound the problem.The immediate concern is not that the gold loan market is facing a broad-based crisis. Rather, rapid portfolio growth, overlapping borrowings and changing repayment patterns warrant closer monitoring. For lenders, the strength of collateral must be matched by a better assessment of borrowers' total indebtedness and ability to repay.
