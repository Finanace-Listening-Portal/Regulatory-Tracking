Summary: Sitaram Denims India Limited filed a draft red herring prospectus dated 3 November 2026 for a proposed 100% book-built IPO of up to 30,938,000 equity shares with a face value of ₹5 each. The offer comprises a fresh issue of up to 26,467,000 shares and an offer for sale of up to 4,471,000 shares by promoter-group selling shareholders, with a possible pre-IPO placement subject to disclosed conditions. The issuer proposes to use fresh-issue proceeds for ₹400 million of borrowing repayments, ₹413.73 million towards a new readymade-garment facility at Banera, Bhilwara, and ₹232.25 million for working capital, alongside general corporate purposes and expenses yet to be finalised. The textile company reports revenue from operations of ₹2,423.84 million, EBITDA of ₹504.79 million and profit after tax of ₹242.26 million for FY2026. For the quarter ended 30 June 2026, it reports revenue of ₹751.07 million and PAT of ₹79.78 million, but cautions against annualising the quarter. Key investor considerations include substantial customer concentration, dependence on cash conversion and working capital, execution risk for the garment project, equity dilution and first-listing risk. The DRHP does not establish the IPO price band, share price, bid lot, subscription dates or final rupee offer size. Those particulars require subsequent official offer documents and exchange disclosures; the draft filing is not itself an open offer or investment recommendation. Fresh issue and promoter-group sale must be separated The draft divides the proposed offer into a fresh issue and an offer for sale. Up to 26.467 million new shares would be issued by the company, changing the equity base and creating dilution. Up to 4.471 million existing shares would be sold by promoter-group selling shareholders. Madhu Nuwal and Mamta Nuwal each propose up to 1,490,333 shares, and Sangeeta Nuwal proposes up to 1,490,334 shares. OFS cash belongs to those sellers after applicable expenses and taxes; it is not available for the issuer’s factories, debt or working capital. The DRHP also contemplates a pre-IPO placement of up to five million shares. If undertaken, the amount and fresh-issue size would be adjusted subject to law and the disclosed 20% cap. The draft itself warns prospective pre-IPO subscribers that there is no guarantee the public offer will proceed, succeed or result in listing. A later RHP, addendum or exchange notice is needed to establish the final capital structure and the number of shares actually offered. Where the company proposes to deploy fresh capital The draft identifies three quantified objects before general corporate purposes and offer expenses. It proposes ₹400.00 million for repayment or prepayment of specified borrowings, ₹413.73 million for a readymade-garment manufacturing facility at Banera in Bhilwara, and ₹232.25 million for working-capital requirements. General corporate purposes remain blank in the draft and must comply with the applicable cap when finalised. Deployment timing is split between fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2028 in the objects table. Debt repayment can reduce finance cost, but it does not eliminate indebtedness or operating risk. The new garment facility is a project rather than an already proven earnings stream; cost overruns, commissioning delays, capacity utilisation and customer acceptance can affect returns. Working-capital funding supports operations but may be tied up in stock or customer credit. These are uses of proceeds, not forecasts of revenue or profit. Business footprint and manufacturing dependence The company describes a textile business spanning woven cotton-spandex fabric, rigid cotton fabric, polyester-viscose and blended fabric, and denim. It also undertakes job-work activities such as weaving, sizing and yarn-related processing. The DRHP reports 204 air-jet looms, fiscal 2026 production of about 24.46 million metres and annual installed capacity of 25.20 million metres. Capacity statistics show operating scale, but they do not establish sustainable margins or demand. The proposed garment unit would extend the operating chain beyond the existing fabric activity. That may increase value addition, but it creates new execution dependencies: project completion, labour and skill availability, utility reliability, compliance, product acceptance and efficient procurement. Raw-material prices and availability can affect both fabric output and working capital. A reader should follow the issuer’s later RHP for updated capacity, orders, financing and project status rather than treating the draft position as frozen. EBITDA rose from ₹203.00 million to ₹418.80 million and ₹504.79 million across those years. Profit after tax rose from ₹52.35 million to ₹159.42 million and ₹242.26 million. For the three months ended 30 June 2026, the draft reports revenue from operations of ₹751.07 million, EBITDA of ₹157.81 million and profit after tax of ₹79.78 million. The DRHP expressly says the three-month results are not annualised and should not be extrapolated. Fiscal 2026 EBITDA margin was 20.83% and PAT margin 9.99%, while the June quarter showed 21.01% and 10.62%. Margin improvement is historical, not a forecast. Customer concentration, working capital and cash conversion The top ten customers generated 63.31% of revenue from operations in fiscal 2024, 64.54% in fiscal 2025, 57.76% in fiscal 2026 and 59.04% in the June 2026 quarter. The largest customer’s share rose from 10.31% in fiscal 2024 to 23.05% in fiscal 2026. The customer group can change between periods and the issuer does not have the benefit of automatic repeat demand. Loss of a large buyer, weaker orders, price pressure or slower collections could have a disproportionate effect. The business also requires inventory and receivables funding. The draft reports net cash used in operating activities of ₹29.18 million in fiscal 2025 and net cash generated from operations of ₹23.63 million in fiscal 2026. Profit growth does not guarantee cash conversion. Readers should monitor debtor days, inventory, supplier terms, working-capital borrowings and whether proceeds are deployed as disclosed. Risk reading checklist for this specific filing Filing-stage risk: observations, updates, market conditions or issuer decisions may change or stop the offer. Pricing risk: no price band or final offer price exists in the DRHP, so valuation and lot-level outlay cannot be calculated. Customer concentration: a small group contributes a majority of revenue, with the largest customer’s share having risen. Execution risk: the Banera garment facility must be funded, completed and operated at commercially useful utilisation. Working-capital risk: inventory and receivables can absorb cash even when reported profit rises. Dilution and OFS distinction: fresh shares dilute; OFS cash goes to selling shareholders rather than the company.
