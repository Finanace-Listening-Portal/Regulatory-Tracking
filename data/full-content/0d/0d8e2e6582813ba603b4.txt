Summary : PNB MetLife Mera Term Plan Plus is an individual, non-linked, non-participating pure-risk life insurance product with UIN 117N126V04. The insurer continues to list it as an active plan. The contract offers three basic benefit options, optional return of premium, spouse coverage, cover-enhancement choices and different death-benefit payout formats. Those features are not interchangeable: each has separate eligibility, premium, waiting-period and claim consequences. This guide reads the current policy document as a contract, explains where benefits reduce the remaining cover rather than add to it, and sets out the Indian tax and GST position without recommending purchase. It does not add terminal-illness, waiver-of-premium or accelerated critical-illness benefits merely because the product brochure describes them elsewhere. Life Plus adds terminal illness and premium waiver Life Plus includes death benefit, terminal-illness benefit and waiver of future premiums following accidental total permanent disability or diagnosis of one of the 50 listed critical illnesses. The terminal-illness payment is an early payment of the Sum Assured on Death, not a second amount on top of death cover. Once it has been paid, the death benefit is not payable again. The waiver can trigger only once and does not apply to a single-premium policy. Under the policy wording, Life Plus premium and benefits reduce to the Life option from the policy anniversary on which the life assured has attained age 75. Life Plus Health accelerates part of the existing cover Life Plus Health adds an accelerated critical-illness benefit. On diagnosis of a covered illness while the policy is in force, 25% of the basic sum assured, capped at ₹50 lakh, is paid immediately. The remaining Sum Assured on Death continues for a subsequent covered death or terminal-illness claim. Because this is accelerated rather than additional cover, the payment reduces the amount left for the later claim. Definitions, severity thresholds, survival conditions and exclusions in the critical-illness annexure control entitlement. The choice is made at inception and is recorded in the schedule. Income instalments do not create an investment return; they are the contractual method of paying the insured amount. The policy wording excludes monthly-income choices for certain second-life and cover-enhancement payouts, so nominees should not assume every component follows the same mode. Return of premium changes maturity and surrender rights Return of premium is available only when selected at inception for an additional premium. If the life assured survives to maturity, the Sum Assured on Maturity is paid and the policy terminates. The option is limited to a maximum policy term of 40 years or maturity age 75, whichever is earlier. Returned premiums do not automatically include taxes, rider premiums or every extra charge. Surrender value is available only with the return-of-premium option and is the higher of guaranteed and special surrender value under the contract. Pure protection without this option ordinarily has no maturity benefit. Spouse coverage is joint administration, not identical protection Spouse coverage can be selected at inception for an additional premium when the first life has a basic sum assured of at least ₹50 lakh. The second life receives death cover only, paid as a lump sum. The second-life sum assured can be up to the first-life amount, subject to underwriting; the wording limits cover for a housewife or non-earning female to up to 50% of the first-life basic sum assured and a maximum ₹50 lakh. Terminal-illness, waiver and accelerated critical-illness benefits apply to the first life, not the second. The option is unavailable with Pay till Age 60. Waiting Periods Exclusions and Disclosure Duties Critical-illness benefits carry a 90-day waiting period from policy inception or revival, whichever is later. The policy also lists disease definitions and exclusions for pre-existing disease, intentional self-injury, war, criminal activity, hazardous pursuits and other specified causes. A claim is tested against the exact diagnosis, evidence, timing and exclusions. A general medical description or hospital bill is not enough if the contractual severity definition is not met. Suicide and misstatement rules For suicide within 12 months from commencement of risk or revival, the nominee or beneficiary is entitled to 80% of total premiums paid until death or the surrender value available at death, whichever is higher, provided the policy is in force. The ordinary death sum assured is not payable under that exclusion. Fraud and misstatement are governed by section 45 of the Insurance Act. Accurate disclosure of health, occupation, smoking, income, existing insurance and medical history is therefore central to underwriting and claim assessment. Premium Default Paid Up Status and Revival Without return of premium, failure to pay by the end of the grace period causes lapse. With return of premium, failure before payment of the first full policy-year premium also causes lapse and cessation of cover. After one full year of premiums under the return-of-premium structure, the policy may acquire reduced paid-up benefits according to the wording. A lapsed or paid-up policy may be revived within five years from the first unpaid premium, subject to arrears, interest, evidence and underwriting. Revival also restarts the critical-illness waiting period. Claims Documents and Grievance Escalation The claimant should notify the insurer promptly and submit the claim form, policy details, death or medical evidence, identity, bank information and any additional documents reasonably required. For an accidental event, police, post-mortem or investigation records may be relevant. Nomination helps identify the recipient but does not remove the need to establish the insured event. Copies of the proposal, schedule, medical disclosures, premium receipts, nomination and correspondence should be retained outside the insured person’s sole control. The insurer lists its toll-free number and service email as first-level channels. An unresolved grievance can be escalated to the designated grievance redressal officer and thereafter to Bima Bharosa and the Insurance Ombudsman where the complaint falls within jurisdiction and applicable time limits. A claimant should record dates, complaint numbers, requested relief and documents supplied rather than relying on oral assurances from a salesperson. Income Tax and GST Position for 2026 Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, section 123 read with Schedule XV retains an aggregate deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh for specified savings payments, including qualifying life-insurance premiums, subject to conditions. The deduction is not available under the new concessional regime under section 202. The premium-to-sum-assured conditions and who is insured must be checked. A policy should not be purchased solely for a deduction because the protection need, premium affordability and disclosure obligations continue for the chosen term. Death proceeds remain specially protected under the life-insurance exclusion rules, while non-death receipts are subject to premium-ratio and aggregate-premium conditions. For non-ULIP policies issued on or after 1 April 2023, the ₹5 lakh aggregate-premium condition can affect exemption of non-death receipts; death receipts are treated differently. The return-of-premium feature therefore needs tax review at maturity rather than a blanket assumption of exemption. The current GST framework exempts individual life-insurance policies, including term policies, from GST. A fresh quote should nevertheless be checked for any non-policy service or future law change.
