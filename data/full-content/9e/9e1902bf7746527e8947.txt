Summary : Suryoday Small Finance Bank Limited’s official product information, checked on 14 November 2026, identifies a resident domestic callable fixed deposit with tenures from seven days to ten years and a minimum deposit of ₹1,000 in multiples of ₹1. The bank’s official material shows a five-year rate of up to 8.25% per annum for the general resident category and up to 8.50% for eligible resident senior citizens. Because the live rate table and booking systems can change, the authenticated quote and deposit receipt on the booking date control the actual rate. The product permits traditional periodic-interest and cumulative or reinvestment choices. For premature closure, the published product terms generally reprice the deposit at the booking-date rate applicable to the period actually completed and apply a 1% penalty, subject to the precise product and tenure conditions. Interest is taxable. For Tax Year 2026-27, section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 sets resident bank-interest TDS thresholds of ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen, subject to all statutory conditions. Suryoday is a small finance bank, not an NBFC, and appears within the DICGC insured-bank framework. DICGC covers principal and interest together up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity; several receipts at the same bank do not create separate limits. Product Identity, Amount and Payout Options Resident domestic callable fixed deposit The relevant product is the ordinary resident domestic fixed deposit that permits premature closure under the published terms. It is not a recurring deposit, bond, mutual fund, savings balance or a non-callable bulk deposit. The bank states a minimum deposit of ₹1,000 and increments of ₹1, with a broad tenure range of seven days to ten years. RBI’s current small finance bank deposit directions define a bulk deposit as ₹3 crore and above; a depositor near that amount must obtain the applicable quote because bulk and retail pricing can differ. Traditional payout and cumulative reinvestment A traditional deposit distributes interest at the chosen periodic frequency, while a cumulative or reinvestment deposit retains interest and compounds it under the product terms. Suryoday’s deposit policy says cumulative deposits compound quarterly and monthly interest payouts are discounted relative to the quarterly rate. Consequently, a monthly payout should not be estimated by dividing the annual rate by twelve, and the cumulative maturity value depends on the actual compounding convention and day count. The receipt should identify the payout instruction and nominated account. For deposits of six months or less, the bank’s product material refers to simple interest, while longer cumulative deposits use the stated compounding approach. Renewal is a new contractual event: unless the receipt provides otherwise, maturity proceeds renewed after five years will ordinarily use the rate and terms available on the renewal date. The original 8.25% or 8.50% display should not be projected beyond the contracted maturity. Premature Closure Reprices the Completed Tenure The bank’s published terms state that on early closure, including relevant rebooking or partial-withdrawal situations, interest is recalculated using the rate that applied on the original booking date for the period the money actually remained with the bank, less a 1% premature-withdrawal penalty. The booked five-year headline rate is therefore not simply paid up to the closure date. The exact calculation can also reflect prior interest payouts, tax deduction, lien, rounding and product-specific waivers or restrictions. Illustration of the method, not a bank quotation If a five-year deposit is closed after a substantially shorter completed tenure, the bank identifies the booking-date rate for that completed period, applies the published penalty and computes interest for the actual holding period. The result should be obtained from the bank before authorisation. A customer should not use the current rate for the completed period, because the published method refers to the rate prevailing when the deposit was originally booked. Product-specific exceptions should be confirmed in writing rather than inferred from a summary page. TDS is a collection mechanism and does not make interest below the withholding threshold exempt. A cumulative deposit can generate taxable interest or reporting information before cash maturity, depending on the applicable law and method. The depositor should reconcile the bank’s interest certificate, Form 26AS, Annual Information Statement and return rather than reporting only the final maturity credit. Section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 governs specified interest withholding for Tax Year 2026-27. For interest other than interest on securities paid by a banking company, the threshold is ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen and ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen, subject to the section. Official departmental guidance describes the ordinary resident rate as 10% where deduction applies, subject to PAN, the rate in force and other provisions. The threshold concerns aggregate covered interest and should not be tested separately for every branch or fixed-deposit receipt in a core-banking environment. Senior deposit rate and senior tax threshold are separate tests The bank’s senior rate depends on its product eligibility, including age and residence. The tax threshold depends on the statutory definition and tax facts. An enhanced product rate does not itself prove eligibility for a nil-deduction declaration, and an absence of TDS does not eliminate final tax. Any declaration or lower-deduction certificate should be used only when the legal conditions are satisfied and should be retained with acknowledgement. DICGC Cover Applies at the Bank Level Suryoday Small Finance Bank is a bank within the DICGC insured-bank framework. DICGC insures eligible savings, current, recurring and fixed deposits up to ₹5 lakh for principal and interest together per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity. Deposits at all branches of Suryoday held in that capacity are aggregated. Splitting ₹10 lakh into two receipts, using separate branches or selecting different maturities does not create two ₹5 lakh limits. Different rights and capacities and qualifying joint-account combinations are assessed under DICGC rules, but separation should not be assumed merely from a different account number. Accrued interest counts within the ceiling, so principal equal to ₹5 lakh can already exceed the insured amount once interest is added. Insurance does not guarantee the contractual rate, immediate access or protection above the statutory ceiling. It also does not remove concentration, liquidity, tax or documentation considerations.
