Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Navneet Gupta Insolvency Professional Vs IBBI (NCLAT Delhi) Summary: The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, Principal Bench, New Delhi, rejected two applications in Navneet Gupta’s appeal against the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India’s disciplinary order dated 21.08.2026. The disciplinary order, as reproduced in the judgment, suspended his registration for one year, effective after 30 days, and directed the Committees of Creditors of corporate debtors where he served to replace him under Section 27 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code. The present order did not decide the merits of that suspension or its enforceability, which remained for determination in the main appeal. Haravtar Singh Arora sought recall of the order dated 10.09.2026 rejecting his impleadment application. He had sought participation under Order I Rule 8A CPC, claiming an interest arising from Gupta’s appointment as liquidator of James Hotel Limited, of which Arora claimed to be a director, promoter and shareholder. The Tribunal held that this did not make him a necessary party to the professional’s disciplinary appeal. It distinguished participation under Order I Rule 8A from impleadment under Order I Rule 10 and noted that the power of attorney promised in the original application had not been placed on record when that application was decided. The replacement direction was subject to Section 27 and addressed the respective CoCs; Arora was not a member of the CoC concerned. The recall applicant alleged fraud and lack of jurisdiction, concentrating on the reply affidavit being notarised as attested rather than expressly sworn. Reading Part XI of the NCLAT Rules, including Rules 68–72, Form NCLAT-4 and Order XIX Rule 3 CPC, the Tribunal rejected the fraud allegation. It emphasised that the contents of the reply and supporting affidavit were not denied and treated the alleged defect as rectifiable rather than a basis to vitiate a contested decision on merits. It held that a bald allegation of fraud, without establishing deception or an intention to obtain an unlawful benefit, could not support recall or permit review of the earlier decision. An interlocutory order dated 17.02.2026 in Company Appeal (AT) No. 43 of 2026, cited concerning affidavit rectification and counsel’s conduct, was held unrelated and not a precedent or ratio decidendi for the present controversy. The Tribunal also rejected reliance on written submissions filed without prior leave on 21.09.2026, observing that they introduced matters beyond the recall application; submissions bearing the title of the other application could not be borrowed for deciding Arora’s recall plea. It rejected I.A. No. 6679 of 2026 with costs of ₹5 lakh. Ajmair Singh Bhullar’s I.A. No. 6680 of 2026 sought limited impleadment only in the stay application, I.A. No. 6168 of 2026, and relief concerning Gupta’s continued assignments and an allegation of unauthorised withdrawal of around ₹65 crore. The Tribunal did not adjudicate that withdrawal allegation. Applying the principle of dominus litis, it held that Bhullar had no legally sustainable interest requiring his participation and could not seek impleadment solely in a stay application without being a party to the main appeal. Characterising the applications as attempts to stall the appeal, it rejected Bhullar’s application with costs of ₹2 lakh and directed listing on 26.11.2026. Cases Discussed Order dated 17.02.2026 in Company Appeal (AT) No. 43 of 2026 (NCLAT). The recall applicant referred to this interlocutory order concerning rectification of affidavit defects. The Tribunal held that it arose in independent proceedings, had no nexus with the present applications and did not constitute a precedent or ratio decidendi supporting recall. So far as the NCLAT Rules are concerned, the provisions in relation to the affidavit is contained under Part-XI of the NCLAT Rules. Part-XI reads as under:- “67. Title of affidavits.- Every affidavit shall be titled as “Before the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal.” followed by the cause title of the application or other proceeding in which the affidavit is sought to be used. 68. Form and contents of the affidavit.- The affidavit as per Form NCLAT-4 shall conform to the requirements of order XIX, rule 3 of Civil Procedure Code, 1908 (5 of 1908). 69. Persons authorised to attest.- Affidavits shall be sworn or affirmed before an Advocate or Notary, who shall affix his official seal. 70. Affidavits of illiterate, visually challenged persons.- Where an affidavit is sworn or affirmed by any person who appears to be illiterate, visually challenged or unacquainted with the language in which the affidavit is written shall be in Form NCLAT-5, the attestor shall certify that the affidavit was read, explained or translated by him or in his presence to the deponent and that he seemed to understand it, and made his signature or mark in the presence of the attestor. 71. Identification of deponent.- If the deponent is not known to the attestor, his identity shall be testified by a person known to him and the person identifying shall affix his signature in token thereof. 72. Annexures to the affidavit.- (1) Document accompanying an affidavit shall be referred to therein as Annexure number and the attestor shall make the endorsement thereon that this is the document marked putting the Annexure number in the affidavit. (2) The attestor shall sign therein and shall mention the name and his designation.” 21. The learned counsel for the Applicant had restricted his argument from the perspective of Rule 72 of the NCLAT Rules, contending thereof that the affidavit filed in support of the reply/objection, to I.A. No. 6230 of 2026 cannot be read for the purposes of deciding I.A. No. 6230 of 2026 preferred by the Applicant for his impleadment in Company Petition (AT) (Ins.) No. 1612 of 2026. In order to deal with the argument extended by the learned counsel for the Applicant, we will have to read the provision of Part-XI in itself. The form and the nature of the affidavit, as contemplated under Part- XI of the Rules, prescribes that it has to be in accordance with Order XIX Rule 3 of the CPC. The purport of Order XIX Rule 3, which finds place under Rule 68 of the NCLAT Rules, provides as to what and how the contents of the affidavit would be. That was only for the purposes of verification of fact as a deponent and its knowledge, to the deponent, regards to the contents, to make the contents of affidavit judicially acceptable, and attach truthfulness to the contents of the affidavit and not only the format of it. Meaning thereby that the allegation of fraud in the affidavit in itself only becomes relevant when the contents of the supporting affidavit are also argued to be based upon fraud and falsity. Order 19 Rule 3: – “3. Matters to which affidavits shall be confined. — (1) Affidavits shall be confined to such facts as the deponent is able of his own knowledge to prove, except on interlocutory applications, on which statements of his belief may be admitted: provided that the grounds thereof are stated. (2) The costs of every affidavit which shall unnecessarily set forth matters of hearsay or argumentative matter, or copies of or extracts from documents, shall (unless the Court otherwise directs) be paid by the party filing the same.” 22. According to Order XIX Rule 3, as made applicable by Rule 68 of the NCLAT Rules, its only to attach fairness to the knowledge of the contents, which has to be proved to be made acceptable in judicial proceedings by affidavit, and when, in the instant case, the contents of the reply itself are not denied by the Applicant Mr. Haravtar Singh Arora, the affidavit filed in support thereto would be treated to be a rectifiable defect and it would not malign the entire proceeding which already stands decided on merits, which could sustain or permit the recall application preferred by the Appellant seeking recall of the Order dated 10.09.2026, which in itself was a contested and concluded proceedings on merits. 23. Reverting back to the contents of Part-XI of the NCLAT Rules, Rule 72, if it exclusively pertains to “verification of a document” accompanying an affidavit, for which an attestor has to make an endorsement that the documents marked in Annexures are true in their contents. Rule 72 in itself only speaks about an “attestation”, which was a requirement under law, which, in the instant case, stood satisfied by the affidavit of 02.09.2026. Hence, the condition of attestation made by the Notary in the affidavit of 02.09.2026, as provided under Rule 72 of the NCLAT Rules, was satisfied. 24. Even in accordance with prescription of the format, prescribed for the affidavits as per the NCLAT Rules, as contemplated under Rule 68, it provides hereunder: – “Form NCLAT-4 [See Rule 68] IN THE NATIONAL COMPANY LAW APPELLATE TRIBUNAL ______________BENCH Appeal/Petition/ /No. of 20_________ Appellant/Applicant vs Respondent/s AFFIDAVIT I, ……………………………. aged…………………. years, son/daughter/wife of ………… ……………………..(name and occupation of the deponent)………………………………. residing at (Full address) ………………………………….do hereby swear in the name of God/solemnly affirm and state as follows: Para. 1 Para. 2 Para. 3 ……………………………….. Contents of Paragraphs Nos. ………………….are within my personal knowledge and contents of Paragraphs Nos……………are based on information received by me which I believe the same to be true (state the source of information wherever possible and the grounds for belief, if any ). Place : Signature of the Deponent Date : Name in Block Letters No. of corrections on page nos. Identified by: Before me *……………………………………………………………….…. Sworn/solemnly affirmed before me on this the …………………..day of …200…………. Signature (Name and Designation of the Attesting Authority with Seal) *To add endorsement in Form No. when necessary” 25. This format of the affidavit too, if it is read in consonance with Part-XI, Form NCLAT-4, issued under Rule 68, prescribes the format of the affidavit, which has been given hereinabove. The counsel for the Applicant has argued there was no “swearing” in the affidavit filed with the reply by the Appellant in opposition to the Impleadment Application No. 6230 of 2026. This argument is not acceptable, also for the reason that, according to the format of the affidavit given above, under NCLAT Form-4, it does not speak about swearing of an affidavit; it only speaks about attestation of the affidavit. In that eventuality too, the affidavit filed by the Appellant in reply to the impleadment application cannot be falsified to be actuated by fraud to deceive in any manner whatsoever. 26. A provision of law has to be pragmatically read in its totality to lead to its logical objective. The authority of authorization given to a person under Rule 69 of the NCLAT Rules, 2016 gives an option that the affidavit shall be “sworn or affirmed” before an officer or a Notary Public. Since the present affidavit dated 02.09.2026 was an affidavit attested by the Notary, as permitted under Rule 69 of the NCLAT Rules, 2016 it cannot be said that the affidavit dated 02.09.2026 was a fraudulent document or that it was vitiated by its contents too, in the light of the provisions contained under Order XIX Rule 3 of the CPC, as attracted by Rule 68 of the NCLAT Rules. 27. Even in the absence of swearing (though not specifically required), the attestation exclusively in itself could still be permissible to read the affidavit in support of an application, which, in the instant case, was an accepted document by the Applicant to the recall application having contested upon it on merits. More glaringly, when the purpose of the affidavit, as per the language of Part-XI of the NCLAT Rules, 2016 is to provide authenticity to the affidavit and its contents, when the contents themselves are not denied by the Applicant even today in an application for recall preferred even today, the affidavit filed in support thereto cannot itself be rendered to be bad in the eyes of law because no element of fraud exists so far as the necessity of swearing of contents of affidavit, as argued by the learned counsel for the Applicant, is concerned. 28. The argument advanced by the learned counsel for the Applicant regarding the necessity of swearing is not acceptable, once again for the reason that, according to Rules 69 and 70 of NCLAT Rules, 2016 it provides that an affidavit has to be “sworn or affirmed”, which means that it could be either way. The affidavit could be taken and considered in the proceedings, whether it is sworn or affirmed. Here, the use of the word “or” in between the expressions given in Rules 69 and 70 is not to be read as the word “and”. Hence, either swearing of an affidavit or attestation of an affidavit in accordance with Part-XI of the Rules would still be an acceptable attestation for reading a document for the purposes of the proceedings. 29. The reference made to Rule 72 by the learned counsel for the Applicant is absolutely untenable because there is no fraud as such, and the same cannot be accepted by this Tribunal while exercising our powers for consideration of recall application, seeking recall of the Order, which is restricted to the Order in itself, and more particularly when the contents of the reply and the supporting affidavit are not denied by the Applicant’s counsel. 30. As we have already observed, the Appellant before this Tribunal, who is an RP, was as against the Order of suspension of the licence for a period of one year in pursuance of the action taken against him by the Disciplinary Committee. The Applicant, in the capacity of being the shareholder, Director or promoter of a third entity, not party to the proceedings, cannot be said to be at all necessary party in the absence of whom the disciplinary proceedings against the Appellant could not be decided because the Applicants claim would not be falling under or within the ambit of Order I Rule 8A of the CPC, as prayed for by the Applicant. 31. As we have already observed, the principal proceedings as against the Appellant had been in the shape of disciplinary proceedings drawn against him in accordance with Regulation 13 of the IBBI (Inspection and Investigation) Regulations, 2017. It was altogether a different subject that was under consideration in the Company Appeal. Which had no nexus to the stand taken by the Applicant in his impleadment application. 32. The application for intervention/impleadment, though it was bad in law because it was not supported with the valid Power of Attorney, as mentioned in the note clause of the application, which was not supplied even till the date when the Order was passed, was also not maintainable on the said ground. 33. Besides that, since the intervention was being sought by the Applicant because of the fact that it impacts upon the liquidation proceedings drawn as against M/s James Hotel Limited, of which the Applicant claims himself to be the Director, Promoter, shareholder, and which has already been placed under liquidation in a pending proceeding, CP (IB) No. 15 of 2026, Punjab National Bank versus James Hotel, in which the present Appellant has been appointed as a liquidator, it is contended by the Appellant that his rights would also be affected because of the impugned Order, which is the subject matter of challenge in the instant Company Appeal. 34. It is argued by the learned counsel for the Appellant that, because of the observations made in Para 3.3 of the impugned Order, since there happens to be a suspension of the Appellant from carrying out any liquidation proceedings in relation to other entities, it may affect the proceedings of the liquidation of M/s James Hotel Limited. For that purpose, Para 3.3 of the impugned Order has to be read together with the first part of Para 3.3. 35. The copy of the Order suspending the licence of the IRP for a period of two years under Section 220 of the Code, to be read with Regulation 13, does not at all affect any right of the Applicant for the reason being that the direction contained in the second part of Para 3.3 of the Order was that the CoC of the respective CDs shall replace Mr. Navneet Gupta with another Resolution Professional in accordance with Section 27 of the Code. 36. The Applicant is not a member of the CoC for whom the direction was issued as per Para 3.3 of the impugned Order for replacing the Resolution Professional. As such, since the Appellant is not part of the CoC to whom the direction has been issued for removal of the Resolution Professional, the Applicant does not become a necessary party at all to be impleaded what to say about recall of Order rejecting his impleadment. Thus, it has been rightly observed in the Order dated 10.09.2026, the Applicant would not be necessary party whose presence would be at all needed to decide the Company Appeal. 37. Because of the fact that there is no fraud which is apparently established, merely because of the execution of the affidavit, the same is not accepted by this Tribunal. Thus the recall though not permissible cannot be permitted to be ventured on its merits and deserves rejection. 38. The argument in the context of Para 3.3, as raised by the Appellant, that it concerns him also is absolutely unfounded, because it was a general direction that was issued for a restraint as against the Appellant from carrying out any proceedings as an Resolution Professional. However, that is to be exercised subject to certain riders, where the CoC of the respective CDs were supposed to restrain the Appellant from functioning as an IRP or a Resolution Professional in the light of the provisions contained under Section 27 of the Code. 39. This direction given in Para 3.3 was not unfettered, as it was not specifically confined to the Applicant; rather, it was restricted for complying with the provisions contained under Section 27 of the Code. Hence, this in itself too, will not entitle the Applicant to be impleaded, as argued by the counsel for the Applicant, that he becomes a necessary party, to the proceedings of this Company Appeal. 40. The rejection of his impleadment by an Order dated 10.09.2026 is backed by the fact that no aspect of fraud is made out by the Applicant as against the Order dated 10.09.2026, particularly in relation to the reply submitted by the Appellant to the impleadment application. The plea of fraud fails, more particularly when the plea of fraud attempts to enter de novo into the merits of the decision taken on merits, i.e., the Order dated 10.09.2026. The same cannot be exercised when fraud itself is not established. 41. The learned counsel for the Applicant to the recall application has primarily concentrated his argument from two perspectives, the one which we have already dealt with hereinabove, with regards the aspect of fraud. To attract those principles of fraud, it is necessary that the act complained of must satisfy the ingredients required to establish fraud. Merely a projected plea by the counsel for the Applicant alleging bald allegation of fraud, cannot, in itself, lead to a conclusion that it is a fraud effecting merits, and more particularly when it is being generated by professional acumen of the counsel, in order to sustain the application for recall, which otherwise is not permissible under law, so far as it relates to the proceedings under the IBC, where a recall cannot be camouflaged to be adopted as a recourse of review. 42. For filing a recall on the basis of fraud, it is necessary for the Applicant to establish the parameters required to be satisfied to conclude that it is a fraud on fact effecting decision. Fraud, in its normal connotation, is a wrongful and criminal deception. It is intended to result in financial or personal gain. In the instant case, none of the ingredients was intended to be attain either a financial or personal gain by the Appellant herein, in whose favour the Order has been passed on an intervention application preferred by the Applicant, are established. 43. The prime object for fraud is that it has to have an intention, “intention to deceive”, by unjustifiably claiming or being credited with accomplishment or qualities of gain. As we have already discussed above, the pleadings raised in the recall application do not satisfy the aspect of fraud merely on the ground of the clauses contained in the affidavit and the way it has been interpreted by the learned counsel for the Applicant, when he had failed to establish, as to what was the intent to illegally gain the benefit, so far as it related to pressing of the application for intervention. 44. Literally, the fraud, as per the Oxford Dictionary, reads as under:- “wrongful or criminal deception intended to result in financial or personal gain: he was convicted of fraud prosecutions for social security frauds. Person or thing intended to deceive others, typically by unjustifiably claiming or being credited with accomplishments or qualities: mediums exposed as tricksters and frauds. – ORIGIN MIDDLE ENGLISH: from Old French fraude, from Latin fraud, fraud-‘deceit, injury” 45. Even according to the Law Lexicon, it deals with as to what actually the term “fraud” means under law, and it has widely described it that it means that it should aim to deceive in the grant of a benefit in a proceeding or while dealing with any other aspect which monetarily benefits the person who exercises fraud. It cannot be utilized as a general expression for the purposes of every misrepresentation or a concealment, which can constitute misrepresentation but not fraud. 46. The Webster’s Third New International Dictionary has defined “fraud” in the following terms: “It is an act or an omission to act or concealment by which one person obtains an advantage against conscious over another or which equity or public policy forbids as being prejudicial to another.” 47. None of these principles would be attracted in the present case, where the fact of establishment of fraud itself is not made out because of the reasons which we have already considered above. 48. The explanation given therein of fraud in the Oxford Dictionary, if it is read in the context of criminal law, which deals with its finalization upon holding of an offence of fraud, is dealt with under the Indian Penal Code (IPC) under Section 415, which is a criminal Act and which has certain background, which is otherwise motivated by an act of deceiving or dishonestly inducing a person to believe something which is not otherwise true. The element of fraud as contemplated under Section 415 of the IPC, to be read with Section 318 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), which requires a satisfaction before a criminal prosecution is required to be carried for the offence of fraud. Even as per the expression given by the statute under Section 415 of the IPC and Section 318 of the BNS, the entire allegation raised by the Applicant to the recall application does not lead to draw an impeccable inference that the Appellant can at all be saddled with the responsibility of having an intention to cheat or dishonestly inducing to gain a right, and that too, as we have already observed above, the Applicant had been contesting the proceedings before this Tribunal. 49. He had with all sincerity, and overwhelming diligence, had participated in the proceedings while pressing upon his application, which has been decided on merits by the Order dated 10.09.2026, rejecting his impleadment application being I.A. No. 6230 of 2026. When this Tribunal had voluntarily completed the proceedings and the same stood rejected based upon the affidavit filed by the Appellant, the Applicant now cannot, at a subsequent stage, take a malicious somersault and fish out the ground of fraud, which otherwise was also available to him to be pressed at the time when the application I.A. No. 6230 of 2026 was being considered by this Tribunal and was being pressed on merits. Having not done so, it is no more open for the Appellant to argue that the reply filed by the Appellant in response to I.A. No. 6230 of 2026 was actuated by fraud, so as to enable him to sustain his recall application, to have a new enmity to press his application under Order I Rule 8A of CPC, preferred in the shape of impleadment, and that in an Appeal by Appellant against disciplinary proceedings. 50. Because of the fact that the parameters those were required to be satisfied for completing and complaining an act of fraud have not been satisfied, owing to the grounds taken by the Applicant, the plea is not acceptable by us. In the absence of the same, the application for recall, which in fact is being camouflaged as seeking a review, which is not otherwise permissible under law, would not be maintainable and, hence, would deserve rejection. 51. Learned counsel for the Applicant to the recall application has raised a peculiar argument and, it would not be out of context to submit, an irrational ground too. That the conduct of the counsel for Appellant itself was bad and he ought to have taken care of the earlier Order passed by the Tribunal in some other matter, where the counsel was granted an opportunity to rectify the defects in the affidavit. In relation thereto, while elaborating his argument, the learned counsel, particularly while remarking upon the conduct of the counsel for the Appellant in person as an individual, who has filed the reply in opposition to the intervention application, has extracted some Order passed in some other Comp. App. (AT) No. 43 of 2026, as against the counsel, on that basis observing thereof that the Appellant was conscious of the fact that the affidavits would be rectifiable defects and thus, in that eventuality, he made reference to the Order dated 17.02.2026 as passed in Comp. App. (AT) No. 43 of 2026, which has no nexus to the present proceedings of the intervention or the recall application. 52. Recall applicant argues, if that be a rectifiable defect, then the counsel ought to have rectified it himself after seeking an opportunity from the Tribunal. We make it very clear that the Order dated 17.02.2026 is not a precedent or a ratio decidendi because that was an Order, which was passed at an interlocutory stage in Comp. App. (AT) No. 43 of 2026, which was altogether an independent proceeding having no relation to the present Appeal. Since it is not a ratio decided, it cannot be taken as an authority, that the counsel for the Appellant who had filed the reply, was conscious of the fact of the probable expected rectification of defects, which he ought to have carried. The gravamen of argument is more of personnel against counsel, rather than the case itself. 53. This stand taken, though it may be professionally laudable, being against the fraternity and for no good cause, is not acceptable by us and stands turned down. 54. The learned counsel for the Applicant has heavily relied upon the written submissions and the Orders as filed in support thereto. Outrightly, we decline to accept the same because the title head of the said written submissions is in relation to I.A. No. 6680 of 2026, that has been preferred in I.A. No. 6168 of 2026. The proceedings of the application, upon the matter being posted before us, were taken up on 21.09.2026. On 21.09.2026 itself, the Applicant, through his counsel, Mr. Deepak Khosla, without a prior leave of the Tribunal filed the written submissions. Filing of written submissions is not customary, and filing of it does not depend upon the whims and fancies of the party to the proceedings to file the written submissions as per their choice. Filing of written submissions is only when the Court or the Tribunal feels upon conclusion of arguments that its necessary and then when it directs the party to file written submissions, it could be filed. Filing of a written submission has to be with the prior leave of Tribunal, when the Tribunal feels its necessary to facilitate and make the judgment dictation convenient. Because by the time matter is argued counsel has sufficient opportunity to raise all his pleadings. 60. For that purpose, the principles have been enunciated under Order XVIII Rule 3C and 3D of the CPC, which would be applicable, and it is not as a matter of choice of the litigant or his counsel to file written submissions without arguing the matter on merits and even being called by the Court, because written submissions only facilitate the Tribunal when the Tribunal or the Court feels that they are required to be filed. 61. Though we have considered the written submissions as filed by the Applicant’s counsel, on 21.09.2026, which contained pleading and material even much beyond the recall application itself, which in impermissible to be introduced at the stage of filing of written submission. When the application itself was taken up on merits, under law, it was not required to be heard on the basis of written submissions which was attempted by the learned counsel, and that too particularly when it was introducing all the new facts which were not even part and parcel when the arguments were extended in the intervention application while passing of an Order dated 10.09.2026, or even in the recall application. 62. Thus, the plea of the written submissions is declined to be considered, and more particularly when it is actuated by malice, intending to delay the proceedings, and a consequence of professional acumen. 63.
