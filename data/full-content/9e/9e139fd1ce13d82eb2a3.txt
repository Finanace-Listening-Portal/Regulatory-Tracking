Karnataka High Court Quashes Prosecution Against Maruti Suzuki Directors Introduction Directors cannot be prosecuted for an alleged offence committed by a company merely because they occupy positions on its Board. The statutory requirements for fastening vicarious criminal liability must first be satisfied. Reaffirming this principle, the Karnataka High Court in Davinder Singh Brar and Others v. State of Karnataka, Criminal Petition No.1663 of 2021, decided on 13 November 2026, quashed proceedings against ten directors of Maruti Suzuki India Limited under the Legal Metrology Act, 2009. Justice H.P. Sandesh held that the prosecution could not proceed when the company itself had not been arraigned as an accused and specific pleadings necessary to attribute liability to the directors were absent. Search at Dealer’s Premises Led to Prosecution The proceedings originated from a search conducted by the Legal Metrology authorities at the premises of R.N.S. Motors Limited, a dealer of Maruti Suzuki India Limited. On 19 May 2016, the authorities seized packets containing seat covers and wax polish. A compounding notice under Section 48 was initially issued to the dealership, identifying Maruti Suzuki as the manufacturer. Correspondence followed in which the alleged contraventions were disputed and withdrawal of the notice was requested. The authorities subsequently issued notices addressed to Maruti Suzuki, including a clarification-cum-compounding notice. Thereafter, a complaint was instituted alleging contravention of Section 18(2), read with Section 36, of the Act. The Magistrate took cognizance on 26 November 2016, and summons were subsequently issued. However, although the authorities had identified Maruti Suzuki as the manufacturer, the company was omitted from the array of accused. Its directors approached the High Court under Section 482 of the Code of Criminal Procedure. Directors Questioned the Foundation of the Complaint The petitioners argued that prosecution of directors for an alleged corporate offence could not survive without prosecuting the company. They relied upon Section 49, which governs offences committed by companies. They also submitted that the complaint lacked clear and precise allegations identifying their respective roles in the alleged contravention. Their position as directors, by itself, could not justify criminal proceedings. On the alleged packaging violations, the petitioners contended that there was no misdeclaration or discrepancy between the commodities and the declarations on their packages. They further relied upon an earlier Karnataka High Court ruling concerning the use of labels to display prescribed information. State Described the Omission as Curable The State did not dispute that Maruti Suzuki had not been made an accused. It nevertheless argued that non-impleadment of the company was a curable defect. It also maintained that the objections concerning the products and their labelling could not justify interference at the stage of a petition under Section 482. According to the State, the Magistrate’s order taking cognizance did not warrant quashing. The High Court rejected the contention that the proceedings against the directors could continue despite the omission of the company. Section 49 Requires the Statutory Conditions to Be Met Section 49(1) provides for liability of the company together with the nominated responsible person or, where no nomination exists, persons who were in charge of and responsible for conducting its business at the relevant time. The provision therefore requires attention to the statutory basis on which an individual is sought to be prosecuted. indiacode.nic.in Applying this framework, the High Court held that the company ought to have been made an accused. Naming it as the manufacturer in notices and correspondence did not satisfy that requirement in the criminal complaint. The Court further emphasised that specific pleadings against the directors were necessary. Without the requisite allegations, vicarious liability could not be imposed merely on account of their designation. Supreme Court Decisions Relied Upon The principal authority was Aneeta Hada v. Godfather Travels & Tours (P) Ltd., (2012) 5 SCC 661, concerning the necessity of arraigning the company when invoking statutory vicarious criminal liability. The petitioners also relied upon Sharad Sanghi v. Sangita Rane, (2015) 12 SCC 781, and Ajit Balse v. Ranga Karkere, (2015) 15 SCC 748. The High Court additionally invoked Pepsi Foods Ltd. v. Special Judicial Magistrate, (1998) 5 SCC 749, while holding that criminal prosecution without supporting material could not continue. On labelling, the Court referred to W.P.No.4502/2020, decided on 1 December 2020, which recognised that affixing labels containing variable information would not, in the circumstances discussed, violate the specified packaging rules when the labels were present at sale. Proceedings Quashed The High Court allowed the petition and quashed the cognizance order, the summons issued against the petitioners and the complaint. Continuing the proceedings would constitute an abuse of the process of law, while refusing intervention would result in a miscarriage of justice. Author’s Comments The ruling highlights the distinction between identifying a company during investigation and prosecuting it through a properly constituted complaint. Notices addressed to the manufacturer cannot compensate for its omission as an accused. Equally, criminal liability demands scrutiny of the statutory provision, the individual’s role and the allegations actually pleaded. The judgment should not be understood as granting directors blanket immunity or permitting every form of sticker labelling. Its central message is that corporate prosecution must satisfy the statutory foundations before individuals are compelled to face criminal proceedings. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT Heard learned Senior counsel appearing for the petitioners and the learned High Court Government Pleader appearing for the respondent. 2. This petition is filed under Section 482 of Cr.P.C. praying this Court to quash the impugned order dated 26.11.2016 passed by the learned Metropolitan Magistrate, Traffic Court-1, Mayo Hall, Bangalore in C.C.No.36292/2016, wherein impugned summons dated 16.10.2020 was issued and so also, the complaint dated 15.10.2016 filed on 15.11.2016 under Section 200 of Cr.P.C. and pass such other order. 3. The factual matrix of case of complainant- respondent is that respondent conducted a search at the premise of R.N.S. Motors Limited, a dealership of Maruti Suzuki India Limited (“Maruti”). Thereafter, respondent sent a compounding notice under Section 48 of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 (“the Act” for short) to R.N.S. Motors Limited, the dealership of Maruti, naming Maruti as the manufacturer. Consequently, the petitioners with a letter stating that there has been no violation of the Act, requested the respondent to withdraw the notice. The respondent sent a compounding notice addressed to the Maruti again naming the latter as the manufacturer and respondent sent a clarification-cum- compounding notice addressed to Maruti reiterating its stand regarding the alleged violation of the Rules and the Act. Thereafter, respondent filed the impugned complaint before the learned Magistrate and issued process. Hence, the present petition is filed challenging the impugned order taking cognizance. 4. The petitioners in the petition mainly contend that complaint is filed without arraigning the company i.e., Maruti as an accused, despite describing Maruti as the manufacturer in the initial compounding notice and correspondence and proceeded against these petitioners and in the absence of company, the Directors in their capacity cannot be prosecuted. In support of this contention, the learned Senior counsel relied upon judgment of the Apex Court in ANEETA HADA vs. GODFATHER TRAVELS reported in (2012) 5 SCC 661 and subsequently, reaffirmed in SHARAD SANGHI vs. SANGITA RANE reported in (2015) 12 SCC 781 and AJIT BALSE vs. RANGA KARKERE reported in (2015) 15 SCC 748. It is contended that there cannot be invoking Section 36 of the Act against these petitioners and this Court has also interpreted Section 36 of the Act and held that alleged violation of Rules, simplicitor do not constitute the offence envisaged under Section 36 of the Act. 5. In the present case, there is no allegation of any mis-declaration or there being any discrepancy between the commodities inside and the declarations on the package. Further, Section 18 of the Act is not a penal offence and penal liability, if any must be under Section 36 of the Act and the same has not been made out. The Apex Court has repeatedly laid down that, in order to proceed against individuals vicariously, it is necessary to have clear and precise averments regarding their role, both in the company and the alleged offence. 6. The learned Senior counsel for the petitioners also in support of his argument relied upon Annexure-A i.e., the proceedings initiated against the petitioners i.e., the order sheet, Annexure-B i.e., copy of complaint, Annexure-C-Form No.1, Annexure-D i.e., violation notice, Annexure-E- Panchanama, Annexure-F-notice dated 19.05.2016, Annexure- G-Certificate of Registration, Annexure-H-letter dated 13.06.2016, Annexure-J-notice dated 14.06.2026, Annexure-K- clarification-cum-compunding notice and Annexure-L-copy of the appeal preferred by Maruthi. The counsel referring these Annexures and the principles laid down in the judgments of the Apex Court contends that Section 49 of the Act mandates company ought to be made a party in a complaint. 7. Learned Senior counsel appearing for the petitioners though filed list of citations, apart from the judgment of Apex Court in Aneeta Hada’s case, the counsel brought to notice of this Court order passed in W.P.No.4502/2020 dated 01.12.2020 and brought to notice of this Court paragraph No.14.16, wherein discussion was made with regard to holding that affixation of a label containing some of the variable information would not amount to an offence and/or violation of Rule 6(1)(a), Rule 6(1)(b) and Rule 6(1)(aa) of the Legal Metrology (Package Commodities) Rules, 2011 so long as the label is present on the package at the time of sale, since the same allegation is made against the petitioners in the case on hand. 8. Per contra, the learned High Court Government Pleader for the respondent-State would contend that non- arraigning the company as party to the proceedings is a curable defect. The product contains all the details while packing and the same cannot be a defence to be considered in a petition under Section 482 of Cr.P.C. It is contended that it is not labelled and it is not a genuine product and the same is confiscated by conducting a search and it is an offence. Hence, there cannot be any quashment of the order passed by the learned trial Judge and the learned trial Judge has not committed any error while passing the order taking cognizance. 9. In reply to this argument of the learned High Court Government Pleader for the respondent-State, learned Senior counsel appearing for the petitioners would vehemently contend that Directors are before the Court and not even R.N.S. Motors Limited and company is not made as a party and there is a foundational defect. 10. Having heard learned Senior counsel appearing for the petitioners and also the learned High Court Government Pleader appearing for the respondent-State, the points that would arise for consideration of this Court are: (i) Whether the prosecution made against the petitioners are sustainable in the eye of law and whether this Court can exercise the power under Section 482 of Cr.P.C. to quash the proceedings? (ii) What order? Point No.(i): 11. The first and foremost contention before the Court is that Marathi Suzuki India Limited is the manufacturer of seat covers and wax polish. No doubt, on 19.05.2016, respondent seized two packets each containing seat cover and wax polish from the premises of R.N.S. Motors Limited, admittedly, R.N.S. Motors Limited is a dealer of Maruti Suzuki India Limited as per Annexure-F, which is filed along with this petition. 12. No doubt, notice was issued by the respondent vide Annexure-J dated 14.06.2016, it is specifically stated that manufacturer of seat cover and wax polish is Maruti Suzuki India Limited and it is categorically stated that manufacturer of seat cover and wax polish is Maruti Suzuki India Limited. Thereafter, clarification-cum-compounding notice was also issued on 14.08.2016 as per Annexure-K and the respondent- State filed the complaint invoking Section 18(2) read with Section 36 of the Act. It is also not in dispute that learned Magistrate has taken cognizance and issued summons. 13. But, the main contention is that Maruti Suzuki India Limited is not made as a party to the proceedings, but Directors are made as parties. The learned Senior counsel appearing for the petitioners would vehemently contend that Section 49(1) of the Act mandates company ought to be made as a party in a complaint. The learned High Court Government Pleader for the respondent-State also not disputes the fact that company has not been arraigned as accused, but only contented that it is a curable defect. 14. Having perused the principles laid down in the judgment of Aneeta Hada’s case and the other judgments which have been relied upon by learned Senior counsel appearing for the petitioners, it is very clear that, if the company is not made as a party, there cannot be criminal prosecution and the same is required to be quashed. So also, in the order passed by this Court in W.P.No.4502/2020 dated 01.12.2020, this Court has held that individual stickers being affixed, does not violate the Act or the Rules, as referred supra. When such being the case, in the absence of company being made as party to the proceedings and unless the company is arraigned as accused, the Directors cannot be held responsible and there cannot be any liability on the individuals, who are working as Directors and there must be specific pleading against them and without any pleading also, there cannot be any vicarious liability. 15. Under the circumstances, I am of the opinion that the grounds which have been urged in the petition substantiate the contention of the petitioners. Hence, the petitioners have made out the ground to invoke the provisions under Section 482 of Cr.P.C. to quash the proceedings, in keeping Section 49(1) of the which mandates company ought to be made as a party in a complaint. Admittedly, in the complaint which is produced along with the petition, the company is not made as an accused and hence, there cannot be any criminal prosecution against the petitioners. If this Court fails to exercise the power under Section 482 of Cr.P.C., it amounts to miscarriage of justice. 16. The Apex Court also in the judgment in PEPSI FOODS LTD. vs. SPECIAL JUDICIAL MAGISTRATE reported in (1998) 5 SCC 749 also held that, if there is no material, there cannot be any criminal prosecution and therefore, continuing the proceedings against the petitioners herein amounts to an abuse of process of law. Hence, I answer point No.(i) accordingly. Point No.(ii): 17. In view of the discussion made above, I pass the following: ORDER (i) The criminal petition is allowed. (ii) The impugned order dated 26.11.2016 passed by the learned Metropolitan Magistrate, Traffic Court-1, Mayo Hall, Bangalore in C.C.No.36292/2016, wherein impugned summons dated 16.10.2020 was issued against the petitioners and so also, the complaint dated 15.10.2016 filed on 15.11.2016, is hereby quashed.
