Articles 2 min read RBI likely to issue FAQ soon to clarify ECL implementation; some provisioning matter still under review The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is set to issue a frequently asked questions (FAQ) document soon to clarify the implementation of the Expected Credit Loss (ECL) framework, following concerns raised by banks over the treatment of certain loan categories and provisioning requirements, bankers have told ETBFSI. The regulator held a meeting with bank CEOs and board directors on November 12 to discuss implementation preparedness and strengthen board-level oversight. Advt Banks flag gaps in ECL implementation, seek RBI clarity on existing provisions, loan classificationThe Reserve Bank of India is set to meet banks on November 12 to discuss the implementation of the expected credit loss (ECL) framework, with lenders looking to flag concerns over the treatment of excess provisions held under existing norms, crop-cycle-based agricultural loans and fair valuation of investment portfolios. Banks are seeking clarity on these issues as they assess the provisioning impact of the transition.The RBI held a meeting with bank CEOs and board directors on November 12 to discuss ECL implementation and familiarise board members with the proposed provisioning framework."It was a preparatory meeting, an orientation for the board of directors to have complete oversight of how ECL is being worked out, the implementation requirements and provisioning. The objective was to give the boards an in-depth understanding of how the banks are preparing for implementation and what they need to take up themselves," the official said.The official said the regulator had provided clarity on several aspects of the framework, while a few specific issues were still being examined."On the majority of the issues, things are finalised. The RBI and banks are more or less aligned. There are only five-six areas which are still under consideration, and the RBI will come back on those," the official said. Advt These include the treatment of certain government-sponsored accounts, partial government-guaranteed exposures, staff loans and agricultural loans."For instance, if a bank lends Rs 1,000 crore to an entity with a Central Government guarantee, no provisioning is required. However, there is some confusion regarding counter-guarantees under government-backed schemes, where only a portion of the exposure is guaranteed," the banker quoted above said."If 60-65 per cent of an exposure is guaranteed, the remaining portion is unsecured. These aspects are still under examination, and the RBI will provide clarity," the official added. Advt Staff loans and government-sponsored accounts are also among the areas where banks are awaiting further guidance.Board oversight to become crucialThe RBI has emphasised the importance of board-level oversight under the new framework, with individual bank boards expected to take responsibility for the assumptions and estimates used in determining expected credit losses."The board will have to convince itself about how the provisioning is being arrived at. The respective boards will be responsible," said another public sector banker ETBFSI spoke to.Under the ECL framework, banks will have to estimate potential future credit losses based on the probability of default, instead of waiting for an account to turn non-performing before making provisions. Advt The official said the RBI had not provided a specific timeline for resolving the outstanding issues.The ECL framework is scheduled to come into effect from April 1, 2027. Banks will use their loan book as of March 31, 2027, as the base for calculating provisions, with the impact reflected in their first-quarter financial results for FY28.
