The financial impact would therefore depend on how banks reorganise staff, branch operations and essential weekend functions if the proposal is eventually approved.The Indian Banks' Association has now constituted a high-level committee with bank unions to examine implementation of a five-day banking week and declaring the remaining Saturdays as holidays. The committee will also examine alternatives and consult stakeholders, including customers. No decision to introduce a five-day week has been taken and banks continue to remain closed on the second and fourth Saturdays while operating on the other Saturdays. Advt The most direct potential saving would come from expenses linked to keeping branches and offices operational on Saturdays. These include electricity, air conditioning, lighting, generators, transport, housekeeping, security and other consumables. Closing on additional Saturdays could therefore reduce some variable operating costs, although there is no publicly established industry-wide estimate of the rupee savings from such a move.The saving would be limited by the fact that salaries and other fixed expenses would continue. State Bank of India's FY25 financial highlights show staff expenses of Rs 64,352 crore and overhead expenses of Rs 53,717 crore, against total operating expenses of Rs 1.18 lakh crore. Employee costs alone therefore account for a substantial part of a bank's operating expenditure, and a change in the number of branch operating days would not by itself eliminate these expenses.The cost impactThe cost impact would also depend on which banking activities could actually stop on Saturdays. Customer-facing branches could close, but banks would still need coverage for functions such as technology operations, cybersecurity, fraud monitoring, data centres, treasury and other activities that cannot simply be suspended over a weekend.Digital banking provides banks with greater scope to reduce the dependence on Saturday branch operations. NPCI data show that UPI processed 24,508.96 million transactions worth Rs 29.82 lakh crore in August 2026. ATMs, mobile banking, internet banking and other digital channels also continue to operate when branches are closed. Advt The shift to digital channels, however, does not eliminate the need for physical branches. Cash transactions, KYC, documentation, loan-related services and other assisted banking activities can still require branch access. The importance of Saturday operations can also vary across locations and customer groups.The existing Saturday arrangement itself dates back to September 2015, when the second and fourth Saturdays were made holidays for banks. Banks currently operate on the first, third and fifth Saturdays where applicable.The five-day proposal therefore does not amount to a straightforward reduction in the cost of running banks. Its immediate financial benefit would be concentrated in variable branch and office expenses, while employee costs, technology expenditure, premises costs and the expense of maintaining critical operations would largely remain. Advt The impact on productivity would depend on how the additional Saturday holidays are accommodated during the rest of the week. The committee examining the proposal has been asked to consider alternatives as well as the interests of customers, leaving the eventual operating model open.For banks, the key financial question is consequently not simply how many Saturdays are closed but how much operating expenditure can be removed without reducing business capacity or requiring equivalent resources to be added elsewhere. Until the committee's discussions produce a specific operating model, any industry-wide estimate of the cost savings would remain speculative.
