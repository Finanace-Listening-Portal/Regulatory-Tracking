SEO Title: Reuse of Old Registered Office Photograph in MGT-7 and MGT-7A Summary: From 14 July 2025, companies filing annual returns in Form MGT-7 or MGT-7A must attach a photograph of their registered office showing the external building and the company’s name prominently visible, along with the prescribed location particulars. This requirement was introduced through the Companies (Management and Administration) Amendment Rules, 2025, notified by G.S.R. 358(E) dated 30 May 2025. A practical question arises whether the photograph submitted with the previous year’s annual return may be reused where the registered office, building and name board remain unchanged. The author examines Sections 12 and 92 of the Companies Act, 2013, the amended annual return forms and the purpose of photographic verification. The article distinguishes between photographs carrying an old date or time stamp, unstamped photographs accurately reflecting the existing premises, and photographs showing premises or signage that have subsequently changed. The author considers reuse potentially acceptable where an unstamped photograph remains accurate, while recommending a fresh photograph whenever changes or doubts exist. However, MCA’s published filing FAQs specifically refer to the latest photograph of the registered office, making fresh photography the safer compliance approach. The article also discusses the importance of accurate geographical details, visible company signage and potential penalties for non-compliance with the registered office and annual return provisions. In the absence of a specific judicial determination on annual reuse, the practical recommendation is to obtain a current photograph before each filing. Section 92(1) and Rule 11 of the Companies (Management and Administration) Rules, 2014: the annual return is filed in Form MGT-7, or in Form MGT-7A by a One Person Company and a small company. G.S.R. 358(E) dated 30 May 2025 (in force from 14 July 2025): this substituted Forms MGT-7 and MGT-7A and added the photograph attachment. Section 92(5) and Section 12(8): these carry the penalties for a defective annual return and for default in displaying the name and address. Relevant Extracts from MGT-7 and MGT-7A Requirements In simple words, the forms ask for a photograph of the registered office showing the external building, with the name of the company prominently visible, along with the latitude and longitude of the office. The MCA has also reminded filers that the photograph must show the outside of the building with the company name clearly visible, linking it to Section 12(3)(a). The words “fresh” or “current year” appear only in professional commentary, not in the form. Legal Position on Reusing Registered Office Photographs Neither the Act, the Rules nor the form says that a new photograph must be taken every year, and none of them bars the reuse of an old one. The form is, however, a statement made on the date of filing. The photograph is the evidence that a real office exists at the registered address and carries the company’s name board, as Section 12 requires. Therefore, what matters is not the age of the file but whether it still tells the truth about today. This gives three situations: Position of last year’s photograph Can it be reused? It shows a date, time or other stamp of last year No. Take a new photograph. No stamp; office, building and board unchanged and matching today’s view Yes, it can be reused. Address, name board, signage or building has changed in any way No. Take a new photograph. Practical Interpretation and Compliance Precautions The author opines that the safest course is to click a fresh photograph before every filing. It takes a few minutes, and the details embedded in a phone photograph also stay consistent with the coordinates entered in the form. Before reusing an old photograph, check its properties for any date or location data, and compare it with the office as it stands today. The name board must show the full name of the company and the registered office address in clear letters. Note that an incomplete or defective return can attract Section 92(5), and a defective name board can attract Section 12(8); the penalty amounts have been revised by recent decriminalisation amendments, so verify them against the current Act. No NCLT or court ruling on this point was found, and the MCA has issued no formal clarification, so the position is not judicially settled. Examples of Reusing Old Photographs in Annual Returns ABC Private Limited filed its return last year with a photograph that carries last year’s date. Its office and board are unchanged. A new photograph is better here, since the old date will show on this year’s filing. XYZ Private Limited used a plain photograph with no stamp, and nothing has changed at its office. XYZ can use the same photograph. Conclusion: Fresh Photograph Is the Safer Compliance Approach The author opines that the intent of the law is to show the registered office as it stands on the date of filing. Therefore, an old photograph with a visible stamp should not be reused, and a plain, unchanged photograph that matches the office exactly can be reused.
