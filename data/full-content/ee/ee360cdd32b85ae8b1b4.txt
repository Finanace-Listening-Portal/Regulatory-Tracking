(Calcutta High Court) Summary: The Calcutta High Court, in Debasish Dutta Vs State of West Bengal & Ors., quashed an ex parte GST adjudication order dated 18 April 2024 and consequential recovery notices after finding that the show cause notice and adjudication order had been uploaded only under the “Additional Notices and Orders” tab of the GST portal, depriving the petitioner of knowledge of the proceedings and violating the principles of natural justice. The writ petition concerned the period April 2018 to March 2019 and challenged an adjudication order passed under Section 73 of the West Bengal Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, along with recovery notices dated 6 March 2025 and 27 February 2026. The petitioner submitted that a show cause notice in Form GST DRC-01 dated 30 November 2023 had been uploaded under the Additional Notices and Orders tab, while the personal hearing date was mentioned as “N.A.”. Subsequently, an ex parte adjudication order was passed on 18 April 2024 and uploaded under the same tab without the petitioner becoming aware of the order. The petitioner further submitted that the authorities had already recovered ₹2,36,299 from the Electronic Credit Ledger on 11 March 2026, representing 38.69% of the disputed tax, and requested that the adjudication proceedings be reconsidered after granting an opportunity of hearing. On behalf of the State, it was submitted that ₹2,36,299 had been recovered towards tax alone, without any recovery towards interest or penalty. After considering the submissions and materials on record, the High Court held that the petitioner had established a prima facie case warranting interference. The Court specifically observed that uploading the show cause notice and adjudication order only under the Additional Notices and Orders tab had deprived the petitioner of knowledge of the proceedings, constituting a violation of natural justice. Considering that 38.69% of the disputed tax had already been recovered, the Court found that the interests of justice required granting the petitioner an opportunity of hearing. It also rejected the State’s contention that the proceedings had attained finality, having regard to the procedural irregularities. Accordingly, the High Court quashed the adjudication order dated 18 April 2024 and consequential recovery notices and directed respondent No. 4 to reconsider the matter, afford the petitioner a personal hearing, and pass a reasoned and speaking order in accordance with law. The Court clarified that it had not examined the merits of the tax dispute and that the adjudicating authority would remain free to decide the matter independently. Although the original order dated 6 October 2026 specified 30 September 2026 as the deadline for completing fresh adjudication, the subsequent correction order dated 12 October 2026 substituted 30 November 2026 as the operative deadline, leaving the remaining directions unchanged. Appellant Was Represented by: Mr. Himangshu Kumar Ray Mr. Saptak Sanyal Mr. Subhasis Podder Ms. Shiwani Shaw FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CALCUTTA HIGH COURT 1. Affidavit-of-service filed in Court today is taken on record. 2. None appears on behalf of the State-respondents in Court today. 3. Ms. Manasi Mukherjee, learned Junior Government Advocate with Mr. Bijitesh Mukherjee, learned Junior Government Advocate are engaged in this matter on behalf of the State. Their appearances be regularized accordingly from the office of the Legal Remembrancer. 4. The present Writ Petition has been filed challenging inter alia, the legality and the validity of the Adjudication Order dated 18.04.2024 passed by the respondent No. 4 under Section 73 of the West Bengal Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (hereinafter referred to as the said Act for the sake of brevity and convenience) for the period from April, 2018 to March 2019. The petitioner also challenges the two recovery notices dated 06.03.2025 and 27.02.2026. 5. The Learned Counsel appearing for the petitioner submits as follows: A) That a notice in Form GST DRC-01 dated 30.11.2023 has been issued to the registered tax payer requiring the petitioner to show cause as to why he should not pay the amount specified in the said notice along with interest and penalty. B) That the said intimation has been uploaded on the GST portal under the Tab “Additional Notices and Orders”. C) That the date of the personal hearing mentioned in the Show Cause Notice as “N.A.”. D) That an ex parte adjudication order dated 18.04.2024, passed under Section 73 of the WBGST Act, 2017 has been passed against the petitioner by the respondent No. 4. E) Although the order of adjudication has been uploaded on the GST portal under the tab “Additional Notice and Orders”, the petitioner has not been made aware of the order passed by the adjudicating authority. F) That the respondent authority has already recovered the tax amounting to Rs 2,36,299/- from the Electronic Credit Ledger, on 11.03.2026. G) The petitioner prays for a reconsideration of the order of adjudication since the 38.69% of disputed tax has been recovered, and the interest of the revenue will not be prejudiced thereby. H) The petitioner also prays that the matter to be remanded back to the adjudicating authority for fresh consideration of the issues involved therein, and also to set aside the two recovery notices both dated 06.03.2025 and 27.02.2026. 6. The Learned Counsel appearing for the State respondent submits that the amount of Rs.2,36,299/- has been recovered on account of tax from the electronic credit ledger without any recovery being made towards interest and penalty. 7. Having heard the parties and upon the considering the materials available on record, this court finds that the petitioner has been able to make out a prima facie case warranting interference at this stage. 8. The court also noted that the Show Cause Notice as well as the Adjudication Order has been uploaded only under the Tab “Additional Notices and Orders”, resulted in the petitioner being deprived of knowledge of the proceedings, which constitutes a violation of principles of natural justice. 9. Since the 38.69% of disputed tax has already been recovered, the ends of justice would be made if an opportunity of hearing is granted to the petitioner. 10. The contention of the State that the matter has attained finality, cannot be accepted in light of the procedural irregularities noted above. 11. In view of the above, this court passes the following directions. a) The order passed by the adjudicating authority dated 18.04.2024 along with the consequential notices are hereby quashed and set aside, since the petitioner cannot be held liable under adjudication order passed ex parte. b) The respondent No. 4 is directed to reconsider the case of the petitioner and pass a reasoned and speaking order in accordance with law upon affording an opportunity of personal hearing to the petitioner. The decision so arrived at shall be communicated to the petitioner forthwith. c) The entire exercise shall be completed by the respondent No. 4 on or before 30th September, 2026. The petitioner shall cooperate with the respondent No. 4 and shall not seek any further adjournment. 12. It is made clear since this court has not gone into the merits of the case, the respondent No. 4 shall not be influenced by any observation made in this order and is at liberty to take an independent decision to consider the issue involved herein. 13. With the above observations and directions, the Writ petition is disposed of since affidavit has not been called for. 14. Urgent Photostat certified copy of this order, if applied for, be supplied to the parties on priority basis upon compliance of all requisite formalities. 1. The matter has appeared in the list under the heading ‘To Be mentioned’ for correction of the order dated 6th October, 2026. 2. In the second line of sub-paragraph c of paragraph 11 at page 4, the sentence shall read as ‘on or before 30th November 2026’ in place and stead of ‘on or before 30th September 2026’. 3. The order dated 6th October, 2026 is corrected accordingly. The remaining part of the said order will remain unaltered. 4. Let this order be read along with the order dated 6th October, 2026. 5. Urgent photostat certified copy of this order be supplied to the parties, if applied for, as early as possible.
