Summary : South Eastern Coalfields Limited filed a Draft Red Herring Prospectus dated 1 November 2026, which SEBI displayed on 4 November 2026. The proposal is an offer for sale of up to 668,056,100 equity shares of face value ₹2 each by promoter Coal India Limited. It contains no fresh issue, so the company will not receive offer proceeds. The DRHP leaves the price band, rupee offer size, lot size and bidding dates blank. This article explains the disclosed business scale, financial figures, customer and regulatory risks and what investors can and cannot conclude at the draft stage. It contains no GMP, valuation prediction or investment recommendation. The draft abridged prospectus identifies the promoters as the President of India acting through the Ministry of Coal and Coal India Limited. Coal India is also the sole promoter selling shareholder. The proposed listing is on BSE and NSE, but the designated stock exchange remains blank at this stage. A DRHP records a proposal and disclosures for regulatory review; it is not an invitation with final commercial terms and it does not mean SEBI has approved the investment. The offer’s stated objects are to carry out the sale and obtain listing benefits, including visibility, liquidity and a public market. South Eastern Coalfields will not receive the offer proceeds. Net proceeds, after the selling shareholder’s offer expenses and relevant taxes, go to Coal India. Investors should therefore avoid describing the proposal as capital raising for new mines, debt repayment or expansion unless a later document introduces a fresh issue, which the present DRHP does not. Promoter ownership and post-offer dilution Coal India held 6,680,561,000 shares representing effectively the entire pre-offer capital, with nominee holdings included in the disclosed total. Selling the maximum proposed shares would represent about 10% of that pre-offer share count. The exact post-offer holding table remains subject to the final price band, offer structure, allotment and other completed fields. The selling shareholder’s weighted average acquisition cost is disclosed as ₹1.02 per share after adjustment for the share subdivision; this historical cost is not an estimate of fair value or likely listing price. Mining Scale Customers and Operating Footprint The draft abridged prospectus describes South Eastern Coalfields as one of India’s largest coal producers by Fiscal 2026 production. It reports 176.29 million tonnes of coal production, equal to 16.94% of India’s production for that year according to the cited CRISIL report. As of 30 September 2026, it operated 53 producing mines: 19 opencast and 34 underground mines across the Central India, Korba and Mand-Raigarh coalfields. It sells mainly unwashed non-coking coal across grades G3 to G16. Power-sector dependence and customer concentration Customers include central and state power generators, independent power producers, captive plants and non-power industries such as aluminium, cement, sponge iron and fertiliser. The top five customers contributed 35.88% of revenue from operations in the six months ended 30 September 2026, compared with 36.38% in the corresponding prior period. This concentration makes fuel-supply arrangements, customer demand, receivables and dispatch infrastructure material to cash flow. A dispute or reduced offtake by a major customer can affect results even if national coal demand remains substantial. Any eventual price-to-earnings or price-to-book analysis must use the final price band and a consistent share base; neither a price band nor an implied valuation is available in the DRHP. Principal Risks Disclosed at Draft Stage Fuel supply agreements and concentrated customers A significant portion of business is generated under fuel supply agreements and memoranda of understanding. Expiry, termination, suspension or adverse renewal can reduce revenue and disrupt planning. Dependence on a limited number of large power-sector customers also creates collection, dispatch and demand risk. The company may have limited ability to replace a major customer quickly with equivalent volume and commercial terms. Land acquisition mining rights and reserve replacement Coal mining requires land, statutory rights and access to reserves. The DRHP highlights litigation and court decisions affecting land-acquisition rights, delays in possession or compensation and the risk of not acquiring or sustaining adequate reserves. These matters can increase project costs, create rehabilitation liabilities, delay mine development and reduce production. Stated resources do not themselves guarantee economically recoverable coal, timely permissions or uninterrupted operations. Energy transition and environmental obligations Most revenue comes from coal sold to the power sector. Expansion of renewable capacity, changes in generation mix, carbon or environmental regulation and customer decarbonisation plans can affect long-term demand and capital needs. Mining also carries safety, rehabilitation, water, land, pollution and mine-closure obligations. A government-owned promoter does not remove operating, environmental or litigation risk, and policy support can change. Operational and logistics exposure Production depends on mine development, machinery, contractors, labour, power, blasting, geological conditions and safety performance. Dispatch depends on railways, merry-go-round systems, conveyors and roads. Disruption at a mine or evacuation corridor can reduce saleable volume even when orders exist. Underground and opencast operations have different cost and safety profiles, while coal quality variation can create grade disputes or adjustments. Application Process Is Not Yet Open The DRHP displays placeholders for the anchor date, opening date, closing date, floor price and cap price. There is therefore no official application window, minimum bid lot or payable amount to calculate. An investor should wait for the RHP, price-band advertisement and exchange notices. The registrar and book running lead managers are disclosed, but their appointment does not convert the draft filing into an open offer. Any grey-market premium or unofficial date circulating before official documents is outside the verified record and is not used here. Tax and Application Records After Final Terms If the offer proceeds to bidding, applicants generally use ASBA or permitted UPI processes through recognised intermediaries under the final offer documents. Bank-blocking, mandate timing, category selection, PAN, demat details and beneficiary ownership must match. Allotment and refunds follow the final basis of allotment and regulatory timetable. After listing, gains or losses on sale are governed by the capital-gains provisions applicable to listed equity shares, including holding period, securities transaction tax conditions and the taxpayer’s facts.
