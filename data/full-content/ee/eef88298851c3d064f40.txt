The clarification is significant because Tata Trusts Chairman Noel Tata, who is opposing listing of Tata Sons, had earlier maintained that the central bank had not specifically mentioned listing in its correspondence with the company. The latest communication leaves little room for ambiguity about the regulator’s position. It also comes amid an intensifying debate within the Tata Group over whether Tata Sons should remain privately held or prepare for a public listing. The RBI’s position is consistent with the remarks made by Governor Sanjay Malhotra at a media summit in October. While declining to comment on the Tata Sons case specifically, Malhotra had explained the regulatory principle governing large holding companies. “As long as it’s an NBFC within our definition, it’s a core investment company (CIC) and has access to funds, direct or indirect, it’s required to be registered. And if it is in the upper layer, then it is required to list,” he had said. The Governor also explained why such entities fall within the RBI’s regulatory ambit. CICs sit at the top of corporate groups and can raise or access funds through their group structures, creating potential systemic risks. The regulatory framework is intended to ensure that such risks are appropriately monitored and controlled.Story continues below this ad NBFC classification, the listing question Under RBI’s Scale Based Regulation framework, NBFCs are classified into four layers — Base, Middle, Upper and Top — depending on their size, activities and perceived risk. Core Investment Companies can be placed in the Middle or Upper Layer, depending on the applicable criteria. Those identified in the Upper Layer are subject to enhanced regulatory requirements, including listing. Tata Sons has been classified as an Upper Layer NBFC since 2022. The company had applied in March 2024 to voluntarily surrender its registration as a Core Investment Company, arguing that it had repaid its debt and should no longer be subject to the same regulatory framework. However, the RBI rejected its request in September 2026 and directed it to comply with the applicable Upper Layer regulations. The regulatory framework also restricts deregistration for entities that have assets exceeding Rs 1,000 crore, even if they do not have public funds or a customer interface. Tata Sons’ assets stood at approximately Rs 2.01 lakh crore as of March 31, 2026, well above the prescribed threshold.
