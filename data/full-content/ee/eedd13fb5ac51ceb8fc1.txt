Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); St. Josephs Church Vs CIT (Exemption) (ITAT Mumbai) Summary: The ITAT Mumbai, in ST JOSEPHS CHURCH Vs CIT (Exemption), considered whether renewal of registration under Section 12AB of the Income-tax Act, 1961, could be rejected solely because a public religious and charitable trust did not possess a written trust deed or Memorandum of Association. The Church, registered under the Maharashtra Public Trusts Act, 1950, since 1955, had applied for renewal through Form 10AB. The CIT(E), Mumbai, rejected its application on 30 March 2026 for failure to furnish a constitutive instrument. The assessee relied upon its 1955 Schedule II application, Charity Commissioner’s registration certificate, earlier Section 12A registration and Form 10AC to establish its longstanding existence and charitable status. The Tribunal held that Rule 17A(2)(a) and (b) expressly recognise institutions established with or without a written instrument. Therefore, absence of a trust deed cannot automatically justify rejection of registration under Section 12AB. The CIT(E) was required to examine alternative documentary evidence, including statutory registration records, instead of insisting upon a document that never existed. Referring to Radhasoami Satsang Vs CIT, St. Joseph’s High School Vs CIT(E) and St. John the Evangelist Church Vs CIT(E), the Tribunal observed that earlier registrations and consistent recognition were relevant considerations, although they did not eliminate the requirement to verify the institution’s objects, genuine activities and statutory compliance. Accordingly, the ITAT set aside the rejection order and restored the application to the CIT(E) for limited reconsideration. The Commissioner was directed to examine the Church’s historical registration documents, previous income-tax registrations, objects, genuineness of activities and compliance with applicable laws. The application could not be rejected again merely for absence of a separate trust deed, Memorandum of Association or settled scheme. After providing an opportunity of hearing, the CIT(E) must pass a reasoned order and grant renewal if the statutory conditions are satisfied. The Tribunal clarified that registration does not automatically establish entitlement to exemption for a particular assessment year. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF ITAT MUMBAI This appeal by the assessee is directed against the order dated 30.03.2026 passed by the Commissioner of Income-tax (Exemptions), Mumbai [“CIT(E)”], rejecting its application in Form No.10AB for renewal of regular registration under section 12AB of the Income-tax Act, 1961 [“the Act”]. The controversy concerns the documentary evidence prescribed by Rule 17A(2) of the Income-tax Rules, 1962 where a public religious and charitable trust was not created under a separate written instrument. 2. The assessee has raised the following grounds, which are reproduced as filed: On the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, 1.] The learned CIT(E) erred in rejecting the Application for Registration under Section 12AB of the Act solely on the ground that the Appellant failed to furnish the instrument of Trust /MOA as it amounts to non-compliance and non-fulfillment of mandatory requirements prescribed under Section 12AB (1)(b) of the Act read with Rule 17A(2) of the Income Tax Rules, 1962. 2.] The learned CIT(E) erred in observing that the application in Schedule II and subsequent registration Certificate issued by the Charity Commissioner do not constitute documents “evidencing the creation of the trust” as prescribed under Rule 17A(2)(b) of Income tax Rules, 1962 by completely overlooking the fact that the appellant is a religious and charitable trust duly registered with the Charity Commissioner under the Maharashtra Public Trusts Act, 1950 based on valid declaration of trust since the year 1955 and exists as a public charitable trust. 3.] The learned CIT(E) failed to consider and appreciate the Certificate of Registration granted by the Charity Commissioner under the Maharashtra Public Trusts Act, 1950 which is a valid document that establishes the existence of the Appellant Trust and is covered by the provisions of Rule 17(A)(2)(b) – any document evidencing the creation or establishment of the trust. 4.] The learned CIT(E) erred in observing that the Canon Law does not have any direct application for management and administrations of charitable trusts. 5.] The learned CIT(E) erred in relying upon the provisions of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 as the same are clearly not applicable in the case of the Appellant Trust – as it is undisputedly a public charitable trust. 6.] The learned CIT(E) erred in summarily rejecting the renewal application without considering the historical context and past records and evidence like audit reports and Return of Income filed over the past several decades that confirm the trust’s long-standing charitable existence. 7.] The CIT (E) failed to appreciate that there has been no change in the objects of the Church, the nature of its activities, or the applicable law, the learned CIT(E) is bound by the “Rule of Consistency” as established by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Radhasoami Satsang vs. CIT. The CIT (E) cannot arbitrarily change its stand to deny a status it has historically recognized and accepted. 8.] The learned CIT(E) failed to appreciate that the Department has granted/renewed registration under Section 12A/12AA/12AB of the Act for several decades in the past based on the same set of documents furnished on earlier occasions. 9.] The learned CIT(E) failed to appreciate that his power under section 12AB of the Act is limited to verifying the genuineness of the activities of the charitable nature of object which in the case of the Appellant has not been doubted and therefore the order passed by the CIT (E) on the alleged ground based on format of the document, rather than the nature of the activities is contrary to the provisions of the Act. 10.] The learned CIT(E) failed to appreciate that the rejection Order passed by him certainly affects the day-to-day management and functioning of the Appellant Trust though he may not have intended so. 3. The grounds arise from a common grievance and are, therefore, considered together. The assessee seeks setting aside of the rejection and a direction to grant registration under section 12AB. 4. The impugned order records that the assessee filed Form No.10AB on 28.09.2025 under section 12A(1)(ac)(ii). The CIT(E) treated the application as incomplete and, by notice dated 14.11.2025, called for documents prescribed under Rule 17A(2). The assessee responded by letter dated 03.12.2025 with certain details and supporting documents. A further notice dated 27.02.2026 specifically required a self-certified copy of the instrument of trust or Memorandum of Association. 5. The assessee explained that it had not been created under a separate trust deed or Memorandum of Association and that its church administration was governed by Canon Law. It furnished the form of application for registration as a public trust. In paragraph 3.1, the CIT(E) held that this form evidenced registration, but not the creation or establishment of the trust. He also recorded that no scheme settled by the Charity Commissioner or a Court had been furnished. His conclusion was that neither an instrument nor any document evidencing creation had been produced. 6. After reproducing section 12AB(1)(b) and clauses (a), (b) and (c) of Rule 17A(2), the CIT(E), in paragraphs 4.3 and 4.4, regarded the instrument of trust as mandatory and as the sole source of the trust’s legal existence. According to him, the objects, beneficiaries, trust property, authority of the trustees and manner of administration could not be ascertained without that instrument. In paragraphs 4.5 and 4.6, he referred to sections 3 and 5 of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882, while acknowledging that the enactment applies to private trusts. He considered that the relevant concepts could nevertheless be adopted in examining a public trust. 7. The CIT(E) rejected the explanation based on Canon Law on the ground that the Income-tax Act and the Maharashtra Public Trusts Act, 1950 govern the applicant. In paragraphs 4.7 to 6, he concluded that without an instrument, or what he regarded as an acceptable document evidencing creation, he could not reach satisfaction about the objects, genuineness of activities or compliance with other material laws. He rejected renewal. The concluding portion states that the existing regular registration remained valid till assessment year 2026-27 and suggests creation of an instrument or procurement of a scheme. 8. During the course of hearing before us the learned Authorized Representative (AR), submitted that clauses (a) and (b) of Rule 17A(2) address alternative modes of creation, and that requiring an instrument from a trust created otherwise would render clause (b) redundant. He referred specifically to the Schedule II application for public-trust registration filed in 1955 (paper-book pages 15 to 18). According to the submissions, it records the trust’s name, trustees and managers, charitable objects, historical income and expenditure, encumbrances, any scheme, and title-deed particulars. It was urged that this is a contemporaneous statutory declaration of the trust’s existence and objects, rather than a bare procedural filing. 9. The learned AR further referred to the Charity Commissioner’s registration certificate dated 20.06.1955, said to follow a statutory inquiry; the certificate under section 12A dated 24.01.1983; and Form No.10AC dated 23.09.2021 granting registration for assessment years 2022-23 to 2026-27. It was argued that the CIT(E) identified no change in constitution, law, objects or activities warranting a departure from prior recognition, and that assessment orders had also acknowledged the registration. The learned AR invoked Radhasoami Satsang v. CIT, (1992) 193 ITR 321 (SC), and relied on the coordinate Bench decisions in St. Joseph’s High School v. CIT(E), ITA Nos.5646 and 5849/Mum/2026, dated 05.08.2026, and St. John the Evangelist Church v. CIT(E), ITA No.5828/Mum/2026, dated 08.09.2026. He sought a direction to grant renewal. 10. The learned Departmental Representative (DR) requested that the Revenue submissions placed on record in the appeal concerning the school (in ITA No. 5715/Mum/2026) be considered in this appeal as well. On that footing, it was urged that no formal deed is required in every case, but the alternative material must sufficiently establish creation, objects, governing authority and genuine activities. The Schedule II application was distinguished from the Charity Commissioner’s findings and register entries; the certificate, though relevant, was said not to establish every requirement of section 12AB. Earlier registration does not preclude an inquiry on renewal. 11. The Revenue further relied on the documentary breadth of the decision relied upon and submitted that relief there followed examination of that institution’s own records. The learned DR sought consideration of the Church’s evidence on its merits and, in the alternative, a limited restoration to the CIT(E). These are the submissions adopted from the school appeal; no additional Church-specific objection to a particular object or activity was identified in the impugned order. 12. We have considered the rival submissions, including the specified pages of the assessee’s written submissions, the impugned order and the decisions cited. The first question is whether a written instrument or scheme is an invariable prerequisite. The second is whether the alternative public-trust records and prior registrations have received the examination required by section 12AB, and what relief follows from the answer. 13. Section 12AB(1)(b) permits the Commissioner to call for documents and make inquiries concerning genuine activities and compliance with other laws material to achieving the institution’s objects. His decision must also rest on satisfaction concerning those objects. The inquiry is substantive, and the assessee must furnish evidence sufficient for that purpose. The provision does not, however, prescribe a written trust deed as the only permissible means of establishing the applicant’s constitution. 14. Rule 17A(2) differentiates between an applicant constituted under an instrument and one constituted otherwise. Clause (a) requires the constitutive instrument in the former situation. Clause (b) applies in the latter and requires a self-certified copy of the document evidencing creation or establishment. Clause (c) separately requires evidence of registration with the relevant Registrar. The distinction between an instrument which creates an institution and a document which evidences its establishment must be maintained. Requiring a constitutive instrument even in the latter situation would deprive clause (b) of its operation. 15. The conclusion in paragraphs 4.3, 4.4 and 4.7 of the impugned order does not give effect to that distinction. The CIT(E) quoted clause (b), but proceeded on the basis that the absence of an instrument necessarily prevented all further inquiry. That is an error of law. The assessee’s inability to produce a document which, according to its explanation, never existed cannot, without examination of the alternative evidence, be equated with failure to comply with a universal statutory requirement to furnish a deed. 16. There is, nevertheless, a legitimate distinction between dispensing with a formal deed and dispensing with proof. Clause (b) preserves the requirement of evidence. The CIT(E) may examine whether the documents relate to this assessee, what they establish about its origin and objects, and whether the activities are genuine. If a particular relevant matter remains unclear, he may require clarification or supporting records. Such a requisition must identify the matter requiring verification; it cannot merely repeat a demand for the unavailable deed. 17. The Schedule II application at paper-book pages 15 to 18 is relied upon for particulars concerning the Church’s origin, objects, management, finances and property. The submission also identifies the certificate dated 20.06.1955 at page 19. These page references identify the material relied upon; the underlying entries and the order or findings in the Charity Commissioner’s inquiry must be evaluated for what they actually establish. Under sections 19 to 21 of the Maharashtra Public Trusts Act, the inquiry concerns the existence and public character of a trust, its origin and objects, trustees and mode of succession, followed by findings and register entries. A declaration in an application and the statutory findings have distinct evidentiary weight, but both are relevant to the inquiry under Rule 17A(2)(b). 18. The CIT(E)’s categorical exclusion of the public-trust application from consideration under clause (b) is, therefore, unsustainable. The separate requirement in clause (c) does not prohibit a statutory registration document from also having evidentiary value for clause (b). Equally, registration under the State enactment does not automatically conclude every inquiry under section 12AB. The correct approach is to assess the contents and cumulative probative value of the documents, rather than accept or reject them merely by their description. 19. The relied-upon decision in St. Joseph’s High School, ITA Nos.5646 and 5849/Mum/2026, concerns a school at Vikhroli. The present assessee is St. Joseph’s Church at Vikhroli. The common location and related name do not make their objects, activities and governing documents identical. The school’s evidence must remain confined to its own record; its interpretation of Rule 17A remains directly relevant. 20. In paragraphs 19 to 23 of that decision, the coordinate Bench explained the distinction between the two modes of establishment recognized by Rule 17A and the evidentiary relevance of public-trust records. Paragraph 20 states: “The emphasis, therefore, shifts from the constitutive instrument itself to the evidentiary material demonstrating the existence and establishment of the institution.” 21. At the same time, paragraph 23 preserves the statutory inquiry in the following terms: “The question is whether the material produced is sufficient to enable the Commissioner to arrive at the satisfaction contemplated by section 12AB.” 22. We respectfully follow that interpretation. The direction to grant renewal in paragraph 33 of the school decision was made on the documentary record and findings in that case. Paragraphs 21, 28 and 31 refer to the earlier registrations, educational recognition and continued activities whose correctness had not been doubted. The decision does not prescribe each document mentioned there as a mandatory checklist for other applicants. It also does not hold that the failure of one reason for rejection necessarily establishes all remaining statutory conditions in every appeal. 23. St. John the Evangelist Church, ITA No.5828/Mum/2026, dated 08.09.2026, applies the same construction to a church which furnished a Schedule II application and registration certificate. In paragraphs 15 and 16 the coordinate Bench restored the application for the confined examination of objects, genuine activities and material compliance, because the CIT(E) had not undertaken that inquiry. It prohibited renewed rejection merely for absence of a deed or scheme. That approach to relief is relevant here. 24. The reference to the Indian Trusts Act does not sustain the impugned conclusion. Section 1 excludes its application to public or private religious or charitable endowments. The CIT(E) himself acknowledges the limited field of that enactment. General principles concerning fiduciary obligations may assist in understanding a trust, and definitions may be adopted where the applicable enactment so provides. That does not justify importing section 5 as an overriding requirement that every public charitable institution must have a registered written deed. Such an approach would be incompatible with the alternative expressly recognized in Rule 17A(2)(b). 25. The CIT(E)’s discussion of Canon Law establishes that internal ecclesiastical administration does not displace the applicable secular statutes. The assessee does not claim otherwise in the submissions recorded before us. The reference explains its claimed historical administration, but its own records must establish the matters relevant to section 12AB. The general observations on Canon Law in the impugned order do not identify an actual statutory breach by this Church. 26. Nor has any provision been identified which requires every such institution to obtain a scheme settled by the Charity Commissioner or a Court as a precondition to registration under section 12AB. If an existing scheme governs the assessee, it is relevant evidence. Its absence cannot be converted into an independent statutory disqualification without identifying an applicable legal requirement. The advice to create a fresh instrument or procure a scheme cannot cure the error in refusing to examine the alternative evidence contemplated by the Rule. 27. Prior registration is material to the evaluation. The assessee identifies the certificate dated 24.01.1983 at paper-book page 20 and Form No.10AC dated 23.09.2021 at pages 21 to 23, said to remain effective through assessment year 2026-27. The impugned order itself acknowledges the existing registration. Radhasoami Satsang v. CIT, (1992) 193 ITR 321 (SC), recognizes the relevance of an unchanged fundamental aspect consistently accepted in earlier years, while explaining that strict res judicata does not govern income-tax proceedings. The plea of unchanged constitution, objects and activities must be examined against the earlier and present records. Prior registration does not, by itself, answer the present statutory inquiry. 28. The jurisdictional decision in The Chamber of Tax Consultants v. CIT(E), Writ Petition (L) No.7587 of 2026 reported at (2026) 184 taxmann.com 374 (Bom.), referred to in the school decision, concerns rejection for want of express irrevocability or dissolution clauses and the related declaration in Form No.10AB. It reinforces the prohibition against introducing a condition unsupported by the statute. Its operative directions should not, however, be read as granting automatic renewal to every institution whose eligibility on the other statutory parameters remains unexamined. 29. The cited pages in the submission make a specific and substantial case for alternative evidence: the contemporaneous Schedule II application, a certificate following the State-law process, and prior income-tax registrations. The CIT(E) did not assess their cumulative contents under Rule 17A(2)(b). His conclusion that the absence of an instrument prevented satisfaction cannot stand. Yet the application’s assertions and the certificate’s evidentiary effect have not been evaluated against the statutory findings, and the impugned order contains no substantive examination of the Church’s objects and activities on that basis. Absence of an independent adverse finding cannot be converted into an affirmative finding on every statutory condition. 30. We therefore set aside the documentary premise and restore the matter for a confined inquiry. The Church has identified specific documents and page references which must receive reasoned consideration. The CIT(E) cannot prescribe the documentary collection of another institution as a universal checklist. A direct unconditional grant on this record would require factual findings on the Church’s own objects, activities and material statutory compliance which the authority has not made. 31. The order dated 30.03.2026 is set aside and the application filed on 28.09.2025 is restored to the CIT(E). He shall examine the Schedule II application, the registration certificate dated 20.06.1955, the section 12A certificate dated 24.01.1983, and Form No.10AC dated 23.09.2021, together with the other relevant material furnished. The paper-book references are drawn from the learned Authorized Representative’s submissions. Where the contents of the Charity Commissioner’s findings or register entries are needed to resolve a specific matter, he may call for those records, identifying that matter to the assessee. He shall assess all material cumulatively and by its substance. 32. The remaining inquiry is confined to the Church’s objects, the genuineness of its activities and compliance with other laws material to achieving those objects within section 12AB. The CIT(E) shall consider the earlier registrations and the asserted absence of change. He shall not again reject the application merely because there is no separate written deed, Memorandum of Association or settled scheme, or because the Schedule II application and registration certificate also serve purposes under the public-trust legislation. Any proposed adverse inference must identify the particular evidentiary deficiency or statutory requirement and be put to the assessee. 33. After affording a reasonable opportunity of being heard, the CIT(E) shall pass a speaking order in accordance with these findings and grant renewal if the statutory conditions are satisfied. The effective period and duration shall be determined under the law applicable to the application, including the proviso inserted in section 12AB(1) by the Finance Act, 2025 where its conditions are fulfilled. Since the matter concerns renewal of an existing registration, it shall be disposed of expeditiously. These directions do not authorize an inquiry into unrelated matters beyond the statutory scope of registration. 34. Registration does not, by itself, determine the availability or computation of exemption in a particular assessment year. Such questions shall be considered in the appropriate proceedings in accordance with law. No appeal concerning section 80G is before us and no direction under that provision is called for. 35. Grounds Nos.1 to 3 and 5 are allowed to the extent of our conclusions on the legal basis of rejection and the consequential restoration. Ground No.4 is disposed of in terms of paragraph 25. Grounds Nos.6 to 8 are restored for consideration of the cited documents and the plea of consistency. Ground No.9 succeeds against rejection based solely on the form of the constitutive document, but its wider proposition that genuineness alone exhausts the inquiry is not accepted. Ground No.10 requires no separate relief. 36. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes. Order pronounced in the open court on 29-10-2026.
