(Calcutta High Court) Summary: The Calcutta High Court, Circuit Bench at Jalpaiguri, in Dilip Kumar Mitra Vs The State of West Bengal & Ors., set aside an ex parte GST adjudication order dated 22 August 2024 passed under Section 73 of the West Bengal Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, together with the consequential recovery notice dated 1 July 2026, on the ground that the adjudicating authority had failed to consider the taxpayer’s request for adjournment and had proceeded without hearing him. Justice Suvra Ghosh held that the adjudication order required interference in the interest of justice because the petitioner’s request for adjournment had not been considered before the ex parte decision was taken. The petitioner contended that, after receiving the show cause notice, he had requested an adjournment on medical grounds and sought an opportunity of personal hearing under Section 75(4) of the GST Act. According to the petitioner, the authority did not accede to the request and proceeded to pass the adjudication order in his absence. It was further argued that the order violated Section 75(6) because it did not disclose the relevant facts and the basis of the decision. The petitioner also raised an issue concerning the uploading of the relevant order on the GST portal. He submitted that it had been placed under the “additional notices/orders” tab rather than the “notices/orders” tab, making it difficult for him to track the communication. Although the two tabs subsequently appeared to have been merged, the date of merger was unclear. The petitioner further informed the Court that, following the adjudication order, the Department had recovered ₹4,89,541 from his electronic credit ledger and electronic cash ledger. He accordingly sought reconsideration of the matter after being granted a reasonable opportunity of hearing. On behalf of the State, it was submitted that the adjudicating authority had fixed a date and time for personal hearing but the petitioner had failed to appear. However, the State’s submissions also indicated that the petitioner’s request for adjournment did not appear to have been considered by the authority. After considering the submissions of both parties, the High Court concluded that the adjudication order had been passed ex parte without consideration of the adjournment request and without hearing the petitioner on the issue. The Court therefore held that the order needed to be set aside in the interest of justice. Accordingly, the adjudication order dated 22 August 2024 and consequential recovery notice dated 1 July 2026 were quashed/set aside. The Assistant Commissioner of Revenue (SGST), Jalpaiguri Charge, was directed to revisit the matter, grant the petitioner a reasonable opportunity of hearing and pass a reasoned decision within six weeks from communication of the High Court’s order. The petitioner was also granted liberty to submit a reply to the show cause notice. The writ petition was disposed of without any order as to costs. Appellant Was Represented by: Adv. Himangshu Kumar Ray, Adv. Abhilash Mittal, Adv. Hiqa Naz Pradhan, Adv. Subhasis Podder, Adv. Kanchan Singha, FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CALCUTTA HIGH COURT Affidavit of service filed by the petitioner is taken on record. The petitioner has assailed the ex parte adjudication order passed under Section 73 of the West Bengal Goods and Services Act, 2017 and the recovery notice issued on 1st July, 2026 on several counts. Learned counsel for the petitioner submits that upon receipt of the show cause notice, the petitioner sought an adjournment on medical grounds as well as an opportunity of personal hearing in terms of Section 75(4) of the Act of 2017. The prayer was not acceded to by the authority who passed the order impugned in absence of the petitioner. The order is in violation of Section 75(6) of the Act since it does not set out the relevant facts and the basis of the decision taken by the authority. The adjournment order was not uploaded by the Revenue under “notices/orders” tab on the GST web portal. It was uploaded under “additional notices/orders” tab for which the petitioner was unable to track the same. It appears that subsequently both the tabs have merged but the date of such merger is not clear. The petitioner seeks a direction upon the authority to revisit the issue upon granting reasonable opportunity of hearing to him. After the order was passed a sum of Rs.4,89,541/- was recovered from the electronic credit ledger and electronic cash ledger of the petitioner. Learned counsel for the State submits that the date and time for personal hearing were fixed by the authority despite which the petitioner failed to appear before them. However, the request for adjournment made by the petitioner does not appear to have been considered. Upon consideration of the submission made on behalf of the parties, this Court of the view that since the adjudication order was passed ex parte without considering the prayer for adjournment made by the petitioner and also without hearing the petitioner on the issue, the order needs to be set aside in the interest of justice. Accordingly, the adjudication order dated 22nd August, 2024 and the recovery notice issued as a consequence thereof on 1st July, 2026 are quashed/set aside. The Assistant Commissioner of Revenue (SGST), Jalpaiguri Charge, being the 4th respondent herein, is directed to revisit the issue upon granting reasonable opportunity of hearing to the petitioner and take a reasoned decision within six weeks from the date of communication of this order. On prayer of the petitioner, liberty is granted to submit a reply to the show cause notice issued by the authority. The writ petition is disposed of. There shall be no order as to costs. Since no affidavit has been invited, allegations contained in the writ petition shall be deemed not to have been admitted. Urgent certified website copy of the order, if applied for, be given to the parties on compliance of requisite formalities. Inadvertent error has crept into the order passed on October 07, 2026. In the third paragraph of the order the word “adjournment” be read as “adjudication”. The department is directed to incorporate necessary correction. This order be treated as part of the order dated October 07, 2026.
