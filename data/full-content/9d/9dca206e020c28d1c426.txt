Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Vrinda Marketing Enterprises Vs ACIT (Karnataka High Court) Karnataka HC Quashes Reassessment Proceedings, Allows Assessee to Furnish Loan Confirmations Introduction The Karnataka High Court partly allowed two writ petitions filed by Vrinda Marketing Enterprises, setting aside the impugned reassessment proceedings and permitting the firm to furnish loan confirmations and further clarification. The dispute arose from amounts treated by the Department as alleged undisclosed business turnover, which the petitioner explained as unsecured loans. The Court considered that the suitability of the cases for reassessment could be examined after allowing the petitioner to provide supporting material. The relief was procedural. The Court did not finally determine whether the disputed receipts constituted loans or taxable income. Background of the Dispute The petitioner, a partnership firm, challenged proceedings concerning Assessment Years 2021-22 and 2022-23, as identified in the substantive discussion of the order. For each year, the challenge covered an order dated 16 June 2026 under Section 148A(3), prior approval dated 12 June 2026 under Sections 148A(3) and 151, and a consequential notice dated 16 June 2026 under Section 148 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The Department had issued show cause notices under Section 148A(1) concerning alleged undisclosed business turnover of ₹72,79,580 for AY 2021-22 and ₹82,24,948 for AY 2022-23. The petitioner was asked to explain the transactions and furnish supporting documents, including financial statements, balance sheets and profit and loss accounts. Petitioner’s Explanation: Receipts Were Unsecured Loans The petitioner submitted that it had filed detailed responses to both notices. According to the firm, the disputed receipts represented unsecured loans, which did not constitute business turnover. It stated that it had furnished particulars of the persons or entities providing the loans, together with copies of their ledger accounts. It had also offered to provide additional information if required. The petitioner’s grievance was that the subsequent orders proceeded on objections concerning the bank statements, a mismatch and the absence of confirmations from the persons who had advanced the loans. The firm maintained that these matters could have been addressed through further clarification and supporting documents before deciding whether reassessment should proceed. Objections Regarding Bank Statements and Confirmations The petitioner explained that there was a typographical error in mentioning the financial year of the bank statement. It asserted that the relevant bank statements had nevertheless been produced. It further submitted that, had it been specifically called upon to furnish confirmations from the persons who extended the unsecured loans, it would have done so. These submissions placed the adequacy of the opportunity to clarify the transactions at the centre of the dispute. The issue before the Court was whether the impugned proceedings should stand without allowing that clarification. The Court heard the Revenue’s standing counsel on whether interference was warranted and, if so, on the appropriate terms. Reference to an Earlier Similar Order The petitioner placed reliance on the High Court’s order in W.P. No. 28272/2026, involving similar circumstances. As recorded in the present order, the Court in that matter had restored proceedings for reconsideration and permitted the taxpayer to furnish further clarification sought by the authority. Although a deficiency in prior approval had also been argued there, the approach highlighted before the Court was reconsideration of the reassessment decision after allowing additional explanation. The High Court found sufficient similarity in the circumstances to adopt a comparable course in the present petitions. High Court’s Findings The Court observed that there could be something amiss in the manner in which prior approval had been obtained. However, it did not develop a separate, detailed ruling on the validity of the approval process. Instead, it considered that the fitness of the cases for reassessment under Section 147 could be adjudged after providing an opportunity to submit confirmations from the persons concerned and any further details required. Accordingly, the Court intervened to enable reconsideration on a fuller factual record. This distinction is material: the order permits further examination of the petitioner’s explanation rather than accepting the loan claim as conclusively established. Operative Directions The writ petitions were allowed in part. The operative order expressly quashed the Section 148A(3) orders, the prior approval orders, and the Section 148 notices identified in its table. The petitioner was directed to appear, without further notice, before the Income Tax Officer, Ward 1 and TPS, Udupi, on 16 October 2026. It was permitted to furnish further clarification sought by the officer. The officer was required to consider the explanation in light of the Court’s observations. Key Takeaway The decision underscores the importance of examining an assessee’s explanation and supporting material before deciding whether reassessment is warranted. Where disputed receipts are explained as unsecured loans, questions concerning bank records and lender confirmations may require further clarification. Here, the Court enabled that exercise while leaving the substantive tax treatment open for reconsideration. Cases Discussed Earlier Karnataka High Court order in W.P.
