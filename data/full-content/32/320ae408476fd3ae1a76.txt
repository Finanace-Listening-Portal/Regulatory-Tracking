Based on its current assessment of the Supreme Court judgment, MD & CEO Vinay Tonse said reinstatement of the AT1 principal amount is expected to have an indicative impact of approximately 240 basis points on CET1, including profits. Advt “Based on our current assessment, which is indicative, and as of 30th September 2026, the reinstatement of the AT1 principal amount is expected to have a pro forma impact of approximately 240 basis points on our common equity tier 1 ratio, including profits. Our overall tier 1 capital ratio is expected to remain around 13.7% on a pro forma basis,” Tonse told analysts on Monday.YES Bank had disclosed in its investor presentation that, on an indicative and unaudited pro-forma basis, CET1 would fall to 11.4% from 13.7%. The bank had also shown a pro-forma swing from its reported Q2 FY27 net profit of Rs 1,080 crore to a Rs 5,217-crore loss, while clarifying that no impact arising from the judgment had been recognised in the reported Q2 results pending assessment of the appropriate accounting and regulatory treatment.Capital raise among optionsBanodkar said the bank would assess capital actions to ensure that it remains well capitalised while continuing to pursue its growth objectives.“CET-1 in the range of pro forma basis, again this is not conclusive, is at about 11.4%. We do believe that we will continue to evaluate areas of capital actions to make sure that the bank remains well capitalized in order to fulfill its growth objectives,” he said.In a subsequent response, he said the bank’s earlier comfort level had been around 13.5-14% CET1 and that it had operated at those levels while remaining comfortably above regulatory requirements. Advt “For us, when we were at 14% CET-1, it was our comfortable level where we were operating in between the 13.5 to 14% level. We do believe it was a level which was comfortable, just keeping our objectives and ambitions in mind,” Niranjan said.“From a regulatory standpoint, there was no stricture to say that this is the CET-1 that you have to operate. There are regulations that cover our CET-1. So we were always comfortably above those regulatory requirements.”He said the pro-forma 11.4% CET1 level would still give the bank headroom to grow, but added that the level was not ideal.“Once we conclude the assessment with the CET-1 pro forma at 11.4%, we do believe that it still allows us headroom to continue to grow,” he said. Advt “But yes, I would say the evaluation for capital actions including any raise, I think we will have to be mindful of that because 11.5% of CET-1 is not ideal.”Quantum, pricing of capital raise to be assessedNiranjan Banodkar said the bank would not take a capital action without considering its impact on stakeholders, including shareholders.“And that's something that we will, irrespective of how the regulator might want to say, we will want to make sure that capital levels are more comfortable. But we will certainly not take any action which is going to be detrimental to our stakeholders, including the shareholders, very important to us,” he said. Advt “The quantum, the value, the rate, I think all of these, we will make an ongoing assessment.”Tonse had similarly said capital planning would remain an ongoing board-level process and that any capital decision would depend on regulatory requirements, the cost and availability of capital, shareholder interests and the bank’s medium-term growth objectives.“We will not pre-empt those decisions today,” Tonse said.He added that the bank would continue to assess the implications for its capital structure, regulatory buffers, balance-sheet strength and future growth plans.No cash outflow from AT1 reinstatementNiranjan clarified that the reinstatement of the AT1 principal should not be interpreted as a corresponding cash repayment by the bank.YES Bank had presented the pro-forma impact through the P&L for disclosure purposes, but Niranjan said the substance of the transaction was a balance-sheet adjustment involving reserves.“What we have done right now is to provide you a pro-forma which has been taken through the P&L. And the impact is available,” he said.“In terms of the substance of this matter, we do believe that the route through the P&L is only just a means. In substance, if you think about the restatement, it is actually a balance sheet structure where the core reserves are being used to reinstate the liability.”“The principal restatement of the AT1 bonds, as I said earlier, is in substance a balance sheet change with no repayment, no cash outflow on the part of the bank,” Niranjan said.Coupon treatment remains unresolvedAsked specifically about prospective coupons, Niranjan said the matter remained part of the bank’s broader assessment of the consequences of the judgment.“The bank is still evaluating the consequential implications of the adjustment, including the decisions, the actions that are required to give effect. And therefore, the manner and the timing of restatement of the bonds as well as payment of the coupon is also under assessment,” he said.In a subsequent exchange, he reiterated that future coupons would also form part of the assessment.“Future coupons will form part of the future assessment. And therefore, I think just from a timeline standpoint, it is important that we first conclude the assessment on the judgment. I think our decisions on the future coupon will also be made at the appropriate time,” Niranjan said.
