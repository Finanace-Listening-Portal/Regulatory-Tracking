Articles 1 min read RBI lowers banks' daily CRR maintenance requirement to 90% from 99% The Reserve Bank of India has reduced the minimum daily cash reserve ratio (CRR) maintenance requirement for banks to 90% from 99%, effective December 1, reversing the tightening introduced in October amid evolving liquidity conditions. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Nov 27, 2026 at 07:55 PM IST var news_msid = 136225656; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook RBI has reduced the minimum daily CRR maintenance requirement from 99% to 90% of the prescribed requirement.The revised requirement will come into effect from the fortnight beginning December 1, 2026.The move reverses the October 9 decision to increase the minimum daily maintenance requirement to 99% from 90%.The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has reduced the minimum daily maintenance requirement for the cash reserve ratio (CRR) to 90 per cent from the existing 99 per cent, effective from the fortnight beginning December 1, 2026.Indian central bank on Friday said the decision was taken following an assessment of the current and evolving macroeconomic situation and liquidity conditions."On the basis of an assessment of the current and evolving macroeconomic situation and liquidity conditions, it has been decided to reduce the minimum daily maintenance of the cash reserve ratio (CRR) from 99 per cent of the requirement to 90 per cent effective from the fortnight beginning December 1, 2026," the RBI said. Advt The decision reverses the central bank's October 9 directive, when it had increased the minimum daily CRR maintenance requirement from 90 per cent to 99 per cent, effective October 16, following a review of prevailing liquidity conditions.Under the earlier framework, scheduled banks were allowed to maintain a minimum CRR of 90 per cent of the required amount on all days during the reporting fortnight, provided the average CRR maintained daily was not below the prescribed requirement.The reversal comes against the backdrop of a sharp turnaround in banking system liquidity, which has moved from a surplus of Rs 11.16 lakh crore in September to a deficit of Rs 47,641 crore as of November 26. RBI's liquidity absorption operations, dollar sales to support the rupee, tax outflows and an increase in currency in circulation have contributed to the tightening.
