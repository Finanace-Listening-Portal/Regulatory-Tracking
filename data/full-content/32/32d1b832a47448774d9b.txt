Meesho's success will depend on underwriting capabilities, borrower quality, repayment performance and funding costs.Meesho's Rs 400 crore investment in its lending services arm and plan to set up a separate wholly owned subsidiary signal an attempt to move beyond facilitating loans towards gaining greater control over the lending business.The e-commerce company wants to build in-house underwriting capabilities, design loan products and manage borrower relationships directly, with the stated aim of capturing lending margins and accessing institutional funding. Its next steps will determine whether it continues to rely on partner banks and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), obtains regulatory approval to lend directly, or adopts a combination of the two models. Advt Meesho invested Rs 399.99 crore in Meesho Payments Private Limited (MPPL), its existing lending services subsidiary, through a rights issue. The shares were allotted on November 2, following board approval on October 28 for an investment of up to Rs 400 crore. The board also approved the incorporation of a new wholly owned subsidiary, whose name is yet to be disclosed. The new entity is intended to build in-house underwriting capabilities, control product design, establish direct borrower relationships and capture lending margins.MPPL is already involved in the lending process. It operates as a Lending Service Provider, facilitating financing arrangements between NBFCs and sellers and consumers on Meesho's platform. It also provides a first-loss default guarantee to its financing partners. This means the company is already exposed to some credit risk through these arrangements, even though partner lenders provide the loans. MPPL reported a turnover of Rs 11.05 crore and a net loss of Rs 24.65 crore in FY26.The new subsidiary could allow Meesho to take on a larger role in the credit value chain. Its marketplace generates information on sellers and transactions that could potentially help assess borrowers, design products and monitor repayment behaviour. But access to such information does not automatically confer permission to lend. Under the Reserve Bank of India's Digital Lending Directions, 2025, regulated lenders remain responsible for assessing borrowers' creditworthiness and for compliance when they engage LSPs. Use of customer data is also subject to consent and privacy requirements.The way forward Advt Meesho has two broad routes. It could continue partnering with banks and NBFCs for funding while expanding its role in customer acquisition, underwriting-related services and loan servicing.Alternatively, it could seek the necessary regulatory authorisation to lend through an appropriately registered entity, taking on the associated capital requirements, credit risk and collection responsibilities. The company has not disclosed whether it intends to pursue an NBFC licence.The experience of other e-commerce platforms offers two examples. Flipkart has established Flipkart Finance, an RBI-registered NBFC, to offer consumer credit. Yet its July 2026 Flipkart Pay Later launch was in partnership with PayU Finance, showing that owning a lending entity does not preclude working with external lenders. Advt Amazon, meanwhile, completed its acquisition of fintech lender Axio in September 2025 after receiving RBI approval. Axio had partnered with Amazon on consumer credit and buy-now-pay-later products since 2018 and had a loan book of about Rs 2,200 crore at the end of June 2025.The key question for Meesho will be how quickly it can build underwriting and risk-management capabilities while keeping borrowing costs and defaults under control. Its large seller and consumer base may provide a distribution advantage, but lending economics will depend on borrower quality, repayment performance, cost of funds and the ability to manage collections. Advt The Rs 400 crore infusion strengthens its existing lending-services arm; the proposed subsidiary signals a more ambitious move towards owning a larger share of the lending business.
