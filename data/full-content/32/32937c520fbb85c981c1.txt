Justice Milind N Jadhav held that the authority to grant sanction rested with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), not the bank’s board, under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949. The court also found that the sanction order -- dated May 15, 2023 -- suffered from non-application of mind, as it did not show that the evidence gathered during the investigation had been independently assessed before approval was granted. It accordingly set aside the sanction and the consequential proceedings before the Special CBI Court. The court also rejected the Central Bureau of Investigation’s (CBI) request for an eight-week stay on the judgment to allow it to challenge the ruling. Kochhar had challenged the board’s sanction under Section 19 of the PC Act, arguing that Sections 35B and 36AA of the Banking Regulation Act vested the RBI with authority over the termination or removal of a bank’s managing director. The RBI was, therefore, also the competent authority to sanction her prosecution, she contended. Rejecting the CBI’s argument that the validity of the sanction could be examined during the trial, the court said the competence of the authority granting approval was a jurisdictional question that could be decided at the threshold. Section 19 of the PC Act requires prior sanction from the authority competent to remove a public servant from office before a court can take cognizance of relevant offences. The court held that the Banking Regulation Act takes precedence over the Companies Act and a bank’s internal arrangements on matters concerning the appointment and removal of senior management. ICICI Bank’s board could not, therefore, exercise statutory powers vested in the RBI. The court also found that the sanction order referred to allegations of bribery and corruption without examining witness statements, documentary evidence or other material linking Kochhar to the alleged offences. The board had relied on the CBI’s report and request for sanction without demonstrating that it had independently assessed the evidence, it said. The judgment also noted that the bank’s own position, as recorded in the sanction order and board meeting minutes, was that its policies had not been violated in the loan disbursals and that it had suffered no wrongful loss.
