On the lending side, public sector banks lowered rates on fresh rupee loans by six basis points to 7.93% from 7.99% in August, while private banks kept their rates unchanged at 9.19%.Despite the sharper deposit rate cuts, public sector banks continued to offer 34 basis points more on fresh deposits while charging 126 basis points less on fresh loans than their private sector counterparts. Advt At the banking system level, the weighted average lending rate (WALR) on fresh rupee loans declined 10 basis points to 8.51% in September from 8.61% in August, indicating an easing in borrowing costs. However, the weighted average rate on outstanding rupee loans remained unchanged at 8.96%.The decline in fresh lending rates was also supported by foreign banks, which reduced their weighted average lending rates by 22 basis points to 7.76% from 7.98% in August.On the deposit side, the weighted average domestic term deposit rate on fresh deposits declined six basis points to 5.61% in September from 5.67% in August. Rates on outstanding term deposits eased marginally by one basis point to 6.55% from 6.56%.The decline in deposit rates was more pronounced over a longer period. Between June and September, private sector banks reduced fresh deposit rates by 45 basis points, compared with a 24-basis-point reduction by public sector lenders.However, the cost of outstanding deposits has been easing more gradually. Public sector banks' weighted average rate on outstanding term deposits declined to 6.61% in September from 6.91% a year earlier, while private banks saw a 25-basis-point reduction to 6.66% from 6.91%.The year-on-year trend in lending rates presents a mixed picture. The weighted average rate on fresh rupee loans stood at 8.51% in September, 12 basis points higher than 8.39% a year earlier. In contrast, the rate on outstanding loans declined 30 basis points to 8.96% from 9.26%, reflecting the transmission of earlier interest rate changes to existing borrowers. Advt Meanwhile, the median one-year marginal cost of funds-based lending rate (MCLR) of scheduled commercial banks declined six basis points to 8.55% in October from 8.61% in September, even as the RBI raised its benchmark repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% earlier in the month.The continued moderation in MCLR and deposit rates comes amid a shift in the interest rate cycle, with banks expected to progressively factor the higher policy rate into their lending and deposit pricing.
