Articles 4 min read NBFC insurance commissions face a massive reset as IRDAI proposes sharp caps Insurance commissions accounted for 3-25% of profit before tax for select NBFCs in FY2026, with lenders heavily exposed to credit-protection products facing the sharpest impact ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Updated On Sep 29, 2026 at 12:37 PM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 134553388; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook IRDAI has proposed capping commissions on credit-linked insurance at 2% for single-premium products and 2.5% in the first year for multi-year products, well below current payouts of up to 57%.Insurance commissions accounted for 3-25% of PBT for select NBFCs in FY2026, with lenders heavily exposed to credit-protection products facing the sharpest impact.The proposal would prohibit compulsory insurance bundling with loans, while lenders taking portfolio insurance would have to bear the premium and borrowers would have greater choice over insurers.IRDAI also proposes all-inclusive commission rules, tighter insurer expense limits, mandatory cost audits and greater disclosure, while allowing distributors to sell non-insurance products.NBFCs face a sharp reset in insurance distribution economics as the insurance regulator has proposed capping commissions on life insurance packaged with credit at 2% for single-premium products and 2.5% in the first year for multi-year products, against much higher payouts currently. First-year commissions to NBFCs across insurance products averaged 42-67%, while commissions on credit-protect single-premium products ranged from 22% on average to as high as 57%.Insurance commissions accounted for 3-25% of profit before tax for select NBFCs in FY2026, with lenders heavily exposed to credit-protection products facing the sharpest impact, according to Kotak Institutional Equities. The brokerage expects NBFCs to move customers towards individual protection products to partially offset the reduction in commissions. Advt The proposed changes are particularly significant for credit-linked insurance, where the current commission structure is substantially higher than the proposed caps. The NBFC channel's life insurance premium nearly tripled from Rs36 billion in FY2023 to Rs103 billion in FY2025, with an effective payout of 42%. About 93% of this business was single-premium group credit life, while commission on group credit life increased from 5% to 28%.The proposal also prohibits compulsory bundling of insurance with loans. A lender seeking portfolio insurance would have to take a group policy and bear the premium itself. Permitted combinations would require borrowers to be informed of the interest rate with and without insurance, remain free to choose the insurer and pay the insurance premium separately rather than from the loan proceeds.Complimentary group term insurance for depositors and borrowers, cards carrying embedded insurance and rate-linked additional security would remain permissible, subject to the proposed conditions. The proposal also removes the Rs50 million risk-cover limit for MSME lending.Wider impactThe commission reset extends beyond credit protection. The regulator has proposed that commissions become all-inclusive, covering incentives, awards, reimbursement of selling expenses, brand-value payments to related parties of distributors and non-cash benefits. Volume-linked and reward-linked incentives for bank and NBFC staff selling insurance would also be prohibited. Advt The proposed changes come as distributor payouts have risen substantially faster than premiums in some channels. Among sampled life corporate agents, new business premium increased 1.3 times from Rs630 billion to Rs800 billion between FY2023 and FY2025, while remuneration increased 2.3 times from Rs96 billion to Rs216 billion. Effective payout reached 27% of first-year premium.In broker-led general insurance, premium increased 1.4 times while commission rose 2.7 times, taking the average commission rate from 8.5% to 17%. Reported commission also understates the cost by a further 30-60% through rewards and incentives, according to the report. Advt The wider proposal would tighten expense controls at insurers and distributors. Life insurers' expenses of management would be brought down to 15% within two years and 12.5% within five years, while general insurers' limit would fall to 25% within two years and 20% within five years. Cost audits would become mandatory for insurers and distribution entities with insurance revenue above Rs1 billion, while entities above Rs500 million would have to publish revenue, expenses, related-party payments and profit after tax.The proposed framework would also consolidate 17 existing categories of insurance distribution into three broad categories: insurance distribution entities, insurance distribution persons and market infrastructure institutions. Entry capital for distribution entities would fall to Rs1 million from the current Rs5 million-Rs50 million range, while registration would become permanent instead of being renewed every three years. Advt Distribution entities would also be allowed open architecture with no limit on the number of insurers, compared with the current nine-insurer cap for banks. Distribution entities and persons would additionally be permitted to sell non-insurance products, potentially reducing their dependence on insurance commissions.For NBFCs, however, the immediate impact is concentrated in credit-linked insurance, where the proposed commission caps are substantially below current payouts. The proposal would therefore require lenders to adjust both their product mix and insurance distribution economics, with individual protection products emerging as one potential route to partially offset the reduction in credit-protection commissions.
