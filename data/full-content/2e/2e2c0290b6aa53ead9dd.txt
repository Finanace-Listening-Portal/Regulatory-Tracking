Summary: Indian Bank’s official IND GROW page and retail deposit-rate schedule, checked on 19 October 2026, list a callable 555-day fixed or money multiplier deposit for amounts from ₹1,000 to ₹2,99,99,999. The rate schedule is effective 4 August 2026 and shows 6.65% per annum for the public, 7.15% for an eligible senior citizen and 7.40% for an eligible super senior citizen. The additional rates are not extended to NRE or NRO deposits. Interest may be paid monthly, quarterly or on maturity, and premature closure is permitted under the bank’s normal term-deposit rules; the exact rate adjustment or penalty should be confirmed at booking because the product page does not state a universal percentage. The page also provides for loan or overdraft, nomination and renewal mechanics. Interest is taxable and tax-deduction rules operate separately from the final tax liability. As an Indian Bank deposit, eligible balances are covered by DICGC only up to the statutory aggregate of ₹5 lakh for principal and interest per depositor per bank in the same capacity and right. This is a factual product guide, not a rate ranking or recommendation. Who Can Open an IND GROW Fixed Deposit and Deposit Limits The official product page allows individuals singly or jointly, minors through guardians, sole proprietorships, partnerships, companies, associations and other eligible entities, subject to KYC, constitution documents and the bank’s acceptance. The minimum is ₹1,000, and the maximum is one rupee below ₹3 crore. A deposit of ₹3 crore or more falls into the bank’s bulk-deposit framework and is not eligible for the retail IND GROW terms shown here. The customer can choose a conventional fixed deposit or a money multiplier deposit, subject to account-opening channels and bank rules. In a periodic-interest option, cash is paid at the selected interval and the maturity amount differs from a cumulative option. A monthly payout may use a discounted monthly value under banking practice. The exact schedule printed by the bank is controlling; a simple annual-rate calculation is only an illustration. IND GROW Interest Rates for Senior and Super-Senior Citizens Indian Bank’s rate schedule shows a 0.50 percentage-point addition for eligible senior citizens and a 0.75 percentage-point addition for eligible super senior citizens for this special bucket, producing 7.15% and 7.40% respectively against the 6.65% public rate. Eligibility depends on age, residency, ownership and the bank’s terms. The product page specifically indicates that these additional rates are not applicable to NRE and NRO deposits. Joint holders should not assume that the oldest holder’s age automatically produces the benefit. The bank’s rules on first holder, beneficial ownership and supporting proof apply. Age and residency documents should be validated before booking. If a depositor changes status during the tenure, the deposit receipt and applicable rules should be reviewed rather than altering the rate informally. IND GROW Premature Withdrawal and Interest Penalty IND GROW is callable, so premature closure is permitted under the bank’s normal term-deposit rules. Callable does not mean that the 555-day headline rate is retained. Banks ordinarily recalculate interest for the period actually completed and may apply the penalty or reduction specified in their current rules. The official IND GROW page does not state one universal penalty percentage, so an article should not invent one. Obtain the premature-closure clause applicable on the booking date. The product page also states that a loan or overdraft can be available against the deposit. This can provide liquidity without closing the receipt, but it is a secured borrowing with interest, margin and documentation. Compare the borrowing cost and duration with the closure impact. Neither option should be described as free liquidity. IND GROW Maturity and Renewal Options The product page indicates automatic renewal under the same scheme if it remains available; if it is discontinued, the deposit is renewed for exactly one year. The rate at renewal follows the applicable rate on the renewal date, not the original 6.65%, 7.15% or 7.40%. Customers should record whether they want renewal or credit to an operative account and review the instruction before maturity. Nomination is mandatory for personal accounts according to the product page. Where the customer seeks not to nominate, the bank’s documentation and applicable rules should be checked rather than leaving the field incomplete. Nomination supports settlement but does not by itself decide beneficial succession against all legal claims. Joint operation and survivor instructions should also be recorded accurately. Income-Tax and TDS on IND GROW Deposit Interest Interest on a bank term deposit is generally taxable according to the depositor’s status and the applicable income-tax law. Tax deduction at source is only a collection mechanism; an absence of TDS does not make interest exempt. The depositor should aggregate relevant interest credited or paid by the bank for the year, reconcile the annual information statement and tax-credit record, and report income using the required method. The official Indian Bank page states that TDS applies and refers to Form 121 under the new rules for eligible non-deduction declarations. A declaration should be submitted only when the statutory conditions are actually satisfied. Senior status alone does not prove nil tax liability. Non-resident deposits follow separate withholding rules and do not receive the resident senior or super-senior interest addition stated for this product. Tax law transitioned from the Income-tax Act, 1961 to the Income-tax Act, 2025 from 1 April 2026, with savings for earlier periods. Depositors should use current Tax Year 2026-27 provisions, forms and portal instructions rather than copying old section numbers or declarations from prior-year advice. The final tax depends on total income, regime choice, deductions and residential status. DICGC Deposit Insurance Limit for Indian Bank Customers Indian Bank is a bank within the deposit-insurance framework. DICGC protects eligible principal and interest together up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in the same capacity and same right. The limit is aggregated across eligible savings, current, recurring and fixed deposits at all branches of the same bank. It is not ₹5 lakh per receipt, per branch or per banking channel. Accounts held in genuinely different capacities or rights can be treated separately under DICGC rules, and joint accounts depend on the names and order of holders. The rules should be applied to actual legal ownership, not artificial splitting. Amounts above the insured aggregate remain an exposure to the bank. DICGC cover is not a guarantee that every contractual or documentation dispute is insured, and it does not remove the need for correct KYC, nomination and ownership records. IND GROW Interest Rate, Liquidity and Bank Exposure IND GROW provides a stated contractual rate for a defined 555-day tenor, subject to the deposit contract. The main practical risks are liquidity cost on early closure, reinvestment at a different maturity rate, tax drag, incorrect renewal instructions and concentration above the DICGC limit. These factors vary by depositor. A factual article should explain them without ranking the deposit, predicting rates or suggesting that the highest headline rate is automatically suitable. Before placement, the customer should match the maturity date with a known cash need and keep emergency liquidity outside any amount that cannot tolerate a reduced return on early closure. The deposit advice, rate schedule, nomination, tax declaration and maturity instruction should be stored together. Any inconsistency should be corrected promptly through the bank’s official channel.
