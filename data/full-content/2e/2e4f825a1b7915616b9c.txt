Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Sarvpreet Singh Vs Assessing Officer (ITAT Delhi) Summary: The Delhi Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) remanded an addition of Rs. 21,92,000 relating to cash deposits for fresh adjudication and verification of supporting documentary evidence. The assessee, Sarvpreet Singh, had filed his income tax return for Assessment Year 2017-18 on 5 August 2017, declaring income of Rs. 3,14,900. Following issuance of notices under Sections 142(1) and 143(2) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, the Assessing Officer completed the assessment on 25 November 2019, determining total taxable income at Rs. 25,06,900 after making an addition towards cash deposits in the assessee’s savings bank account maintained with the State Bank of India. The assessee challenged the order of the National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC) dated 24 April 2025 before the Tribunal. Although nobody appeared on behalf of the assessee during the hearing, written submissions had been furnished. The assessee contended that the Assessing Officer had disregarded the replies and documentary evidence submitted during the assessment proceedings and completed the assessment without providing an adequate opportunity to furnish further clarifications. It was further submitted that the disputed cash deposits were sourced from receipts, bank withdrawals, savings and gifts, and that the assessee possessed the requisite documentary evidence to substantiate these transactions. The assessee also referred to the case of his mother, Mrs. Harvinder Kaur, wherein an addition of Rs. 18 lakh had been made on similar grounds for the same assessment year. The Tribunal had remanded that matter to the Assessing Officer vide its order dated 19 January 2023 in ITA No. 2674/Del/2022. According to the assessee’s written submissions, the subsequent assessment in his mother’s case had been completed accepting the returned income. The assessee accordingly requested similar relief and an opportunity to produce the supporting documents concerning the disputed cash deposits. Considering the factual circumstances and in the interest of justice, the Tribunal directed the Assessing Officer to adjudicate the issues afresh after verifying each documentary evidence furnished by the assessee in respect of the disputed cash deposits. The Departmental Representative raised no objection to the remand. The assessee was also directed to cooperate fully with the Assessing Officer during the fresh proceedings. Accordingly, the appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. The Tribunal did not finally determine the genuineness of the cash deposits or delete the addition on merits, leaving the matter open for fresh examination by the Assessing Officer. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI This appeal by the assessee is emanating from the order of the National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi dated 24.04.2025 relating to assessment year 2017-18. 2. None appeared on behalf of the assessee, despite issue of notice, thus, I am proceeding exparte qua the assessee, after hearing the Ld. DR and perusing the records. However, it is noted that Ld. AR has submitted the Written Submissions wherein, it has been stated the assessee filed his income tax return on 05.08.2017 declaring it at income of Rs. 3,14,900/-. It was the further contention that notice u/s. 142(1) and 143(2) of the Act were issued and all the information, as called for, were filed during income tax proceedings but ignoring all the replies / documentary evidences, AO computed the total taxable income at Rs. 25,06,900/- vide order dated 25.11.2019 after making an addition of Rs. 21,92,000/- on account of cash deposit in her State Bank of India saving A/c No. 10505620802, without giving due opportunity. It was further submitted that the case was time barring on 31.2.2019, but order was passed on 23.11.201 without giving any further opportunity for any clarification. It was further contended that the cash deposit of Rs. 21,92,000/- was fully justified and it was also submitted during the income tax proceedings which was done out of receipts, bank withdrawals, savings, gift etc. and further submitted that assessee has all the requisite documentary evidences in support of cash deposit of RS. 21,92,000/- during AY 2017-18 and can produce if the matter is remitted back to the AO for deciding the issues afresh, after giving adequate opportunity of being heard to the assessee. It was further submitted that addition of Rs. 18,00,000/- was also made on the similar grounds in the case of Mrs. Harvinder Kaur, mother of the assessee, whose case was also set aside and sent back to the AO for reassessment by the ITAT vide order dated 19.1.2023 in ITA No. 2674/Del/2022 (AY 2017-18) and accordingly the said case was reassessed and the fresh income tax assessment order was passed at the returned income filed by the assessee. In view of above, it was contended that similarly the instant case may also be remanded back to the file of the AO for fresh adjudication, after verification of all the documentary evidences in respect of cash deposits. 2.1 In view of the aforesaid factual matrix and in the interest of justice, I remit back the issues to the file of the Assessing Officer with the directions to decide the same afresh, after verifying each and every documentary evidence in respect of cash deposits in dispute, to be filed by the Assessee, for which Ld. DR has no objection. I hold and direct accordingly. However, the assessee is directed through his Ld. AR to fully cooperate with the AO during the proceedings. 3. In the result, the Assessee’s appeal is allowed for statistical purposes. Order pronounced in the Open Court on 09.09.2025.
