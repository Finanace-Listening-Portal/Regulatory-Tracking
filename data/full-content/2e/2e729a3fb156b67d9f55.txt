(ITAT Jodhpur) ITAT Upholds Section 80P(2)(d) Deduction on Interest from Rajasthan State Co-operative Bank Summary: The Jodhpur Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) upheld the deduction claimed by Hanumangarh Kendriya Sahakari Bank Ltd. under Section 80P(2)(d) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, on interest earned from investments in Rajasthan State Co-operative Bank Ltd., Jaipur. The Tribunal relied on the finding that the payee bank was a co-operative society and on its earlier ruling in the assessee’s own case. Facts of the Case Hanumangarh Kendriya Sahakari Bank Ltd., a co-operative bank, earned interest from Rajasthan State Co-operative Bank Ltd. and claimed a deduction under Section 80P(2)(d). The Assessing Officer disallowed the claim. In an earlier round of litigation for AYs 2013–14 to 2016–17, the ITAT restored the matter to the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) [CIT(A)] with a specific direction to ascertain the status of the payee bank. On remand, the CIT(A) found that Rajasthan State Co-operative Bank Ltd. was a co-operative society and allowed the deduction. The CIT(A) also referred to the Tribunal’s order dated 29 May 2025 in the assessee’s own case for AYs 2018–19 and 2020–21. In that order, the Tribunal had upheld the deduction on interest and dividend income from investments in the same bank. The Revenue challenged the CIT(A)’s decision. ITAT’s Findings Section 80P(2)(d) concerns interest or dividend income that a co-operative society derives from investments with another co-operative society. The finding on the payee bank’s status was therefore material to the claim. The Tribunal held that its earlier decision in the assessee’s own case covered the issue. No material was placed before the Bench to show that a higher judicial forum had reversed that decision. Accordingly, the Tribunal declined to interfere with the CIT(A)’s order and dismissed the Revenue’s appeals. It also dismissed two appeals as duplicates because the orders enclosed with them bore the same Document Identification Number (DIN). Cases Discussed: Hanumangarh Kendriya Sahakari Bank Ltd. (ITAT Jodhpur); ITA Nos. 68–71/Jodh/2022; 12/10/2023; AYs 2013-14 to 2016-17. The Tribunal restored the deduction dispute to the CIT(A) for verification of the payee co-operative bank’s status. The present Bench followed this unreversed decision. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT JODHPUR 1. Aforesaid appeals by the assessee have identical facts. First, we take up ITA No. 1005/Jodh/2025 for Assessment Year (AY) 2013-14 which arises out of an order as passed by the Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC) Delhi [hereinafter referred to as CIT(A)] on 22.09.2025 u/s 250 r.w.s. 254. 2. From the facts, it emerges that the assessee is a cooperative bank and it earned interest income from Rajasthan State Co- operative Bank Limited (Jaipur). The only dispute is qua assessee’s claim of deduction u/s 80P(2)(d) on interest income so earned. The Ld. AO denied the deduction and the matter reached up-to Tribunal vide ITA No. 68 to 71/ Jodh/2022 (AYs 2013-14 to 2016-17) dated 12.10.2023 wherein the matter was restored to Ld. CIT(A) with specific direction to ascertain the status of the payee co-operative bank. In the set aside proceedings, Ld. CIT(A) rendered findings that the payee bank was cooperative society and therefore, the assessee was eligible for impugned deduction in terms of various judicial pronouncements. It was also noted that in subsequent decision by Tribunal in assessee’s own case for AY 2018-19 & 2020-21 in ITA Nos 369/Jodh/2023 and 02/JODH/2024, order dated 29.05.2025, similar claim was allowed by dismissing the departmental appeal. It was held that the assessee was eligible to claim deduction u/s 80P(2)(d) for dividend and interest income earned from its investment in Rajasthan State Cooperative Bank Limited, Jaipur. Therefore, the impugned deduction was allowed to the assessee. Aggrieved, the revenue is in further appeal before us. 3. Quite clearly, the impugned issue stand covered in assessee’s favor by the subsequent decision of Tribunal in assessee’s own case as cited above. Nothing has been shown that the aforesaid decision has been reversed by any higher judicial forum, in any manner. This being so, we do not see any reason to interfere in the impugned order. The appeal stands dismissed. 4. The sole issue as contested by revenue in its grounds of appeal in ITA No.1006/Jodh/2025 is identical i.e., assessee’s claim of deduction u/s 80P(2)(d). The impugned order is on similar lines. Therefore, the adjudication of Ld. CIT(A), could not be faulted with. Similar are the facts in ITA No.1008, 1009 & 1010/Jodh/2025 for AYs 2015-16, 2016-17 & 2022-23. All these appeals stand dismissed. 5. ITA Nos.1007/Jodh/2025 & 1008/Jodh/2025 for AYs 2013-14 are merely duplicate appeals for AYs 2013-14 which is evident from the fact that the enclosed orders bear same DIN No. Therefore, both these appeals stand dismissed as duplicate appeals. 6. All the appeals stand dismissed in terms of our above order.
