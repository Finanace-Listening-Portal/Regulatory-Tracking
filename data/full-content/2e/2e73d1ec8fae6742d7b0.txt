print in 29 months at 9.92 per cent from 9.78 per cent in July, as a fresh hardening in energy costs and a series-high reading for manufactured products outweighed a moderation in primary articles, according to data released by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry. That leaves wholesale prices rising at more than double the retail rate. Economists attribute this gap to producer-level pressures in fuel and metals that have yet to feed fully into consumer prices. Rajeev Sharan, head of research criteria & model development at Brickwork Ratings, said the divergence largely reflected fuel and metals costs at the producer level that were not fully reaching consumers, given that the CPI basket is services-heavy and retail fuel prices remain cushioned. But several economists flagged the risk of a lag rather than a lasting divergence. Madan Sabnavis, chief economist at Bank of Baroda said the elevated rates across all three WPI segments — primary articles, fuel and power and manufactured products — were “an indication of core inflation rising well above 4 per cent in CPI in the next couple of months.” Food is back as the primary driver on both gauges — a reversal from a year ago, when the food basket was in outright deflation. Food inflation on the CPI, measured by the Consumer Food Price Index (CFPI), rose to 5.95 per cent in August from 5.52 per cent in July. In the year-ago period, the CFPI was running at -0.64 per cent. Onion inflation more than doubled to 48.27 per cent from 22.54 per cent in July, garlic climbed to 43.60 per cent and ginger stayed extremely high at 73.82 per cent. The food basket showed divergence with tomato and potato deepening into deflation at -31.09 per cent and -13.14 per cent, respectively. Rajani Sinha, chief economist at CareEdge Ratings, pointed to price pressure across several categories beyond vegetables, including sugar, rice and edible oil. “Onion prices have been impacted by delayed new plantings due to late monsoon, resulting in a delay in the arrival of the new harvest in the market. Meanwhile, lower-than-expected sugarcane yields and a shift towards ethanol production have tightened the domestic availability of sugar, thereby pushing up sugar prices,” she noted. The inflation for sugar skyrocketed to 24.2 in August per cent from 4.92 per cent in July. On the wholesale side, the WPI food index — which combines primary and manufactured food — touched a 20-month high of 7. 05 per cent. Rahul Agrawal, principal economist at Icra, said WPI food inflation was led by fruits and vegetables, milk, spices and sugar, and warned it could harden further into September-October on an unfavourable base. The data landed on the same day that RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra met Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman in New Delhi, amid heightened attention to the inflation outlook, crude oil prices and the future course of monetary policy. The meeting came weeks after India posted a record 7.8 per cent GDP growth in the first quarter of the current financial year despite an uncertain global backdrop, and just ahead of the October monetary policy review. Among the 12 major CPI divisions, personal care, social protection and miscellaneous goods and services remained the single biggest driver at 15.17 per cent, as gold and silver prices held at elevated global levels amid geopolitical risk aversion. Silver jewellery inflation stood at 107.11 per cent and gold, diamond and platinum jewellery at 35.53 per cent. Industry body PHDCCI cautioned that demand for precious metals could take a hit in the coming festival and wedding season if prices stay this high. Fuel pressures showed up across both indices. WPI fuel and power inflation accelerated to 22.93 per cent from 20.05 per cent, as crude petroleum and natural gas jumped to 34.41 per cent and mineral oils to 38.48 per cent on the back of the West Asia conflict. “Despite deflation in electricity prices, renewed spurt in crude petroleum prices in August due to West Asia crisis has pushed fuel and power inflation and is expected to remain elevated until a permanent solution to West Asia crisis is achieved,” noted Devendra Pant, chief economist at India Ratings and Research (Ind-Ra). On the CPI, the pass-through was visible in transport, where inflation edged up to 4.6 per cent, and in restaurants and accommodation services, which recorded 8.38 per cent. Sabnavis linked the firming in transport, restaurants and even glassware to the rise in petrol, diesel and other energy prices. Even the normally benign information and communication segment of the CPI ticked up to 2.01 per cent from 0.63 per cent. “This could be partly attributed to robust global demand for memory chips amid the AI boom, which is gradually feeding through into domestic retail prices,” Sinha reckoned. Categories such as health (1.34 per cent), recreation (1.74 per cent) and housing (2.61 per cent) stayed benign, while education services (3.73 per cent) and clothing and footwear (3.56 per cent) were only mildly elevated. Manufacturing pressures, meanwhile, are broadening. WPI manufactured products inflation rose to 8.37 per cent, a series 29-month high, with several sub-sectors — including chemicals, metals and textiles — in double digits. Pant said the persistence of double-digit prints across manufacturing suggested "WPI inflation is turning structural." The output producer price index (PPI), which tracks prices received by producers, rose to 110.8 in August from 109.9 in July, translating into an inflation rate of about 9.8 per cent, up from 9.57 per cent in July. The trial input PPI for manufacturing, published on an experimental basis since March, eased to 104.2 in August from 105.9 in July. Retail inflation in rural areas, at 5.23 per cent, ran well ahead of the 4.31 per cent in urban areas, widening the rural-urban gap. Among states, Telangana recorded the highest inflation at 6.27 per cent, followed by Tamil Nadu (5.92 per cent), Madhya Pradesh (5.55 per cent), Andhra Pradesh (5.53 per cent) and Odisha (5.52 per cent). The twin readings leave the RBI’s rate-setting Monetary Policy Committee (MPC), which meets next on October 5-7, with a harder call. The central bank has kept the repo rate unchanged at 5.25 per cent for four consecutive reviews. With headline CPI still below 5 per cent, Sabnavis said an immediate policy change was unlikely, though he flagged that the inflation threat remained and pegged full-year FY27 inflation at 5-5.2 per cent. Sinha said the October decision would be crucial and that a sustained uptick could strengthen the case for a rate hike, projecting CPI to peak in the third quarter of FY27 and average around 5 per cent for the year.
