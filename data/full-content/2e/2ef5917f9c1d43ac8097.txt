Articles 2 min read RBI DG Murmu says UPI MDR unlikely to push up cash usage; initial apprehension may not come true The introduction of merchant discount rate on large value UPI transactions is unlikely to lead to a rise in cash usage, RBI Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu said, adding that the concern around cash demand could be an initial apprehension as the payments ecosystem adjusts to the new framework. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 18, 2026 at 03:51 PM IST var news_msid = 134332330; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The introduction of merchant discount rate (MDR) on UPI transactions is unlikely to result in a significant shift back towards cash, Reserve Bank of India Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu said.Responding to a question on whether the new MDR regime could lead to greater use of cash, Murmu said he did not expect the apprehension to materialise.“No, I don't think that apprehension will come true. It will be just initial apprehension,” Murmu said during a fireside chat.He said the change represents a major shift in the way costs in the digital payments ecosystem are recovered, which could explain the initial concerns around its impact on cash usage. Advt “I don't think this will have any impact, unlike some voices about whether cash will go up because of this NDR,” he said.Under the new UPI framework announced this week, MDR will apply to specified person-to-merchant transactions above Rs 2,000, while person-to-person transactions will continue to remain free. Payments to merchants up to Rs 2,000 and transactions covered under the zero-MDR framework for small merchants will also remain free. The government has said around 96 per cent of P2M transactions will remain unaffected.RBI issued over 600 draft, final amendment circulars in past yearMurmu also said RBI had issued more than 600 draft circulars and final amendment circulars over the past year, while cautioning that the number should not be interpreted as 600 separate substantive regulatory changes.The central bank has reclassified regulated entities into around 11 categories, and some of the circulars therefore apply across multiple categories, he said.The objective, according to Murmu, is to reduce the compliance burden on regulated entities by making it easier for them to identify which regulations are relevant to their business.“From regulated entity point of view, we are reducing their burden, and shifting towards us,” he said. Advt Murmu also differentiated between the procedural and substantive aspects of regulation. While procedural regulations add to administrative and compliance costs, he said these were “low-hanging fruits” that RBI could clean up.The substantive part of regulation, on the other hand, relates to areas such as permitted activities, conditions attached to those activities, minimum capital and risk management.He said RBI's faster pace of regulatory changes should not necessarily be viewed as deregulation being undertaken too quickly. Rather, the regulator is attempting to keep pace with changes in the market. Advt “When things are changing very fast, in the field, naturally we have to make that speed,” Murmu said. “We are basically tuning ourselves with the way the market is developing.” By Shrishti Sharma , ETBFSI Published On Sep 18, 2026 at 03:51 PM IST Telegram Facebook Copy Link Comments What are your thoughts?
