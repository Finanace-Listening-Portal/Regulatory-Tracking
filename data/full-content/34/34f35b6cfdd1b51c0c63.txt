Articles 4 min read IRDAI proposes tighter commission caps as distributor payouts rise 125% against 28% premium growth The regulator has proposed an all-inclusive commission framework covering incentives, awards, reimbursements and non-cash benefits, with separate caps based on product, channel and distribution effort. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Sep 23, 2026 at 09:52 PM IST var news_msid = 134442303; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) has proposed a major overhaul of insurance distribution commissions, including hard caps linked to product complexity, distribution channel and the effort involved in selling and servicing a policy.The move comes after the regulator found that distributor remuneration in a representative corporate-agent sample grew 125% between FY23 and FY25, while new business premium increased 28% during the same period.Distributor remuneration grows 4-5 times faster than premiumAccording to IRDAI's consultation paper released today, the sample covers approximately 92% of premium procured through corporate agents. Advt Distributor remuneration now accounts for nearly 27% of first-year premium, with rewards and incentives adding another 30%-60% over the base commission.IRDAI said this means remuneration is growing four to five times faster than the business on which it is paid, making base commission alone an incomplete measure of the actual cost of distribution.The regulator also found wide variation in effective payouts across products. Effective payouts on ULIPs range from around 5% to nearly 40%, while other savings products attract effective commissions of 29%-60% of first-year premium, exceeding 65% in some cases.The NBFC corporate agency channel has nearly tripled since FY23, with effective payouts averaging around 42% of new business premium. Around 93% of this business comprises single-premium Group Credit Life products sold at loan origination, while effective payouts on Group Credit Life have risen to around 45%.IRDAI proposes product- and channel-specific capsThe regulator has proposed moving away from a largely flexible commission structure towards maximum commission limits differentiated by product, segment, distribution channel and the effort and complexity involved.Under the proposed framework, open-distribution entities would face lower commission limits than distributors operating under closed architectures. Mandatory or near-mandatory products would have nil or very low commission limits, while complex products that require greater selling and servicing effort could attract higher limits. Advt IRDAI has also proposed lower commissions for group products, lower renewal commissions for products where customers already have high awareness, and lower commissions for single-premium and tax-incentivised products.The framework would also provide additional commission limits for business sourced from underserved areas: 10% over the applicable limit for towns and cities with populations below 10 lakh, excluding the smaller-town category, and 20% for towns with populations below 50,000 and rural areas.For life insurance, the proposed first-year commission for individual non-linked and linked products would range from 5%-20% for distribution entities depending on the premium payment term, compared with 6.25%-25% for individual agents. Advt For policies with a premium payment term of 10 years or more, the proposed first-year cap is 20% for distribution entities and 25% for agents. Renewal commission would generally be capped at 3% for distribution entities and 5% for agents, with a provision for renewal commission to increase gradually after the sixth year, subject to a maximum of 7%.The framework also proposes 1% commission for distribution entities and 2% for agents on individual single-premium savings products. For individual pure-term single-premium policies, the proposed limits are 7.5% and 10%, respectively, while multi-year pure-term policies could attract 25% and 30%. Advt For group pure-term single-premium policies, the proposed commission is 1.5% for distribution entities, subject to a ₹20 lakh cap, and 2% for agents, subject to a ₹30 lakh cap.Motor insurance commissions also targetedThe consultation paper separately flags motor insurance, where the average commission is estimated at 24%, with a range of 13%-50%.OEM brokers and motor insurance service providers (MISPs), which together have around 30% market share across new and old vehicles, receive an average 24% commission, with the maximum going up to 31%.In FY25, these channels generated around ₹29,000 crore of motor premium and received almost ₹7,050 crore in commissions.IRDAI has proposed a nil commission for new-vehicle third-party motor insurance for distribution entities, while the proposed caps for own-damage, personal accident and legal-liability covers are substantially lower than some prevailing payouts.The regulator has also proposed a 16% commission cap for lending institutions in certain motor-insurance arrangements, alongside measures to prevent compulsory bundling of loans and insurance.All incentives to be treated as commissionA key proposal is to make the definition of commission all-inclusive.IRDAI has proposed that direct and indirect payments, monetary and non-monetary incentives, awards, reimbursement of selling expenses, brand-value payments to related parties, gifts and trips should all be treated as commission for regulatory purposes.The regulator has also proposed that commissions paid to PoSPs engaged by an insurance distribution entity should come out of the principal entity's commission, rather than being treated as an additional layer of remuneration.The proposed framework seeks to move distribution towards a single level of remuneration and away from layered arrangements.The consultation paper also proposes that insurers conduct mandatory cost audits covering all expenses, including payouts and non-monetary incentives to intermediaries. IDEs with insurance-related revenue above ₹100 crore would also be subject to cost audit.The proposals are part of IRDAI's broader attempt to bring distribution costs and remuneration closer to the actual effort involved in selling and servicing insurance. Stakeholders have been invited to submit comments on the consultation paper by October 25, 2026.
