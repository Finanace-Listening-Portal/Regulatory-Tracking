Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Textronix India Private Limited Vs ACIT (Karnataka High Court) Failure to Give Effect to an ITAT Order Within Section 153 Limitation Bars Further Proceedings and Requires Deletion of the Outstanding Demand: Karnataka High Court Summary: The Karnataka High Court held in Textronix India Private Limited Vs Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax and Others that further proceedings to give effect to an Income Tax Appellate Tribunal order were barred once the applicable deadline under Section 153 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 had expired. The Assessing Officer had taken no action for more than a year and a half after the deadline. The Court restored the return of income for Assessment Year 2018–19 and directed the department to remove references to the outstanding demand and pending proceedings from its income tax portal. Scrutiny Assessment and Tribunal Relief The dispute arose from the scrutiny assessment of Textronix India Private Limited for Assessment Year 2018–19. The company challenged that assessment before the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Bengaluru, in IT(TP)A No. 759/Bang/2022. By an order dated 8 May 2023, the Tribunal partly allowed the company’s appeal. The first respondent, the Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax, was required to give effect to the Tribunal’s directions. The Assessing Officer did not pass an order implementing those directions. Meanwhile, the income tax portal continued to show an outstanding demand of ₹5,28,33,000 and accrued interest of ₹2,64,16,500. Penalty proceedings initiated by a notice dated 3 March 2023 also continued to appear on the portal as open. The company approached the High Court under Article 226 of the Constitution to obtain a declaration on limitation and have the portal records corrected. Company’s Claim That the Deadline Had Expired Textronix argued that, under Section 153, the Assessing Officer had until 31 March 2025 to give effect to the Tribunal’s order. By the time the High Court heard the petition, more than one and a half years had passed since that date without an order giving effect to the Tribunal’s directions. The company submitted that further proceedings were therefore time-barred and that the position disclosed in its filed return should be restored. The company also sought consequential relief from the Centralised Processing Centre (CPC). It asked the Court to direct the CPC to delete the outstanding demand and interest from the portal and remove the entry showing the penalty proceedings as open. Its request for portal correction depended on the Court first deciding the legal consequence of the Assessing Officer’s failure to act within time. Revenue Did Not Dispute the Deadline or Inaction Counsel for the Revenue did not dispute that the limitation period for giving effect to the Tribunal’s order expired on 31 March 2025. Counsel also did not dispute that the Assessing Officer had passed no order implementing the Tribunal’s directions even after another year and a half had elapsed. The Court recorded that the Revenue could not contest the consequence of that failure in the circumstances of the case. The Court considered whether an extension could affect the outcome. It observed that even if the respondents could plead some extension, it could not cover a delay of that length. The Court therefore accepted the company’s submission that the Assessing Officer could no longer take further proceedings to implement the Tribunal’s order for the assessment year in question. Restoration of the Return and Correction of the Portal The Court treated the direction to the CPC as consequential to the declaration on limitation. Once the company succeeded on that issue, the CPC had no separate adjudication to undertake before implementing the Court’s direction. Its task was to correct the portal records to reflect the outcome of the writ petition. The operative order declared that further proceedings after the Tribunal’s order dated 8 May 2023 stood barred under Section 153. It restored the income tax return as filed and declared that the petitioner would not be liable to pay any taxes for Assessment Year 2018–19, with the filed return restored. The Court separately directed the fourth respondent, the Assistant Director of Income Tax at the CPC, to delete references to the demand and pending proceedings for that year upon filing of a certified copy of the High Court’s order. Final Decision Justice B. M. Shyam Prasad partly allowed Writ Petition No. 33551 of 2026 on 3 November 2026. The judgment is reported as 2026:KHC:61033. The relief followed from the undisputed deadline and the Assessing Officer’s failure to issue an order within it. The Court’s direction covered both the demand and the pending proceedings reflected on the portal for the relevant year. The ruling concerns limitation for giving effect to an appellate order. The Tribunal had already partly allowed the company’s appeal, and the High Court addressed what could still be done after the implementation deadline expired. On the facts before it, the continued display of the assessment demand and pending proceedings on the portal could not preserve those entries once the Court declared further proceedings time-barred and restored the filed return. FULL TEXT OF THE ORIGINAL JUDGMENT/ORDER The petitioner has sought for the following reliefs: i. Declaring that any proceedings for passing of an order giving effect to the order dated 08.05.2023 (Annexure – G) passed by the Income-tax Appellate Tribunal for the assessment year 2018-19 is barred by limitation in terms of Section 153 of the Act; ii. Declaring that the petitioner is not liable to remit any taxes for the assessment year 2018-19 and the admitted tax liability in its return of income be restored; iii. Direct the 4th respondent to delete the outstanding demand of Rs.5,28,33,000/- and accrued interest of rs.2,64,16,500 reflecting on the Income Tax Portal; iv. Declaring that the penalty proceedings initiated vide notice dated 03.03.2023 Annexure-F as being unsustainable and directing the 4th Respondent to delete the penalty proceedings erroneously shown on the Income-Tax portal as being ‘open’. “ The proceedings relate to scrutiny assessment for the assessment year 2018-19. The petitioner has succeeded in the appeal with the ITAT in IT(TP)A No.759/Bang/2022 with the appeal being partly allowed vide the order dated 08.05.2023 [Annexure-G], and the first respondent had to give effect to the ITAT’s order dated 08.05.2023. 2. Ms. Mahima Goud, the learned counsel for the petitioner, submits the following. The first respondent, given the provisions of Section 153 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 [for short, ‘the IT Act’], had to give effect to the ITAT’s order before 31.03.2025. The first respondent has not given effect to this order for more than 1½ years after the expiry of the time and therefore the petitioner is entitled for declaration in terms of prayer [a] and [b]. The learned counsel also submits that consequently the fourth respondent may be directed to delete the reference to any outstanding under the assessment order or the pending proceedings relating to the assessment. 3. Mr. Nirmal Mathew, a learned standing counsel for the first to third respondents and fifth respondent who accepts notice for these respondents, along with Mr. E.I. Sanmathi, a learned senior standing counsel, is heard for the disposal of the petition observing that if the petitioner succeeds in this petition, the direction to the fourth respondent will be consequential without any adjudication by the fourth respondent. Mr. Nirmal Mathew cannot contest that with the ITAT’s order being on 08.05.2023, the limitation for giving effect to the same would expire on 31.03.2025 or that the first respondent has not passed orders to give effect to those directions even after 1½ years from such date. 4. Crucially, Mr. Nirmal Mathew cannot dispute the consequence that would befall if the ITAT’s orders are not given effect to, in which event, the petitioner must succeed. The fact that the orders have not been passed to give effect to the Tribunal’s order for more than 1½ years from 31.03.2025 also indicates that even if the respondents could plead some extension, it could not be for this long. In which event, the petitioner must succeed and there must be a declaration as sought for with a direction to the fourth respondent to delete the reference to an outstanding demand as also drop the pending proceedings. As such, the following: ORDER [A] The petition is allowed in-part declaring that further proceedings after the ITAT’s order dated 08.05.2023 [Annexure-G] stands barred by limitation under Section 153 of the IT Act and consequentially, the petitioner will not be liable to pay any taxes for the assessment years 2018-19 with the ITR filed being restored. [B] The fourth respondent, when a certified copy of this order is filed, shall ensure that the reference to the demand and pending proceedings for this assessment year are deleted from the website the department’s IT portal.
