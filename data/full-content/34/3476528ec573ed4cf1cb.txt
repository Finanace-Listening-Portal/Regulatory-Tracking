Summary: The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), Chennai Bench, in LTM Limited Vs Commissioner of GST & Central Excise, Chennai South Commissionerate (GSTAT Chennai); Filing No. 2626059101000236, dated 6 October 2026, held that a taxpayer need not make a fresh pre-deposit under Section 112(8)(b) of the CGST Act, 2017, where the amount already deposited during the first appeal sufficiently covers the cumulative statutory requirement calculated on the tax demand surviving before the Tribunal. The dispute originated from an adjudication order confirming a tax demand of Rs. 3,29,47,353/-, against which the taxpayer deposited Rs. 32,94,735/- under Section 107(6). Subsequently, the First Appellate Authority upheld only Rs. 53,90,420/- while remanding the remaining demand. Consequently, the cumulative pre-deposit requirement for the Tribunal appeal, calculated at 20% of the surviving disputed tax, amounted to Rs. 10,78,084/-. The Revenue nevertheless insisted upon a separate additional deposit of Rs. 5,39,042/-, relying on the expression “in addition to” appearing in Section 112(8)(b). Rejecting this interpretation, the Tribunal held that the statutory provision establishes a cumulative monetary requirement rather than mandating a separate payment where sufficient funds already stand deposited with the Government. It emphasised that pre-deposit is not an independent levy but an amount available for adjustment against the disputed tax liability. Requiring another payment despite an existing excess deposit would create an unnecessary circular exercise of refunding and redepositing public money. The Tribunal relied upon Ashirwad Food Industries v. Union of India, Manoranjan Dash v. Commissioner, CT & GST, Odisha, and MMTC-PAMP India (P.) Ltd. v. Deputy Commissioner, besides referring to CBIC Circular No. 224/18/2024-GST dated 11.07.2024. Accordingly, it directed appropriation of Rs. 10,78,084/- from the existing deposit, generation of FORM GST APL-04, necessary adjustments through the Electronic Liability Register and FORM GST DRC-03A, and admission of the appeal upon completion of the prescribed procedural requirements. The ruling reinforces that GST appellate pre-deposit provisions must secure revenue interests without compelling taxpayers to make duplicative payments. Table of Contents▸ 1. Introduction 2. Introduction The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), Chennai Bench, in a significant order, has held that a taxpayer is not required to make a fresh pre-deposit for filing an appeal before the Tribunal, where the amount already deposited at the first appellate stage sufficiently covers the cumulative statutory requirement computed on the tax demand remaining in dispute. The Bench comprising Shri Praveen Kumar Jain, Judicial Member and Shri Shaik Khader Rahman, Technical Member, has thereby given a purposive and pragmatic interpretation to the expression “in addition to” appearing in Section 112(8)(b) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. 2. Factual Matrix The dispute emanated from an adjudication order dated 29.10.2021, whereby a tax demand of Rs. 3,29,47,353/- along with interest and penalty was confirmed against M/s LTM Limited. In order to prefer its first appeal under Section 107 of the CGST Act, 2017, the Appellant deposited a sum of Rs. 32,94,735/- through FORM GST DRC-03 on 28.01.2022, being 10% of the original disputed tax. Vide Appellate Order dated 31.03.2023, the First Appellate Authority remanded a demand of Rs. 2,75,56,933/- and upheld a demand of Rs. 53,90,420/-, comprising transitional credit of Rs. 17,965/- and input tax credit of Rs. 53,72,455/-. The remanded demand was subsequently reduced to Rs. 17,43,997/- by a fresh adjudication order dated 13.08.2024, which formed the subject matter of separate proceedings. The appeal before the Tribunal was confined only to the demand of Rs. 53,90,420/- upheld by the First Appellate Authority. The Registry raised a defect, seeking reflection of the pre-deposit in the Electronic Liability Register against the relevant demand ID. 3. Contentions of the Parties On behalf of the Appellant, it was contended that: a. The combined pre-deposit requirement under Sections 107(6) and 112(8), computed on the surviving disputed tax, amounted to Rs. 10,78,084/-; b. Since a sum of Rs. 32,94,735/- already stood deposited and verified, no additional payment was warranted; c. The demand ID and FORM GST APL-04 had not been generated on the portal, thereby preventing adjustment through FORM GST DRC-03A. On behalf of the Revenue, though the earlier payment and issuance of DRC-04 were admitted, it was contended that Section 112(8)(b) mandates a separate payment of Rs. 5,39,042/-, inasmuch as the Tribunal-stage deposit must be paid “in addition to” the amount deposited under Section 107(6). 4. Computation Adopted by the Tribunal The Tribunal adopted the following computation: Sl. No. Particulars Basis Amount (Rs.) (a) Tax in dispute in present appeal, upheld by OIA No. 16/2023 — 53,90,420 (b) Pre-deposit attributable to first appeal Section 107(6)(b): 10% of (a) 5,39,042 (c) Further pre-deposit for this appeal Section 112(8)(b): 10% of (a) 5,39,042 (d) Aggregate pre-deposit required [(b) + (c)] 20% of (a) 10,78,084 (e) Amount actually deposited through DRC-03 dated 28.01.2022 Verified 32,94,735 5. Findings and Ratio Decidendi The Hon’ble Tribunal, rejecting the Revenue’s interpretation, held as under: i) Relevant demand is the surviving demand: The computation must proceed on Rs. 53,90,420/-, being the tax arising from the impugned appellate order and remaining in dispute before the Tribunal, and not on the much larger original demand. The remanded demand is outside the scope of the present appeal. ii) “In addition to” denotes cumulative requirement, not fresh challan: The words “in addition to” in Section 112(8)(b) establish a cumulative requirement of 10% + 10% = 20%. They do not mandate a fresh payment where sufficient money already lies with the Revenue against the same disputed demand. iii) Nature of pre-deposit: A pre-deposit is not a separate levy. It is a portion of the disputed tax itself, liable to be adjusted against the demand if the appeal fails, and refundable with applicable interest if the appeal succeeds. iv) No watertight compartments: Deposits under Sections 107(6) and 112(8) are not isolated compartments. Book adjustment inter se is permissible. v) Avoidance of circular exercise: The Revenue’s interpretation would compel it to refund the excess deposit with interest and thereafter require the taxpayer to re-deposit a part of the same money. Such a circular exercise serves no revenue purpose and is contrary to legislative intent. vi) Procedural condition, not charging provision: Section 112(8) is a procedural condition governing access to appellate remedy, and not a charging provision requiring duplicative payment. 6. Precedents and Circular Relied Upon The Tribunal placed reliance upon: Ashirwad Food Industries v. Union of India – Jharkhand High Court, wherein an earlier deposit exceeding the requirement arising after reduction of demand was held sufficient; Manoranjan Dash v. Commissioner, CT & GST, Odisha – GSTAT, Cuttack Bench, including the order affirming its position after Revenue sought reconsideration; MMTC-PAMP India (P.) Ltd. v. Deputy Commissioner – Madras High Court, which accounted for amounts already remitted while determining the remaining pre-deposit requirement; and CBIC Circular No. 224/18/2024-GST dated 11.07.2024, recognising adjustment of qualifying payments, including amounts paid through DRC-03 and adjusted through DRC-03A, towards pre-deposits under Sections 107 and 112. The principle governing the cumulative computation of pre-deposit has also been examined in the TaxGuru analysis GSTAT Pre-Deposit Quantification: Emerging Principle After Manoranjan Dash. 7. Directions Issued The Tribunal directed that: a. A sum of Rs. 10,78,084/- from the verified earlier deposit be appropriated towards the pre-deposit for the present appeal, which shall not be counted towards any other proceeding. The Appellant’s legal rights qua the balance amount were expressly preserved; b. The jurisdictional proper officer shall map Rs. 10,78,084/- against the impugned demand in the Electronic Liability Register once the demand ID is generated, whereafter the Appellant shall file DRC-03A in accordance with the CBIC Circular; c. The First Appellate Authority shall electronically generate APL-04 within one month of the order; d. The departmental representative shall upload the same to the GSTAT portal immediately upon generation; and e. The Appellant shall upload an undertaking preventing reuse or adjustment of the relevant pre-deposit against any other demand. The filing defect raised by the Registry was accordingly held to stand cured, and admission of the appeal was directed after compliance with the portal, adjustment and undertaking requirements. 8. Conclusion The order lays down a salutary principle that pre-deposit provisions must be construed to secure the Revenue, and not to harass the taxpayer by demanding duplicative payments. Where the Revenue already holds an amount in excess of the aggregate statutory requirement qua the surviving demand, insisting upon a fresh deposit would be hyper-technical, unjust and contrary to the scheme of the Act. ***** Disclaimer: This article is for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice.
