Articles 4 min read GFF 2026: Financial inclusion has to move beyond bank accounts to credit, investments, protection: BoB MD Debadatta Chand India’s financial inclusion journey has reached a stage where opening bank accounts is no longer enough, with the next challenge being to connect millions of customers to credit, investments and protection, Bank of Baroda MD & CEO Debadatta Chand said at GFF 2026. He said digital architecture, financial and digital literacy, and AI-led credit assessment will be critical to move customers from basic banking access to genuine financial empowerment. Advt He outlined the next stage of banking around three broad needs, deposits, borrowing and investments, arguing that customers who have entered the formal financial system need to be connected with credit as well as insurance and investment products.“The maturity has been achieved in terms of connecting with banks. Now the time has come to translate this maturity into financial empowerment and into the financial economy," he said.He said the large customer base created through financial inclusion schemes represents a significant opportunity for banks, provided they can build the digital architecture needed to offer customers more relevant financial products.The challenge, according to Chand, is particularly important in the context of protection and investment, where large sections of newly banked customers remain under-served.Digital is the only way to reach millions at scaleChand said physical branches alone cannot support the next stage of financial inclusion, given the sheer size of India's customer base.Banking therefore needs to become increasingly digital and conversational, making transactions and financial services simpler for customers who may not be comfortable navigating conventional banking interfaces.He said Bank of Baroda is working on an upgraded version of its digital banking platform, including conversational banking capabilities that could allow customers to interact with the bank through voice and natural language. Advt "If you have a large number of customers, asking them to come to physical branches is not going to be possible. There are limitations to how many customers physical branches can handle. You necessarily have to do things digitally," he added.Chand said the ability to transact through conversational interfaces could be particularly empowering for customers who are not digitally sophisticated but still need access to banking services.Financial and digital literacy critical alongside technologyHowever, simply putting financial services on digital platforms will not be enough, Chand cautioned. Banks will also need to build financial and digital literacy among customers as technology expands access. Advt He flagged cybersecurity and the complexity of multiple digital systems as key challenges that need to be addressed while taking customers towards more sophisticated financial products.“There are two things which are very critical for a bank like us. One is financial literacy and the second is digital literacy. Unless you create capacity amongst your customers with regard to both, there will always be challenges," he said.The need for literacy will become even more important as customers move beyond simple payments and deposits towards borrowing, investments and insurance, he said.AI could change small-ticket credit Advt The next major opportunity, Chand said, is using digital data and AI to improve credit access for small businesses and individuals who may not fit traditional lending models.Traditional banking remains heavily dependent on established credit parameters, making it difficult to efficiently serve borrowers with limited credit histories or small-ticket requirements.Chand said technology can help banks create better credit linkages for these customers by using digital platforms and AI-based assessment.“Traditional banking loans are all given against existing credit parameters. There is a huge opportunity to upgrade these customers in terms of their credit linkages, and obviously the digital platform and usage of AI are going to be critical," he said.This could be particularly relevant for small businesses and SMEs, where the ticket size may be too small for traditional underwriting processes to be economically viable.The opportunity is to use digital footprints and technology-led assessment to understand customers better and enable faster, more targeted access to credit.For Chand, this marks the transition from the first chapter of financial inclusion, getting people into the banking system, to the next chapter, where technology enables them to actually use the financial system to build businesses, access credit, invest and protect themselves.Click here to read our coverage of the Global FinTech Fest 2026.
