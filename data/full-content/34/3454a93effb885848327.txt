} } Listen to This ArticleThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is likely to take further measures to contain rupee volatility, Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said on Tuesday, after recent steps by the central bank helped the currency recover from near-record lows. “The rupee has reached a low and the RBI is making a lot of efforts through its regulatory powers to make sure that the rupee stays stable. I am sure there are efforts which the RBI would take further to make sure that the rupee doesn’t become too volatile,” she said at the Bloomberg New Economy Forum. The finance minister also said she would keenly hear discussions on long-term capital gains (LTCG) tax and the securities transaction tax (STT) on equities, amid a wider debate over tax treatment across asset classes. Domestic equities posted their biggest single-day gains in four months on Tuesday, helped by Sitharaman’s remarks. She declined to say whether the government was considering scrapping LTCG tax to attract foreign portfolio investment (FPI), citing the upcoming Union Budget. “We are very close to the Budget, and preparation for the Budget has started. It may not be the best time for me to say what I will or what I will not consider,” she said. Sitharaman acknowledged that India was facing capital outflows as investors increasingly channelled funds into artificial intelligence (AI) capabilities in the US. Sitharaman said aligning the tax treatment of different asset classes remained a priority, arguing that differences in taxation should not purely drive investors towards one asset over another. “Two years ago, we had brought similarity in all the asset classes, and people thought I’m increasing the tax. I wasn’t increasing the tax, I was bringing all of them to be at par… so that the investor is not speculating as to which one gives them greater leverage,” she said. The RBI announced a series of measures over the weekend to curb volatility in the foreign exchange market. These included opening a special dollar window for three state-owned oil marketing companies, restricting the rebooking of cancelled foreign exchange derivative contracts and lowering the threshold for foreign exchange derivative transactions that can be undertaken without establishing an underlying exposure to $5 million from $100 million. The rupee settled at 95.28 against the dollar on Tuesday; it was at 96.73 on Friday. Sitharaman said policymakers remained focused on maintaining sound economic fundamentals through fiscal discipline and adequate foreign exchange reserves, attributing much of the rupee’s depreciation to external factors. The currency has come under particular pressure since the West Asia conflict began in late February, weakening about 6 per cent against the dollar. Tejas Desai, partner and leader of financial services tax at EY India, said global factors, relative valuations and corporate earnings remained key drivers of foreign investor flows, but the combined burden of LTCG tax and STT also affected the appeal of Indian equities. “India should undertake a holistic review of the tax burden on foreign portfolio investors, including the role of STT, to improve net returns and reduce friction costs,” he said. Naitik Doshi, director at Nangia Global, said listed equity gains in India attract LTCG tax of 12.5 per cent, in addition to STT, while competing financial hubs such as Singapore and Hong Kong do not levy capital gains tax on listed shares. “Any rationalisation, whether through LTCG relief, STT calibration or parity across asset classes, would improve post-tax returns, deepen domestic participation and make India more attractive to foreign investors,” he added.
