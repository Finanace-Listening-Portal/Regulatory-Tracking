Gross NPA fell to 1.11%.The brokerage retained BUY, cut its target price to Rs 950 from Rs 975 and expects RoA of 1.8% to 1.9% over FY27-29.HDFC Bank’s net interest margin (NIM) may have bottomed out in the September quarter, but the cost of foreign currency non-resident (FCNR(B)) deposits could temper the pace of recovery in the coming quarters. The bank’s reported NIM contracted 10 basis points sequentially to 3.16% in Q2FY27, despite slower corporate growth, as its cost of funds remained flat. The impact of a 25 basis points rate hike is expected to begin reflecting in margins from December 2026, although the full quarter cost of FCNR(B) deposits will partly offset the benefit.The bank expects its NIM trajectory to remain neutral to positive in Q3FY27 and improve thereafter, supported by the repricing of floating rate loans and a gradual shift towards better yielding retail and micro, small and medium enterprise (MSME) advances. About 70% of its loan portfolio carries floating interest rates, the majority of which is linked to the external benchmark lending rate (EBLR). The assessment is based on Axis Securities’ analysis of the bank’s Q2FY27 performance and outlook. Advt The bank’s deposit mobilisation strategy remains central to the margin outlook. FCNR(B) mobilisation contributed 400 basis points to deposit growth, while the bank mobilised Rs 1,103.4 billion under these deposits. Against this, it provided $5.7 billion in loans through its overseas branches. However, a shift in the funding mix towards FCNR(B) term deposits and greater reliance on borrowings offset the benefits of additional tier 1 bonds called in August 2026 and back-book repricing.The growth trajectoryHDFC Bank’s deposits grew 19% year on year and 5% sequentially in Q2FY27, led by term deposits, which increased 23% year on year and 6% sequentially. CASA deposits grew at a slower pace of 11% year on year and 3% sequentially, taking the CASA ratio to 31.6%, against 33.9% a year earlier and 32.3% in the preceding quarter. The bank attributed the softer CASA performance to one-off outflows from small MSME and garment-trade deposits, as well as deposits inherited from e-HDFC, and expects these trends to normalise.HDFC Bank added about 1.7 million liability relationships during the quarter, an increase of 25% sequentially. Its retail CASA ratio remained steady at around 40%, in line with its longer-term trend. Faster term-deposit growth was a conscious decision as the bank sought to substitute Rs 2.6 trillion of borrowings inherited from e-HDFC over the past three years. Over time, it aims to reduce reliance on corporate and bulk liabilities and improve its retail deposit mix.The bank sees further scope to expand its deposit franchise through its branch network. About 80% of deposit mobilisation takes place through branches, and more than 54% of its 765 districts have a market share 2 to 3 percentage points below its average. This provides room to strengthen its liability base and improve the deposit mix. Advt Operational improvements and faster retail and MSME lending could also support the recovery. Retail disbursements exceeded Rs 900 billion in Q2FY27, compared with Rs 760 billion in the preceding quarter. Business banking growth accelerated to 28% year on year and 9% sequentially, while advances grew 16% year on year and 5% sequentially. The bank is targeting a better mix of higher yielding retail and MSME loans while maintaining a measured approach to corporate lending.The bank’s reported asset quality improved during the quarter. Gross and net non-performing asset ratios declined to 1.11% and 0.37%, respectively, from 1.17% and 0.41% in the preceding quarter. The slippage ratio fell to 1% from 1.25%, while calculated credit costs rose to 45 basis points from 41 basis points sequentially. Advt The brokerage expects HDFC Bank’s return on assets to improve to 1.8% to 1.9% over FY27 to FY29, supported by margin expansion, improving operating productivity, fee income and benign credit costs. It has raised its net interest income estimates by 2.5% for FY28 and 3.3% for FY29, and its profit after tax estimates by 3.7% and 4.5%, respectively. It has, however, cut its target price to Rs 950 per share from Rs 975 while maintaining its BUY recommendation.The key risks to the recovery remain the bank’s ability to mobilise deposits to sustain credit growth and the pace at which higher-cost borrowings are replaced with lower-cost deposits. These factors will determine how much of the expected improvement in lending yields translates into higher margins.
