Summary: The amendments to Sections 15(3)(b) and 34 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, scheduled to take effect from 1 November 2026, introduce an important change in the GST treatment of post-sale discounts. Following the recommendations of the 56th GST Council meeting, the earlier requirements that discounts must be established under an agreement entered into before or at the time of supply and specifically linked to relevant invoices are being removed. Under the amended framework, suppliers may reduce the taxable value of supplies by issuing GST credit notes under Section 34, provided the recipient reverses the input tax credit attributable to the discount. Notification No. 03/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026 appoints the commencement date for these amendments. The corresponding amendment to Section 34 expressly recognises post-supply discounts as a ground for issuing credit notes. The article also examines the withdrawal of Circular No. 212/6/2024-GST through Circular No. 253/10/2025-GST dated 1 October 2025. The earlier circular prescribed declarations or professional certificates to establish recipient ITC reversal where portal verification was unavailable. Its withdrawal removes that particular documentary mechanism. The revised provisions are expected to reduce procedural difficulties, provide greater commercial flexibility and limit disputes over discounts determined after supply. However, recipient ITC reversal remains an essential statutory safeguard, and suppliers must continue to comply with the applicable requirements governing GST credit notes. Post-Sale Discounts to Be Granted Through Credit Notes: Agreement Requirement Omitted The Goods and Services Tax (GST) Council, in its 56th meeting held on 3rd September 2025, made significant recommendations to amend the CGST Act, 2017, particularly in relation to the treatment of post-sale discounts. The recommended amendments include the omission of Section 15(3)(b)(i) and (ii) of the CGST Act, 2017, and the substitution of Section 15(3)(b) to provide for post-sale discounts through GST credit notes under Section 34, along with consequential amendments to Section 34. These changes also provide for corresponding ITC reversal by the recipient. Further, Circular No. 212/6/2024-GST dated 26 June 2024 is proposed to be rescinded. The amendments to Sections 15(3)(b) and 34 have now come into effect from 1 November 2026 vide Notification No. 03/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026. Omission of Section 15(3)(b)(i) of the CGST Act, 2017 (a) Under the existing provisions, a supplier can reduce the value of supply by a post-supply discount only if the following conditions are satisfied: The discount is established in an agreement entered into at or before the time of supply. The discount is linked to the relevant invoice. The ITC attributable to the discount has been reversed by the recipient, based on the document issued by the supplier. (b) In the author’s view, businesses found it challenging to compute the discount amount accurately before or at the time of supply. (c) The requirement to link credit notes to specific invoices was removed with effect from 1 February 2019. However, the existing rules continued to require that each discount be linked to the corresponding invoice. (d) In practice, suppliers have no reliable way to verify whether their customers have actually reversed the ITC after receiving GST credit notes. This makes it difficult for suppliers to justify reducing their taxable value and GST liability. (e) The statute did not clearly specify the documentation required to reduce the taxable value when discounts are given. In practice, however, the industry has generally used GST credit notes to account for post-supply incentives. (f) Omitting Section 15(3)(b)(i) resolves these persistent practical challenges and better reflects commercial reality. Amendment of Section 15(3)(b) and Section 34 of the CGST Act, 2017 (a) The amendment introduces a clear mechanism under Section 15(3)(b) for adjusting post-supply discounts by issuing a GST credit note to reduce the value of supply. (b) At present, Section 34 allows a GST credit note to be issued in four situations: The taxable value charged is higher than the actual value. A higher GST rate has been applied. Goods are returned by the recipient. The goods or services supplied are found to be deficient. (c) The amendment adds a fifth situation: a post-sale discount under Section 15(3)(b) is now a valid reason for issuing a GST credit note. (d) It is also important to note the amendment made by Section 126 of the Finance Act, 2025, which inserted a proviso in Section 34. The proviso states that no reduction in the supplier’s output tax liability is allowed if: the recipient, if registered, has not reversed the ITC attributable to the credit note; or in other cases, the tax burden has been passed on to another person. (e) The amendment recommended by the Council in its 56th meeting is aligned with this Finance Act amendment of 2025, which came into effect on 1 October 2025 vide Notification No. 16/2025-Central Tax dated 17 September 2025. 3. Rescinding Circular No. 212/6/2024-GST dated 26 June 2024 (a) The above circular was issued to provide a mechanism for suppliers to comply with the conditions of Section 15(3)(b)(ii), ensuring that recipients reverse the ITC on post-supply discounts received. (b) The circular acknowledged that the common portal did not have any functionality for suppliers or tax officers to verify whether the recipient had proportionately reversed the ITC. (c) It therefore clarified that, until such functionality was made available, the supplier could obtain a declaration or certificate from the recipient confirming that the required proportionate ITC reversal had been made for the credit note issued. The requirements were: Tax amount (CGST + SGST + IGST + Cess) up to Rs. 5,00,000: self-declaration by the recipient. Tax amount (CGST + SGST + IGST + Cess) exceeding Rs. 5,00,000: certificate from a Chartered Accountant or Cost Accountant of the recipient. (d) With the amendment and the introduction of the Invoice Management System (IMS), these declarations are no longer required. The mechanism provided by the circular has therefore become redundant, leading to its rescission. (e) The circular has been rescinded by the issuance of Circular No. 253/10/2025-GST dated 1st October 2025. Implications of the Amendments Ease of doing business By removing the requirements of a pre-supply agreement and invoice linkage, the amendment reduces paperwork and disputes between suppliers and tax authorities. Businesses gain more flexibility to grant trade discounts or incentive rebates after the sale. Tax neutrality and compliance balance: While suppliers have been given relief, the safeguard of ITC reversal by recipients ensures that the exchequer does not suffer revenue leakage. This maintains a balance between business facilitation and revenue protection. Reduction in litigation: Post-sale discounts have been a contentious issue since the inception of GST, with taxpayers and authorities often taking conflicting interpretations. The amended framework provides a uniform mechanism for treating such discounts, which is likely to reduce interpretational differences and litigation. Alignment with trade practices: In practice, discounts are often agreed after observing sales volumes, seasonal performance or market conditions. Linking discounts to credit notes, instead of rigid invoice-linkage conditions, makes the law more consistent with how businesses actually operate. Conclusion The amendments to Section 15(3)(b) and Section 34 of the CGST Act mark a significant reform in the valuation rules for post-sale discounts. By eliminating procedural rigidities and aligning the law with trade realities, the amendments simplify compliance while preserving tax neutrality through ITC reversal. Rescinding the earlier circular and providing statutory clarity will not only reduce disputes but also encourage a transparent and consistent approach for both suppliers and recipients. These changes bring long-awaited relief to businesses, while ensuring that GST continues to operate on its foundational principle of tax on value addition. ***** Suggestions or feedback can be sent to the author at [email protected].
