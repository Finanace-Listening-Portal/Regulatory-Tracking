Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Updated On Sep 24, 2026 at 05:42 PM IST var news_msid = 134455407; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook New Delhi, Sep 24 (IANS) The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) has proposed a new ceiling for commissions on individual life insurance policies, aiming to curb upfront sales incentives and encourage policyholders to persist with multiyear payment plans.The proposed overhaul of insurance distribution regulations could sharply compress commissions for distributors, particularly in health and term insurance, while creating a more mixed impact for insurers, according to Jefferies.The brokerage said the proposed framework could cut new-business commissions in health and term insurance by at least half, while commissions on new motor own-damage business could fall by about one-third. Advt For distributors such as PB Fintech and Turtlemint, the impact could be material because Jefferies estimates that a 10% reduction in new-business commission rates could translate into a 10-12% decline in earnings.Health, term commissions face steep cutsUnder the proposed structure, commissions would be capped based on product, distribution channel and architecture, with different limits for insurance distribution entities and agents.For general insurance, Jefferies highlights proposed caps including 15% for first-time individual health business through insurance distribution entities and 20% for agents, while renewal commissions would be capped at 5% and 10%, respectively. For motor own-damage, the proposed cap is 5% for insurance distribution entities and 10% for agents, while motor third-party insurance would carry lower caps.The framework also proposes specific limits for insurance sold alongside loans or credit. For motor OD/PA/LL products packaged with loans or credit, the proposed commission cap is 5%, while first-time business would also be capped at 5% and renewal at 2.5%.For life insurance, the proposed caps vary significantly by policy duration. For individual savings products, the first-year cap rises from 5% for policies below five years to 20% for policies of 10 years or more for insurance distribution entities, with corresponding higher caps for agents. Renewal commissions would be capped at 2-3% for distribution entities depending on policy duration and at 5% for agents. Advt The proposal also brings all distributor payments and non-cash incentives within the definition of commission, limiting insurers’ ability to compensate distributors outside the formal commission structure.Insurers face growth risk, but lower costs could offset impactThe impact on insurers is less straightforward.Jefferies said SBI Life and LIC are already relatively close to the proposed expense-of-management thresholds, with EOM ratios of 10.6% and 11.9%, respectively. Other insurers would need to reduce commissions to meet the proposed thresholds.The proposed EOM framework would bring life insurers to 15% within two years and 12.5% within five years. For general insurers, the limit would move to 25% of GDPI within two years and 20% within five years. Advt For life insurers, Jefferies sees a possible near-term growth impact as lower commissions could affect distribution incentives. It specifically notes that banks operating on open architecture, including HDFC Bank and Axis Bank, could push their key life-insurance partners if economics change.For non-life insurers, the brokerage flags motor insurance as a particular area of concern. Go Digit and ICICI Lombard could see slower motor own-damage growth because of the proposed one-third reduction in commissions. Star Health, meanwhile, is seen as relatively less exposed because around 85% of its distribution is through its agency network, according to Jefferies. Advt Niva Bupa could face challenges in its bank-based group health business, which accounts for around 20%, the brokerage said.At the same time, lower acquisition costs could improve insurer profitability if the reduction in commissions flows through to expenses rather than being absorbed elsewhere.Jefferies estimates that a 10% reduction in acquisition expenses could raise margins by 100-400 basis points and increase VNB by 5-15% for life insurers. For non-life insurers, a 100-basis-point improvement in the combined ratio could translate into a 7-9% improvement in EPS.Three scenarios for insurersJefferies has outlined three possible outcomes from the proposed commission reset.In the first, lower commissions reduce distribution incentives and cause APE/GDPI growth to slow by 5%. This could result in a 6-8% reduction in FY28 VNB for life insurers and a 1-2% adverse impact on EPS for non-life insurers, according to the brokerage.In the second, lower acquisition costs improve insurer economics. A 10% reduction in acquisition expenses could generate the 100-400-basis-point margin improvement and 5-15% VNB uplift estimated by Jefferies.The third scenario is that competition between insurers reduces as commission structures converge. Jefferies estimates that 5% higher APE could translate into 7-8% higher VNB, while 5% higher gross premium growth could result in a 1-2% EPS improvement for non-life insurers.The consultation paper also proposes changes beyond commissions, including new categories for distributors, lower entry-capital requirements, greater scope for intermediaries to distribute non-insurance financial products, digital market infrastructure such as Bima Sugam and a regulator-owned Public Insurance Registry.The feedback deadline is October 25, 2026, with implementation proposed from FY28. Jefferies said the availability of supporting data published separately by IRDAI reduces the scope for a material rollback of the proposals.For distributors, Jefferies expects the immediate pressure to be on commission income and profitability. For insurers, the eventual impact will depend on whether lower distribution costs translate into weaker growth, improved margins, or reduced competitive intensity.
