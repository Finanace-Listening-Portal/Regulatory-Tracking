(Representational image)Photo : ANIfollow usfollow usThe Supreme Court has given four major financial institutions a strict seven-day deadline to unconditionally approve the prosecution of officials allegedly linked to a builder-financier nexus that has caused financial hardship for homebuyers. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Sammaan Capital and PNB Housing Finance have been directed to grant the necessary prosecution sanctions within a week. The court also made it clear that non-compliance would have serious consequences, warning that the chairpersons and managing directors of the institutions could be summoned to appear personally.The matter relates to allegations surrounding home loan subvention arrangements, which were intended to ease the financial burden on property buyers but allegedly became a source of distress for several individuals.A Bench headed by the Chief Justice of India (CJI) issued the direction while considering a series of petitions concerning home loan subvention schemes.The court stressed that financial institutions must not delay the process of granting prosecution approval to their employees where investigations have identified alleged wrongdoing.In its order, the Bench stated, "We direct all the banks or financial institutions referred to above to issue unconditional sanction for prosecution within one week, failing which the CMD and Chairman shall remain present in Court to show cause as to why appropriate action be not taken against them."Home Loan Subvention Schemes Under Judicial ScrutinyThe proceedings focus on home loan subvention schemes, a financing arrangement commonly associated with under-construction residential properties.Under these arrangements, developers agreed to pay the equated monthly instalments (EMIs) on behalf of buyers until possession of the property was handed over or a mutually agreed deadline was reached.Such schemes were designed to reduce the immediate repayment burden on purchasers waiting for their homes to be completed. However, allegations of irregularities involving builders and financial institutions have raised concerns about how these arrangements were implemented.The alleged builder-financier connection has reportedly left several homebuyers struggling financially, bringing the practices associated with these schemes under greater scrutiny.CBI Flags Obstacles In Securing Prosecution ApprovalThe Supreme Court's intervention came after the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) highlighted difficulties in obtaining the necessary approvals to prosecute officials of financial institutions.According to the agency, its investigations had identified officials allegedly involved in the matter, but securing prosecution sanctions had emerged as a significant hurdle.The CBI specifically referred to Section 19 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, which requires the prescribed sanction before prosecution can proceed against public servants covered by the provision.The agency also raised concerns over conditional sanctions issued by HDFC Bank, indicating that the nature of the approvals being granted was creating additional complications in moving forward with the cases.Latest News worldTrump's Big Oil Gambit: US Pushed Two Founding Members To QuitworldMid-Air Scare On Thanksgiving: Delta Air Lines Flight With 177 Aboard Makes Emergency LandingworldUS Strikes Iran-Bound Ship in Gulf of Oman, Tehran Reports Mine Strike in HormuzworldNameless In Afghanistan: Taliban Replace Women's Names With 'Granny' And 'Goat' In Bizarre New CrackdownworldCanada's Top Court Upholds Quebec Law Banning Hijabs, Turbans In Public JobsworldList Of Trump Cabinet Secretaries And Officials Now Living In Military BasesworldTrump's Thanksgiving Call With Marine Commander Turns Awkward: 'We Can Replace You Very Easily'entertainment newsSarwar Ahuja Regrets Upsetting Ekta Kapoor For Turning Down Yeh Hai Mohabbatein Offer: Good Equation Turned Sour -
