The MDR will be capped at Rs 300 per transaction, limiting the fee on very high-value UPI payments.Individual banks' gains will depend on their role as issuer, acquirer or PSP bank and the volume of eligible transactions.Banks are set to capture the largest share of the new revenue pool from UPI merchant discount rate, with Yes Bank, Bank of Baroda, Punjab National Bank and IndusInd Bank among those identified by analysts as potential beneficiaries. The new framework, effective October 15, will levy a 0.4% MDR on eligible person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, with issuing banks getting 40% of the fee.The MDR will be split among the issuing bank, acquiring bank, UPI app and the app's payment service provider bank, giving banks the largest collective share of the fee pool. Brokerage estimates put the potential annual revenue pool in the Rs 16,000-20,000 crore range, although the actual amount will depend on eligible transaction volumes, exemptions and the eventual transaction mix. Advt For every Rs 100 of MDR collected, the issuing bank will receive Rs 40, the acquiring bank Rs 30, the UPI app Rs 20 and the PSP bank Rs 10. This makes banks the largest collective beneficiaries, although the benefit to individual lenders will depend on whether they act as issuers, acquirers or PSP banks and the scale of their eligible UPI transactions.The change marks a shift from the zero-MDR model that has governed most UPI payments since 2020. While UPI itself remains free for consumers and person-to-person transactions, the new framework creates a direct revenue stream from specified higher-value merchant transactions.The gainersYes Bank could see a relatively significant benefit because of its role as a PSP bank for major UPI applications, while Bank of Baroda, Punjab National Bank and IndusInd Bank have also been identified by analysts as potential beneficiaries. SBI was earlier estimated to have the potential to earn around Rs 3,000 crore annually from UPI MDR, although that estimate predates the final revenue-sharing framework.The headline 0.4% rate, however, will not apply across the UPI ecosystem. The charge is restricted to eligible person-to-merchant transactions above Rs 2,000, with exemptions and concessional rates for specified categories. The MDR is also capped at Rs 300 per transaction. Advt The new framework could therefore give banks a meaningful fee-income opportunity, but the eventual impact on individual lenders will depend on their position in the UPI payment chain, the proportion of transactions that qualify for MDR and the costs associated with processing those payments.
