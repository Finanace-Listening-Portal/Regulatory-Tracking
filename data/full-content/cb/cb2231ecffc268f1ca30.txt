} } Listen to This ArticleMicrofinance borrowers are increasingly accessing larger loans, with the average ticket size rising 17.5 per cent year-on-year (Y-o-Y) to ₹63,100 in the July - September quarter (Q2 FY27), as loans above ₹1 lakh accounted for a growing share of originations, according to CRIF’s September 2026 MicroLend report. The share of loans in the ₹80,000-₹1 lakh category rose to 18.5 per cent in Q2 FY27 from 14.7 per cent a year earlier. Loans above ₹1 lakh accounted for 15.1 per cent of originations, compared with 11.2 per cent in the corresponding quarter last year. Disbursement value in this segment grew 41.3 per cent Y-o-Y. Loans in the ₹50,000-₹80,000 category continued to account for the largest share of origination value at 41.3 per cent. Overall microfinance disbursements grew 5.1 per cent Y-o-Y to ₹56,956 crore during the quarter, although they declined 7.5 per cent sequentially. First-time borrowers accounted for 19.7 per cent of sanctioned loans and 16.5 per cent of the sanctioned amount. The number of active loans fell 22.4 per cent to 96 million during the same period, while the industry’s gross loan portfolio (GLP) declined 5 per cent to ₹3.28 trillion. The contraction suggests that growth in average loan size has partly cushioned the impact of a shrinking borrower and loan base. The segment’s live customer base stood at 64 million borrowers.
