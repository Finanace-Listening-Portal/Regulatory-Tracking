Articles 3 min read UPI MDR: What changes from October 15? Here’s what merchants and consumers need to know New framework from October 15 introduces 0.4% MDR on eligible P2M transactions above ₹2,000, with a ₹300 cap on transactions of ₹75,000 and above. Here's a detailed FAQ on what changes for consumers & merchants. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Updated On Sep 16, 2026 at 02:40 PM IST var news_msid = 134275850; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook UPIThe revised UPI Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) framework will introduce charges on select high-value Person-to-Merchant (P2M) transactions from October 15, 2026, while keeping UPI free for consumers and small-value transactions.Here is a detailed FAQ on what changes under the new framework.1. What is changing in UPI payments?For the first time, a 0.4% MDR will apply to P2M UPI transactions above ₹2,000. The MDR will be paid within the merchant payment ecosystem and will not be charged to consumers.For transactions of ₹75,000 and above, the MDR will be capped at ₹300 per transaction.2. When will the new MDR framework come into effect? Advt The revised framework will take effect from October 15, 2026.Banks, payment aggregators, fintech apps and other ecosystem players have been given time to update their systems and billing arrangements.3. Will consumers have to pay for UPI?No. Consumers will continue to make UPI payments free of cost.There will be no customer-facing MDR, platform fee or other UPI payment charge.4. Will UPI payments below ₹2,000 attract MDR?No. P2M transactions up to ₹2,000 will continue to carry zero MDR.According to the framework, these transactions account for more than 95% of UPI P2M transaction volume.5. How much MDR will a merchant pay?The standard MDR will be 0.4% of the transaction value for eligible P2M transactions above ₹2,000.Transaction valueMDRUpto Rs 2000Zero ChargesRs 3000Rs 12Rs 50000Rs 200Rs 75000 & aboveRs 300For example, a ₹1 lakh transaction would ordinarily attract ₹400 at 0.4%, but the ₹300 cap means the merchant pays only ₹300.6. Which transactions will have a special flat ₹5 MDR?Certain specified categories will attract a flat ₹5 MDR for transactions above ₹2,000, instead of the standard 0.4%.These include categories such as:RailwaysTelecommunicationsInsuranceFuelOther designated industry-program categoriesThe flat rate is intended to prevent large transaction values in these sectors from translating into substantially higher percentage-based charges. Advt 7. Will mutual fund and capital-market payments attract the 0.4% MDR?No. A separate concessional MDR has been specified for capital-market transactions.Payments relating to mutual funds, securities, stockbrokers and dealers will attract 0.02% MDR, capped at ₹300 per transaction.8. Will small local merchants have to pay MDR?No, if they qualify under the P2PM framework.Small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh per month through UPI QR directly into their bank accounts will continue to benefit from zero MDR.The framework is aimed at protecting micro and small businesses, particularly those in the unorganised retail sector. Advt 9. What happens if a merchant consistently crosses the ₹1 lakh monthly P2PM limit?According to the FAQ released by NPCI, merchants whose UPI inward credits exceed ₹1 lakh per month for three consecutive months will be transitioned from the P2PM category to the P2M category.10. How many UPI merchant transactions will actually be affected?The framework says MDR will apply to about 4% of merchant transactions, meaning approximately 96% of merchant transactions will remain unaffected.This is because most transactions are either below ₹2,000 or fall under the zero-MDR framework for eligible small merchants. Advt 11. Can merchants pass the MDR on to customers?No.Banks have been advised to ensure that merchants do not pass MDR charges on to customers while accepting UPI payments.Consumers will continue to pay the listed price.12. Will the MDR money go to the government?The framework says the MDR will be distributed among UPI ecosystem players rather than being treated simply as a government charge.It also proposes a dedicated fund for small merchants, with 5% of total MDR collections earmarked for the fund.13. What will the small-merchant fund be used for?The proposed fund will support:Merchant onboardingExpansion of UPI acceptanceDigital payment infrastructureIncentives for UPI transactions by small merchantsExpansion in rural and semi-urban areasTier 3–6 centres, including the Northeast, J&K and Ladakh14. Does MDR apply to UPI AutoPay or recurring payments?Prescribed MDR will not apply to UPI Mandates/AutoPay recurring transactions, including recurring utility payments, OTT subscriptions and recurring investments.
