Articles 6 min read AI-led lending must deliver returns, not just automation, say NBFC leaders From AI-assisted technology development and automated collections to credit decisions in as little as 15–30 minutes, NBFCs are reporting measurable gains from artificial intelligence. However, executives at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders’ Retreat 2026 said scaling AI-led lending will depend on cost economics, reliable data, risk controls and human accountability. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Updated On Oct 9, 2026 at 07:15 PM IST var news_msid = 134835532; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Artificial intelligence is moving beyond pilot projects across NBFCs, enabling faster credit decisions, more efficient customer engagement and greater automation of technology operations. However, lenders cautioned that automating processes does not mean handing over lending decisions to machines.Industry leaders at a panel discussion at the ETBFSI NBFC Leaders' Retreat, moderated by ETBFSI Editor Amol Dethe, said the next phase of AI adoption would be measured by its impact on productivity, credit quality and profitability, rather than the number of processes automated.AI delivers gains across technology and operations Advt AI is increasingly being deployed beyond customer-facing applications, with lenders reporting improvements in software development, infrastructure costs and operational efficiency.Animesh Sharma, CTO, Indifi said more than 40% of Indifi’s technology team's output had been generated with AI assistance over the preceding two to three quarters, covering code generation, quality assurance and performance optimisation.AI-assisted identification of processing bottlenecks helped improve the performance of certain batch jobs by approximately 90%. Performance optimisation also contributed to a roughly 30% reduction in hosting costs over the preceding three quarters, Sharma said.Indifi has also been testing AI in sales and operational workflows, with one sales-related pilot delivering a 40% improvement in operational efficiency. AI-assisted security scans helped identify critical vulnerabilities that had not been detected through existing quarterly audit processes.For KreditBee, AI is helping shift operational management from retrospective reporting to faster intervention. Ajeet Kumar, COO, KreditBee said lenders could identify customer drop-offs, telecalling errors and operational anomalies in near real time, allowing teams to take corrective action sooner.AI is also enabling lenders to analyse a much larger proportion of customer calls, rather than relying on manual audits of limited samples. Kumar noted that generative AI builds on the automated underwriting and rule-based decision engines that digital lenders have used for years, rather than replacing them. Advt Collections emerge as a major AI opportunityCollections is another area where NBFCs are seeing operational benefits from AI, particularly in businesses with high volumes of customer interactions.Paromesh Chatterjee, CIO, Capri Global Capital said Capri Global Capital is deploying AI agents for automated reminders, customer engagement and risk assessment. AI-enabled conversations can help identify preferred payment methods and provide signals about borrowers’ repayment circumstances.Sentiment analysis can offer additional context on whether a customer is facing temporary financial distress, helping lenders determine suitable follow-up strategies. AI is also being used to standardise responses to regulatory queries and reduce inconsistencies across compliance submissions. Advt Dvara KGFS, which serves customers in remote rural markets, has expanded the use of conversational AI in outbound calling.Vidya Sambasivam, CIO, Dvara KGFS said the organisation now handles comparable outbound calling volumes with approximately half its earlier team size. Nearly 80–90% of outbound calls begin with conversational bots, with interactions transferred to human agents when necessary.The bots can record promises to pay, guide customers through digital payment processes and manage routine conversations. Dvara KGFS initially tested approximately 10,000 calls, evaluating customer responses, promises to pay and actual payment outcomes before expanding the programme. Advt The company continues to refine calling scripts, conduct quality checks and restrict the information available to bots. It also uses AI for document verification, customer risk profiling and operational checks during assisted onboarding.AI can accelerate lending, but product complexity mattersThe extent of automation varies by lending product, with straightforward credit assessments offering greater scope for speed than loans requiring extensive physical, legal or collateral verification.Jitendra Tanwar, MD & CEO, Namdev Finvest Limited said Namdev Finvest uses AI-supported credit assessment across products including electric vehicle and solar financing. By combining predefined credit rules with KYC checks, banking information and financial analysis, the lender can arrive at initial credit decisions in approximately 15–30 minutes for certain products.The system can evaluate bank-account activity, cheque-bounce patterns and average balances to support faster preliminary assessments.However, secured MSME lending continues to require legal verification, technical assessments and collateral valuation, extending processing timelines to approximately four to five days.AI can accelerate eligibility checks and preliminary decisions, but cannot eliminate product-specific verification requirements. The pace of automation therefore depends not only on technology but also on the nature of the loan, the quality of available information and the checks required.Cost economics will determine the scale of adoptionAs AI deployment expands, NBFCs are also weighing the costs of implementation, integration and ongoing oversight.Kapil Kapoor, CTO & CPO, CredAble said lenders should prioritise use cases where automation potential is high, risks are manageable and outcomes can be measured. Pilots should establish whether AI outperforms conventional methods before wider deployment.The cost of AI extends beyond model subscriptions to data preparation, integration, cybersecurity, monitoring, validation and human oversight. Kapoor cautioned that the cheapest model does not necessarily deliver the lowest overall cost, as more capable models may reduce errors and rework.Sharma said Indifi combines locally deployed language models, on-demand computing resources and purpose-built automation to control expenditure. Smaller locally hosted models can be more economical for routine tasks such as documentation and email drafting.Kumar highlighted the need for enterprise-level spending controls and employee training to prevent unnecessary consumption of generative AI resources. Chatterjee, meanwhile, said smaller language models and locally hosted infrastructure could reduce recurring costs, while regulatory compliance and cybersecurity must be included in investment calculations.Executives agreed that AI investments need a clear business case, with lenders tracking productivity improvements, operational savings and risk outcomes against the full cost of deployment.Human accountability remains non-negotiableDespite growing automation, the panel distinguished between AI executing established rules and systems independently determining credit outcomes.Kumar said AI can perform predefined checks faster and more consistently, but human teams must remain responsible for setting lending policies and risk parameters. Kapoor expects technology and product development to become increasingly AI-native, while other functions move towards automation where reliable data and appropriate safeguards are available.Sharma expects routine tasks to be extensively automated, with human intervention focused on exceptions and underwriting oversight. Chatterjee said AI can make decisions more evidence-driven by bringing more information into the process within shorter timelines, without transferring ultimate responsibility to machines.Sambasivam described the desired model as one of responsible autonomy, in which AI expands operational capacity while accountability remains with people.“Autonomy will be powered by AI. The responsibility has to lie with the humans,” she said.Tanwar also emphasised that human involvement remains essential for final decisions and complex assessments, even as AI supports a substantial share of information processing and operational workflows.For NBFCs, the next phase of AI adoption will be defined not by how much of the lending process can be automated, but by whether the technology can accelerate credit delivery, improve collections and lower costs without weakening underwriting discipline or accountability.
