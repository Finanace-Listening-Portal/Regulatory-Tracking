Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) Consequential Assessment Cannot Survive Once the Original Reassessment Is Quashed for Lack of Jurisdiction: ITAT Hyderaba Hyderabad ITAT ruling in Sri Light Chit Fund Can a fresh assessment made under a CIT(A) remand order survive when the Tribunal subsequently quashes the original reassessment for lack of jurisdiction? The Hyderabad ITAT answered this question in Sri Light Chit Fund P. Ltd. v. ITO, ITA No. 2581/HYD/2026, decided on 20 November 2026 for AY 2018–19. The Bench held that the remand direction and the assessment made to give effect to it fell with the original reassessment. The fact that the Assessing Officer completed the consequential assessment before the Tribunal ruled on jurisdiction did not preserve that assessment. How the dispute began The company had not filed its return for AY 2018–19. Information on the Insight Portal showed cash deposits of ₹1,34,96,695 with Canara Bank and ₹31,12,015 with Warangal Urban Bank, besides cash withdrawals, a time deposit and interest receipts. The Assessing Officer reopened the assessment under Section 147 by issuing a notice under Section 148 on 28 April 2022. In response, the company filed a return declaring nil income. The Assessing Officer completed the reassessment on 15 March 2024 under Section 147 read with Section 144B, making an addition of ₹84,41,355 under Section 69A. The first appeal sent the evidence back to the AO The assessee challenged the validity of the Section 148 notice before the CIT(A). By order dated 14 February 2025, the CIT(A) set aside the assessment and sent the matter back to the Assessing Officer to verify the evidence the assessee had furnished in appeal. The jurisdictional ground remained undecided. The assessee therefore carried that issue to the Tribunal in ITA No. 105/Hyd/2026. While that appeal was pending, the Assessing Officer acted on the remand and passed a consequential assessment on 18 February 2026 under Section 147 read with Sections 250 and 144B. This gave rise to a separate appeal. By the time the CIT(A) disposed of that appeal on 6 August 2026, the Tribunal had already quashed the original reassessment. The assessee challenged the survival of the consequential assessment and the additions aggregating to ₹45,74,790. Why the original reassessment was quashed In its first-round order dated 24 July 2026, the Tribunal found that the Section 148 notice was issued more than three years after the end of AY 2018–19. Under Section 151(ii), as applicable on the date of the notice, approval had to come from the Principal Chief Commissioner or Principal Director General, or, where applicable, the Chief Commissioner or Director General. The Assessing Officer had obtained approval from the Principal Commissioner, who lacked statutory authority to approve that notice. The first Bench also considered Section 292BC, inserted by the Finance Act, 2026 with deemed effect from 1 April 2021. It held that the provision addressed matters such as insufficiency of recorded reasons and defects in the form, authentication or communication of an approval. Approval by an authority other than the one specified by statute fell outside that protection. The Bench referred to ACIT, International Taxation v. LinkedIn Singapore Pte. Ltd. [2025] 180 taxmann.com 158 (SC) in support of its conclusion. It accordingly quashed the reassessment for want of valid jurisdiction. The remand and consequential assessment fell together In the second round, the Revenue relied on the fact that the Assessing Officer had examined the assessee’s evidence in accordance with the CIT(A)’s remand. The Tribunal traced the authority for that exercise back to the original reopening. The remand direction arose from the appeal against that reassessment, and the order dated 18 February 2026 was passed solely to give effect to the remand. Once the original reassessment was quashed, the remand direction ceased to survive. The consequential assessment and the CIT(A)’s order dated 6 August 2026 consequently fell. The Tribunal allowed the assessee’s appeal and treated the remaining grounds as infructuous. The additions thus fell on jurisdictional grounds, leaving the merits of the disputed transactions undecided. Author’s comments The decision illustrates the importance of pursuing a jurisdictional ground left undecided in a remand order. A remand for verification gives the assessee an opportunity to explain the evidence, but it leaves open the anterior question of whether the Assessing Officer lawfully assumed jurisdiction. Here, the assessee’s first Tribunal appeal resolved that question even though the remand proceedings had already produced a second assessment. The distinction between the two Tribunal orders is also important. The first order decided who could validly approve the reopening under the law applicable on the notice date. The second order applied the consequence of that finding to proceedings taken under the same reopening. Where an Assessing Officer has already passed a giving-effect order, identifying its sole link to the quashed reassessment explains why the consequential demand falls even though the officer examined the evidence afresh. The Tribunal also held that Section 292BC did not protect approval granted by an incompetent authority. Applied in the present proceedings. ACIT, International Taxation Vs LinkedIn Singapore Pte. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT HYDERABAD This appeal by the assessee is directed against the Order dated 06.08.2026 of the learned CIT(A)-National Faceless Appeal Centre [in short “NFAC”], Delhi, for the assessment year 2018-2019. 2. The assessee has raised the following grounds of appeal: 1. “The order of the learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) dated 06.08.2026 is contrary to law, facts and circumstances of the case. 2. The learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) failed to appreciate that the assessee had challenged the order dated 14.02.2025 before the Hon’ble Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Hyderabad on the legal and jurisdictional grounds and that the Hon’ble Tribunal, by order dated 24.07.2026, decided the jurisdictional issue in favour of the assessee and granted complete relief. 3. The learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) ought to have appreciated that the second assessment order dated 18.02.2026 was passed only pursuant to the earlier order of the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) dated 14.02.2025 and, therefore, once the Hon’ble ITAT quashed the order of assessment on jurisdictional issue, the consequential assessment order dated 18.02.2026 and the additions made therein cannot survive. 4. The learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) erred in sustaining the additions aggregating to Rs.45,74,790/- notwithstanding the order of the Hon’ble Income Tax Appellate Tribunal dated 24.07.2026 granting relief to the assessee on the validity of the reassessment proceedings. 5. In view of the order of the Hon’ble Income Tax Appellate Tribunal dated 24.07.2026 on the jurisdictional issue, the consequential assessment order dated 18.02.2026 and the additions made thereunder are liable to be quashed/deleted and the CIT(A) ought to have quashed the assessment order. 6. The appellant craves leave to add, amend, alter or withdraw any of the above grounds at or before the time of hearing.” 3. This is the second round of appeal before the Tribunal. Briefly stated facts of the case are that the assessee Sri Light Chit Fund P. Ltd., has not filed return of income for the impugned assessment year under consideration. As per NMS Module of Insight Portal the assessee company had carried out substantial financial transactions i.e., cash deposits in current account with Canara Bank amounting to Rs.1,34,96,695/- and cash deposit with Warangal Urban Bank Ltd., amounting to Rs.31,12,015/- and cash withdrawals from Canara Bank account amounting to Rs.5,93,855/- and time deposits with Canara Bank of Rs.10,00,000/- and receipt of interest income on account held with Warangal Urban Cooperative Bank Limited amounting to Rs.2,57,899/-. Therefore, the Assessing Officer initiated proceedings u/sec.147 of the Act and issued notice u/sec.148 of the Act dated 28.04.2022. In response to the notice, the assessee filed its return of income on 26.05.2022 declaring income at Rs. NIL. Thereafter, the Assessing Officer after considering the submissions of the assessee made addition of Rs.84,41,355/- u/sec.69A of the Income Tax Act [in short “the Act”], 1961. 3.1. Against the order of the Assessing Officer, the assessee carried the matter in appeal before the learned CIT(A) challenging the validity of notice issued by the Assessing Officer u/sec.148 of the Act. The learned CIT(A) after considering the submissions of the assessee vide Order dated 14.02.2025 set aside the Order of the Assessing Officer and remanded the matter to the file of Assessing Officer for verification and examination of the evidence filed by the assessee before the learned CIT(A) but not decided the legal ground raised by the assessee. Therefore, the assessee had filed an appeal before the Tribunal challenging the validity of the notice u/sec.148 of the Act dated 28.04.2022 issued by the Assessing Officer beyond a period of three years from the end of the assessment year under consideration and the Tribunal vide Order dated 24.07.2026 in ITA.No.105/Hyd./2026 quashed the assessment framed by the Assessing Officer u/sec.147 r.w.s.144B of the Act dated 15.03.2024 for want of valid assumption of jurisdiction to issue a notice u/sec.148 of the Act. 4. The learned Authorised Representative of the Assessee has submitted that the Assessing Officer has issued notice u/sec.148 of the Act dated 28.04.2022 without valid of assumption of jurisdiction. Therefore, the Tribunal vide Order dated 24.07.2026 has quashed the assessment framed by the Assessing Officer u/sec.147 r.w.s.114B of the Act dated 15.03.2024. She submitted that since the assessment itself has been quashed by the Tribunal, therefore, the giving effect order passed by the Assessing Officer dated 18.02.2026 would not survive and consequently, the impugned order of the learned CIT(A) dated 06.08.2026. She accordingly submitted that the order of the Assessing Officer dated 18.02.2026 giving effect to the Order of the learned CIT(A) would not survive. She accordingly submitted that consequently, the impugned order of the learned CIT(A) dated 06.08.2026 is not sustainable in law and submitted that the order of the learned CIT(A) be set aside and the giving effect assessment order dated 18.02.2026 be quashed. 5. The learned DR on the other hand submitted that in the first round of appeal the learned CIT(A) has remanded the issue back to the file of Assessing Officer for verification and examination of the evidence filed by the assessee before the learned CIT(A) and the Assessing Officer has passed the giving effect order dated 18.02.2026 after considering the submissions/evidence of the assessee. He has relied upon the Orders of the authorities below. 6. We have considered the rival submissions as well as relevant material on record. It is manifest from the record that the Assessing Officer has reopened assessment u/sec.147 of the Act by issuing notice u/sec.148 of the Act dated 28.04.2022 and after considering the submissions of the assessee made addition of Rs.84,41,355/- u/sec.69A of the Act vide order dated 15.03.2024 passed u/sec.147 r.w.s.144B of the Act, against which, the assessee filed an appeal before the learned CIT(A) and the learned CIT(A) vide order dated 14.02.2025 has set aside the assessment order passed by the Assessing Officer and remanded the evidence to the file of Assessing Officer for verification and examination. The assessee challenged the Order of the learned CIT(A) dated 14.02.2025 before the Tribunal in ITA.No.105/Hyd./2026 and the Tribunal vide Order dated 24.07.2026 has quashed the re-assessment order dated 15.04.2024 passed by the Assessing Officer u/sec.147 r.w.s.144B of the Act for want of valid assumption of jurisdiction to issue notice u/sec.148 of the Act. The relevant finding of the Tribunal in Para nos.16 to 19 are as under: “16. We find that the Hon’ble High Court in its aforesaid order had not only observed that in the case of the assessee before them. i.e., for AY 2018-19, the specified authority for granting approval under section 151 of the Act was the Principal Chief Commissioner or Principal Director General or Chief Commissioner or Director General as a period of more than three years had lapsed from the end of the relevant Assessment Year, but had also rejected the claim of the revenue that the “proviso” to section 151 of the Act as had been made available on the statute vide the Finance Act, 2023 w.e.f. 01/04/2023 was to be given a retrospective effect. 17. We, thus, in terms of our aforesaid observation, concur with the Ld. AR that in the present case before us for A.Y. 2018-19, wherein notice under Section 148 of the Act was issued on 28/04/2022, i.e., beyond a period of three years from the end of the assessment year, the A.O. was statutorily obligated to have obtained the approval from either of the authorities specified u/s. 151(ii) of the law as was then available on the statute, viz. Principal Chief Commissioner or Principal Director General or, where there is no Principal Chief Commissioner or Principal Director General, Chief Commissioner or Director General. However, as the A.O. had obtained the approval from the Pr. Commissioner of Income Tax, i.e. an authority that was not vested with any jurisdiction as per the mandate of Section 151 of the Act (as made available on the statute w.e.f 01.04.2021), therefore, the assessment so framed by him under Section 147 r.w.s.144B of the Act, dated 15/03/2024, being devoid and bereft of any valid assumption of jurisdiction, is liable to be quashed. Accordingly, we quash the assessment framed by the A.O. under Section 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act, dated 15/03/2024, in terms of our aforesaid observations. 18. Before parting, we may herein observed that though the Finance Act, 2026 has made available on the statute section 292BC of the Act, which is deemed to have been inserted w.r.e.f 01/04/2021, and contemplates that notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or in any judgment, order or decree of any Court, for the removal of doubts, it is hereby clarified that any approval given by an income-tax authority in relation to any assessment, reassessment or re-computation proceedings under this Act shall be deemed to be administrative and supervisory in nature and shall not be invalid or shall not be deemed to be invalid by reason of any insufficiency of the reasons recorded or by reason of any defect in the form or manner of its authentication or communication including whether digital signature have been appended to such approval or not, where such approval is granted electronically; but granting of an approval by an authority other than that specified under the Act does not fall within the scope and gamut of the said statutory provision. On the contrary, we find that in case once the approval for initiation of reassessment proceedings was granted by the Commissioner of Income Tax after expiry of three years from the end of the relevant assessment year, which ought to have been granted by the Principal Chief Commissioner of Income Tax, the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of ACIT, International Taxation v. LinkedIn Singapore Pte. Ltd. (2025) 180 Taxmann 158 (SC) had upheld the order of the Hon’ble High Court, which had quashed the impugned order passed by the AO under section 148A(d) as well as the notice issued under section 148 of the Act for want of valid assumption of jurisdiction. 19. We thus, in terms of our aforesaid deliberations, quash the assessment framed by the AO under section 147 r.w.s 144B of the Act, dated 15/03/2024, for want of a valid assumption of jurisdiction to issue a notice under section 148 of the Act. As we have quashed the assessment, we refrain from dealing with the other contentions raised before us, which are thus left open.” 6.2. Thus, it is manifest from the record that the Tribunal vide order dated 24.07.2026 quashed the assessment framed by the Assessing Officer u/sec.147 r.w.s.144B of the Act dated 15.03.2025 for want of valid assumption of jurisdiction to issue notice u/sec.148 of the Act. Consequently, the Order dated 14.02.2025 of the learned CIT(A) [in first round of appeal] remitting the evidence filed by the assessee for verification and examination by the Assessing Officer will not survive. 6.3. In view of quashing the reopening of the assessment by the Tribunal vide Order dated 24.07.2026 (supra) in the first round of appeal, the Order dated 18.02.2026 passed by the Assessing Officer u/sec.147 r.w.s.250 r.w.s.144B of the Act giving effect to the Order of the learned CIT(A) dated 14.02.2025 would not survive. Hence, the impugned order of the learned CIT(A) dated 06.08.2026 in the present appeal challenging the assessment order dated 18.02.2026 also would not survive and therefore, the same is set aside. 7. Since we have quashed the re-assessment order, the other grounds raised by the assessee would become infructuous. 8. In the result, appeal of the Assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 20.11.2026.
