(This is an AI-generated image)Shares of major Tata Group companies plunged up to 9 per cent in Friday's early trade, rattled by an unprecedented boardroom clash over the conglomerate's leadership and the public listing of Tata Sons.The heavy market sell-off followed a dramatic board meeting on Thursday, where Tata Trusts, the majority shareholder of Tata Sons, declared a resolution seeking to reappoint N Chandrasekaran as Chairman to be legally void. Advt Mayhem in stocksThe market reaction was across the board. Tata Chemicals Ltd witnessed the sharpest decline, plunging as much as 8.89 per cent to a day’s low of ₹711 apiece on the BSE against its previous close of ₹780.40.This massive sell-off erased a large chunk of investor wealth, dragging the company's market capitalisation down to around ₹18,198 crore.IT bellwether Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) also faced heavy pressure, slipping over 3.5 per cent to a day's low of ₹2,116, pulling its overall market value below the ₹ 7,73,294 crore mark.The ripple effect was felt throughout the conglomerate's listed entities. Tata Investment Corporation Ltd took a significant hit, tanking about 5 per cent to trade at ₹682.95 per share.In the automotive pack, Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles dropped 2.91 per cent to ₹305.50, while Tata Motors Ltd fell over 1 per cent to touch a low of ₹430.05 apiece.Extension dubbed a ‘legal nullity’The severe market anxiety stems from official statements released by Tata Trusts.Tata Trusts are the majority owners of Tata Sons, and Tata Sons is the holding company at the centre of the Tata Group.The trusts provide ultimate shareholder control, while Tata Sons runs and promotes the operating businesses.Tata Trusts said that Chandrasekaran had already communicated his decision on August 12, 2026, not to seek reappointment at the end of his current tenure, which ends on February 20, 2027. Advt Despite this, a resolution for his reappointment was suddenly pushed during Thursday's board meeting of Tata Sons.With four directors voting in favour and Tata Trusts Chairman Noel N. Tata voting against it, the Trusts asserted that the move was a legal nullity, as their Articles of Association strictly require a majority of the Trusts' nominee directors to vote in favour of such an appointment.Pushback against RBI’s listing mandateAdding fuel to the leadership crisis is a profound disagreement over the potential initial public offering of Tata Sons. Following a September 11, 2026 communication from the Reserve Bank of India, which requires shadow banks of a certain scale to be publicly held, the board was forced to debate a listing. Advt However, Tata Trusts reiterated that all permissible options must be explored to avoid a public float. The board ultimately agreed to assess these alternatives thoroughly before convening a separate meeting to chart their final course of action.This fierce resistance to an IPO is deeply rooted in the conglomerate's unique philanthropic ownership structure. Tata Trusts controls 66 per cent of Tata Sons through the Sir Ratan Tata Trust and the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust, while the Shapoorji Pallonji Group remains the largest minority shareholder with an 18.37 per cent stake.The Trusts had previously passed unanimous resolutions in July 2025 to keep the holding company privately owned. Warning against the commercialisation of the group, Noel Tata argued that the charity-driven operating structure exists fundamentally for public purpose and nation-building. Advt Defending the century-old framework, Noel Tata stated, "That is not sentiment. It is the operating model of this House, and it has stood the test of time for more than a century. A listing will destroy its character and strike at the heart of this principle."
