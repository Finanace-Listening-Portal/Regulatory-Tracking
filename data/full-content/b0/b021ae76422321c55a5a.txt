RBI/2026-27/189 Circular No.: DOR.STR.REC.170/21-04-048/2026-27 Date: July 16, 2026 Effective Date: October 1, 2026 The Amendment Directions introduced a new Chapter VII-A titled Prudential Norms on Specified Non-Financial Assets into the existing framework. Further, RBI’s October 2026 CIMS filing manual identifies the reporting return as: Dynamic Return on SNFAs for NBFCs, AIFIs – Return Code R899. The return is designed to facilitate reporting of the stock and movement of specified non-financial assets through the CIMS reporting system. Important: NBFC-HFCs may furnish the relevant SNFA details to the National Housing Bank (NHB), as provided under paragraph 138O of the Amendment Directions. Meaning of Specified Non-Financial Assets (SNFAs) Paragraph 10(15A), inserted through the Amendment Directions, defines a Specified Non-Financial Asset as: “An immovable asset acquired by a NBFC in satisfaction or part satisfaction of its claims on the borrower.” In simple terms, an SNFA represents an immovable property acquired by an NBFC against the outstanding dues of a borrower whose account has become non-performing. For example, where an NBFC has granted a secured loan to a borrower and subsequently acquires the mortgaged property in settlement of the outstanding loan exposure, such property may qualify as an SNFA, subject to the prescribed regulatory conditions. Key characteristics of an SNFA 1. The asset must be an immovable asset. 2. It must be acquired in satisfaction or part satisfaction of the NBFC’s claims against the borrower. 3. The NBFC must obtain legal title to the asset. 4. The NBFC must be in a clear position to deal with the asset independently. 5. The acquisition must comply with the prescribed prudential requirements. Merely taking possession of a secured property for recovery purposes does not, by itself, satisfy the recognition condition under paragraph 138C. The title must be transferred in the name of the NBFC. Important Prudential Requirements Governing SNFAs The newly inserted Chapter VII-A prescribes specific conditions governing the acquisition, valuation, disposal and disclosure of SNFAs. 4.1 SNFA Policy – Paragraph 16A Every NBFC must incorporate appropriate provisions in its policy governing the acquisition and disposal of SNFAs. The policy should specifically address: Limit on SNFAs as a percentage of total assets. Eligibility criteria for acquisition. Delegation of authority and approval matrix. Recovery efforts to be explored before acquisition. Maximum permissible disposal period, which cannot exceed seven years. Accordingly, NBFCs should review and suitably modify their existing stressed asset resolution and recovery policies to incorporate these requirements. 4.2 Acquisition Conditions – Paragraphs 138A to 138F The following conditions must be considered before recognising an asset as an SNFA: Coverage of SNFAs – Paragraph 138A The provisions cover all SNFAs, including assets acquired through: Bilateral acquisitions. Proceedings under the Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest (SARFAESI) Act, 2002. Legacy SNFAs – Paragraph 138B For SNFAs outstanding in the books of an NBFC as on September 30, 2026, classified as Legacy SNFAs, compliance with the new directions must be achieved by September 30, 2027. Transfer of Legal Title – Paragraph 138C An asset will be considered acquired only when: Its title is transferred in the name of the NBFC; and The NBFC is in a clear position to deal with the asset on its own. NPA Classification – Paragraph 138D An SNFA can be acquired only where the NBFC’s exposure to the borrower is classified as non-performing. Extinguishment of Exposure – Paragraph 138E An SNFA may be acquired against full or partial extinguishment of the NBFC’s exposure to the borrower, on a non-recourse basis. Partial Extinguishment – Paragraph 138F Where only part of the exposure is extinguished through acquisition of an SNFA, the transaction shall be treated as restructuring. The residual exposure to the borrower will attract the applicable prudential treatment prescribed for restructuring. Valuation of SNFAs Valuation is one of the most important aspects of the new framework. 5.1 Initial Recognition – Paragraph 138G Upon acquisition, the SNFA must be recorded in the NBFC’s balance sheet at the lower of: 1. Net Book Value (NBV) of the extinguished exposure; or 2. Distress Sale Value (DSV) of the SNFA, determined by at least two independent external valuers. Accordingly, the NBFC cannot recognise the asset at a value exceeding the lower of these two amounts. 5.2 Valuation in Case of Partial Extinguishment – Paragraph 138H Where only a portion of the loan exposure is extinguished, the NBV of the extinguished exposure must be calculated proportionately. Illustration given in the RBI Amendment Directions: Assume the following: Outstanding loan amount: ₹2,00,000 Specific provision maintained: 10% NBV of the total loan: ₹1,80,000 Portion of exposure extinguished: ₹1,50,000 (75%) Distress Sale Value of SNFA: ₹1,40,000 The proportionate NBV of the extinguished exposure would be: ₹1,80,000 × 75% = ₹1,35,000 Therefore, the SNFA shall be recognised at the lower of: Proportionate NBV: ₹1,35,000 Distress Sale Value: ₹1,40,000 Initial carrying value of SNFA = ₹1,35,000. The residual loan exposure will be ₹50,000, with the associated specific provision of ₹5,000. 5.3 Subsequent Valuation – Paragraph 138I At every subsequent reporting date, the SNFA must be carried at its revised NBV. The revised NBV will represent the value of the extinguished exposure, net of the notional provisions that would have been applicable had the original loan exposure continued in the books of the NBFC. In cases of partial extinguishment, the revised NBV will be calculated proportionately based on the extinguished portion of the original exposure. Therefore, NBFCs must establish appropriate mechanisms for periodic valuation and computation of notional provisions. Disposal of SNFAs The RBI has prescribed a maximum holding period to ensure that NBFCs do not retain such assets indefinitely. 6.1 Maximum Disposal Period – Paragraph 138J An NBFC must dispose of an SNFA within the maximum period prescribed under its internal policy, subject to an overall ceiling of seven years. The policy may prescribe a shorter period, but cannot permit disposal beyond seven years. 6.2 Public Auction – Paragraph 138K NBFCs must make all efforts to dispose of SNFAs at the earliest, preferably through public auction. For conducting public auctions, NBFCs must adhere to the principles of auction prescribed under the SARFAESI Act, 2002. 6.3 Restriction on Sale to Borrower or Related Parties – Paragraph 138L An SNFA cannot be sold back to: The original borrower; or Its related parties. The meaning of related parties shall be determined with reference to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. Importantly, this restriction continues even after the asset ceases to be classified as an SNFA under paragraph 138M. 6.4 Conversion into Own-Use Asset – Paragraph 138M Where an NBFC puts an SNFA to its own use, the asset will cease to be classified as an SNFA from the date it is put to use. Thereafter, it must be recorded under the accounting head ‘Fixed Assets’ or any other relevant accounting head, as applicable. Accounting Treatment and Disclosure Requirements 7.1 Separate Balance Sheet Disclosure – Paragraph 138N SNFAs must not be included in: Total stock of residual exposure. Gross NPA. Net NPA. Stressed exposures. Provisioning Coverage Ratio (PCR). Instead, such assets must be separately disclosed under the relevant accounting head in the balance sheet as Specified Non-Financial Assets, in accordance with applicable regulations and accounting standards. This requirement is particularly important because the acquisition of an SNFA results in the extinguishment of the corresponding loan exposure to the extent agreed, while the acquired immovable asset is separately recognised. 7.2 Regulatory Reporting – Paragraph 138O NBFCs must report SNFA details in accordance with the formats prescribed in Annex-2 of the Amendment Directions through the RBI CIMS portal. In the case of NBFC-HFCs, the details may be furnished to NHB. Understanding the Reporting Format under Annex-2 Annex-2 of the RBI Amendment Directions prescribes two principal reporting tables. Table 1: Stock Position of SNFAs This table captures the position of SNFAs outstanding as on the balance sheet date. Particulars Reporting details Total SNFAs as on the B/S date Total value of SNFAs SNFAs of age 0–3 years Value of SNFAs held for 0–3 years SNFAs of age 3–5 years Value of SNFAs held for 3–5 years SNFAs of age 5–7 years Value of SNFAs held for 5–7 years The table also requires the corresponding number of SNFAs in each category. Thus, reporting entities should maintain both the monetary value and the number of properties held in each prescribed age bracket. Table 2: Movement of SNFAs This table captures the movement of SNFAs during the financial year. Sr. No. Particulars 1 Total SNFAs at the beginning of the year 2 SNFAs acquired during the year 3 SNFAs disposed of during the year 4 SNFAs put to own use during the year 5 Total SNFAs at the end of the year (1 + 2 – 3 – 4) The table requires reporting of both the amount (₹ in crore) and the number of SNFAs. Important reconciliation requirement: Closing SNFA position should be reconciled with the stock position reported as on the balance sheet date. NBFCs should ensure consistency between the accounting records, asset register and regulatory return. Effective Date and Transitional Compliance The RBI Amendment Directions became effective from October 1, 2026. However, a specific transitional arrangement has been provided for legacy SNFAs. Particulars Applicable date Effective date of Amendment Directions October 1, 2026 Identification of Legacy SNFAs Assets outstanding as on September 30, 2026 Deadline for compliance in respect of Legacy SNFAs September 30, 2027 NBFCs should accordingly identify existing SNFAs and undertake a gap assessment covering recognition, valuation, policy requirements, disposal plans and disclosures. Dynamic Return on SNFAs – R899 on RBI CIMS Portal To operationalise the regulatory reporting requirements, RBI’s Department of Statistics and Information Management (DSIM) has issued the October 2026 manual titled: Dynamic Return on SNFAs for NBFCs, AIFIs (R899). The below procedure explains the end-to-end procedure for filing the return through the RBI’s Centralised Information Management System (CIMS). The return code for this reporting requirement is R899. 10.1 Login to the CIMS Portal The reporting entity must access the RBI CIMS portal: https://cims.rbi.org.in Users are required to enter: User ID. Password. Captcha. After entering the required credentials, click on the Login option to access the portal. 10.2 Role Assignment and User Access The return code for the Dynamic Return on SNFAs is R899. The reporting entity should contact its CIMS administrator for creation and assignment of the necessary access credentials to the concerned users. The administrator should ensure that the appropriate reporting permissions are available before initiating the filing process. 10.3 Downloading the Return Template The prescribed data template can be downloaded after logging into CIMS. Navigation Path: DRM → Downloads → Download Return Template/Taxonomy and Return Format Thereafter: 1. Select Dynamic Return on SNFAs for NBFCs, AIFIs. 2. Identify the return code R899. 3. Click on the Download button available in the corresponding row. 4. Download the prescribed template for preparing the return. Reporting entities should use the prescribed format available on the portal rather than preparing an independent reporting format. 10.4 Data Submission Procedure The CIMS manual provides two methods for submission of the return: Method A: Screen-Based Data Submission Under this method, the reporting entity can enter the required information directly into the CIMS portal. The general procedure is: 1. Log in to the CIMS portal. 2. Navigate to the Return Submission section. 3. Select the relevant return and reporting parameters. 4. Choose the Dynamic Return on SNFAs (R899). 5. Enter the reporting period and other required information. 6. Fill in the relevant SNFA reporting fields. 7. Add multiple rows wherever required. 8. Review the information before submission. The screen-based facility allows reporting entities to enter data directly into the portal. Method B: File Upload Channel Alternatively, reporting entities may submit the return through the file upload facility. The procedure is: 1. Download the prescribed Excel template. 2. Populate the required information in the prescribed format. 3. Validate the data before uploading. 4. Navigate to the Return Submission section. 5. Select the relevant return and reporting parameters. 6. Click on the ‘Choose File’ option. 7. Select the prepared reporting file. 8. Upload and submit the return. The reporting entity should ensure that the file follows the prescribed template and taxonomy requirements. 10.5 Viewing Return Filing Status After submission, the reporting entity should verify the filing status. The CIMS manual provides two methods for tracking the return. Method 1: Tracker Facility The reporting entity may use the tracker facility available on the portal to monitor the status of the submitted return. Method 2: Return Filing Information Navigation Path: DRM → Report → Return Filing Information The user is required to select the relevant parameters, including: Upload Start Date. Upload End Date. Filing Status. Upload Channel. Return Name, wherever required. The manual specifies that the Upload Start Date and Upload End Date should cover a period of not more than 90 days at a time. After applying the relevant filters, the reporting entity can view the Filing ID and corresponding filing status. The Filing ID should be retained for future reference and correspondence with the CIMS support team. 10.6 Understanding Validation Status The filing tracker provides visibility into different stages of return validation. The process broadly involves: Upload. Metadata Validation. Business Validation. If business rule validation fails, the tracker reflects the failed validation stage. If the filing status displays: ‘Invalid file, please contact the Administrator’, the reporting entity should raise a ticket through the Incident Management Tool (IMT) by mentioning the relevant Filing ID. 10.7 NIL Return Submission Where there is no reportable SNFA position, the reporting entity should examine the NIL filing requirement and submit the NIL return through the prescribed facility. The procedure provided in the CIMS manual is: Navigate to Return Submission. Select File Upload. Click the NIL Filing checkbox. Select return code R899 from the Return Name dropdown. Choose the relevant reference date. Enter the reason for NIL filing. Click Submit. The reporting entity should retain evidence of the NIL filing and verify the corresponding filing status. 10.8 Reporting Entity Code Information The reporting entity code can be identified through the entity master available on the CIMS portal. Navigation Path: DRM → Downloads → Download Masters Select ‘Entity’ in the Table Name field to access and download the entity master. The reporting entity should verify the relevant code before proceeding with submission. 10.9 Incident Management Tool (IMT) For technical difficulties during filing, reporting entities can access the Support option available at the top-right corner of the DRM portal. The IMT User Manual is available through: Downloads → IMT Manual Users may raise tickets with the CIMS Support Team for technical issues encountered during the reporting process. Practical Compliance Checklist for NBFCs and AIFIs Before submitting the R899 return, reporting entities should undertake the following checks: Sr. No. Compliance Activity Status 1 Identify all qualifying SNFAs held by the reporting entity. ☐ 2 Verify that legal title has been transferred in the name of the NBFC. ☐ 3 Verify the underlying borrower’s NPA classification at acquisition. ☐ 4 Ensure that the acquisition complies with the prescribed conditions. ☐ 5 Obtain valuation from at least two independent external valuers. ☐ 6 Verify initial recognition at the lower of NBV and DSV. ☐ 7 Recalculate revised NBV and applicable notional provisions. ☐ 8 Review the SNFA acquisition and disposal policy. ☐ 9 Monitor the seven-year maximum disposal period. ☐ 10 Verify that SNFAs are separately disclosed in the balance sheet. ☐ 11 Ensure that SNFAs are excluded from the specified NPA and PCR measures. ☐ 12 Reconcile the stock and movement information with accounting records. ☐ 13 Verify the prescribed R899 template and reporting parameters. ☐ 14 Submit the return and verify the final filing status. ☐ 15 Preserve the Filing ID and acknowledgement for records. ☐ Key Takeaways The introduction of the SNFA framework represents an important development in the prudential treatment of immovable assets acquired by NBFCs in settlement of stressed loan exposures. The key areas requiring attention are: Appropriate identification and recognition of SNFAs. Acquisition only against non-performing exposures. Recognition based on the lower of NBV and independently assessed DSV. Periodic revision of the carrying value. Disposal within the policy-prescribed period, not exceeding seven years. Prohibition on sale back to the borrower or related parties. Separate accounting and regulatory disclosure. Timely and accurate reporting through the R899 return on RBI CIMS. NBFCs should not treat the R899 return merely as a data-upload exercise. The return should be supported by appropriate internal controls, accurate asset records, valuation documentation and reconciliation with financial statements. Conclusion The RBI’s new prudential framework on Specified Non-Financial Assets aims to bring greater discipline, transparency and consistency in the treatment of immovable assets acquired against stressed loan exposures. The introduction of Dynamic Return R899 through the CIMS portal provides a structured mechanism for reporting the stock and movement of such assets. NBFCs and other reporting entities covered by the R899 reporting framework should review their existing systems, policies, accounting processes and reporting controls to ensure accurate regulatory reporting. A coordinated approach involving the finance, credit, recovery, legal, risk management and compliance departments will be essential for effective implementation.
