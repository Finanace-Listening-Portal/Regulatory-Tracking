} } Listen to This ArticleBanks have raised concerns that the Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI’s) new cash reserve requirement on foreign exchange (forex) derivatives transactions could tie up rupee liquidity needed for other treasury operations. The Foreign Exchange Dealers’ Association of India (Fedai), the industry body representing authorised dealers, is taking up these concerns with the RBI and seeking clarity on how the rules will be implemented. Under the Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR) framework, banks must maintain a cash reserve with the RBI equivalent to 20 per cent of the rupee value of specified derivatives transactions exceeding $2 million. Lenders have proposed replacing the requirement with an additional charge on clients, Reuters reported on Monday. Banks have also sought the removal of the $2 million threshold, warning that it could encourage clients to split larger hedging transactions into smaller deals to avoid the reserve requirement. They have said such practices would be difficult to monitor. “Fedai is taking it up because it is the self-regulated organisation and an industry body representing all the banks, to discuss with the RBI so that the whole industry has the same practices,” said a senior banker with a state-owned bank. The discussions follow the RBI’s October 10 measures to curb speculative activity and excessive trading in rupee-linked forex derivatives, as the currency faces pressure from elevated crude oil prices The RBI has also introduced a dedicated dollar supply window for the three state-run oil-marketing companies, routing their dollar requirements through the central bank instead of the spot market.
