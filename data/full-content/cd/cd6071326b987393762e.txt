It handles a large share of India's daily equity and derivatives trading volumes. The NSE IPO opened for subscription on 17 September and closes today, 21 September 2026. With bidding wrapping up today, the conversation is no longer about whether to apply. It is about what comes next for NSE once it starts trading as a public company. Its business model, where it stands in the market, and where it could go from here are worth understanding before the listing on 24 September.What the NSE IPO RepresentsMost companies go public to raise money for growth. NSE is a different case entirely. It is the system through which Indian markets actually function, this listing puts ownership of that system into public hands for the first time. The current IPO is entirely an offer for sale (OFS), meaning existing shareholders are selling their shares and NSE itself will not receive the proceeds. The issue comprises up to 12.64 crore shares and is valued at around ₹22,561.57 crore at the upper end of the ₹1,700–₹1,785 price band. With bidding closing on 21 September, the next key milestones are allotment, expected on 22 September, followed by the BSE listing on 24 September. Investors can check their IPO Allotment Status once the allotment is announced. NSE IPO Subscription Status As of the latest update on 21 September, the final day of bidding, the NSE IPO was subscribed 5.62 times overall. The QIB category was subscribed 12.65 times, NII 6.50 times, retail 1.25 times and the employee quota 2.28 times. NSE's Business ModelAs a listed company, NSE's earnings come from several sources, most of which grow alongside broader market activity. When trades go through the exchange, NSE collects a transaction charge based on the value of that trade. This is the biggest single contributor to revenue.Derivatives trading fees add considerably to that, and India's derivatives volumes have been substantial for several years now.Market data and technology services are provided to brokers, institutional participants and financial technology firms.Listing fees are charged to companies that list their securities on the exchange.Index licensing generates recurring revenue from Nifty-branded indices used in mutual funds, ETFs and structured products.The NSE business model has a built-in natural growth link. More investors, more trades and higher volumes tend to push most of these revenue lines upward without NSE having to fundamentally change what it does. For investors coming in through this listing, that natural growth link is really the core of the long-term story. As India's retail investor base keeps expanding, NSE stands to benefit without having to reinvent its model. NSE's Market LeadershipNSE holds a dominant position in India's equity markets, supported by strong participation across cash and derivatives segments. According to The Economic Times, NSE commanded nearly 93% of India's cash-market share, 99.7% of equity futures and 68.5% of equity options premium turnover in FY26. The Nifty 50 is one of those indices that shows up everywhere in Indian finance. Mutual funds track it, derivatives are built on it, and investment products across the country use it as a reference. Its widespread use also supports NSE's index-related businesses and reinforces the role of its market infrastructure in India's financial ecosystem. Technology has played a big part in keeping that position strong. NSE’s trading infrastructure can handle large volumes reliably and without disruption, which is important for large institutional traders as well as individual investors. More traders mean deeper liquidity, and deeper liquidity pulls in even more participants. That cycle has worked quietly in NSE's favour for years. Post-listing, this dominance gives NSE a strong floor. As NSE moves into its post-listing phase, the focus will be on how effectively it can sustain its market position while expanding participation, developing products and services, and growing its data, technology and index-related businesses. Key Risks to the Outlook The growth opportunity also comes with factors that investors may monitor:Trading activity: Lower market volumes can affect transaction-linked revenue.Derivatives exposure: NSE remains significantly dependent on derivatives activity, particularly options.Regulatory changes: Changes to trading rules, transaction charges or financial products can affect operations and revenue.Technology and operational risks: System resilience remains critical for a market infrastructure institution.Competitive dynamics: Changes in market share and the development of new financial products can influence NSE's position. What Investors Should UnderstandThose examining information about the NSE IPO India may consider several factors.NSE's revenue mix and its dependence on trading activityMarket-share trends across equity and derivatives segmentsThe continued growth of India's retail and institutional investor basePotential opportunities in market data, technology, index licensing and other servicesThe company's ability to sustain its market position as a listed entityRegulatory, technology and operational risks that could affect future performanceThese are factual considerations rather than recommendations for or against participation.ConclusionWith the NSE IPO closing today, 21 September, attention moves to NSE's debut as a publicly listed company and what its growth trajectory looks like from here. NSE enters this phase with an established market position, significant scale in derivatives and multiple avenues for revenue diversification.The post-IPO outlook will depend on how effectively NSE can sustain market participation, develop new products and services, diversify its revenue base and respond to changes in trading activity and the regulatory environment.
