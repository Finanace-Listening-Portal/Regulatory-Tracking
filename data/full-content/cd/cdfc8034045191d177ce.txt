Articles 3 min read India sees up to 45% cut in property insurance rates; cyber insurance sees steepest decline: Report Strong insurer capacity and increased competition drove commercial insurance rates lower across property, financial and professional liability, and cyber insurance in India during the first three quarters of 2026, giving businesses greater room to negotiate premiums, coverage limits and policy terms, according to Marsh’s Global Insurance Market Index. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Oct 22, 2026 at 02:16 PM IST var news_msid = 135113288; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Commercial insurance rates in India continued to decline through the first three quarters of 2026, with increased insurer capacity and intensifying competition easing pricing across property, financial and professional liability, and cyber insurance, according to Marsh’s Global Insurance Market Index.The latest assessment by Marsh pointed to a more favourable market for corporate insurance buyers, with India recording some of the steepest rate reductions across the India, Middle East and Africa (IMEA) region. The trend has also strengthened businesses’ negotiating power on coverage, limits, deductibles and policy terms. Advt The findings come amid a broader global softening in commercial insurance prices. Global rates fell 5% in the third quarter of 2026, marking the ninth consecutive quarter of declines, while the IMEA region recorded the steepest composite rate reduction among all regions at 14%, according to Marsh’s latest index.Property insurance sees sustained rate declinesProperty insurance rates in India declined for the fourth consecutive quarter, supported by increased capacity and competition among insurers. During the first quarter of 2026, some Indian clients secured rate reductions of up to 45%, although decreases were typically lower. This compared with an average 10% decline across IMEA during the quarter.Competition strengthened as the year progressed, aided by ample insurer capacity and the removal of tariffs in India. The resulting market conditions have increased insurers’ willingness to compete for well-managed risks, allowing businesses to negotiate not only lower premiums but also improvements in coverage, limits and policy conditions.The broader regional trend remained favourable in the third quarter. Property insurance rates across IMEA declined 15%, compared with a 10% fall globally, reflecting the availability of capacity and continued competition.Cyber and liability insurance costs ease Advt Financial and professional lines also recorded rate reductions in India for the fourth consecutive quarter, supported by strong insurer appetite and available capacity. Directors’ and officers’ (D&O) liability rates declined by as much as 20% in India during the first quarter, while professional indemnity rates also fell.Cyber insurance pricing remained under pressure as insurer capacity expanded and competition continued. India recorded the steepest cyber insurance rate decreases in IMEA during the third quarter, when the regional average declined 10%. Across IMEA, cyber rates have fallen for 13 consecutive quarters, according to Marsh’s latest global index. Advt Despite lower prices, businesses continue to face evolving cyber risks driven by increasing digital dependence, emerging technologies and changing threat patterns. The favourable pricing environment offers companies an opportunity to reassess their cyber insurance programmes and ensure coverage reflects their current exposures.Globally, cyber insurance rates declined 4% in the third quarter, while financial and professional lines rates fell 3%. Casualty insurance was an exception, with rates rising 1% worldwide, largely because of continued claims severity and litigation pressures in the US.The decline in commercial insurance rates is creating opportunities for businesses to reassess their insurance programmes rather than focusing exclusively on premium savings. Greater competition is allowing buyers to explore higher limits, lower deductibles and broader coverage, depending on their risk profile and insurers’ underwriting appetite. Advt Marsh said businesses should use the current market conditions to review insurance protection against geopolitical uncertainty, cyber threats, supply-chain disruptions and natural catastrophe exposures, while preparing for the possibility of a shift in the insurance pricing cycle.
