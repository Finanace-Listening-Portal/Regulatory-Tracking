Summary: SEBI Investment Adviser Registration is a regulatory requirement for individuals and entities providing investment advisory services covered by the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013, unless an exemption applies. Registration involves assessing the proposed advisory activities, satisfying educational and certification requirements, preparing application documents, maintaining the prescribed deposit and establishing systems for client protection and regulatory compliance. The regulatory framework has undergone important changes, including revised educational eligibility, removal of the former general experience requirement and replacement of earlier net-worth requirements with client-based deposits. Applicants must consider the applicable NISM certifications, the role of the principal officer and persons associated with investment advice, and the requirements for individual and non-individual business structures. The registration process involves preparing business and compliance documents, submitting the application through the prescribed channel, responding to regulatory clarifications and completing applicable registration conditions. Registered advisers must also comply with requirements relating to advisory fees, client agreements, risk profiling, investment suitability, disclosures, complaint handling, record maintenance and annual compliance audits. The guide also explains the distinction between regulatory deposits and registration fees, the applicable client advisory fee models, advance fee collection and common registration mistakes. It highlights the importance of reviewing the latest SEBI regulations, amendments, master circulars and supervisory directions before commencing investment advisory activities. Professional investment advice therefore requires more than knowledge of financial markets. It involves understanding a client’s circumstances, assessing investment suitability, explaining risks and managing conflicts of interest. In India, investment advisers are regulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India under the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013, as amended. Individuals and businesses intending to provide investment advisory services must assess whether they require registration before accepting clients or promoting their services. This guide explains SEBI Investment Adviser Registration, including eligibility, qualifications, certification, deposits, fees, application preparation and ongoing compliance. What Is SEBI Investment Adviser Registration? SEBI Investment Adviser Registration is the regulatory authorisation required to conduct investment advisory activities covered by the Investment Advisers Regulations, unless a specific exemption applies. Investment advice broadly includes advice concerning investment in, purchase or sale of securities, recommendations relating to a securities portfolio and financial planning within the regulatory definition. Such advice may be communicated through written reports, meetings, telephone calls, emails, mobile applications or other digital platforms. A registered investment adviser is commonly called an RIA, meaning Registered Investment Adviser. Registration establishes a regulated within which the adviser must operate. It does not guarantee investment performance or assure returns to clients. Why Is Registration Important? Registration provides a for accountability in investment advisory services. It requires advisers to meet applicable eligibility requirements, follow standards of conduct and establish processes for protecting client interests. For clients, the regulatory supports transparency regarding services, fees, risks and potential conflicts. For advisers, it creates a foundation for operating a compliant advisory practice. An important principle is that the nature of the activity determines its regulatory treatment. Describing a service as “financial coaching,” “wealth guidance” or “portfolio mentoring” does not automatically place it outside the regulations if the actual service involves regulated investment advice. Legal Structure Governing Investment Advisers The principal legal is the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013, together with subsequent amendments, circulars and directions. The regulations address registration, eligibility, qualifications, certification, deposits, client relationships, disclosures, risk profiling, suitability, records and compliance obligations. SEBI’s Master Circular for Investment Advisers, issued on 6 February 2026, consolidates relevant regulatory directions. Applicants and registered advisers must also consider subsequent applicable changes and requirements issued by the Investment Adviser Administration and Supervisory Body, commonly called the IAASB. A business should therefore review the operative instead of relying only on the original regulations. Who Requires SEBI Investment Adviser Registration? Registration should be assessed where an individual or business intends to provide investment advice for consideration and the activity falls within the regulations. Examples include personalised securities recommendations, paid investment portfolio reviews and financial planning involving securities advice. Consider a business that collects a client’s income, liabilities, goals and existing investments, then recommends a securities portfolio for a fee. Such a business should examine the investment adviser registration requirements before commencing operations. The assessment should consider the actual recommendations, whether the service is personalised, the method of remuneration and whether a specific exemption applies. Exemptions from Investment Adviser Registration The regulations provide conditional exemptions for certain persons and activities. These include specified incidental advice provided by mutual fund distributors, legal professionals, accounting and secretarial professionals, and certain registered intermediaries. However, these exemptions have defined boundaries. A professional qualification or registration under another does not automatically permit an unrestricted investment advisory business. For example, advice incidental to an existing professional assignment must be distinguished from a separate business offering investment recommendations. The proposed services, remuneration and client relationships should be examined before relying on an exemption. Eligibility for SEBI Investment Adviser Registration Educational Qualifications The qualification was amended in November 2025. A recognised graduate degree or equivalent educational qualification, or a CFA Charter, accompanied by relevant NISM or NISM-accredited certification, is an available eligibility route under the amended provisions. The structure also recognises specified NISM postgraduate programmes. The applicable qualification requirements cover individual investment advisers, principal officers of non-individual advisers and persons associated with investment advice. Applicants should assess each relevant person rather than assuming that one qualified founder satisfies every personnel requirement. Experience Requirement The former general requirement of five years of relevant experience has been removed. Applicants should therefore use the amended eligibility provisions when assessing their qualifications. Older descriptions of the registration may continue to refer to requirements that have since changed. NISM Certification Under the standard examination route, the relevant certifications are: Examination Certification level NISM-Series-X-A: Investment Adviser Level 1 NISM-Series-X-B: Investment Adviser Level 2 Both levels apply to the relevant individual advisers, principal officers and persons associated with investment advice. Passing the examinations does not itself authorise a person to commence a business requiring SEBI registration. Certification and registration are separate requirements. Eligible holders of valid Level 1 and Level 2 certificates may use the applicable NISM-Series-X-C renewal examination route. Advisers should monitor certification validity and complete the relevant renewal requirements within the prescribed time. Infrastructure and Operational Readiness An applicant must be capable of delivering investment advisory services responsibly. Preparation should cover office arrangements, communication systems, secure record storage, client onboarding and compliance monitoring. The systems should enable the adviser to demonstrate what information was collected, what recommendations were made and why those recommendations were considered suitable. Even a small advisory practice benefits from clearly documented responsibilities and a consistent process for handling clients. Business Structures for Investment Advisers Investment advisory businesses may operate through eligible individual or non-individual structures, subject to the applicable conditions. The choice of structure should reflect ownership, management, staffing, intended scale and operating costs. A solo practitioner may have different requirements from a business with several advisers, multiple owners and a wider service offering. For a non-individual applicant, management responsibilities and the principal officer’s role should be settled early. Changes to these arrangements during application preparation may require revisions across several documents. Deposit Requirement for Investment Advisers The earlier investment adviser net-worth has been replaced by a client-based deposit requirement. Maximum number of clients on any day of the preceding financial year Required deposit Up to 150 clients ₹1 lakh 151–300 clients ₹2 lakh 301–1,000 clients ₹5 lakh 1,001 or more clients ₹10 lakh The deposit must be maintained with a scheduled bank and marked under lien in favour of the IAASB in the prescribed manner. Where the applicable deposit amount changes, the annual adjustment must be completed by 30 April of the subsequent financial year under the prescribed. Applicants should distinguish this deposit from registration fees and operating expenses. The deposit represents funds committed to a regulatory requirement and should be separately considered when estimating business capital. Documents Required for Registration The final document requirements depend on the applicant category and the current official checklist. Applicants can organise their preparation as follows: Document category Information to prepare Identity and address Applicant’s identification and address records Entity documents Incorporation or constitution documents, where applicable Ownership and management Ownership structure and management details Qualifications Educational credentials of relevant persons Certifications Applicable certification records and validity dates Business plan Proposed services, client categories and revenue model Infrastructure Office, technology and record-management arrangements Compliance documents Policies, disclosures and proposed client documentation Financial arrangements Deposit-related records and fee-payment arrangements All documents should consistently identify the applicant and describe the same proposed business. Differences in names, addresses, ownership details or service descriptions should be resolved before submission. Step-by-Step SEBI Investment Adviser Registration Process Step 1: Define the Proposed Advisory Services Begin with a precise description of the services the business intends to provide. Identify whether the offering will include financial planning, portfolio reviews, securities recommendations or other advisory services. A clear service scope helps determine the appropriate regulatory and supports consistent application documents. Step 2: Choose the Business Structure Select a structure that reflects the proposed ownership, management and scale of operations. For an entity application, confirm that its constitutional documents and internal approvals support the proposed business. Identify the principal officer and other relevant personnel. Step 3: Review Eligibility and Certifications Assess the qualifications and certifications of each relevant person. A practical internal schedule should record the person’s role, educational qualification, certification details and expiry dates. Any gaps should be addressed before the application is submitted. Step 4: Prepare the Business and Compliance Documents Prepare the business plan, supporting records, declarations and proposed compliance arrangements using the applicable checklist. The business plan, client agreement and proposed marketing language should describe the same service. Unusual arrangements, such as multiple business activities or related entities, should be explained clearly. Step 5: Submit the Application Submit the application through the prescribed channel and pay the applicable application charges. The involves assessment through the IAASB and recommendation to SEBI, subject to the applicant satisfying the registration conditions. Step 6: Respond to Clarifications The reviewing authority may request additional information or revised documents. Responses should directly address each query, identify supporting evidence and explain any changes. Maintaining a consolidated record of queries and replies helps avoid inconsistent submissions. Step 7: Complete Registration Conditions Complete the prescribed fee payments, deposit arrangements and other conditions relevant to the grant of registration. Before commencing operations, review the certificate details, conditions of registration and any outstanding operational requirements. Advisory Fees That Can Be Charged to Clients Fees paid for registration are separate from advisory fees charged to clients. For applicable individual and HUF clients, the provides two principal charging modes: Charging mode Prescribed ceiling Fixed-fee mode ₹1,51,000 annually per client family Assets Under Advice mode 2.5% of AUA annually per client family Statutory charges are excluded from these ceilings. Different treatment applies to non-individual clients and accredited investors, whose fee arrangements may be bilaterally negotiated. Changes between fee modes must follow the applicable conditions. Advisers should ensure that their agreements, invoices and billing systems apply the correct method. Advance Collection of Advisory Where agreed by the client, an investment adviser may collect fees in advance for a period of up to one year under the applicable. Fee arrangements should clearly explain the service period, payment terms and treatment of early termination. Different client categories may be subject to different contractual treatment. Client Agreement and Onboarding An investment adviser must enter into an investment advisory agreement incorporating the applicable Most Important Terms and Conditions, or MITC. The agreement should make the service scope, fees, risks and responsibilities understandable to the client. Key safeguards include restrictions on accepting client investment funds or securities, the prohibition on guaranteed returns and the requirement for specific client consent where applicable to trade execution. Cash payment of advisory fees is not permitted under the prescribed MITC. Onboarding should also establish a reliable understanding of the client’s financial circumstances before recommendations are provided. Risk Profiling and Suitability Assessment Risk profiling evaluates the client’s circumstances, investment experience, financial goals, time horizon and ability to absorb losses. Suitability assessment connects that information with the proposed recommendations. For example, two clients of the same age may require different portfolios because one has substantial debt and near-term expenses while the other has stable income and a long investment horizon. A sound process records the reasons for a recommendation and provides a basis for reviewing it when the client’s circumstances change. Ongoing Compliance After Registration Registration creates continuing responsibilities. Advisers must maintain appropriate client records, provide required disclosures, manage conflicts, monitor certifications and comply with applicable reporting requirements. The Investor Charter should be brought to clients’ attention. Advisers must also make the prescribed complaint disclosures on their website and mobile application, where applicable. Monthly complaint information is required to be updated by the seventh day of the following month. A compliance calendar can help track submissions, disclosures, periodic fees, certification renewals and corrective actions. Annual Compliance Audit Investment advisers must undergo an annual compliance audit under the applicable. The prescribed requirements include completing the audit within six months of financial year-end and submitting the report within one month of its date. Adverse findings require reporting and corrective action within the applicable timelines. Maintaining evidence throughout the year makes the audit more effective and helps identify weaknesses before they affect clients. Common Mistakes to Avoid Applicants should avoid relying on superseded qualification or net-worth requirements, treating NISM certification as permission to start business, and submitting inconsistent descriptions of their services. Other weaknesses include confusing the regulatory deposit with registration fees, using generic client agreements and budgeting only for the initial application. A realistic business plan should account for ongoing compliance, technology, staffing and record management. Launch plans should also allow time for regulatory clarifications instead of assuming approval within an unsupported fixed period. Conclusion SEBI Investment Adviser Registration is an important step towards establishing a credible and responsible investment advisory practice in India. Meeting the applicable qualification, certification, deposit and documentation requirements provides the foundation for registration. However, building a sustainable practice also requires transparent client communication, appropriate risk profiling, suitable recommendations and consistent compliance with regulatory obligations. Applicants should carefully assess their business structure, prepare accurate documents and budget for both registration and ongoing operational responsibilities. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ’s) Q1. Is NISM certification sufficient to start an investment advisory business? Ans. No. NISM certification and SEBI registration are separate requirements. Activities requiring registration should commence only after the applicable registration requirements are completed. Q2. Can a graduate apply for registration? Ans. Yes. The amended recognises a graduate degree or equivalent qualification together with the relevant certification, subject to the applicable conditions. Other recognised qualification routes also exist. Q3. Is five years of experience mandatory? Ans. The former general five-year relevant experience requirement has been removed. Applicants must assess eligibility under the amended provisions. Q4. Is the earlier ₹50 lakh net-worth requirement still applicable? Ans. The investment adviser now uses client-based deposits. Separate financial requirements may arise if the business undertakes other regulated activities. Q5. Can an LLP apply for investment adviser registration? Ans. An LLP may apply under the relevant non-individual, subject to satisfying the applicable eligibility, personnel, deposit and other registration conditions. Q6. What is the minimum deposit? Ans. The prescribed deposit starts at ₹1 lakh for the category covering up to 150 clients. Higher deposits apply as client numbers increase. Q7. Does registration expire after five years? Ans. The certificate continues unless suspended or cancelled, subject to compliance and applicable periodic fee payments. The five-year payment cycle is separate from certificate validity. Q8. Can advisory fees be collected in advance? Ans. Yes. Where agreed by the client, advance fees may be collected for up to one year under the applicable. Q9. Does SEBI registration guarantee investment returns? Ans. No. Registration does not assure performance, profits or risk-free investments. Advisers must not make guaranteed-return claims. Q10. What costs should an applicant budget for? Ans. Applicants should consider SEBI fees, IAASB charges, certifications, the regulatory deposit, staffing, technology, record management and ongoing compliance expenses.
