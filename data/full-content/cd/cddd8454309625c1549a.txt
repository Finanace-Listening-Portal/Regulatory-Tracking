Summary: The increasing pendency of GST appeals in Tamil Nadu has raised serious concerns about the effectiveness of statutory remedies and the time required for taxpayers to obtain justice. According to the State Government’s figures recorded in G.O. (Ms) No. 157 dated 16 October 2026, 84,022 appeals were pending as on 5 July 2026, increasing to 84,707 appeals involving disputed revenue of approximately ₹31,398.25 crore as on 27 July 2026. Only 6,954 appeals were disposed of between January and June 2026. Section 107 of the CGST and TNGST Acts provides the statutory first appeal, with prescribed pre-deposit requirements and protection against recovery of the remaining disputed amount. Although Section 107(13) contemplates disposal within one year wherever possible, substantial pendency can delay the final resolution of disputes, affecting working capital, business planning and legal certainty. The Tamil Nadu Government has responded by approving additional appellate forums, but their effectiveness will depend on adequate staffing, case allocation, regular hearings and timely disposal. The constitutional question is whether taxpayers alleging jurisdictional errors, violations of natural justice, orders beyond show cause notices or other serious statutory defects should invariably be directed to pursue departmental appeals. Supreme Court decisions including Radha Krishan Industries and Commercial Steel Limited recognise that an alternative statutory remedy does not completely exclude writ jurisdiction under Article 226, although High Courts ordinarily require exhaustion of effective appellate remedies unless established exceptions apply. The article examines appellate delays, regional pendency in Tiruvallur and surrounding divisions, the legal limits of the alternative-remedy rule and practical reforms needed to ensure timely, reasoned decisions. The position in Tiruvallur and surrounding areas Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Introduction The introduction of the Goods and Services Tax (GST) was expected to simplify the indirect tax system in India. It brought different indirect taxes under one common framework and introduced a uniform system of tax administration. However, as the GST system has developed, disputes between taxpayers and the department have also increased considerably. A large number of taxpayers are now facing assessment orders, demands for reversal of Input Tax Credit (ITC), interest and penalties. Many of these disputes involve complicated questions of law, interpretation of notifications, limitation, procedural irregularities and violation of the principles of natural justice. When an assessment order is passed against a taxpayer, the normal remedy is to file an appeal before the First Appellate Authority under Section 107 of the CGST/TNGST Act, 2017. There is no dispute about the importance of this statutory remedy. However, a practical difficulty has emerged. The number of appeals filed before the Appellate Authorities has increased substantially, and the time taken to obtain a final decision has become a matter of serious concern. This raises an important question: when a taxpayer approaches the High Court under Article 226 of the Constitution, should the mere availability of an appeal under Section 107 be sufficient reason to direct the taxpayer back to the departmental appellate authority, without examining the nature of the grievance and the circumstances of the case? This issue deserves serious consideration. 1. Why do taxpayers prefer approaching the High Court? There is a common impression that taxpayers approach the High Court to avoid the statutory appeal procedure or to escape payment of the required pre-deposit. However, this is not always the reason. In many cases, the taxpayer is prepared to comply with the financial requirements of the appellate process. The taxpayer is willing to pay the prescribed pre-deposit and contest the demand in accordance with law. The real difficulty is the time involved in obtaining a decision. For example, consider a small business owner who receives an assessment order raising a demand of several lakhs of rupees. The taxpayer believes that the order is legally unsustainable because the department has: Passed an order beyond the scope of the show cause notice. Failed to consider the reply submitted by the taxpayer. Denied a reasonable opportunity of personal hearing. Confirmed a demand beyond the statutory limitation period. Failed to consider the documents and explanations already available on record. Passed an order contrary to the provisions of the GST Act. In such circumstances, the taxpayer may prefer approaching the High Court for judicial review rather than waiting for a lengthy departmental appellate process. The intention is not necessarily to avoid the appellate system. It is to obtain an effective remedy within a reasonable period. There is an important difference between avoiding the payment of tax and seeking a speedy decision on a genuine legal grievance. These two situations should not be confused. 2. What does Section 107 say about the time limit for disposal of appeals? Section 107 of the CGST Act provides the statutory remedy of appeal against an order passed by the adjudicating authority. Section 107(6) prescribes the pre-deposit requirements for filing an appeal. Once the prescribed payment is made, Section 107(7) provides that recovery proceedings for the balance amount shall be deemed to have been stayed. Therefore, the taxpayer who complies with the statutory pre-deposit requirement receives protection against recovery of the balance disputed amount. However, the real concern is not merely recovery. The more important issue is the time taken to obtain the appellate order. Section 107(13) specifically provides: “The Appellate Authority shall, where it is possible to do so, hear and decide every appeal within a period of one year from the date on which it is filed.” The words “where it is possible to do so” are important. The provision recognises a one-year period as the intended timeframe for disposal, but it does not guarantee that every appeal will be decided within one year. In practice, when the number of pending appeals is high and the number of available appellate authorities is limited, taxpayers may have to wait considerably longer. For a small trader, manufacturer or service provider, a pending tax dispute is not merely a legal issue. It can affect financial planning, working capital, business expansion and future transactions. A taxpayer who has already paid the prescribed pre-deposit and is willing to contest the matter should not have to face unnecessary uncertainty for an indefinite period. 3. The actual GST appellate backlog in Tamil Nadu The seriousness of the problem can be understood from the figures recorded by the Tamil Nadu Government itself. According to G.O. (Ms) No.157 dated 16 October 2026, the number of pending appeals before the State GST First Appellate Authorities stood at 84,022 as on 5 July 2026. The disputed revenue involved in these appeals was approximately Rs.31,271.93 crore. The Government’s records also indicate that only 6,954 appeals were disposed of during the period from January to June 2026. The position further worsened, with the pending appeals reportedly increasing to 84,707 as on 27 July 2026, involving disputed revenue of approximately Rs.31,398.25 crore. These figures are significant. They clearly indicate that the problem is not confined to individual taxpayers or a particular district. It is a wider administrative issue affecting the GST appellate system in Tamil Nadu. The following figures provide an idea of the pendency: Particulars Number Pending appeals as on 5 July 2026 84,022 Pending appeals as on 27 July 2026 84,707 Appeals disposed between January and June 2026 6,954 Disputed revenue as on 27 July 2026 Approximately Rs.31,398.25 crore If the disposal rate recorded during the first six months of 2026 were to continue, and no fresh appeals were filed, the existing backlog of 84,022 appeals would take approximately 72 months to clear. This is only a mathematical illustration based on the reported disposal rate. It is not a prediction of the actual disposal period, particularly because the Government has since approved additional appellate forums. Nevertheless, the figures demonstrate the scale of the challenge. 4. How many appellate authorities are available in Tamil Nadu? The Tamil Nadu Government has already taken steps to strengthen the appellate infrastructure. Through G.O. (Ms) No.155 dated 5 November 2025, the Government reorganised the State GST appellate wing by creating 15 First Appellate Forums, consisting of: One Joint Commissioner (Appeals) Forum. Fourteen Deputy Commissioner (Appeals) Forums. Four supporting camp offices. Subsequently, after reviewing the number of pending appeals, the Government approved further expansion through orders dated 16 October 2026. The additional arrangements include four regular Deputy Commissioner (Appeals) forums, ten additional temporary forums for a period of three years and one Joint Commissioner (Appeals) forum at Madurai. Thus, the Government has approved an expansion of approximately 15 additional forums over the earlier structure. This is a welcome administrative measure. However, the creation or sanctioning of additional appellate forums alone will not immediately solve the problem. The actual benefit will depend on the appointment and availability of officers, supporting staff, distribution of pending cases, regular scheduling of hearings and timely pronouncement of orders. The Government has recognised the problem and taken steps to increase the appellate capacity. The next important question is how quickly these additional arrangements will become fully functional and reduce the existing backlog. 5. The position in Tiruvallur and surrounding areas The issue is particularly relevant to taxpayers in Tiruvallur, Kancheepuram and Chennai. According to the division-wise figures recorded in the Government Order, the pending appeals as on 5 July 2026 included: Division Pending appeals Tiruvallur 2,645 Kancheepuram 4,443 Chennai Central 5,363 Chennai North 4,337 Chennai South 5,515 The Tiruvallur and Kancheepuram divisions together accounted for 7,088 pending appeals. These are not insignificant numbers. A taxpayer from Tiruvallur who receives an assessment order has the statutory right to approach the designated First Appellate Authority. But the availability of that remedy and the actual time taken to obtain a decision are two different matters. The taxpayer needs an effective remedy, not merely a forum where an appeal can be filed.
