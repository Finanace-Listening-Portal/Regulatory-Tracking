Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Chhitubhai Jagubhai Patel Vs PCIT (ITAT Surat) Section 263 Revision Can Cover Unexamined Indexed Costs, but Cannot Reopen Section 50C Valuation Already Decided in Appeal The Scope of Revision After an Appeal The Surat ITAT held that the doctrine of merger applies issue by issue. Where an appellate authority has considered and decided the full value of consideration under Section 50C, the Principal Commissioner cannot reopen that issue through revision under Section 263. However, the Principal Commissioner can revise the assessment on an independent issue, such as the indexed cost of acquisition and improvement, where that issue remained outside the earlier appeal and the Assessing Officer failed to examine the claims. Both issues arose from the sale of the same property and entered the same capital gains computation. Reassessment and the Section 50C Addition The assessee had originally filed no return for AY 2014–15. After receiving a notice under Section 148 concerning the sale of immovable property, he filed a return declaring total income of ₹81,10,510, including capital gains computed using the actual sale consideration of ₹1.50 crore. The Assessing Officer proposed to substitute the stamp duty valuation of ₹1,85,52,000 under Section 50C. At the assessee’s request, the Assessing Officer referred the property to the Departmental Valuation Officer (DVO) under Section 50C(2). The report was pending when the Assessing Officer completed the reassessment on 29 September 2021, adopting the stamp duty value and making an addition of ₹35.52 lakh. The assessment order recorded that it was subject to amendment under Section 155(15) upon receipt of the valuation report. Valuation Dispute Ends in the Quantum Appeal The DVO valued the property at ₹1,61,28,000. Acting on the assessee’s rectification request, the Assessing Officer reduced the addition to ₹11.28 lakh. The Commissioner (Appeals) also directed that the addition be restricted to the difference between the actual sale consideration and the DVO’s valuation. In the further appeal, the Surat ITAT found that the difference of ₹11.28 lakh was within 10% of the actual sale consideration. In its order dated 12 October 2023, the Bench granted the benefit of the third proviso to Section 50C(1) and deleted the balance addition. The Assessing Officer gave effect to that decision on 28 November 2023. Why the Principal Commissioner Invoked Section 263 The Principal Commissioner subsequently found that the Assessing Officer had failed to examine the claimed indexed cost of acquisition of ₹63,87,454 and indexed cost of improvement of ₹5,41,197, aggregating to ₹69,28,651. He set aside the reassessment and directed a fresh assessment covering the issue identified in revision as well as issues already considered. The assessee argued that the original reassessment was invalid because the Assessing Officer had completed it without awaiting the DVO’s report. He relied on Rajni Arvind Birla v. ITO, ITA No. 930/Ahd/2025. The Surat Bench declined to entertain this collateral challenge in the revision appeal. It noted that the valuation dispute had already been adjudicated after rectification and appeal, and that the assessee had raised no specific ground challenging the validity of the original reassessment in the present appeal. Merger Protects the Issue Actually Decided The Tribunal applied Explanation 1(c) to Section 263(1), which preserves revisionary jurisdiction over matters that were not considered and decided in appeal. It referred to CIT v. Shri Arbuda Mills Ltd. (1998) 231 ITR 50 (SC) and considered the assessee’s reliance on CIT v. Sanvijay Rolling & Engg. Ltd. (2022) 137 taxmann.com 123 (Bombay). The Bombay High Court decision protected issues specifically adjudicated in appeal. Here, the earlier appellate orders had dealt with sale consideration under Section 50C, while the indexed cost claims remained unadjudicated. The assessee could also point to no specific inquiry by the Assessing Officer or documentary material that the officer had examined to verify those costs. The Principal Commissioner’s finding of lack of inquiry therefore remained unrebutted. The Tribunal upheld revision on the indexed cost claims. Fresh Proceedings Restricted to Indexed Costs The Tribunal partly allowed the appeal by modifying the Principal Commissioner’s directions. It confined the consequential proceedings to verification of the indexed cost of acquisition and indexed cost of improvement, after giving the assessee a reasonable opportunity of being heard. The earlier deletion of the Section 50C addition stood protected by the appellate decision. Author’s Comments This ruling illustrates why the scope of an earlier appeal matters even where the disputed items form part of a single capital gains computation. The consideration adopted for the transfer and the deductions claimed for acquisition and improvement are distinct matters. To determine what has merged, read the findings and operative directions in the earlier appellate order alongside the issues identified in the Section 263 notice. Where revision rests on an alleged lack of inquiry, the assessment record can show precisely what the Assessing Officer asked, what evidence the assessee furnished, and which claims the officer actually examined Cases Discussed CIT Vs Shri Arbuda Mills Ltd. — (1998) 231 ITR 50 (SC); revision over matters not decided in appeal. CIT Vs Sanvijay Rolling & Engg. Ltd. — (2022) 137 taxmann.com 123 (Bombay); merger of issues decided on appeal. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF ITAT SURAT Feeling aggrieved by the revision-order dated 06.03.2024 passed by learned Pr. Commissioner of Income-Tax, Surat – 1 [“Ld. PCIT”] u/s 263 of Income- tax Act, 1961 [“the Act”], which in turn arises out of the assessment-order dated 29.09.2021 passed by learned National Faceless Assesssment Centre, Delhi [“Ld. AO”] u/s 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act, for Assessment-Year [“AY”] 2014-15, the assessee has filed this appeal on following grounds: “1. The Principal C.I.T. erred in law and on fact in holding that assessment order passed u/s.147 r.w.s. 144B dated 29.09.2021 is erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of Revenue by invoking the provision of sec.263 of the Act and thereby directing the Assessing Officer to pass fresh assessment order after examining the issue discussed in the order passed u/s.263 of the Act. 2. The Principal C.I.T. erred in law in directing the Assessing Officer to carry out de novo assessment, and thereafter, directed to pass fresh assessment order after taking into consideration the issue which has already been considered in addition to the issue discussed in the order passed u/s. 263 without appreciating that direction to carry out de novo assessment cannot be issued by exercising power u/s. 263 as the Assessing Officer assuming the jurisdiction in response to the order passed u/s.263 is only entitled to inquire the issue on which revision power is exercised by the Commissioner. 3. The appellant craves leave to add, amend or alter the grounds of appeal at the time of hearing, if need arise.” 2. The background facts leading to present appeal are as under: (i) Assessment proceeding – The assessee did not file any return of income of AY 2014-15. The Ld. AO, taking into account information in his possession revealing that the assessee had entered into a transaction of sale of immovable property during the relevant year, issued notice u/s 148 to initiate proceeding of assessment u/s 147. In reply, the assessee filed return declaring a total income of Rs. 81,10,510/- (including the capital gain from impugned transaction of sale of property). While computing capital gain, the assessee adopted full value of consideration at Rs. 1,50,00,000/- equal to the actual sale proceed. However, the Ld. AO found that the valuation done by stamps authority was Rs. 1,85,52,000/-. Accordingly, the Ld. issued draft assessment-order to assessee proposing to substitute the actual sale proceed by the valuation of stamps authority in terms of section 50C of the Act and thereby make an addition of Rs. 35.52 lakhs [Rs. 1,85,52,000 (-) Rs. 1,50,00,000]. In response, the assessee made a request to refer his case to the Departmental Valuation Officer [“DVO”] u/s 50C(2) and considering assessee’s request, the Ld. AO made reference to the DVO on 20.09.2021 asking the DVO to submit report by 26.09.2021. However, considering that the report was not received from DVO by the designated date, the Ld. AO completed assessment vide assessment-order dated 29.09.2021 adopting the valuation of Rs. 1,85,52,000/- with this noting in assessment-order: “However, the assessment order is further subject to rectification /amendment u/s 155(15) of I.T. Act as per the valuation report received from the Valuation Officer.” Thus, the AO made addition of Rs. 35.52 lakh and assessed total income at Rs. 1,16,62,510/-. (ii) Rectification proceeding – Subsequently, the assessee filed letter dated 08.01.2022 to the Ld. AO and requested to pass a rectification order on the basis of DVO’s report. Accordingly, the Ld. AO passed Rectification-Order dated 11.01.2023 u/s 154 reducing the addition from Rs. 35.52 lakh to Rs. 11.28 lakh in consonance with valuation finalized in DVO’s Report. The relevant paras of the Rectification-Order passed by Ld. AO, read as under: “2. On perusal of record it is seen that Valuation Officer of Income- tax Department has finalized valuation of the property on 12.10.2021 and copy of the same submitted to this office on 29.04.2022. Further, in response of recovery letter issued by the department vide Din no. ITBA/RCV/F/17/2021-22/1037808823(1). In response of above mentioned letter, the assessee filed reply on 08.01.2022 and requested to pass an rectification order on the basis of DVO’s report, in which the value of the property was determined at Rs. 1,61,28,000/-instead of DLC value of Rs. 1,85,52,000/-. 3. After perusal of the assessment order and Valuation Officer’s report of dated 20.09.2021, it is observed that difference between actual sale consideration received by the assessee and Valuation made by the Departmental Valuation Officer comes at Rs.11,28,000/-. Accordingly, assessment order passed by the Assessing Officer of the NFAC is modified as per provisions of section 155(15) of the I.T. Act. Thus, addition of Rs. 11,28,000- is made in the return of income of the assessee instead of Rs.35,52,000/-which was made in the original assessment order. Thus, total income of the assessee is recomputed as under: Particulars Amount (in Rs.) Income declared by the assessee 81,10,510/- Addition as per Valuation Report 11,28,000/- Total Income 92,38,510/- (iii) Appellate proceeding – On other side, the assessee also went in appeal to Ld. CIT(A) and contested the addition of Rs. 35.52 lakh made by Ld. AO in assessment-order. The Ld. CIT(A) disposed of assessee’s appeal vide order dated 20.03.2023 wherein he also took note of the report of DVO and directed the Ld. AO to restrict the addition to the extent of difference between the sale value declared by assessee and the value estimated by DVO [effectively, the Ld. CIT(A) directed the AO to restrict the addition to Rs. 11.28 lakh]. The assessee, however, carried matter in further appeal to the ITAT, Surat Bench in ITA No. 224/SRT/2023. The co-ordinate bench of ITAT, Surat has disposed of assessee’s appeal vide order dated 12.10.2023, copy of same is available in case file. The co-ordinate bench observed that the difference of Rs. 11.28 lakh was less than 10% of the actual sale consideration and accordingly, granted benefit of the 3rd Proviso to section 50C(1) and directed the AO to delete the addition of Rs. 11.28 lakh also. In pursuance of ITAT’s direction, the Ld. AO has passed “Order Giving Appeal Effect” dated 28.11.2023 deleting the remaining addition of Rs. 11.28 lakh also. (iv) Revision proceeding – Subsequently, Ld. PCIT examined the record of assessment-proceeding and viewed that the aforesaid assessment-order dated 29.09.2021 passed by Ld. AO was erroneous-cum-prejudicial to the interest of revenue, which attracted revisionary-jurisdiction u/s 263. According to Ld. PCIT, the AO had not examined the indexed cost of acquisition and indexed cost of improvement claimed by assessee at Rs. 63,87,454/- and 5,41,197/- respectively, aggregating to Rs. 69,28,651/-, in computing taxable capital gain, which had resulted into loss of tax to the revenue. The Ld. PCIT issued show-cause notice dated 07.02.2024 u/s 263 which culminated into passing of revision-order dated 06.03.2024. In concluding Para 9 of revision-order, the Ld. PCIT passed this order: “9. Accordingly, the assessment-order for the A.Y. 2014-15 passed u/s 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act dated 29.09.2021 is set aside with a direction to the A.O. to pass fresh assessment-order after taking into consideration the issues as may have been already considered together with the issue discussed herein above also. Needless to mention that while passing the fresh assessment-order, consequent to this order u/s 263 of the Act, the A.O. shall grant reasonable and sufficient opportunity of being heard to the assessee”. (v) Present Appeal – Aggrieved by aforesaid revision-order, the assessee has come in present appeal before us. 3. Before us, the Ld. AR for assessee raised two-fold contentions to assail the impugned revision-order passed by Ld. PCIT, as under: (i) Firstly, Ld. AR submitted that the original assessment-order dated 29.09.2021 itself was bad and illegal in as much as the Ld. AO completed the assessment without awaiting the DVO’s report, despite having made a reference to DVO u/s 50C(2) of the Act. In this regard, Ld. AR placed reliance upon the decision of ITAT, Ahmedabad bench in Rajni Arvind Birla Vs. Income-tax Officer, ITA No. 930/Ahd/2025, order dated 28.11.2025. He submitted that, in the said decision, the ITAT had quashed the assessment completed without awaiting the DVO’s report and the subsequent rectification-order passed u/s 154 of the Act. According to Ld. AR, the same principle would apply to present case and therefore the assessment-order is bad in law and as a consequence, the revision-order founded upon such illegal assessment- order cannot survive. (ii) Secondly, the Ld. AR submitted that the assessment-order had already undergone appellate proceedings up to the stage of the ITAT. The ITAT had passed its order on 12.10.2023 and the Ld. AO had also passed the consequential “Order Giving Appeal Effect” on 28.11.2023. However, the Ld. PCIT has initiated revisionary proceedings subsequently by issuing a notice dated 07.02.2024. Therefore, according to the Ld. AR, once the assessment-order had merged with the appellate order of the ITAT, the Ld. PCIT could not assume revisionary jurisdiction u/s 263 of the Act. In support, Ld. AR placed reliance upon the decision of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in Commissioner of Income-tax-III Vs. Sanvijay Rolling & Engg. Ltd. (2022) 137 taxmann.com 123 (Bombay). 4. Per contra, Ld. DR for revenue supported the revision-order. He submitted that the assessment-order suffered from lack of enquiry qua as noted by Ld. PCIT. Therefore, the assessment-order was erroneous-cum- prejudicial to the interest of revenue and the Ld. PCIT has rightly invoked action u/s 263. Accordingly, the Ld. DR requested that the impugned revision-order be upheld. 5. We have considered the rival contentions of both sides and carefully perused the impugned revision-order as well as the material available on record to which our attention has been drawn. We proceed to examine the rival contentions of parties in subsequent discussions. 6. The first contention of the Ld. AR is that the original assessment-order dated 29.09.2021 itself was invalid since the Ld. AO completed the assessment without awaiting the DVO’s report despite having made a reference u/s 50C(2) of the Act. In support, reliance has been placed upon the decision in Rajni Arvind Birla (supra). However, we find that the assessment- order in the present case had subsequently undergone rectification as well as appellate proceedings. The assessee himself had sought rectification of the assessment-order on the basis of the DVO’s report. Further, the assessee had also pursued appellate remedies before the Ld. CIT(A) and the ITAT in respect of the addition made u/s 50C of the Act. Ultimately, the ITAT, vide order dated 12.10.2023, adjudicated the quantum dispute concerning valuation of the property u/s 50C of the Act on the basis of the DVO’s report and directed deletion of the remaining addition. The Ld. AO also passed the consequential “Order Giving Appeal Effect” to the ITAT’s order on 28.11.2023. Thus, the issue concerning valuation of the property u/s 50C of the Act has already attained finality in the aforesaid proceedings. We further notice that the assessee has not raised any specific ground in the present appeal challenging the validity of the original assessment-order. The present appeal arises from the revision-order passed u/s 263 of the Act and the controversy before us essentially concerns the validity and scope of the revisionary action initiated by the Ld. PCIT. In these peculiar circumstances, particularly having regard to the subsequent rectification and appellate proceedings wherein the dispute relating to valuation u/s 50C of the Act already stands adjudicated, we are not inclined to entertain this collateral challenge to the original assessment- order in the present proceedings. Accordingly, the first contention raised by the Ld. AR is rejected. 7. Now, we turn to the second contention of Ld. AR that the assessment- order had merged with the appellate order of the ITAT and, therefore, the Ld. PCIT was precluded from invoking revisionary jurisdiction u/s 263 of the Act. In this regard, the Ld. AR has placed reliance upon the decision of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in Sanvijay Rolling & Engg. Ltd. (supra), wherein the Hon’ble High Court, after considering the facts of that case, observed that the issues which had been specifically considered and decided by the appellate authorities could not thereafter be subjected to the revisionary jurisdiction u/s 263 of the Act. The relevant observations of the Hon’ble High Court read as under: “8. The facts on record indicate that the Assessing Officer in proceedings under section 143(3) of the said Act was pleased to disallow the amount of Rs. 63,500/- that was claimed towards recovery of amount written-off in the earlier year as well as amount of Rs. 6,20,334/- being towards receipt of commission. The Assessing Officer disallowed these deductions under section 80-IA of the said Act. This order was challenged by the assessee before the Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals) and on 31-1-2007 the aforesaid two deductions that were disallowed by the Assessing Officer came to be permitted. A finding was recorded that the deduction as claimed by the assessee under section 80-IA/80- IB was in accordance with law and was hence granted. This appellate order was challenged by the Revenue before the Tribunal. In the judgment dated 29- 10-2007 the Tribunal subsequently considered the aforesaid two heads of disallowances and proceeded to dismiss the appeal preferred by the Revenue on merits. It was thus clear from the aforesaid sequence of events that the order passed by the Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals) dated 31-1-2007 merged in the order passed by the Tribunal on 29-10-2007 and attained finality. In other words, the order of the Commissioner Income-tax (Appeals) having merged with the order of the Tribunal, there was no scope for the Commissioner of Income- tax to initiate proceedings under section 263 of the said Act. The legal position that on merger of the order passed by the Commissioner with that passed by the Tribunal, the jurisdiction under section 263(1)(c) of the said Act could not have been invoked is settled in view of judgment of this Court in Slum Rehabilitation Authority (supra) wherein the judgment of the Gujarat High Court in Nirma Chemicals Works (P.) Ltd. (supra) was followed. The reliance placed by the learned counsel for the Revenue on the decision in Shri Arbuda Mills Ltd. (supra) would not assist the case of the Revenue for the reason that the aspect of disallowances towards the amount written-off as well as receipts towards commission was specifically considered and decided against the Revenue by the Tribunal. The Honourable Supreme Court therein has held that the powers of the Commissioner under section 263 of the said Act would extend and shall be deemed always to have been extended to matters that have not been considered and decided in appeal filed by the assessee. The case in hand indicates that the items of disallowances were specifically considered by the Tribunal and decided against the Revenue. In that view of the matter the substantial question of law as framed is answered by holding that the Tribunal was justified in nullifying the exercise of Commissioner under section 263 of the said Act. In view of aforesaid ITL No. 37/2009 stands dismissed with no order as to costs.” 8. We have carefully considered the aforesaid decision and find that the legal position emerging therefrom is that the jurisdiction u/s 263 of the Act cannot be exercised in respect of matters which have already been considered and decided by the appellate authorities. In fact, this principle is also prescribed in Explanation 1(c) to section 263(1) of the Act which provides that the revisionary powers of the Commissioner shall extend to the matters which have not been considered and decided in appeal. The relevant provision of Explanation 1(c) is re-produced below for an immediate reference: “Explanation 1 – For the removal of doubts, it is hereby declared that, for the purposes of this sub-section, – (a) XXX (b) XXX (c) where any order referred to in this sub-section and passed by the Assessing Officer had been the subject matter of any appeal filed on or before or after the 1st day of June, 1988, the powers of the Principal Commissioner or] Commissioner under this sub-section shall extend and shall be deemed always to have extended to such matters as had not been considered and decided in such appeal.” The Hon’ble Supreme Court in CIT Vs. Shri Arbuda Mills Ltd. (1998) 231 ITR 50 (SC) has also held that the revisionary powers of the Commissioner extends to the matters which have not been considered and decided in appeal. 9. In present case before us, the earlier appellate proceedings before the Ld. CIT(A) and the ITAT were confined to the dispute concerning the adoption of full value of consideration for the purposes of section 50C of the Act. The ITAT examined the difference between the actual sale consideration and the DVO’s valuation and, applying the third proviso to section 50C(1) of the Act, directed deletion of the remaining addition of Rs. 11,28,000/-. However, the issue relating to the indexed cost of acquisition and indexed cost of improvement, which formed the basis of revisionary action initiated by Ld. PCIT, was neither the subject matter of adjudication before the Ld. CIT(A) nor considered and decided by the ITAT in its order dated 12.10.2023. Accordingly, the issue forming the basis of revisionary proceedings was an independent matter and remained outside the scope of earlier appellate adjudication. Consequently, in terms of Explanation 1(c) to section 263(1) of the Act, the doctrine of merger does not preclude the Ld. PCIT from exercising revisionary jurisdiction in respect of such an issue which was not considered and decided in the appellate proceedings. We, therefore, find that the decision in Sanvijay Rolling & Engg. Ltd. (supra) does not come to the rescue of the assessee in respect of the issue concerning the cost of acquisition and cost of improvement. Accordingly, the second contention raised by the Ld. AR based on the doctrine of merger is also rejected. 10. Further, in so far as Ground No. 1 is concerned, we find that the revisionary action of the Ld. PCIT is founded upon the failure of the Ld. AO to examine the assessee’s claims towards indexed cost of acquisition of Rs. 63,87,454/- and indexed cost of improvement of Rs. 5,41,197/- while computing the taxable capital gains. During the course of hearing before us, the Ld. AR has not brought to our notice any specific enquiry conducted by the Ld. AO or any verification made by him in respect of the aforesaid claims during the assessment proceedings. Nor has the Ld. AR demonstrated that these claims were duly supported by documentary material which had been examined and verified by the Ld. AO. In these circumstances, the finding of the Ld. PCIT regarding lack of enquiry remains unrebutted. We, therefore, find no infirmity in the exercise of revisionary jurisdiction u/s 263 of the Act on this issue. Ground No. 1 is accordingly dismissed. 11. However, we find some merit in Ground No. 2 concerning the scope of directions issued by the Ld. PCIT. In Para 9 of the impugned revision-order, the Ld. PCIT has directed the Ld. AO to pass a fresh assessment-order after taking into consideration the issues as may have been already considered together with the issue discussed in the revision-order. We notice that although the ITAT’s order dated 12.10.2023 and the consequential order giving effect thereto dated 28.11.2023 preceded the show-cause notice issued by the Ld. PCIT on 07.02.2024, the assessee did not bring these developments to the notice of the Ld. PCIT during revision proceedings. Had these developments been brought to the notice of the Ld. PCIT, perhaps the directions in the revision-order would have been appropriately framed. Nevertheless, we are bound to apply the statutory position of Explanation 1(c) to section 263(1) according to which the matters already considered and decided in appeal, fall outside the scope of revisionary jurisdiction. As discussed hereinabove, the issue concerning adoption of the full value of consideration u/s 50C of the Act had already been considered and decided by the ITAT in the earlier quantum appeal. The Ld. AO had also passed the consequential order giving effect to the ITAT’s directions, deleting the remaining addition of Rs. 11,28,000/-. Accordingly, such concluded issue cannot be reopened in the consequential proceedings arising out of the impugned revision-order. The revisionary jurisdiction exercised by the Ld. PCIT is confined to the independent issue concerning examination and verification of the indexed cost of acquisition and indexed cost of improvement claimed by the assessee. We, therefore, modify the directions issued by the Ld. PCIT and direct the Ld. AO to confine the consequential proceedings to examination and verification of the assessee’s claims towards indexed cost of acquisition and indexed cost of improvement, in accordance with law and after affording reasonable opportunity of being heard to the assessee. Ground No. 2 is accordingly partly allowed. 12. In view of foregoing discussion, the impugned revision-order passed by the Ld. PCIT u/s 263 of the Act is upheld, subject to the aforesaid modification and restriction of the consequential directions, as discussed in preceding para. 13. In result, this appeal is partly allowed.
