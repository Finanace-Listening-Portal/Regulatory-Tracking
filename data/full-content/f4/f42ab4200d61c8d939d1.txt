Ram Phal Bhardwaj (ICAI Disciplinary Committee) Every Accounting Error Is Not Professional Misconduct: Auditor Cleared of Presentation and Compliance Allegations Summary: The Disciplinary Committee of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI), in Shri Nirmal Kumar Bolia v. CA. Ram Phal Bhardwaj, DC/1981/2025, pronounced its decision on 9 September 2026, holding the respondent not guilty of professional or other misconduct concerning the statutory audit of M/s. Lovely Home CGHS Ltd. for FY 2020-21. The auditor had issued his report on 16 August 2021. Three allegations were examined under Item (7) of Part I of the Second Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949: allegedly incorrect accounting for an old lift replaced during the year, inconsistency between comparative figures in the balance sheet and supporting schedules, and certification concerning compliance with managing committee meeting requirements. On the lift-accounting allegation, the auditor explained that a new lift costing ₹13,20,205 had been recorded after adjusting ₹1,10,000 realised from disposal of the old lift, resulting in a net addition of ₹12,10,205. He produced audit working papers and subsequent audited financial statements demonstrating the treatment and disclosure of scrapped lifts. The Committee found no material misstatement or evidence of gross negligence. Regarding the difference of ₹3,86,551 between the comparative balance-sheet and schedule figures, the Committee accepted that the discrepancy arose from reclassification of an excess ground-rent payment and an Excel presentation error. The figures appearing on the face of the balance sheet agreed with the preceding year’s audited accounts, and corrected schedules had been communicated to the society on 5 September 2021. As regards managing committee meetings, the auditor established that fifteen meetings had been held during FY 2020-21 and their proceedings were recorded in the minutes book. The Committee accepted the explanation that no meetings were held during April 2020, July 2020 and January 2021 because of COVID-19 disruptions. It found no substantive evidence that the auditor’s certification was false or that he had failed to exercise due diligence. Considering the documentary evidence, subsequent corrections and absence of material misstatement, the Committee held the respondent not guilty on all three allegations. The proceedings were closed under Rule 19(2) of the Chartered Accountants (Procedure of Investigations of Professional and Other Misconduct and Conduct of Cases) Rules, 2007, without imposing any punishment. Background of the Complaint CA. Ram Phal Bhardwaj was the statutory auditor of M/s. Lovely Home CGHS Ltd. for FY 2020-21 and issued his audit report on 16 August 2021. The complaint raised several allegations concerning the society’s accounts and audit reporting. Three allegations proceeded for adjudication: failure to appropriately account for disposal of an old lift, inconsistency between comparative figures in the balance sheet and supporting schedules, and allegedly incorrect certification of compliance concerning managing committee meetings. The Director (Discipline) had formed a prima facie opinion against the auditor on two allegations. While considering that opinion, the Disciplinary Committee added the allegation concerning the lift. However, after examining the explanations and supporting documents, the Committee held the respondent not guilty on all three allegations and ordered closure of the case. Replacement of Lift and Treatment of Scrap Proceeds The complainant alleged that an old lift replaced during the year had not been deducted from the gross block, although the new lift had been added. It was further alleged that the accounts effectively recorded five lifts when only four existed physically. The auditor explained that expenditure on the new lift amounted to ₹13,20,205. After adjusting ₹1,10,000 received from disposal of the old scrapped lift, a net addition of ₹12,10,205 was shown in the fixed-asset schedule. He submitted that complete documentation concerning the addition and disposal was available in his audit working papers. He also stated that the error had been rectified in the subsequent financial year. The Committee called for and examined the audited financial statements for FY 2021-22. These contained a disclosure concerning scrapping of three old lifts and adjustment of their proportionate value, including the accounting treatment of scrap proceeds and the remaining value. Considering the working papers, accounting entries and subsequent disclosure, the Committee found no material misstatement affecting the financial statements and no evidence establishing gross negligence. The auditor was therefore cleared of this allegation. Mismatch Between Balance Sheet and Comparative Schedules The second allegation concerned the previous-year figures for Sundry Creditors and Provisions. The balance sheet showed ₹3,78,838, while Schedule B showed ₹7,65,389. The difference of ₹3,86,551 arose from reclassification of a debit balance representing excess ground rent paid. This amount was removed from the creditors schedule and presented under loans and advances for better presentation. Consequently, the comparative totals of both schedules increased by that amount. However, the corresponding figures on the face of the balance sheet were not updated, which the auditor attributed to a technical error in the Excel sheet. The Committee accepted that the inconsistency was confined to presentation and classification within the comparative schedules. The figures on the face of the balance sheet agreed with the audited figures of the preceding year. It also noted that the auditor had communicated the error to the society by a letter dated 5 September 2021, together with corrected schedules and reconciliation. This demonstrated transparency and prompt corrective action. Since the discrepancy did not affect the current-year financial statements, create a material misstatement or prejudice users, the Committee held the auditor not guilty on this charge. Managing Committee Meetings During the Pandemic The third allegation concerned the auditor’s reporting that an earlier objection relating to managing committee meetings had been complied with. The complainant maintained that the deficiency continued. The auditor explained that fifteen managing committee meetings had been held during FY 2020-21 and their proceedings were properly recorded in the minutes book. No meetings were held in April 2020, July 2020 and January 2021, which he attributed to disruption caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. The Committee noted that the auditor had verified the minutes book. It accepted the explanation for the absence of meetings in those months in light of the exceptional circumstances. It further observed that the complainant had produced no substantive evidence showing that the certification was false or that continuing deficiencies materially affected the society’s governance. The Committee therefore found that the auditor had exercised reasonable professional judgment and cleared him of this allegation as well. Final Decision The allegations were examined under Item (7) of Part I of the Second Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949, concerning failure to exercise due diligence or gross negligence in professional duties. After considering the submissions and documentary record, the Committee held the respondent not guilty of professional or other misconduct and closed the proceedings under Rule 19(2). No punishment was imposed. Author’s Comments An error deserves examination; it does not automatically justify a finding of professional misconduct. This order recognises the importance of assessing the nature of the discrepancy, its financial impact, the audit evidence and the corrective steps taken. The auditor’s defence succeeded because it was supported by working papers, reconciliations, contemporaneous communication and subsequent audited accounts. Merely describing a discrepancy as a typographical error would not provide the same evidentiary support. Equally, subsequent correction should not be read as an automatic cure for every audit failure. Here, the Committee considered correction alongside its findings that there was no material misstatement and no established failure of due diligence. The pandemic-related conclusion is also fact-specific. The order does not establish a general exemption from meeting requirements. It accepts the auditor’s judgment on the records and circumstances presented. For practitioners, the useful message is that documented professional judgment and transparent correction can distinguish an explainable error from disciplinary misconduct. Submissions of the Respondent and Findings of the Committee: 5.1 Allegation 2: – In respect of instant allegation, it is alleged that the old lift, which is being replaced, had neither been deducted from the Gross Block (though the addition of the new lift is shown) nor was there any mention of its disposal under ‘Comments on Asset Side Items’ in PART B of the Audit Report. The Complainant also alleged that five lifts (4 original + 1 addition) are recorded in the books, whereas only four lifts exist physically. The Committee noted that the Respondent during the course of hearing and through his written submissions had inter-alia stated as under: 5.1.1 The Respondent, in his defence, contended that the total additions to the New Lift account as per the books of accounts amounted to Rs. 13,20,205. After deducting the sale proceeds of the old & scrapped lift amounting to Rs. 1,10,000, a net addition of Rs. 12,10,205 was reflected in Schedule D (Schedule of Fixed Assets) as on 31.03.2021. Accordingly, there were only four lifts as per both the books of accounts and physical verification. He further submitted that there was no intention on his part to misstate, suppress, or conceal any information. Complete documentation relating to the addition and disposal was available in the audit working papers, clearly indicating the net addition of Rs. 12,10,205 after adjusting the scrap sale from the gross addition. The net impact on the fixed asset block was transparently disclosed in the schedule, and the necessary accounting entries were duly recorded in the books of accounts maintained by the society. He also submitted that the said error was rectified in the subsequent financial year. 5.1.2 During the hearing held on 27th March 2026, the Committee directed the Counsel of the Respondent to provide the audited financial statements for the FY 2021-22 of the Society along with detailed submissions in respect of instant charge. In response, the Respondent submitted the audited financial statements for FY 2021-22 of the Society vide email dated 03rd April 2026. The Committee perused the Audit Report for FY 2021-22 and observed that under the head “Comments on Assets Side Item,” the Respondent had stated as follows: COMMENTS ON ASSET SIDE ITEMS: 3. LAND & BUILDING Rs.145,965,272.92: There is a change of Rs. 25,44,780.00 in balance as on 31-03-2022 under the head of account as compared to balance as on 31-03-2021 (Annexure “E” attached). Three old lifts have been scrapped till 31.03.2022 and proportionate value of Rs.25,44,780/- has been adjusted out of which scrap of old lifts sold during the year amounts to Rs. 2,20,000/- and the remaining value of Rs 23,24,780/- has been adjusted through Income and Expenditure Appropriation account. Accordingly, upon perusal of the audited financial statements for FY 2021-22, as submitted by the Respondent, the Committee noted that an appropriate disclosure regarding scrapping of lifts and adjustment of their value has been made in next financial year under “Comments on Asset Side Items.” This indicates that the matter was duly considered and appropriately disclosed in the subsequent year. 5.1.3 The Committee also observed that the Respondent maintained complete documentation in the audit working papers evidencing the addition and disposal of the lift, including the adjustment of scrap sale proceeds. The accounting treatment adopted reflects that the net impact was duly recorded in the books of accounts, and there was no material misstatement affecting the financial statements. Accordingly, the Committee observed that based on accounting treatment made in the subsequent year, the Respondent had no intention to misstate, suppress, or conceal any material fact and there is no evidence on record to establish any mala fide intent or gross negligence on the part of the Respondent. Accordingly, the Committee decided to hold the Respondent NOT GUILTY of any Professional or Other Misconduct in respect of instant allegation. 5.2 Allegation 3: – Under this allegation, the complainant had alleged that in the previous year’s column of the Balance Sheet, Sundry Creditors & Provisions were shown as Rs. 3,78,838/- but the same as per Schedule B appearing at Rs. 7,65,389/-. The Committee noted that the Respondent during the course of hearing and through his written submissions had inter-alia stated as under: 5.2.1 The Respondent, in his defence, submitted that, for better presentation, the negative figure of Rs. 3,86,551 was removed from Schedule B and disclosed under Schedule “G” as Item No. 9, “Excess paid to DDA against ground rent” in the schedules forming part of the Balance Sheet as at 31.03.2021. Consequently, the totals of both schedules in the previous year column increased by Rs. 3,86,551. Accordingly, the total of Schedule B became Rs. 7,65,389 (Rs. 3,78,838 + Rs. 3,86,551), and the total of Schedule G became Rs. 11,51,879 (Rs. 7,65,328 + Rs. 3,86,551). However, due to a technical error in the Excel sheet, the corresponding figures on the face of the Balance Sheet were not updated. He further submitted that the discrepancy between the previous year figures in Schedule B (List of Sundry Creditors & Provisions) and the main Balance Sheet arose from a bona fide typographical error, wherein the debit balance of Rs. 3,86,551 relating to “Excess paid to DDA” was inadvertently reflected in Schedule G (Loans and Advances) instead of Schedule B. The Respondent stated that this error was duly acknowledged and communicated to the Society through a letter dated 05.09.2021, along with corrected schedules. He emphasized that the error did not impact the current year’s financial reporting, nor did it mislead stakeholders. He also submitted that the figures presented on the face of the Balance Sheet were correct and in agreement with the Balance Sheet as at 31.03.2020, and that the discrepancy was limited to presentation within internal schedules. He asserted that there was no intent to misstate or suppress material facts, no negligence on his part, and no prejudice caused to users of the financial statements. The correction, according to him, was made in good faith and in accordance with the responsibilities of a statutory auditor and did not affect the integrity of the audit. 5.2.2 The Committee, after examining the documents placed on record by the Respondent, observed that the negative balance of Rs. 3,86,551, pertaining to “Excess paid to DDA against ground rent,” was reclassified from Schedule B and disclosed under Schedule G for better presentation. As a consequence, the totals of both Schedule B and Schedule G were increased by Rs. 3,86,551 in the previous year column. However, the corresponding figures on the face of the Balance Sheet were not updated. The Committee further observed that the Respondent had explained that the same arose due to a bona fide typographical and presentation error, without any impact on the correctness of the financial statements. The Committee also noted that the figures on the face of the Balance Sheet were consistent with the audited figures of the previous year, and the inconsistency was confined to the internal presentation within schedules. 5.2.3 The Committee noted that the Respondent had duly identified and communicated the error to the Society vide letter dated 05.09.2021, along with corrected schedules and reconciliation. This demonstrates that the Respondent acted in a transparent manner and took prompt corrective action upon identification of the error. The Committee opined that the discrepancy did not affect the current year’s financial statements, nor did it result in any material misstatement or prejudice to the users of the financial statements. The error was limited to presentation and classification within schedules and did not impact the true and fair view of the financial position of the Society. Accordingly, the Committee decided to hold the Respondent NOT GUILTY of any Professional or Other Misconduct in respect of instant allegation. 5.3 Allegation 4: – The Complainant has alleged that there was discrepancy in relation to number of meetings of managing committee held during the year, however, the Respondent in his audit report for the financial year 2020-21 has certified them as “complied with”. In response to the same, the Respondent submitted that during the year under audit (FY 2020-21), total 15 managing committee meetings were held, and the proceedings of Managing Committee Meetings were found properly recorded in the Minutes Books. However, no meeting was conducted in the month of April 2020, July 2020 and January 2021 due to outbreak of Covid 19 pandemic. 5.3.1 The Committee observed that the Respondent, in the course of audit, had verified the Minutes Book and found that the meetings conducted during the year were duly recorded. The absence of meetings in certain months has been reasonably explained in light of the prevailing pandemic situation, which disrupted normal functioning of institutions across the country. The Committee further noted that the Complainant had not brought on record any substantive evidence to demonstrate that the Respondent’s certification was false or that the deficiencies continued in a manner that would materially affect the functioning or governance of Society. Mere absence of meetings in certain months, particularly under exceptional circumstances, does not by itself establish non-compliance, especially when an adequate number of meetings were held during the year. 5.3.2 The Committee opined that the Respondent had exercised reasonable professional judgment in relying upon the records produced before him, including the Minutes Book, and there is no material on record to suggest that such reliance was misplaced or that the Respondent failed to exercise due diligence. Accordingly, the Committee decided to hold the Respondent NOT GUILTY of any Professional or Other Misconduct in respect of instant allegation. 6. CONCLUSION In view of the above observations, considering the oral and written submissions of the parties, material on record and deliberations, the Committee decided to hold the Respondent NOT GUILTY of any Professional or Other Misconduct. Accordingly, in terms of Rule 19(2) of the Chartered Accountants (Procedure of Investigations of Professional and Other Misconduct and Conduct of Cases) Rules, 2007, the Committee passes an Order for closure of this case against the Respondent.
