Mahesh Gajendraprasad Vyas (ICAI Disciplinary Committee) Non-Submission of Working Papers Does Not, by Itself, Prove Issuance of False Certificates Summary: The ICAI Disciplinary Committee, Bench II, held CA. Mahesh Gajendraprasad Vyas not guilty of professional and other misconduct arising from circulation and performance certificates issued for Buniyad, a daily newspaper published in Patan, Gujarat. The complainant alleged that the respondent had fabricated documents and certified inflated newspaper circulation figures without examining the underlying records, enabling unlawful gains to the publisher. He also raised questions concerning the publisher’s tax audit reporting and GST compliance. However, the Committee found that no specific circulation entry, figure or statement had been demonstrated to be false. The Director (Discipline) initially found the charges unsubstantiated, although the respondent had failed to submit a written defence or produce the requested working papers. The Board of Discipline disagreed, treating the failure to respond as a serious omission warranting further inquiry. The matter was consequently referred to the Disciplinary Committee. Background The complaint concerned circulation and performance certificates issued by CA. Mahesh Gajendraprasad Vyas for “Buniyad”, a daily newspaper published in Patan, Gujarat. The complainant alleged that the respondent had created false documents and certified inflated circulation figures without examining the underlying records, thereby facilitating unlawful gains to the publisher. He also questioned the publisher’s tax compliance, alleging that circulation of approximately 78,000 copies daily was not supported by corresponding disclosures in GST records. Further allegations concerned non-payment of GST and non-filing of tax audit reports despite substantial sales. The disciplinary inquiry concerned whether the respondent had issued false circulation certificates, failed to exercise due diligence or acted in a manner bringing disrepute to the profession. Director Initially Found the Allegations Unsubstantiated The Director (Discipline) examined the certificates and the circulation-verification guidelines issued by the Registrar of Newspapers for India (RNI). The certificates contained particulars relating to copies printed, sold, distributed free and remaining unsold. However, the complainant did not establish how any specific figure or statement was false. No adverse report from the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, RNI or another competent agency was produced to support the allegation of fabrication. The Director nevertheless criticised the respondent for neither filing a written defence nor producing the working papers sought during the inquiry. Despite that omission, the absence of evidence substantiating the underlying allegations led to a prima facie finding of not guilty. Board Viewed Failure to Respond Seriously The Board of Discipline disagreed with the Director’s conclusion. It noted that the respondent had not submitted his written statement or the additional documents sought, despite reminders. In the Board’s view, failure to counter the allegations without reasonable cause could be treated adversely and, in the circumstances, appeared to amount to an admission. The matter was therefore referred to the Disciplinary Committee for a full inquiry. The procedural history was significant: the final proceedings had to determine whether non-participation and missing working papers could substitute for substantive proof of false certification. No Specific Inaccuracy Was Established The Committee found that the certificates contained the particulars ordinarily required for circulation verification. Nothing on record demonstrated that they were inconsistent with the prescribed formats or verification requirements. During the final hearing, the respondent explained that he was not the publisher’s statutory auditor when he issued the certificates. He stated that he had verified relevant particulars with reference to previous records and reports made available to him and was appointed auditor subsequently. The Committee noted that the complainant had not identified a specific entry, circulation figure or statement proved to be incorrect. Nor was evidence produced showing that the respondent knowingly certified false information or ignored material facts. It emphasised that suspicion or apprehension cannot, by themselves, constitute proof of misconduct. Publisher’s Alleged Tax Defaults Did Not Prove False Certification The Committee separately addressed the allegations concerning the publisher’s GST compliance and tax audit reporting. It observed that, even assuming those defaults were correct, they would not automatically establish that the circulation certificates were false. A connection had to be demonstrated between the alleged defaults and a professional failure by the respondent. No such nexus was established on the evidence. Accordingly, allegations against the publisher could not, without supporting material, establish misconduct by the certifying Chartered Accountant. Missing Written Defence and Working Papers Were Not Conclusive The Committee expressly recorded that the respondent had not filed a written statement or furnished the requested working papers. However, he appeared at the final hearing on 5 June 2026 and provided oral explanations concerning the certificates. The Committee held that non-submission of a written statement or working papers, though undesirable, could not by itself justify a finding of guilt in the absence of substantive evidence establishing the charge. It also noted that no competent authority’s rejection, adverse finding or questioning of the certificates had been produced. Taken together, the evidence did not establish false certification, lack of due diligence or gross negligence. Decision The respondent was held not guilty of professional and other misconduct under Clause (7) of Part I of the Second Schedule and Clause (2) of Part IV of the First Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949. The proceedings were closed under Rule 19(2). Author’s Comments The decision draws an important distinction between failure to defend an allegation adequately and proof that the allegation is true. A deficient response does not automatically establish that a certificate was fabricated. However, the finding should not encourage professionals to disregard disciplinary communications or requests for working papers. The Committee itself described the omissions as undesirable, and the respondent’s conduct contributed to the matter proceeding to a full inquiry. Equally, an adverse finding from another regulator is supporting evidence, rather than an indispensable prerequisite in every disciplinary case. False certification or inadequate verification may be established independently through reliable records. The practical lesson is to retain the basis of certification, document verification procedures and respond fully to disciplinary notices. Here, exoneration followed because the substantive charge remained unproved; it was not an endorsement of inadequate documentation or non-cooperation. Mahesh Gajendraprasad Vyas (Through VC) 1. Background of the Case: Newspaper named “Buniyad” is published in Patan, Gujarat. The Complainant stated that the Respondent created fake documents and issued the certificates regarding circulation of the aforesaid newspaper to publisher of Buniyad daily newspaper for the unlawful gain to the publisher. The Complainant further submitted that the Respondent issued the said certificates without checking any documents. 1.1. The Complainant has further apprehended that the publisher has not submitted detail of purchase to GST Department in monthly, quarterly, yearly returns from 30th June,2017 to till date and they have not paid any GST tax for it. The circulation shown by Buniyad newspaper for 78000 copies daily is not shown in the GST office as per HSN code. Further as per Complainant the Buniyad newspaper had more than 2 crores sales despite that it has not filed tax audit report to GST department and Income tax department. 2. Charges in brief: 2.1. The Respondent has issued false form of performance certificate regarding circulation of Buniyad newspaper for financial year 2017-18 dated 25th June,2017 and for year 2018-19 dated 01st May,2018 and certificate of circulation for year 2017-18 dated 23rd September,2018. 3. The relevant issues discussed in the Prima Facie Opinion dated 17th December 2020 formulated by the Director (Discipline) in the matter in brief, are given below: 3.1. On perusal of the papers submitted by the Complainant it was noted that the Respondent had certified the Form of performance particulars certificate which had average details of newspaper printed, sold, free distribution and unsold details per day. 3.2. It was further noted that the Complainant had attached the Guidelines for Circulation Check/Verification by the team of the Registrar of Newspapers for India (RNI), along with other enclosures issued by the Ministry of Information & Broadcasting. The same had been issued prescribing the procedure for circulation verification of printing premises. It was noted that Report Performa had been enclosed for RNI team/Chartered Accountants doing circulation verification and as per same, the necessary details as required were mentioned. In the Forms of performance also particulars regarding circulation for the year 2016-17 and 2017-18 certified by the Respondent all the details were mentioned. 3.3. In this case, it was noted that the Complainant had failed to bring on record any adverse report issued by the Ministry of Information & Broadcasting or any other agency which showed that the Respondent had issued fake or incorrect certificates or had been involved in preparing fake documents. He also could not explain how these documents were fake or incorrect. 3.4. It was also noted that the Respondent had neither submitted his defence by way of a written statement nor produced any working papers which were asked for at the stage of Rule 8(5), which was condemnable on his part. It would have been appropriate if the Respondent had come forward with his defence against the charges alleged by the Complainant in the instant complaint. However, since the Complainant could neither explain how the certificates issued by the Respondent were fake or incorrect, as claimed, nor provide any documentary evidence to substantiate the allegations, it was felt that, despite the Respondent’s non-submission of a written statement, the benefit of doubt ought to have been extended to him. 3.5. The Director (Discipline) in his Prima Facie Opinion dated 17th December 2020 opined that the Respondent was Prima Facie Not Guilty of Professional and Other Misconduct falling within the meaning of Clause (7) of Part I of Second Schedule and Clause (2) of Part IV of First Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949. The said Clause of the Schedule to the Act, states as under: Clause (7) of Part I of the Second Schedule: “A Chartered Accountant in practice shall be deemed to be guilty of professional misconduct if he: X X X X X X (7) does not exercise due diligence or is grossly negligent in the conduct of his professional duties.” Clause (2) of Part IV of the First Schedule: “A member of the Institute, whether in practice or not shall be deemed to be guilty of other misconduct, if he- X X X X X X (2) in the opinion of the Council, brings disrepute to the profession or the Institute as a result of his action whether or not related to his professional work.” 3.6. The Prima Facie Opinion formed by the Director (Discipline) was considered by Board of Discipline in its meeting held on 29th June 2022. The Board noted that it had been alleged in the complaint that the Respondent had issued the Performance certificate regarding circulation of Buniyad newspaper for the F.Y. 2016-17 and 2017-18 dated 25th June 2017 and 1st May 2018 respectively and Certificate of circulation for the F.Y. 2016-17 dated 23rd September 2018 without verification of the underlying documents. However, the Respondent neither submitted his defence on the charges alleged against him in the form of his written statement nor the additional documents sought from him despite reminders. It was expected from the Respondent to counter the allegations alleged him which had not been done by him without reasonable cause which in a way amounted to admission of misconduct on his part. Thus, the Board did not concur with the reasons given against the charge(s) and Board did not agree with the Prima Facie Opinion of the Director (Discipline) that the Respondent is NOT GUILTY of Professional Misconduct/Other Misconduct falling within the meaning of Item (7) of Part I of the Second Schedule and item (2) of Part IV of the First Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949 read with Section 22 of the said Act and accordingly decided to refer the case to the Disciplinary Committee to proceed under Chapter V of the Chartered Accountants (Procedure of Investigations of Professional and Other Misconduct and Conduct of Cases) Rules, 2007 in terms of the provisions of Rule 9(3)(b) of the aforesaid Rules. 4. Findings of the Committee: – 6.1 The Committee noted that the charge against the Respondent (as mentioned in para 2.1 above) is that he had issued false form of performance certificate regarding circulation of Buniyad newspaper for financial year 2017-18 (SIC. 2016-17) dated 25th June,2017 and for year 2018-19 (SIC 2017-18) dated 01st May,2018 and certificate of circulation for year 2017-18 (SIC 2016-17) dated 23rd September,2018. 6.2 The Committee observed that as per the allegation, the circulation figures certified by the Respondent were incorrect and that the publisher of the newspaper had not disclosed corresponding sales in GST and Income Tax records. The Committee further noted that the allegations were primarily founded on the Complainant’s doubts regarding the genuineness of the circulation figures and the financial reporting of the publisher. 6.3 On perusal of the documents placed on record, the Committee noted that the Respondent had certified Forms of Performance Particulars and circulation-related certificates containing details of newspapers printed and sold. The Committee further noted that the Complainant had also placed on record guidelines issued by the Registrar of Newspapers for India (RNI) relating to circulation verification. 6.4 The Committee observed that the said guidelines prescribe the procedure to be followed during circulation verification and contain formats for recording relevant information. The Committee noted that the certificates issued by the Respondent contained the particulars ordinarily required for circulation verification and nothing was brought on record to demonstrate that the certificates were inconsistent with the prescribed formats or verification requirements. 6.5 During the hearing 05th June 2026, the Respondent submitted that at the time of issuance of the circulation/performance certificates, he was not acting as the statutory auditor of the publisher. He stated that he had initially issued the certificates after verifying the relevant details with reference to the previous records/reports made available to him and that he was appointed as the auditor subsequently. 6.6 The Committee further noted that although serious allegations had been levelled against the Respondent, no documentary evidence was produced by the Complainant to establish that the circulation figures certified by the Respondent were false. The Committee observed that mere suspicion or apprehension cannot by themselves constitute proof of misconduct. The Committee noted that the Complainant had not pointed out any specific entry, figure, or statement in the certificates issued by the Respondent that was incorrect. Further, no evidence was brought on record to show that the Respondent had knowingly certified wrong information or had ignored any material facts while issuing the certificates. 6.7 The Committee further observed that the allegation regarding non-payment of GST by the publisher and non-filing of tax audit reports, even if assumed to be correct, would not by itself establish that the circulation certificates issued by the Respondent were false. No nexus was established between the alleged defaults of the publisher and any professional misconduct on the part of the Respondent. 6.8 The Committee noted that the Respondent had not filed any written statement or written submissions and had also not furnished the working papers sought from him. However, the Respondent appeared before the Committee during the hearing held on 05th June 2026 and made oral submissions, wherein he provided clarifications regarding the issuance of the circulation/performance certificates. The Committee took note of the explanations furnished by the Respondent during the course of the hearing. However, the Committee is of the view that non-submission of a written statement or working papers, though undesirable, cannot by itself lead to a finding of guilt in the absence of substantive evidence establishing the charge. 6.9 The Committee also noted that, despite opportunities available during the proceedings, no material was brought on record to show that the Ministry of Information & Broadcasting, the Registrar of Newspapers for India (RNI), or any other competent authority had rejected, questioned, or disbelieved the circulation certificates issued by the Respondent. No adverse report or finding of any such authority was produced to substantiate the allegation that the Respondent had issued false or incorrect certificates or was involved in the preparation of fabricated documents. 6.10 The Committee noted that the allegations against the Respondent were not supported by any cogent evidence. No specific inaccuracy in the certificates was established, nor was any material produced to show lack of due diligence or gross negligence. Accordingly, the charges remained unsubstantiated, and no professional misconduct was proved against the Respondent. The Committee was therefore of the opinion that the Respondent was NOT GUILTY of Professional and Other Misconduct falling within the meaning of Clause (7) of Part I of Second Schedule and Clause (2) of Part IV of First Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949. 7.
