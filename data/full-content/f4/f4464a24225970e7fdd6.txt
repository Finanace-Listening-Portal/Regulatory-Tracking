Axis Mutual Fund is the mutual fund, Axis Asset Management Company Limited is the asset manager and Axis Mutual Fund Trustee Limited is the trustee identified in the scheme documents. The scheme’s allotment date is 18 February 2011. The current scheme page and statutory KIM establish that the product remains available; this is not a historical or merged scheme entry. The investment objective is to seek long-term capital appreciation through equity and equity-related instruments of mid-cap companies. The objective is an intention, not an assurance. The benchmark shown on the current page is BSE Midcap 150 TRI, and both scheme and benchmark riskometers are displayed as Very High. A total-return index includes reinvested distributions and is therefore more appropriate than a price-only index for benchmark comparison, but a benchmark does not guarantee that the portfolio will track or outperform it. Mid-cap status is based on SEBI’s market-cap classification, so a company can move between large-, mid- and small-cap lists as rankings change. Portfolio rebalancing, inflows, redemptions, derivatives and cash can cause the observed allocation to vary. A factsheet is a dated snapshot; it should not be represented as a permanent portfolio. Why mid-cap exposure can behave differently Mid-cap companies can have narrower trading liquidity, higher impact cost and greater sensitivity to financing, demand and execution shocks than established large-cap issuers. An actively managed fund can diversify issuer risk but cannot remove category-wide drawdowns. Redemption pressure may require the manager to sell into weak markets, while concentrated sector views or valuation discipline can cause returns to differ significantly from the benchmark and peers. Regular, Direct, Growth and IDCW Are Not Interchangeable The Direct Plan excludes distributor commission and therefore must have a lower expense ratio than the corresponding Regular Plan, but it does not provide advice or portfolio planning. The Regular Plan may involve distributor services and a higher recurring cost. Both plans hold the same underlying scheme portfolio; differences in expenses lead to different NAVs and returns over time. An investor should compare the exact plan name on the application, statement and bank mandate. Growth retains distributable surplus within the scheme subject to portfolio results. IDCW permits the trustee to declare a distribution from available distributable surplus; neither the amount nor frequency is assured. IDCW reduces the NAV to the extent of the payout and applicable adjustments and is not equivalent to interest. Reinvestment creates additional units and record-keeping entries rather than a guaranteed compounding rate. AUM can change with subscriptions, redemptions and market values, so it is not a quality score or a measure of guaranteed liquidity. Current TER, AUM, portfolio and riskometer should always be read with their as-of dates. Combining a current NAV with an older portfolio and presenting all figures as one-day data would be misleading. Use the exact plan and option when reading NAV or TER. Record the as-of date for every quantitative fact. Do not subtract TER again from published NAV-based returns; it is already reflected in NAV. Treat AUM as scale information, not as a recommendation or safety rating. Check statutory disclosures for addenda that may amend an older SID or KIM. Exit Load: The Current Page and the Older KIM Do Not Match The current Axis scheme page states that, for redemption or switch-out within three months from allotment, 10 percent of the investment is exempt and the remaining amount bears a 1 percent exit load. After three months, the page states that no exit load applies. This current operational disclosure differs from the KIM dated 28 November 2025, which still shows a twelve-month period. The difference demonstrates why a dated KIM should not be read in isolation. Load structures can be changed prospectively through an addendum, and the applicable rule can depend on the allotment date and the effective date of the change. An investor should verify the prevailing load page or addendum and the transaction-specific statement before redeeming. This article uses the current public scheme page for the 12 November 2026 snapshot while preserving the older KIM as evidence of the scheme’s mandate. Indian Tax Treatment for Equity-Oriented Fund Units in 2026 Axis Midcap Fund is designed as an equity-oriented scheme because its mandate requires at least 65 percent in mid-cap equity and equity-related instruments. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, units of an equity-oriented fund held for more than 12 months are long-term; units held for 12 months or less are short-term. The current special-rate table places qualifying short-term gains under section 196 at 20 percent, subject to STT and other statutory conditions. Qualifying long-term gains on equity-oriented fund units fall under section 198 at 12.5 percent on the aggregate covered long-term gain exceeding Rs 1.25 lakh for the tax year, again subject to the statutory conditions. Surcharge and health and education cess can apply. The exemption threshold is aggregate, not a fresh amount for each scheme or folio. Grandfathering and cost rules can matter for older units. Risks the Scheme Name Does Not Remove The Very High riskometer is consistent with substantial equity exposure. Mid-cap shares can experience sharp valuation changes and thinner liquidity. The fund also carries active-management, concentration, derivatives, counterparty, operational and regulatory risks. Debt and money-market holdings can introduce credit, duration and reinvestment risk, while overseas securities, if used, add currency and foreign-market risk. A long operating history, large AUM or experienced manager does not guarantee preservation of capital. Fund-manager assignments can change, and a portfolio can underperform its benchmark or category for extended periods. The appropriate plan, option and allocation depend on the investor’s horizon, cash-flow needs, other holdings, tax position and ability to tolerate loss. What minimum mid-cap allocation does the scheme document require? The KIM sets a 65 percent minimum and 100 percent maximum for equity and equity-related instruments of mid-cap companies under normal circumstances. 3. Why do the current page and older KIM show different exit-load periods? The current page shows three months, while the November 2025 KIM shows twelve months. What is the current exit-load wording on the scheme page? Within three months, 10 percent of the investment is shown as load-free and the remaining amount bears 1 percent; after three months, the displayed load is nil. No. They are plans of the same scheme and share the portfolio and mandate. The Direct Plan excludes distributor commission and therefore has a lower expense ratio and a different NAV path. 6. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, qualifying short-term gains are generally taxed at 20 percent under section 196, while qualifying long-term gains are taxed at 12.5 percent above the aggregate Rs 1.25 lakh threshold under section 198, plus applicable surcharge and cess. 8. Can a switch avoid exit load or capital-gains tax? Not automatically. A switch-out is generally a redemption of the outgoing units, so the prevailing exit load, holding period and tax result must be checked for those units.
