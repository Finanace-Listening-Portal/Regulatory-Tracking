In the case of a bullet-repayment plan, the full principal and interest fall due together at maturity. “Many borrowers fail to come up with the larger sum all at once,” says Shetty. RBI’s rule changes Prior to April 1, 2026, borrowers could simply roll over a bullet loan. From that date, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) capped the tenure of consumption-oriented bullet-repayment gold loans at 12 months. It also made it compulsory to clear the accruedinterest before the loan could be renewed. “If borrowers cannot arrange the interest payment, renewal becomes difficult,” says Raoul Kapoor, co-CEO, Andromeda Sales and Distribution. The pledged gold could be auctioned by the lender. The changed rules are meant to encourage greater repayment discipline, but are spelling trouble for some. “They could create short-term cash-flow pressures, particularly for small business owners and borrowers with irregular incomes or multiple existing debts,” says Kapoor. Expect greater scrutiny Experts believe that if the stress in this segment worsens, lenders could tighten their loan sanction norms. Scrutiny of overall debt, overdue accounts, income stability and repayment capacity may intensify. “Factors such as a borrower’s Cibil score, credit information report, and other bureau-based insights can provide valuable signals regarding credit behaviour and repayment capacity,” says Bhavesh Jain, managing director (MD) and CEO, TransUnion Cibil. Renewals and top-ups may require fresh credit appraisals. Highly indebted or delinquent borrowers may find it harder to obtain credit. “They may receive smaller loan amounts and shorter tenures,” says Shetty. Little is expected to change for borrowers with clean repayment records. Read the warning signs Watch out for signs that point towards an impending debt trap. “Taking fresh loans or using credit cards to meet existing EMIs or outstanding dues, or to cover routine expenses, are warning signs,” says Harsh Grover, co-founder, LoansJagat. Repeatedly paying only the minimum due on credit cards, missing EMI deadlines or allowing bills to become overdue are other indicators of repayment stress. “Using a large part of the monthly income to service debt, leaving little room for essential expenses or emergencies, is another indicator,” says Singh. A repayment plan that depends on a bonus or uncertain future income is another warning sign, as is delaying one lender’s payment to pay another. Repeatedly renewing short-term loans, extending repayment periods or pledging more assets to keep up with existing repayments also signals stress. Devise a repayment plan Start with a complete picture of what you owe. “Borrowers should list all outstanding loans, interest rates, EMIs and due dates to understand their total debt burden,” says Grover. Account for essential household expenses and required instalments on all loans first. Cut non-essential spending. “Extra funds should be directed towards reducing high-interest debt such as credit card dues,” says Grover. Pay close attention to secured loans where non-payment could cost you an asset. “Borrowers with a gold loan approaching maturity should establish a realistic repayment or settlement plan before the due date,” says Singh. Borrowers may also consider the debt consolidation option. “Consolidation may help only if the revised borrowing lowers the overall cost and provides an affordable repayment schedule,” says Singh.
