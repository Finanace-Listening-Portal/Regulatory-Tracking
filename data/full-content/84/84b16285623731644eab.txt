Mumbai's weather has changed over the past week, with easing humidity making the city considerably more pleasant. As I went through the second-quarter earnings, I sensed a similar change in the weather for India Inc's profits.,A look at the latest corporate results reveals a decisive shift. India Inc's profits rose 32% in the September quarter, according to Motilal Oswal's assessment of its coverage universe. The growth was significantly ahead of the brokerage's estimate of 21%. Sales grew 25%, against an expected 19%, while EBITDA rose 23%, compared with an estimated 17%. Advt The results show that India Inc's earnings recovery is gathering pace, offering a more favourable backdrop for corporate investment and, potentially, a fresh phase of credit demand.For banks, the emerging opportunity comes with a funding challenge: credit is expanding faster than deposits, while money market indicators point to higher marginal costs of raising funds.The divergence between credit and deposit growth is becoming more pronounced. Bank credit grew 19.5% year on year to Rs 231.8 lakh crore in the fortnight ended October 31, compared with deposit growth of 16.9% to Rs 282.5 lakh crore. The gap widened to 268 basis points from 151 basis points in the previous fortnight. The overall credit-deposit ratio rose to 82.1% from 81.6%.Credit increased by Rs 4.40 lakh crore during the fortnight, while deposits rose by Rs 3.75 lakh crore. The latest increase in credit more than reversed the decline recorded in the preceding fortnight. Across the two fortnights since September 30, credit was up Rs 1.88 lakh crore, while deposits remained Rs 1.45 lakh crore below their September-end level.The pressure on marginal funding costs is also visible in money markets. The weighted average call rate rose 13 basis points to 5.69% in the week ended October 30, placing it 19 basis points above the 5.50% repo rate. Banks issued Rs 52,000 crore of certificates of deposit during the fortnight, against Rs 46,000 crore in the previous one. The highest issuance rate climbed to 8.04% from 6.92%. These indicators point to a higher cost of raising short-term funds, although they do not represent the average cost of funds across the banking system. Nifty companies recorded 27% profit growth, their strongest expansion in 17 quarters, while the earnings upgrade-to-downgrade ratio stood at 1.1 times. Large companies reported 31% profit growth, mid-caps 22% and small-caps 32%, excluding oil marketing companies.The outlook, however, is not uniformly strong. Motilal Oswal expects earnings growth for its coverage universe to moderate to 11% in the second half of FY27 from 22% in the first half. Excluding oil marketing companies, growth is expected to be 20%. For banks, the quality and composition of credit demand will therefore matter as much as the headline growth in lending. Advt A sustained recovery in corporate investment would create opportunities for lenders across working capital, project finance and term lending. Government spending on infrastructure has supported demand, but private investment will be important to broaden the cycle and create additional demand for credit. Manufacturing, electronics, semiconductors, renewable energy, defence, electric vehicles, data centres and healthcare are among the sectors where capacity expansion could generate financing requirements. Production-linked incentive schemes are also intended to encourage investment in domestic manufacturing. Advt There are already signs of activity in some sectors. Cable and wire companies reported revenue growth of 47%, while cement volumes rose 8%. UltraTech Cement is evaluating expansion beyond its planned capacity of 240 million tonnes per annum by FY28. Automobile manufacturers and hospital operators are also expanding capacity. These developments suggest that investment plans are taking shape in parts of the economy, although they do not yet establish a broad-based private capex revival.The funding challenge for banksThe widening gap between credit and deposit growth does not, by itself, mean that banks are running out of funds or are unable to lend. The overall credit-deposit ratio remains a measure of the relationship between outstanding loans and deposits, not a direct measure of lending capacity. Nevertheless, the divergence makes deposit mobilisation and the management of incremental funding increasingly important.Banks can draw on several sources, including deposits, certificates of deposit and other market borrowings. But the cost and stability of these sources differ. A rise in marginal borrowing rates can put pressure on funding costs, particularly if banks have to rely more heavily on wholesale funding to support loan growth. The eventual impact on net interest margins will depend on how quickly deposit rates and borrowing costs adjust, the repricing of loans and the extent to which lenders can pass on higher costs to borrowers.The investment opportunity also comes with execution risks. Higher costs of capital can affect the viability of projects, while volatility in steel, rubber, copper and other inputs can squeeze corporate margins. Automobile manufacturers have passed on some cost increases to customers, while cement companies continue to face pressure on margins. Consumer durables companies, too, must contend with input costs and demand conditions.For banks, project finance carries a different set of risks from the working capital and retail lending that have supported credit growth. Land acquisition, regulatory approvals, infrastructure readiness, skilled labour shortages and delays in commissioning can push up project costs and postpone cash flows. Capacity additions made in anticipation of demand can also create repayment pressure if utilisation takes longer than expected. Lenders will need to assess project viability and promoter execution capabilities carefully rather than rely on the broader optimism around the investment cycle.External uncertainties add to the challenge. Crude oil prices, geopolitical tensions, currency movements and trade-related disruptions can alter input costs and investment calculations. A weaker rupee can raise the cost of imported equipment and raw materials, while uncertainty over global demand can cause companies to defer expansion plans.Bank profitability to improve in FY27 on margin recovery, healthy credit growth: SystematixThe banking sector's investment testThe September quarter's earnings performance has improved the backdrop for private investment, but the transition from better corporate profitability to a sustained capex cycle will take time. Banks are likely to see opportunities if companies move ahead with expansion plans, but they must balance growth ambitions with the cost and stability of their funding.The widening credit-deposit growth gap, rising short-term borrowing rates and higher loan-to-deposit ratio underline the importance of maintaining a durable funding base. Yet the answer is not simply to slow lending. It is to mobilise deposits, price loans and liabilities carefully, and direct credit towards projects with sound cash flows and credible execution plans.For the banking sector, the next phase of growth will be determined not only by how much credit it can extend, but also by how efficiently it can fund that credit without compromising margins, liquidity or asset quality. India's investment cycle may be improving; the test for lenders is whether they can finance it sustainably.Please share your feedback, suggestions if any.
