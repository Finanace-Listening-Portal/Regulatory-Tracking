On this basis, the effective benefit could be around Rs 1 lakh crore annually, translating into around Rs 5 lakh crore over five years, according to SBI Research. Advt The calculation assumes that the $127 billion of deposits can support additional credit through a credit multiplier of 2.5. Applying an effective yield of 7.5 per cent to around Rs 25 lakh crore of additional credit results in a notional yield of around Rs 1.8 lakh crore per annum for banks.144444After accounting for interest outgo of around Rs 75,000 crore on the deposits, the resulting notional benefit is around Rs 1 lakh crore a year. Over five years, this amounts to around Rs 5 lakh crore.On double countingThe report also challenges the calculation of an around Rs 5 lakh crore cost for the FCNR(B) scheme, arguing that it amounts to double counting the same foreign exchange exposure. The estimate combines around Rs 1.75 lakh crore of additional interest cost with around Rs 3.18 lakh crore of foreign exchange depreciation cost based on an assumed 5 per cent annual depreciation of the rupee over five years.The special USD-INR swap facility is intended to hedge the exchange rate risk associated with the FCNR(B) deposits. Once the exchange rate risk on the principal has been hedged, a subsequent depreciation of the rupee does not generate an additional contractual loss on the principal for the banks or the RBI in the manner assumed in the Rs 3.18 lakh crore calculation.A more realistic assessment of hedging costs would take into account the maturity profile of the mobilisation. The $127 billion has been divided into three maturity buckets, with around $63.5 billion assigned to the five year bucket, $57.15 billion to the three year bucket and $6.35 billion to the one year bucket. Advt Applying an average annual USD-INR hedging cost of 3 per cent to the respective tenors results in a cumulative hedging cost of around $15 billion.The large liquidity inflow can be absorbed over time through festive season demand, credit disbursal pipelines, new advance sanctions and outflows on account of advance taxes and GST. Banks, however, would not need to set aside prudent risk management principles and deploy all the liquidity into advances or government securities.
