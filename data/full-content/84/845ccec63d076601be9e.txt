Rosamma Joy (Delhi High Court) Aadhaar Date of Birth Cannot Justify Early Retirement Contrary to Employer’s Own Records: Delhi High Court An employer cannot rely solely on the date of birth appearing in an employee’s Aadhaar card to retire her one year earlier when its own longstanding ESIC record indicates a different date. The Delhi High Court has reiterated that Aadhaar is not, by itself, proof of date of birth, and conflicting records must be evaluated according to their evidentiary value. In M/s J. J. Electronics v. Smt. Rosamma Joy, W.P.(C) 16476/2022, decided on 13 November 2026, Justice Manoj Kumar Ohri dismissed the management’s challenge to a Labour Court award holding the workwoman’s termination illegal. Background of the Dispute The workwoman claimed that she had been employed as a “Finishing Man” since 1 December 1981, with last drawn wages of ₹10,500 per month. According to her, the management became annoyed when she demanded payment of the increased minimum wages notified in March 2017. She alleged that her services were terminated on 20 May 2017 when she demanded her unpaid wages for April. The management maintained that she had attained the establishment’s retirement age of 58 years on that date. It relied upon her Aadhaar card, which recorded her date of birth as 20 May 1959.
