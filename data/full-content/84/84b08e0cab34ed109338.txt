Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Tata Capital Housing Finance Limited Vs Dharan Infra-EPC Limited (NCLT Mumbai) Introduction The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Mumbai Bench, rejected an application seeking withdrawal of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against Dharan Infra-EPC Limited following a settlement with Tata Capital Housing Finance Limited. The Tribunal held that settlement with the initiating financial creditor could not, by itself, justify withdrawal when substantial claims of other creditors remained unresolved. Once insolvency proceedings are admitted, they acquire a collective character, requiring consideration of all concerned stakeholders rather than merely the parties to the original petition. Background of the Case Tata Capital Housing Finance Limited initiated proceedings under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, alleging a default of ₹28,04,91,115 by the corporate debtor. The Tribunal admitted the petition on 12 December 2025 and appointed an Interim Resolution Professional (IRP). A public announcement followed, inviting creditors to submit their claims. Before constitution of the Committee of Creditors (CoC), Tata Capital informed the IRP that it had reached a settlement with the suspended director. It acknowledged receipt of ₹10 crore towards full and final settlement and submitted Form FA dated 1 January 2026. Payment of CIRP costs was also confirmed. The IRP consequently filed an application under Section 12A read with Regulation 30A, seeking withdrawal of the proceedings. Objections by Other Financial Creditors Six other financial creditors opposed withdrawal through intervention petitions. They argued that admission of CIRP transformed the proceedings into proceedings in rem, involving the interests of the entire creditor body. Several creditors had already submitted claims to the IRP. Some had earlier filed independent Section 7 petitions, which were dismissed or otherwise affected because CIRP had commenced in the present matter. The intervenors also highlighted that the IRP had informed the appellate tribunal of claims aggregating approximately ₹600 crore. One intervening creditor’s claim alone was approximately ₹183 crore. They contended that a bilateral settlement could not extinguish their rights or justify terminating the collective insolvency process. Submissions Supporting Withdrawal Tata Capital submitted that its dues stood fully settled and that it had no surviving claim against the corporate debtor. It therefore had no objection to closure of CIRP. It argued that the other creditors could pursue their independent remedies under Section 7. It also raised limitation objections concerning certain intervenors’ claims. The suspended director supported withdrawal, submitting that continuation of CIRP served no insolvency resolution purpose once the claim triggering the proceedings had been settled. Applicable Statutory Framework The Tribunal noted that Section 12A was amended with effect from 26 May 2026. However, admission of CIRP, submission of Form FA and filing of the withdrawal application had occurred before that date. Accordingly, it considered the application under the pre-amendment framework. Under that framework, Regulation 30A(1)(a) permitted a withdrawal application before constitution of the CoC through the IRP. Nevertheless, permission to withdraw remained subject to judicial scrutiny, rather than following automatically from settlement. Tribunal’s Findings Relying on the Supreme Court’s decision in GLAS Trust Company LLC v. BYJU’S Raveendran & Others, the Tribunal emphasised that admission fundamentally changes the nature of insolvency proceedings. After admission, creditors other than the original applicant become necessary stakeholders. The adjudicating authority must therefore hear concerned parties and consider relevant circumstances before deciding whether withdrawal should be permitted. Here, the settlement covered only Tata Capital’s claim. No material established that the substantial claims of other creditors had been settled or that any settlement with them was proposed. The Tribunal held that payment to one creditor did not establish the corporate debtor’s ability to resolve its wider financial distress. It also referred to the 90% CoC voting threshold under Section 12A as reflecting the statutory objective of protecting the interests of a significant majority of creditors. Independent Remedies and Limitation The Tribunal rejected the suggestion that other creditors should simply restart separate proceedings. Withdrawal could require revival or fresh filing of multiple petitions, causing multiplicity of litigation and further delay. Questions concerning limitation were left for consideration during verification and consideration of claims, as applicable. The withdrawal proceedings did not finally determine the admissibility of every intervenor’s debt. Final Decision The Tribunal dismissed I.A. No. 166/2026 and the suspended director’s application seeking the same relief. It additionally observed that the amended Section 12A would not assist the applicants, noting its restriction on withdrawal before CoC constitution. The intervention petitions were disposed of without findings on their remaining independent issues or allowing impleadment. The ruling underscores that settlement with the initiating creditor does not create an automatic right to withdraw admitted CIRP, particularly where substantial liabilities to other creditors remain unresolved and require collective consideration. Reply of the Respondent: 9.7 The reply filed by Respondent IRP is identical to the reply filed by her in I.V.N. P. No. 8/2026. Therefore, the submissions contained therein are not reproduced for the sake of brevity and to avoid repetition. Rejoinder of the Applicant: 9.8 It is submitted that the IRP’s reliance on the expression “no further action” in the NCLAT Order is wholly misconceived. The immediate relief sought is limited to transparency and disclosure of the claims that the IRP was specifically permitted or directed to collate. The IRP cannot simultaneously contend that the Applicant’s claim has been duly collated and refuse to disclose the List of Creditors or the outcome of the collation exercise. 9.9 The Applicant further submits that the IRP’s assertion that the Applicant is “not left remediless” is wholly misconceived. The Applicant is not merely seeking to preserve an independent recovery remedy but seeks to protect its statutory rights as a Financial Creditor arising from the CIRP already admitted by this Tribunal. 10. Brief facts of IVN. P./129/2026: 10.1 This Petition has been filed by the Applicant, in his capacity as a Suspended Director of the Corporate Debtor, seeking to intervene and be impleaded as parties in the pending proceedings arising out of an application under Section 12A of the Code. 10.2 In addition to the facts recorded hereinabove, it is submitted that the Financial Creditor which had instituted the proceedings under Section 7 of the Code has itself acknowledged receipt of the settlement amount and has confirmed that the payment of Rs. 10 Crores constitutes full and final settlement against its claim. In furtherance of the said settlement, Tata Capital, furnished Form FA to the IRP for the purpose of seeking withdrawal of the CIRP under Section 12A of the Code read with Regulation 30A of the CIRP Regulations. Accordingly, the IRP has filed IA 166 of 2026 seeking withdrawal of the CIRP. 10.3 It is submitted that continuation of the CIRP serves no insolvency resolution purpose in respect of the claim which triggered the proceedings. 10.4 In view of the aforesaid, the Applicant seek to intervene and be impleaded as party Respondent in the Section 12A Application filed by the IRP. 11. Analysis and Findings 11.1 Heard the Ld. Counsels for the parties and perused the record. We have given our thoughtful consideration to the pleadings and submissions. 11.2 The I.A. 166 of 2026 has been filed by the IRP seeking withdrawal of the CIRP under Section 12A of the Code. It is submitted that, prior to the constitution of the CoC, the Financial Creditor informed the IRP that it had arrived at a settlement with the Suspended Director and had received a sum of Rs. 10 Crore towards full and final settlement. Subsequently, the Financial Creditor submitted Form FA dated 01.01.2026 and confirmed that the CIRP costs had been paid. Further, vide order dated 06.01.2026, the Hon’ble NCLAT directed the IRP to collate the claims, however, restrained him from taking any further steps, while granting liberty to the IRP to file an application under Section 12A of the Code. 11.3 The six other Financial Creditors have filed their respective Intervention Petitions. The primary submission of the Intervenors is that, upon admission of the Corporate Debtor into CIRP, the proceedings become a collective in rem proceeding involving all creditors as necessary stakeholders, and therefore, withdrawal of the CIRP ought not to be permitted by this Tribunal solely on the basis of a settlement between the original Financial Creditor and the Corporate Debtor. Further, two of the Intervenors submitted that their respective applications under Section 7 of the Code were either dismissed or kept pending on account of the CIRP initiated vide order dated 12.12.2025. In certain cases, this Tribunal directed the concerned creditors to submit their claims before the IRP, while granting liberty to revive their respective Section 7 petitions in the event of withdrawal of the CIRP. All the Intervenors have submitted their respective claims in Form C to the IRP, however, no communication has been received from the IRP in respect thereof. It is further submitted that the IBBI portal does not reflect any claims, whereas the IRP, before the Hon’ble NCLAT on 09.07.2026, stated that claims amounting to approximately Rs. 600 Crore had been received. It is also submitted that the CoC had not been constituted as on 04.01.2026. In view of the aforesaid submissions, the Intervenors seek dismissal of I.A. No. 166/2026, verification of their claims, and constitution of the CoC. 11.4 On the other hand, the original Financial Creditor submits that, its dues were settled by the one-time settlement dated 30.12.2025, and that it has no surviving claim against the Corporate Debtor and has no objection to closure of the CIRP. It is submitted that the Hon’ble NCLAT did not grant any liberty to the Intervenors to file an intervention application before this Tribunal, and the liberty was confined to the appeal filed by the Suspended Director of the Corporate Debtor. Further, it is submitted that the Intervenors may file their own Section 7 petitions and can pursue their independent remedies. With regards to the IVN. P. 9 of 2026 and IVN. P. 15 of 2026, it is stated that the claims of the Applicants therein are barred by limitation and therefore the same deserves to be dismissed. 11.5 The IRP has also filed her reply to the aforesaid Intervention Petitions, all these replies are identical. It is stated that the role of the IRP is neutral and is bound by the commercial wisdom of the CoC as well as the directions of this Tribunal. Accordingly, the Respondent No. 1 seeks appropriate directions from this Tribunal. 11.6 The I.A. No. 129/2026 has been filed by the Suspended Director of the Corporate Debtor. He submits that the Financial Creditor has acknowledged receipt of Rs. 10 Crore towards full and final settlement and has furnished Form FA. Accordingly, it is submitted that continuation of the CIRP would serve no insolvency resolution purpose in respect of the claim on the basis of which the CIRP was initiated. He, therefore, seeks withdrawal of the CIRP. 11.7 Before proceeding to consider the issue arising in the present case, it is relevant to note that Section 12A of the Code was amended with effect from 26.05.2026 by the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 6 of 2026). In the present case, the Corporate Debtor was admitted into CIRP vide order dated 12.12.2025, the Financial Creditor submitted Form FA dated 01.01.2026, and I.A. No. 166/2026 seeking withdrawal of the CIRP was filed on 06.01.2026. Thus, all the aforesaid events occurred prior to 26.05.2026. Under these circumstances, the present application is being considered in accordance with the provision as it stood prior to the amendment. 11.8 It is relevant to take note of the Section 12A, as it stood before the amendment. The same is reproduced hereinbelow: “12A. The Adjudicating Authority may allow the withdrawal of application admitted under section 7 or section 9 or section 10, on an application made by the applicant with the approval of ninety per cent. voting share of the committee of creditors, in such manner as may be prescribed.” 11.9 Further, Regulation 30A(1)(a) of the CIRP Regulations reads as under: “30A. (1) An application for withdrawal under section 12A may be made to the Adjudicating Authority – (a) before the constitution of the committee, by the applicant through the interim resolution professional; …” 11.10 A conjoint reading of the aforesaid provisions makes it evident that Section 12A of the Code permits withdrawal of the CIRP with the approval of 90% voting share of the CoC, while Regulation 30A(6) of the CIRP Regulations confers discretion upon this Tribunal to permit withdrawal where the CoC has not been constituted. In the present case, admittedly, the CoC has not been constituted, and the IRP has filed the application seeking withdrawal under Regulation 30A(1)(a) of the CIRP Regulations. 11.11 In these circumstances, it is apposite to take note of the judgment passed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in GLAS Trust Company LLC v. BYJU’S Raveendran & Ors. (Civil Appeal No. 9986 of 2024), wherein the Hon’ble Supreme Court, while considering the issue of withdrawal of CIRP prior to the constitution of the CoC, observed as follows: “42. From this scheme of Chapter II of the IBC, it appears that the admission of an application is a significant event that alters the nature of the proceedings, and the stakeholders involved. Initially, when the petition is filed by the financial creditor, operational creditor or corporate applicant, as the case may be, the proceedings are in personam and the only relevant stakeholders are the applicant creditor and the corporate debtor. However, once the petition is admitted and CIRP is initiated, several significant changes take place, including the transfer of the management of the affairs of the corporate debtor to the IRP, the declaration of the moratorium, and the collation of the claims against the corporate debtor. Therefore, the proceedings now change character – they become in rem and are no longer the preserve of only the applicant creditor and the corporate debtor and even creditors who were not the original applicants, become necessary stakeholders. … 63. In essence, after a series of deliberations by the legislature, the executive and nudges by this Court, the framework created by Rule 8 of the NCLT Rules and Section 12A of the IBC read with Rule 30A of the CIRP Regulations lays down an exhaustive procedure for the withdrawal of an application filed by creditors under Sections 7, 9, or 10 of the IBC. Withdrawal may be sought at four stages, all of which have a procedure prescribed under the existing framework. These may be summarized as follows: i. … ii. After an application under Sections 7, 9, or 10 is admitted, but before the CoC has been constituted: Although Section 12A continues to be silent on this aspect, after the decision in Swiss Ribbons (supra), Regulation 30A was amended to provide for this eventuality. An application for withdrawal in such cases may be made by the applicant through the IRP. The IRP will then place the application before the NCLT, which may pass an order either approving or rejecting the application. As noted above, once the application has been admitted, the proceedings are no longer the sole preserve of the applicant creditor and the corporate debtor. They are now in rem and at this stage, the NCLT must hear the concerned parties and consider all relevant factors before approving or rejecting the application for withdrawal. The NCLT being a quasi-judicial body, must not act as a mere post office, which stamps and approves every settlement agreement, without application of judicial mind. ,,,” 11.12 From the aforesaid extract, it is clear that the Hon’ble Supreme Court has held that once the Corporate Debtor is admitted into CIRP, the proceedings assume the character of proceedings in rem. Accordingly, where withdrawal of CIRP is sought after its initiation but before the application after hearing all parties concerned and taking into account the relevant factors, having regard to the facts and circumstances of each case. 11.13 In the present case, six other Financial Creditors have not only submitted their respective claims to the IRP but have also filed Intervention Petitions opposing withdrawal of the CIRP and seeking its continuation. The settlement in question is confined to one Financial Creditor for Rs. 10 Crore against the claimed default of Rs. 28,04,91,115/-. The claims of the Intervenors are substantial, one of them being approximately Rs. 183 Crore. Further, the IRP had informed the Hon’ble NCLAT that claims aggregating to approximately Rs. 600 Crore had been received. The other Financial Creditors are not parties to the settlement, and no material has been placed on record to establish that their claims have also been settled or that any such settlement is proposed. The ability of the Corporate Debtor to resolve its financial liabilities vis-a-vis its creditors is a relevant consideration while determining an application for withdrawal of CIRP. A settlement with one Financial Creditor, in the wake of significant larger amount pertaining to the other creditors remaining outstanding, does not, by itself, establish the Corporate Debtor’s ability to resolve its financial distress. Furthermore, it is also relevant to note that the higher threshold of approval by 90% of the voting share of the CoC under Section 12A of the Code is intended to safeguard the interests of the significant majority of creditors and ensure that withdrawal of CIRP is not permitted solely on the basis of a settlement with an individual creditor or some creditors. 11.14 With regard to the contention of the original Financial Creditor that the Intervenors may pursue their respective petitions under Section 7 of the Code, it is relevant to note that, pursuant to the admission order dated 12.12.2025 passed in C.P. No. 729 of 2025, the other Section 7 petitions were dismissed, with directions to the concerned creditors to submit their respective claims before the IRP. Permitting withdrawal of the CIRP proceedings, thereby leading to multiplicity of litigation and further delay, which would not serve the interests of either the creditors or the Corporate Debtor. The collective framework of the Code is intended to facilitate resolution in the interests of all creditors, rather than permit individual enforcement or settlement to the exclusion of other creditors. As regards the question of limitation of enforcement of claims, if any, the same shall be considered by the IRP and the CoC, as applicable, at the stage of verification and consideration of claims. 11.15 In addition, and without prejudice to the aforesaid, even if the amended provisions of Section 12A are applied, the present application would not merit acceptance. Sub-section (1) of the amended Section 12A requires the approval of ninety per cent of the voting share of the Committee of Creditors for withdrawal of the CIRP, while sub-section (2)(a) expressly bars withdrawal before the constitution of the Committee of Creditors. In the present case, the Committee of Creditors has not been constituted, and the settlement relied upon is confined to the claim of the original Financial Creditor. In the absence of the requisite approval of the CoC under Section 12A(1), the statutory requirements for withdrawal are not satisfied. Accordingly, the present application is liable to be dismissed even under the amended provision. 12. Having considered the facts and circumstances of the present case, the implications of withdrawal, and the objectives of the Code, we are of the considered opinion that the CIRP initiated by admitting Petition No. 729 of 2025, cannot be permitted to be withdrawn solely on the basis of a settlement with one Financial Creditor. Accordingly, I.A. No. 166/2026 is dismissed. 13. Since I.A. No. 129/2026 seeks the same relief and the findings recorded herein apply equally to the said Application, I.A. No. 129/2026 is also dismissed. 14. In view of the order of dismissal of I.A. No. 166/2026, decision in regard to other independent issue raised and/or impleadment in Intervention Petitions IVN. P. Nos. 8/2026, 9/2026, 15/2026, 24/2026, 25/2026 and 81/2026 become academic in nature. Accordingly, these Intervention Petitions are disposed of without returning findings as to the other independent issues raised therein or allowing their impleadment.
