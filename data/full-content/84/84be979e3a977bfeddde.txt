Summary: Fixed deposit safety depends not merely on attractive interest rates but also on deposit insurance, bank concentration, liquidity and taxation. Under the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) framework, eligible bank deposits are insured up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity, including principal and accrued interest. Multiple fixed deposits, savings accounts and balances across branches of the same bank are generally aggregated rather than separately insured. Deposits with different insured banks receive separate coverage, while joint-account combinations and ownership capacities may affect insurance eligibility. For example, ₹4.80 lakh in principal and ₹30,000 in accrued interest produces ₹5.10 lakh in deposits, exceeding the insurance ceiling by ₹10,000. Importantly, deposit insurance does not guarantee immediate access to funds during bank restrictions or failure; payments follow statutory procedures and timelines. Investors should verify whether their bank is insured, examine financial disclosures, compare premature withdrawal conditions and avoid relying exclusively on higher advertised returns. Fixed deposit interest remains subject to applicable income-tax provisions, while TDS requirements depend on the relevant tax year and depositor status. Depositors should diversify across insured banks, monitor accrued interest, maintain liquidity, stagger maturities and preserve nomination and KYC records. Effective FD planning requires balancing capital protection, post-tax returns, accessibility and regulatory safeguards.
