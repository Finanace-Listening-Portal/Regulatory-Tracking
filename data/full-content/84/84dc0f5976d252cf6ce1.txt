Several other large banks, including PNB, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank, have also revised their deposit rate cards. Other large lenders, including Punjab National Bank (PNB), ICICI Bank, Axis Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank, have also revised their deposit rate cards during the period.The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee raised the policy repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% in October, marking a shift towards monetary tightening. The rate increase followed an earlier easing cycle, during which the central bank had reduced the repo rate by a cumulative 125 basis points. Advt The transmission of the policy rate hike to deposit rates, however, is not uniform or immediate. Banks consider their individual liquidity positions, deposit growth, credit demand and funding requirements while deciding the rates offered to depositors.Largest private sector lender HDFC Bank has increased interest rates by up to 50 basis points on select FD tenures, effective November 4. The bank has raised rates on deposits with tenures of 61 days to six months to 4.75% from 4.25% earlier. Advt Indian Bank has also raised rates on its special-tenure deposits by 15 basis points. The rate on its 555-day deposit has been increased to 6.80% from 6.65%, while the rate on its 777-day deposit has been raised to 6.75% from 6.60%, effective October 23.Bank of India has revised its FD rates effective November 2 and introduced a new 500-day BOI Akshay Deposit. The special deposit offers 6.85% to general customers, 7.35% to senior citizens and 7.50% to super senior citizens.PNB has revised its domestic term deposit rates effective November 5. Its latest rate card offers general depositors 6.70% on the 444-day deposit and 6.80% on the 680-day deposit. Senior citizens can earn up to 7.30%, while super senior citizens can earn up to 7.55% on select maturities. Advt Among private sector lenders, ICICI Bank has revised its FD rates effective October 24. Kotak Mahindra Bank's revised rates came into effect on November 4, while Axis Bank revised rates on select maturities from October 29.The banking system has continued to operate with surplus durable liquidity, although the RBI has stepped up measures to absorb excess funds. According to the RBI's October Bulletin, average daily net liquidity absorption under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) stood at Rs 3.60 lakh crore in October up to October 19, compared with Rs 7.8 lakh crore in September and Rs 5.5 lakh crore in August. The central bank also conducted open market sale operations of Rs 1 lakh crore in September and Rs 25,000 crore in October, alongside variable rate reverse repo auctions to absorb surplus liquidity. Advt As per the RBI's latest money market operations data for November 11, the banking system had a net liquidity deficit of Rs 1.34 lakh crore when current and outstanding operations were considered. However, net durable liquidity remained in surplus at Rs 7.93 lakh crore as of October 15. This indicates that while the system continues to have a structural liquidity surplus, short-term liquidity conditions have tightened following the RBI's absorption measures.
