Summary: Secret Network is an active Cosmos-SDK-based proof-of-stake blockchain whose native SCRT coin pays network fees, supports validator delegation and participates in governance. Its current official operator documentation identifies the live mainnet as secret-4 and the current node release as version 1.27.2. Secret Network’s privacy proposition is narrower than the phrase ‘private blockchain’: native SCRT transfers are public by default, while privacy-preserving behaviour depends on particular contracts, token standards, permits, viewing keys and confidential execution. Users also face wallet, validator, bridge, smart-contract, hardware-attestation and regulatory risks. For an Indian taxpayer, a disposal of SCRT or another VDA can trigger the special VDA income-tax regime and section 194S withholding rules, while staking receipts, cross-chain transfers and self-custody create valuation and recordkeeping questions. This article explains verified mechanics without forecasting price or recommending acquisition, staking or use. The same documentation specifies the current mainnet package as version 1.27.2. Those facts support continued technical activity, but an operating chain is not evidence of investment quality, exchange availability, liquidity or legal suitability for a particular Indian user. SCRT is the network’s native coin. It is used to pay gas, can be delegated to validators, and supplies voting weight in on-chain governance. Official project material also makes an important privacy distinction: SCRT itself is a public asset on the base chain. An address, balance movement or fee involving native SCRT should not be assumed confidential merely because the network supports privacy-preserving applications. Privacy is implemented through particular contracts and token designs, not by making every base-layer fact invisible. The chain uses delegated proof of stake. The current validator documentation states that the active-set ceiling is 80 validators, the staking denomination is uscrt, one SCRT equals 1,000,000 uscrt, and the unbonding time is 1,814,400 seconds, or 21 days. These are protocol parameters, not guaranteed withdrawal times: a wallet, validator, relayer, application or exchange may impose additional processing and review time. Its current documentation describes Intel SGX prerequisites and remote-attestation steps for validator infrastructure. In practical terms, the chain can verify code execution and state transitions while protected execution environments handle sensitive inputs. This design introduces a different trust profile from a system that relies only on public deterministic execution: users must consider enclave software, hardware assumptions, attestation services, key management and upgrade governance alongside ordinary blockchain risks. Privacy-preserving tokens and contracts can conceal selected data such as an amount or recipient, yet the surrounding transaction may still reveal that an address interacted with a contract, paid a fee or called a function. Viewing keys and signed permits are access-control mechanisms for authorised queries; they do not erase all metadata, protect a compromised endpoint or prevent a recipient from disclosing information. The official SNIP-20 explanation specifically distinguishes private token data from the public fact of an on-chain execute transaction. The project’s newer Secret Zone material describes separately operated confidential programs, hardware proofs and key-release checks. Some pages are explicitly labelled as details pending. Readers should therefore treat those features as evolving architecture and verify live availability before describing them as completed services. A proposal, roadmap page or community discussion is not equivalent to an activated mainnet feature. Seed phrases, hardware-wallet applications, browser extensions, chain identifiers and destination addresses must be independently checked. A malicious extension, clipboard substitution, leaked seed or incorrect network can cause an irreversible loss even when the protocol itself operates correctly. Delegation transfers voting power and economic exposure to a validator without handing over the underlying private key. Rewards are variable, validator commission can apply, and slashing or downtime can reduce outcomes. Unbonding creates a waiting period during which market exposure continues. Anyone reporting staking for tax or accounting should retain delegation transactions, reward-claim records, validator commission information, dates, quantities, rupee values and subsequent disposal details rather than relying only on a wallet’s current balance. IBC and bridge transactions add another layer. A token represented on another chain may use a denomination trace or contract representation that is not identical to native SCRT. Bridge custody, relayer availability, channel status and destination-chain contracts can fail independently. Before transfer, the user should record the source asset, source chain, transaction hash, destination representation, fees and beneficial ownership so that a later tax or audit review can reconstruct the movement without treating an internal transfer as an unexplained acquisition or disposal. Indian VDA Tax Treatment Depends on the Event, Not the Label India’s special VDA framework applies to transfer income from a virtual digital asset. The current statutory regime generally taxes qualifying transfer income at 30%, permits only the limited acquisition-cost deduction specified by law, and restricts set-off and carry-forward of VDA losses. Surcharge and health and education cess can also apply. The tax is event-based: acquiring SCRT, moving it between self-controlled wallets, swapping it, selling it for rupees, paying with it, receiving a reward and bridging it are not automatically the same legal event. Section 194S can require one per cent tax deduction at source on consideration for transfer of a VDA when its conditions and thresholds are met. The person responsible, exchange process, in-kind component, taxpayer category and transaction date all matter. The presence or absence of a platform deduction does not by itself settle the recipient’s final tax liability. Taxpayers should reconcile platform statements, wallet histories and bank entries rather than treating TDS as the tax calculation. Staking rewards need fact-specific advice. Questions include when the recipient obtains enforceable control, what rupee value is supportable on that date, whether the receipt is business or other income, and what cost should be retained for a later transfer calculation. Privacy features do not eliminate statutory reporting obligations. A taxpayer claiming that records cannot be produced because the protocol hides data may face practical difficulty proving acquisition cost, ownership and source of funds. Operational Risks Are Specific to Secret Network’s Design Confidential execution can reduce public disclosure of selected data, but it also makes independent observation harder. A user may need a viewing key, permit or trusted interface to inspect balances and history. If those credentials are lost, an application is discontinued or an endpoint misreports data, reconstruction can be more difficult than reading a fully public token ledger. Users should preserve signed transaction confirmations and exported records outside the application interface. Validator concentration, governance changes, software upgrades, enclave vulnerabilities, bridge failures, smart-contract defects and token-liquidity conditions can each affect utility. The current documentation’s detailed SGX installation and attestation requirements demonstrate that hardware and operational dependencies are material, not incidental. No privacy claim should be converted into an assertion of anonymity, legal immunity, guaranteed confidentiality or freedom from counterparty risk. Indian residents also need to distinguish tax recognition from regulatory permission. A tax rule for VDAs does not amount to RBI or government approval of a token, wallet, exchange, cross-border transfer or service provider. FEMA, anti-money-laundering obligations, FIU registration requirements for service providers, sanctions screening and banking controls may apply separately to the surrounding transaction. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Is every SCRT transfer private? No. Official project material says native SCRT is public by default. Particular secret contracts or token standards can conceal selected fields, while the transaction call, gas payment or contract interaction may remain observable. 2. What does SCRT do on the network? SCRT pays transaction fees, can be delegated for network security and supports governance participation. Those utilities do not guarantee demand, price, liquidity or a staking return. 3. How long does protocol unbonding take? The current validator documentation reports 1,814,400 seconds, or 21 days. Wallets, applications or exchanges can add operational processing beyond that protocol parameter. 4. Does a viewing key make a wallet anonymous? No. It authorises specified private queries. It does not hide every metadata point, cure endpoint compromise or stop an authorised recipient from sharing information. 5. Is moving SCRT between my own wallets always taxable? A genuine transfer between wallets under the same beneficial ownership is not automatically the same as a sale, but bridges, wraps, fees and changed beneficial rights require fact-specific review and complete evidence. 6. How can section 194S affect a sale or swap? One per cent withholding on VDA consideration can apply when statutory conditions and thresholds are met. Exchange workflows, in-kind consideration and the responsible person must be checked for the actual transaction. 7. Does Indian VDA taxation mean SCRT is officially approved? No. Tax recognition is not regulatory approval. FEMA, AML, FIU, banking and service-provider rules can apply independently. 8. What should a staking taxpayer retain? Keep dated delegation and reward records, quantities, rupee valuation evidence, validator commission, wallet addresses, claim transactions and later disposal evidence.
