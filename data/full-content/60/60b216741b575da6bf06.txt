Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Dell International Services India Private Limited Vs Commissioner of Central Tax (CESTAT Bangalore) Summary: The CESTAT Bangalore, in Dell International Services India Private Limited Vs Commissioner of Central Tax, considered a dispute concerning rejection of a service tax rebate claim relating to exported services for the period April 2012 to June 2012. The appellant, formerly known as EMC IT Solutions India Private Limited, was engaged in providing Business Auxiliary Services, Business Support Services and Information Technology Services. It had filed a rebate claim under Rule 5 of the Cenvat Credit Rules, 2004 read with Notification No. 11/2005-ST dated 19 April 2005. The original authority sanctioned the claim partly and rejected ₹45,52,322. The Commissioner (Appeals) upheld the rejection on the ground that the notification’s conditions had not been complied with. The appellant contended that the original authority had already accepted that the services qualified as exports, consideration was received in convertible foreign exchange and service tax had been paid on the exported services. According to the appellant, the rebate could not subsequently be rejected by questioning eligibility for input service credit or the nexus between input and exported output services. Reliance was placed on Textron India Pvt. Ltd., Ivy Comtech Pvt. Ltd. and ANZ Support Services India Private Limited. The Revenue defended the rejection, submitting that the appellant had failed to produce necessary documents and that invoices relating to ₹6,06,027 did not contain particulars such as PAN, valid service tax registration numbers and correct addresses. It also argued that, because the impugned orders were passed after 1 July 2017, the refund was required to be processed under Section 142(3) of the CGST Act, 2017. Reliance was placed on Rakcon India Pvt. Ltd.. Examining the claim, the Tribunal observed that although the appellant had claimed ₹1,38,19,578, the original authority had considered only ₹1,04,60,578 because category-wise details had not been furnished. The difference of ₹33,59,000, rejected for non-production of documents, required reconsideration after allowing the appellant to submit the necessary records. The Tribunal further held that where services had been exported and the prescribed notification conditions were satisfied, rebate could not be denied merely on the question of nexus between input services and output services. Accordingly, the nexus-based rejection was not sustainable. However, the disputed credit of ₹6,06,027 required verification to ascertain whether tax relating to the relevant invoices had been discharged. The Tribunal directed verification of the documents and an opportunity to the appellant to produce them. The appeal was allowed by way of remand, with the document-dependent issues requiring examination by the original authority. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CESTAT BANGALORE The appellant i.e., EMC IT Solutions Pvt. Ltd. now known as M/s. Dell International Services Pvt. Ltd. were rendering taxable services under the category of Business Auxiliary Service, Business Support Service and Information Technology Services. The appellant filed a rebate claim for the period from April 2012 to June 2012 in terms of Rule 5 of the Cenvat Credit Rules, 2004 read with Notification No.11/2005-ST dated 19/04/2005. The original authority sanctioned part of the amount and rejected an amount of Rs.45,52,322/- for which the appellant filed an appeal before the Commissioner (Appeals). The Commissioner (Appeals) upheld the order of the original authority on the ground that the conditions of the Notification No.11/2005-ST dated 19.04.2005 were not complied with. Aggrieved by this order, the appellant has filed this appeal. 2. The Learned Counsel submitted that the original authority had recorded that in terms of the Notification No.11/2005-ST dated 19.04.2005 the appellant had rendered services which qualified as export services, consideration was received in convertible foreign exchange and service tax was paid on the exported services; and hence, the appellant had fulfilled the conditions, limitations and procedures prescribed under the above said Notification but rejected the refund/rebate claim on the ground that the appellant was not eligible for credit on some of the input services. Therefore, it is her submission that the Commissioner (Appeals) cannot now reject their refund claim on the ground that was accepted by the original authority. She relies on the decision of this Hon’ble Tribunal in the case of Textron India Pvt. Ltd. vs. Commissioner of Service Tax Bangalore: 2015 (39) STR 468 (Tri.-Bang.) wherein the Tribunal had held that while considering a rebate claim of service tax paid on exported services, the nexus issue cannot be raised and the credit cannot be denied for which separate proceedings for denial of credit had to be issued. She also relied on the decision of Ivy Comtech Pvt. Ltd. vs. Commissioner of Central Excise, Customs and Service Tax, Hyderabad: 2016 (42) STR 66 (Tri-Bang) and also the decision in the case of ANZ Support Services India Private Limited vs. Commissioner of Central Tax (Appeals), Bangalore: Final Order No. 20392/2026 dated 09/01/2026. 3. The learned Authorised Representative (AR) reiterating the findings of the Commissioner (Appeals) submits that the original authority had rejected the refund claim on the ground that documents were not produced for processing the rebate claim. It is his submission that an amount of Rs.6,06,027/- was rejected on the ground that the input invoices did not mention PAN, valid service tax registration number and correct address etc; hence, the question of extending input credit on these invoices cannot be sustained; therefore, the rebate claim to that extent needs to be rejected. He further submits that from 01.07.2017, certain Acts are repealed and Section 142 of the CGST Act allows refund of cenvat credit in certain contingencies as transitional measures. He further submits that, in the instant case, since the impugned orders are passed after 01.07.2017, the refund of credit are liable to be processed only under Section 142(3) of the CGST Act, 2017 and since the appellant has not claimed the refund under Section 142 of the CGST Act, 2017 the same needs to be rejected. He relies on the decision of this Bench in the case of M/s. Rakcon India Pvt. Ltd. vs. Commissioner of Central Tax, Bangalore: Final Order No.21416/2023 dated 19.12.2023 wherein the refund was rejected on similar grounds. 4. Heard both sides. Though the appellant had filed rebate claim for Rs.1,38,19,578/-, the original authority has considered only an amount of Rs. 1,04,60,578/- since category-wise details have not been mentioned; however, observes that all the conditions of the Notification have been satisfied by the appellant. The difference between the above amounts i.e., Rs.33,59,000/- for non-production of documents has been rejected, hence the same is to be remanded to enable the appellant to produce the documents before the original authority for necessary verification. 5. With regard to the amounts rejected on the question of nexus of input services with the output services is a settled law that as long as services have been exported and the appellant has satisfied the conditions of Notification No. 11/2005-ST dated 19.05.2005, the refund or rebate claim cannot be rejected.; hence, the same is to be allowed. The original authority has disallowed cenvat credit of Rs.6,06,027/- on the ground that it could not be ascertained whether tax in respect of such invoices has been discharged. This also needs to be verified. Accordingly, on these two issues it is being remanded for necessary verification of documents. Needless to say that the appellant is to be given an opportunity to produce the documents for necessary verification. Appeal is allowed by way of remand. (Order pronounced in Open Court on 06.11.2026.)
