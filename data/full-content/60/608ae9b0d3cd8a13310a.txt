Why fallen angels could outperform market leadersElara analysts concluded that a lower price alone is not enough to pick fallen angels, as conviction on earnings recovery and profit-pool expansion is what lifts the hit rate. Companies that went on to grow PAT strongly, expand margins or raise their share of the NSE 500 profit pool beat the index in about 75 per cent of cases, against 57 per cent for the average fallen angel, its analysis showed. According to Elara, several conditions that historically accompanied market bottoms are visible in the current market. First, the recent correction has been driven largely by global factors, while domestic corporate earnings have remained resilient. Second, the valuation gap between market movers and fallen angels has widened. Market movers currently trade at 44 times trailing earnings, compared with 29 times for fallen angels, translating into a valuation premium of 1.5 times. This is close to the historical median of 1.6 times observed around previous market bottoms. Fourth, the number of fallen angels in the NSE 500 has increased from 61 in April to 80 in October. These stocks now account for 16 per cent of the index, an increase of four percentage points. "We see sufficient conditions in place for the pivot," it concluded. Entry timing matters lessElara back-tested the strategy by investing in 10 large-caps, 10 mid-caps and 10 small-caps at every month-end from six months before to six months after each low, giving 78 entry points, and held each position for 12 and 24 months. Of these, it found out that 60 stocks outperformed the NSE 500 over the following 12 months, and median excess return was positive at 12 of the 13 entry months. Thus, staggered accumulation around the low has been sufficient to capture the outperformance, the brokerage concludes. The principal risk to its theory remains a 2011-style domestic inflation-and-tightening cycle, making earnings revisions and margin recovery the key tests over the 12-24 month holding period.
