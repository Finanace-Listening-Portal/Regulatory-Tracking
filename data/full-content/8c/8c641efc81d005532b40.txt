Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); New Era Travel & Cargo Agencies Vs Commissioner of GST & Central Excise (CESTAT Chennai) Summary: The Chennai Bench of the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT) decided the appeal of M/s. New Era Travel & Cargo Agencies against service tax demands arising from its activities as a Customs House Agent (CHA) and an international air cargo booking intermediary. The dispute covered receipts described as airline and shipping-line incentives, commissions and discounts, cargo-related agency charges, and reimbursements collected from customers. The department had sought to tax different amounts under Business Auxiliary Services (BAS), Customs House Agent services and Goods Transport Agency (GTA) services, relying on the assessee’s records and the nature of its freight transactions. The assessee contended that it booked and sold cargo space on a principal-to-principal basis and that the difference between the price paid to airlines or shipping lines and the amount collected from customers represented a trading margin, not consideration for promotion or marketing services. It argued that airlines and shipping lines were not its clients for BAS purposes, and that discounts and incentives were not automatically taxable commissions. The assessee also challenged the use of profit-and-loss account entries as the basis for demands without demonstrating a corresponding taxable service. It relied on several judicial decisions addressing cargo-space transactions, freight differentials and the need to establish the actual nature of a service before imposing tax. On the CHA-related demand, the assessee maintained that sums recovered from customers for handling, agency and other third-party expenses were reimbursements rather than taxable consideration for its own services. It invoked the Supreme Court’s ruling in Union of India v. Intercontinental Consultants and Technocrats Pvt. Ltd. concerning the scope of valuation under Section 67 of the Finance Act, 1994 and Rule 5(1) of the Service Tax (Determination of Value) Rules, 2006. The assessee further disputed the invocation of the extended limitation period, arguing that the receipts were reflected in its accounts and that the controversy concerned the interpretation of service tax law rather than deliberate suppression. The original two-member Bench differed in its conclusions. The Judicial Member considered the BAS demand on discounts and incentives and the CHA demand on reimbursements unsustainable, while upholding the GTA demand and interest but setting aside penalties. The Technical Member considered that several factual matters, including the nature of the relationship with airlines and shipping lines, required further examination and proposed remand of the disputed issues. Because of this disagreement, questions concerning the BAS and CHA demands and the need for further fact-finding were referred to a third member. The separate opinions must therefore be read together with the later majority order, rather than as independent final dispositions. The third member agreed with the Judicial Member. The opinion emphasized that the department could not sustain demands by travelling beyond the allegations and grounds contained in the show cause notice. It also referred to the judicial treatment of cargo-space incentives and reimbursements, including the principle that taxability depends on the nature of the underlying transaction and the statutory charging and valuation provisions. The third member accepted that the demands under BAS and CHA services, and the associated interest and penalties, should be set aside; the Judicial Member’s position consequently became the majority view. By the final majority order pronounced on 29 May 2024, CESTAT set aside the service tax, interest and penalties imposed under Business Auxiliary Services and Customs House Agent services. The demand of service tax and interest under Goods Transport Agency services was upheld, but the penalties relating to GTA services were set aside. The appeal was partly allowed in those terms, with consequential relief, if any. The ruling is significant for disputes over airline and shipping-line incentives, freight differentials, reimbursement of third-party expenses and the requirement that service tax demands remain within the allegations set out in the show cause notice. Cases Discussed: Union of India v. Intercontinental Consultants and Technocrats Pvt. Ltd. — Supreme Court decision on reimbursements and service tax valuation.
