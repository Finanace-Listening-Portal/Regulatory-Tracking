Transactions up to Rs 2,000 and Person-to-Person payments will remain free. The MDR will be borne by merchants and cannot be passed on to consumers. More than 95% of P2M transactions by volume are below Rs 2,000. Advt The potential cash impact is concentrated in higher-value transactions. Only about 4% of P2M UPI transactions in FY26 were above Rs 2,000, but these transactions accounted for roughly two-thirds of P2M transaction value. The new charge therefore applies to a relatively small proportion of transactions but a much larger share of merchant-payment value.This could create an incentive for some merchants, particularly those operating on thin margins, to encourage cash or another payment method for larger purchases. A Rs 10,000 UPI payment would attract Rs 40 under the standard rate, while a Rs 50,000 payment would attract Rs 200. However, there is no evidence yet that merchants will systematically shift to cash once the MDR takes effect.The impact is also likely to depend on the relative convenience and cost of different payment methods. UPI gives merchants immediate digital records and reduces the need to handle and reconcile physical cash, while cash carries its own handling, security and deposit costs. These factors could limit any migration from UPI even where the MDR adds a transaction cost.India's payment data also show that cash and digital payments can grow simultaneously. Currency in circulation rose 11.9% in FY26 to about Rs 41.6 lakh crore, according to SBI Research, even as UPI transaction value rose 20.6% to around Rs 314 lakh crore and transaction volumes increased 30%. Advt The rise in currency in circulation, however, should not be interpreted as evidence that Indians are making more purchases with cash. Currency in circulation measures the stock of physical currency in the economy rather than the volume of cash transactions. SBI Research has also noted that the cash-to-GDP ratio declined to about 12.1% in FY26 from 14.4% in FY21, suggesting that cash has not regained its earlier relative importance despite the increase in absolute currency holdings.The frameworkThe MDR framework limits the scope for a broad shift to cash by keeping low-value UPI payments free. More than 95% of P2M transactions by volume are below Rs 2,000, while small merchants within the specified exemption threshold will also remain outside the standard MDR. This protects much of the everyday UPI ecosystem from the new charge. Advt The framework also provides concessional rates for several categories. Railways, telecom, insurance and fuel transactions above Rs 2,000 will attract a flat Rs 5 MDR, while capital-market transactions will attract a 0.02% MDR capped at Rs 300.The more important test will come after October 15. If high-value P2M UPI volumes decline materially while cash withdrawals or other payment methods increase, it would provide evidence of payment substitution. Without such data, it would be premature to conclude that the MDR is driving merchants or consumers back to cash.The policy is therefore more likely to change the economics of selected high-value digital transactions than to reverse India's broader digital-payment trend. UPI processed about 24 billion transactions in August, demonstrating the scale of the network before the MDR comes into force. Advt The new MDR may give cash a marginal advantage in some high-value merchant transactions, but a broad return to cash is far from established. The real indicator to watch after October 15 will be whether high-value UPI volumes fall and whether that decline is accompanied by an increase in cash usage.
