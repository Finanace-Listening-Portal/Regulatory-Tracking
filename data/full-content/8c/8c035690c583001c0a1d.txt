Summary: Paras Healthcare Limited’s red herring prospectus was listed by SEBI on 15 October 2026, with an abridged prospectus describing an offer of up to ₹1,450 crore: a fresh issue of up to ₹500 crore and an offer for sale of up to ₹950 crore. The document identifies an anchor investor bidding date of 19 October and a public offer period of 21–23 October 2026. The hospital operator reported FY26 consolidated revenue from operations of ₹1,605.952 crore and restated profit after tax of ₹43.834 crore, compared with a loss in FY25; its June 2026 quarter figures are also disclosed. The filing specifies debt-related uses for part of the fresh proceeds and details hospital capacity, operating metrics and business risks. This article examines those primary-source disclosures, distinguishes issuer proceeds from shareholder sale proceeds, and explains the general income-tax and GST framework for IPO equity transactions without discussing grey-market prices or projecting returns. The actual number of shares in certain offer fields depends on the final price and other finalisation steps, so the money amounts should not be mechanically converted into a share count. How Paras Healthcare Operates Paras Healthcare describes itself as a clinical-specialty-led hospital platform offering tertiary and quaternary healthcare. At 30 June 2026, the network comprised eight hospitals and 2,211 total beds, including 559 ICU beds. The disclosed operational-bed figure was 1,502, which is not interchangeable with the larger total bed-capacity figure. The network had 1,084 doctors and 1,937 nurses as of that date. These are dated operating facts rather than assumptions about future expansion. The hospitals are located in Gurugram, Panchkula, Patna, Darbhanga, Kanpur, Udaipur, Ranchi and Srinagar, across five states and one Union Territory. The group reports healthcare services as a single business segment and India as its single geographic segment. The document uses the term CONGOR for a collection of specialties including cardiac, oncology, neurosciences, gastroenterology, orthopaedics and sports injury, and renal sciences. These specialties accounted for 74.35% of revenue from operations for the June 2026 quarter. The company’s revenue comes from inpatient services, outpatient services, pharmacy products and other operating income. The disclosed payor categories include self-pay patients, insurance and third-party administrators, and government schemes and public-sector undertakings. This mix matters when examining reimbursement cycles, claims processing and receivables; it is not equivalent to a guarantee of timely collection from any particular payor. Where the Fresh Issue Proceeds Are Intended to Go The company proposes to allocate ₹3,209 million (₹320.90 crore) of net proceeds towards prepayment or scheduled repayment, in full or in part, of certain outstanding borrowings availed by the company. A further ₹541 million (₹54.10 crore) is earmarked for investment in wholly owned subsidiary PMHPL, which also operates the Srinagar hospital, for repayment or prepayment of that subsidiary’s borrowings. The remaining amount is described under general corporate purposes and is subject to the applicable cap and final offer price. Together, the two identified debt-related objects total ₹375 crore. This is an allocation disclosed in the filing, not proof that repayments have already occurred. The company’s borrowings at 30 June 2026 were ₹888.893 crore, so the prospective use of issue proceeds and the historical balance-sheet number should not be netted together as if they were contemporaneous completed transactions. The OFS proceeds of ₹950 crore belong to the selling shareholders and should not be counted as available for these company-level uses. Risks and Qualifications in the RHP Operating and financial risks The prospectus describes exposure to clinical and patient-care risks, the cost of healthcare delivery, dependence on qualified doctors and staff, and the operational challenges of hospitals in different locations. A significant part of revenue is generated by core clinical specialties. Changes in case mix, demand, reimbursement terms or hospital operations may therefore affect results. These are risk disclosures made by the issuer, not findings that a specific adverse event has occurred. Borrowings and capital expenditure are material to the financial position. At 31 March 2026, total borrowings of ₹854.101 crore were reported, and the June 2026 balance was ₹888.893 crore. The FY26 investing cash outflow of ₹239.410 crore was larger than FY26 operating cash flow of ₹163.512 crore. These amounts should be examined alongside financing cash flow, balance-sheet liabilities, cash balances and the debt-repayment objects rather than interpreted as a standalone solvency conclusion. Payor mix and concentration For FY26, government schemes and public-sector undertakings accounted for 41.28% of revenue from operations, insurance/TPA payors 24.84%, and self-pay patients 33.88%, as classified by the company. In the June 2026 quarter the corresponding shares were 43.63%, 25.07% and 31.30%. Reimbursement processing and collection patterns can differ across these groups. The filing also highlights geographic and hospital-level concentration, which should not be concealed by quoting only the overall network bed count. The abridged prospectus contains a disclosure that the name of one promoter-group member appears in the CIBIL wilful-defaulter database. That is a prospectus risk disclosure about the named individual and should not be rewritten as a finding that Paras Healthcare Limited itself has been declared a wilful defaulter. The full wording and context in the prospectus control any republication of this sensitive matter. IPO Tax and GST: What the Transaction Does and Does Not Trigger Allotment, sale and capital gains An IPO application and allotment are not, by themselves, a taxable capital gain for an ordinary investor merely because shares are allotted. A later transfer can generate a capital gain or loss, computed using the actual consideration, eligible cost and transfer expenses under the Income-tax Act, 2025. For equity shares listed on a recognised exchange, the long-term holding-period test is generally twelve months; the correct acquisition and transfer dates and the status of the holding must be determined from records. For qualifying short-term gains from specified equity transactions on which the required securities transaction tax (STT) is paid, the Income-tax Act, 2025 contains a special 20% rate framework, subject to the applicable conditions, surcharge and cess. For qualifying long-term listed-equity gains, section 198 of that Act provides a 12.5% rate on the relevant aggregate gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh, again subject to its STT requirements and statutory exceptions. The exemption threshold applies to aggregate eligible gains, not separately to each IPO application or share lot. Other cases may follow different rules, including non-resident and business-income situations. Why equity subscription is not charged to GST The CBIC’s sectoral GST FAQ explains that securities are excluded from the definitions of both goods and services under sections 2(52) and 2(102) of the CGST Act, 2017. Accordingly, GST is not charged merely on the issue or transfer of equity securities as such. This does not exempt all services connected with a securities transaction: brokerage and other taxable intermediary services can attract GST under their own classification and invoice rules. STT and stamp duty are distinct statutory levies and must not be described as GST.
