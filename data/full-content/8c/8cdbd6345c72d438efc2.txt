Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Birendra Kumar Agrawal Vs S2 Tech.com India Pvt Ltd and another (Supreme Court of India) Resolution Plan Cannot Be Invalidated Without Hearing Successful Applicant: Supreme Court Introduction The Supreme Court, in Birendra Kumar Agrawal v. S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd. and Another, 2026 INSC 1225, decided on 16 November 2026, set aside an NCLAT judgment invalidating a resolution plan without hearing the successful resolution applicant. The Court held that denying a hearing to the party most directly affected constituted a flagrant violation of natural justice. However, it did not decide the plan’s substantive validity. All objections were left open for consideration in separate appeals challenging the NCLT’s approval order. Background of the Dispute Insolvency proceedings were initiated against Manjeera Retail Holdings Private Limited, the principal borrower, and Manjeera Constructions Limited (MCL), its corporate guarantor, at the instance of Catalyst Trusteeship Limited. The NCLT admitted the insolvency application against MCL on 18 July 2023. Birendra Kumar Agrawal acted as its Resolution Professional. Following an invitation for expressions of interest, four prospective applicants submitted resolution plans. The plan submitted by Valentis Laboratories Private Limited (VLPL) received approval from 91.64% of the Committee of Creditors’ voting share on 10 June 2024. Minority Financial Creditor Challenged the Plan S2 Tech.com India Private Limited, a financial creditor holding 3.3% voting share, applied to the NCLT seeking rejection of VLPL’s plan. One objection was that VLPL had not remitted the earnest money deposit within the prescribed deadline, as required by the Request for Resolution Plan. The NCLT rejected the application on 30 July 2024. It reasoned that S2 Tech, having participated in the meeting approving the plan, lacked locus to challenge the majority’s approval. The NCLT nevertheless noted that it would independently examine the plan within the statutory framework. Subsequently, it approved VLPL’s resolution plan on 26 March 2025. Successful Applicant Was Not Made a Party S2 Tech appealed against the rejection of its objection application. Although it knew that the CoC had approved VLPL’s plan, it did not implead VLPL in the appeal. Even after the NCLT formally approved the plan, the successful resolution applicant was not brought on record. The Supreme Court was informed that an impleadment application had been filed, but it was not pursued and remained unnumbered when the appeal was decided. Nevertheless, on 19 March 2026, the NCLAT allowed S2 Tech’s appeal and invalidated VLPL’s resolution plan. It also issued directions to the Resolution Professional. Natural Justice Required a Hearing The Supreme Court identified a fundamental procedural defect: the NCLAT had passed an order directly prejudicing VLPL without affording it an opportunity to be heard. The successful applicant faced serious civil consequences from the invalidation of its plan. Its absence therefore could not be treated as an insignificant omission. The Court applied audi alteram partem, the principle requiring a hearing before adverse action. This violation was sufficient to render the NCLAT judgment unsustainable. The ruling demonstrates that substantive objections cannot justify deciding against an affected party without first permitting that party to respond. Subsequent Plan Approval Was Material The Supreme Court also noted that the NCLT’s 26 March 2025 approval order had a direct bearing on the appeal pending before the NCLAT. S2 Tech had not challenged that approval order, yet the NCLAT invalidated the plan while deciding its appeal against the earlier rejection of objections. The Supreme Court observed that the NCLAT had travelled beyond the issues raised in the original application. However, it expressly declined to examine the judgment’s validity on that broader ground. Its decision rested solely on the breach of natural justice. Why the Supreme Court Did Not Order Remand Ordinarily, denial of a hearing may lead to reconsideration after the affected party is joined. Here, however, separate appeals already challenged the NCLT’s plan-approval order. Those proceedings had been brought by another resolution applicant, homebuyers and a third party. The objection concerning timely payment of earnest money had also been raised there. The Supreme Court therefore considered a fresh hearing in the earlier appeal unnecessary. It preferred allowing the parties to raise their positions in the pending appeals directly challenging plan approval. What the Judgment Leaves Undecided The ruling does not establish that VLPL complied with the earnest money requirements, nor does it finally affirm the plan’s legality. It also does not endorse the proposition that a creditor loses every right to challenge a plan merely because it holds a minority voting share. The Court left all issues open for determination in the pending appeals. Accordingly, its decision must be understood as correcting a procedural illegality rather than resolving the commercial or statutory objections. Practical Implications The Supreme Court disposed of both appeals by setting aside the NCLAT judgment, with each party bearing its own costs. For creditors and insolvency professionals, the ruling highlights the importance of impleading the successful resolution applicant, addressing intervening approval orders, and pursuing objections in properly constituted proceedings. For resolution applicants, it confirms the right to a hearing before an approved plan is invalidated, while preserving substantive scrutiny through pending statutory appeals. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT OF SUPREME COURT OF INDIA 1. Challenge in these appeals filed under Section 62 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 20161, is to the judgment dated 19.03.2026 passed by the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal at Chennai2 in Company Appeal (AT) (CH) (Ins) No. 456 of 2024. 2. Civil Appeal No. 4171 of 2026 was filed against the said judgment by Birendra Kumar Agrawal, the Resolution Professional of Manjeera Constructions Limited (MCL), the corporate debtor, while Civil Appeal No. 4585 of 2026 was filed by Surya Treasure Island Pvt. Ltd., representing the consortium of Valentis Laboratories Pvt. Ltd. (VLPL) and Surya Treasure Island Pvt. Ltd. VLPL is the successful resolution applicant in the corporate insolvency resolution process of the corporate debtor, MCL. 3. By the impugned judgment dated 19.03.2026, the NCLAT allowed the appeal filed by respondent No. 1 in these appeals, viz., S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd., a financial creditor of the corporate debtor, MCL, and held that the order dated 30.07.2024 passed by the National Company Law Tribunal, Hyderabad Bench-II3, rejecting its plea in IA(IBC) 1565/2024 in Company Petition (IB) No. 320/7/HDB/2022 was without application of mind and that, in consequence, the resolution plan of VLPL was liable to be declared as invalid. Various directions were also issued by the NCLAT to Birendra Kumar Agrawal, the Resolution Professional of MCL. 4. By order dated 13.04.2026 passed in these appeals, this Court stayed the operation of the impugned judgment dated 19.03.2026. 5. Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process was initiated against Manjeera Retail Holdings Private Limited, the principal borrower, and its corporate guarantor, MCL, under Section 7 of the IBC at the instance of Catalyst Trusteeship Limited, a financial creditor. Owing to default in repayment by both, the financial creditor initiated proceedings against them under the provisions of the IBC. Insofar as the corporate guarantor, MCL, is concerned, Company Petition (IB) No. 320 of 2022 was filed by the said financial creditor and it was admitted by the NCLT on 18.07.2023. 6. S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd., respondent No. 1 herein, was a financial creditor of MCL with a 3.3% voting share in its Committee of Creditors (CoC). Pursuant to the Invitation for Expression of Interest issued on 10.01.2024 by Birendra Kumar Agrawal, the Resolution Professional, four prospective resolution applicants, viz., Vasavi Realty Pvt. Ltd., VLPL, Boorugu Infra Projects Pvt. Ltd. and Gajjala Yoganand, submitted their resolution plans. The resolution plan submitted by VLPL was approved by the CoC at its 12th Meeting held on 10.06.2024 with a 91.64% voting share. While so, S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd., respondent No 1, filed IA(IBC)1565/2024 in CP(IB) No. 320/7/HDB/2022 on 18.07.2024, seeking rejection of VLPL’s resolution plan. One of the grounds raised by it was that the earnest money deposit was not remitted by VLPL within the due date, as required by the Request for Resolution Plan (RFRP). This application was rejected by the NCLT on 30.07.2024, opining that, as S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd., with a 3.3% voting share in the CoC had participated in the meeting wherein VLPL’s resolution plan was approved, it had no locus to challenge such approval by the majority. Noting that it still had to examine the resolution plan within the parameters of the IBC, the NCLT dismissed the IA. 7. As noted above, by the date of passing of this order, VLPL’s resolution plan already stood approved by the CoC of the corporate debtor, MCL. Pursuant thereto, Birendra Kumar Agrawal, its Resolution Professional, filed IA(IBC)(Plan) No. 14 of 2024 in CP(IB) No. 320/7/HDB/2022 seeking approval of VLPL’s resolution plan under Sections 30(6) and 31(1) of the IBC. NCLT approved the said resolution plan, vide order dated 26.03.2025. 8. Meanwhile, S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd., respondent No. 1, filed Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 456 of 2024 assailing the order dated 30.07.2024 passed by the NCLT, dismissing IA(IBC)1565/2024. This appeal was filed by it on 14.09.2024. At that point of time, S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd., the appellant therein, was well aware of the fact that the CoC had already approved VLPL’s resolution plan but it did not implead VLPL, the successful resolution applicant, therein. Even after the approval of VLPL’s resolution plan by the NCLT, the appellant did not get the successful resolution applicant made a party to the appeal. We are informed that an application was, in fact, filed by S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd. in the said appeal to implead VLPL, the successful resolution applicant, but for some reason the same was not pursued and it was not even numbered by the time the appeal was disposed of on 19.03.2026. 9. In the judgment dated 19.03.2026 passed in the said appeal, the NCLAT went far beyond the issues raised by S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd. in its application in IA(IBC)1565/2024 before the NCLT. However, we are not inclined to go into the validity of the said judgment in that context. We may note that, by its final decision, the NCLAT invalidated the resolution plan submitted by VLPL, the successful resolution applicant, though VLPL was not even a party before it and despite S2 Tech.com India Pvt. Ltd., the appellant, failing to take steps to challenge the NCLT’s order dated 26.03.2025 approving VLPL’s resolution plan. 10. In essence, without even affording a hearing to the affected party, viz., VLPL, the successful resolution applicant, the NCLAT passed a judgment to its detriment and visiting it with dire civil consequences. This flagrant violation of the principle of audi alteram partem by the NCLAT is sufficient to render its judgment dated 19.03.2026 unsustainable in law. 11. We are informed that appeals have separately been filed against the NCLT’s order dated 26.03.2025 approving VLPL’s resolution plan. Gajjala Yoganand, one of the resolution applicants, filed Company Appeal No. 200 of 2025, while the homebuyers of MCL filed Company Appeal No. 7 of 2026. A third party, viz., Saasa Villa LLP, filed Company Appeal No. 348 of 2025. These appeals are pending consideration before the NCLAT. 12. We are further informed that the issue with regard to payment of the earnest money deposit within time by VLPL has also been raised in those appeals. As the NCLAT proceeded in ignorance of the approval of VLPL’s resolution plan by the NCLT, but that intervening factor had a direct impact on the subject appeal before it, we are not prepared to remand the matter to the NCLAT for consideration afresh after giving an opportunity of hearing to VLPL. We are of the view that it would suffice at this stage to set aside the NCLAT’s judgment dated 19.03.2026 on the sole ground that it was passed in blatant violation of the principles of natural justice, as the most vitally affected party thereby, viz., VLPL, was not given a hearing, and allow all issues to be raised in the pending appeals filed against the order dated 26.03.2025 passed by the NCLT, approving VLPL’s resolution plan. This, in our opinion, would serve the interest of justice while enabling all parties concerned to effectively put forth their respective stands in those duly constituted appeals. 13. The appeals are accordingly disposed of, setting aside the judgment dated 19.03.2026 passed by the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal at Chennai in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 456/2024. All issues are left open to be considered in the appeals pending before it, wherein the order dated 26.03.2025 passed by the National Company Law Tribunal, Hyderabad Bench-II, approving Valentis Laboratories Pvt. Ltd.’s resolution plan is subjected to challenge. Pending applications, if any, shall stand disposed of. Parties shall bear their own costs.
