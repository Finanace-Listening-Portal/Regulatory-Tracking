Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Priyanshu Malhotra Vs Inspector (Anti-Evasion) CGST Commissionerate (Punjab And Haryana High Court) Summary: The Punjab and Haryana High Court, in Priyanshu Malhotra Vs Inspector (Anti-Evasion), CGST Commissionerate, Ludhiana, granted regular bail to the petitioner in a complaint concerning alleged fraudulent availment of input tax credit (ITC) under Section 132(1)(b), (c) and (l) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. The Court considered the petitioner’s incarceration for four months and ten days, his willingness to furnish heavy surety and the fact that the demand raised by the tax authorities had already been stayed by a Division Bench of the High Court. The respondent alleged that the petitioner was the mastermind behind the alleged GST fraud and had created a company in his brother’s name while receiving all OTPs and updates concerning the company on his own mobile phone. It was further alleged that the petitioner had availed input tax credit without actual supply of goods. The respondent also expressed apprehension that the petitioner might leave the country during the pendency of the trial. The petitioner’s counsel submitted that the demand raised by the authorities had already been stayed by the Division Bench of the High Court and that further incarceration was unnecessary. Considering the period of custody, the petitioner’s willingness to furnish heavy surety and the subsisting stay on the demand, the Court held that no useful purpose would be served by keeping the petitioner in custody. Accordingly, the petition was allowed without expressing any opinion on the merits of the allegations. The High Court directed the petitioner’s release on furnishing heavy bail bonds and surety bonds to the satisfaction of the concerned Trial Court or Duty Magistrate, provided he was not required in any other case. The petitioner was directed to disclose his ordinary place of residence and mobile number, keep his mobile phone switched on, provide an alternative number in case of technical difficulties and mark his presence before the concerned Station House Officer every fifteen days. He was further prohibited from leaving the country without prior permission of the Court and was required to submit a copy of his passport or furnish an undertaking if he did not possess one. The Court directed compliance with the imposed conditions in addition to Section 483 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023. It expressly clarified that the bail order would not constitute a final expression of opinion on the merits of the case or provide a basis for claiming parity by any co-accused. The State was also granted liberty to seek cancellation of bail if the petitioner was subsequently found involved in similar activities. All pending applications were accordingly disposed of. FULL TEXT OF THE HIGH COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER ALOK JAIN, J. (Oral) 1. The present petition has been filed seeking grant of regular bail to the petitioner in complaint case No. COMA-83444/2026 filed by the respondent under Section 132(1)(b), (c) and (l) of the Central Goods & Services Tax Act, 2017. 2.. Contention of learned counsel for the petitioner has been recorded in the order dated 25.09.2026. 3. Learned counsel for the respondent submits that the petitioner is a master mind of the entire scam as he created a frivolous company in the name of his brother, whereas all the OTPs and updates were being received by the petitioner on his mobile phone. It is further submitted that the petitioner took the benefit of ITC on various transactions whereas no actual sale took place and in fact, goods were never supplied. 4. Learned counsel for the petitioner reiterates that the demand raised by the authorities has been stayed by the Division Bench of this Court and further incarceration of the petitioner is not required and the petitioner is ready to submit heavy surety, hence, he is entitled to the grant of concession of regular bail. 5. Learned counsel for the respondent further submits that surety will not be encashed till the pendency of the trial, in case, the petitioner does not abscond from the process of law as there is high risk of the petitioner leaving the country. 6. Heard learned counsel for the parties. 7. In light of the above and considering the fact that the petitioner is in custody for the last 04 months and 10 days and he is ready to submit heavy surety coupled with the fact that the demand raised by the authorities already stands stayed by the Division Bench of this Court, no useful purpose would be served by keeping the petitioner in custody. Accordingly, the petitioner is held entitled to the concession of regular bail. 8. Without commenting upon the merits of the case, the present petition stands allowed and the petitioner is ordered to be released on bail if not required in any other case on furnishing heavy bail bonds and surety bonds to the satisfaction of the trial Court/Duty Magistrate, concerned. The petitioner shall, however, be released on the following conditions: i The petitioner shall declare his ordinary place of residence and the mobile number used by him. ii The petitioner will not switch off his mobile and in case of any technical glitch, he has to give an alternate number, which will be available in his absence. iii The petitioner will mark his presence before the SHO concerned, after every 15 days and in case the SHO refuses to mark his presence, he is permitted to make an application before the Illaqa Magistrate, concerned. iv The petitioner will not leave the country without the prior permission of the Court, for which he will submit the copy of his passport also. However, in case the petitioner does not possess a passport, then he shall file an undertaking to the said effect before being released. 9. The petitioner shall abide by the terms and conditions as imposed in addition to Section 483 of BNSS, 2023. 10. However, nothing stated above shall be construed as a final expression of opinion on the merits of the case and this order shall not be considered as parity qua any other co-accused in any manner whatsoever. 11. It is further made clear that, in case, the petitioner is found involved in any such activity once again, the State is at liberty to promptly move an appropriate application for cancellation of bail detailing out the circumstances and violation of conditions of bail. 12. Pending application(s), if any, also stands disposed of.
