Summary: Zerodha Nifty LargeMidcap 250 Index Fund is an open-ended passive equity index scheme that seeks to track the Nifty LargeMidcap 250 Total Return Index, subject to tracking error. The index combines the Nifty 100 and Nifty Midcap 150 universes and targets an equal 50:50 allocation between the large-cap and mid-cap segments at rebalancing, so it is not simply a market-cap-weighted broad-market fund. The AMC’s official scheme page, updated with data as at 5 November 2026, showed a base expense ratio of 0.25%, exit load of nil, tracking error of 0.04% and no lock-in. It also displayed current holdings and named Kedarnath Mirajkar as fund manager. These are dated disclosures, not promises. The scheme carries market, mid-cap liquidity, concentration, index-reconstitution and tracking risks. Qualifying equity-oriented-fund gains are subject to the current Indian capital-gains framework, while IDCW, loss set-off, NRI withholding and plan-specific expenses need separate treatment. The investment objective is to invest in securities represented by that index in broadly the same proportions and to seek returns corresponding to the index before expenses, subject to tracking error. The SID expressly states that achievement is not assured. The benchmark combines the Nifty 100 large-cap universe and the Nifty Midcap 150 universe. Its methodology targets 50% weight for the large-cap segment and 50% for the mid-cap segment at periodic rebalancing. This differs from a conventional market-cap-weighted composite in which the much larger capitalisation of the largest companies could dominate the allocation. Between rebalances, price movement can cause the two segment weights to drift. A 50% mid-cap target creates materially different risk from a Nifty 50 or pure large-cap index. Mid-cap securities can have thinner liquidity, wider price gaps and greater sensitivity to valuation changes. The large-cap component does not guarantee low volatility, capital protection or positive returns. Passive management reduces security-selection discretion but does not remove market, methodology or implementation risk. It is normally affected by TER, cash holdings, index changes, corporate actions, transaction costs, taxes and implementation timing. Tracking error measures the variability of that return difference; it does not state whether the scheme will outperform or underperform on a future date. The 0.04% tracking-error figure on the AMC page is a dated statistical measure, not a maximum. A new investor should verify the period and methodology behind any displayed number and compare like with like. A low historic tracking error does not protect against a sudden index-rebalance cost, illiquid constituent, operational disruption or market closure. The fund can hold cash or cash equivalents for subscriptions, redemptions and operational needs. It may use index derivatives within the SID and regulatory limits. These tools can help implementation but create basis, counterparty, liquidity and rollover risks. The scheme’s objective remains replication, not discretion to avoid an index decline or exit an expensive segment. Purchase, Redemption and Plan Choices Change the Investor’s Outcome The scheme is open-ended, so eligible investors transact with the mutual fund at the applicable NAV under SEBI cut-off and realisation-of-funds rules. Direct and Regular plans invest in the same underlying portfolio but bear different expense structures and therefore have different NAVs. An investor should not compare a Direct-plan NAV with a Regular-plan purchase without considering the expense path. Growth and IDCW options, where offered under current documents, handle distributable surplus differently. IDCW is not a guaranteed dividend and normally reduces NAV when paid. The tax treatment of an IDCW receipt is different from capital gains on redemption. A selection should be based on the current KIM, application process and the investor’s facts, not on the word ‘income’ in an option label. The AMC page showed no lock-in and nil exit load. Redemption can still be delayed by non-business days, transaction cut-off, lien, pledge, KYC problem, bank-account validation, payment-system issue or an extraordinary regulatory measure. Investors should retain the allotment statement, folio, plan, option, transaction date, units, NAV, stamp duty and bank evidence for tax-lot reconstruction. Index Construction Creates Concentration and Rebalance Risks Although the benchmark contains 250 companies, economic concentration can remain significant. The top holdings and sectors can dominate returns, and the 50% mid-cap allocation makes the portfolio sensitive to liquidity and valuation conditions in that segment. A high constituent count is not the same as equal weighting or equal risk contribution. Index eligibility, free-float market capitalisation, review dates and corporate events can change constituents. The fund must implement announced changes near the effective date, when many other passive products may trade the same securities. This can produce market-impact costs or temporary tracking gaps. A constituent’s inclusion is a rules-based outcome, not an AMC credit endorsement. The riskometer should be read from the latest AMC disclosure. Equity index funds can be placed in the Very High category even when they are passively managed. The investor bears market risk and can lose capital. Neither the benchmark name nor replication objective gives a safety net, minimum return or guarantee from the sponsor, AMC, trustee, index provider, SEBI or AMFI. Indian Tax Depends on Holding Period and Investor Status For an equity-oriented mutual fund satisfying the statutory conditions, units held for not more than 12 months are generally short-term capital assets and units held for more than 12 months are generally long-term. Under the current regime, qualifying short-term gains are generally taxed at 20%, while qualifying long-term gains are generally taxed at 12.5% above the aggregate annual threshold of ₹1.25 lakh, subject to the applicable law, surcharge and cess. Securities transaction tax, stamp duty, grandfathering for older assets, set-off and carry-forward of losses, business classification and corporate-action adjustments can change the computation. IDCW is generally taxed in the investor’s hands at applicable rates and may be subject to withholding. Non-residents can face withholding and treaty questions that are not resolved by the resident summary. Tax rates should never be treated as a return estimate. The investor needs scheme-wise tax lots, acquisition NAV, units, stamp duty, redemption NAV, exit load if any and transaction dates. Capital-gain statements are helpful but should be reconciled with account statements and the return because switches and systematic transactions can create separate disposals.
