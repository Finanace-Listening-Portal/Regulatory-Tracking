The company expects credit costs to rise in the December quarter due to potential drought-related stress and seasonal factors, including festivals, but remains confident of keeping full-year credit costs within its guidance of 3-4%.The repayment requests remain at an early stage, and the company has not observed comparable stress in other geographies. Collections in flood-affected northern states have normalised, while expectations of a normal northeast monsoon provide some comfort, according to an Axis Securities report following the lender’s second-quarter results. Advt CreditAccess Grameen reported its best asset quality performance in 10 quarters, with gross non-performing assets falling to 1.79% in the September quarter from 2.18% in the previous quarter. Net non-performing assets declined to 0.59% from 0.76%, while X-bucket collection efficiency stood at 99.71%, broadly stable sequentially.The lender attributed the temporary deterioration in portfolio-at-risk accretion during August and September mainly to excessive rainfall, flooding in some operating regions and seasonal festivities, rather than weakening borrower behaviour. Collection trends in the affected regions had largely normalised by October. The company also added a 45-basis-point provisioning buffer, taking expected credit loss provisioning to 3.31%.Despite the emerging drought risk, CreditAccess Grameen raised its FY27 net interest margin guidance to 14.4-14.7% from 12.8-13.2% earlier. It lowered its cost-to-income ratio guidance to 29-30% from 33-35% and raised its return on assets guidance to 5.2-5.8% from 4-4.8%. Return on equity guidance was increased to 21-24% from 16-20%.Net interest margin expanded to 15.1% in the September quarter from 14.4% in the preceding quarter, supported by a 50-basis-point increase in portfolio yields and a 10-basis-point decline in the cost of funds. Net interest income rose 33% year on year, while pre-provision operating profit increased 39%. Net profit grew 298% year on year but rose just 1% sequentially. Advt Improved performanceThe lender began passing on some of its improved operating performance to borrowers through a 50-basis-point lending-rate reduction effective October. The cut applies across products except mortgages and two-wheeler loans, with its impact expected to flow through over four to six quarters. Management expects yields to moderate gradually as the rate reduction takes effect. Further pricing cuts will depend on the cost of funds and credit costs, and the company may defer additional reductions if credit costs rise significantly.Growth is increasingly being driven by retail finance as the lender shifts its portfolio beyond group microfinance loans. Retail finance assets under management grew 151% year on year, increasing their share of the total portfolio to 23.3% from 11.1% a year earlier. The company retained its FY27 assets under management growth guidance of 20-25%, while expecting microfinance growth of 10-12%. Advt Customer additions remained subdued in the first half, partly due to accelerated write-offs as the lender worked through an adverse credit cycle and sought to graduate quality customers to retail finance. Customer retention improved to 88% in September from 85% in March. The lender expects monthly customer additions to rise to more than one lakh in the second half from the current run rate of 80,000.The key near-term risk is whether drought-related repayment stress persists or spreads, pushing credit costs higher and weighing on profitability. Management believes its current risk-adjusted margins can absorb credit costs of up to 4.5-5% and sees scope to defend return on assets through calibrated pricing actions. However, the effectiveness of those actions remains untested if stress persists for longer.
