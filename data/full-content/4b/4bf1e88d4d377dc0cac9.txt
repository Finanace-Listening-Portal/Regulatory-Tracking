The first phase of this initiative commenced at the Sanghi Plant in Gujarat, with plans to introduce a fleet of 410 electric vehicles (EVs) by March 2027. This move marks a significant step in the company’s sustainability and green logistics strategy. The introduction of these electric trucks is projected to reduce carbon dioxide emissions by over 25,000 tonnes annually, displacing the equivalent of 11 million litres of diesel per year. By 2030, Adani Cement aims to achieve 10% of its logistics road volume through EVs, with an ambitious target of deploying over 2,000 electric trucks. This initiative aligns with the company’s commitment to achieving net-zero emissions and reducing Scope 3 emissions, which encompass indirect emissions from the supply chain. Vinod Bahety, CEO of Adani Group’s Cement Business, emphasised the strategic importance of this deployment, stating that electrifying the transport fleet not only reduces the carbon footprint but also mitigates the impact of diesel price volatility on logistics costs. The economic viability of heavy-duty EV trucks in India’s cement sector has been enhanced by a significant decrease in lithium-ion battery costs and solar tariffs, coupled with a substantial rise in diesel prices over the past decade. India, as the world’s second-largest cement producer, faces the challenge of carbon-intensive logistics due to the extensive movement of limestone, clinker, and cement. Adani Cement’s initiative addresses both environmental and economic aspects of this challenge, positioning the company as a leader in sustainable practices within the industry. Analysts view this development as a pivotal moment for the cement sector, potentially setting a precedent for other companies to follow suit in reducing their carbon footprint through innovative logistics solutions.
