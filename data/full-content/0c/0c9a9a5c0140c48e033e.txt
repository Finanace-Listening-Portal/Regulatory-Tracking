Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Assistant Commissioner Vs Siemens Gamesa Renewable Power Private Limited (Supreme Court of India) The Supreme Court of India, in Assistant Commissioner Vs Siemens Gamesa Renewable Power Private Limited, dismissed the Special Leave Petitions filed against the High Court judgment concerning the GST classification of Wind Turbine Generators (WTGs). The dispute arose from contracts executed by Siemens Gamesa for designing, manufacturing, supplying, assembling, erecting and installing WTGs during 2017–18 and 2018–19. The company treated the transactions as composite supplies of goods and services and paid GST at 5%. The tax authorities considered the installed generators to be immovable property and classified the contracts as works contracts attracting GST at 18%. Assessment orders dated 30 December 2020 and subsequent penalty orders were upheld by the appellate authority, leading to proceedings before the Andhra Pradesh High Court. The High Court examined the technical characteristics of WTGs, including their components, foundations, erection and dismantling procedures. It observed that the generators comprised separately manufactured components transported to the installation site and assembled on foundations using bolts and other fastening mechanisms. Although the foundations were embedded in the earth, the generators themselves could be dismantled, transported and reinstalled elsewhere without damage. The Court considered the principles governing movable and immovable property, including the nature and purpose of annexation, intention of the parties, functionality, permanency and marketability. Relying particularly on the Supreme Court’s decisions in Commissioner of Central Excise, Ahmedabad Vs Solid and Correct Engineering Works and Bharti Airtel Ltd. Vs Commissioner of Central Excise, it concluded that attachment to the earth for operational stability did not necessarily convert movable equipment into immovable property. The High Court accordingly held that WTGs constituted movable goods and that their supply and installation could not be classified as works contracts involving immovable property. It set aside the assessment, appellate and penalty orders and remanded the assessments to the tax authority for reconsideration in accordance with its judgment. Subsequently, the Supreme Court passed a short order condoning the delay and dismissing the Special Leave Petitions, stating that it found no grounds to interfere with the High Court’s judgment and orders. Pending applications were also disposed of. The Supreme Court did not provide independent substantive reasoning on GST classification in the supplied order; the detailed legal findings concerning movability and composite supply belong to the High Court judgment.
