The process also exposes certain assets acquired during the bankruptcy proceedings to creditors' claims, while providing statutory protection to specified personal assets. Discharge can be sought after one year, but is not automatiThe National Company Law Tribunal's (NCLT) decision to declare Venugopal Dhoot bankrupt marks a significant shift in the recovery proceedings against the former Videocon Industries promoter, transferring control of his personal bankruptcy estate to a trustee and restricting his ability to conduct business and manage his financial affairs.The November 5 order, passed by the Mumbai bench on a petition filed by State Bank of India (SBI), stems from personal guarantees Dhoot furnished for credit facilities extended to Videocon Industries Ltd (VIL) and facilities originally extended to Videocon Telecommunications Ltd. SBI's claim, including interest and penalties, stood at Rs 12,668.06 crore as of September 30, 2026. Advt The immediate consequence is that Dhoot loses control over the property forming part of his bankruptcy estate. The estate has vested in bankruptcy trustee Asish Narayan, who is responsible for taking possession of the relevant assets, examining Dhoot's financial affairs, inviting creditors' claims and administering the estate for distribution among them.Personal assets to come under trustee's controlUnder the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), bankruptcy is a formal process through which an individual's assets are administered and distributed among creditors. Unlike corporate insolvency, which deals with a company's assets and liabilities, this process applies to Dhoot personally because he had guaranteed the repayment of loans taken by Videocon group companies.Section 154 of the IBC provides for the bankrupt's estate to vest in the bankruptcy trustee immediately upon appointment. Under Section 155, the estate includes property belonging to or vested in the individual on the bankruptcy commencement date, as well as certain property subsequently acquired before discharge.The trustee will examine Dhoot's property holdings, investments and other financial interests to establish the assets available for creditors. He can take possession and control of the property, books, papers and records relating to the estate. Dhoot and others holding such property or documents are required to deliver them to the trustee. Advt The process is not necessarily restricted to assets held on the date of the bankruptcy order. Under Section 159, the trustee can claim certain assets acquired or inherited by Dhoot during the bankruptcy process, before his discharge, by issuing the prescribed notice.However, not everything Dhoot owns automatically becomes part of the estate. The IBC excludes specified assets, including unencumbered tools and equipment necessary for personal use or work, essential household furniture and provisions, certain personal ornaments, eligible life insurance policies and pension plans, and a single dwelling unit subject to the prescribed value limit. Property held in trust for another person and specified provident fund, pension and gratuity dues are also excluded. Advt The bankruptcy order does not automatically extend to assets independently owned by Dhoot's family members. The trustee's authority is over the estate legally belonging to Dhoot, although ownership and beneficial interests may need to be examined where disputed.Restrictions on business and financial activitiesThe bankruptcy declaration also imposes restrictions under Section 141 of the IBC, significantly curtailing Dhoot's ability to return to business management.He cannot act as a director of any company or directly or indirectly participate in its promotion, formation or management. The restriction applies from the bankruptcy commencement date and remains in force until the bankruptcy order is recalled or modified, or he obtains a discharge. Advt Dhoot cannot create any charge on his bankruptcy estate or take further debt without the prior sanction of the bankruptcy trustee. He is also required to inform his business partners that he is undergoing bankruptcy proceedings and disclose this status before entering into prescribed financial or commercial transactions.He cannot maintain legal proceedings relating to bankruptcy debts without the prior permission of the adjudicating authority. The Code also bars him from travelling overseas without the NCLT's permission.These restrictions are intended to prevent the bankrupt from dealing with assets independently, accumulating further liabilities or taking actions that could prejudice creditors while the estate is being administered.Trustee to assess assets and invite creditors' claimsThe next stage will involve establishing the extent of Dhoot's assets and liabilities and determining the claims of creditors.The NCLT has directed Dhoot to submit a statement of his financial position to the trustee within seven days of the bankruptcy commencement date. The trustee must notify creditors within 10 days and issue a public notice inviting claims.Within 90 days, the trustee must submit a preliminary report to the NCLT and the committee of creditors, setting out the position of the estate and the proposed course of administration. Quarterly progress reports will follow.After verifying creditors' claims and identifying the assets available for distribution, the trustee will realise the estate in accordance with the IBC.The proceeds will be distributed under Section 178, which prescribes a statutory order of priority. The costs and expenses of the bankruptcy process rank first, followed by workmen's dues for the preceding 24 months and debts owed to secured creditors. Employee dues, government dues and other debts, including unsecured claims, follow in the prescribed order.The extent of SBI's recovery will depend on the assets available in Dhoot's personal estate, their valuation and the claims admitted in the process. The Rs 12,668.06 crore claim represents the bank's stated dues, not the value of Dhoot's personal assets or the amount that will necessarily be recovered.What happens after one year?Bankruptcy does not automatically end after one year. Under Section 138, the bankruptcy trustee must apply to the NCLT for a discharge order on the expiry of one year from the bankruptcy commencement date. If the administration and distribution of the estate are completed earlier and the committee of creditors approves the completion, the trustee can seek discharge within seven days of that approval.A discharge under Section 139 releases the bankrupt from bankruptcy debts, subject to statutory exceptions. These include debts incurred through fraud or breach of trust and excluded debts under the Code. It does not reverse the administration of the estate or undo distributions already made to creditors.Until the discharge order is passed, Dhoot remains an undischarged bankrupt and the restrictions on his business and financial activities continue to apply.The declaration is a civil insolvency proceeding and does not amount to a criminal conviction or an order for imprisonment. Nor does it mean that SBI has already recovered its dues or that all Dhoot's assets have been sold. It places his personal estate under the administration of a trustee, with the eventual recovery depending on the assets identified, their realisation and the claims established by creditors.
