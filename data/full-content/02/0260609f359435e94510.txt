There is a 60 per cent probability of a 50 bps increase and a 40 per cent probability of a 25 bps hike. Within the 50 bps scenario, a calibrated tightening stance carries a 55 per cent probability, while a neutral stance has a 5 per cent probability. The expected vote split is 5-1, according to Nomura. Advt Food price trackers point to the sharpest monthly increases in several years. November consumer price inflation is estimated at 6.4 per cent year on year, compared with 6.1 per cent in October. Inflation in the October-December quarter is projected to average 6.3 per cent, against the RBI's forecast of 6 per cent. Sticky cereal and pulses prices could keep headline inflation above 6 per cent for at least two quarters, compared with the central bank's earlier expectation of one quarter.Core inflation momentum has also picked up as companies pass on higher input costs to consumers. Third-quarter GDP growth is expected to exceed 7.5 per cent, against the RBI's forecast of 7.2 per cent. High-frequency indicators also remained stable in the early part of the fourth quarter.GDP growth forecastThe RBI could raise its FY27 GDP growth forecast by 40 basis points to around 7.5 per cent from 7.1 per cent. Its FY27 CPI inflation forecast could be revised upwards by 10 basis points to 5.3 per cent. Quarterly inflation projections could be raised to above 6 per cent for the next two quarters, while the core inflation forecast is expected to remain unchanged at 4.4 per cent.A 50 bps hike would constitute frontloaded tightening aimed at anchoring inflation expectations. A 25 bps increase would be less disruptive given market expectations, but could risk being seen as insufficient if headline inflation remains elevated. A 50 bps hike accompanied by a neutral stance carries a 5 per cent probability and could signal the end of the rate-hiking cycle, diluting the message of frontloaded tightening. Advt The expected terminal repo rate is 6 per cent after the December policy, followed by a pause as core inflation and economic growth are expected to moderate after the first quarter. However, another 25 bps hike in February or April remains a possibility. The RBI's guidance will be crucial. Communicating the December increase as frontloaded tightening would reduce the likelihood of a February hike, while an approach aimed at catching up with the higher inflation trajectory could keep the February meeting open for another increase.
