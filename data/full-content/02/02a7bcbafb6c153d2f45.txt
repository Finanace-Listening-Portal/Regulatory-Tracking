Advt Foreign currency assets (FCAs), the largest component of India's foreign exchange reserves, declined by USD 266 million to USD 583.186 billion.Expressed in dollar terms, foreign currency assets also include the effect of appreciation or depreciation of non-US currencies, such as the euro, pound and yen, held in the foreign exchange reserves.The Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) declined by USD 67 million to USD 18.451 billion, while India's reserve position with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) fell by USD 15 million to USD 4.743 billion during the reporting week.Despite the latest weekly decline, India's foreign exchange reserves remained USD 12.794 billion higher compared to the corresponding period last year and USD 9.791 billion higher than their level at the end of March 2026.Foreign currency assets registered a year-on-year increase of USD 22.586 billion, while gold reserves declined by USD 9.663 billion during the same period.Since the end of March 2026, foreign currency assets have increased by USD 30.903 billion, while gold reserves have declined by USD 20.877 billion.Foreign exchange reserves serve as an important buffer against external economic shocks, helping the country meet its external payment obligations and enabling the central bank to manage excessive volatility in the foreign exchange market.
