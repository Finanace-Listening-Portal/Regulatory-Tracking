Articles 2 min read India, Thailand discuss linking payment systems, local currency trade settlement India and Thailand are exploring the potential linkage of UPI with Thailand's PromptPay to facilitate trade. They are also focusing on local currency settlement and fintech cooperation between their central banks. This initiative aims to reduce transaction costs and promote bilateral trade. Additionally, both countries discussed enhancing the use of the ASEAN-India Trade in Goods Agreement. PTI Published On Nov 17, 2026 at 08:05 AM IST var news_msid = 135907918; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook New Delhi, India and Thailand on Monday discussed the possibility of linking UPI (Unified Payments Interface) with the Thai PromptPay, local currency settlement and fintech cooperation between the central banks of the two countries.The move would help promote two-way trade and cut down transaction costs for businesses."The two sides also deliberated on payment and financial connectivity, including the linkage of India's UPI with Thailand's PromptPay, local currency settlement and FinTech cooperation between the Reserve Bank of India and the Bank of Thailand," the commerce ministry said in a statement. Advt These issues were deliberated upon at the 14th Meeting of the India-Thailand Joint Trade Committee (JTC) here.They also discussed enhancing the utilisation of the ASEAN-India Trade in Goods Agreement (AITIGA) and the India-Thailand Early Harvest Scheme.Both sides also explored a mechanism for the annual exchange of bilateral trade data and the data on the utilisation of preferences to identify areas for enhancing preferential trade and addressing implementation-related challenges, it said.Thailand is a member of the ASEAN bloc, a Southeast Asian grouping.The two sides discussed ways to ensure effective market access for both sides and resolve issues faced by traders, including market access for agricultural, livestock and marine products, sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) measures and certification processes, quality and regulatory requirements, and the removal of non-tariff barriers.The bilateral trade stood at USD 21.49 billion in 2025-26, registering a growth of 12.6 per cent over the previous year. Thailand accounted for 16.7 per cent of India's total trade with ASEAN.In a separate statement, the Commerce Ministry said India and Belarus discussed the Roadmap for Cooperation for 2027-2031 as an important step towards identifying future priorities and providing direction to bilateral economic engagement."Key sectors discussed included industry, financial and banking services, agriculture and food processing, pharmaceuticals, healthcare, tourism, education, culture, science and technology, construction and customs cooperation," it said. Advt Speaking at the meeting of the India-Belarus Inter-Governmental Commission on Trade, Economic, Scientific, Technological and Cultural Cooperation, Minister of State for Commerce and Industry Jitin Prasada encouraged businesses to explore joint ventures, technology partnerships, investment opportunities and greater business-to-business engagement.
