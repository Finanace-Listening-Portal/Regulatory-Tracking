Ramakrishnaraju Yadav (GSTAT Visakhapatnam) Summary: The GST Appellate Tribunal, State Bench Vishakhapatnam (“GSTAT”) in Padala Satyanarayana Reddy v. G. Ramakrishnaraju Yadav, Joint Commissioner (GST Appeals), Guntur & Ors. [APL/3/VSK/2026 dated October 14, 2026] set aside the order of the First Appellate Authority rejecting the refund of excess interest paid under Section 50(1) of the CGST Act, 2017 on the portion of tax discharged through the electronic credit ledger, on the fresh ground of limitation raised without notice to the assessee, and held that the claim must be examined in light of the retrospective amendment to Section 50(1) with effect from July 01, 2017, and that limitation must be freshly worked out after excluding the period from March 01, 2020 to February 28, 2022 in terms of Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax. Facts: Padala Satyanarayana Reddy (“the Appellant”) furnished its GST returns for FY 2017-18 and FY 2018-19 after the due dates and discharged the tax liability partly by utilising input tax credit (“ITC”) from the electronic credit ledger and partly by debiting the electronic cash ledger. Along with the tax, the Appellant paid interest under Section 50 of the CGST Act on the entire tax liability, without restricting it to the portion paid through the electronic cash ledger, as the law then stood. Section 112 of the Finance Act, 2021 subsequently substituted the proviso to Section 50(1) with retrospective effect from July 01, 2017, restricting interest to the portion of tax paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger. Notification No. 16/2021-Central Tax dated June 01, 2021 appointed June 01, 2021 as the date on which Section 112 came into force. The Appellant claimed refund of the excess interest (CGST ₹3,97,127 and SGST ₹3,74,560, aggregating ₹7,71,687), relying on the Madras High Court ruling in Refex Industries Ltd.The refund sanctioning authority rejected the claim by order dated May 07, 2021 as premature, since no notification giving effect to the amendment was available at that time. In appeal, the Joint Commissioner (GST Appeals), Guntur, dismissed the appeals vide Order-in-Appeal dated March 31, 2022 on an altogether different ground, namely that the refund claims were barred by limitation under Section 54 of the CGST Act. The Appellant had neither been put to notice on this ground nor heard on it. Aggrieved, the Appellant filed appeals before the GSTAT. Contentions: Appellant The retrospective substitution of the proviso to Section 50(1) from July 01, 2017 made the interest paid on the ITC-discharged portion legally not payable. Notification No. 16/2021-CT merely fixed the commencement date and did not dilute the deeming of retrospectivity. The Gujarat High Court in Sumilon Polyster Ltd. v. Union of Indiahad also noticed that the substituted proviso operates from July 01, 2017. The refund authority erred in ignoring CBIC’s administrative instructions dated September 18, 2020 directing recovery of interest only on net cash tax liability. The First Appellate Authority introduced limitation as a new and decisive ground without notice or hearing, which violates the principles of natural justice. Limitation under Section 54 could not be computed without giving effect to Notification No. 13/2022-CT dated July 05, 2022, which excludes March 01, 2020 to February 28, 2022. On such recomputation, the refund applications are within time. Revenue The refund claim was rightly rejected as premature in the absence of a notification giving effect to the amendment. The Revenue sought to justify the First Appellate Authority’s order on the footing that Notification No. 13/2022-CT was not available when the appeals were decided. It admitted that no notice on limitation was issued and prayed that the matter be remanded to the First Appellate Authority for fresh consideration. Issues: Whether the First Appellate Authority could reject the refund claim on a fresh ground of limitation under Section 54 of the CGST Act without putting the Appellant to notice and affording an opportunity of being heard? Whether limitation for the refund claim can be determined merely with reference to the date of the original rejection, without giving effect to the exclusion of the period from March 01, 2020 to February 28, 2022 under Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax? Whether the refund of excess interest could be denied on the ground that no notification giving effect to the amendment was available, when Section 50(1) stands substituted with retrospective effect from July 01, 2017? Whether the matter warrants an open-ended remand, or can be decided by the Tribunal with limited directions for verification and quantification of the refund? Held: The GSTAT, State Bench Vishakhapatnam in APL/3/VSK/2026 held as under: Observed that, the original authority rejected the claim because no notification giving effect to the amendment was available, whereas the First Appellate Authority rejected it on a different ground, namely limitation under Section 54, which was never the basis of the original rejection. Noted that, the First Appellate Authority introduced limitation as a fresh and decisive ground without notice to the Appellant, a fact admitted by the Revenue’s Authorised Representative. Held that, when an appellate or adjudicating authority proposes to decide a matter on a ground not put to the affected party, reasonable notice and an effective opportunity to respond must be given. Failure to do so violates natural justice and vitiates the finding on limitation, relying on Saci Allied Products Ltd. v. CCE, Meerut[(2005) 7 SCC 159], Reckitt & Colman of India Ltd. v. CCE [1996 (88) ELT 641 (SC)] and Mukherjee v. Union of India [(1990) 4 SCC 594]. Observed that, the amendment is not merely prospective from the date of notification but was declared by Parliament to operate retrospectively from July 01, 2017. Interest under Section 50(1) is therefore leviable only on the portion of tax paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger, and refund entitlement must be determined under the law as retrospectively amended. Held that, the original authority’s reasoning cannot be sustained, particularly in view of CBIC’s administrative instructions dated September 18, 2020, and the Appellant’s earlier voluntary payment of interest cannot by itself validate an amount that was not legally payable. Noted that, Notification No. 13/2022-CT dated July 05, 2022, issued under Section 168A, excludes March 01, 2020 to February 28, 2022 for computing limitation for refund applications under Sections 54 and 55, with effect from March 01, 2020, and that the Delhi High Court in Mahalaxmi Exportsand the Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh High Court in Bharat Oil Traders have applied it to set aside refund rejections. Held that, limitation “has to be freshly worked out” and cannot be decided merely on the date of original rejection. At the same time, the notification does not dispense with limitation altogether, and the actual relevant date and the date of filing the refund application must still be examined. Held that, an open-ended remand sought by the Revenue would serve no useful purpose, as the power of remand is meant to cure a genuine deficiency requiring factual adjudication and should not be exercised mechanically. Limited verification of the interest actually paid and the portion attributable to ITC is not a remand for reconsidering legal entitlement. Directed that, the competent authority shall consider the claim in accordance with the retrospectively amended Section 50(1), Section 54 and Notification No. 13/2022-CT, recompute limitation after giving effect to the exclusion, examine whether the case falls within the exception in the proviso (proceedings under Sections 73 or 74), verify and quantify the refund, and pass a consequential order after giving the Appellant a reasonable opportunity of being heard, within three months from receipt of the order. Hence, the appeal is allowed and the Order-in-Appeal is set aside insofar as it rejects the refund claim solely on the ground of limitation, with the matter sent back to the competent authority for consequential action in the limited manner directed. Our Comments: Section 50 – interest on net cash liability. Section 50(1) of the CGST Act requires a person who fails to pay tax within the prescribed period to pay interest on his own for the period of default. As originally enacted, it did not distinguish between tax paid through ITC and tax paid in cash, and departments therefore computed interest on the gross liability. Following the recommendations of the GST Council, Section 100 of the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019 inserted a proviso limiting interest to the portion paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger, effective September 01, 2020 vide Notification No. 63/2020-CT dated August 25, 2020. The GST Council, in its 39th meeting held on March 14, 2020, recommended that this be given effect from July 01, 2017, and CBIC’s instruction dated September 18, 2020 directed field formations to recover interest only on net cash liability for July 01, 2017 to August 31, 2020 and to keep notices raised on gross liability in the call book. Section 112 of the Finance Act, 2021 finally substituted the proviso with effect from July 01, 2017, brought into force from June 01, 2021. The proviso carves out cases where the return is furnished after commencement of proceedings under Section 73 or Section 74 (and now Section 74A), which is why the Tribunal has asked the authority to examine this exception as well. Section 54 and Notification No. 13/2022-CT – limitation. Section 54(1) permits a claim for refund of tax, interest or any other amount paid to be filed before the expiry of two years from the relevant date. Notification No. 13/2022-CT dated July 05, 2022, issued under Section 168A, followed the Supreme Court’s order dated January 10, 2022 in In Re: Cognizance for Extension of Limitation [Suo Motu Writ Petition (C) No. 3 of 2020] and excludes the period from March 01, 2020 to February 28, 2022 for computing limitation under Sections 54 and 55. The ruling is a useful reminder that refund orders passed before July 05, 2022 and rejected as time-barred must be re-tested against this exclusion. Pari materia judgments. On the substantive issue, the Madras High Court in Refex Industries Ltd. v. Assistant Commissioner of CGST & Central Excise [W.P. Nos. 23106 and 23108 of 2019 dated January 06, 2020] held that interest under Section 50 can be levied only on belated cash payment and not on ITC lying to the credit of the assessee, treating the proviso as clarificatory. The Gujarat High Court in Sumilon Polyster Ltd. v. Union of India considered the challenge to interest on gross liability in light of the proviso substituted with effect from July 01, 2017. On limitation, the Delhi High Court in Megicon Impex Pvt. Ltd. v. Commissioner of CGST, Delhi West [W.P.(C) 6556/2020 dated February 06, 2023] directed processing of a refund as the benefit of the notification had not been accorded, and in Zenon Analytics Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India [W.P.(C) 8338/2022 dated March 21, 2024] restored refund claims rejected solely on limitation by orders passed before the notification was issued. The Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh High Court in Bharat Oil Traders v. Assistant Commissioner [dated December 30, 2025] likewise set aside an appellate order that had not considered the notification. Contrary and qualifying views. The Telangana High Court in Megha Engineering and Infrastructures Ltd. v. Commissioner of Central Tax [W.P. No. 44517 of 2018 dated April 18, 2019] had earlier held that interest is payable on gross liability. That view has since been overtaken by legislation, and the Court later allowed the batch of petitions in view of the Finance Act, 2021 amendment, directing the authorities to hear the petitioners and quantify interest as per the amended provision. On limitation, the Karnataka High Court in Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Ltd. v. Union of India [W.P. No. 23995 of 2022 dated January 13, 2023] read the notification together with the Supreme Court’s order and held that where limitation expired during the excluded period, the claimant gets 90 days from March 01, 2022 or the longer balance period, whichever is greater. This shows that the exclusion is not a blanket waiver, a caution the Tribunal has itself voiced. Takeaway. Taxpayers who filed returns late for FY 2017-18 onwards and paid interest on the ITC-discharged portion should re-examine whether a refund can still be claimed, working out the relevant date and the COVID exclusion carefully and keeping the cash and credit ledger split ready, since the authority will verify the portion attributable to ITC. Where a refund has been rejected as premature or as time-barred, this ruling provides a strong basis to seek reconsideration. Appellate authorities, on the other hand, must put the assessee to notice before relying on a ground that the original order did not adopt. As this is an order of a State Bench of the Tribunal, it carries persuasive value and the outcome will ultimately turn on verification of facts by the competent authority. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF GSTAT VISAKHAPATNAM 1. Facts of the case 1. The present appeals are directed against the common Order-in-Appeal Nos. VIZ-GST-000-APP-065 TO 066-21-22 43/VSK/2026 & 44/VSK/2026 dated31-3-2022 passed by the Joint Commissioner (GST- APPEALS) Guntur, whereby the appeals filed by the appellant against rejection of its refund claim of interest for the financial years 2017-18 and 2018-19 came to be dismissed. 2. The appellant had furnished its GST returns for the financial year 2017-18 and 2018-19 after the prescribed due dates and had discharged the tax liability partly by utilisation of input tax credit available in the electronic credit ledger and partly by debit to the electronic cash ledger. 3. Along with the tax, the appellant also discharged interest under Section 50 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (“the CGST Act”), calculating the interest on the tax liability without restricting the computation to the portion discharged through the electronic cash ledger in terms of Section 50 of CGST Act 2017 as was prevailing. 4. Subsequently, Parliament amended Section 50(1) by Section 112 of the Finance Act, 2021 whereby the interest liability is restricted to the portion of tax which is paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger. The substituted proviso expressly provided that it “shall be deemed to have been substituted with effect from the 1st day of July, 2017.” 5. The amended Section 50 of CGST Act 2017 reads as follows: ”Section 50. Interest on delayed payment of tax.- (1) Every person who is liable to pay tax in accordance with the provisions of this Act or the rules made thereunder, but fails to pay the tax or any part thereof to the Government within the period prescribed, shall for the period for which the tax or any part thereof remains unpaid, pay, on his own, interest at such rate, not exceeding eighteen per cent., as may be notified by the Government on the recommendations of the Council: 1Provided that the interest on tax payable in respect of supplies made during a tax period and declared in the return for the said period furnished after the due date in accordance with the provisions of section 39, except where such return is furnished after commencement of any proceedings under section 73 or section 74 3[or section 74A] in respect of the said period, shall be levied on that portion of the tax that is paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger. (2) The interest under sub-section (1) shall be calculated, in such manner as may be prescribed, from the day succeeding the day on which such tax was due to be paid. 2[(3) Where the input tax credit has been wrongly availed and utilised, the registered person shall pay interest on such input tax credit wrongly availed and utilised, at such rate not exceeding twenty-four per cent. as may be notified by the Government, on the recommendations of the Council, and the interest shall be calculated, in such manner as may be prescribed] 6. The amended proviso provides, inter alia, that where a return is furnished after the due date, interest shall be payable only on that portion of the tax which is paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger, except where the return is furnished after commencement of proceedings under Section 73 or Section 74 in respect of the relevant period. 7. The appellant filed a refund claim seeking refund of the excess interest paid by it, contending that, in view of the retrospective amendment, interest was not payable on the portion of tax discharged through utilisation of input tax credit. The appellant has placed reliance on the judgment of the Hon’ble Madras High Court in the case of M/s. Reflex Industries Limited V. Assistant Commissioner of CGST & Central Excise in W.P.Nos. 123106 and 23108 of 2019 dated 6-1-2020 before the original Authority. 8. The original refund sanctioning authority however rejected the claim principally on the ground that, at the relevant point of time, there is no notification giving effect to the amendment was available and, therefore, the claim for refund cannot be considered. 9. Aggrieved by the said orders, the appellant preferred separate appeals before the First Appellate Authority under Section 107 of the CGST Act. 10. During the pendency of the said appeals before the first appellate authority Notification No. 16/2021- Central Tax dated 1 June 2021 came to be issued appointing 1 June 2021 as the date from which Section 112 of the Finance Act, 2021 will come into force. 11. The First Appellate Authority, however, rejected the appeals on a different ground, namely, that the refund applications were barred by limitation under Section 54 of the CGST Act. It is not in dispute that the appellant was not specifically put to notice that limitation under Section 54 was proposed to be made the basis for rejection, nor was the appellant afforded an opportunity to make submissions on the question of limitation and, in particular, on the effect of the retrospective amendment to Section 50. 12. It is pertinent to refer another important notification issued by the Government extending the limitation on account of Covid by invoking power under Section 168A of CGST Act 2017 vide Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax dated 5 July 2022. 2. Submissions of the learned Authorised Representative of the Appellant 1. The learned counsel appearing for the appellant submitted that the impugned Order-in-Appeal is unsustainable both on facts and in law. He submitted that the appellant had discharged its GST liability for the relevant tax periods partly by utilisation of eligible Input Tax Credit and partly by payment through the electronic cash ledger. Since the returns were filed belatedly, interest under Section 50(1) of the CGST Act was paid by the appellant on the entire tax liability, including the portion which was discharged through the electronic credit ledger. 2. Learned counsel submitted that, subsequently, Parliament, by Section 112 of the Finance Act, 2021, substituted the proviso to Section 50(1) of the CGST Act and expressly provided that the substituted proviso “shall be deemed to have been substituted with effect from the 1st day of July, 2017.” The amended provision restricts the levy of interest, in cases covered by the proviso, to that portion of the tax which is paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger. The legislative intent was therefore clearly to treat the net cash liability as the basis for levy of interest retrospectively from 01.07.2017. The GST Council records also expressly record that the retrospective amendment was intended to remove ambiguity and disputes concerning levy of interest on gross or net tax liability. 3. He further submitted that Notification No. 16/2021-Central Tax dated 01.06.2021 merely appointed 01.06.2021 as the date on which Section 112 of the Finance Act, 2021 came into force. It did not alter the express legislative declaration that the substituted proviso to Section 50(1) was to be deemed to have been substituted with effect from 01.07.2017. Therefore, the substantive liability to pay interest has to be determined in accordance with the retrospectively substituted provision. He further submitted that the Hon’ble Gujarat High Court in the case of M/s.Sumilon Polyster Ltd., V. Union of India, while considering the very amendment, has also noticed that the substituted proviso operates with effect from 01.07.2017. 4. Learned counsel submitted that, consequently, the interest which had been paid by the appellant on the portion of tax discharged through the electronic credit ledger was not legally payable in view of the retrospective amendment. The appellant was, therefore, entitled to seek refund of the excess interest paid. 5. He submitted that the original refund sanctioning authority had rejected the refund claim essentially on the ground that, at the relevant time, the notification bringing the retrospective amendment into force was not available. Such reasoning, according to the learned counsel, cannot survive after the enactment of Section 112 of the Finance Act, 2021, particularly when Parliament itself has declared the substituted proviso to be effective retrospectively from 01.07.2017. 6. Learned counsel further submitted that, significantly, the learned First Appellate Authority did not sustain the rejection on the ground recorded by the original authority. Instead, the appeal was dismissed on an altogether different ground, namely, that the refund claim was barred by limitation under Section 54 of the CGST Act. 7. It was emphatically contended that the appellant was never put to notice that limitation under Section 54 was proposed to be made the basis for rejection of the appeal. No opportunity was afforded to the appellant to explain the relevant date, the computation of limitation or the effect of subsequent statutory developments. The First Appellate Authority, therefore, travelled beyond the controversy adjudicated by the original authority and introduced a new and decisive ground without affording the appellant an opportunity of being heard. 8. Learned counsel submitted that an appellate authority cannot sustain the rejection of a claim on a completely new ground without putting the affected assessee on notice and giving a reasonable opportunity to meet that ground. Such an approach is contrary to the fundamental principles of natural justice and has the effect of depriving the appellant of an effective appellate remedy. 9. Learned counsel also submitted that the original authority while considering the refund claim of the appellant grossly erred in not following the administrative instructions issued in this regard by the Ministry of Finance, Department of Revenue, Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs through policy wing dated 18-09-2020. 10. Without prejudice to the above, learned counsel submitted that the computation of limitation under Section 54 could not have been undertaken by the First Appellate Authority without taking into account the Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax dated 5 July 2022 where the period from 1-3-2020 to 28-2-2022 was directed to be excluded for computation of period of limitation for considering refund applications filed under Section 54 or 55 of the CGST Act 2017. 11. He submitted that the statutory exclusion introduced by Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax materially affects the computation of limitation and, therefore, the appellant’s refund claim cannot be held as barred by limitation. The limitation, according to the learned counsel, has necessarily to be recomputed after excluding the period in the light of the by Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax dated 5-7-2022 and if the limitation is recomputed in terms of the notification the applications filed for refund of interest by the appellant are within the limitation. 12. Learned counsel placed reliance upon judicial pronouncements wherein the courts have given effect to Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax in determining limitation under Section 54, including cases where the refund claim had earlier been rejected before the notification was issued. He submitted that a subsequent statutory notification governing computation of limitation cannot be ignored merely because the original authority had passed an order before its issuance. 13. Learned counsel further submitted that since the material facts relating to date of filing of return, discharge of tax liability, payment of interest and date of application claiming refund are very much available on record, the only issue involved for consideration is essentially about the entitlement of refund of interest by the appellant in the light of the retrospective amendment to Section 50(1), the applicability of Section 54 and the statutory exclusion of the specified period under Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax. 14. He accordingly prayed that the impugned Order-in-Appeal, insofar as it rejects the refund claim on the ground of limitation, be set aside; that the benefit of the retrospective amendment to Section 50(1) be given to the appellant; that the limitation be recomputed after giving effect to Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax; and that the eligible amount of excess interest paid by the appellant be ordered to be refunded, subject to such verification and consequential formalities as may be legally required. 15. Learned counsel also submitted that the appellant should not be made to suffer for the fact that the statutory amendment was brought into force by notification subsequent to the period in which the original refund claim was considered, when Parliament itself has expressly given the amendment retrospective effect from 01.07.2017. The substantive liability must, therefore, be determined in accordance with the law as retrospectively amended and not on the basis of the position which prevailed before the amendment. 3. DISCUSSION AND FINDINGS 1. I have carefully considered the submissions advanced by the learned counsel for the appellant, the submissions of the learned Authorized Representative and perused the material available on record. The controversy before me is essentially relates to the appellant’s claim for refund of that portion of interest which was paid on the tax liability discharged through utilisation of Input Tax Credit, in the light of the retrospective amendment to Section 50(1) of the CGST Act, and the consequential question of limitation under Section 54 of the Act. 2. It is not in dispute that the appellant had furnished the relevant returns belatedly and had discharged the tax liability partly through the electronic credit ledger and partly through the electronic cash ledger. Interest was paid on the entire tax liability. Subsequently, Section 112 of the Finance Act, 2021 substituted the proviso to Section 50(1) of the CGST Act and expressly provided that the substituted proviso shall be deemed to have been substituted with effect from 01.07.2017. Thus, the amendment is not prospective merely from the date of its notification, but has been given retrospective operation by the Legislature from 01.07.2017. 3. The effect of the retrospective amendment is that, subject to the conditions contained in the proviso itself, interest under Section 50(1) is leviable only on that portion of the tax which is paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger. Therefore, while examining the appellant’s entitlement to refund, the liability to pay interest has to be determined with reference to the law as retrospectively amended. The mere fact that the appellant had voluntarily paid interest earlier cannot, by itself, validate an amount which was not legally payable in view of the retrospective amendment. 4. I am therefore unable to sustain the reasoning of the original authority that the claim could not be considered merely because the notification bringing the amendment into force was not available at the relevant point of time particularly in the light of the administrative instructions issued in this regard by the Government of India dated 18-09-2020 wherein specific directions were issued to all The Principal Chief Commissioners / Chief Commissioners / Principal Commissioners /Commissioners / of Central Tax ,The Principal Director Generals / Director Generals to issue necessary instructions to the field formations of the respective jurisdiction offices to recover interest only on the net cash tax liability i.e. that portion of the tax that has been paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger or is payable through cash ledger pending issuance of suitable legislative amendment in pursuance of the 39th GST Councils meeting where it was recommended to amend Section 50 retrospectively. The Notification No. 16/2021-Central Tax dated 01.06.2021 brought the relevant provision into force, but the substantive amendment itself was expressly declared by Parliament to operate retrospectively from 01.07.2017. The rights and liabilities of the parties must consequently be examined in accordance with the retrospectively amended statutory provision. 6. The next issue which requires consideration is the finding of the learned First Appellate Authority that the refund claim was barred by limitation under Section 54 of the CGST Act. I find that this ground was not the ground on which the original refund application was rejected. The First Appellate Authority, while disposing of the appeal, introduced limitation as a fresh and decisive ground for rejecting the appellant’s claim. 7. It is the settled principle of natural justice that when an appellate or adjudicating authority proposes to decide a matter on a ground which has not been put to the affected party, reasonable notice and an effective opportunity to meet such ground must be afforded. The appellant ought to have been given an opportunity to explain the relevant date, the computation of limitation and the applicability of the statutory extensions or exclusions governing the relevant period. In the absence of such opportunity, the finding on limitation cannot be sustained. In support of the proposition that dismissing of appeal on a fresh ground for the first time, without prior notice to the appellant violates principles of natural justice and vitiates the order. Reliance is placed on the decisions of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in M/S Saci Allied Products Ltd.,U.P vs Commissioner Of Central Excise, Meerut on 26 April, 2005 reported in 2005 (7) SCC 159 and in Reckitt & Colman of India Ltd. vs. CCE, 1996(88)ELT 641(SC). 8. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in N. Mukherjee v. Union of India [(1990) 4 SCC 594 referring to the doctrine of audi alteram partem has held that “the principle of natural justice has twin ingredients; firstly, the person who is likely to be adversely affected by the action of the authorities should be given notice to show cause thereof and granted an opportunity of hearing and secondly, the orders so passed by the authorities should give reason for arriving at any conclusion showing proper application of mind. Violation of either of them could in the given facts and circumstances of the case, vitiate the order itself.” 9. There is also a further and important aspect which, in my considered view, has a direct bearing upon the computation of limitation. Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax dated 05.07.2022, issued under Section 168A of the CGST Act, specifically provides for exclusion of the period from 01.03.2020 to 28.02.2022 for computation of the period of limitation for filing refund applications under Section 54 or Section 55 of the Act. The notification has also been given effect from 01.03.2020. Reliance is placed on the decisions of the Hon’ble the Hon’ble Delhi High Court in M/s. Mahalaxmi Exports v. Commissioner of Delhi GST and the judgment of the Hon’ble High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh in the case of the case of M/s. Bharat Oil Traders v. Assistant Commissioner wherein it was held that in terms of the Notification No. 13/2022 the period from 1 March 2020 to 28 February 2022 has to be excluded while computing the limitation period for a refund application under Section 54. The Courts have set aside the rejection of the refund claims and directed the authorities to process the claims. 10. Consequently, the period prescribed under Section 54 cannot be computed by simply applying the ordinary period of limitation without taking into account the statutory exclusion specifically introduced by Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax. The limitation has to be freshly worked out in accordance with the statutory provision as applicable to the claim, including the aforesaid exclusion. At the same time, I make it clear that the notification does not dispense with the requirement of satisfying the prescribed limitation altogether; the actual relevant date and the date of filing of the refund application have necessarily to be examined. 11. I also find considerable force in the submission of the learned counsel for the appellant that the issue cannot be decided merely on the basis of the date on which the refund was originally rejected. Once the Legislature has retrospectively altered the substantive liability under Section 50(1), and a statutory notification has subsequently prescribed exclusion of a specified period for computation of limitation under Section 54, both these statutory developments have to be given their proper legal effect while determining the appellant’s entitlement. 12. The learned Authorized Representative appearing for the Department has suggested that the matter may be remanded to the Appellate authority for fresh consideration. I however unable to accept the suggestion for an open-ended remand either to the 1st Appellate authority or the original authority. The original authority has already examined the refund claim, and the dispute before me is substantially one concerning the legal effect of the retrospective amendment to Section 50(1), the computation of limitation under Section 54 and the applicability of Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax. No substantive objection has been raised before me disputing the appellant’s claim on the ground that interest continues to be legally payable on the portion of tax discharged through the electronic credit ledger even after giving effect to the retrospective amendment. 13. In these circumstances, remanding the matter to the 1st Appellate authority merely for a fresh adjudication would serve no useful purpose and would unnecessarily prolong the litigation. The power of remand is intended to cure a genuine deficiency requiring factual adjudication by the lower authority and should not be exercised mechanically where the principal controversy is capable of being determined on the basis of the statutory provisions and the material already available on record. 14. However, if any factual verification remains necessary for determining the precise amount refundable—for example, verification of the amount of interest actually paid, the portion attributable to the tax discharged through the electronic credit ledger, or any other statutory requirement—the same may appropriately be undertaken by the competent authority who is authorised to process the refund application. Such limited verification shall not be treated as a remand for reconsideration of the legal entitlement afresh. 15. I therefore hold that the finding of the learned First Appellate Authority that the refund claim is barred by limitation, having been recorded without affording the appellant an opportunity to meet that ground and without considering the effect of Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax, cannot be sustained. The impugned Order-in-Appeal is accordingly liable to be set aside. 16. The appellant’s claim shall consequently be considered in accordance with the retrospective amendment to Section 50(1), the provisions of Section 54 of the CGST Act and Notification No. 13/2022-Central Tax. The competent authority shall recompute the limitation after giving effect to the statutory exclusion contained in the said notification and shall verify and quantify the eligible amount of refund in accordance with law. 17. While undertaking such exercise, the competent authority shall also examine whether the case falls within any exception contained in the amended proviso to Section 50(1), including the statutory reference to proceedings under Sections 73 or 74, and shall verify the actual amount of interest paid and the portion, if any, which was attributable to the tax discharged through the electronic credit ledger. The authority shall thereafter pass an appropriate consequential order after affording the appellant a reasonable opportunity of being heard. The entire exercise shall be completed within three months from the date of receipt of this order. ORDER 18. For the reasons discussed above, I hold that the impugned Order-in-Appeal Nos. VIZ-GST-000-APP-065 TO 066-21-22 43/VSK/2026 & 44/VSK/2026 dated 31-3-2022 cannot be sustained insofar as it rejects the appellant’s refund claim solely on the ground of limitation. The appeal is accordingly allowed by setting aside the impugned order and directing consequential action in the limited manner indicated hereinabove.
