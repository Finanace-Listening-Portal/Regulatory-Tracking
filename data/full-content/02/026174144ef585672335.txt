Radha Trading and upheld the Commissioner (Appeals)’s decision setting aside the enhancement of the declared transaction value of imported mixed lots of polyester knitted fabrics from China. The importer had paid customs duty on the enhanced value to avoid demurrage and detention charges but subsequently challenged the reassessment. The CESTAT Kolkata dismissed the Revenue’s appeal against Radha Trading concerning enhanced valuation of imported mixed lots of polyester knitted fabrics from China. The importer had paid duty on the enhanced value to avoid demurrage and detention charges, but challenged the reassessment. The Tribunal held that Section 17(5) of the Customs Act, 1962 requires a speaking order within 15 days where reassessment under Section 17(4) differs from self-assessment and is not accepted in writing. No such speaking order had been issued. The Tribunal also found that the Proper Officer had supplied neither reasons nor evidence supporting rejection of the transaction value. The Commissioner (Appeals) had correctly applied Section 14 of the Customs Act and the Customs Valuation Rules, 2007, along with relevant judicial precedents. Accordingly, the Tribunal found no infirmity in the Commissioner (Appeals)’s order and dismissed the Revenue’s appeal, sustaining assessment at declared values with consequential relief.
