I think, I don't know why we keep on comparing the institution which is today as against what it was pre-merger. These are two different institutions altogether,” Jagdishan said. Advt “In fact, when you look at this institution and compare, our matrices are much better than what it was for that,” he added.Jagdishan said investors should instead focus on a narrower set of profitability metrics as the bank moves beyond the balance-sheet transition following the merger.“For me, as it may, the reality is we need to only look at one metric, and that is either the return on assets or earnings per share growth,” he said.The outgoing CEO said the bank had made investments in technology and distribution and had also worked through the borrowing structure that came with the merger. As these investments translate into greater efficiency, the bank expects its cost structure to improve.“I think all of you need to just focus on one aspect, that is how the earnings per share of the return on asset moves from here into the future,” Jagdishan said.He also indicated that investors should not get overly focused on comparing every individual operating metric with the pre-merger HDFC Bank.“All of the matrices are frankly not necessary,” he said, adding that the focus should be on how the bank’s ROA or EPS develops over the next two to three years.The bank’s investor presentation showed that trailing twelve-month profit after tax rose to Rs 76,400 crore in September 2026 from Rs 28,500 crore in September 2020, while EPS increased to Rs 50 from Rs 26 over the same period. Advt At the same time, some of the traditional profitability metrics remain different from their pre-merger levels. The bank’s NIM was 4.2 per cent in September 2020, compared with 3.3 per cent on a trailing twelve-month basis in September 2026, while ROA stood at 1.9 per cent in September 2026 compared with 2.0 per cent in September 2020.How did Q2 FY27 fare for HDFC Bank?HDFC Bank reported a 4.7 per cent year-on-year increase in standalone profit after tax to Rs 19,510 crore in Q2 FY27, compared with Rs 18,640 crore a year earlier. Net interest income rose 9.7 per cent YoY to Rs 34,620 crore, while net revenue increased 5.6 per cent to Rs 48,450 crore. Provisions were at Rs 3,570 crore, up 2.1 per cent YoY and 16.8 per cent sequentially. Advt The bank’s average deposits stood at Rs 31,664 billion, up 16.8 per cent YoY and 5.1 per cent sequentially, while average advances under management rose 14.1 per cent YoY and 4.9 per cent QoQ to Rs 31,872 billion.
