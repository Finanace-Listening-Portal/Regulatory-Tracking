Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Valeo India Private Limited (CAAR Mumbai) Summary : In In re Valeo India Private Limited, the Customs Authority for Advance Rulings (CAAR), Mumbai, examined the classification of imported Auto Light Sensors under the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, and their eligibility for exemption from Basic Customs Duty under Notification No. 69/2011-Customs dated 29 July 2011. The ruling, dated 11 November 2026, arose from an application filed by Valeo India Private Limited, a manufacturer of automobile components. The applicant proposed to import Auto Light Sensors from Japan in both fully assembled and disassembled forms. These sensors automatically detect changes in ambient light and facilitate the switching ON and OFF of vehicle headlights without manual intervention. The applicant sought clarification on whether the goods were classifiable under Customs Tariff Item (CTI) 8541 49 00, whether the same classification applied to disassembled components imported together, and whether the goods qualified for preferential customs duty exemption under the India-Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement. The applicant contended that Auto Light Sensors were essentially photosensitive semiconductor devices and therefore appropriately classifiable under CTI 8541 49 00, covering other photosensitive semiconductor devices. It explained that the sensors incorporated photodiodes or phototransistors that detected variations in surrounding light intensity and generated corresponding electrical signals. According to the applicant, the semiconductor-based sensing mechanism constituted the essential character of the imported goods. Reliance was placed on the relevant Harmonised System of Nomenclature (HSN) Explanatory Notes, Chapter 85, Note 12 to Chapter 85, and the General Rules for Interpretation of the Customs Tariff. The applicant further submitted that disassembled components imported together for assembly into complete sensors should receive the same classification under General Interpretative Rule 2(a), since they possessed the essential character of the finished product. It also argued that classification under Heading 8708 as motor vehicle parts was inappropriate because the goods were more specifically covered by Heading 8541 and that Heading 9031 was inapplicable because the sensors did not perform independent measuring or checking functions. During the personal hearing held on 5 August 2026, the applicant’s authorised representative reiterated the proposed classification under CTI 8541 49 00. The Authority examined the technical characteristics, construction, functional capabilities and intended use of the imported Auto Light Sensors. It observed that the sensors were not merely individual photosensitive semiconductor elements but integrated components of an automobile’s automatic headlamp control system. The sensing element detected ambient light variations and transmitted corresponding electrical signals to the vehicle’s Body Control Module (BCM). The BCM processed those signals according to programmed light-intensity thresholds and switching delays, automatically activating or deactivating the headlights. The Authority noted that classification must be determined by considering the complete imported article and its objective functional characteristics rather than identifying only one semiconductor component incorporated within it. Consequently, the mere presence of a photodiode or phototransistor did not establish classification of the complete Auto Light Sensor under Heading 8541. The Authority also examined whether the goods could be classified under Heading 8708 as parts and accessories of motor vehicles. Although the Auto Light Sensors were specifically designed for automotive applications and satisfied the requirements of suitability for use solely or principally with motor vehicles, classification under Heading 8708 was subject to the relevant Section Notes and exclusions. In particular, Note 2(f) to Section XVII excludes electrical machinery or equipment of Chapter 85 from classification as motor vehicle parts and accessories. The Authority therefore considered the competing classification under Heading 9031, which covers measuring or checking instruments, appliances and machines not specified or included elsewhere in Chapter 90. It found that the sensors detected variations in ambient light and generated electrical signals for automatic headlamp control, rather than independently measuring, analysing or evaluating light intensity. Their primary function was sensing changes in surrounding illumination and communicating those changes to the vehicle’s electronic control system. The Authority accordingly concluded that the goods were appropriately classifiable under CTI 9031 80 00 as other instruments, appliances and machines. In reaching its conclusion, the Authority referred to the decision of the CESTAT, New Delhi, in M/s Denso Haryana Pvt. Ltd. v. Commissioner of Customs (I&G), New Delhi, Appeal No. C/732/2011-CU(DB), decided on 15 July 2013. That decision concerned the classification of automotive sensors and recognised that sensors detecting variations in physical conditions and generating corresponding electrical signals could fall under Heading 9031, notwithstanding that they did not display conventional numerical measurements. Applying the relevant tariff provisions, HSN Explanatory Notes and General Interpretative Rules, the Authority held that the imported Auto Light Sensors were not appropriately classifiable under CTI 8541 49 00 merely because they incorporated photosensitive semiconductor elements. It further determined that the classification under CTI 9031 80 00 applied to both fully assembled sensors and completely disassembled sensors imported together with all components necessary for assembly into a complete and functional Auto Light Sensor. Regarding the claim for Basic Customs Duty exemption under Notification No. 69/2011-Customs dated 29 July 2011, the Authority examined Serial No. 726 of the notification, which prescribed a nil duty rate for specified goods falling under Chapter 85 tariff sub-headings 8541 10 to 8542 33, subject to the applicable conditions. Since the imported Auto Light Sensors were correctly classifiable under CTI 9031 80 00 of Chapter 90, they did not fall within the tariff headings covered by the relevant exemption entry. Accordingly, the Authority ruled that the goods were not eligible for exemption under Serial No. 726 of Notification No. 69/2011-Customs. The ruling clarified that classification under CTI 9031 80 00 would apply to both assembled and completely disassembled imports, while the jurisdictional customs authorities retained their powers to verify the imported goods, examine the relevant documents and determine compliance with applicable customs laws and regulations.
