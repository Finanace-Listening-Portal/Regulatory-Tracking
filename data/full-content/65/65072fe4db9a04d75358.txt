Speaking about the impact of higher interest rates, Setty said, “Interest rates will definitely influence the composition as well as the quantum of credit demand, though not necessarily immediately. I think the impact will become evident as we go forward. If we see another rate hike in December, rate-sensitive sectors, particularly those linked to discretionary spending and borrowing, will probably see moderation in demand." “One of the important things to recognise is that we are entering a rate-hike cycle with robust credit growth. We have a rate hike as well as a projected growth rate of 7.1 per cent. This means there will be sustained credit growth driven by demand for credit from the real economy". The RBI raised its FY27 consumer price index (CPI)-based inflation forecast to 5.2 per cent from 5 per cent, citing higher food and fuel inflation, a pickup in core inflation, a deficient monsoon, strong El Niño conditions and elevated energy and commodity prices. RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said further rate decisions would depend on the trajectory of inflation and economic growth, the extent to which price pressures broaden, and the pace at which supply shocks feed through to prices. The central bank also raised its FY27 real GDP growth forecast to 7.1 per cent from 6.7 per cent, citing strong private consumption and investment, a rebound in merchandise exports and sustained growth in services exports. On the external risks to the economic outlook, Malhotra said the ongoing West Asia conflict continued to pose risks to domestic economic activity through energy prices and supply chain pressures.
