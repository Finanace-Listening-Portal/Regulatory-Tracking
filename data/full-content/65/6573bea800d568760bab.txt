Will it moderate going forward? I think it will continue to be strong. A few percentage points here and there should not really matter too much in the overall scheme of things, but it will continue to support,” Malhotra said. Advt RBI Deputy Governor Swaminathan Janakiraman said credit growth at the current level is high compared with its long-term average, and some moderation is expected as the latest rate increase transmits through the system.“As we noted earlier as well, the credit growth, we can probably say if not all-time high, at least we get a high at about 18-19 per cent only. But if you take a long-term average of say 10 years, generally it is seen in the range of 12-14 per cent, which is very sustainable and very supportive of growth. So some moderation in any case will occur,” Swaminathan said.He added that monetary policy transmission typically takes a couple of quarters and would affect both demand and rates.“Yes, as these rates transmit, as we have seen, it typically takes about a couple of quarters for transmissions to materialize. So some moderation will occur relating to demand as well as rate together. But moderation from 18 per cent to 20 per cent is not bad and will be adequate enough to support growth,” he said.The comments come after the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) on Wednesday raised the policy repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50 per cent and shifted its stance from neutral to calibrated tightening by a 4-2 vote.FCNR(B) mobilisationThe Governor also said the mobilisation of FCNR(B) deposits of around USD 133 billion reflects the strength of India's macroeconomic fundamentals, while banks are expected to deploy the funds prudently. Advt “First of all, I would like to say that the FCNR(B) deposit of $135 billion speaks about the strength of the macroeconomic fundamentals of our country. As far as banks are concerned, I am confident that the bank's deposits, both in the past and in the future, will be prudently deployed. As you can see, 19 per cent of the credit has increased. This is due to the high demand for credit. Banks will continue to provide credit,” Malhotra said.He said banks are already using the FCNR(B) deposits, as reflected in the high rate of credit growth, but cautioned against deploying the entire amount too quickly. Advt “We are already seeing the use of the FCNR(B) deposits as evidenced by the high growth rate, but of course the amount is so large, we do not want them to use them overnight. They need to do their due diligence properly and take their time to use these deposits,” he said.Malhotra also linked the strong credit growth and deployment of these deposits to the improving domestic macroeconomic fundamentals, while describing the current pressure on the external sector as temporary.“As far as giving credit back is concerned, as I told you, the macroeconomic fundamentals of our country are improving. This is due to the temporary external headwinds. The pressure on the current account is a temporary problem. Sooner or later, our external sector will also increase. We are getting all the signs of this,” he said. Advt He pointed to foreign direct investment, services exports, remittances and various government measures as factors that could support the external sector.“Whether it is about cross-FDI, whether it is about services exports, whether it is about remittances, whether it is about the various steps taken by the Indian government for reforms, ease of doing business, the areas of FDI, such as the insurance sector, and to increase them, the government securities, the tax system, and other policies that have been implemented, because of all this, we have complete faith that our BOP deficit will come to a surplus soon,” Malhotra said.He also said the RBI has adequate foreign exchange reserves to manage external pressures.
