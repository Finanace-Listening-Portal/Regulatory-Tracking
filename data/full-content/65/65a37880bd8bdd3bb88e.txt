Articles 3 min read Can UPI become commercially self-sustaining with MDR? A new MDR on high-value merchant payments will create a sizeable revenue pool, but current estimates suggest it may not be enough to cover the full cost of running India's UPI ecosystem. The revenue will reduce the gap between the cost of operating UPI and the income generated by the ecosystem, but will not make the payment system fully self-sustaining through MDR.From October 15, a 0.4% MDR will apply to specified person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, subject to a maximum charge of Rs 300 for transactions of Rs 75,000 and above. About 96% of P2M transactions will remain outside the MDR framework, while person-to-person transactions will continue to be free. Advt The National Payments Corporation of India expects the new MDR framework to generate Rs 13,000-15,000 crore in the first year against an estimated annual UPI infrastructure cost of about Rs 21,000 crore. The objective is to recover a substantial part of the cost and create revenue for banks and other payment participants to continue investing in the ecosystem, rather than recover the entire cost through MDR.Government support will continue for low-value paymentsThe government has budgeted Rs 2,000 crore for FY27 under the incentive scheme for RuPay debit cards and low-value BHIM-UPI P2M transactions. The incentive was introduced after the zero-MDR regime began in 2020 to compensate banks for the cost of processing such transactions. The subsidy for transactions below Rs 2,000 is also likely to continue beyond October 15, according to finance ministry sources.The Rs 2,000 crore incentive should not, however, be treated as additional MDR revenue. It supports a different category of transactions and remains part of the government's mechanism for supporting low-value UPI payments.The new pricing structure is deliberately narrow. MDR will apply to only about 4% of merchant transactions, leaving about 96% unaffected. Payments to merchants up to Rs 2,000 and transactions covered under the zero-MDR framework for small merchants will remain free.Essential and thin-margin sectors including railways, telecommunications, insurance, fuel and agricultural inputs will have a flat Rs 5 MDR on eligible transactions above Rs 2,000. Capital-market transactions involving mutual funds, securities, stockbrokers and dealers will attract an MDR of 0.02%, capped at Rs 300. Advt The structure limits the revenue available from UPI because a large proportion of the network will continue to operate without merchant charges.Large merchants will generate most of the MDR poolThe new MDR revenue is expected to be concentrated among large businesses rather than small merchants. Around 80% of the MDR value is expected to come from businesses with annual digital-payment collections of more than Rs 1,000 crore. The government has also directed that MDR should not be passed on to customers.UPI processed 24,161.69 crore transactions worth Rs 314.23 lakh crore in FY26, underlining the scale of the infrastructure that needs to be maintained and expanded. Advt The immediate question is therefore not whether MDR will make UPI completely self-sustaining. At the proposed rate, the expected Rs 13,000-15,000 crore revenue would cover only about two-thirds of the estimated annual cost.The bigger change is that UPI will move from an almost entirely zero-MDR model towards a hybrid system in which high-value merchant payments generate commercial revenue while low-value payments continue to receive government support.
