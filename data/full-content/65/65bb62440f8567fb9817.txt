Summary: AAVE is the governance token used by the Aave decentralised lending protocol, where token holders and delegates participate in decisions affecting protocol parameters and upgrades. Aave’s official governance documentation distinguishes AAVE, staked AAVE (stkAAVE) and AAVE supplied to the Ethereum market (aAAVE) for voting purposes. Its Umbrella risk-management mechanism permits staking specified aTokens and GHO to absorb certain bad debts, and must not be confused with a guaranteed return or an automatic AAVE staking facility. For Indian tax year 2026-27, disposal of AAVE is ordinarily a transfer of a virtual digital asset: section 194, Table Sl. No. 4 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 prescribes 30% tax on eligible transfer income, with stringent cost and loss restrictions, while section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 8(vi) contains the 1% TDS framework for consideration paid to a resident. Form 141 Schedule D and Form 142 are relevant to specified buyer and exchange arrangements. The article addresses trading, token swaps, DeFi rewards, recordkeeping, FIU-IND/PMLA, GST, FEMA and accounting without implying that taxation or FIU registration makes any token or platform an RBI-approved investment. AAVE is primarily a governance asset: its ownership or delegated voting power can be used in the protocol’s on-chain governance process. The token is not a deposit with a bank, a share in Aave Labs, a sovereign-backed currency or a contractual claim for fixed interest. The market value of AAVE can fluctuate independently of the quantity of loans outstanding on Aave markets. Aave Governance v3 separates core-network voting-power records from voting networks and execution networks. Official Aave documentation states that AAVE, stkAAVE and aAAVE holders on Ethereum mainnet can have governance power. Voting may take place on lower-fee networks while the underlying voting asset remains recorded on Ethereum. A holder who delegates voting power does not thereby transfer ownership of the tokens, although the exact smart-contract permissions should be examined. Aave Governance and Umbrella: Distinct Economic Functions Governance proposal and voting process Aave proposals can move from off-chain discussion and sentiment polling to formal on-chain governance, including activation, voting and timelocked execution. Quorum and majority requirements are specified by the relevant governance contracts and proposal class. Token holdings alone do not ensure that a particular proposal will be approved, nor does a vote create an enforceable dividend entitlement. Umbrella and legacy Safety Module Aave’s official Umbrella documentation describes a modular risk-management system designed to cover bad debt in Aave v3 pools. Umbrella staking assets include specified aTokens and GHO, with coverage linked to the relevant asset and network. In defined deficit situations, staked assets may be burned to absorb bad debt. Aave also describes a transition from its legacy Safety Module, including existing stkAAVE arrangements. Therefore, a generic claim that “staking AAVE earns a fixed yield” is inaccurate: the token, contract, programme, rewards, slashing exposure and withdrawal mechanics must be identified separately. For tax year 2026-27, the relevant special-rate provision is section 194, Table Sl. No. 4, not the former section 115BBH of the repealed Income-tax Act, 1961. Income from a qualifying transfer of a virtual digital asset is taxable at 30%, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. The legal rule concerns income from transfer, not a tax of 30% on the gross sale proceeds. A loss-making sale does not become a taxable profit merely because TDS was deducted on consideration. See also TaxGuru: New Act VDA framework. Cost, losses and the transfer-by-transfer approach The special computation generally allows the cost of acquisition, if any, but disallows other expenditure and allowances in computing the specified VDA transfer income. Losses from VDA transfers cannot be set off against other income or carried forward; a loss on one token should not automatically be netted against a gain on another. The official Schedule VDA guidance requires transaction-level reporting and ordinarily treats a negative result as nil for the special transfer-income schedule. Purchase records, fees, wallet addresses and historical INR conversions are essential to a defensible calculation. See also TaxGuru: TaxGuru VDA loss and deduction analysis. Example of gain, tax and TDS Illustration only: suppose a resident investor acquired AAVE for ₹1,80,000 and transferred the same holding for ₹2,60,000, with no adjustment other than the permitted acquisition cost. The transfer income is ₹80,000; the 30% basic income tax is ₹24,000, and 4% cess on that tax would be ₹960 if no surcharge applies. Separately, 1% TDS on ₹2,60,000 consideration would be ₹2,600 where the withholding rule applies. The TDS is creditable against the final tax liability, not an additional final tax on top of the computed income tax. Transaction fees and unusual acquisition events require separate fact-specific review. The commonly applicable 1% rate is on the consideration, not on the gain, subject to payer-category thresholds and the statutory conditions. For specified individual/HUF buyer cases, Form 141 Schedule D is the consolidated challan-cum-statement route from 1 April 2026; the Department separately prescribes Form 142 for qualifying exchange arrangements. Exchanges, brokers and peer-to-peer buyers should not assume that the same person is always the deductor. Consideration in kind or exchanged for another token also requires attention to the mechanism for ensuring payment of TDS. See also TaxGuru: Form 141 Schedule D procedure. The Income Tax Department’s Form 141 guidance distinguishes Schedule D from the other schedules and identifies the current section/table reference. Form 142 is a quarterly statement for specified exchanges that agree to deposit tax under the CBDT mechanism. A resident buyer should verify the correct form, month, deductee PAN and challan before relying on an exchange’s TDS statement. Tax withheld abroad does not automatically discharge the Indian buyer’s statutory obligations. DeFi Rewards, Interest-like Receipts and Token Swaps Aave protocol users may receive or realise rewards, incentives or yield-like accretions. The legal character of receipt depends on the arrangement: a reward for an activity, a token distribution, a return on supplied assets, a borrowing transaction and a subsequent token disposal are not necessarily the same taxable event. The 30% VDA transfer provision does not itself settle the initial tax treatment of every reward on receipt. The recipient should record the date, wallet, token, quantity, INR fair value, vesting conditions and subsequent transfer so that acquisition cost and head-of-income questions can be examined. AAVE-to-USDC, AAVE-to-ETH and other token-to-token exchanges can involve transfer of a VDA even when no rupees enter an Indian bank account. Borrowing against collateral is not automatically a sale of the collateral, but liquidation, repayment through token disposal or redemption of tokenised claims may involve separate taxable transfers. Smart-contract traces and exchange records should be reconciled rather than treating all wallet outflows as deductible costs. FIU-IND, PMLA, FEMA and GST FIU-IND reporting-entity obligations Specified VDA service-provider activities fall within the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 framework. FIU-IND’s updated AML/CFT guidelines for VDA service providers, shown on its official downloads page as updated 8 January 2026, require applicable reporting entities to address registration, customer due diligence, recordkeeping, transaction monitoring and suspicious-transaction reporting. These are obligations of covered service providers; simply owning AAVE does not automatically make an individual a VDA reporting entity. Registration is not a certification that the token is safe or that an offshore trading product is lawful. See also TaxGuru: TaxGuru FIU enforcement background. FEMA and cross-border flows Cryptographic tokens are not automatically foreign currency or RBI-authorised remittance instruments. The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 governs the resident’s foreign-exchange transactions, remittances, overseas investments and payments. Cross-border acquisition of AAVE, use of an offshore platform, payment for imported services and transfers between a resident and non-resident can raise different FEMA issues. It is unsafe to conclude that a transaction is permitted merely because a foreign app accepts Indian KYC or because a bank permits a card payment. The underlying purpose, authorised-dealer route and applicable restrictions should be tested independently. See also TaxGuru: TaxGuru crypto and FEMA analysis. GST on services versus token transfers Exchange commissions, platform facilitation, custody or advisory services may constitute taxable supplies under the CGST/IGST framework, depending on supplier, place of supply and classification. The GST treatment of the underlying transfer of a crypto token itself should not be asserted categorically without a specific applicable legal basis. A flat claim that “18% GST applies to every AAVE purchase” confuses a taxable platform fee with the token’s transfer value. Businesses should obtain proper tax invoices, consider reverse charge or import-of-service issues where relevant, and reconcile any input-tax-credit claim with the statutory conditions. See also TaxGuru: TaxGuru discussion of crypto service fees. Accounting, Audit Trail and Reporting For an entity holding AAVE as inventory in the ordinary course of trading, AS 2 or Ind AS 2 inventory principles may be relevant. For holdings not meeting inventory criteria, an intangible-asset analysis under AS 26 or Ind AS 38 may be required, subject to facts and applicable reporting framework. AAVE is not cash or a cash equivalent merely because it trades continuously. Recognition, cost determination, impairment or subsequent measurement, financial-statement disclosures and auditor evidence should be documented consistently. Tax treatment under the VDA regime need not mirror book measurement. A reliable ledger should reconcile exchange statements, on-chain transaction hashes, opening and closing wallet balances, token swaps, staking receipts, custody fees, tax withheld and bank settlements. Businesses should identify whether any crypto-asset information-reporting obligations under section 509 and related provisions apply to them; the Finance Act, 2026 has strengthened reporting and penalty provisions. Taxpayers must not assume that an overseas wallet is outside disclosure or assessment scrutiny. See also TaxGuru: TaxGuru crypto reporting amendments. No. It is a governance token of the Aave protocol and does not provide statutory deposit insurance or ordinary company-share rights. 2. Is AAVE the same as an Umbrella staking asset? No. Umbrella documentation describes staking specified aTokens and GHO; legacy stkAAVE arrangements must be distinguished. 3. What is the Indian tax rate on an AAVE transfer gain in tax year 2026-27? Section 194, Table Sl. No. 4 generally prescribes 30% basic tax on qualifying VDA transfer income, plus surcharge and cess. 4. Is one percent TDS computed on profit? No. Where section 393(1) applies, 1% TDS is generally computed on consideration subject to the statutory conditions. 5. Can an AAVE loss reduce salary income or another crypto gain? The special VDA transfer-loss rules prohibit set-off and carry-forward in the manner specified by the Act. 6. Is staking income always taxed only when AAVE is sold? Not necessarily. Initial receipt and later transfer can be different events; the arrangement and tax head must be assessed. 7. Does FIU-IND registration make a crypto exchange RBI-approved? No. FIU-IND reporting-entity registration is an AML/CFT compliance obligation, not an investment-safety endorsement. 8. Does using an offshore wallet eliminate Indian reporting? No. Indian tax and FEMA consequences depend on the taxpayer, transaction and residential status, not merely wallet location.
