Articles 5 min read RBI flags risk to future bank rescues in Supreme Court review plea on YES Bank’s Rs 8,415-crore AT-1 bonds The Reserve Bank of India, through its review plea dated November 13, has raised that the Supreme Court's ruling overturning the Rs 8,415-crore AT-1 bond write-off at YES Bank could create uncertainty around the loss absorption framework for banks, potentially complicating future resolution exercises, ETBFSI has learnt from the official privy of the matter. Advt The RBI filed its review petition on November 13, the same day YES Bank moved the Supreme Court seeking a review of its October 15 judgment setting aside the Rs 8,415-crore AT-1 bond write-off."RBI's grounds for the petition include the impact that this judgment is going to have on the banking sector. Whatever decision it had taken at the time was for a particular reason, and it apprehends that the judgment is not in sync with what the regulator had intended to achieve," a person privy to the matter told ETBFSI.The concern stems from the role AT-1 bonds play in a bank's capital structure. These instruments are designed to absorb losses when a bank's financial position deteriorates and carry contractual conditions under which investors can face a complete write-off of their investments."These bonds are a form of capital meant to withstand shocks that can happen to a bank. The underlying condition is that if something goes wrong, investors are the first to take the hit and can lose their entire investment. That is the very purpose for which these instruments are issued," the person quoted above said.Why the RBI is contesting the judgmentA key issue in the Supreme Court's judgment was the deletion of the provision relating to the AT-1 bond write-off from the final reconstruction scheme.During the preparation of the reconstruction scheme in March 2020, the initial draft provided for writing down the AT-1 bonds. Following representations from stakeholders, the provision was modified to allow for conversion of the instruments into equity. Advt However, when the scheme was finally submitted to the Union government for approval, the provision was removed.The rationale, as explained by the person privy to the matter, was that investors belonging to the same class could not be given differential treatment. YES Bank had issued an earlier tranche of AT-1 bonds before the introduction of the relevant Basel III framework, under which the option of conversion into equity was not available."The understanding was that investors belonging to the same class could not be given differential treatment, with some being allowed to convert their bonds into equity while others were not. There was also an apprehension that such differential treatment could lead to litigation. The decision was therefore to leave the contractual conditions of the bonds intact and allow them to operate as stipulated," the person said. Advt The Supreme Court, however, interpreted the deletion of the clause as a conscious decision by the government not to write down the bonds, raising questions over the administrator's authority to proceed with the write-off in the absence of an explicit provision in the final reconstruction scheme.The court also raised concerns over the manner and sequence in which the write-off was carried out.Why RBI has filed a separate review petition"RBI is the apex regulator for banks. When a decision has implications for the banking sector, its regulatory framework and its policy decisions, it has a role to play. This is a considered decision of the institution as a whole and not something initiated by an individual," the person said. Advt The RBI's concerns extend beyond the immediate impact on YES Bank to the implications for future bank-resolution exercises and the protection of depositors."The concern is not limited to the banking sector. Depositors were also protected through the reconstruction exercise. The bank was brought back from a difficult position through the capital infusion and the actions taken at the time. If the write-off had not happened, YES Bank could not have been recovered even with the investments that came in," the person said.YES Bank was rescued in March 2020 after the RBI stepped in, imposed a moratorium and facilitated a reconstruction plan involving State Bank of India and other investors. The bank subsequently returned to profitability.The RBI is concerned that revisiting the write-off years after the reconstruction could introduce uncertainty for investors and institutions participating in future bank-rescue exercises."Tomorrow, if a similar situation arises, who will be willing to bring in money? Investors may put in capital today, but if there is uncertainty over whether the decisions taken during a reconstruction will hold up five or six years later, it could deter them from participating. That uncertainty could have a negative impact on the entire banking sector," the person said.ETBFSI has reached out to the RBI for its comments on the review petition and the grounds on which it is seeking reconsideration of the Supreme Court judgment.
