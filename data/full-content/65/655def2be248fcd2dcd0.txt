Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Praddum Trading Company Private Limited Vs Registrar of Companies (NCLT Mumbai) Summary : The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Mumbai Bench, in Praddum Trading Company Private Limited Through Director Sunil Khandelwal Vs Registrar of Companies, Mumbai, directed restoration of the company’s name under Section 252(3) of the Companies Act, 2013, notwithstanding its admitted inactivity at the time of strike-off. The company, incorporated on 30 March 2007 and engaged in trading and distribution activities, was struck off by the Registrar of Companies in September 2018 following defaults in filing financial statements and annual returns. The petitioner initially asserted that the company had remained operational and relied upon its financial statements and income-tax returns. However, during the hearings, counsel admitted that the company was not carrying on business when its name was struck off and requested that the petition be considered under Section 252(3) rather than Section 252(1). The Tribunal criticised the inaccurate pleadings and casual drafting, observing that such practices unnecessarily consumed valuable judicial time. The Tribunal examined the company’s substantial investments in five other companies, aggregating to ₹68,78,000, supported by a Chartered Accountant’s certificate dated 27 July 2026. It held that Section 252(3) permits restoration not only where a company was carrying on business or was operational at the time of strike-off, but also where restoration is otherwise just. The existence of valuable investments justified revival because continued strike-off status would adversely affect the interests of stakeholders. Accordingly, the Tribunal directed the Registrar of Companies to restore the company’s status from “Struck Off” to “Active”. However, considering prolonged statutory non-compliance, misleading averments and judicial time consumed, it imposed costs of ₹5,00,000, payable to the Pay and Accounts Officer, Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Proof of payment must be submitted within two weeks of the order, a certified copy must be filed with the Registrar within 30 days of uploading, and all outstanding statutory returns must be filed within 60 days of restoration with applicable fees. The Tribunal clarified that restoration would not prevent the Registrar from pursuing action for other statutory violations. FULL TEXT OF THE NCLT JUDGMENT/ORDER 1. Discussions 15. Heard Ld. Counsel for the Petitioner and the representative of the RoC and perused the record. 16. The RoC has filed a report dated 25.10.2024 stating that the Company was not carrying out any business and also failed to file its Financial Statements and Annual Report from F.Y. 2016 onwards. Therefore, RoC had issued a Public Notice in Form No. STK-5 dated 19.07.2018 to the Company and its directors proposing striking off the name of the Company and inviting objections, if any. In the absence of any representation, RoC struck off the Company’s name from the Register of Companies vide STK-7 dated 11.09.2018. It is thus submitted that RoC has complied with the due process of law. RoC has also submitted that non-compliance with statutory filings by the company shows its neglect of statutory duties and therefore if this Tribunal is inclined to allow revival of the Company, then exemplary costs may be imposed. 17. We note that the present petition has been filed under Section 252 (1) of the Companies Act, 2013 and as per the averments in the petition, the company was in operation at the time of strike-off. 18. However, during the course of hearing on different dates, upon seeking details of how the company was in operation, Ld. Counsel for the Company has candidly admitted that the company was not in operation at the time of strike-off, but the company has valuable investment and therefore earnestly submitted that the present petition be treated under Section 252 (3) of the Act instead of 252(1) of the Act as mentioned in the petition. 19. We note the conduct of the Company and the falsity in the averments which are made in a very casual manner without placing on record substantiating documents. It is only during interaction with the Bench that the Company has admitted that it was not in operation and the petition be treated under section 252(3) of the Act. A wrong or incorrect citation of a section or sub-section in the pleading will not be fatal in outcome of the petition and may be accommodated, as the technicality is mere handmaid to substantial justice. However, what worries this Tribunal is that in the path of discovery of truth, much judicial time is consumed. If the counsel/professionals would take serious efforts in proper drafting to focus on correct and true averments in a particular case without any copy & paste material (available on shelf), then it would be a great service to the judiciary by saving valuable judicial time. 20. Admittedly, the Company has defaulted in filing Financial Statements and Annual Returns from F.Y. 2017-18 onward, which has led to the strike-off of the company’s name from the Register of Companies maintained by the RoC. 21. This Tribunal can order for restoration of the Company’s name to the register of companies under section 252(3) of the Companies Act, 2013 on the grounds mentioned therein. Section 252(3) is reproduced below for ease of reference: “(3) If a company, or any member or creditor or workman thereof feels aggrieved by the company having its name struck off from the register of companies, the Tribunal on an application made by the company, member, creditor or workman before the expiry of twenty years from the publication in the Official Gazette of the notice under sub-section (5) of section 248 may, if satisfied that the company was, at the time of its name being struck off, carrying on business or in operation or otherwise it is just that the name of the company be restored to the register of companies, order the name of the company to be restored to the register of companies, and the Tribunal may, by the order, give such other directions and make such provisions as deemed just for placing the company and all other persons in the same position as nearly as may be as if the name of the company had not been struck off from the register of companies..” 22. As the Petitioner was not carrying on business or in operation at the time of strike-off, therefore, the issue that is required to be examined is whether the Company has made out a case for restoration of its name on the just ground. The Petitioner has submitted that there is substantial non- current investment made by the company, which is valuable property of the company. 23. It is a settled law that in case a struck-off company has valuable property (movable or immovable), such property belongs to the stakeholders of the company and strike-off status would deprive the stakeholders of such property. Therefore, it would be otherwise just that the name of the company be restored to the register of companies. Accordingly, this Tribunal directs the order in the following terms. ORDER a. The Tribunal directs the concerned Registrar of Companies to restore the name of the Petitioner Company i.e. Praddum Trading Company Private Limited in the register of companies maintained by it as if the name of the Company had not been struck off, with all consequential and incidental actions, including changing the status of the Company from “Struck Off” to “Active”. b. This Tribunal agrees with the submission of the RoC that the non-filing of Financial Statements and Annual Return from F.Y. 2016 indicates negligence and blatant disregard to the compliance provisions of the Companies Act, 2013. Further, considering the judicial time consumed in this case with repeated clarifications from the Company, the restoration of the name of the Petitioner Company shall be subject to payment of costs of Rs. 5,00,000/-, which shall be paid to the Pay and Accounts Officer, Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Proof of payment shall be submitted to the Registrar of Companies, Mumbai, within two weeks from the date of this order. c. The Petitioner Company is directed to file a certified copy of this order with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days from the date of uploading of this order. The Registrar of Companies shall give effect to this order only after verification of compliance with payment of costs. d. The Company is directed to comply with all pending statutory filings/ returns electronically as provided under the Companies Act, 2013, with applicable fees, additional fees within 60 (Sixty) days of restoration. e. This order is confined to the violations, which ultimately led to the action of striking off the Company and it will not come in the way of RoC to take appropriate action(s) for other violations like loan from directors in breach of public deposit rules etc., in accordance with the law. The Petitioner Company shall make good the offences, if any, arising out of non-compliance with various sections under the Companies Act, 2013. f. Urgent certified copy of this order, if applied for, be issued upon compliance with all requisite formalities. e.
