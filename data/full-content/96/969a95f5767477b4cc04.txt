Articles 5 min read RBI expands definition of bank negligence in digital frauds, proposes stronger customer safeguards The RBI has proposed widening the definition of negligence by banks to include failures such as not implementing mandatory authentication, delaying action on fraud complaints and not providing customer-set controls, while introducing a Stop Switch and a Trusted Person Mechanism to prevent digital payment frauds. Advt The proposed changes apply across commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, regional rural banks, local area banks, urban co-operative banks and rural co-operative banks.The RBI said the move was aimed at bringing greater uniformity in banking practices and protecting customers against specific acts of omission or commission by banks. The draft builds on the amendments issued on June 24, 2026, which strengthened the framework for limiting customer liability in unauthorised electronic banking transactions.While the earlier amendments primarily addressed customer liability after a fraudulent transaction, the latest proposals seek to strengthen preventive safeguards by giving customers greater control over the use of digital payment channels. The proposals also draw on public feedback received on the RBI's discussion paper, 'Exploring Safeguards in Digital Payments to Curb Frauds', issued on April 9, 2026.What will constitute negligence by banks?The draft proposes to define negligence by a bank as any act of omission or commission on its part that results in or contributes to a fraudulent electronic banking transaction in a customer's account.For commercial banks, the specified instances include failure to implement prescribed authentication factors for electronic banking transactions and multi-factor authentication for adding, modifying or deleting beneficiaries, changing account details or revising fund transfer limits.The list also includes failure to send mandatory alerts for electronic banking transactions, provide round-the-clock channels for reporting fraudulent transactions or lost debit and credit cards, and act promptly when a customer reports fraud or card loss. Advt Banks could also be considered negligent if they fail to block a digital payment channel or notify customers after the maximum number of failed login or authentication attempts, where such failure contributes to the fraud.The proposed definition further includes failure to provide customer-induced controls or implement the controls set by customers, and failure to offer or implement the Trusted Person Mechanism.System malfunction or security breaches at a bank's end, as well as collusion or connivance involving bank employees or third parties engaged by the bank, would also be covered where they facilitate or result in fraudulent transactions. Advt The draft, however, links these failures to the occurrence of a fraudulent transaction. It defines negligence in terms of an omission or commission that results in or contributes to the fraudulent electronic banking transaction.Customer controls and Stop SwitchThe RBI has proposed requiring banks to provide customers with the option to disable one or more digital payment channels linked to their accounts and subsequently re-enable them.Customers will also be able to set or modify transaction limits at multiple levels, including per transaction, per beneficiary and aggregate daily limits for individual digital payment channels. They can also set an overall daily limit across all digital payment channels and specify time windows during which selected channels remain disabled. Advt For cards, banks will have to provide customers with the facility to switch on or off different categories of transactions and set or modify limits for domestic and international transactions, ATM, point-of-sale, online and contactless payments.The draft also proposes a Stop Switch facility that will allow customers to immediately disable all future customer-initiated debits associated with their accounts. Once activated, customers will not be able to initiate debit transactions, create new payment mandates or standing instructions, or register their accounts on new third-party UPI applications.The facility will not affect incoming credits. Ongoing recurring payment mandates and standing instructions, as well as certain bank-initiated debits such as recovery of charges and loan instalments, are excluded from its scope.Additional authentication through Trusted Person MechanismUnder the proposed Trusted Person Mechanism, customers will be able to register another individual aged 18 years or above as an additional factor of authentication for electronic fund transfers exceeding a threshold set by them.For transactions above the specified threshold, the bank will notify the trusted person with details including the transaction amount and beneficiary information. The trusted person will have one hour to approve or reject the transaction. If the person rejects it or does not respond within the prescribed time, the transaction will be rejected.The mechanism will be voluntary and require the explicit consent of both the customer and the trusted person. The trusted person will not be given access to the customer's account, balance, deposits, investments or insurance details, and will not acquire the status of a joint account holder, nominee or authorised signatory.The mechanism will not apply to UPI transactions, merchant payments, irrespective of the channel or instrument used, or recurring payments through standing instructions, e-mandates or NACH mandates.The RBI has proposed that the directions come into effect from July 1, 2027, or an earlier date if a bank chooses to implement them. Specified provisions relating to the expanded definition of bank negligence are proposed to take effect from January 1, 2027.
