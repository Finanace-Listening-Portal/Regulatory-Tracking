Summary: GST on real estate and Joint Development Agreements (JDAs) continues to be governed principally by the rate and exemption framework introduced on 1 April 2019, under which under-construction residential apartments attract an effective GST rate of 1% for affordable housing and 5% for other residential apartments without input tax credit (ITC), while sales after the completion certificate or first occupation fall outside GST. This article examines the statutory framework under the CGST Act, 2017, relevant notifications, CBIC FAQs and the treatment of development rights, Transferable Development Rights (TDR), Floor Space Index (FSI), long-term leases and construction services supplied to landowners. It explains the reverse charge mechanism applicable to promoters, the 80% registered-purchase requirement, valuation rules, the deemed one-third land deduction, ITC restrictions and transitional provisions for ongoing projects. The discussion also covers the September 2025 GST rate rationalisation, the retrospective amendment to Section 17(5)(d) by the Finance Act, 2025, and important judicial developments involving Safari Retreats, Munjaal Manishbhai Bhatt and Arham Infra Developers. Through practical illustrations, comparative tables and compliance checklists, the article highlights the tax implications for developers and landowners, including GST on area-sharing and revenue-sharing JDAs, unbooked flats, construction services and cancellation of bookings. It emphasises the importance of proper JDA drafting, project-wise accounting, accurate valuation, timely reverse charge compliance and documentation of ITC adjustments, while recognising that the taxability of development rights under JDAs remains subject to judicial consideration. Constructing a residential apartment that is sold before completion carries an effective GST of 1% (affordable) or 5% (others) without input tax credit (ITC). Selling it after the completion certificate attracts no GST, because that is a sale of immovable property. Joint Development Agreements (JDAs) add a second supply: the landowner transfers development rights, and the developer constructs and hands over flats in return. The scheme of 29 March 2019 deals with both supplies through exemptions and reverse charge. This paper is built on the Circular Master: Real Estate compilation provided. That compilation covers: the CBIC FAQs of 07-05-19 and 14-05-19 Notifications 11/2017, 12/2017 and 4/2018 (Central Tax (Rate)) Notifications 3/2019 to 8/2019 and 23/2019 (Central Tax (Rate)) Notification 16/2019 (Central Tax), which amended Rules 42 and 43 for real estate the Scheme of Classification of Services It adds the developments up to November 2026: the GST 2.0 rate rationalization, the Safari Retreats judgment and the Finance Act, 2025 amendment that followed, and court rulings on JDAs and the deemed land deduction. Scope: CGST Act, 2017 and the rate notifications (with matching State notifications), as in force on 02-11-26. Statutory framework GST on real estate rests on three provisions of the CGST Act: Schedule II para 5(b), Schedule III para 5, and Section 148. On these sit a set of rate, exemption, reverse charge and time-of-supply notifications. Schedule II, para 5(b).Construction of a complex, building or civil structure intended for sale is a supply of service. This covers sale before the completion certificate or first occupation, where any consideration is received before that event. Schedule III, para 5.Sale of land, and of a building after the completion certificate or first occupation, is neither goods nor services. It is outside GST. Section 7 and “business”.Transfer of development rights by a landowner is a supply even by an individual, because “business” includes any adventure or similar activity (FAQ Part I, Q.39). Section 9(4).The promoter pays reverse charge on purchases from unregistered persons that make up the shortfall from the 80% registered-purchase requirement (Notification 7/2019). Section 148.This lets the Government set a special time of supply. Notifications 4/2018 and 6/2019 use it for JDAs. Section 17(5)(c) and (d).These block ITC on works contracts and on goods and services used to construct immovable property on one’s own account, other than plant and machinery. Other points from the FAQs Landowner-promoter and developer-promoter must make the same option (old or new rates) for an ongoing project (FAQ Part II, Q.1). The landowner does not have to reverse ITC under the 180-day rule. Its “payment” (the development rights) is made before the service is received (FAQ Part II, Q.24). The developer pays reverse charge on TDR whether the landowner is registered or not (FAQ Part II, Q.12). For development rights transferred before 01-04-19, Notification 4/2018 still applies, even where flats are allotted later. Tax arises when possession or allotment is given (FAQ Part I, Q.27 and Q.38). In redevelopment and slum rehabilitation, flats given free to existing occupants are taxable supplies. The consideration is the TDR or FSI (FAQ Part II, Q.8, Q.9). Revenue-sharing JDAs. Here the landowner receives a share of sale proceeds, not flats. The development right is still a supply, and the 18% reverse charge rule applies on the portion for unbooked or commercial flats. There is no barter construction service. The developer pays 1% / 5% on all sales. Some commentators argue that the landowner here is a co-venturer selling its own share, not a supplier of rights; the issue is open (see Arham Infra, below). Land, TDR, long-term lease and other special issues Deemed land deduction (para 2, Notification 11/2017). Where land or an undivided share of land is transferred along with construction, the land value is deemed to be one-third of the total amount charged. The FAQs say the actual land value cannot be used instead (FAQ Part I, Q.36). In Munjaal Manishbhai Bhatt v. UoI (Gujarat HC, 2022), the court held that the mandatory deeming is not valid where the actual land value is known, and read the provision down. In those cases, the actual land value may be deducted. Outside Gujarat, departments still apply one-third, and the issue is being litigated. TDR / FSI bought from another developer. This is taxable at 18% with ITC under Sl. 16(iii) of 11/2017 (heading 9972), payable by the promoter under reverse charge (FAQ Part I, Q.28). The Entry 41A exemption applies only to TDR used by a promoter for residential flats (FAQ Part II, Q.25). Long-term lease (30 years or more). The upfront premium (salami, development charges) for residential flats is exempt to the extent the flats are booked before completion (Entry 41B). The premium for unbooked flats is taxed under reverse charge on completion, within the 1% / 5% cap. The premium for commercial flats is taxed immediately. Annual or monthly lease rent is fully taxable (FAQ Part I, Q.15, Q.16). ITC transition for ongoing projects (Rules 42 and 43, as amended by 16/2019). Where an ongoing project moved to 1% / 5%, the ITC already taken is apportioned on carpet area. ITC on flats booked after completion is reversed through GSTR-3B or DRC-03, by September following the year of completion. Flats cancelled after 01-04-19 and re-booked at new rates need reversal of the old ITC taken on them (FAQ Part I, Q.22). Cancellation and credit notes. If a booking is cancelled and the full amount is refunded, the developer may adjust the GST paid through a Section 34 credit note (FAQ Part I, Q.22). The credit note is now subject to the deadline of 30 November after the year end and to the Section 34(2) proviso on ITC reversal by the recipient. That proviso does not bite where the buyer is an unregistered individual. ITC on commercial buildings for letting. In Safari Retreats (SC, 03-10-24), the Supreme Court held that a mall built for letting could be a “plant” under a functionality test, and so outside the Section 17(5)(d) block. The Finance Act, 2025 then amended Section 17(5)(d) with retrospective effect from 01-07-17, substituting “plant and machinery” for “plant or machinery”. The amendment removes that route, so ITC on buildings constructed for letting stays blocked. CBIC FAQs and clarifications The two sets of TRU FAQs (F. The project is a new residential project (non-affordable, commenced after 01-04-19), intra-State. Illustration 1: Area-sharing JDA The facts: 100 flats of 100 sq m each, 10,000 sq m in all. The landowner gets 40 flats and the developer sells 60. Similar flats were sold to outside buyers at 50 lakh each on the date nearest to the JDA. At the completion certificate, 10 of the developer’s flats are unbooked. Step Working Tax Construction service to landowner (value) 40 × 50 lakh = 20 crore Less deemed land (1/3) 20 crore − 6.67 crore = 13.33 crore GST at 7.5% on 13.33 crore (= 5% effective) Payable by developer on CC date 1.00 crore TDR value Same as the flats given: 20 crore GST on TDR but for exemption 20 crore × 18% = 3.60 crore Attributable to unbooked flats 3.60 crore × 1,000 ÷ 10,000 = 36 lakh Cap: 5% of value of unbooked flats 10 × 50 lakh × 5% = 25 lakh Reverse charge payable on TDR (lower of the two) Paid in cash on CC date 25 lakh The landowner sells 20 of its flats before the completion certificate at 55 lakh each. GST is 5% of 11 crore = 55 lakh. Against this, the landowner may use ITC of the developer’s tax on those 20 flats: 1 crore × 20/40 = 50 lakh (FAQ Part II, Q.2). The net cash payable is 5 lakh. If it sells its flats after the completion certificate instead, no GST arises, and the 50 lakh credit is lost. Illustration 2: the 80% registered-purchase test (FY 2025-26) Item Amount Inputs and input services (excluding TDR, lease premium, fuel, electricity, salaries) 10.00 crore Required from registered suppliers (80%) 8.00 crore Actually bought from registered suppliers 7.00 crore Shortfall taxed under reverse charge 1.00 crore GST at 18% (cement included at 18% after 22-09-25) 18.00 lakh The test is applied for each financial year (or up to the completion certificate). The tax is paid by June of the next year. It cannot be paid using ITC. Illustration 3: is it an affordable flat? A flat in Guwahati (not a metro city) has a carpet area of 85 sq m. The base price is 44 lakh, and parking and amenity charges are 1.5 lakh. The gross amount is 45.5 lakh, which is above the 45 lakh limit, so the flat is taxed at 5%, not 1%. Stamp duty and the maintenance deposit are left out of the gross amount. Compliance checklist Promoter / developer Classify each RERA project: RREP or REP (commercial carpet area ≤ 15%?), and affordable or other, flat by flat (carpet area and Rs. 45 lakh gross amount) Pay 1% / 5% output tax in cash; take no ITC on project inputs Keep project-wise accounts and report ITC not availed in GSTR-3B Track the 80% registered-purchase test each financial year; pay reverse charge on any shortfall (18%) by June of the next year Pay reverse charge on capital goods bought from unregistered persons On the completion certificate or first occupation: pay tax on construction for the landowner; compute reverse charge on TDR / FSI / lease premium for unbooked flats (with the 1% / 5% cap) Pay reverse charge immediately on TDR / FSI or lease premium for commercial flats Value the landowner’s flats at the price charged to independent buyers nearest to the JDA date; keep the comparable booking on file On cancellations, issue credit notes within the Sec 34(2) deadline and refund the buyer in full Keep the RERA registration, commencement certificate, architect’s certificate and completion certificate on file Landowner Register if the flats you receive will be sold before the completion certificate (you become a landowner-promoter) Charge 1% / 5% on such sales; take ITC only for the developer’s tax on your flats Choose the same old/new rate option as the developer for an ongoing project For JDAs signed before 01-04-19, check tax under Notification 4/2018 at allotment or possession Both parties Draft the JDA to state clearly what is transferred (land, development rights, TDR / FSI), the sharing ratio, and who bears GST Watch Arham Infra (SC) and keep a protective position on notices about JDA development rights Conclusion The 2019 scheme still governs residential real estate in November 2026. Flats sold before completion pay 1% or 5% without ITC, and flats sold after completion pay nothing. In a JDA, the developer’s construction for the landowner and its reverse charge on unbooked TDR both fall due at the completion certificate. The pressure points have moved elsewhere: contractors now charge 18% that promoters cannot recover ITC on rental buildings is blocked retrospectively whether a JDA is a supply at all is pending before the Supreme Court in Arham Infra Until that is decided, draft JDAs carefully, document valuations, and keep protective positions on notices.
