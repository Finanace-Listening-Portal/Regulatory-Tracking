Summary: Internet Computer Protocol (ICP) is a blockchain network that uses independent subnets to execute decentralised applications known as canisters. These canisters combine WebAssembly code, persistent state and messaging capabilities, while the Network Nervous System (NNS) manages protocol governance. The native ICP token supports transfers, governance participation, node rewards and conversion into cycles used to pay for computation and storage. ICP holders can participate in governance through neurons, although staking involves variable rewards, asset-price exposure and restrictions arising from dissolve delays. The article examines network architecture, ledger transfers, canister upgrade controls, custody arrangements and associated security risks. It distinguishes exchange custody from self-custody and highlights vulnerabilities involving private keys, application code, governance decisions and irreversible transactions. For Indian taxpayers, ICP transactions may attract virtual digital asset taxation, including 30% tax on qualifying transfer income, applicable surcharge and cess, restrictions on deductions and loss set-off, and 1% TDS subject to prescribed conditions and thresholds. Governance rewards, swaps, gifts and cross-border transactions may require separate examination. The article also explains FIU-IND compliance considerations, transaction reporting and record-keeping requirements. It concludes that investors and users should independently verify asset identity, canister controls, custody safeguards, taxation and regulatory obligations before undertaking ICP transactions. Key Takeaways Show all 18 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Internet Computer Architecture: Subnets, Nodes and Canisters The Internet Computer Protocol is not a single replicated chain on which every node performs every task. It is composed of subnets. Each subnet is a group of nodes running its own instance of the protocol, maintaining a blockchain and executing canisters assigned to that subnet. Subnets operate in parallel, so additional subnets can add capacity without forcing every application to compete for the same execution lane. A user still encounters one network because message routing, chain-key cryptography and common governance coordinate the underlying components. Canisters combine code, state and a message interface A canister bundles compiled WebAssembly code with persistent state. It behaves as an actor: it receives messages, updates its private state and may send messages to other canisters. Update calls pass through subnet consensus and can change state; query-style access may be faster but has a different verification model. Stable memory is designed to persist across upgrades, while ordinary heap handling depends on the canister and language. A controller can upgrade, stop or delete a canister. Control may remain with one developer, be shared through multisignature arrangements, be transferred to an SNS governance system or be removed. Users therefore need to inspect the controller and upgrade model of the particular application, not assume that every canister is immutable. What ICP Does Inside the Protocol Governance neurons and dissolve delay ICP can be locked in an NNS neuron. A neuron has stake, dissolve delay, age and voting behaviour. Voting power is affected by these parameters, and voting rewards accrue as maturity before they are converted into newly minted ICP or merged into stake. Locking is not a bank deposit: the holder accepts protocol rules, a delay before disbursement and exposure to the ICP asset throughout the lock. Following another neuron delegates voting behaviour but does not remove the need to understand proposals or the consequences of automated voting. Cycles separate computation cost from the token balance Developers convert ICP into cycles through the Cycles Minting Canister. Cycles are consumed by canister computation and storage, and the protocol uses an XDR-linked conversion design to make infrastructure costs more stable in reference-currency terms than a raw ICP price would be. Converting ICP to cycles burns the ICP. Conversely, node-provider rewards and realised governance rewards can mint ICP. Supply is therefore dynamic; it is not accurately described by a fixed-cap slogan. Transaction fees and cycle conversion reduce supply, while protocol rewards can increase it. Ledger transfers and account formats The ICP ledger records balances, transfers, mints and burns. Current developer documentation identifies the ledger as ICRC-1 and ICRC-2 compatible and advises software to query fee and decimal information at runtime instead of hard-coding it. The published example fee for an ordinary ICP transfer is 0.0001 ICP, but a wallet or exchange may add its own withdrawal fee. A valid on-chain transfer cannot ordinarily be reversed by a customer-care desk. Test the address format, network and small transfer before moving a material amount. Custody and Application Risks Are Separate Holding ICP on an exchange gives the account provider control of the private keys and introduces counterparty, withdrawal, operational and compliance risk. Self-custody removes that intermediary but transfers responsibility for seed phrases, device security, signing prompts and recovery. Governance staking adds neuron permissions, dissolve-delay choices and maturity handling. Using a canister application adds code, controller, oracle, cross-chain and frontend risks. Chain-key integrations can reduce reliance on conventional bridges in some designs, but they do not make an application or its user interface immune from defects, governance decisions or social engineering. Verify the ICP ledger or supported network before depositing or withdrawing; similarly named assets and wrapped representations are not interchangeable. Review controller status and audit information for the specific canister rather than treating network security as an application guarantee. Record acquisition cost, transfer consideration, fees and TDS transaction by transaction; wallet history alone may not establish rupee values. Do not treat governance rewards, maturity or staking displays as guaranteed income; protocol parameters and tax character can require case-specific review. Indian Tax, TDS and AML Position for ICP Transactions For Indian tax purposes, ICP will ordinarily fall within the virtual digital asset framework when the statutory definition applies. Income-tax Department guidance states that income from transfer of a VDA is taxed at 30% plus applicable surcharge and cess, with deduction limited to cost of acquisition and without set-off of a VDA transfer loss against other income. Transaction-wise Schedule VDA reporting is required in relevant returns. From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 reorganised withholding: consideration paid to a resident for transfer of a VDA is covered by section 393(1), Table serial number 8(vi), at 1%, subject to the statutory thresholds and operational rules. A specified individual or HUF has a ₹50,000 tax-year threshold; the threshold is ₹10,000 for other payers. TDS is a collection mechanism, not the final tax. Crypto-to-crypto swaps and consideration in kind can still create transfer and withholding questions. Mining, governance rewards, airdrops, business inventory, gifts, foreign-source receipts and non-resident transfers may require different timing, valuation and reporting analysis. FIU-IND guidance applies to service providers carrying on notified VDA activities for or on behalf of others; an exchange registration does not make ICP legal tender, guarantee solvency or approve a token. Indian users should retain exchange statements, wallet identifiers, INR valuation support, TDS certificates and evidence of purpose and residency. No. ICP is the native digital asset used for transfers, governance and protocol economics. Cycles are computational fuel created by converting and burning ICP; canisters consume cycles for execution and storage. Does a canister run on only one computer? A canister is assigned to a subnet and its state and execution are replicated across the subnet nodes. The subnet reaches consensus on ordered messages and resulting state. Can an ICP transfer be cancelled? A confirmed ledger transfer is not a normal bank payment that customer service can reverse. Recovery depends on the recipient and any applicable custodian process, so address and network checks matter before signing. Does staking ICP guarantee a return? No. Voting rewards depend on protocol rules, neuron settings and participation, while ICP value and liquidity remain variable. Dissolve delay can restrict access to stake. How is an ICP sale taxed in India? When the VDA provisions apply, positive transfer income is subject to the statutory 30% rate plus surcharge and cess, with only cost of acquisition deductible and restricted loss treatment. Facts and taxpayer status still matter. Is 1% TDS the final tax on an ICP transfer? No. The 1% deduction under the current withholding provision is a credit mechanism. Final liability is computed under the applicable VDA income rules and reported in the return. Does FIU registration mean an exchange or token is government-approved? No. Reporting-entity registration addresses AML obligations for the service provider. It is not a token approval, solvency guarantee, price validation or legal-tender status. Key Takeaways ICP separates canister computation, protocol governance and the native token, so each layer needs its own risk review. The network uses subnets, chain-key cryptography, an NNS governance system and ledger canisters; individual applications can still retain upgrade controllers. Indian VDA tax and TDS depend on transaction facts, not on whether value stays inside a wallet or is exchanged for another token.
