The forecast implies another 50 basis points of tightening, although the cycle is expected to remain shallow as underlying inflation pressures remain relatively contained.The RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee unanimously raised the repo rate by 25 basis points at its October 5 to 7 meeting and shifted its stance to calibrated tightening from neutral. Two external members favoured retaining the neutral stance, while most members emphasised that the change did not signal a series of hikes. Instead, it indicated that rate cuts were off the table for now, with the future policy path allowing for further increases or a pause, according to Barclays India. Advt The brokerage said the probability of a consecutive hike in December had increased. It expects core consumer price inflation at 4.2%, below the MPC’s projection of 4.4%, suggesting that second round price effects and demand side price pressures remain relatively contained. It has raised its FY27 consumer price inflation forecast to 5.2%, in line with the RBI’s projection, and expects inflation to soften to 4.9% in FY28.The minutes revealed differing views among MPC members on whether inflationary pressures were spreading beyond supply side shocks and becoming entrenched. Some members cited rising wholesale price inflation, enterprise surveys and resilient demand as evidence of broader price pressures, while others saw limited signs of generalisation.The viewsGovernor Sanjay Malhotra highlighted increasing signs of generalisation, the difficulty of distinguishing indirect and second round effects of supply shocks, and risks from demand side pressures. He said the depth of the hiking cycle would depend on actual growth and inflation developments, particularly underlying inflation, the broadening of price pressures and the impact of demand impulses. Underlying inflation is measured as core CPI excluding precious metals, which stood at 3.2% year on year in September.Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta supported the rate hike because headline inflation was projected to reach the upper tolerance limit of 6% in the coming quarter. She argued that monetary policy needed to act before more definitive evidence of generalisation emerged because its effects operate with a lag. Indranil Bhattacharyya also favoured a pre emptive increase, citing persistent supply pressures, strong demand and growth in monetary and credit aggregates. Advt Other members cautioned against an extended tightening cycle. Ram Singh favoured incremental, mild repo rate increases rather than keeping rates high for long, warning that excessive tightening could undermine demand and weaken momentum in private investment. Nagesh Kumar supported a rate increase but preferred retaining a neutral stance amid geopolitical and trade uncertainties.The brokerage does not expect outsized rate increases or a repeat of the 2013 style rate defence to support the rupee in 2026. It said the RBI appeared inclined to use non rate instruments to address currency pressures and liquidity conditions, following measures announced for the rupee and steps to tighten liquidity.
