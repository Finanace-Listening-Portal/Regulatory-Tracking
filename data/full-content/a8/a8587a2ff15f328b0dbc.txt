While several requirements were already part of the regulatory framework, their consolidation and wider operational requirements could increase the compliance burden on payment operators. Advt The directions apply to all authorised non-bank PSOs, including the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), card networks, payment aggregators, prepaid payment instrument issuers and white-label ATM operators. Large and medium operators must comply from the date the directions were placed on RBI's website, while small operators have until April 1, 2028. Payment aggregators, NPCI and card networks are classified as large operators and are therefore subject to immediate compliance.The framework requires operators to establish a Security Operations Centre (SOC) for centralised monitoring of network and system logs, supported by automated tools to detect suspicious activity and coordinate responses. They must also maintain a manned facility operating round the clock throughout the year to address customer reports of unauthorised or fraudulent transactions and respond to law enforcement agencies.Tightening rulesOperators must subject all applications to security testing at least once a year, including source-code reviews, vulnerability assessments and penetration testing. They must also submit annual System Audit Reports within two months of the close of their financial year. The audits must be conducted by CERT-In-empanelled auditors, CISA-qualified auditors registered with ISACA or professionals holding the ICAI's Diploma in Information System Audit qualification. Auditors and their sister concerns must not have provided services to the entity during the preceding two financial years. Advt The rules also tighten controls over third-party technology providers. Operators must obtain the source code of critical applications procured from external vendors. Where this is not possible, they must put in place an escrow arrangement to ensure continuity of services. If the operator does not own the source code, it must obtain a certificate from the developer confirming that the application is free of vulnerabilities, malware and covert channels. A fresh certificate is required whenever the source code is changed.For vendors involved in critical processes and activities, operators must obtain independent auditor-certified assurance of their cyber resilience capabilities. They must also ensure that unregulated entities connected to their digital payments ecosystem, including payment gateways and technology vendors, adhere to the prescribed requirements, subject to mutual agreement and a board-approved policy. Advt The framework also requires operators to strengthen business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements. Disaster recovery facilities must be located in a different seismic zone from the primary data centre, with drills conducted at least once every six months. Operators must strive to achieve a near-zero Recovery Point Objective, which measures the amount of data that could be lost following a disruption.The board of each operator will be responsible for overseeing information security risks, including cyber risks and resilience. It must approve an information security policy, which will be reviewed annually, as well as a separate Cyber Crisis Management Plan to detect, contain, respond to and recover from cyber threats and attacks. Primary oversight may be delegated to a board sub-committee headed by a member with information or cybersecurity expertise, which must meet at least once every quarter. Advt The directions also require unusual incidents, including cyberattacks, critical system outages, internal fraud and settlement delays, to be reported to RBI within six hours of detection. Cybersecurity incidents must also be reported to the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team.The additional requirements could weigh more heavily on smaller operators, particularly those that need to build security-monitoring capabilities, strengthen vendor oversight and upgrade disaster recovery arrangements. However, the RBI has given small PPI issuers and instant money transfer operators until April 2028 to comply. The consolidation also provides a single reference point for operators, replacing six earlier circulars and directions.
