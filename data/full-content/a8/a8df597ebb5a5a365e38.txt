The bank is yet to complete its assessment of the implications, according to a report by ICICI Securities, which cut its target price on the stock to Rs17 from Rs24 and retained its HOLD rating. Advt The brokerage cut its FY27 growth estimates to about 9% amid uncertainty over the implications of the ruling. Yes Bank’s loans grew 24% year on year and 9% sequentially in the September quarter, while deposits rose 12% sequentially, driven by foreign currency non-resident (FCNR) deposits of Rs18,600 crore. However, the potential capital impact of the ruling could weigh on the outlook for further expansion.The bank’s net interest margin (NIM) contracted 10 basis points sequentially to 2.60% in the second quarter, even as net interest income rose 23% year on year and 1% sequentially. Yes Bank expects NIM to remain stable sequentially and rise in the fourth quarter, supported by repo-rate tailwinds and a reduction in its Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) balances, although uncertainty remains over prospective coupon payments.Outstanding RIDF balances declined about 6% sequentially to Rs25,800 crore, equivalent to 5% of assets, and are expected to fall further in the second half of FY27. The bank’s cost of deposits rose 10 basis points sequentially, while yield on advances declined 20 basis points, partly reflecting the impact of strong FCNR deposit growth.Asset quality a concernAsset quality remains another concern. Retail loan slippages have remained in the Rs840-900 crore range over the past two to three quarters, lagging the improvement seen at peers, according to the brokerage. Special Mention Account 1 and 2 loans remained stable at 0.9%, while credit costs were about 30 basis points. The brokerage estimates credit costs of around 0.4% for FY27 and FY28. Advt Separately, the transition to the expected credit loss framework is estimated to have a capital impact of 50-60 basis points.The brokerage lowered its target price to Rs17 from Rs24, valuing the stock at about 0.9 times estimated FY28 adjusted book value, compared with 1.2 times earlier. It identified slower resolution of the AT1 issue as a downside risk, while stronger fee income from a potential merchant discount rate on UPI transactions could provide an upside. Yes Bank could be a significant beneficiary of such a change, although the actual gains would depend on competitive dynamics.
