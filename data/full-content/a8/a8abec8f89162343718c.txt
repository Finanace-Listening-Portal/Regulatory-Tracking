Equitas Small Finance Bank, IDFC First Bank, Bandhan Bank and RBL Bank are among the banks with the highest reported exposure. IndusInd Bank also has significant bancassurance income, although its FY26 profit ratio is distorted by exceptional losses.Insurance distribution income was equivalent to 70.7 per cent of Equitas Small Finance Bank's FY26 profit before tax (PBT), 48.8 per cent of IDFC First Bank's, 30.8 per cent of Bandhan Bank's and 28.5 per cent of RBL Bank's. The corresponding ratio was 21 per cent for DCB Bank and 19.3 per cent for Yes Bank. The figures highlight the importance of insurance distribution as a source of fee income for these lenders. Advt IndusInd Bank recorded a much higher ratio of 75.6 per cent of FY26 PBT. However, the figure needs to be viewed in the context of exceptional losses that reduced the bank's profit during the year. Its FY26 bancassurance income was equivalent to 18 per cent of its FY27 estimated normalised profit, offering a more meaningful perspective on its exposure.The proposed changes by the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) could affect the economics of banks distributing life and general insurance policies. Its September 23 consultation paper proposes recalibrating commissions according to the product, distribution channel, complexity and effort involved in selling and servicing policies. It also proposes including indirect and non-monetary remuneration within the definition of commission and prohibiting volume-linked or reward-linked incentives for bank and NBFC employees selling insurance. The proposals are not final regulations.SFB exposure highSmall finance banks have particularly high exposure relative to their balance sheets. Insurance distribution income accounts for an estimated 13-25 basis points of average assets across the segment, compared with 1-3 basis points for public sector banks. Among individual small finance banks, Equitas reported insurance income equivalent to 0.13 per cent of average assets, while the ratio was 0.25 per cent for Ujjivan Small Finance Bank and 0.22 per cent for AU Small Finance Bank.Among larger private sector banks, Axis Bank has the highest reported exposure, with insurance income equivalent to 12.5 per cent of FY26 PBT. The ratio was 7.3 per cent at HDFC Bank, 5.8 per cent at Kotak Mahindra Bank and 0.6 per cent at ICICI Bank. City Union Bank and Federal Bank reported ratios of 8.3 per cent and 7.3 per cent, respectively. Advt Public sector banks have relatively lower exposure. Insurance commission income was equivalent to 2.6 per cent of SBI's FY26 PBT, 2.2 per cent of Canara Bank's, 1.6 per cent of Punjab National Bank's and 1.4 per cent of Bank of Baroda's. SBI, however, earned Rs 2,795 crore in insurance commissions during FY26, indicating that a low ratio relative to PBT does not necessarily mean a small absolute income contribution.The impact will also depend on the type of insurance products distributed by each lender and the structure of its tie-ups with insurers. Banks with multiple insurance partnerships and incentive-led distribution arrangements could face greater pressure than those operating under more restricted distribution arrangements. Across the banks covered in the assessment, insurance distribution income rose from 3.5 per cent of PBT in FY23 to 5.1 per cent in FY26, while its contribution relative to average assets increased from four to seven basis points. Life insurance accounted for 84 per cent of total insurance income. Advt Banks are exploring alternative fee-income streams to offset any reduction. Bandhan Bank has estimated that the proposed changes could affect its annual third-party product income by around Rs 200 crore, although this estimate covers its broader third-party distribution business. The bank has said its insurance distribution is predominantly retail and does not include credit-linked insurance. It is looking to expand mutual fund distribution, foreign exchange and trade finance to diversify fee income.RBL Bank, meanwhile, has estimated that its bancassurance income could decline by 50-60 per cent if the proposals are implemented in their current form. Its FY26 bancassurance income stood at Rs 296.49 crore, comprising Rs 272.49 crore from life insurance and Rs 24 crore from general insurance. This would imply a potential annual income impact of around Rs 148-178 crore, based on FY26 income. Advt The eventual earnings impact will depend on the final commission framework, the transition period and banks' ability to compensate for lower payouts through higher volumes, changes in product mix and other fee-generating businesses. The FY26 ratios indicate the relative importance of insurance distribution to bank earnings, but do not represent estimates of the actual reduction in profits.
