Key Takeaways Show all 17 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); MultiversX and EGLD in the Current Protocol MultiversX is a public layer-1 blockchain that settles transactions on its own network. EGLD is its native coin, not an ERC-20 token issued on Ethereum and not a contractual claim against a company. Network users spend EGLD for transaction fees and smart-contract interactions; validators lock EGLD to participate in consensus; delegators can assign stake to a staking provider; and governance participants may use staked voting power where a proposal’s rules permit. None of those functions creates a guaranteed redemption right, a promised market price or a regulated deposit. The project launched its mainnet in July 2020 and converted the earlier ERD unit into EGLD at 1,000 ERD for one EGLD. That historical redenomination matters when reconstructing old acquisition records: a taxpayer should not treat a quantity shown in a pre-mainnet ERD statement as the same number of EGLD. Wrapped or bridged representations also require separate identification because a token on another chain can carry bridge, smart-contract and issuer risks that native EGLD does not. Each execution shard processes a portion of accounts and transactions rather than every validator executing the full global workload. A metachain coordinates shard information and notarizes shard blocks. The current documentation states that mainnet has operated with three execution shards plus the metachain; the word adaptive describes the protocol capability to alter the shard configuration, not a claim that the live network constantly adds and removes shards. A cross-shard transfer is not equivalent to a single database write. The sending shard, destination shard and metachain coordination must align. Wallets, explorers and applications may present intermediate states differently, so a user should verify the transaction hash, sender, receiver, asset identifier, nonce, status and final amount. Display latency is not necessarily a protocol reversal, but a copied address, malicious application or incorrectly selected token cannot be cured by waiting. Secure Proof of Stake and finality The network uses Secure Proof of Stake. Validators are selected into consensus groups, a proposer creates a block and the group supplies signatures. Official documentation describes deterministic finality once the required consensus proof is produced. This design does not eliminate software bugs, validator concentration, governance disputes, network partitions, compromised keys or application-level exploits. Finality means the chain has accepted the transaction under its rules; it does not mean the recipient is trustworthy or that an off-chain bargain was lawful. Staking V5, Delegation and the Revised Emissions Model The economic model changed materially in late 2025. Official release notes for protocol version 1.11.1 describe Staking V5 and activation of tail inflation, replacing the earlier genesis-based fixed-supply model. The release divides theoretical annual emission among staking rewards, ecosystem growth, a user growth dividend and protocol sustainability, with some buckets dependent on ecosystem metrics. Accordingly, older descriptions of a permanent 31.4 million hard cap are no longer a complete account of current protocol economics. Wallet, Bridge and Smart-Contract Evidence Self-custody transfers control to whoever holds the signing key. Loss of a seed phrase, malware, a fraudulent browser extension or an incorrect address can result in irreversible loss. Hardware wallets reduce some exposure but do not validate the commercial purpose of a transaction. Centralized platforms add custody, withdrawal, solvency, account-freeze and record-availability risks. A platform listing is not proof of Indian regulatory approval, FIU registration, tax compliance or suitability. Bridges and decentralized applications introduce contracts beyond the base chain. Before signing, a user should identify the chain ID, exact contract, requested permission, amount limit, destination and whether the transaction is a transfer, delegation, token approval or contract call. Screenshots alone are weak accounting evidence because they can omit timestamps and identifiers. Exported histories, transaction hashes, wallet addresses, exchange statements and contemporaneous INR valuation workpapers form a stronger audit trail. Maintain a wallet-by-wallet opening balance and reconcile every receipt, transfer, fee and closing balance. Label native EGLD separately from wrapped, bridged or liquidity-pool positions. Record the INR valuation source and timestamp used for each taxable receipt or transfer. Keep staking rewards separate from disposal proceeds and document any provider commission. Retain proof of cost after moving assets between self-controlled wallets; an internal transfer should not erase acquisition history. Indian Tax Treatment for EGLD Transactions in 2026 The Income-tax Act, 2025 has applied from 1 April 2026. Its VDA framework retains the core policy that income from transfer of a virtual digital asset is taxed at 30 percent, with applicable surcharge and cess, and permits the cost of acquisition while denying other expenditure deductions and loss set-off or carry-forward for this special computation. Under the new Act, the corresponding special-rate table appears in section 194. Readers should distinguish that current numbering from legacy references to section 115BBH of the repealed 1961 Act. For payments or credits on or after 1 April 2026, the consolidated TDS provision is section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. The official transition guidance states that the VDA TDS rate and thresholds were retained, while individuals and HUFs using the challan-cum-statement route use Form 141 under the 2026 Rules. The person responsible for deduction depends on how the transaction is structured and whether an exchange has contractually assumed the obligation. A token-for-token exchange can create both a transfer event and an in-kind TDS funding issue. Risk Boundaries for Indian Readers MultiversX documentation establishes what the protocol is designed to do; it does not certify the legal status, solvency or cybersecurity of every service that supports EGLD. Price volatility can be severe, liquidity can fragment across venues, and a delisting or compliance restriction can impair access. Staking can lock assets while market conditions change. Smart contracts, bridges and token issuers can fail independently of the base chain. Indian residents should separately verify the identity and compliance status of any intermediary they use. The base protocol is not a grievance authority for an exchange account, a bridge or an impersonation scam. No protocol metric, emission schedule, validator count or historical return supports a prediction. The correct decision depends on the reader’s legal capacity, source of funds, custody competence, tax records and tolerance for total loss. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Is EGLD the same asset as the former ERD token? EGLD is the current native coin. The project converted the former ERD unit at 1,000 ERD to one EGLD. Historical records should preserve that conversion instead of copying the old quantity into a current ledger. 2. Does MultiversX still have a fixed maximum supply of about 31.4 million EGLD? That description is outdated after the 2025 economic change. Staking V5 introduced tail inflation and new reward buckets, so current analysis should use the live protocol rules and official release notes rather than the earlier hard-cap narrative. 3. How is validator staking different from delegation? Running a validator requires infrastructure, validator keys and the protocol stake per node. Delegation assigns EGLD to a staking-provider contract and shifts day-to-day node operation to the provider, while leaving the delegator exposed to provider and contract performance. 4. Can unstaked EGLD be withdrawn immediately? No. An unstake request begins an unbonding period. Official documentation describes roughly 10 epochs for staking-provider funds before unbonding can make them withdrawable. 5. Is a finalized blockchain transaction proof that the recipient is legitimate? No. Finality shows that the network accepted the signed transaction. It does not validate an off-chain promise, reverse a scam, prove regulatory approval or guarantee that a smart contract is safe. 6. Which Indian provisions apply to a VDA transfer after 1 April 2026? The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies. Its special VDA rate is reflected in section 194, while VDA TDS is consolidated in section 393. Rates, thresholds and reporting mechanics should be checked for the transaction date and taxpayer category. 7. Should a recovery phrase be shared for audit or tax verification? Never. A recovery phrase or private key grants control. Provide transaction exports, hashes, statements, addresses and suitably redacted or view-only evidence instead. Adaptive state sharding uses parallel execution shards and a coordinating metachain, while Secure Proof of Stake provides consensus and deterministic finality.
