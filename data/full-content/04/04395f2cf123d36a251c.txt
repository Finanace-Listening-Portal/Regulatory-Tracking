The permitted pairs are USD-INR, EUR-INR, GBP-INR, JPY-INR, EUR-USD, GBP-USD and USD-JPY. NSE’s current product pages show futures and options on the four INR pairs and cross-currency products. Contract availability, expiries and order acceptance are exchange and broker matters, so the trading screen should be checked before an order is entered. NSE states that one contract represents 1,000 units for USD, EUR and GBP pairs and 100,000 yen for JPY-INR. INR-pair contracts are cash-settled in rupees. Its published specifications describe daily settlement on T+1 and final settlement on T+2 for the relevant currency-option framework, with the final settlement price linked to the applicable RBI reference rate. Margins are calculated under exchange risk systems and can change; a displayed margin is not the maximum possible loss. Purpose restrictions are different for INR and cross-currency pairs For foreign-currency versus INR contracts, the permitted purpose is hedging a valid contracted exposure. RBI allows a user to take long or short positions without first establishing the underlying exposure only within the prescribed aggregate threshold, but the exposure must still exist, remain unhedged elsewhere and be capable of proof. For foreign-currency versus foreign-currency pairs, RBI’s direction does not impose the same purpose restriction. This distinction does not waive exchange, broker, tax, suitability, margin or position-limit rules. The limit is a single aggregate across all INR currency pairs and all recognised stock exchanges. The circular took immediate effect. It did not convert the facility into permission for an exposure-free position: the user must still have a valid contracted exposure that has not been hedged through another derivative. IIFL’s publicly accessible currency declaration still recites the earlier USD 100 million threshold and cites the January 2024 framework. That document is useful evidence that the broker offers the currency segment and requires an exposure undertaking, but its numeric threshold is no longer current. A client should follow the later RBI direction, any implementing exchange circular and the broker’s updated onboarding instruction. Reliance on a stale declaration cannot expand FEMA permission. RBI also restricted rebooking of cancelled INR-involving derivative contracts while continuing to permit rollover at maturity subject to the rules. Its separate Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve directions apply to specified derivative contracts undertaken by Authorised Dealers; they should not be presented as a brokerage charge automatically payable by every exchange-traded retail client. The relevant exchange margin, broker ledger and contract note remain the client-facing records for the service profiled here. Account Opening, KYC and Segment Activation A trading account and a registered bank account are required. Standard KYC normally involves PAN, proof of identity and address, bank evidence, contact verification and the applicable FATCA or tax-residency declarations. Currency-derivative activation may require income or financial-capacity evidence and acceptance of derivative risk disclosures. The exact list depends on investor type, current KYC status, mode of onboarding and the broker’s risk policy. Verify that the account-opening screen and payment handle identify IIFL Capital Services Limited and the disclosed SEBI registration. Complete KYC and link a bank account in the same name; do not transfer funds to a personal account or an unverified collection agent. Request currency-segment activation and provide the financial evidence or declarations currently required. Read the risk disclosure, tariff sheet, exchange contract specifications and the current RBI exposure undertaking. Confirm that only the intended recognised-exchange segment is enabled before placing an order. Retain the order log, trade confirmation, contract note, daily margin statement, ledger and bank movement for each transaction. An authorised person or relationship manager may assist with onboarding, but the legal broker remains the registered entity. The intermediary should not be described as an RBI-authorised money changer merely because it facilitates a currency-derivative account. Client credentials, one-time passwords and remote-screen access should not be shared with a salesperson or support caller. Trading profits or losses may be taxable according to the taxpayer’s facts, classification and current Income-tax Act, 2025 provisions. GST on the broker’s services is not the same as income tax on the client’s trading result. Professional advice is appropriate where turnover, business classification, audit, set-off or reporting questions arise. Settlement, Payouts and Account Closure Risks Currency derivatives are margined instruments. A client may have to supply additional funds when prices move, margins increase or positions approach risk limits. The broker can square off positions under its risk policy, and an intraday label does not guarantee execution at the expected price. Options buyers pay premium, while option writers and futures positions can face substantial margin and mark-to-market demands. Cash settlement avoids delivery of foreign currency but does not cap loss by itself. IIFL’s funds-payout FAQ allows an online payout request to a bank account mapped to the trading account and describes processing subject to clear credit and exchange settlement. The page gives timing illustrations, but a user should treat them as operational guidance rather than a guaranteed deadline. Open positions, unsettled obligations, margin blocks, bank holidays, compliance review or an incorrect bank mapping can delay availability. A payout should never be redirected to a third-party account. For a disputed trade or ledger entry, preserve the order identifier, exchange time, contract note, communications and bank proof. Prompt written notice is preferable to an undocumented phone call. Closing a segment or account should include confirmation that open positions are closed, obligations are settled, securities or cash are transferred and no standing instruction remains active. Grievance Escalation and Practical Limitations IIFL publishes client support and broking-grievance email channels, together with a compliance-officer address. A client should begin with the broker using the registered contact details and retain the ticket. If unresolved, exchange investor-service and SEBI SCORES mechanisms may be available according to the grievance type and current procedure. Fraud, impersonation or unauthorised bank movement may also require immediate notice to the bank and relevant authorities.
