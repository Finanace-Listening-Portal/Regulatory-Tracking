Articles 3 min read Sebi mulls early payout of up to 80% of sale proceeds to FPOs in commodity derivatives market Sebi introduced a proposal for an early pay-out framework designed to assist Farmer Producer Organisations. This framework would allow FPOs to access up to 80 percent of sale proceeds before contract expiry. The initiative aims to alleviate liquidity challenges by providing timely payments to farmers. Corporate trading members will offer this facility based on certain conditions and net worth requirements. Public comments on the proposal are invited until December 16. Advt FPOs aggregate the agricultural produce of small and marginal farmers and facilitate their access to organised markets, including exchange-based trading platforms."When FPOs deposit stocks in accredited warehouses for sale on the exchange, they have to wait till the contract expiry for realisation of funds from that sale transaction. Due to this, FPOs face a liquidity crunch till their funds are realised and hence have to operate with limited liquidity," Sebi noted."While aggregating produce, FPOs pay farmers almost immediately but have to wait for the stocks to be sold to realise payments, to enable fresh aggregations. Most FPOs operate with limited working capital and are required to make prompt payments to member farmers. Delays in realisation of funds may adversely affect their liquidity position, farmer confidence, and participation in exchange-based markets."Under the proposed framework, the early pay-out facility would be available only to FPO clients dealing in the agricultural commodity derivatives segment who have marked Early Pay-In (EPI) of goods against executed sale transactions.The facility would be offered by corporate trading members having a minimum net worth of Rs 3 crore. Further, the maximum exposure of a trading member towards the EPO facility would be capped at three times its net worth."Trading members may then release early pay-out to FPO clients against the goods marked for EPI and for which the sale transactions of derivatives contracts have been executed by the FPO client on the exchange platform. A maximum of 80 per cent of the value of the sale transaction of the derivatives contract executed on the exchange may be released as early pay-out to the FPO clients," Sebi said. Advt They may also levy charges for providing the facility, subject to prior disclosure of the terms and conditions and obtaining the FPO's consent.The regulator has proposed that trading members can use their own funds or raise resources through scheduled commercial banks, RBI-regulated NBFCs, commercial papers, unsecured long-term loans from promoters and directors, and listed non-convertible debentures.However, funds belonging to other clients cannot be used for providing the facility.The proposal also provides that FPO clients receiving early payouts would not be allowed to square off the corresponding positions. Advt Sebi said the proposal was deliberated by a working group reviewing the regulatory framework for delivery and settlement in agricultural commodity derivatives.It was subsequently placed before the Commodity Derivatives Advisory Committee (CDAC), which broadly agreed with the recommendations.The regulator has invited public comments on the proposed framework by December 16.
