(Image Source: Pixabay)follow usfollow usThe government could consider scrapping the long-term capital gains (LTCG) tax without significantly compromising its fiscal consolidation targets, as the potential revenue loss may be limited to Rs 40,000–50,000 crore, according to a report by SBI Research. The estimated shortfall is considerably lower than the Rs 1.3 lakh crore collected through capital gains taxation in FY25, when Indian equity markets recorded returns of nearly 30 per cent. SBI Research cautioned against treating the FY25 collection figure as a realistic estimate of the revenue the government would forgo if LTCG tax were abolished.“Thus, it is incorrect to say that the Government is going to lose Rs 1.3 lakh crore of revenue from capital gains tax,” the report said.The research report pointed out that capital gains tax collections are expected to moderate amid subdued stock market performance and continued selling by foreign institutional investors (FIIs). This could give the government greater room to reconsider the existing taxation framework.“Even if we assume that if the Government decides to abolish LTCG tax, the shortfall for FY28 will be only in the range of Rs 40,000–50,000 crore,” SBI Research said.The report added that the estimated revenue impact would represent approximately 0.1 per cent-0.2 per cent of India's GDP, making it a potentially manageable adjustment for the Centre.“We believe the Government would be able to take this hit if hypothetically, the entire capital gains taxes were abolished.”Why SBI Research Expects Lower LTCG Tax CollectionsAccording to SBI Research, LTCG collections could have reached approximately Rs 1 lakh crore in FY26. However, the figure may decline further to Rs 40,000–50,000 crore in FY27.The report attributed the expected moderation to relatively weak equity market returns and persistent foreign investor withdrawals. The average Nifty return over the three-year period covering FY25 to FY27 so far stands at just 0.5 per cent.Meanwhile, cumulative foreign institutional investor (FII) outflows have touched Rs 4.8 lakh crore, adding to the pressure on the domestic equity market.These developments suggest that using the exceptional tax collections recorded during FY25 to calculate the potential cost of eliminating LTCG taxation could present an exaggerated picture of the actual revenue impact.Fiscal Deficit Concerns Remain On Government's RadarThe proposal comes at a time when the Centre is dealing with mounting pressure on its fiscal position.SBI Research estimates that the government's fiscal deficit in FY27 could overshoot the Budget Estimate by approximately Rs 1.2 lakh crore. The expected slippage has been linked to lower excise duties, increased fertiliser subsidies and higher expenditure on petroleum and LPG subsidies.The report has projected the fiscal deficit at 4.7 per cent of GDP, against the government's budgeted target of 4.3 per cent.To manage the additional pressure, the Centre may have to rationalise expenditure, maintain stronger cash balances and reduce borrowings below the initially planned levels.The government's estimated borrowing requirement has already declined to Rs 15.97 lakh crore from the budgeted Rs 17.2 lakh crore following debt switches and buybacks.Additionally, SBI Research estimates that the Centre's cash balance, after adjusting for core liquidity, stood at around Rs 8.5 lakh crore at the end of October 2026.What Changes Has SBI Research Proposed For LTCG Tax?Instead of continuing with the existing taxation structure, SBI Research has outlined several changes that could make the system more favourable for long-term equity investors.One of the key recommendations is to increase the LTCG tax exemption limit from the existing Rs 1.25 lakh to Rs 2 lakh. The report also suggested linking the exemption threshold to inflation so that it keeps pace with rising asset prices and the expanding retail investor base.Another proposal involves reducing the tax burden on investments held for longer periods. Under the suggested framework, the existing 12.5 per cent LTCG tax rate could continue for investments held between one and two years.For investments retained for two to three years, the proposed tax rate would fall to 5%. Gains from investments held for more than three years could attract no LTCG tax.SBI Research has also suggested increasing the securities transaction tax (STT) to compensate for part of the revenue the government could lose through changes to capital gains taxation.Higher STT Rates Could Offset Revenue LossThe report estimates that increasing STT rates by 50 per cent could generate approximately Rs 45,000 crore in additional revenue for FY28.Under the proposed changes, STT on equity delivery transactions could be increased from 0.10 per cent to 0.15 per cent. The rate applicable to futures transactions may rise from 0.05 per cent to 0.075 per cent, while the options STT rate could move from 0.15 per cent to 0.225 per cent.SBI Research believes that removing LTCG taxation could encourage higher trading activity in the equity market, potentially increasing transaction-based tax collections.Its estimates suggest that STT revenue could reach Rs 1.36 lakh crore under the proposed framework in FY28, compared with Rs 0.91 lakh crore if the existing rates continue.The report's recommendations come amid discussions around easing the tax burden on foreign investors. On Monday, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said the Centre would "consider" the request from foreign investors to review the structure of taxes levied on equity capital gains.According to reports, market regulator SEBI has also recommended that the government consider removing LTCG tax imposed on FIIs.Mutual Fund Investors May Get Tax Deferral BenefitApart from changes to equity taxation, SBI Research has suggested providing relief to mutual fund investors through a tax deferral mechanism.Under the proposal, investors could postpone their LTCG tax liability if they redeem units of one mutual fund and reinvest the proceeds into an eligible equity mutual fund within 90 days.This could allow investors to move their money from schemes delivering relatively weak returns to better-performing alternatives without having to immediately pay capital gains tax.The report has also highlighted the need to maintain a balance between taxation on equity investments and traditional bank deposits.
