(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India, in Venky Steels Pvt. Ltd. Vs CIT-II & Anr., declined to interfere with the Patna High Court judgment upholding the validity of reassessment sanction under Section 151 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. After condoning the delay, the Supreme Court dismissed the special leave petition, finding no good ground or reason to interfere with the impugned judgment. The controversy concerned whether the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax was required to record independent and elaborate reasons while approving a reassessment notice under Section 148 or whether express satisfaction based on the Assessing Officer’s recorded reasons was sufficient. The Supreme Court’s brief order did not contain independent reasoning on the statutory question. Read HC Judgment in this case: Section 151 Reassessment Sanction Valid Without Separate Reasons: Patna HC The dispute arose from a reassessment notice dated 26 March 2019 issued to Venky Steels Pvt. Ltd., engaged in the iron and steel business, for assessment year 2013-14. The assessee filed a return in response on 24 April 2019 and requested the reasons recorded for reopening the assessment, relying on GKN Driveshafts (India) Ltd. v. Income Tax Officer and Others, (2003) 259 ITR 19 (SC). Although the reasons were subsequently supplied, the assessee challenged the notice before the Patna High Court, alleging that the necessary approval under Section 151 had not been properly obtained or was granted mechanically. During the proceedings, the Department produced the approval documents showing that the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax-I, Patna, had separately sanctioned reassessment notices for assessment years 2012-13 and 2013-14. The approval recorded by the Principal Commissioner stated: “Based on the reasons recorded, I am satisfied that this is a fit case for issuing notice under Section 148.” The assessee argued that this endorsement was mechanical because it did not disclose separate reasons demonstrating the approving authority’s independent application of mind. Reliance was placed on Principal Commissioner of Income Tax v. N. C. Cables Ltd., (2017) 391 ITR 11 (Delhi), Principal Commissioner of Income Tax v. Pioneer Town Planners Pvt. Ltd., (2024) 465 ITR 356 (Delhi), and Chhugamal Rajpal v. S. P. Chaliha and Others, (1971) 79 ITR 603 (SC), where approvals consisting merely of expressions such as “Yes” or “Approved” were examined in the context of mechanical sanction and inadequate recorded reasons. The Patna High Court distinguished those decisions, observing that the Assessing Officer had recorded reasons for believing that income had escaped assessment and had placed those reasons before the Principal Commissioner. The approving authority expressly stated that, based on the recorded reasons, he was satisfied that the case warranted issuance of a notice under Section 148. The Court held that Section 151 requires the competent authority to record satisfaction regarding the Assessing Officer’s reasons, but does not require the authority to formulate a separate set of reasons. The Court also noted that even the decision in N. C. Cables Ltd. recognised that satisfaction could be recorded briefly, provided it reflected consideration of the particular case. Accordingly, the Patna High Court found no reason to interfere with the reassessment notice under Section 148 and dismissed the writ petition. However, it preserved the assessee’s liberty to challenge the assessment order through the statutory appellate remedy. Since the writ petition had remained pending from 17 February 2020, the Court observed that this period could be considered for condonation of delay if an appeal was filed within two months from the date of uploading its order. The Supreme Court subsequently dismissed the special leave petition, leaving the High Court judgment undisturbed. The ruling distinguishes an unexplained mechanical endorsement from an express approval based on the Assessing Officer’s recorded reasons and clarifies that, under the statutory provisions applicable to the notice in question, separate elaborate reasons from the sanctioning authority were not mandatory. List of Cases Discussed: 1. GKN Driveshafts (India) Ltd. v. Income Tax Officer and Others – Supreme Court, (2003) 259 ITR 19 (SC) – Relied upon regarding the procedure for obtaining recorded reasons after issuance of a reassessment notice under Section 148. 2. Principal Commissioner of Income Tax v. N. C. Cables Ltd. – Delhi High Court, (2017) 391 ITR 11 (Delhi) – Considered regarding mechanical sanction under Section 151 and the requirement that the approving authority record satisfaction, even if expressed briefly. 3. Principal Commissioner of Income Tax v. Pioneer Town Planners Pvt. Ltd. – Delhi High Court, (2024) 465 ITR 356 (Delhi) – Considered regarding the insufficiency of a mechanical approval consisting merely of the word “Yes”. 4. Chhugamal Rajpal v. S. P. Chaliha and Others – Supreme Court, (1971) 79 ITR 603 (SC) – Considered concerning the requirement of prima facie reasons for reopening an assessment and the invalidity of mechanical sanction. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER Delay condoned. We do not find any good ground and reason to interfere with the impugned judgment; hence, the present special leave petition is dismissed. Pending application(s), if any, shall stand disposed of.
