Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); CIT Vs Dell International Services India Private Limited (Karnataka High Court) Transfer Pricing: Earlier Comparable Rulings Can Be Followed When Facts Match-Karnataka High Court Dismisses Revenue’s Appeal Background of the Dispute The Karnataka High Court has reaffirmed that the Tribunal can follow earlier decisions on transfer pricing comparables where the material facts and functional profiles are similar. A fresh examination need not become a repetitive exercise merely because the earlier decision concerned another taxpayer. The Court also upheld the Tribunal’s treatment of advances received from an associated enterprise for future services as working capital, where audited financial statements and remittance certificates supported that finding. The respondent, Dell International Services India Private Limited, was the successor-in-interest of EMC Data Storage Systems (India) Private Limited. The assessee operated through software development, information technology enabled services and marketing support divisions. For AY 2006–07, the TPO proposed adjustments aggregating approximately ₹8.72 crore, comprising ₹8.41 crore for software development services and ₹30.88 lakh for ITeS. Following the DRP’s directions and the final assessment, the assessee obtained relief before the Tribunal. The Revenue challenged that relief. Supreme Court Remand Did Not Automatically Validate Revenue’s Objections The High Court had initially dismissed the appeal in 2018, referring to its decision in Softbrands India Private Limited. Subsequently, the Supreme Court, in SAP Labs India (P) Ltd. v. ITO, 2023 INSC 394; [2023] 149 taxmann.com 327, clarified that examining whether an arm’s length price was determined in accordance with the Act and Rules could give rise to substantial questions of law. The matter was therefore remanded for reconsideration. However, the remand did not mean that every disagreement concerning comparables necessarily became a substantial question of law. The Revenue still had to establish a sustainable challenge to the Tribunal’s reasoning. Earlier Decisions Can Be Followed Where Material Facts Are Similar The Revenue argued that the Tribunal had excluded comparables by relying upon decisions in other taxpayers’ cases without independently undertaking the required functions, assets and risks—FAR—analysis. The High Court rejected this general objection. Relying upon SAP Labs India Private Limited v. ITO, ITA No.10/2011, decided on 28 August 2026, and CIT v. Logica Private Limited, ITA No.338/2013, decided on 28 October 2026, the Court held that earlier findings can be followed where the relevant facts, functional analysis and other material circumstances remain identical. Either party remains free to demonstrate that the earlier conclusion was incorrect or that distinguishing circumstances justify a different result. Thus, precedent is permissible, but factual comparability remains essential. The objections concerning exclusion of KALS Information Systems, Accel Transmatics, Lucid Software and Tata Elxsi were covered by the Court’s decision in Logica. Fifteen Per Cent RPT Filter Upheld The Revenue also challenged the Tribunal’s adoption of a 15% related party transaction filter, instead of the TPO’s 25% threshold. This affected the exclusion of Megasoft, Aztec Software and Geometric Software. The High Court recorded that the issue stood covered in the assessee’s favour by its decisions in SAP Labs India and Logica. The ruling therefore supports the filter applied in the present circumstances. It should not be understood as prescribing 15% as an inflexible statutory threshold for every transfer pricing exercise. ITeS Comparables: Specific Findings Could Not Be Displaced by General Objections The Revenue questioned the exclusion of Vishal Information Technologies, Goldstone Infratech, Datamatics Financial Services, Maple e-Solutions and Nucleus Netsoft & GIS. The Tribunal had followed HSBC Electronic Data Processing India Limited v. ACIT, ITA No.1624/Hyd/2010, dated 28 June 2013. The High Court found that the Tribunal had considered specific factors concerning these companies, including outsourcing models, export earnings, related party transactions, reliability of financial data and amalgamation-related changes. The Revenue produced no material contradicting those findings. It also failed to identify an erroneous factual conclusion or frame a question challenging a finding as perverse. A general allegation that the Tribunal had merely followed another decision was therefore insufficient. Advances for Future Services Form Part of Working Capital The TPO had excluded advances from the working capital computation on the premise that they were used to acquire fixed assets. The assessee demonstrated that substantial amounts represented advances for contractual services to be rendered subsequently. The Tribunal relied upon Schedule 9 to the audited accounts and Citibank-authenticated Foreign Inward Remittance Certificates. The Revenue did not dispute these documents. The High Court held that the conclusion rested on reliable evidence and concerned an essentially factual determination, raising no substantial question of law. Author’s Comments The Revenue’s appeal was dismissed. The Section 10A turnover questions were recorded as not arising for consideration, with reference to CIT v. HCL Technologies Ltd., (2018) 404 ITR 719 (SC). For practitioners, the decision highlights two essentials: connect comparable precedents to the taxpayer’s actual functional profile, and substantiate working capital claims through contemporaneous documents. Equally, an appellate challenge must identify a concrete legal or factual infirmity; broad objections cannot displace supported Tribunal findings. Software Development Services [SwD]; ii. Information Technology enabled Services [ITeS]; and iii. Marketing Support Services. 5. It also provides ITeS to EMC (Benelux) BV/s.a.r.l.Netherlands. 6. The Assessee had filed its return of income for the AY 2006- 2007 on 24.10.2006, declaring its income at `4,66,18,718/-. In the said decision, the Court observed as under: “19.1 The said contention cannot be accepted. Once the Tribunal, in the case of any taxpayer, records findings regarding the functions performed, assets employed, or risks assumed and, on that basis, finds justification to include or exclude a comparable, there can be no prohibition in following such decision where it relates to the same assessment year or to the preceding years, as permissible under Rule 10B, provided the relevant facts and circumstances remain identical. If the functional analysis and other relevant factors continue to be the same, and the issue has already been adjudicated by the Tribunal, no fault can be found with the Tribunal following its earlier decision instead of undertaking a repetitive exercise. Merely because the Tribunal has relied upon its earlier decision, it cannot be said that its findings are not in conformity with Section 92C of the Act and Rule 10B of the Rules. 19.2 However, it is always open to the Revenue or the assessee to demonstrate that the inclusion or exclusion of a particular comparable in the earlier decision was incorrect or that the facts of the present case are distinguishable so as to warrant a different conclusion.” 18. In the recent decision in M/s. Logica Private Ltd (supra), this Court had observed as under: “60. The question whether the learned ITAT could rely on an order passed in case of another assessee must be answered in the affirmative, provided that the learned ITAT concludes that the material facts of the case relied upon are similar to the facts relating to the Assessee. In the context of transfer pricing adjustments, if the learned ITAT comes to the conclusion that the business activities carried on by the Assessee in another case are similar to the business activities carried on by the Assessee, it would be appropriate for the learned ITAT to take a similar view unless it finds other reasons to distinguish the case from the earlier precedent.” 19. Datamatic Financial Services Ltd. 11. The assessee’s objection to inclusion of this comparable is on the basis of Related Party Transactions filter. It was submitted that RPT exceeds 25% of the sales and therefore, to be excluded. The assessee relied on the decision of the coordinate Bench of the Tribunal (Mumbai Bench) in the case of Stream International Services (supra), wherein, wherein with reference to this comparable company, it was held as under : “11. The first is M/s. Datamatics Financial Services Limited. It can be observed that the TPO applied filter of “Companies with less than 25% related party transactions”. The learned Counsel for the assessee took us through the Annual accounts of Datamatics Financial Services Limited, and submitted that the gross income of this company for the year ending on 31.03.2006 was at Rs.2.31 crore as against total expenses of Rs.1.84 crore. Referring to pages 319 and 320 of the paper book, our attention was drawn towards Annexure-2 to demonstrate that the “Transactions with the Associated Parties within the meaning of section 92A and 92B of the Income-tax Act, 1961″ showed one major transaction with Datamatics Limited towards Reimbursement of expenses’ at Rs.99.14 lakh. The learned AR contended that the transactions of Datamatics Financial Services Limited with other AEs amounted to Rs.14.31 lakh making total of transactions with the AEs at Rs.1.13 crore. It was submitted that the percentage of transaction with related parties is much more than 25%, being, the filter adopted by the TPO himself and hence the same should be excluded. 12. In the opposition, the learned Departmental Representative contended that the major transaction of Rs.99.14 lakh of Datamatics Financial Services Limited with Datamatics Limited was towards ‘Reimbursement of expenses’. Since the reimbursement of expenses does not include any profit element, the Id. DR urged that the same be excluded. He stated that once this transaction is excluded, the other transaction of Rs.14.31 lakh are less than 25% of the total transaction with related parties. 13. We do not find any force in the contention advanced by the learned Departmental Representative for the exclusion of transactions with Datamatics Limited towards ‘Reimbursement of expenses’ from the overall transactions entered into by Datamatics Financial Services Ltd. with its AEs. Section 92F(v) defines ‘transaction’ in the context of transfer pricing provisions to include an arrangement, understanding or action in concert whether or not it is formal or in writing or whether or not it is intended to be enforceable by legal proceeding. There is no reference to any transaction having necessarily including profit element or mark-up so as to fall within the definition of ‘transaction’ under Chapter X of the Income-tax Act. Since the TPO applied filter of having companies with less than 25% related party transactions, it is not open to argue that the transactions of reimbursement of expenses duly reported by Datamatics Financial Services Limited as an ‘international transaction’ within the meaning of section 92B should be ignored simply because they represent reimbursement of expenses. If the contention of the Id. DR that the reimbursement of expenses not involving profit element should not be construed as a transaction, is taken to a logical conclusion, it would mean that all such dealings will cease to be transactions for the purposes of Chapter-X of the Act. Once these dealings are not considered as ‘transactions’, these will also cease to be international transactions, going out of the purview of section 92 itself. Obviously, such a view point is contrary to the clear intention and the language of the relevant provisions. A pure reimbursement of expenses by one AE to another AE is very much a ‘transaction’ as per section 92F(v) and consequently is equally an international transaction as per section 92B requiring consideration as per section 92 of the Act. Be that as it may, the learned Departmental Representative could not demonstrate the fact that such reimbursement of expenses was without any markup. As the so called comparable case of Datamatics Financial Services Limited was included by the TPO in the final list of comparables, in our considered opinion, the same is liable to be excluded as it involves related party transactions at much higher level, as against the filter adopted by the TPO himself, being companies with less than 25% related party transactions. We order accordingly.” In view of the above, since this company fails in this filter adopted by the TPO, we direct the TPO to exclude this company from the list of comparables adopted. As is apparent from the above, the learned Tribunal had referred to the specific facts relating to the companies that were excluded and had accepted the findings recorded by the Tribunal in its earlier decision. We note that the Revenue has not produced any materials to controvert the facts that were relied upon by the Tribunal in HSBC Electronic Data Processing India Limited (supra), which were expressly accepted by the Tribunal. The Revenue has merely framed a general question on the assumption that the learned Tribunal did not examine the facts in the present case. However, we note that the Tribunal noted the functional profile of the Assessee and, in that context, found that the aforesaid companies, selected as comparables, ought to be excluded on the basis of a FAR analysis. 28. We asked the learned counsel for the Revenue whether there was any specific fact as noted by the learned Tribunal, which was ex-facie erroneous on the material placed on the record. However, the learned counsel is unable to point out any material that would controvert the facts recorded by the Tribunal. Importantly, no question of law has been framed challenging any finding of fact as perverse. 29. Further, learned counsel submits that the TPO gave reasons for including the aforesaid companies. The Tribunal’s finding that the selected comparables ought to be excluded must be tested against the reasons set out by the learned Tribunal. Since we find no material to controvert the said findings recorded by the Tribunal relating to the FAR analysis, we cannot accept that any substantial question of law arises for consideration in this Court. Re: Question No.11: 30. This question relates to the working capital adjustment. The Assessee had challenged the working capital adjustment made by the TPO on the ground that adjustment did not take into account the advances received by the Assessee from its AE. The TPO had excluded the same on the ground that the advances had been utilized for acquiring fixed assets. However, the Assessee has pointed out that the advances were received to render contractual services in the future. Thus, the learned Tribunal examined the said contention and found merit in the Assessee’s contention. Paragraph No.26 of the impugned order is set out below: “26. After excluding the aforesaid 5 companies from the list of comparable, the arithmetic mean of the OP/TC margin of the remaining companies before working capital adjustment comes to 17.85% and after working capital adjustment allowed by the TPO comes to 16.74%. The Assessees OP/TC margin is however at 8.00%. The Assessee has in its grounds of appeal challenged the manner in which working capital adjustment had been allowed by the TPO. In this regard the Assessee has pointed out that while working out the adjustment to be made to the profit margins on account of working capital adjustment, the TPO did not take into account the advance received by EMC India (Assessee) from its Associated Enterprise on the ground that these advances were utilized for acquiring fixed assets. The Assessee pointed out before DRP that the advances received by the Assessee from its AE were monies paid in advance by the AE for contractual services to be rendered by the Assessee to them in future. It was pointed out that out of the total advances received from AE of Rs.56,13,36,329 a sum of Rs.49,24,92,142 pertains to advance for future services. Similar advances as on March, 31st 2005 was Rs.43,31,75,394 out of which Rs.38,73,53,427 pertains to advances for future services. The Assessee also pointed out that the above factum of advances having been received for rendering future services were also pointed out in Schedule 9 to the Notes on Accounts to the Audited financials of the Assessee for the year ending 31st March, 2006. It was also pointed out that the Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) authenticated by Citibank under which the Assessee received payments also clearly reveal the fact that the monies received were advance for services to be rendered. Thus it was argued that the advances received were for rendering services and should be considered in computation of working capital adjustment. The Assessee also pointed out that the cash profits for the year ending 31stMarch, 2006 of the Assessee was Rs.29,80,73,006/- which was much higher than the investment in fixed assets made by the Assessee during the previous year. This according to the Assessee was sufficient to demonstrate that the assessee did not utilize the advances received for purchase of fixed assets.” 31. The learned counsel for the Revenue submits that the question as to whether the advances were for working capital or capital is not supported by any factual finding. However, we find that the said contention is ex facie erroneous. The learned Tribunal had noted that Schedule 9 to the Notes on Accounts to the Audited Financial Statements of the Assessee for the year ending 31.03.2006 reflected the receipt of the advance. The Tribunal also noted that the Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) authenticated by Citibank also reflected that the advances had been received towards services to be rendered in the future. 32. Thus, the finding of the learned Tribunal that the amounts were received as working capital is based on material on record. The Revenue has not contested either the final accounts or the FIRCs, which evidence the receipt of the payments. 33. The question whether the advances have been received as working capital or otherwise is essentially a question of fact. Since the Tribunal’s decision is based on reliable material, no question of law arises for consideration. 34. In view of the above, no substantial question of law arises for consideration before this Court in this appeal. The appeal is accordingly dismissed.
