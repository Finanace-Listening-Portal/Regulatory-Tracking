(Photo:PTI)Our Bureau New Delhi 4 min read Last Updated : Oct 29 2026 | 5:00 PM IST @media (min-width:1100px) and (max-width:1280px) { .storymeta .meta-info { flex: 0 0 45%; } } Listen to This ArticleLeader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha Rahul Gandhi on Thursday questioned the Union government's decision to sell a majority stake in IDBI Bank to Canadian investment firm Fairfax Financial Holdings, raising concerns over employee rights, reservation policies and the privatisation of a bank that has returned to profitability. In a letter addressed to IDBI Bank employees on October 29, Gandhi said the government's decision to proceed with the sale had come despite questions raised in Parliament over the need to privatise a profitable bank and the protection of its employees and depositors. "An institution built with public money belongs to the public," Gandhi wrote, asserting that public sector banks should be treated as instruments of national development rather than assets to be sold. The letter comes two weeks after the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA), on October 14, approved a subsidiary of Fairfax Financial Holdings as the strategic buyer for a 60.72 per cent stake in IDBI Bank for ₹52,878.08 crore. Under the agreement, the government will sell a 30.48 per cent stake and LIC will divest 30.24 per cent. Following the sale, the Centre will retain 15 per cent and LIC 19 per cent, with both becoming public shareholders and withdrawing their nominees from the bank's board. IDBI Bank employees have planned a two-day nationwide hunger on November 2 and 3. The United Forum of IDBI Officers and Employees (UFIOE), comprising the All India IDBI Officers' Association and the All India Industrial Development Bank Employees' Association, has issued a strike notice opposing the proposed sale to Fairfax. The forum has demanded that the government acquire at least another 6 per cent stake in the bank to restore its majority holding.
