Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); ACIT Vs Rahee Jhajharia E to E JV (ITAT Kolkata) The Kolkata Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal decided cross-appeals filed by the Revenue and Rahee Jhajharia E to E JV for AY 2014–15 against the CIT(A)-9 order dated 12 February 2019. The assessee declared income of ₹1,13,79,250, while the assessment determined income at ₹7,32,93,932 after a transfer pricing reference involving specified domestic transactions with related persons under Sections 40A(2)(b), 92BA(i) and 92CA. The assessee raised additional grounds contesting the ₹6,19,14,682 transfer pricing addition after omission of Section 92BA(i) by the Finance Act, 2017. Relying on coordinate Bench decisions including DVC Emta Coal Mines Ltd., Texport Overseas Pvt. Ltd., Amman India Pvt. Ltd. and Raipur Steel Casting India (P) Ltd., the assessee argued that the omitted clause could not sustain the transfer pricing reference. The Revenue cited Sections 6 and 6A of the General Clauses Act and later Supreme Court decisions on repeal and omission. The Tribunal followed its coordinate Bench decisions holding that, in the absence of an applicable saving provision, the omission of Section 92BA(i) rendered the related domestic transfer pricing adjustment unsustainable. It deleted the addition and dismissed the Revenue appeal. As to TDS credit of ₹1,22,317 on a mobilisation advance, the Tribunal noted that the CIT(A) had already directed the Assessing Officer to grant appropriate credit and concurred with that direction. The assessee appeal was allowed. The order was pronounced on 12 July 2022. Cases Discussed DVC Emta Coal Mines Ltd.
