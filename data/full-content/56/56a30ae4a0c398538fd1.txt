India is the country with more Earthshot Prize winners and finalists than any other, he said. Advt "Too often, the world's emerging economies are presented with false choices -- growth or the environment, prosperity or protection. But India shows us a different picture," William said.The future will not be transformed by those who simply describe what is wrong, but by individuals who dare to show the world what is possible, he stated.Founded by William, the Earthshot Prize aims to find solutions to climate change. The environmental initiative awards five winners USD 1.3 million each to expand solutions addressing climate change. Mumbai will host the sixth Earthshot Prize Awards Night on November 17."The Earthshot Prize has seen what happens when bold ideas are backed and given the chance to grow. They create jobs, improve lives, and change what we believe is possible. And nowhere is that possibility more evident than here in India," William added.In 2021, Earthshot recognised Takachar, a Delhi-based social enterprise, as its first-ever winner in "Clean Our Air" category.Takachar designed a portable, tractor-mounted technology that converts agricultural crop and forest waste, which would have been burned, into sellable products like fuel and fertilizer. This idea reduced smoke by 98 per cent, while increasing farmers' income, the Prince said. Advt He said there are two incredible Indian solutions among the 2026 Earthshot finalists.The first is the the Mahila Housing Trust, which is empowering low-income women to cope with extreme heat through practical solutions such as cooler roofs and affordable insurance. The second one is the Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, which is making electric rickshaws an affordable choice, whilst cleaning the air for people living in big cities across the country, William said.These are not distant environmental ambitions -- they are solutions that improve lives. But this is not India's responsibility alone, he insisted. Advt They show that no country, company, or community has all the answers, said the British Prince."We must learn from one another. Because when a solution works, others must follow. It took the world almost 70 years to install its first terawatt of solar power. The second took just two years. That is how change gathers momentum -- one breakthrough makes the next easier, until what once seemed impossible becomes inevitable," he maintained.William, however, underscored that daunting challenges continue to persist."But we cannot wait for everyone to agree before we act. We all want clean air to breathe, a chance to build good lives, and a natural world that endures for our children and grandchildren. These are not ambitions that divide us. They are ambitions that unite us," he added. PTI Published On Nov 16, 2026 at 07:45 PM IST Telegram Facebook Copy Link Comments What are your thoughts?
