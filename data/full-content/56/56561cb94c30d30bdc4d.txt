Banks are required to disclose applicable rates for bulk deposits on their websites at 10:00 am on each business day, with a 10-minute grace period, making 10:10 am the latest deadline.The revised framework applies to single rupee term deposits of Rs 3 crore and above. It also requires interest rates on deposits, including bulk deposits, to be uniform across branches and customers for deposits of similar amounts accepted on the same date. At the same time, banks have been allowed to offer differential rates on bulk deposits based on the differential run-off rates applicable under the Liquidity Coverage Ratio framework. Advt The new regime means banks will have to make their bulk deposit pricing publicly visible each business day, allowing large depositors and competing lenders to monitor changes in rates. The rules apply not only to commercial banks but also to small finance banks, regional rural banks, local area banks, payment banks and urban cooperative banks under their respective revised directions.Daily pricing becomes more transparentUnder the revised framework, banks must disclose their bulk deposit rates by 10:00 am every business day, with a 10-minute grace period. The rates payable on deposits must conform to the schedule of interest rates disclosed in advance on the bank’s website.The rules also restrict differential pricing between customers and branches for deposits of similar amounts accepted on the same date. This is intended to bring greater consistency to bulk deposit pricing and reduce the scope for different rates being offered for comparable deposits.However, the RBI has retained flexibility for banks to price bulk deposits differently where deposits or unsecured wholesale funding carry different run-off rates under the LCR framework. This means the new rules do not impose a single rate across all bulk deposits irrespective of their liquidity characteristics.Implications for bank funding costs Advt The change comes at a time when banks are closely monitoring deposit mobilisation and funding costs. With bulk deposit rates becoming visible on a daily basis, banks will have to balance the need to attract large deposits with their overall funding strategy and liquidity requirements.For corporate treasuries and other large depositors, the daily disclosure provides a more transparent reference point when deciding where to place funds. It also gives banks greater visibility into the rates being offered by competitors, potentially making pricing decisions more responsive to changes in liquidity conditions. Advt The impact on funding costs could become more significant around periods of changing interest-rate expectations. Banks may adjust their disclosed rates depending on their deposit requirements, liquidity position and the prevailing cost of alternative sources of funds.The new framework does not alter the contractual terms of existing deposits. Instead, it changes how banks disclose and price deposits covered by the revised directions, including the requirement that applicable rates conform to the rates disclosed in advance.RBI retains LCR-linked flexibilityThe LCR provision is an important qualification to the uniform-pricing requirement. Banks can offer differential interest rates on bulk deposits by taking into account the applicable run-off rates under the LCR framework. Advt This allows banks to distinguish between deposits based on their liquidity characteristics while maintaining the broader requirement that similar deposits accepted on the same date should not be priced differently across branches and customers.The framework therefore combines greater transparency in bulk deposit pricing with some flexibility for banks to manage the liquidity implications of different categories of funding.Rate-setting gains importanceThe new rules have taken effect ahead of the RBI’s October monetary policy meeting, at which market participants are closely watching the interest-rate outlook. Expectations around policy rates can influence deposit pricing as banks assess the cost and availability of funds.For treasury departments, the daily disclosure requirement could make bulk deposit rate-setting a more visible part of the funding strategy. Banks will need to consider deposit requirements, liquidity conditions and LCR implications while determining the rates they publish each day.The immediate change is therefore not a prescribed level of interest rates, but greater visibility and consistency in how banks price bulk deposits. While lenders retain the ability to compete for large deposits and differentiate rates where permitted under the LCR framework, their pricing will now be more transparent to depositors and competing banks.
