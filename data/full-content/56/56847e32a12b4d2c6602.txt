Summary: For Tax Year 2026–27, resident individuals holding units in Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) should identify each component of a distribution before preparing their income-tax returns. Under the amended Schedule V of the Income-tax Act, 2025, qualifying dividends from underlying special purpose vehicles (SPVs) are exempt in the hands of unit holders regardless of whether the SPV has opted for the concessional corporate tax regime. Interest passed through by the REIT and rental income from property it owns directly generally remain taxable at the investor’s applicable slab rates, under the appropriate heads of income. The tax treatment of a capital repayment depends on the statutory cumulative test, which takes account of eligible distributions in respect of the unit and the original issue price. Amounts treated as a return of capital may reduce the unit’s acquisition cost for capital gains purposes, while any taxable excess falls under Income from Other Sources. On sale of listed REIT units, eligible short-term gains on units held for up to 12 months are taxed at 20%, while eligible long-term gains on units held for more than 12 months are taxed at 12.5% on aggregate eligible gains above ₹1.25 lakh, subject to the applicable securities transaction tax conditions. Investors should reconcile Form 77 with their return and report taxable distributions, exempt income and pass-through amounts in the appropriate schedules. Distributions received from REITs may comprise dividends, interest, rental income and repayment of capital, each having different tax implications under the Income-tax Act. Tax Treatment of REIT Distributions and Listed Units For Tax Year 2026-27, following the amendments introduced vide Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026, qualifying dividend distributions from REITs are generally exempt irrespective of whether the underlying SPV has opted for section 200 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (corresponding to section 115BAA of the Income-tax Act, 1961). However, interest and rental income distributed by REITs are generally taxable at the individual’s applicable slab rates. Repayment of capital may also have tax implications depending on the applicable statutory conditions. Long-term capital gains (LTCG) on units held for more than 12 months are taxable at 12.5% on aggregate eligible gains exceeding Rs. 1.25 lakh under section 198 of the ITA 2025 (corresponding to section 112A of the ITA 1961), without indexation, subject to applicable STT conditions. Other income taxed at the REIT level Generally exempt in the hands of unit holders. Form 77 and ITR Reporting To minimise the possibility of tax notices, individuals should carefully examine Form 77 (corresponding to the earlier Form 64B) issued by the business trust, which provides a breakup of the income distributed. Taxable interest should generally be reported under Income from Other Sources, and direct rental income should be reported under the applicable income head, generally Income from House Property, while exempt distributions should be disclosed in Schedule Exempt Income (EI). Relevant pass-through income should also be reported in Schedule PTI, wherever applicable. Correct classification of taxable and exempt distributions, supported by proper documentation, is essential to avoid reporting mismatches and reduce the likelihood of unnecessary scrutiny or tax notices.
