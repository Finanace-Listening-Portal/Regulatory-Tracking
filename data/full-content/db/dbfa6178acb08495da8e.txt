Articles 4 min read Will banks lose transaction data edge after UPI MDR? UPI's new MDR could test the usefulness of transaction data as a proxy for small-business sales if merchant payment behaviour changes. ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Sep 25, 2026 at 07:00 AM IST var news_msid = 134469838; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook SBI is developing UPI based lending for small businesses without GST registration, using transaction data as a proxy for sales.Transactions above Rs 2,000 account for about 4% of P2M volumes but 67% of P2M value, making merchant behaviour after MDR important for lenders.A change in UPI payment patterns could make transaction data a less complete proxy for sales, even if the underlying business remains stable.Banks will need to distinguish changes in digital payment behaviour from actual changes in business performance when using UPI data for lending.Banks could face a new challenge in using UPI transaction data for cash-flow based lending if the introduction of Merchant Discount Rate changes how merchants receive payments. The issue is not access to UPI data, but whether the digital transaction trail continues to accurately reflect a merchant's overall sales.State Bank of India is developing a lending solution that will use UPI transaction data as a proxy for sales to extend loans to small businesses that do not have GST registration. The initiative illustrates how payment data is increasingly being used to assess businesses that have limited conventional financial records. Advt From October 15, a 0.4% MDR will apply to specified person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, subject to a maximum charge of Rs 300 for transactions of Rs 75,000 and above. About 96% of P2M transactions will remain outside the MDR framework, while P2P transactions will continue to be free.The transactions affected by MDR represent a small proportion of UPI merchant payments by volume but a much larger share by value. Transactions above Rs 2,000 accounted for about 4% of P2M volumes in August 2026 but around 67% of P2M transaction value. Their absolute number has more than doubled over the past three years.This makes the response of merchants to the new charge important for lenders. If merchants continue using UPI and absorb the cost, the transaction trail used for lending should remain broadly intact. If payment behaviour changes, however, the UPI data could provide a less complete picture of business activity.The distinction is important because a fall in UPI receipts does not necessarily mean a fall in sales. A merchant could have broadly stable business activity while receiving a larger proportion of payments through other channels.UPI is becoming a lending inputSBI's planned lending solution shows how banks are beginning to use digital transaction data to assess small businesses outside the GST system. The bank intends to use UPI and other payment behaviour to build a profile of a business's revenue and spending patterns. Advt For lenders, this can provide a more frequent view of cash flows than traditional financial statements. It can also help assess businesses that may not have the formal documentation required for conventional automated lending.The model therefore depends not simply on the availability of UPI data but on its relationship with the underlying business. If UPI represents a large and stable share of a merchant's sales, transaction data can act as a useful proxy. If payment patterns become fragmented across several channels, that relationship becomes harder to establish.The introduction of MDR does not by itself indicate that merchants will move back to cash. The Reserve Bank of India has said concerns about a shift towards cash following the introduction of MDR are unlikely to materialise. Advt The more relevant question for lenders is therefore whether merchants alter the mix of digital payment methods.The impact is also likely to vary by merchant. The standard 0.4% MDR applies only to specified transactions above Rs 2,000, while several categories have lower or flat charges and small merchants covered by the zero-MDR framework remain outside the charge.The data advantage could become more complexUPI transaction data is only one potential input into cash-flow based underwriting. Lenders can combine payment information with bank-account activity, credit bureau records and other permitted financial data. Advt The introduction of MDR therefore does not remove the usefulness of UPI data or eliminate banks' ability to underwrite small businesses digitally. Instead, it could make it more important for lenders to distinguish between a change in digital payment behaviour and a genuine change in business performance.That distinction will become particularly relevant for automated lending models, where a decline in UPI receipts could otherwise be interpreted as weaker sales.What lenders will watch after October 15The first indicators will be changes in merchant UPI volumes, average transaction values and the share of payments received through other channels. Lenders will also need to assess whether changes in UPI receipts correspond with changes in other measures of business activity.For banks developing cash-flow based lending, the test will be whether UPI continues to provide a sufficiently representative picture of a merchant's sales after MDR comes into effect.The new charge therefore creates a less visible consequence for the lending industry. UPI data is not becoming unavailable to banks, but the value of that data will depend on whether the payment trail continues to mirror the underlying business.
