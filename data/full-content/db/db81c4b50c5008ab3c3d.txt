Summary : CBDT Circular No. 07/2026 dated 28 September 2026 moved the assessment year 2026-27 return deadline from 31 October 2026 to 21 November 2026 for the persons in serial number 2 of the table below Explanation 2 to section 139(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. This guide identifies that class, separates it from section 92E cases, and converts the remaining filing work into an evidence-based reconciliation process. The relevant previous year is 2025-26; the return therefore remains governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961 even though the Circular also invokes section 536 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 for transition support. Key Takeaways Show all 17 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); The 21 November extension is class-specific, not universal The operative wording is narrower than a general extension for every taxpayer. It covers a company, a non-company assessee whose accounts must be audited under the Income-tax Act or another law, and a partner of an audited firm, including the spouse of such partner where section 5A applies, provided section 92E does not apply. Teams should map the return to the statutory class before relying on the date. A calendar entry or portal message is not a substitute for that classification note. Section 92E cases remain in a separate serial number with the ordinary 30 November due date. The Circular expressly refers to serial number 2; it does not move serial number 1. Likewise, an assessee outside the audit class cannot assume that the 21 November date cures an earlier default. The first control is therefore a signed eligibility sheet recording entity type, audit trigger, partner or spouse linkage where relevant, and whether an international or specified domestic transaction requires a section 92E report. Its charging, computation, audit and filing provisions are those of the Income-tax Act, 1961. References to the Income-tax Act, 2025 in the Circular facilitate the transition but do not convert this return into a tax-year 2026-27 return under the new Act. Review templates should therefore avoid mixing new-Act section numbers into the computation or audit trail for this assessment year. The audit-report deadline has already passed The same Circular moved the specified audit-report date to 21 October 2026. By 16 November, the audit report should already have been uploaded and accepted where acceptance is required. The filing file should contain the final report, form and acknowledgement number, the accountant’s UDIN or equivalent professional evidence, acceptance status, and a comparison of the uploaded particulars with the final tax computation. If a revision or corrective action is being considered, its legal availability and portal status should be checked independently rather than assumed. Build a return-to-audit reconciliation before uploading The most material last-mile risk is a return that is internally complete but inconsistent with the audit report. Create a two-way bridge from audited financial statements and tax-audit clauses to the return schedules. Differences may be legitimate, but each should have an owner, statutory basis and evidence. The bridge should cover turnover, method of accounting, ICDS adjustments, depreciation, disallowances, TDS or TCS, GST-related balances, brought-forward losses, minimum alternate tax or alternate minimum tax where applicable, and partner information. For each material mismatch, determine whether the third-party report is correct, duplicated, timed differently or belongs to another taxpayer. Record the conclusion and, where appropriate, use the portal feedback mechanism or obtain confirmation from the reporting person. Do not simply force the books to equal an incorrect information statement, and do not ignore a correct statement merely because it was not booked. Credit claims require special care. A tax credit may be economically attributable to the assessee but still unavailable in the return if the reported identifier, year or amount is wrong. Conversely, a matching entry does not prove the underlying income has been offered in the correct head or period. The return file should connect each material credit to the income schedule, deductor record and ledger, with any unmatched balance separately tracked. Complete tax payment, filing and verification as one controlled event The return is not ready merely because the XML or online schedules validate. Recompute interest and tax immediately before filing, confirm the correct assessment year and payment type on any self-assessment-tax challan, and verify that the challan is visible or otherwise claimable. Preserve the computation generated from the final return data, not an earlier spreadsheet version. A maker-checker sign-off should confirm the ITR form, filing section, bank details, refund destination, tax regime position and audit particulars. Electronic verification should be completed within the legally available period and the acknowledgement preserved. If verification is delayed or fails, the practical filing date and consequences may differ from what the team assumes. Capture the final acknowledgement, verification status and checksum or exported return copy in a read-only closing pack. Where a digital signature certificate is required or chosen, test it before the deadline and avoid leaving token, browser or signer-utility failures to the final hours. What to do in the final five days By 16 November, the remaining work should be exception-driven. Freeze the audited trial balance, assign unresolved variances, confirm audit-report acceptance, complete the return-to-audit bridge, and circulate a single final computation. On the penultimate day, refresh AIS, Form 26AS and challan visibility; do not reopen immaterial presentation issues that do not affect a correct return. On filing day, retain screenshots or system logs only as supplementary evidence—the statutory acknowledgement remains the primary filing record. Does 21 November 2026 apply to every assessee whose accounts are audited? It applies to the serial-number-2 class under Explanation 2 to section 139(1), including specified companies, audited non-company assessees and specified partners or spouses, only where section 92E does not apply. The audit trigger and section 92E position should both be documented. 2. Is 30 November 2026 the safer date for an audit case? No. A non-section-92E audit case covered by Circular No. 07/2026 has 21 November 2026. The 30 November date belongs to the separate section 92E class and should not be used to postpone an ordinary audit-case return. 3. Which Income-tax Act governs this return? The return for previous year 2025-26 and assessment year 2026-27 is governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from tax year 2026-27 and should not be used to relabel the AY 2026-27 computation. 4. Can a return be filed if the audit report has a mismatch? A mechanical upload may be possible, but unexplained inconsistencies create processing, assessment and penalty risk. The correct approach is to determine whether the return, audit report or books require lawful correction and retain a reasoned reconciliation. 5. Should AIS figures always be copied into the return? No. AIS is an information source. Each material item should be matched to the taxpayer’s records and offered in the correct head and period. Incorrect or duplicate reporting should be documented and addressed through available feedback or payer correction. 6. Does uploading the return complete filing? Not necessarily. The return must also be validly verified within the applicable period and by the appropriate method. Preserve the acknowledgement and verification status; an unverified upload can fail to achieve the intended compliance result. 7. What is the most important final control? A locked bridge showing that the final return agrees with the audited financial statements, audit report, tax computation, tax-credit records and challans, with every material variance explained and approved. Key Takeaways 21 November 2026 is limited to the non-section-92E audit class identified in Circular No. 07/2026. AY 2026-27 remains governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961. The audit report was due by 21 October 2026; reconcile its final uploaded data to the return.
