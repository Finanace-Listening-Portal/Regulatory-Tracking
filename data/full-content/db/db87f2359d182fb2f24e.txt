Summary : A GST deductor filing GSTR-7 for October 2026 should treat 10 November 2026 as the statutory due date unless a valid later notification changes it. Section 51 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 requires notified deductors to deduct GST TDS when the taxable value under a contract exceeds ₹2.5 lakh, excluding GST and cess shown on the invoice. The standard deduction is 2% in aggregate—1% central tax plus 1% State or Union territory tax for an intra-State supply, or 2% integrated tax for an inter-State supply—subject to the statutory place-of-supply exception. The return is not merely a payment form: it is the data bridge through which the deductee receives the TDS amount in the electronic cash ledger. This guide focuses on contract-threshold testing, invoice and payment cut-off, tax-head selection, deductee-GSTIN accuracy, ledger reconciliation, amendments and evidence. It distinguishes GST TDS from input tax credit and from income-tax withholding, both common sources of reporting error. Registration is compulsory for a person required to deduct under section 51. The finance team should therefore begin with the deductor registration and notification trail, not with the supplier’s status alone. A private customer does not become a GST TDS deductor merely because it pays a large invoice. The October return captures deductions made when payment is made or credited to the supplier during October 2026. The underlying invoice date, accounting credit date and payment date may differ. A controlled cut-off file should show which event triggered deduction, the reporting month and whether the same transaction was included earlier. GSTR-7 is filed monthly even where there are few transactions; the portal position on a nil return should be verified for the specific registration and period. Credit must reach the intended supplier registration GST portal validation and vendor master The ₹2.5 Lakh Test Belongs to the Contract, Not Each Invoice Exclude GST Shown on the Invoice The threshold uses the value of taxable supply under a contract and excludes central tax, State tax, Union territory tax, integrated tax and cess indicated in the invoice. Splitting a single contract into monthly bills does not create a fresh ₹2.5 lakh test for each invoice. Conversely, unrelated contracts should not be automatically aggregated without analysing their commercial and legal relationship. Variations, renewals and change orders should be linked to the original contract where they modify the same engagement. Deduct on the Taxable Base The deduction base is the payment or credit attributable to taxable supply, not the GST component separately charged. The official CBIC TDS material illustrates 1% CGST and 1% SGST for an intra-State supply and 2% IGST for an inter-State supply. The team should separately identify retention money, advances, reimbursements, credit notes and non-taxable components. A gross-payment percentage applied without reading the invoice can overstate the deduction and distort the supplier’s cash-ledger credit. Apply the Place-of-Supply Proviso No deduction is made where both the supplier location and place of supply are in a State or Union territory different from the State or Union territory in which the recipient is registered. This exception is easy to miss in centralised payment systems. The return preparer should not infer the tax head from the supplier GSTIN alone; the contract, nature of supply, place-of-supply rule and recipient registration used for the procurement must be reviewed together. Build GSTR-7 from a Transaction Register, Not a Payment Total A reliable register carries one traceable line for each reportable supplier transaction and links the contract, invoice, taxable amount, GST amount, credit date, payment date, deductee GSTIN, tax heads and challan or cash-ledger funding. The register should also identify prior-period amendments and rejected or corrected vendor data. A control total from this register should reconcile to the general ledger and to the draft return before filing. It is not input tax credit arising from a purchase invoice. The supplier should therefore reconcile the contract receipt and TDS credit separately from GSTR-2B and the electronic credit ledger. Calling the amount ITC can lead to incorrect accounting, return mapping and utilisation assumptions. The deductor and deductee should exchange a transaction-level schedule after filing. The supplier can then match GSTIN, taxable value, tax amounts and period. Differences should be classified as timing, wrong GSTIN, incorrect taxable base, wrong tax head, duplicate reporting or missing transaction. A broad confirmation that “TDS is deducted” is not enough when the portal credit belongs to another registration or period. Corrections Need a Preserved Original-to-Amended Trail Errors discovered before filing should be corrected in the working register and draft return with reviewer approval. Errors in an already filed period require the portal’s amendment process; they should not be hidden by netting a current deduction against an old error. The correction file should retain the original entry, amended entry, reason, supplier confirmation and impact on the electronic cash ledger. Where excess tax has already reached the deductee, refund or adjustment rights depend on the statute and portal status and require case-specific review. A wrong GSTIN is especially sensitive because the economic supplier may not receive the credit while another registration may see it. Vendor-master changes should use independent GSTIN validation and maker-checker approval. If the contract spans multiple registrations, the purchase order and place-of-supply analysis should identify which registration receives the supply rather than defaulting to the group’s head-office GSTIN. Filing-Day Controls for 10 November 2026 Freeze the October payment and credit population and reconcile it to the general ledger. Retest contract-level taxable value above ₹2.5 lakh, including approved variations. Validate every deductee GSTIN and map the correct place of supply and tax heads. Reconcile IGST, CGST and SGST or UTGST totals to the amount deposited and available for filing. Separate current transactions from amendments and retain original-period references. Generate, review and file GSTR-7 by 10 November 2026 unless an official extension is issued. Download the acknowledgement and share a transaction schedule with deductees for credit matching. It is 2% in aggregate: 1% CGST plus 1% SGST or UTGST for intra-State supply, or 2% IGST for inter-State supply. Is GST TDS input tax credit for the supplier? No. It is credited through the electronic cash-ledger mechanism, not the electronic credit ledger. Should GST be included in the deduction base? The statutory explanation excludes GST and cess indicated in the invoice from the relevant value. Can a current return silently absorb an old-period error? No. Use the permitted amendment process and retain the original-to-corrected audit trail. Does every large private buyer file GSTR-7? No. Liability is limited to Government bodies and other persons specified or notified under section 51. What evidence proves completion? Retain the filed return, acknowledgement, transaction register, tax-payment reconciliation and deductee matching schedule.
