Dayanand Shetty (ICAI Board of Discipline) Incorrect Adjustment of Partner’s Capital: ICAI Reprimands CA and Imposes ₹10,000 Fine Summary: The Board of Discipline of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) reprimanded CA. Dayanand Shetty and imposed a fine of ₹10,000 for other misconduct arising from incorrect accounting treatment in audited financial statements. The matter concerned failure to give effect to a partnership arrangement regarding a deceased partner’s debit balance and its subsequent adjustment against the complainant’s capital account. The Board held that a professional accountant cannot allow bias, conflict of interest or undue influence to override professional judgment. It also found that the subsequent retrospective correction of the accounts did not erase the earlier errors or their consequences. Facts of the case Late Mr. Pravin Kumar Shetty was a partner in M/s Apoorva Restaurant & Bar and M/s Apeksha Veg. Restaurant, along with his father, Mr. Kunjappa P. Shetty, and his sisters. At the time of his death on 24 May 2011, his capital account in M/s Apoorva Restaurant & Bar carried a debit balance of approximately ₹24.98 lakh. A partnership deed executed on 1 June 2011 recorded that his father agreed to take over his liability and share. However, the balance sheets for financial years 2011-12 and 2012-13 continued to show the outstanding amount separately under the retired partner’s capital account. The debit balance was not adjusted against the father’s capital account. Following the father’s death on 29 December 2013, the complainant, the deceased partner’s wife, was admitted as a partner in both firms with a 25% share under a partnership deed dated 1 January 2014. The Board found that her husband’s debit balance was subsequently adjusted against her capital entitlement, although no partnership deed provided for her taking over that liability. Interest charged on the debit balance further increased its amount. Submissions of the respondent The respondent submitted that the father’s intention to absorb his son’s debit balance appeared in the prelude to the partnership deed dated 1 June 2011, but was not incorporated in its operative terms and conditions. He also relied on the fact that the father had signed the balance sheets for financial years 2011-12 and 2012-13 without the adjustment. According to the respondent, this supported the treatment adopted in the accounts. The respondent further stated that he had helped resolve the dispute by advising the partners to make retrospective accounting adjustments. At a meeting held on 25 October 2019, the partners agreed to the correction. He sought the benefit of doubt regarding interpretation of the partnership deeds and relied on the corrective steps taken. Findings of the Board The Board rejected the respondent’s explanation that the accounting treatment could be justified by the father’s alleged instructions or his signatures on the balance sheets. It observed that there was no evidence on record establishing that the father had instructed the respondent to refrain from adjusting his son’s liability against his own share. The partnership deed dated 1 June 2011, according to the Board, indicated a different position. The Board stated that the respondent was expected to discharge his duties impartially and advise the partners about the fundamental omission. A professional accountant should not permit the influence of others to override professional judgment. The Board also found that transferring the deceased partner’s debit balance to the complainant’s capital account was wrong because her assumption of that liability was never stipulated in any partnership deed. The continuing levy of interest compounded its effect. Although the partners subsequently agreed to rectify the accounts retrospectively, the Board held that errors existed in the balance sheets audited and signed by the respondent. It found that his conduct had deprived the complainant of her legitimate share of capital for six years and had brought disrepute to the profession. Decision and penalty The Board held the respondent guilty of “Other Misconduct” under Item (2) of Part IV of the First Schedule to the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949, read with Section 22. After considering his oral and written representations, the Board passed its order under Section 21A(3), imposing: A reprimand; and A fine of ₹10,000, payable within 60 days from receipt of the order. Conclusion The order highlights the responsibility of an auditor to examine partnership arrangements carefully and ensure that capital account adjustments reflect their substance. Signatures on financial statements and claims of client instructions did not, on the facts of this case, justify the incorrect treatment. Subsequent correction of the accounts also did not absolve the respondent of the misconduct already established.
