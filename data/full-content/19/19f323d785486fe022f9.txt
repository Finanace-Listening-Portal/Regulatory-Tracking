The instructions have come into effect immediately.The RBI said the consolidation was aimed at harmonising the existing instructions issued through various circulars governing cash withdrawal transactions at ATMs and PoS terminals. Advt Under the directions, savings bank account holders will continue to be entitled to five free transactions every month at their own bank's ATMs, inclusive of financial and non-financial transactions.At other-bank ATMs, customers will be entitled to three free transactions per month at metro centres and five at non-metro centres. Where transactions are carried out across both metro and non-metro centres, the total free transactions will remain capped at five.The six metro centres identified by the RBI are Mumbai, New Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata, Bengaluru and Hyderabad.Cash withdrawals at other-bank ATMs will be subject to a maximum limit of Rs 10,000 per transaction.ATM charges and customer safeguardsBanks can levy charges beyond the mandated number of free transactions, subject to a maximum of Rs 23 per transaction plus applicable taxes. Banks will be required to frame a Board-approved policy for levying such charges.The central bank has also directed banks to put in place mechanisms to alert customers about the number of free transactions already utilised during the month and the charges that may apply on subsequent transactions.Transactions that fail due to technical reasons, including hardware, software or communication issues, non-availability of currency notes, or other declines attributable directly or wholly to the bank or service provider, will not be counted as valid ATM transactions. No charges can be levied on such transactions. Advt Banks will also have to ensure that ATMs clearly display whether they are located in metro or non-metro centres to help customers identify the applicable free transaction limits. Information on maximum withdrawal limits, free transactions and customer charges must be displayed in English, Hindi and local languages.PoS cash withdrawals and cardless transactionsThe RBI has also consolidated instructions governing cash withdrawals through PoS terminals. The facility will be available through debit cards, full-KYC prepaid payment instruments (PPIs) issued by banks and non-bank issuers in India, as well as UPI. Advt The maximum cash withdrawal limit will be Rs 2,000 per transaction, subject to an overall monthly limit of Rs 10,000 across all locations, covering Tier 1 to Tier 6 centres. Customer charges, if levied, cannot exceed 1 per cent of the transaction amount.Banks can offer the facility based on their Board-approved policies. Merchant establishments offering the service will have to undergo due diligence and clearly display the availability of the facility and applicable charges.The RBI has clarified that customers can avail the PoS cash withdrawal facility irrespective of whether they make a purchase at the merchant establishment. Where cash is withdrawn along with a merchandise purchase, the receipt must separately indicate the amount withdrawn. Advt The directions also provide for interoperable card-less cash withdrawal (ICCW) at ATMs. Under this facility, UPI will be used for customer authorisation, while settlement will take place through the National Financial Switch or ATM networks.Banks, ATM networks and White Label ATM Operators may provide the ICCW facility. Withdrawal limits will be aligned with those applicable to regular ATM withdrawals, while ATM interchange fees will be determined by the respective ATM network.The RBI has also brought provisions on customer grievance redressal under the consolidated framework, linking ATM and PoS withdrawal complaints to the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, guidelines on turnaround time and compensation for failed transactions, and the Online Dispute Resolution framework for digital payments.
