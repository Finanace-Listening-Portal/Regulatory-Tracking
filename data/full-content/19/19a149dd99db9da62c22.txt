Transactions above the threshold account for only about 4% of merchant payment volumes but close to two thirds of their value, making them a relatively small but financially important part of the UPI market.The new framework takes effect on October 15. Specified person to merchant transactions above Rs 2,000 will attract an MDR of 0.4%, capped at Rs300 for transactions of Rs75,000 and above. P2P payments will remain free, as will P2M transactions up to Rs2,000 and transactions covered by the zero MDR framework for eligible small merchants. About 96% of P2M transactions will therefore remain unaffected. Advt The change gives payment apps a financial reason to focus more on higher ticket transactions. A Rs10,000 eligible payment would attract Rs40 of MDR, while a Rs50,000 payment would attract Rs200. Payments below Rs2,000 do not attract the standard MDR. The charge is paid within the merchant payment ecosystem and cannot be passed on directly to the customer.This changes the importance of transaction mix. An app processing a large number of small payments can have a bigger transaction count but a smaller share of the revenue generating pool. An app with fewer transactions but a greater concentration of payments above Rs 2,000 could capture more MDR revenue per transaction.PhonePe and Google Pay have the largest existing UPI transaction base. Together they accounted for 78.3% of UPI transaction volume in August. The two accounted for about 80% of UPI payment value during the month. The difference matters because the UPI market share ceiling is measured by transaction volume, while the new MDR revenue is linked to the value of eligible merchant payments.Smaller apps therefore have an incentive to pursue higher ticket payments rather than compete only on transaction numbers. Ticket bookings, business payments, utility bills and digital commerce are among the higher value categories that could become more important as the MDR regime takes effect. Smaller rivals are looking at such transactions as an area for expansion. Advt The MDR revenue itself will be divided across the payment chain. Under the framework, 40% goes to the issuing bank, 30% to the acquiring bank, 20% to the UPI app and 10% to the bank providing the UPI app's account relationship. On a Rs40 MDR generated by a Rs10,000 eligible transaction, the shares would be Rs16, Rs12, Rs8 and Rs4 respectively.This means the headline 0.4% rate is not revenue retained by the payment app. An app's earnings will depend on the value of eligible transactions processed through it and its role in the payment chain. The revenue opportunity is concentrated in the high value segment because most merchant transactions remain below the Rs2,000 threshold. Advt The structural changeThe structure could also change how payment app performance is assessed. Transaction volume will continue to determine an app's position under the 30% UPI market share framework, but transaction value will become more important for revenue. An app can have a smaller share of total transactions while processing a larger share of high value payments.Paytm, which processed about 1.98 billion UPI transactions worth Rs2.04 lakh crore in August, had an 8.1% share of transaction volume. Navi processed about 1.08 billion transactions worth Rs52,513 crore and had a 4.4% volume share. Their different transaction values show why volume share alone will not capture the economics of the new MDR regime. Advt The first test after October 15 will be whether payment apps gain or lose share of the Rs 2,000-plus segment even when their overall transaction volumes change little. Monthly UPI rankings will continue to show transaction volumes, but transaction value and MDR revenue will provide another measure of competition.For payment apps, the new question will not simply be how many UPI transactions they process. It will also be how much of the relatively small pool of high value merchant payments they can capture.
