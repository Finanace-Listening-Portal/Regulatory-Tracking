Articles 2 min read RBI imposes ₹4.8 crore fine on BPTP over FDI diversion The Reserve Bank of India has announced a hefty fine of ₹4.8 crore against BPTP Ltd due to breaches of FEMA regulations concerning foreign direct investment from the years 2007 and 2008. The company will pay ₹4 crore, and its two directors will each incur a penalty of ₹40.36 lakh. Manvir Saini TNN Published On Oct 3, 2026 at 02:12 AM IST var news_msid = 134646566; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook CHANDIGARH: The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has issued a compounding order imposing a total penalty of ₹4.8 crore on real estate developer BPTP Ltd (formerly Business Park Town Planners Pvt Ltd) and its directors for Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) violations linked to foreign direct investment (FDI) received in 2007 and 2008.Passed on Sept 17, 2026, the order brings an end to long-pending adjudication proceedings initiated by the Enforcement Directorate (ED), Gurugram zonal office. The settlement follows BPTP’s voluntary compounding application and a subsequent no-objection certificate (NOC) issued by the ED. Advt Under the breakdown, BPTP Ltd must pay ₹4 crore, while directors Kabul Chawla and Sudhanshu Tripathi have been fined ₹40.36 lakh each, bringing the total compounded penalty to ₹4,84,35,000.The case originated from an ED probe into FDI inflows totaling ₹537.5 crore received via the automatic route. The foreign funds included $77.67 million (approx ₹322.5 crore) from Mauritius-based CPI India I Ltd in Aug 2007 and $49.83 million (approx ₹215 crore) from Harbour Victoria Investment Holding Ltd in July 2008.The ED filed a complaint in Dec 2025 detailing two core violations against the developer. First, the company incorporated swap and put option clauses in agreements with foreign investors to promise assured returns, directly breaching FEMA norms and RBI guidelines. Second, it diverted ₹320 crore of the foreign direct investment funds into fixed deposits and mutual funds rather than deploying the capital into real estate projects as mandated by foreign investment utilisation rules.Following show-cause notices in Jan 2016, BPTP applied for compounding under Section 15 of FEMA. Since FEMA is civil legislation, Section 15 allows eligible non-serious contraventions—excluding money laundering or national security threats—to be compounded to ensure swift disposal, minimise litigation, and encourage voluntary compliance.Under Section 15(2), once the prescribed sum is paid within the stipulated window, all pending legal proceedings against the company and its directors stand terminated. The ED issues NOCs in compoundable cases without legal hurdles to foster ease of doing business across corporate entities.
