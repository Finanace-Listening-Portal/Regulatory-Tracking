} } Listen to This ArticleDelinquencies in India’s gold loan portfolio nearly doubled between March and August, although they remained below year-ago levels, as the portfolio almost trebled since August 2023 to ₹22.5 trillion. Growth is shifting towards larger-ticket loans and repeat borrowing, but stress remains concentrated among borrowers with multiple gold loans, smaller-ticket loans, and a history of missed payments, TransUnion Cibil said in a report released on Thursday. “On a year-on-year basis, delinquencies have declined compared to August 2025. However, one important trend is the increase in stress between March and August. During this five-month period, the delinquency rate has nearly doubled,” said Bhavesh Jain, managing director and chief executive officer, TransUnion Cibil. He chalked up the rise to the number of gold loans held by a borrower, previous missed payments, and loan size. Loans overdue by 90 days or more accounted for 0.3 per cent of outstanding balances in August. The portfolio has 55 million borrowers, or 21 per cent of credit-active consumers. Borrowers with four or more gold loan trade lines had a delinquency rate of 0.45 per cent, 1.5x the overall rate. They also accounted for 27 per cent of the gold loan book, Jain said. “Interestingly, this is comparable to the delinquency rate in the automotive loan segment, which is around 0.5 per cent. This is despite the fact that gold is an appreciating asset, while a vehicle is largely a depreciating asset,” he said. Borrowers with sanctioned loans of ₹10 lakh or more had a delinquency rate of 0.2 per cent. Of the 42 million consumers who took a gold loan between July 2024 and June 2025, 17 per cent already had at least one loan 30 days or more past due in the preceding month. “It is important to look beyond the underlying gold collateral and assess the borrower’s overall credit profile,” Jain said. Among borrowers with Cibil scores of 800 or below, 29 per cent of those with gold loans of up to ₹2.5 lakh and 27 per cent of those with larger loans had an overdue obligation before borrowing. The comparable range for personal, business, and property loans was 5-18 per cent. About 42 per cent of these gold loan borrowers were current on all loans 12 months later. Cibil calls this the “curing rate”: the share of borrowers who are no longer delinquent 12 months after taking a loan. “Borrowers under financial stress who take gold loans are more likely to regularise their repayment behaviour within 12 months compared to stressed borrowers taking certain other unsecured credit products,” Jain said. He cautioned against drawing a direct link between gold loans and debt repayment. “We do not have a direct correlation to establish whether gold loans are being used to repay existing debt. We do not have visibility into how borrowers manage their cash flows or utilise the funds. However, our data clearly shows that these customers demonstrate an improvement in their repayment behaviour over time,” he said. Cibil also tracked about 30 million active borrowers with scores above 730, at least two years of credit history and no overdue loans in December 2025. Between January and June, 7 per cent became overdue on a loan. The share was 24 per cent among those who had missed more than one payment in the previous six months, 15 per cent among those who also held business-oriented loans, and 10 per cent among those with non-mortgage balances of ₹5 lakh or more. Lenders disbursed 37 million gold loans worth ₹8.2 trillion in the first quarter (April-June/Q1). Volumes fell 15 per cent year-on-year (Y-o-Y), while value rose 12 per cent. Loans above ₹2.5 lakh grew 19 per cent. “Growth in the gold loan segment is increasingly being driven by value rather than volume. There has been a shift towards higher ticket sizes since March, particularly from April onwards,” Jain said. The average outstanding amount per borrower has doubled since 2023. Around 67 per cent of customers taking a gold loan already had one in their credit history. Jain said borrowers also had a high probability of taking another gold loan within 12 months, pointing to a strong pattern of repeat borrowing. GenZ accounted for one in five gold loan originations, with numbers up 19 per cent Y-o-Y, the highest growth among age groups. Women accounted for 41 per cent, up from 40 per cent, with a higher share in semi-urban and rural areas. Two-thirds of originations came from semi-urban and rural markets, the report said. Over the five months to August, growth was stronger in Western, Eastern, and Northeastern states, while the South recorded a marginal decline. The South continued to lead in outstanding loans. Jain cited credit demand, convenience, and rising gold prices as drivers of growth. “It is a combination of demand, convenience, and appreciation in gold prices,” he said. The bureau had no data linking tighter unsecured lending to demand for gold loans. The report observed that balances continued to rise even as gold prices moderated.
