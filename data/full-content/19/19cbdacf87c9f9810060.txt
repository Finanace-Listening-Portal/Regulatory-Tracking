Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Varun Hemant Keskar Vs ITO (ITAT Pune) Fresh DTAA Claim Can Be Raised in Appeal Even When CPC Accepts the Return Without Adjustment Background of the Case The assessee was a non-resident individual working in Singapore and possessed a Tax Residency Certificate issued by the competent Singapore authority for the year 2023-24. He filed his return of income on 26 June 2024, declaring total income of ₹17,73,990. This included capital gains of ₹17,15,446 arising from the sale of mutual fund units, which he had offered to tax in India. The Centralised Processing Centre processed the return under Section 143(1) on 6 September 2024, accepting the returned income without making any adjustment. Tax liability of ₹1,70,372 was adjusted against the available TDS credit. Subsequently, the assessee became aware of the Mumbai Tribunal’s decision in Mrs. Anushka Sanjay Shah v. ITO, dated 26 March 2025. That decision had treated the capital gains under consideration as exempt in India under Article 13(5) of the India–Singapore Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement. Relying on that decision, the assessee approached the first appellate authority, claiming that his mutual fund capital gains should also be excluded from taxation in India. First Appellate Authority Rejects the Appeal as Not Maintainable The Additional/Joint Commissioner (Appeals) dismissed the appeal without examining the treaty claim on merits. According to the appellate authority, Section 246(1)(a) permitted an appeal against an intimation under Section 143(1) only where the assessee objected to adjustments made during processing. Since CPC had accepted the return exactly as filed, there was no adjustment against which an appeal could be maintained. The authority considered that the grievance arose from the assessee’s own omission to claim treaty relief in the return, rather than from any modification made by CPC. It therefore held that the appropriate remedy was either a revised return within the prescribed period or an application under Section 119(2)(b) before the competent authority. Appellate jurisdiction, in its view, could not substitute those remedies. Assessee’s Contentions Before the Tribunal The assessee argued that appellate authorities possessed wide powers to entertain fresh claims, even where those claims had not been made in the original return. He contended that his objection concerned the tax determined under the intimation. Although he had originally offered the capital gains to tax, his correct liability had to be determined by applying the relevant treaty provisions. The assessee also explained that the Mumbai Tribunal’s decision became available after the statutory opportunity to revise his return had expired. Accordingly, he sought consideration of the treaty claim through the appellate proceedings. He relied principally on the Bombay High Court’s judgment in CIT v. Pruthvi Brokers & Shareholders, (2012) 23 taxmann.com 23 (Bom.). Appellate Authorities Can Entertain Claims Omitted From the Return The Tribunal accepted the assessee’s argument concerning the power to consider a fresh claim. It relied on Pruthvi Brokers & Shareholders, where the Bombay High Court recognised the jurisdiction of appellate authorities to entertain an additional claim that had not been made in the return of income. The judgment also explained the scope of the Supreme Court’s decision in Goetze (India) Ltd. The restriction discussed in that case concerned the Assessing Officer’s power to entertain a claim otherwise than through a revised return. It did not negate the powers of appellate authorities to consider additional claims. Thus, the omission to claim a benefit in the original return did not, by itself, prevent its consideration at the appellate stage. The Tribunal also referred to its decisions concerning BSNL employees, including Prathibha Jgadish Unawane v. ITO and Makarand Prabhakar Abhyankar v. ADIT, CPC, in which claims raised for the first time before the first appellate authority had been entertained. Fresh Computation and Verification Directed Following the jurisdictional Bombay High Court’s judgment, the Tribunal set aside the first appellate order. It directed the assessee to furnish a revised computation concerning the capital gains before the jurisdictional Assessing Officer. The Assessing Officer was directed to recompute the tax liability according to the facts and law, in light of the Mumbai Tribunal’s decision in Mrs. Anushka Sanjay Shah. Any consequential refund was to be granted after verification of the documents relating to the capital gains. The Tribunal also directed that the assessee be given a reasonable opportunity of hearing. Correspondingly, the assessee was required to respond to notices and furnish the relevant evidence and submissions without seeking unnecessary adjournments. Decision and Significance The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. The decision supports consideration of a fresh treaty claim in appellate proceedings even where CPC has processed the return without adjustment and the assessee himself had originally offered the income to tax. The Tribunal did not itself finally exempt the capital gains or quantify the refund. It directed the jurisdictional Assessing Officer to examine the claim, verify the supporting documents and determine the correct tax liability in light of the cited precedent. Facts of the case, in brief, are that the assessee is an individual non-resident working in Singapore and also in possession of tax residency certificate for the year 2023-24 from the competent authority at Singapore and has furnished his return of income u/s 139 of the IT Act on 26.06.2024 in the capacity of non-resident by declaring income of Rs.17,73,990/- which includes capital gain of Rs.17,15,446/- on sale of mutual funds unit along with other income. Vide intimation dated 06.09.2024, the return was processed by CPC as it is, without making any adjustments and accepting the income returned by the assessee. The assessee furnished appeal before Ld. CIT(A), who vide order dated 01.12.2025 dismissed the appeal by observing as under :- “7. Decision: Appellant has raised 7 grounds of appeal. 7.1. Background of the case The appellant filed his return of income for AY 2024–25 declaring his residential status as Non-Resident and contended that he is a tax resident of Singapore, placing reliance on Tax Residency Certificates issued by Singapore authorities. During processing of return u/s 143(1), capital gains of Rs.17,15,446 arising from sale of mutual fund units were subjected to tax, resulting in tax liability of Rs.1,70,372 being adjusted against TDS credit. Aggrieved by such taxation, the appellant filed the present appeal contending that such gains are not taxable in India by virtue of Article 13(5) of the India–Singapore DTAA and also relied upon the decision of ITAT Mumbai in the case of Mrs. Anushka Sanjay Shah v. ITO. 7.2. Maintainability of Appeal – Jurisdictional Issue At the outset, it is necessary to examine whether the present appeal is maintainable in law. The right to appeal is a statutory right and can only be exercised strictly in accordance with the provisions of the Act. Section 246 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 provides as under: “Appealable orders before Joint Commissioner (Appeals). 246. (1) Any appellant aggrieved by any of the following orders of an Assessing Officer (below the rank of Joint Commissioner) may appeal to the Joint Commissioner (Appeals) against— 1. an order being an intimation under sub-section (1) of section 143, where the appellant objects to the making of adjustments, or any order of assessment under sub-section (3) of section 143 or section 144, where the appellant objects to the amount of income assessed, or to the amount of tax determined, or to the amount of loss computed, or to the status under which he is assessed; 2. …………………………………………………………………… 3. …………………………………………………………………. (h) an order under section 154 or section 155 amending any of the orders mentioned in clauses (a) to (g)………………………………” From the plain reading of clause (a) of Section 246(1), it is clear that an appeal against intimation u/s 143(1) is maintainable only where the appellant objects to the making of adjustments. 7.2.1. On careful perusal of the intimation issued u/s 143(1), it is evident that: The return has been processed purely on the basis of data furnished by the appellant. No adjustment within the meaning of section 143(1)(a) has been made by CPC. There is no variation, disallowance or modification made by CPC which could be termed as an “adjustment”. 7.2.3. Thus, the grievance raised by the appellant does not arise out of any adjustment made by CPC, but arises from his own omission to claim DTAA benefit in the return of income. Accordingly, the essential jurisdictional condition for entertaining an appeal against an intimation u/s 143(1), as prescribed in Section 246(1)(a), is not satisfied. Therefore, the present appeal is not maintainable in law. In cases where the appellant seeks to claim a fresh exemption or treaty benefit which was not originally claimed in the return, the statute prescribes specific remedies, namely: 1. Filing of a revised return under section 139(5), if within the prescribed time limit; or 2. Filing an application for condonation of delay under Section 119(2)(b) before the competent authority for permitting filing of revised return or making such claim. 7.2.4. The appellate jurisdiction cannot be invoked as a substitute for these statutory remedies. The appellate authority cannot assume jurisdiction where the statute does not confer one. Accordingly, the only lawful course open to the appellant was either: To revise the return of income (if within limitation), or To approach the prescribed authority under Section 119 of the Act for condonation of delay in making the claim. 7.2.5. Since the appeal itself is held to be not maintainable, no adjudication on merits regarding DTAA applicability, taxability of capital gains, or reliance on judicial precedents is called for at this stage. The same would amount to assuming jurisdiction not vested under the statute. 7.2.6. In view of the above facts and legal position, the appeal filed against the intimation u/s 143(1) is not maintainable as no adjustment was made by CPC. The grievance of the appellant arises from his own omission in the return and not from any action of CPC. The appropriate remedy lies under Section 119 of the Income Tax Act or by way of revised return, and not by way of appeal. 7.2.7. Therefore, the appeal filed by the appellant for Assessment Year 2024–25 is hereby dismissed as not maintainable for want of jurisdiction. However, the appellant is at liberty to seek appropriate remedy as available under law, including filing an application for condonation of delay before the prescribed authority under Section 119 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. 7.3. In result, the appeal of the appellant is hereby dismissed as not maintainable for want of jurisdiction.” 4. It is the above order against which the assessee is in appeal before this Tribunal. 5. We have heard Ld. Counsels from both the sides and perused the material available on record including the paper book furnished by the assessee. In this regard, we find that the assessee non-resident has shown taxable capital gain of Rs.17,15,446/- in his return of income which was accepted by CPC on 06.09.2024, however, the assessee came to know about coordinate bench decision passed in the case of Mrs. Anushka Sanjay Shaha vs. ITO, IT(IT)A No.174/MUM/2025 order dated 26.03.2025 wherein the Tribunal has treated impugned capital gain as exempt under India-Singapore DTAA more particularly under Article 13(5) thereof. In the light of this decision, assessee requested before Ld. CIT(A) to allow him the similar benefit as in the above coordinate bench decision (supra). Ld. CIT(A) dismissed the appeal by saying that the assessee has not claimed above capital gain as exempt in his return of income and he was required to file a revised return and may request before CBDT to condone the delay u/s 119(2)(b) of the IT Act. 6. Before us, Ld. Counsel of the assessee placed reliance on various decisions/judgements including the case of CIT vs. Pruthvi Brokers & Shareholder [2012] 23 taxmann.com 23 (Bom.) wherein Hon’ble Court dismissed the Revenue’s appeal and upheld the Tribunal’s order and CIT(A)’s order wherein new claim made by the assessee was allowed which was not claimed in the return of income by observing as under :- “18. In the case before us, the CIT(A) and the Tribunal have held the omission to claim the deduction of Rs.40,00,000/- to be inadvertent. Both the appellate authorities held, after considering all the facts, that the assessee had inadvertently claimed a deduction of Rs.20,00,000/- paid after the end of the year in question. We see no reason to interfere with this finding. We see less reason to interfere with the exercise of discretion by the appellate authorities in permitting the respondent to raise this claim. That the respondent is entitled to the deduction in law is admitted and, in any event, clearly established. In the circumstances, the respondent ought not be prejudiced. 19. The orders of the CIT(A) and the Tribunal clearly indicate that both the appellate authorities had exercised their jurisdiction to consider the additional claim as they were entitled to in view of the various judgments on the issue, including the judgment of the Supreme Court in National Thermal Power Corpn. Ltd. (supra) . This is clear from the fact that these judgments have been expressly referred to in detail by the CIT(A) and by the Tribunal. 20. We wish to clarify that both the appellate authorities have themselves considered the additional claim and allowed it. They have not remanded the matter to the Assessing Officer to consider the same. Both the orders expressly direct the Assessing Officer to allow the deduction of Rs. 40,00,000/- under section 43B of the Act. The Assessing Officer is, therefore, now only to compute the respondent’s tax liability which he must do in accordance with the orders allowing the respondent a deduction of Rs. 40,00,000/- under section 43B of the Act. 21. The conclusion that the error in not claiming the deduction in the return of income was inadvertent cannot be faulted for more than one reason. It is a finding of fact which cannot be termed perverse. There is nothing on record that militates against the finding. The appellant has not suggested, much less established that the omission was deliberate, mala-fide or even otherwise. The inference that the omission was inadvertent is, therefore, irresistible. 22. It was then submitted by Mr. Gupta that the Supreme Court had taken a different view in Goetze (India) Ltd ( supra). We are unable to agree. The decision was rendered by a Bench of two learned Judges and expressly refers to the judgment of the Bench of three learned Judges in National Thermal Power Comp. Ltd. ( supra). The question before the Court was whether the appellant-assessee could make a claim for deduction, other than by filing a revised return. After the return was filed, the appellant sought to claim a deduction by way of a letter before the Assessing Officer. The claim, therefore, was not before the appellate authorities. The deduction was disallowed by the Assessing Officer on the ground that there was no provision under the Act to make an amendment in the return of income by modifying an application at the assessment stage without revising the return. The Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals) allowed the assessee’s appeal. The Tribunal, however, allowed the department’s appeal. In the Supreme Court, the assessee relied upon the judgment in National Thermal Power Co. Ltd. (supra) contending that it was open to the assessee to raise the points of law even before the Tribunal. The Supreme Court held :- “4. The decision in question is that the power of the Tribunal under section 254 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, is to entertain for the first time a point of law provided the fact on the basis of which the issue of law can be raised before the Tribunal. The decision does not in any way relate to the power of the Assessing Officer to entertain a claim for deduction otherwise than by filing a revised return. In the circumstances of the case, we dismiss the civil appeal. However, we make it clear that the issue in this case is limited to the power of the assessing authority and does not impinge on the power of the Income-tax Appellate Tribunal under section 254 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. There shall be no order as to costs.” [Emphasis supplied] 23. It is clear to us that the Supreme Court did not hold anything contrary to what was held in the previous judgments to the effect that even if a claim is not made before the assessing officer, it can be made before the appellate authorities. The jurisdiction of the appellate authorities to entertain such a claim has not been negated by the Supreme Court in this judgment. In fact, the Supreme Court made it clear that the issue in the case was limited to the power of the assessing authority and that the judgment does not impinge on the power of the Tribunal under section 254. 24. A Division Bench of the Delhi High Court dealt with a similar submission in CIT v. Jai Parabolic Springs Ltd. [2008] 306 ITR 42 / 172 Taxman 258 . The Division Bench, in paragraph 17 of the judgment held that the Supreme Court dismissed the appeal making it clear that the decision was limited to the power of the assessing authority to entertain a claim for deduction otherwise than by a revised return and did not impinge on the powers of the Tribunal. In paragraph 19, the Division Bench held that there was no prohibition on the powers of the Tribunal to entertain an additional ground which, according to the Tribunal, arises in the matter and for the just decision of the case. 25. In the circumstances, it is not necessary to decide the other questions raised by Mr. Mistri. 26. The appeal is, therefore, dismissed.” 7. Apart from above, we find that the coordinate bench of this Tribunal in the case of various BSNL Employees’ case has allowed the deduction u/s 10(10B) of the IT Act which was not claimed originally in the return of income and its claim was made for the first time before Ld. CIT(A). In this regard, we find support form coordinate bench decision passed in the case of Prathibha Jgadish Unawane vs. ITO in ITA No.1117 & 1118/PUN/2026 and others order dated 29.04.2026 wherein the Tribunal allowed the claim which was raised for the first time before Ld. CIT(A). The above coordinate bench decision was subsequently followed by the Tribunal in the case of Makarand Prabhakar Abhyankar vs. ADIT, CPC, Bengaluru in ITA No.1497/PUN/2026 order dated 05.10.2026 wherein the Tribunal allowed the claim made by the assessee for the first time before Ld. CIT(A). 8. Respectfully following the judgement of Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of Pruthvi Brokers & Shareholder (supra), we find force in the argument of Ld. Counsel of the assessee and accordingly, deem it appropriate to set-aside the order passed by Ld. CIT(A) and direct the assessee to furnish revised computation regarding capital gain income before the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer, who shall re-compute the tax liability afresh and as per fact and law in the light of coordinate bench decision passed in the case of Mrs. Anushka Sanjay Shah (supra) and grant the refund, if any, arising to the assessee after due verification of documents with regard to capital gain income. Needless to say, the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer shall decide the above issue after providing reasonable opportunity of hearing to the assessee. The assessee is also directed to respond to the notices issued by the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer in this regard and to produce relevant documents, evidences and submissions in support of his claim without taking any adjournment under any pretext, otherwise the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer shall be at liberty to pass appropriate orders as per law. Thus, the grounds of appeal raised by the assessee are allowed for statistical purposes. 9. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes.
