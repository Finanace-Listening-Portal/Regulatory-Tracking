Summary: Companies must file their financial statements and prescribed supporting documents with the Registrar of Companies under Section 137 of the Companies Act, 2013. The statutory filing deadline generally falls 30 days after the annual general meeting (AGM), while One Person Companies have 180 days from the financial year-end. Different requirements apply when an AGM is not held or financial statements are not adopted. For FY 2025-26, MCA General Circular No. 05/2026 dated 24 October 2026 permits specified AOC-4 forms to be filed without additional fees until 31 December 2026 and MGT-7 and MGT-7A until 31 January 2027. The relaxation does not extend AGM timelines or waive normal filing fees. Companies filing after the relief period must consider additional fees from the original statutory due date. The article explains the selection of AOC-4, AOC-4 CFS, AOC-4 XBRL and applicable Ind AS NBFC variants, together with the separate filing requirements for formally dormant companies. It outlines the financial statements, reports, linked forms, attachments, digital signatures and certification checks required before submission. It also distinguishes normal filing fees, additional fees of Rs. 100 per day and statutory penalties under Section 137(3), including possible reduced penalties under Section 446B for eligible entities and persons. Companies should reconcile reported figures with signed accounts, retain filing acknowledgements and use the relief period to complete pending compliance rather than postpone submission. It is the company’s formal disclosure of its financial statements and related documents to the Registrar of Companies, and it should reflect the financial statements approved by the Board and adopted by the members. For FY 2025-26, a company must distinguish between the statutory filing date and the additional-fee relief announced by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Under MCA General Circular No. 05/2026 dated 24 October 2026, covered AOC-4 forms can be filed up to 31 December 2026 without additional fees. However, this relief does not extend the AGM deadline and does not remove the normal filing fee. What Is Form AOC-4? Form AOC-4 is used to file financial statements and related documents with the Registrar of Companies. The obligation arises undersection 137 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with the applicable rules. It applies to standalone financial statements and, where required, consolidated financial statements. A company should not assume that having no turnover or no business activity automatically removes its filing obligation. Formal dormant status requires a separate assessment. MCA prescribes a Return of Dormant Company inForm MSC-3, which is to be filed annually within 30 days from the end of the financial year by a company retaining dormant status. Which AOC-4 Form Applies? Selecting the wrong form can lead to rejection, resubmission or incorrect reporting. The form a company must use depends on how it prepares and reports its financial statements, and whether standalone or consolidated statements are being filed. Form General purpose AOC-4 Standard financial statement filing route AOC-4 CFS Consolidated financial statements under the applicable non-XBRL route AOC-4 XBRL Financial statements of company covered by XBRL requirements AOC-4 NBFC (Ind AS) Standalone statements of applicable Ind AS-reporting NBFCs AOC-4 CFS NBFC (Ind AS) Consolidated statements of applicable Ind AS-reporting NBFCs The standard AOC-4 is the regular route for filing financial statements. AOC-4 CFS is used for consolidated financial statements under the applicable non-XBRL route, while AOC-4 XBRL is meant for businesses covered by XBRL requirements. For NBFCs reporting under Ind AS, AOC-4 NBFC (Ind AS) is used for standalone statements, and AOC-4 CFS NBFC (Ind AS) is used for consolidated statements, wherever applicable. Check the current forms and instructions through theMCA portal. MCA provides separate annual-filing forms, includingAOC-4 CFS. Companies should separately check whether XBRL applies to them, including exclusions and continuing filing requirements. Where applicable, use the current validation tool identified by MCA. AOC-4 Due Dates The AOC-4 deadline depends on the company’s circumstances, not merely the financial year end. Situation General statutory requirement Statements adopted at an AGM File within 30 days of the AGM AGM not held File within 30 days of the last date on which the AGM should have been held, with the prescribed explanation Statements not adopted at the AGM File unadopted statements within 30 days; subsequently file adopted statements within 30 days of the adjourned AGM One Person Company File member-adopted statements within 180 days from financial year-end Where a company adopts its financial statements at the AGM, it must file them within 30 days of that AGM. If the AGM is not held, the 30-day period is counted from the last date on which the AGM should have been held, and the company must also file the prescribed statement of facts and reasons for not holding the AGM. Where financial statements are not adopted at the AGM, the company first files the unadopted statements within 30 days. The Registrar treats those statements as provisional until the adopted statements are filed within 30 days of the adjourned AGM. A One Person Company follows a different timeline because it does not hold an AGM. It must file member-adopted financial statements within 180 days from the closure of the financial year. For example, an AGM held on 30 September 2026 ordinarily produces an AOC-4 filing deadline of 30 October 2026. For an OPC with a 31 March 2026 year-end, the ordinary 180-day date is 27 September 2026. For FY 2026-27, the same calculation ordinarily gives 30 October 2027 for an AGM held on 30 September 2027, and 27 September 2027 for an OPC closing on 31 March 2027. These examples do not assume that future filing relief will be granted. FY 2025-26: Additional-Fee Relief MCA’sofficial announcement on annual-filing relief identifies the following dates: Covered filing Last date without additional fees AOC-4, AOC-4 CFS, AOC-4 XBRL and specified NBFC variants 31 December 2026 MGT-7 and MGT-7A 31 January 2027 Under the MCA relaxation, a company can file AOC-4, AOC-4 CFS, AOC-4 XBRL and the specified NBFC variants without additional fees up to 31 December 2026. For annual-return forms, MGT-7 and MGT-7A, the last date without additional fees is 31 January 2027. Normal fees remain payable, and AGM timelines are not extended. Filings after these windows attract applicable additional fees calculated from the original statutory due date, not merely from the closing date of the relief window. A company should retain the circular in its compliance records and check subsequentMCA updates before relying on the relief. Documents and MCA Filing Checklist A company should prepare the final financial statements, notes to accounts, Auditor’s Report, Board’s Report, consolidated statements and supporting disclosures, as applicable. MCA’sannual-filing FAQs require authenticated financial statements, including the Board’s Report, auditor’s report and other documents, for the relevant filings. Linked web forms do not remove the need to attach the required signed documents. Before submission: Confirm the form variant and reporting year. Verify the AGM or member-adoption date. Reconcile entered figures with the signed accounts. Complete applicable linked forms and attachments. Check signatory authority, DSC validity and professional certification requirements. Complete validation, payment and submission. Retain the submitted form, SRN, challan and acknowledgement. Check the latest form instructions before deciding who must sign and certify the filing. The requirements should not be assumed merely from the company’s size or its previous filing. Fees and Consequences of Delay Normal filing fees, additional fees and statutory penalties are different charges and should be tracked separately. For delayed annual financial-statement filings, the additional-fee framework generally provides Rs. 100 per day without an upper cap. The FY 2025-26 relief should be considered separately when calculating the payable amount. Undersection 137(3), the company faces a penalty of Rs. 10,000, with a further Rs. 100 per day for continuing failure, subject to Rs. 2 lakh. The specified responsible officers face corresponding penalties capped at Rs. 50,000. Liability depends on the statutory allocation of responsibility. The reduced-penalty provisions ofsection 446B should also be considered for eligible businesses and persons. Paying additional filing fees does not automatically resolve every statutory consequence. Frequently Asked Questions Q1. Is 30 October the Deadline for Every Company? A: No. It follows where the AGM is held on 30 September. The ordinary AGM-linked deadline is 30 days from the actual AGM. Q2. Does the December 2026 Relief Extend the AGM Deadline? A:No. It provides additional-fee relief for specified filings; it does not extend AGM timelines. Q3. Does Filing AOC-4 Complete the Annual Return Obligation? A:No. AOC-4 concerns financial statements. MGT-7/MGT-7A concerns the annual return under section 92; it is a separate obligation. Q4. Can a Company File Without Checking the Latest MCA Instructions? A: That is not advisable. Confirm the current form, attachments, linked filings, signatures and certification requirements before submission. Conclusion A company should use this relief window to complete pending work rather than wait until the last date. The Company Secretary and CFO should maintain a filing-status report identifying the applicable form, statutory date, outstanding documents, responsible person and planned submission date. A complete filing means more than successful payment. It means the correct form, consistent figures, proper approvals, required attachments and retained evidence of submission.
