Summary: Listed Non-Convertible Debentures (NCDs) carry continuing compliance obligations under the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, the Companies Act, 2013, the debenture trust deed and other applicable regulatory frameworks. Issuers must coordinate with stock exchanges, debenture trustees, credit rating agencies, depositories and the Registrar of Companies throughout the life of the securities. The requirements include quarterly financial results, utilisation statements, security cover disclosures, event-based intimations, investor grievance redressal, annual corporate filings and timely payment of interest and principal. Additional corporate governance requirements apply to High Value Debt Listed Entities, subject to the applicable outstanding debt threshold. Debenture trustees require periodic certificates and information concerning security, guarantees, covenants and payment defaults. The Companies Act may also require Debenture Redemption Reserve, specified investments, registration and satisfaction of charges, annual returns and other statutory filings, depending on the issuer’s classification. This article brings together the principal obligations, reporting frequencies, regulatory references and an annual compliance calendar for issuers with listed NCDs. It also provides practical checklists for interest payments, redemption and handling potential defaults. Since reporting requirements and deadlines may change through SEBI amendments, exchange circulars and other regulatory instructions, issuers should confirm the latest applicable provisions before acting. Maintaining a coordinated compliance calendar, assigning responsibility for each filing and monitoring payment dates can help reduce delays, penalties and investor-servicing risks. From the day its debentures are listed, an issuer answers to five authorities at once: the stock exchange (under the SEBI LODR Regulations), the debenture trustee, the Registrar of Companies, the credit rating agency, and the depositories. Most penalties for listed debenture issuers come from missed periodic filings, not from the issue itself. This article is the companion to our guide on Listing of Non-Convertible Debentures on BSE / NSE. It maps every recurring and event-based compliance for an entity with listed NCDs, gives the current timelines after the 2024–2026 amendments, and ends with a month-wise calendar and an interest-payment checklist. Applicability: which compliances apply to whom The compliance load depends on what else the issuer has listed and how large its listed debt is. Issuer LODR obligations Companies Act status Debt-only listed entity (listed NCDs below ₹5,000 crore outstanding) Chapter III (common obligations) and Chapter V (Reg. 50–62A) Not a “listed company” under Rule 2A if only privately placed NCDs are listed; a private company stays private High Value Debt Listed Entity (HVDLE) – listed NCDs outstanding of ₹5,000 crore or more Chapter III, Chapter V, plus corporate-governance norms in Chapter VA (board, committees, related-party transactions) As above Equity-listed entity with listed NCDs Full LODR (Chapter IV for equity) plus Chapter V for NCDs Listed company Key points: HVDLE threshold. Under the LODR (Amendment) Regulations, 2026, effective 22 January 2026, an entity becomes an HVDLE only when outstanding listed NCDs reach ₹5,000 crore (earlier ₹1,000 crore). A new HVDLE has six months from the trigger to comply. An entity whose listed NCDs fall and stay below the threshold for three consecutive financial years stops being an HVDLE. Mandatory listing. Under Reg. 62A, once an entity has listed NCDs, every NCD it issues on or after 1 January 2024 must be listed. Compliance officer. Every listed entity must appoint a qualified Company Secretary as compliance officer (Reg. 6), who is responsible for filings, investor grievances and coordination with the exchange and trustee. 2. Stock exchange compliances (SEBI LODR) The exchange filings fall into two groups: periodic filings tied to the quarter, and event-based disclosures tied to board decisions and payments. All filings go through the exchange portals (NSE NEAPS / Digital Exchange, BSE Listing Centre), most in XBRL. A. Debenture trustee compliances The debenture trustee monitors the issuer for the whole life of the NCDs, and since the SEBI circular of 25 November 2025 the issuer’s reporting to the trustee runs on fixed deadlines from the quarter ended 31 December 2025. Pay trustee fees on time; unpaid fees can delay the trustee’s certifications to the exchange. Consent for changes. Any release, substitution or creation of further charge on secured assets, and any change in terms, needs the trustee’s prior consent (and NCD holders’ approval where the deed or Reg. 59 requires). Recovery Expense Fund deposited at issue stays with the designated exchange until redemption; on full redemption, the issuer applies for its refund with the trustee’s no-objection. On default, the trustee may convene NCD-holder meetings, enforce security and enter into an inter-creditor agreement; the issuer must cooperate with information and access. 4. Companies Act compliances Listing on an exchange does not replace any Companies Act obligation on debentures; several of them, such as DRR and the 15% liquid investment, apply every year until redemption. Payment of interest and redemption: step-by-step checklist Every coupon and redemption runs on the same cycle: record date 15 days before the due date, payment on the due date, and a certificate to the exchange within one working day. Interest payment D-30: Confirm the due date, amount and day-count from the term sheet / KID; check holiday convention (if the due date is a holiday, follow the convention stated in the offer document). D-20: Arrange funds; inform the RTA and paying bank; check that any Debt Service Reserve Account is funded as per the deed. Before record date: Intimate the record date and purpose to the exchange (Reg. 60), at least 3 working days in advance. D-15 (record date): Obtain the beneficiary position from the depositories through the RTA; freeze the list of holders entitled. D-7 to D-2: Compute TDS holder-wise (PAN, Form 15G / 15H, treaty or exemption claims); prepare the payment file. D (due date): Credit interest to holders’ bank accounts electronically; prepare the paid / unpaid list. D+1 working day: File the Reg. 57(1) certificate of payment status with the exchange; inform the debenture trustee. Post-payment: Deposit TDS by the due date; issue TDS certificates; transfer any unpaid amounts to a separate unpaid account; respond to holder queries. Redemption (full or partial) All interest steps above, plus the principal amount. For redemption on maturity, the depositories debit the NCDs on the redemption date through the RTA’s corporate action; for partial redemption by face value, the RTA updates the face value. For call / put options, give the notice period required in the offer document and disclose the exercise to the exchange under Reg. 51. After full redemption: extinguishment of ISIN and delisting by the exchange; refund of the Recovery Expense Fund on the trustee’s no-objection; satisfaction of charge in CHG-4 within 30 days; release of security documents by the trustee. Close DRR and the 15% liquid investment for that series; disclose redemption in the next quarterly results. If payment cannot be made Disclose an expected default to the exchange and trustee as soon as it becomes apparent (Part B of Schedule III). Report the default in the Reg. 57 certificate within 1 working day and to the credit rating agency. Cooperate with the trustee on NCD-holder meetings, inter-creditor arrangements and enforcement; default interest accrues as per the deed. 8. Penalties and common lapses Late or missed filings are penalised automatically: the exchanges levy per-day fines under the SEBI Standard Operating Procedure for non-compliance, and repeated or serious lapses can lead to suspension of trading in the NCDs and action by SEBI under the SEBI Act. Under the Companies Act, default in redemption or interest can lead to NCLT orders under Sec. 71(10), and filing delays attract additional fees and penalties. Lapses we see most often in practice: Missing the first quarterly results. New debt-only issuers often assume results are half-yearly; they are quarterly, within 45 days. Reg. 57 certificate filed late because the payment date fell on a holiday or the RTA confirmation came late. Prepare the certificate the day before. Security cover certificate not ready by day 60. Line up the statutory auditor in advance; the Q4 deadline (75 days) clashes with annual audit work. Reg. 52(7) / 52(7A) statements omitted once the proceeds are “mostly” used. They continue until the proceeds are fully used. Website not updated with rating changes, trustee details and Reg. 51 disclosures. Board meeting intimation missed when NCD terms or payment dates are on the agenda along with other business. DRR and 15% liquid investment overlooked by private and unlisted public companies that assume the “listed company” exemption applies to them. CHG-4 not filed after redemption, leaving the charge open on the MCA records. New NCD issued unlisted in breach of Reg. 62A. Conclusion A listed debenture issuer runs on three clocks: the quarter (results, security cover, utilisation), the payment date (record date, payment, Reg. 57 certificate) and the financial year (DRR, 15% investment, annual filings). Mapping all three into one calendar at the time of listing, and assigning an owner for each line, prevents almost every penalty described above. This article reflects the law as of 20 October 2026.
