Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Updated On Sep 11, 2026 at 04:57 PM IST var news_msid = 134063377; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has urged the Reserve Bank of India to further advance its wholesale and retail central bank digital currency (CBDC) pilots and sharpen its digital currency capabilities as tokenisation gains momentum globally.Speaking at the Global FinTech Fest 2026 in Mumbai, Sitharaman said tokenisation was becoming a defining development in the financial architecture and highlighted the role of CBDC in enabling faster settlement.“Every tokenisation architecture being tested across the world eventually returns to the foundational question. What is the money leg made of?” she said, adding that this was why the RBI should further advance what it had initiated through both its wholesale and retail CBDC pilots. Advt She was referring to the recent corporate bond tokenisation pilot involving the RBI and SEBI. Sitharaman said the bond and digital money moved at the same instant, with the transaction closing on the day the payment was made.She said the monetary settlement was enabled by CBDC and highlighted the synergy between the two regulators.The RBI and SEBI have separately launched the Demat 2.0 pilot, using tokenised securities, CBDC and smart contracts to enable faster settlement of corporate bonds.‘Soft touch regulation’ without hurting innovationSitharaman had earlier raised the question of whether there could be “soft touch regulation” that would address emerging risks without affecting the innovative pressure within the sector.“Is there a way in which we can do a soft touch regulation? Is there a way in which, without affecting the innovative pressure, is there a way in which we can do something?” she said.Her comments come as fintech and AI-enabled financial services increasingly operate across regulatory boundaries,with technology platforms often performing functions that may not fit neatly within traditional regulatory categories.She said regulators, competition authorities, data-protection authorities and cybersecurity agencies need clear responsibilities and reliable coordination mechanisms.“No activity should escape appropriate oversight, merely because it sits at a boundary between sectors or is driven through a technology platform rather than a conventional financial institution,” she said. Advt Tokenisation, agentic AI can accelerate finance and risksSitharaman said tokenisation could eliminate unnecessary intermediaries and make asset transfers almost instantaneous, while agentic AI could move beyond recommendations to directly taking actions and compress multi-day processes into seconds.Quantum computing, meanwhile, could deliver computing power and security capabilities that were previously considered impossible.“When decisions and transactions become instantaneous, software errors, fraud, and market shocks can spread at the same rapid pace,” she said.She said financial institutions would therefore need safeguards, human oversight and circuit breakers alongside technological innovation.
