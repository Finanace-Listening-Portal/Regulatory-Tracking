Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Laxmanbhai Bhikhabhai Kapadia Vs ITO (ITAT Rajkot) Profit Estimated on Disputed Cash Deposits and Loans Is Taxable at Normal Rates, With Section 115BBE Inapplicable to the Estimate: ITAT Rajkot Rajkot ITAT Restricts Addition of ₹37.38 Lakh to ₹3.74 Lakh The Rajkot Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal restricted additions relating to demonetisation cash deposits and unsecured loans from ₹37,37,750 to ₹3,73,775. The Tribunal adopted a 10% estimate on the combined disputed amount, having regard to the facts of the case. It expressly directed the Assessing Officer to tax the estimated addition at normal income-tax rates, without applying Section 115BBE of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The appeal was partly allowed. Cash Deposits During Demonetisation The assessee, an individual engaged in repairing gold ornaments and carrying on agricultural activities, filed his return declaring taxable income of ₹2,82,290 and agricultural income of ₹6,73,089. During scrutiny, the Assessing Officer called for an explanation of ₹11,50,000 deposited in the assessee’s bank accounts during the demonetisation period. The assessee attributed the deposits to agricultural earnings, earlier bank withdrawals, past savings and gifts. He furnished cash books and agricultural records and submitted that part of the cash had accumulated over earlier years. He also explained that the crop had been sold during the relevant year, while much of the expenditure on cultivation had been incurred in the preceding year. The Assessing Officer allowed a combined benefit of ₹3 lakh for agricultural income, earlier savings and withdrawals, taking household and agricultural expenses into account. He treated the balance ₹8.50 lakh as unexplained and added it under Section 69A. The assessee also challenged the restriction of his declared agricultural income to ₹3 lakh in his grounds before the Tribunal. Unsecured Loans of ₹28.88 Lakh Also Added The Assessing Officer noticed that the return for the preceding year showed nil unsecured loans, whereas the return for the year under appeal disclosed unsecured loans of ₹28,87,750. The assessee furnished creditor particulars, PAN details, confirmations and bank statements. The officer considered the material insufficient to establish the identity and creditworthiness of the creditors and the genuineness of the transactions. He added the entire amount under Section 69A. The CIT(A) upheld the additions. Before the Tribunal, the assessee argued that the creditor particulars enabled the Assessing Officer to make independent enquiries, which he had failed to undertake. As regards the deposits, the assessee reiterated his explanation about agricultural income and past savings and also referred to cash receipts from ornament repairs. In the alternative, he requested that only the profit embedded in the deposits be assessed and that any addition concerning the loans be estimated. The Revenue proposed an ad hoc estimate of 15% on the aggregate disputed amount. Tribunal Relies on the Principle That Tax Is Levied on Income The Tribunal referred to CIT v. Williamson Financial Services, [2007] 165 Taxman 638 (SC), in which the Supreme Court explained that the charge under Section 4 is on total income computed in accordance with the Act. It also relied on CIT v. President Industries, [2002] 124 Taxman 654 (Gujarat), which concerned suppressed sales. The Gujarat High Court held that the sale proceeds include recovery of the cost of acquiring or processing the goods. Consequently, it is the profit embedded in the undisclosed sales that constitutes income. Taking account of the facts and the amounts involved, the Tribunal considered it fair to adopt a 10% estimate on the combined amount of ₹37,37,750, comprising ₹8,50,000 in disputed cash deposits and ₹28,87,750 in unsecured loans. It directed the Assessing Officer to restrict the total addition to ₹3,73,775 and tax it at normal rates. The operative direction dealt with the two additions together and gave no separate direction to accept the agricultural income at the returned figure. Author’s Comments The distinction for Section 115BBE purposes lies in the character of the amount ultimately assessed. The original assessment treated the disputed amounts as unexplained money under Section 69A. The Tribunal replaced those additions with a single estimate and expressly prescribed the rate treatment for that estimate. The relief thus covered both the quantum of the addition and the rate of tax. The scope of the decision deserves attention. President Industries dealt with suppressed sales, where the gross proceeds include an identifiable cost component. Here, the Tribunal drew on that principle while fixing a combined estimate for two different categories of disputed amounts, including unsecured loans. Its adoption of 10% was a fact-specific determination made after hearing the parties’ alternative submissions. In applying the decision to another case, the character of the receipts and the evidence supporting their source will matter, as will whether the final addition is sustained under a deeming provision or determined by estimating income. I have heard, both the parties and carefully gone through the submission put forth on behalf of the assessee along with the documents furnished and the case laws relied upon, and perused the fact of the case including the findings of the ld CIT(A) and other materials brought on record. 12. Learned Counsel for the assessee, in respect of cash deposited in the bank account to the tune of Rs.8,50,000/-, argued that the said cash was deposited out of agricultural income and previous year’s past savings. As the assessee is engaged in the repairing work of old ornaments therefore he had received cash from the client against the services rendered by him, said amount was deposited in the bank account therefore no addition should be made in the hands of the assessee. Alternatively and without prejudiced to the above, Ld. Counsel for the assessee submitted that entire cash deposited in the bank account should not be treated as income of the assessee and only profit element embedded in the cash deposit in bank account may be added in the hands of the assessee. 13. About unsecured loan, Ld. Counsel for the assessee, argued that the assessee during the assessment proceedings, submitted the name and address of the creditors, confirmation of the creditors, and bank statement of the creditors were also submitted, therefore no addition should have been made in the hands of the assessee. Before, the Assessing Officer, the assessee had submitted name and address of the creditors and assessee had also submitted the Permanent Account Number of the creditors and their bank statements, therefore, Assessing Officer could initiate independent inquiry against the creditors, however, assessing officer failed to do so. Alternatively, and without prejudice to the above, Ld. Counsel for the assessee submitted that an estimated addition may be made in the hands of the assessee. 14. On the other hand, Ld. DR for the revenue submitted that in case of cash deposited in the bank account to the tune of Rs.8,50,000/- and about unsecured loan of Rs.28,87,750/- total of both comes to Rs.37,37,750/- (8,50,000 + 28,87,750), as the assessee has not submitted the complete documents and evidences, in respect of unsecured loan and cash deposit, therefore an ad hoc estimated addition @ 15% may be made in the hands of the assessee to protect the interest of the revenue. 15. I have heard, both the parties. Learned Counsel for the assessee relied on the submissions made before the lower authorities. On the other hand, the Ld. DR for the Revenue has primarily reiterated the stand taken by the Assessing Officer, which I have already noted in my earlier para and is not being repeated for the sake of brevity. I note that taxing the receipts only has never been the motto of the Income-tax Act. In this regard, the observation of the Supreme Court in CIT v. Williamson Financial Services [2007] 165 Taxman 638 (SC) is reproduced below: “It is important to bear in mind that u/s 4, the levy is on total income of the assessee computed in accordance with and subject to the provisions of the Income Tax Act. What is chargeable to tax under the Income Tax Act is not the gross receipt but the income under the Income Tax Act. The tax is on income but not on gross receipts.” 16. Where suppression of sales receipts is involved, the question is whether the entire sales or only a percentage of profit should be adopted as income. In CIT v. President Industries [2002] 124 Taxman 654 (Gujarat), the Assessing Officer had found evidence of suppression of sales. He adopted the entire receipt (sales) as income but the Hon’ble Jurisdictional High Court has held that the entire undisclosed receipts (sales) cannot constitute income. The sales only represent the price received by the seller of the units for which the seller has already incurred the cost in order to acquire or process the inventory. Therefore, it is the realization of excess consideration over the cost incurred which should be assessed as profit or income. In other words, profit component embedded in the sales could be treated as income. 17. Therefore, respectfully following the ratio laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court and in view of the facts of the assessee’s case, and considering the smallness of the amount, it would be fair, if reasonable rate of profit is adopted to tax the unaccounted income of the assessee. Therefore, to meet the end of justice, I direct the Assessing Officer to make the estimated addition on Rs.37,37,750/- (8,50,000 + 28,87,750), at the rate of @10%, which comes to Rs.3,73,775/-. Hence, I direct the Assessing Officer to make the estimated addition, in respect of both the additions, in the hands of the assessee to the tune of Rs.3,73,775/-, by following the normal rate of income tax (not under section 115BBE of the Act), hence, assessee’s appeal is partly allowed. 18. In the result, appeal of the assessee is partly allowed, in the terms indicated above. Order is pronounced in the open Court Today on 23/11/2026.
