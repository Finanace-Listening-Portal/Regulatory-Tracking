Chandrashekar (Karnataka High Court) The Karnataka High Court, in Office of Principal Commissioner, GST and Central Excise, Mysuru Vs R. Chandrashekar & Others, examined whether directions issued by a Single Judge concerning additional GST liabilities arising from government works contracts could override statutory requirements relating to filing of returns, payment of interest, penalties and limitation. The Division Bench comprising Chief Justice Vibhu Bakhru and Justice K.S. Hemalekha considered five connected writ appeals filed by the Central GST authorities against the common judgment dated 11 April 2023. The principal controversy concerned contracts awarded before the introduction of GST on 1 July 2017, where contractors had quoted prices based on the prevailing tax regime but subsequently became liable to pay GST on works executed after its implementation. The Court distinguished contractual claims for reimbursement of additional tax from the independent statutory obligations of registered taxpayers under GST law. The contractors had entered into agreements with various departments of the Government of Karnataka, public sector undertakings, local authorities and other government agencies for executing civil construction and infrastructure works. Many contracts had been awarded through tenders issued before 1 July 2017, and the quoted rates were based on the applicable Schedule of Rates, which included taxes payable under the earlier regime. Several contractors had opted for the composition scheme under Section 15 of the Karnataka Value Added Tax Act, 2003, involving payment of tax at 4% of turnover, while others were governed by the regular VAT provisions. Following the introduction of GST, works contracts attracted GST at 18% between 1 July 2017 and 21 August 2017 and at 12% thereafter, as recorded in the judgment. The contractors maintained that the additional tax liability had not been contemplated while submitting their bids and that requiring them to bear the differential amount would impose an unforeseen financial burden. The contractors submitted representations dated 24 November 2018 requesting the concerned government employers to reimburse the additional tax burden resulting from the transition to GST. They also sought protection against coercive action by the GST authorities, who had issued notices requiring compliance with statutory obligations, including filing of returns and payment of applicable taxes. The contractors approached the Karnataka High Court seeking directions for revision of contractual arrangements, determination of the differential tax liability and reimbursement of the additional GST payable on works executed after 1 July 2017. Their contention was that contracts concluded under the earlier tax structure should not result in contractors bearing the entire financial impact of a subsequent statutory change, particularly when the works were being executed for government departments and public authorities. By its common judgment dated 11 April 2023, the learned Single Judge granted relief to the contractors and issued directions concerning the calculation and reimbursement of additional GST liabilities. The government departments and agencies were directed to determine the value of works executed before and after the introduction of GST and calculate the differential tax burden arising from the change in the applicable tax regime. The Single Judge also directed consideration of appropriate contractual adjustments or reimbursement of additional GST. However, the judgment went further by permitting the contractors to file GST returns or amended returns without insisting upon statutory interest, penalties or limitation requirements. The GST authorities were additionally restrained from taking precipitative action against the contractors for a period of six months. These directions affecting the administration and enforcement of GST law became the principal subject of the writ appeals. Before the Division Bench, the Central GST authorities challenged the directions insofar as they interfered with the statutory framework governing tax compliance. The Department submitted that the provisions of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, and the corresponding State and Integrated GST enactments prescribed the obligations of taxpayers regarding filing of returns, payment of tax, interest, penalties and applicable time limits. Particular reliance was placed on Section 50 of the CGST Act, which governs interest on delayed payment of tax. According to the appellants, a dispute between contractors and their government employers regarding reimbursement of additional GST could not justify directions exempting taxpayers from statutory consequences. The authorities maintained that the Single Judge could not grant a general relaxation from interest, penalties or limitation where the governing legislation did not authorise such relief. The Division Bench accepted that the contractual issue concerning reimbursement of additional GST was distinct from the statutory liability of contractors under GST law. The Court observed that the question whether contractors were entitled to recover the differential tax burden from their respective employers depended upon the contractual arrangements between those parties. Such contractual claims could be considered independently, but they could not alter the statutory obligations imposed upon registered taxpayers. The Court held that directions permitting filing or amendment of GST returns contrary to statutory provisions, or granting general exemptions from interest, penalties and limitation, could not be sustained. It clarified that directions concerning calculation or reimbursement of differential GST were matters involving the contractors and the respective government employers and could not be construed as modifying the powers or duties of the GST authorities under the applicable legislation. Accordingly, the Karnataka High Court set aside the Single Judge’s judgment to the extent that it issued directions against the GST authorities. The Court did not disturb the contractual aspect of the dispute concerning possible reimbursement of additional GST by the government departments and agencies. The five connected writ appeals, namely W.A. Nos. 1587/2023, 1599/2023, 1638/2023, 1647/2023 and 25/2024, were disposed of by the judgment dated 10 June 2026, bearing neutral citation 2026:KHC:28975-DB. The decision establishes that claims arising from increased tax costs under pre-GST government works contracts must be distinguished from statutory GST compliance. While contractors may pursue contractual reimbursement of the differential tax burden, directions granting relief against interest, penalties, limitation or return-filing requirements cannot override the provisions of the GST enactments. Cases Discussed Common judgment dated 11 April 2023 in W.P. No. 19067/2019 and connected writ petitions (Karnataka High Court, Single Judge) — the order under appeal. The Division Bench considered its directions regarding differential GST reimbursement and set aside only those addressed to GST authorities. No separately named judicial precedent is relied upon in the Division Bench’s substantive reasoning.
