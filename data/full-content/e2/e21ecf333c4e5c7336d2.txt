Summary: The time and place of supply provisions constitute an essential component of GST compliance, determining the timing of tax liability and the nature of tax applicable to transactions involving goods and services. This article examines Sections 12, 13 and 14 of the CGST Act, 2017, covering time of supply under forward charge, reverse charge, invoice issuance, payment receipt, special circumstances and changes in tax rates. It also discusses the relationship between time of supply and invoicing requirements under Section 31, supported by practical illustrations involving manufacturers and service providers. The place of supply provisions under Sections 10, 11, 12 and 13 of the IGST Act, 2017 are examined with reference to domestic supplies, bill-to-ship-to transactions, imports, exports and cross-border services. Specific rules applicable to immovable property, training, event management, transportation, telecommunications, banking, insurance, advertising and online information services are discussed alongside the general recipient-location rule. Particular attention is given to the omission of Section 13(8)(b) by the Finance Act, 2026, with effect from 30 March 2026, and its implications for intermediary services involving overseas suppliers or recipients. The article further outlines the statutory conditions for qualifying a transaction as an export of services. Businesses must understand the applicable provisions, correctly identify the nature of each transaction, maintain supporting documentation relating to invoices, payments, movement of goods and recipient locations, and periodically review their GST positions in light of legislative amendments and regulatory developments. A question that arises in the mind of every GST-registered supplier is: When does the tax liability arise? This question is answered by the time and place of supply rules under the GST Act, 2017. The applicable provisions are contained in Sections 12 (goods), 13 (services), and 14 (change in the rate of tax) of the CGST Act, 2017. These provisions are closely linked to Section 31, which prescribes the time limits for issuing invoices. A.1 Time of Supply of Goods (Section 12) Forward Charge (Supplier Pays the Tax) The time of supply is the earlier of: The date of issue of the invoice or the last date on which the invoice should have been issued under Section 31 (generally, on or before the removal of goods or their delivery/making available where movement is not involved); and The date on which the supplier receives the payment. The date of receipt of payment is the earlier of the date on which the payment is entered in the supplier’s books of account or the date on which it is credited to the supplier’s bank account. Where an advance is received up to 竄ｹ1,000 in excess of the invoice value, the supplier may, at their option, treat the invoice date as the time of supply for that excess amount. Reverse Charge (Recipient Pays the Tax) The time of supply is the earliest of: The date of receipt of goods; The date of payment (the earlier of the date of entry in the books of account or the date of debit to the bank account); and The date immediately following 30 days from the date of the supplier’s invoice. If none of these dates can be determined, the time of supply shall be the date on which the recipient records the receipt of goods in their books of account. Vouchers Where the supply is made against a voucher, the time of supply is the date of issue of the voucher if the supply is identifiable at that point; otherwise, it is the date of redemption. Residuary and Special Cases If the time of supply cannot be determined under the rules mentioned above, it shall be the due date of the periodical return or, where no return is due, the date on which the tax is paid. For interest, late fees, or penalties arising from delayed payment of consideration, the time of supply is the date on which the supplier receives such payment. Illustration: A manufacturer dispatches products on 10 September and issues the invoice on the same date. However, the manufacturer receives an advance on 4 September. The time of supply is 4 September, being the earlier of the payment date and the invoice date, to the extent of the advance received. A.2 Time of Supply of Services (Section 13) Forward Charge The time of supply depends on whether the invoice is issued within the period prescribed under Section 31. For most services, the prescribed period is 30 days from the date of supply of the service (45 days for insurers, banks, and NBFCs). If the invoice is issued within the prescribed period, the time of supply is the earlier of the invoice date and the date of receipt of payment. If the invoice is not issued within the prescribed period, the time of supply is the earlier of the date of provision of the service and the date of receipt of payment. If neither of these dates can be determined, the time of supply shall be the date on which the recipient records the receipt of the service in their books of account. Reverse Charge The time of supply is the earlier of the date of payment or the date immediately following 60 days from the date of the supplier’s invoice. If the invoice is not available, the time of supply shall be the date of entry in the recipient’s books of account. Where services are received from an associated enterprise located outside India, the time of supply is the earlier of the date of entry in the recipient’s books of account or the date of payment. Vouchers and Residuary Rule The same logic as applicable to goods applies to vouchers, and the residuary rule applies where the time of supply cannot otherwise be determined. Illustration: A consultant completes an assignment on 15 June. The invoice is raised on 25 July, which is beyond the prescribed 30-day period, and payment is received on 30 June. Since the invoice was issued beyond the prescribed period, the time of supply is 15 June, being the date of provision of the service, and not the invoice date. A.3 Change in Rate of Tax (Section 14) When the rate of tax changes, the date of supply determines which rate is applicable. In broad terms, if the goods or services are supplied before the rate change but the invoice is issued and payment is received after the change, the old rate applies. If the supply takes place after the rate change, the new rate applies. Where the invoice date and payment date fall on different sides of the rate-change date, the relevant rule considers which of these events occurred before or after the change. Special care is required when dealing with advances and supplies that span a rate-change date. Part B: Place of Supply The place of supply is governed by the IGST Act, 2017. The location of the supplier and the location of the recipient (defined under Sections 2(15) and 2(14), respectively) are relevant to every place of supply rule. A supply is considered an inter-State supply where the location of the supplier and the place of supply are in different States or Union Territories (Section 7). It is considered an intra-State supply where both are in the same State or Union Territory (Section 8). The Act divides the place of supply provisions into four sections: Section 10: Goods, other than imports and exports. Section 11: Goods imported into or exported from India. Section 12: Services where both the supplier and the recipient are in India. Section 13: Services where either the supplier or the recipient is outside India. B.1 Place of Supply of Goods within India (Section 10) Goods involving movement: The place where the movement of goods terminates for delivery to the recipient. Bill-to-ship-to transactions: Where goods are delivered by the supplier to a third person on the direction of the recipient, the goods are deemed to have been received by the recipient. The place of supply is the principal place of business of the recipient (the person who directed the delivery). Goods not involving movement: The location of the goods at the time of delivery. Goods assembled or installed at a site: The place where the goods are assembled or installed. Goods supplied on board a conveyance (such as a ship, aircraft, or train): The location where the goods are taken on board. Other cases: Where the place of supply cannot be determined under the above provisions, it shall be determined in the prescribed manner. Illustration: A Bengaluru-based supplier sells goods to a buyer in Chennai and ships them to the buyer’s warehouse in Hyderabad. The movement of goods terminates in Hyderabad. Therefore, the place of supply is Telangana, and the supply is an inter-State supply (IGST). If, instead, the Chennai buyer directs the supplier to deliver the goods to a third party in Hyderabad (bill-to-ship-to transaction), the place of supply would be Tamil Nadu, being the buyer’s principal place of business. B.2 Imports and Exports of Goods (Section 11) For imports, the place of supply is the location of the importer. For exports, the place of supply is a location outside India. Both imports and exports are treated as inter-State supplies for the purposes of IGST. B.3 Place of Supply of Services within India (Section 12) Default Rule (Section 12(2)) If the recipient is registered, the place of supply is the location of the recipient. If the recipient is unregistered, the place of supply is the recipient’s location where the address is available in the records of the supplier. Otherwise, it is the location of the supplier. Specific Rules Specific rules override the default rule for certain services, including: Immovable property: Includes services provided by architects, surveyors, and services relating to hotel accommodation and venue rental. The place of supply is where the property is located. Restaurant, catering, personal grooming, fitness, beauty, health, and similar services: The place of supply is where the services are actually performed. Training and performance appraisal: The recipient’s location in the case of registered recipients and the place where the services are performed in the case of unregistered recipients. Admission to events (sports, cultural, and entertainment) and organisation of events: The place where the event is held. For the organisation of events provided to registered recipients, the recipient-location rule applies. Transportation of goods (other than by mail or courier): The recipient’s location in the case of registered persons and the place where the goods are handed over for transportation in the case of unregistered persons. Passenger transportation: The recipient’s location in the case of registered persons; otherwise, the place where the passenger embarks. Services provided on board a conveyance: The first scheduled point of departure of the conveyance. Telecommunication services: The place of supply depends on the type of service, such as the installation address for fixed-line services, the billing address for post-paid mobile services, and the place of recharge or sale for pre-paid services. Banking and financial services: The recipient’s location as per the supplier’s records, and the supplier’s location otherwise. Insurance services: The recipient’s location in the case of registered persons; otherwise, the location available in the insurer’s records. Advertisement services provided to the Government: The place of supply is determined for each State or Union Territory in proportion to the amount attributable to it. Illustration: A Mumbai-based training firm provides a workshop to an unregistered individual in Pune. Since the training is physically conducted in Pune, the place of supply is Maharashtra, and the supply is an intra-State supply. If the same training is provided to a registered company in Karnataka, the place of supply is Karnataka, and IGST applies. B.4 Place of Supply of Services with a Cross-Border Element (Section 13) Default Rule (Section 13(2)) The place of supply is the location of the recipient. If the recipient’s location is not available in the ordinary course of business, the place of supply is the location of the supplier. Exceptions The following exceptions apply: Services requiring the physical presence of the recipient or goods: Where services are provided to an individual requiring their physical presence, or in respect of goods made available by the recipient, the place of supply is where the services are actually performed. Services relating to immovable property: The place of supply is where the property is located. Admission to or organisation of events: The place of supply is where the event is held. Services covered under Section 13(8): The place of supply is the supplier’s location for specified services, such as banking and financial services provided to account holders and hiring of means of transport for a period of up to one month. Passenger transportation: The place of supply is where the passenger embarks on the conveyance. Services provided on board a conveyance: The first scheduled point of departure of the conveyance. Online information and database access or retrieval services: The location of the recipient. Recent Change: Intermediary Services Until recently, intermediary services were governed by Section 13(8)(b), which prescribed the place of supply as the location of the supplier. The Finance Act, 2026, omitted this clause with effect from 30 March 2026. Intermediary services are now governed by the default rule, under which the place of supply is the location of the recipient. This change is significant for Indian intermediaries providing services to overseas clients, as such services may now qualify as exports if all the other prescribed conditions are satisfied. Similarly, services provided by foreign intermediaries to Indian recipients may amount to imports of services, attracting reverse charge. The statutory definition of an “intermediary” under Section 2(13) remains unchanged. Conditions for Export of Services The place of supply is only one of the conditions for qualifying a transaction as an export of services. Under Section 2(6), a supply qualifies as an export of services only if all the following conditions are satisfied: The supplier of the service is located in India. The recipient of the service is located outside India. The place of supply of the service is outside India. Payment for the service is received in convertible foreign exchange or in Indian rupees, wherever permitted by the RBI. The supplier and recipient are not merely establishments of a distinct person. Conclusion The time and place of supply rules form the backbone of GST compliance. The time of supply determines when the tax liability arises and which rate of tax applies, while the place of supply determines which tax is applicable and which State is entitled to receive the tax. Since both sets of rules are detailed and subject to regular amendments, businesses should map each type of transaction to the relevant provisions, maintain proper documentation of the facts that determine the applicable rules (such as the movement of goods, registration status and location of the recipient, and invoice and payment dates), and review their positions whenever there is a change in the law. ****** Disclaimer: This article is intended for general informational purposes only and should not be treated as professional tax advice.
