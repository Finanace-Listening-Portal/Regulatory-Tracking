Summary: The ABSLI DigiShield Plan, issued by Aditya Birla Sun Life Insurance Company Limited under UIN 109N108V13, offers ten distinct benefit designs whose protection, payout, survival and maturity outcomes depend on the selected option. Options 1 to 8 address different cover and income requirements; Option 9 provides monthly survival payments after age 60 that reduce the later death benefit; and Option 10 provides a maturity benefit based on contractually defined Total Premiums Paid rather than every amount collected. Under the current V13 customer information sheet, Option 6 pays monthly income at 1.25% of sum assured during the selected income-benefit term, while Option 7 combines an immediate sum assured with monthly income at 0.5% for ten years. Terminal-illness and optional accelerated critical-illness payments reduce the remaining death cover and should not be double counted. Premium-payment grace periods, lapse, revival, surrender, reduced-paid-up treatment and suicide exclusions vary with contractual conditions and selected options. Prospective policyholders should examine the product version, benefit illustration, policy schedule, proposal disclosures, exclusions, rider conditions and claims documentation rather than relying on a marketing illustration. The article also explains grievance escalation through the insurer, Bima Bharosa and the Insurance Ombudsman. For tax year 2026–27, qualifying individual life-insurance services are generally covered by the GST exemption effective 22 September 2025, whereas income-tax premium deductions under section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 depend on the applicable tax regime and statutory requirements. Policy receipts, particularly Option 9 survival payments and Option 10 maturity proceeds, require separate tax analysis. Ten benefit designs that should not be treated as interchangeable Options 1 to 4: level, increasing and age-linked protection Level Cover keeps the selected sum assured structure straightforward. Increasing Cover permits a simple annual increase at the selected 5% or 10% rate, subject to the product terms. Sum Assured Reduction allows a scheduled reduction rather than pretending that cover needs remain constant. Whole Life Cover is structured to continue to age 100 and permits a selected reduction at a stated later age. These choices affect premium, affordability and the amount available to nominees, so the year-by-year benefit illustration should be retained with the policy. Options 5 to 8: age-100, income, combined and lower-cover mechanics The product’s age-100 design, income-based death benefit, level-cover-plus-income design and low-cover design meet different cash-flow needs. Under Option 6, the V13 customer information sheet states that the death benefit is paid in monthly instalments equal to 1.25% of sum assured during the selected income-benefit term. Under Option 7, the V13 sheet states that the sum assured is paid immediately and a level monthly income of 0.5% of sum assured is paid for ten years, beginning from the first policy monthiversary on or after death. These are contractual payout patterns, not investment yields. Option 9 survival payments reduce the later death benefit Option 9 begins a monthly survival benefit of 0.12% of sum assured from the first policy monthiversary after the life insured turns 60, continuing until death or the end of the policy term, whichever occurs first. On a later death during the term, survival benefits already paid are deducted from the sum assured on death. A reader should therefore not add the lifetime payments and the original cover as though both remain fully payable. Option 10 returns defined premiums, not every rupee collected If Option 10 remains in force and the life insured survives to maturity, the sum assured on maturity equals Total Premiums Paid as defined by the contract. The policyholder should distinguish that term from taxes, rider premiums, underwriting extras, modal loadings and other amounts that may not form part of the defined return. Options 1 to 9 have no maturity benefit under the V13 customer information sheet. Accelerated benefits, riders and the risk of double counting The plan includes a terminal-illness benefit under the current product description. It pays 50% of the applicable sum assured on death, subject to the stated maximum, and waives future base premiums; a later death benefit is reduced by the accelerated amount already paid. The payment is therefore an advance of cover, not a second full sum assured. The diagnosis must satisfy the contractual definition and claim requirements. An optional Accelerated Critical Illness benefit covers the listed illnesses only when selected, underwritten, paid for and in force. It is also accelerated: a paid critical-illness amount reduces the remaining death cover. Other riders shown in the V13 customer information sheet have separate UINs, premiums, waiting periods and exclusions. The sheet lists a 90-day waiting period for the accelerated critical-illness benefit and certain riders, while other riders have different waits. A base-policy summary cannot be used to infer rider coverage. Lapse, surrender and suicide clauses can change the expected outcome For Options 1 to 8, the current customer information sheet says a regular-pay or limited-pay policy lapses if the entire instalment premium is not received by the end of grace during the premium-paying term; benefits then cease immediately unless revival succeeds. Reduced paid-up treatment is not available for these options. Option 9 and Option 10 have their own value-acquisition and reduced-paid-up rules. A policyholder who cannot continue should request a written surrender or paid-up quotation for the exact policy rather than assuming a refund. The base suicide exclusion applies if death by suicide occurs within 12 months from commencement of risk or revival. The V13 sheet describes payment of Total Premiums Paid plus specified extras and modal loadings, excluding applicable taxes, or surrender value where acquired and higher. The issued contract controls the calculation. Separate rider exclusions may be broader and should be read independently. GST and income-tax treatment for the 2026–27 tax year Individual life-insurance GST exemption Government material records that qualifying individual life-insurance services became exempt from GST from 22 September 2025. DigiShield is an individual product, so a qualifying base-policy premium is generally within that exemption. This should not be read as a statement that every rider, group cover or separately supplied service is exempt. The premium receipt and tax invoice remain the documents to check, particularly where an optional rider is included or a payment spans a rule change. Premium deduction depends on regime and statutory conditions The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026. A qualifying life-insurance premium may fall within the aggregate deduction framework under section 123 when the taxpayer uses the regime in which that deduction is available, subject to the overall statutory ceiling and conditions such as the relationship to the insured and premium-to-capital-sum tests. The default concessional regime does not generally allow this deduction. A payment receipt alone does not establish deductibility. Policy receipts are not uniformly tax-free Death benefits receive distinct statutory treatment, while a maturity, surrender or other non-death receipt can depend on policy issue date, annual premium thresholds, capital-sum ratios and statutory exceptions. Option 9 survival payments and Option 10 maturity proceeds should be tested against the law for the actual taxpayer and year. Tax deducted by the insurer, if any, is not the final tax determination. Frequently Asked Questions on DigiShield V13 1. Is every DigiShield option a pure term plan? No. The V13 customer information sheet classifies Options 1 to 9 as pure risk, while Option 10 is non-linked other than pure risk and pension because it carries a maturity feature. 2. Which current document should be checked when two income figures conflict? Use the issued policy schedule and current V13 contract materials. The V13 customer information sheet states 1.25% of sum assured monthly for Option 6; an inconsistent promotional line should not displace it. 3. Does the terminal-illness payment sit on top of the full death cover? No. It is accelerated. A later death benefit is reduced by the amount already paid, although the current product description also states that future base premiums are waived after an accepted terminal-illness claim. 4. Can Option 9 pay survival income and the full original cover later? Not as a simple addition. The contract deducts survival benefits already paid from the later sum assured on death. 5. Does Return of Premium include GST and rider charges? The benefit is linked to the defined Total Premiums Paid. Readers should not assume taxes, rider premiums, underwriting extras or modal loading are included without checking the definition and illustration. 6. What happens if a monthly premium is late? The monthly grace period is 15 days. Cover continues during grace, but an unpaid premium may be recovered from a death benefit. After grace, lapse or value rules depend on the selected option and payment pattern. 7. Is life-insurance premium automatically deductible in 2026–27? No. The tax regime, statutory ceiling, insured relationship and premium-to-capital-sum conditions matter. The default concessional regime generally does not permit the section 123 deduction.
