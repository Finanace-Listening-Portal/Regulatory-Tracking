The funding will support its Rs 45,000 crore three-year network expansion plan. While the involvement of multiple lenders has resulted in the overall process taking a little longer than what we had earlier anticipated, we remain confident in our engagement with the consortium," Kishore said during the company's Q2 FY27 analyst call. Advt ETBFSI had reported on November 8 that SBI, the lead lender, had approved the proposal and circulated its appraisal to other participating lenders. Punjab National Bank (PNB) had indicated its willingness to participate, with its individual exposure yet to be finalised, while Union Bank of India was expected to take a decision by the end of November. Infrastructure financier National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development (NaBFID) was also expected to approve an exposure of Rs 4,000 crore.SBI-led consortium advances Vodafone Idea loan; NaBFID to approve ₹4,000 cr, PNB and Union Bank to decideVodafone Idea's proposed Rs 35,000 crore consortium financing has made further advances, with atleast Punjab National Bank, Union Bank of India expected to decide its exposure by the end of the month, bankers aware of the discussions have told ETBFSI. Infrastructure financier NaBFID is also expected to approve a Rs 4,000 crore exposure shortly.Vodafone Idea CEO added that the company was engaging with three sets of lenders, the PSU bank consortium led by SBI, Indian private sector banks and foreign banks for external commercial borrowings (ECBs), to conclude its overall funding plan."I think that conversation is the large kitty. And that is, I hope that when I meet the next time, I'm able to share a good thing with that. But yeah, that's very close to the closure," Kishore said, referring to the PSU bank consortium.The proposed funding package comprises Rs 25,000 crore in funded facilities and Rs 10,000 crore in non-funded facilities, including letters of credit, and is intended to support the telecom operator's Rs 45,000 crore capital expenditure programme over three years.Under the consortium arrangement, individual participating banks are required to take their own credit decisions and finalise their respective exposures based on SBI's appraisal. The lenders will then need to complete their internal sanction processes and documentation before the financing arrangement can be operationalised. Advt Funding progress and lender comfortThe progress in funding discussions comes after the government reassessed Vi's adjusted gross revenue (AGR) liabilities, reducing the amount to Rs 64,046 crore from Rs 87,695 crore and restructuring the payment schedule, with payments deferred to FY36 onwards. The relief has given the company greater visibility on its near-term statutory cash outflows and room to prioritise network investments.CapexMeanwhile, Vi's capital expenditure more than doubled sequentially to Rs 3,946 crore in Q2 FY27, compared with the previous quarter, as the company accelerated investments in network expansion. Advt Chief Financial Officer Tejas Mehta said the company had arranged more than Rs 4,000 crore in incremental bank funding during the quarter. The acceleration in capital expenditure was supported by the incremental funding secured along with operating cash flows.The company has placed capex orders worth more than Rs 13,200 crore as part of its three-year network investment programme, of which nearly Rs 6,000 crore has been executed.Mehta said the company did not require the entire Rs 25,000 crore funded facility to be disbursed upfront and would draw down funds as and when required to sustain its investment programme. Advt He also clarified that a substantial part of the initial utilisation was through non-funded facilities, including letters of credit, which do not reflect in the company's reported bank borrowings.The company's bank debt stood at Rs 943 crore as of September 30, 2026, while cash and bank balances stood at Rs 5,470 crore. Mehta said the company's legacy bank debt had now been fully repaid and the current drawdowns represented incremental funding secured by the company.The CFO added that more funded drawdowns would be visible in Q3 as the company exhausts existing letters of credit and makes payments against them.Vi also paid more than Rs 4,300 crore in spectrum dues in October, taking its total payments against approximately Rs 7,000 crore of FY27 spectrum obligations to over Rs 6,000 crore.
