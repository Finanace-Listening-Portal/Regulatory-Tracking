The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India’s consultation paper has emerged as a key uncertainty for the insurer, although lower commission payouts could also reduce its expense ratio.Commissions account for more than half of HDFC Life’s expense ratio of around 21%. If payouts fall to half their current level or lower, the expense ratio could mathematically decline to around 15%, though the actual outcome will depend on the final rules and their implementation. Management expects agency distribution to be least affected, followed by banks, while brokers and corporate agents could face a greater impact, according to JM Financial. Advt HDFC Life’s annualised premium equivalent (APE), a measure of new business sales, grew 13% year on year in the September quarter. APE sourced through HDFC Bank rose 12%, with the bank accounting for around 48% of the insurer’s APE in the quarter, against 47% in the June quarter. The recovery was supported by stronger branch-level engagement, competitive non-participating product rates and the fading impact of incentive-led business in the year-earlier period.Other bank partnerships and non-bank alliances each grew 13%, while the agency channel recorded 13% growth in the first half of FY27. HDFC Life has also announced a partnership with Jammu and Kashmir Bank. Management is targeting growth in line with the industry in FY27.Wider impactThe regulatory changes could also affect the economics of HDFC Life’s relationship with its parent bank. Commission payments to HDFC Bank have remained rangebound after the bank opted for an open-architecture model. HDFC Life hopes deeper engagement with the bank will help it gain a greater share of business.The insurer’s value of new business (VNB) margin stood at 24.7% in the first half of FY27, compared with 24.5% a year earlier, despite absorbing the full impact of the goods and services tax change. Management has said most benefits from scale are being reinvested in distribution and technology, and expects VNB growth to be in line with or slightly ahead of APE growth. Advt Management has also argued that restrictions on credit-life insurance could make it harder to achieve the government’s Insurance for All by 2047 objective. Implementation of the proposed changes is considered unlikely before April 1, 2027, giving insurers time to assess the final rules and their implications for distribution.HDFC Life’s growth outlook remains supported by its bank channel recovery. The brokerage estimates APE growth of 15% annually and VNB growth of 18% over FY26-FY29. The effect of the commission proposals on the insurer’s costs and business volumes will depend on the final regulatory framework and how distribution partners respond.
