} } Listen to This ArticleThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) does not normally use monetary policy to address financial stability risks, but the two cannot be separated completely, Governor Sanjay Malhotra said in an interview with Moneycontrol, amid pressure on the rupee over the past few weeks. “The mandate of price stability cannot be achieved without financial stability, as a stable financial system is essential for the effective transmission of monetary policy,” Malhotra said. Responding to a question on whether the monetary policy committee (MPC) would hike rates to defend the currency, he said the RBI generally follows a separation principle under which monetary policy is used to achieve price stability, while targeted regulatory, supervisory, and macroprudential tools address financial stability risks. “However, they cannot be separated completely,” he said, adding that monetary policy can affect financial stability. On the rupee, Malhotra said the RBI was determined to take all necessary steps to ensure orderly movement in the foreign exchange (forex) market and curb excessive speculation. “The RBI is determined to do whatever is necessary to ensure orderly movement in the forex market,” he said. “The purpose of these measures is primarily to ensure an orderly movement in the forex market. The measures taken — for example, disallowing the rebooking of cancelled forex derivatives contracts — will also check undue speculative bets. We have brought in a forex reserve requirement because we observed excessive speculative positioning on the buy side,” he said. The RBI has announced a raft of measures to ease pressure on the rupee, including a dedicated dollar window for state-run oil-marketing companies and tighter rules on forex derivatives.
