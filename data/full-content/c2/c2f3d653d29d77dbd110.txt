Vs DCIT (ITAT Bangalore) Summary: The Bangalore Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal considered the assessee’s appeal for assessment year 2016–17 against the final assessment order dated 31 March 2021. The assessee challenged a transfer-pricing adjustment of ₹1,18,75,36,269 relating to international transactions, disputed the selection and rejection of software-development comparables, and contested an adjustment on outstanding receivables. It also challenged disallowance of ₹18,18,00,000 in employee stock option plan (ESOP) expenditure under section 37 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. In the software-development segment, the Tribunal examined functional comparability and followed decisions of coordinate benches concerning Larsen & Toubro Infotech Ltd., Persistent Systems Ltd. and Infosys Ltd. It directed exclusions and considered the inclusion of other disputed companies on the basis of the cited rulings and facts relevant to the year. Other general transfer-pricing grounds required no separate adjudication, while the ground relating to computation of margins was not pressed. On receivables from associated enterprises, the assessee argued that its average collection period was short, that the contractual credit period was longer, and that no separate notional-interest adjustment was warranted. The Tribunal examined authorities concerning whether deferred receivables constitute a separate international transaction and the appropriate benchmarking of such balances, and gave directions in accordance with the legal principles discussed in its order. Regarding ESOP expenditure, the Tribunal relied on the Karnataka High Court ruling in CIT v. Biocon Ltd. and coordinate-bench decisions recognising the deductibility of qualifying employee stock-option costs under section 37. It allowed the assessee’s relevant grounds, while the independent withholding-tax objections under section 195 were not separately adjudicated in the cited reasoning. Other corporate-tax grounds were not pressed; interest under sections 234B and 234C was consequential. The appeal was ultimately partly allowed for statistical purposes.
