} } Listen to This ArticleUttar Pradesh's microfinance outstanding portfolio grew 6 per cent year-on-year to ₹ 34,446 crore at the end of September 2026, signalling a recovery in the sector after a period of contraction and rising delinquencies. The portfolio stood at around ₹ 32,500 crore at the end of September 2025. This marks a turnaround from the 20 per cent contraction recorded in the corresponding quarter of FY26, when outstanding microfinance loans fell to around ₹ 32,500 crore from nearly ₹ 40,000 crore a year earlier. According to Sudhir Sinha, chief executive officer of the Uttar Pradesh Microfinance Association (UPMA), the sector is gradually returning to growth after a period of relative decline and rising delinquencies. “We must empower microfinance institutions (MFIs) to develop a sustainable and future-ready model. While technology is essential, we must ensure that human beings are not left behind,” he said. With nearly 9 million borrowers, Uttar Pradesh is India's second-largest microfinance market, accounting for 12 per cent of the country's outstanding portfolio.
