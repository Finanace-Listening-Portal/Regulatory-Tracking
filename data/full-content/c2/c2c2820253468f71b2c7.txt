Articles 4 min read Exclusive: FIDC to represent NBFCs' case to IRDAI for gradual reset of insurance commissions Non-bank lenders are seeking a phased reduction in insurance distribution commissions proposed by IRDAI, saying that the sharp reduction could make credit-linked insurance distribution unviable and disrupt the network through which insurance reaches customers. Five industry sources told ET BFSI that the SRO body FIDC is aggregating feedback from NBFCs and will submit its representation to IRDAI by October 24. Advt IRDAI's consultation paper dated September 23 has proposed tighter commission norms, with the framework capping commissions for distributors at 2 per cent of premium for certain products. The proposals could result in 50–66 per cent cuts in commissions from FY28 onwards, according to Jefferies, which said NBFCs derive part of their fee income from insurance distribution and would consequently be negatively impacted.Insurance income accounts for about 20 per cent of FY27 estimated PBT at L&T Finance, 13 per cent at Piramal Finance and 12 per cent at Chola Finance, according to Jefferies. HDB Financial Services and Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services follow at about 10 per cent each.NBFCs seek recognition of distribution costsNBFCs are saying that the proposed framework needs to recognise the infrastructure, manpower and operational costs involved in distributing insurance, particularly for lenders with large rural and semi-urban networks.“Insurance companies really do not have any distribution network, they use this distribution network currently,” one senior NBFC executive told ET BFSI.NBFCs, particularly those operating in rural and microfinance segments, have thousands of branches, meeting centres and field staff spread across smaller geographies, the executive said. Insurance distribution involves not only infrastructure and employee costs but also operational bandwidth, including processing claims and collecting documents from families in the event of a borrower's death. Advt Industry seeks phased transition for distributorsThe industry is therefore seeking a glide path for distributors similar to the transition proposed for insurers under the consultation framework.“We are saying that the glide path, similar glide path should be worked on as far as the distributors are concerned,” the executive said.The demand is not necessarily to retain existing commission levels indefinitely, but to implement the reduction in stages so distributors have time to adjust their cost structures and business models, industry executives said.L&T Finance MD & CEO Sudipta Roy said the company expects to be able to offset at least part of the impact through changes across its business lines. Advt “There will be, obviously, scope for revenue optimisations, different lines of business. So, I do expect that over the medium to short term, we will be reasonably able to sort of paper over any sort of impact that comes from this particular guideline. There could be a glide path for commission optimisation to distributors in line with the glide path proposed by the manufacturers in the treatment of expense of management,” Roy told analysts.For Piramal Finance, the impact is more nuanced because of its ownership of an insurance business.Piramal Finance MD & CEO Jairam Sridharan told ET BFSI in an interview that the gross impact of the proposed changes on the company would be equivalent to about 18–24 basis points of ROA. Advt The company, however, has a natural hedge because it owns 50% of an insurance business and distributes all of its insurance, he said.“The reason I say use the word gross is that our situation is a bit unique in the sector. In that we are also 50% owners of an insurance business,” Sridharan said.Piramal Finance distributes 100% of its insurance through the business, he said, meaning that any loss of value from lower distribution income is partly offset by the benefit accruing to the insurance company, of which Piramal Finance is a 50% owner.The industry is also flagging the potential impact on insurance penetration if distribution through lenders becomes economically unviable.“The basic assumption that this is an attachment product and it does not lead to any effort, I think that is just to be re-looked at. Once that is re-looked at and the effort is priced in, automatically there will be an understanding between distributors, insurance companies as well as the regulator,” the senior NBFC executive quoted above said.The executive said the cost assessment should include infrastructure, people and credit-related costs associated with the business. FIDC is aggregating such data from lenders to arrive at an industry-wide assessment, with NBFCs willing to share auditable cost data from sample companies, the executive said.The industry is also concerned that a sharp reduction in commissions could result in lenders reducing their focus on insurance distribution, particularly in smaller towns and rural markets where NBFCs provide a significant part of the distribution infrastructure.A second senior industry executive said a phased approach would allow distributors to absorb the impact rather than moving directly from current commission levels to the proposed cap.An email written to FIDC seeking clarification didn’t elicit any response.
