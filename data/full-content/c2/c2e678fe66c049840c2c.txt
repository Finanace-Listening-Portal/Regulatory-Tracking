Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Minoti Malhotra Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Delhi Bench ‘A’, in Minoti Malhotra Vs DCIT, ITA No. 6405/Del/2025, decided on 25 March 2026, quashed an assessment framed under Section 153C read with Section 143(3) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, for Assessment Year 2021-22. The Tribunal held that the jurisdictional requirement for initiating proceedings against a person other than the searched person was not satisfied because the relevant satisfaction note was recorded on 21 March 2023, after the statutory cut-off of 1 April 2021. Applying the first proviso to Section 153C(1) and the restriction contained in Section 153C(3), the Tribunal accepted the assessee’s additional legal ground, annulled the assessment order and allowed the appeal. The dispute originated from search and seizure operations conducted under Section 132 on 3 February 2021 and subsequent dates at different business and residential premises connected with the Samir Modi and Shivani Modi Group. During these operations, the Income Tax Department seized various documents and papers considered incriminating. Subsequently, the Assessing Officer of the searched person recorded a satisfaction note on 21 March 2023, following which proceedings were initiated against Minoti Malhotra under Section 153C. The Assessing Officer issued the notice under Section 153C(1) on 23 March 2023 and made additions in the consequential assessment. The assessment order was passed on 30 January 2024 by the Deputy Commissioner of Income Tax, Central Circle-14, Delhi. The Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)-26, Delhi, by order dated 13 August 2025, did not grant the relief subsequently sought before the Tribunal. The assessee therefore preferred an appeal before the Delhi ITAT. At the hearing, her authorised representative raised an additional ground challenging the very jurisdiction of the Assessing Officer to initiate proceedings under Section 153C. The contention was that the statutory provision had ceased to apply to searches treated as initiated on or after 1 April 2021 and that the relevant date in the case of a person other than the searched person was the date of recording the satisfaction note. The Tribunal admitted the additional ground because it raised a pure question of law going to the root of jurisdiction and arose from facts already available on record. The assessee relied upon the Delhi High Court’s judgment in PCIT Vs Ojjus Medicare (P.) Ltd., [2024] 465 ITR 101 (Delhi), to submit that, in proceedings involving a person other than the searched person, the relevant date was the date on which the jurisdictional Assessing Officer received the seized material or recorded the satisfaction note. Applying that principle, the relevant date in the assessee’s case was 21 March 2023 rather than the original physical search date of 3 February 2021. The assessee further relied upon Harigovind Vs ACIT, [2025] 180 taxmann.com 197 (Madras High Court), and Smt. Geetanjali Bhayana Vs DCIT, [2026] 183 taxmann.com 95 (Delhi Tribunal). These decisions supported the proposition that the date applicable to the other person under the statutory deeming provision determines whether the pre-amendment Section 153C assessment mechanism remains available. Since the satisfaction note was recorded after 1 April 2021, the assessee argued that the Assessing Officer could not invoke Section 153C. The Departmental Representative principally objected that the jurisdictional ground had not been raised before the lower authorities. However, the Revenue did not cite any contrary legal authority. The Tribunal noted that the Finance Act, 2021 amended the statutory framework with effect from 1 April 2021 and that, under the amended law, action under Section 153C could not be initiated where the relevant search date fell on or after that date. It observed that assessment proceedings in such circumstances would have to be undertaken under the applicable provisions of Section 143(3) or Section 148, as appropriate, rather than under Section 153C. In reaching its conclusion, the Tribunal followed the coordinate Bench decision in Smt. Geetanjali Bhayana Vs DCIT, which had considered the same jurisdictional issue. In that case, the satisfaction note had been recorded on 3 December 2021, although the original search of the third party occurred on 23 July 2019. The coordinate Bench, relying upon Harigovind Vs ACIT, held that the date of handing over the seized material or recording the relevant satisfaction was the deemed date applicable to the other person. Since that date fell after 1 April 2021, the notice under Section 153C and the consequential assessment were quashed. The Tribunal considered this reasoning applicable to Minoti Malhotra’s case. Accordingly, the Tribunal sustained the additional legal ground and held that the assessment under Section 153C could not survive. It expressly ordered that the impugned assessment order stood quashed and allowed the assessee’s appeal. The decision illustrates the jurisdictional importance of identifying the relevant date for a person other than the searched person when applying the transitional provisions of Section 153C(3). Importantly, the Tribunal decided the matter on the legal validity of the assessment proceedings and did not independently adjudicate the substantive additions arising from the seized documents. Cases Discussed 1. PCIT Vs Ojjus Medicare (P.) Ltd. (Delhi High Court); [2024] 465 ITR 101. Relied upon for determining the relevant date under Section 153C in proceedings against a person other than the searched person. The Tribunal applied the principle to the satisfaction note recorded on 21 March 2023. 2. Harigovind Vs ACIT (Madras High Court); [2025] 180 taxmann.com 197; 28/10/2025; AY: 2012-13. Relied upon for the interpretation of Section 153C(3) and the deemed initiation date applicable to a person other than the searched person. The High Court quashed notices where the relevant date fell after 1 April 2021. 3. Smt. Geetanjali Bhayana Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi); [2026] 183 taxmann.com 95; 27/01/2026 as recorded in the principal order; AY: 2018-19. Followed. The coordinate Bench quashed Section 153C proceedings where the satisfaction date relevant to the other person was after 1 April 2021. Its operative reasoning was reproduced in paragraph 6 of the present order. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI This appeal preferred by the assessee against the order dated 13.08.2025 of Ld. Commissioner of Income Tax, (Appeal)-26, Delhi (hereinafter referred to as the First Appellate Authority or ‘the ld. FAA’ for short) in DIN & Order No : ITBA/APL/S/250/2025-26/1079588703(1)arising out of the assessment order dated 30.01.2024 u/s 153C r.w.s 143(3) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’) passed by DCIT, CC-14 AY: 2021-22. 2. Heard and perused the records. At the time of hearing ld. AR has argued on the additional ground, by which the assessment order is challenged on the basis that same is initiated on the basis of a notice u/s 153C of the Act but same was not applicable after 1st April 2021. The issue being purely legal in nature and goes to the root of jurisdiction, and arise from facts already available on record is thus admitted for hearing. 3. On hearing on merit of this additional ground we find that the notice dated 23.03.2023 was issued by the Ld. Assessing Officer under section 153C(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 in the case of the appellant on the basis of search operation u/s 132 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 carried out on 03.02.2021 and on subsequent dates at different business and residential premises of Sh. Samir Modi & Smt. Shivani Modi Group of cases. Various incriminating papers/documents were found and seized during the course of search & seizure operation. Further satisfaction note was recorded on 21.03.2023 by the AO of person searched and proceedings u/s 153C were initiated in the case of the appellant and additions were made. 4. Now as per the settled law the date of search in case of ‘other person’ should be date of recording of satisfaction note i.e. 21.03. 2023 as held in PCIT vs. Ojjus Medicare (P.) Ltd. [2024] 465 ITR 101 (Delhi). 5. The issuance of the impugned notice dated 23.03.2023 is asserted tobe grossly in controverting of section 153C(3) of the Act as the said provision become inapplicable in relation to searches initiated on or after 01.04.2021. Reliance was placed on decision in Harigovind vs. ACIT [2025] 180 taxmann.com 197 (Madras) and Smt. Geetanjali Bhayana vs. DCIT [2026] 183 taxmann.com 95 (Delhi – Trib.). 6. The only assertion of ld. DR was that this ground was not raised below but no law to the contrary was cited. However, it can be seen that the law was amended w.e.f. 1.4.2021 by Finance Act, 2021, and as per the amended law, no action u/s 153C of the Act could be taken on the searchcarried out on or after 01.04.2021.The assessment should be either u/s 143(3) of the Act or should be completed u/s 148 of the Act as per the amended provisions. Reliance for this can be placed on the decision of a bench in the case of Smt. Geetanjali Bhayana vs. Deputy Commissioner of Income Tax /2026] 183 taxmann.com 95 [Delhi-Trib.] decision dated 27.01.2026 where in both of us were on bench and we had concluded as follows: “18. The facts of the aforesaid case of hon’ble Madras High court are squarely applicable to the facts of the present case. It is also a matter of fact that the AO himself has initiated the proceedings u/s 153C of the Act for AY 2018-19 to AY 2020-21 though the date of search in the case of Shri Kuldeep Bishnoi was 23.07.2019 and as per the same, the search year was AY 2020-21. Thus, by respectfully following the judgement of hon’ble Madra High court in the case of Harigovind (supra), we set aside the notice issued u/s 153C on the assessee as the date of handing over the material i.e. 03.12.2021 is the date of initiation of search. The issuance of notice us 153C dt. 06.12.2021 is without any authority and contrary to subsection (3) of section153C of the Act and thus the consequent order passed u/s 153C of the Act is quashed. The additional grounds of appeal Nos. I to Ill are thus, allowed.” 7. Thus we are inclined to sustain the additional ground and allow the appeal. The impugned assessment order stands quashed. Order pronounced in the open court on 25.03.2026.
