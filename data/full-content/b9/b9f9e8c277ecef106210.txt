Articles 3 min read RBI DG asks primary dealers to bid responsibly in G-Sec underwriting auctions PTI Published On Nov 24, 2026 at 03:14 PM IST var news_msid = 136126042; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Mumbai, Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Deputy Governor Rohit Jain has urged primary dealers to place bids responsibly in government securities underwriting auctions, flagging instances of volatile pricing that was not in line with market conditions even when the market was orderly.Jain said underwriting commissions sought by primary dealers are generally read by the market as an indication of prevailing conditions and may impact the cost of government borrowing."On a few occasions, the pricing in the underwriting auction has been volatile and seen to be not in line with the market-relevant price, even when market conditions improved and were orderly," he said at the Primary Dealers' Association of India (PDAI) Seminar in Mumbai on Monday. Advt The central bank published the speech on Tuesday."PDs are urged to place bids in underwriting auctions responsibly, having regard to prevailing market conditions, and to make greater efforts to ensure success in both the underwriting auction and the main auction," Jain said.Primary dealers have exclusive access to underwriting auctions for dated central government securities and are paid underwriting fees based on the cut-offs in these auctions, Jain said, noting that there has been no development recently.He also highlighted challenges in the government securities market, particularly the concentration of liquidity in a handful of actively traded securities."The secondary market liquidity has generally been concentrated in the top 5 traded securities having a share of about 72 per cent during the last 12 months," he said.Trading in short-tenor securities of up to five years accounted for only around 7 per cent of the market in the first half of the current financial year, while securities with maturities of more than 15 years also accounted for around 7 per cent.Jain said improving the distribution of liquidity across the yield curve remains a challenge and called on primary dealers to increase market-making activities, particularly by providing two-way quotes in off-the-run securities. Advt He also flagged weak secondary market activity in state government securities, which accounted for only about 5 per cent of total secondary market turnover in the first half of FY27, despite an increase in state government borrowings over the years.On the demand side, Jain said the government securities market remained largely domestic and institution-driven, with banks, insurers, provident funds and pension funds being the principal holders.Commercial banks accounted for 35 per cent of government securities holdings as of March 2026, followed by insurers and provident funds.However, demand from banks, insurers and pension funds for long-term sovereign debt has marginally declined, with insurance and pension funds shifting towards more equity exposure, he said. Advt "This has structurally reduced such demand for government securities," Jain added.He said asset managers and other investors were increasingly becoming selective on duration, liquidity and mark-to-market risk, making it more challenging to issue longer-dated securities at attractive yields in an uncertain interest-rate environment.Jain also asked standalone primary dealers to accelerate preparations for entering the foreign exchange market, noting that the initial uptake of the expanded permissions had been slow.He said standalone primary dealers have been permitted to offer all foreign exchange market-making facilities currently allowed to Category-I Authorised Dealers, subject to prudential guidelines. Advt The measure is aimed at broadening the spectrum of market-makers in the forex market and improving their ability to support primary issuance and secondary market activities in government securities.Jain said rising borrowing requirements of both the Centre and states made the stability of the government securities market increasingly important."Even a small increase in yields can have a meaningful impact on the government's interest burden," he said.He called for credible fiscal and debt-management policies, a transparent and predictable auction calendar, deeper secondary-market liquidity and broader participation from long-term institutional investors.
