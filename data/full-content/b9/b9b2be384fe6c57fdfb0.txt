Star Union Dai-ichi's retail weighted premiums rose 61.4% year-on-year, followed by Bandhan Life at 50.5% and Birla Sun Life at 32.2%.The sharp gains came as the private life insurance sector recovered from its July slowdown, with retail weighted premiums rising 14.9% year-on-year in August compared with 8% in July. Industry retail weighted premiums increased 14.3% to Rs 10,900 crore, while the private sector accounted for 72.3% of the market, according to JM Financial. Advt Among other private insurers, Kotak Life's retail weighted premiums grew 20.8%, Tata AIA's rose 18.8% and Reliance Life's increased 11.6%. The growth was not uniform, however, with PNB MetLife declining 22.3%, IndiaFirst Life falling 22.1% and Edelweiss Tokio declining 3.7% year-on-year.Larger playersThe larger private insurers continued to contribute significantly to the industry's expansion. SBI Life's retail weighted premiums rose 21.8% to Rs 1,861 crore in August, while HDFC Life grew 17.4% to Rs 1,293 crore. IPRU Life recorded 11.2% growth and Axis Max Life grew 10.2%. SBI Life remained the largest private insurer in the segment, with a 23.6% market share, followed by HDFC Life at 16.4%.The improvement also extended to annualised premium equivalent (APE), with total industry APE rising 18.8% year-on-year in August. Private-sector APE grew 15.4%, while LIC recorded 25.5% growth. Among private insurers, Birla Sun Life posted particularly strong APE growth of 47.4%, while Star Union Dai-ichi grew 40.8%.The recovery in premiums came alongside an increase in average ticket sizes. Industry average ticket size rose 11.1% year-on-year to Rs 51,262 in August. SBI Life's average ticket size increased 11.8% to Rs 98,572, while HDFC Life's declined 1.8% to Rs 1.11 lakh and IPRU Life's fell 4% to Rs 1.05 lakh. Advt Despite the broad-based improvement, the report said equity-market uncertainty continued to weigh on ULIP sales, while traditional products faced competition from elevated deposit yields. It expects the private sector's growth to move towards a more sustainable 10–15% trajectory, with structural demand for protection and guaranteed savings remaining intact.
