Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Yeruva Vinod Kumar Reddy Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) Agricultural Land Classification U/S 2(14)(iii) Requires Verification of Municipal Limits, Population and Distance: ITAT Hyderabad Hyderabad ITAT admits fresh evidence on the status of Dundigal land Can agricultural land be treated as a capital asset based on the population of an urban area without verifying where the land lay in relation to the relevant municipal limits? In Yeruva Vinod Kumar Reddy Vs ITO, ITA No. 2452/HYD/2025, decided on 20 November 2026 for AY 2018–19, the Hyderabad ITAT held that the issue required verification of the location of the land, the population of the relevant municipality and the distance from its limits. The Tribunal admitted additional evidence under Rule 29 and remitted the matter to the Assessing Officer for fresh adjudication. Why the Assessing Officer taxed the entire ₹61 lakh The assessee sold 1.21 acres of inherited agricultural land at Dundigal, Telangana, for ₹61 lakh during FY 2017–18. Based on information uploaded by the Sub-Registrar in Form No. 61 and the absence of a return for the year, the Assessing Officer initiated reassessment proceedings and issued a Section 148 notice dated 22 April 2022. The assessee subsequently stated that he had sold agricultural land, but failed to furnish adequate documents supporting his claim that the property was outside the definition of a capital asset. The Assessing Officer referred to online data showing a 2011 Census population of 40,817 for the area he treated as relevant. He concluded that the property was urban agricultural land under Section 2(14)(iii) and also held that Section 10(37) did not apply. Taking note that the land was inherited, he treated the entire ₹61 lakh sale consideration as long-term capital gain. The assessee’s first appeal was unsuccessful. The assessee relied on the municipal position at the time of sale Before the Tribunal, the assessee argued that Dundigal was a Gram Panchayat when the land was sold in January 2018. His representative cited Schedule 10 of the Telangana Gazette dated 30 March 2018 to support the claim that Dundigal became a municipality only later. On that basis, he argued that the municipal status existing at the time of assessment could not determine the character of the land at the time of transfer. The assessee also produced a 2011 Census extract for Dundigal village and online map material. The documents indicated a distance of 22 kilometres from Dundigal to Quthbullapur and approximately 10 to 12 kilometres from Dundigal to the municipal limits of Kukatpally, which the assessee identified as the nearest municipal area. These figures supported his request for reconsideration. The statutory inquiry, however, required verification of the distance from the specific parcel sold to the applicable municipal boundary. Why the Tribunal admitted additional evidence under Rule 29 The Revenue opposed admission on the ground that the assessee could have furnished the documents before the lower authorities. The assessee explained that he was an agriculturist principally dependent on agricultural income and was unfamiliar with the proceedings. The Tribunal took a liberal view because the documents had a direct bearing on whether the land was a capital asset. Admission under Rule 29 of the Income-tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963 brought the new material onto the record for examination. Since the lower authorities had never examined it, the Tribunal directed the Assessing Officer to verify the population, location and distance, apply the relevant provisions and give the assessee a reasonable opportunity of being heard. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes, leaving the classification of the land and the resulting tax liability open. The order also records the assessee’s challenge to the notice issued by the jurisdictional Assessing Officer, but contains no separate ruling on that ground. Author’s comments The practical issue under Section 2(14)(iii) is to identify the relevant municipality and its limits as they stood on the date of transfer. If the land lay outside those limits, the inquiry turns to the aerial distance from the boundary and the population of the relevant municipality. The statutory distance bands are 2, 6 or 8 kilometres, depending on that population. A map showing the distance between two named localities may help locate the property, but the measurement must ultimately connect the land itself to the relevant municipal boundary. There is also a separate computation issue if the Assessing Officer ultimately finds that the land was a capital asset. Since the property was inherited, Section 49(1) ordinarily looks to the cost to the previous owner for computing capital gains. The Tribunal left that question open. The finding on municipal limits and aerial distance will therefore determine whether a capital gains computation is required at all. If it is, the ownership and acquisition records become relevant to determining the taxable gain. Cases Discussed: Kankanala Ravindra Reddy Vs ITO (Telangana High Court); W.P. FULL TEXT OF THE ORIGINAL JUDGMENT/ORDER The present appeal filed by the assessee is directed against the order passed by the CIT(A), NFAC, dated 03.10.2025, which, in turn, arises from the order passed by the Assessing Officer (for short, ‘AO’) under section 147 r.w.s. 144 and 144B of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (for short, ‘the Act’), dated 31.10.2023, for A.Y. 2018-19. The assessee has assailed the impugned order on the following grounds of appeal raised before us. “1) On the facts and in the circumstances of the case the order passed by the learned CIT(A), NFAC, Delhi u/s. 250 of the Act for the AY 2018-19 is erroneous and bad in Law. 2) On the facts and in the circumstances of the case and on legal and technical grounds, the order passed u/s. 148A(d) of the Act by the JAO for the A.Y. 2018-19 on 22.04.2022 and the notice u/s 148 of the Act dated 22.04.2022 issued by the JAO instead of the Faceless A.O are contrary to the e-assessment of Income Escaping Assessment Scheme, 2022, read with CBDT notification no. 18/2022/F. No. 370142/16/2022-TPL dated 29-03-2022. Thus, all proceedings emanating from such invalid notice, including the reassessment proceedings and the assessment order passed u/s 147 r.w.s 144 r.w.s 144B of the Act are bad in law, null &amp; void and are liable to be quashed. 3) On the facts and circumstances of the case, it is submitted that the order passed u/s 148A(d) of the Act dated 22.04.2022 and the notice u/s 148 of the Act dated 22.04.2022 are invalid and illegal as the same are contrary to the amended provision of Section 151A read with Section 144B of the IT Act. As per the said section, the said notice(s)/orders must have been issued by the Faceless Assessing Officer (FAO) and not by the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer (JAO), as there is no concurrent jurisdiction of FAO and JAO to issue a notice/order under the Income Tax Act. As the impugned notice(s) ought/shall have been issued by the FAO, the issuance of the impugned notice(s) by the JAO are void &amp; illegal and therefore, liable to be quashed. 4) On the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the notice u/s. 148 of the Act dated 22.04.2022 issued by the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer (JAO) for the AY 2018-19 is invalid in law in view of the decision of the honourable jurisdictional High Court of Telangana in the case of (i) Kankanala Ravindra Reddy vide WP 25903/2022 reported in reported in (2023) 22 ITR-OL 728 (Telangana) &amp; (2023) 156 taxmann.com 178 (Telangana) (14-09-2023) and (ii) Kotha Kanthaiah in W.P. no: 344 of 2025 vide order dated 24.02.2025, and also (iii) the Honourable Bombay High Court in the case of Hexaware Technologies Ltd. v. ACIT (2024) 464 ITR 430 (Bombay)(03-05-2024), wherein it has been held that as per the amended provisions, w.e.f. 1.4.2021 notices u/s 148 of the Act can be only given by the Officers of the faceless assessment unit, and as such the notice u/s 148 of the Act issued by the JAO, being the ITO, Ward -11(1), Hyderabad is without jurisdiction. Hence, the additions need to be deleted. 5) On facts and circumstances of the case, the learned CIT (A), NFAC, Delhi erred in upholding the act of the learned A.O who ought to have considered the fact that the sold property was a rural agriculture land and the sale consideration received is completely exempted from tax. 6) On facts and circumstances of the case, the learned CIT (A), NFAC, Delhi erred in upholding the act of the learned A.O who ought to have appreciated the fact that the place Dundigal was not a municipality as on the date of sale but was declared as municipality at a later point of time. 7) On facts and circumstances of the case, the learned CIT (A), NFAC, Delhi erred in upholding the act of the learned A.O who misinterpreted the municipality status of Dundigal area as on the date of assessment proceedings instead of the municipality status as on the date of sale to determine whether the land is a Rural agricultural land OR Urban Agricultural Land. 8) On the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the learned CIT (A), NFAC, Delhi erred in upholding the act of the learned A.O who raised the erroneous demand of Rs. 30,32,378/- (being tax of Rs. 13,25,610/-, interest of Rs. 8,08,616/- u/s. 234A, interest of Rs. 8,88,152/- u/s. 234B and Fees of 10,000/- u/s 234F) for the AY 2018-19 and needs to be deleted. 9) On the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the Hon. Income Tax Appellate Tribunal is requested to admit additional grounds/evidence, if any, as per the decision of the honourable Supreme Court in the case of NTPC vs. CIT (229 ITR 383) and consider the same in the interest of justice. 10) The appellant seeks leave to raise any other further/additional grounds OR modify the grounds during the course of the appellate proceedings before your honourable selves, being the First Appellate Authority.” 2. Succinctly stated, the AO, based on information uploaded by the office of the Sub-Registrar in Form No. 61, observed that the assessee during the year under consideration had sold an immovable property for a consideration of Rs. 61 lakh but had not filed his return of income for the year under consideration. Accordingly, the AO initiated proceedings under section 147 of the Act. The AO issued a notice under section 148A(b) of the Act to the assessee, wherein he was called upon to explain as to why a notice under section 148 of the Act should not be issued on the basis of the aforesaid information. Thereafter, the AO passed an order under section 148A(d) of the Act dated 20.04.2022. A notice under section 148 of the Act dated 22.04.2022 was issued to the assessee. However, the assessee failed to furnish his return of income in response to the aforesaid notice. 3. During the course of the assessment proceedings, the AO issued notices under section 142(1) of the Act on various dates to the assessee. However, the assessee failed to provide complete information and furnish the requisite explanation. As is discernible from the assessment order, the assessee, vide his reply dated 05.07.2023, had merely submitted before the AO that he had sold agricultural land admeasuring 1.21 acres during the financial year 2017-18. Thereafter, the AO, vide notice under section 142(1) of the Act dated 04.08.2023, called upon the assessee to substantiate on the basis of supporting documentary evidence that the agricultural land sold by him did not fall within the meaning of a capital asset. The assessee was specifically called upon to substantiate his claim on the basis of supporting documents in the backdrop of the provisions of section 2(14)(iii) and section 10(37) of the Act, which would reveal that the agricultural land did not come within the purview of a capital asset and that the capital gain arising therefrom was not exigible to tax under the provisions of the Act. However, the assessee failed to substantiate his aforesaid claim. 4. The AO, taking cognizance of the fact that the assessee had sold agricultural land admeasuring 1.21 acres situated at Dundigal village, Gram Panchayat, Quthbullapur Mandal, Medchal-Malkajgiri District, Telangana, to Shri Mallikarjun (PAN: AJAPK851) for a consideration of Rs. 61 lakh, observed that the assessee had failed to offer the capital gain arising from the aforesaid sale transaction for tax. It was further observed by the AO that the data available on the public portal revealed that Dundigal village was situated in the Medchal-Malkajgiri District of Telangana State. The AO further observed that the relevant urban area had a population of 40,817 as per the 2011 Census. Accordingly, the AO, on the basis of the aforesaid facts, was firmly convinced that the agricultural land sold by the assessee during the year under consideration was situated in an urban area with a population exceeding ten thousand. Based on the aforesaid observations, the AO concluded that the subject land was in the nature of urban agricultural land and would not stand excluded from the definition of a capital asset under section 2(14)(iii) of the Act. The AO further held that the capital gain arising from the sale of the said land was not exempt under section 10(37) of the Act. Accordingly, the AO, taking cognizance of the fact that the subject land was inherited by the assessee, treated the entire sale consideration of Rs. 61 lakh as long-term capital gain (LTCG) in the assessee’s hands. 5. Aggrieved, the assessee carried the matter in appeal before the CIT(A) but without success. 6. The assessee, being aggrieved with the order of the CIT(A), has carried the matter in appeal before us. 7. We have heard the learned authorized representatives of both parties and perused the orders of the authorities below as well as considered the material available on record. 8. Shri Y.V. Bhanu Narayan Rao, CA, learned authorized representative (for short, ‘AR’) for the assessee, at the threshold of hearing of the appeal submitted that the assessee has moved an application under Rule 29 of the Income-tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963, seeking admission of certain documentary evidence as additional evidence, which would substantiate that the subject property sold by the assessee during the year under consideration was rural agricultural land. Elaborating on his contention, the learned AR submitted that the assessee was an agriculturist whose principal source of income was agriculture and that he was not conversant with the proceedings before the lower authorities. It was submitted that, for the aforesaid reasons, the assessee had failed to place on record the relevant documentary evidence during the proceedings before the lower authorities. 9. The Ld. AR submitted that the assessee was furnishing documents which would establish that Dundigal village, where the subject land was situated, was outside the prescribed municipal limits. It was submitted that the said documents would reveal that the population of Dundigal village, as per the Census of 2011, and its distance from the nearest municipality were material for determining whether the subject land fell within the meaning of a capital asset under section 2(14)(iii) of the Act. The Ld. AR submitted that the distance of the said village from the nearest municipality, viz. Quthbullapur/Kukatpally municipal limits, was substantially more than eight kilometers. It was submitted that the aforesaid documents, produced as additional evidence, have a strong bearing on the adjudication of the present appeal and, therefore, in all fairness and in the interest of justice, deserved to be admitted. 10. Per contra, Shri K Prasad, Learned Senior Departmental Representative (for short, ‘Sr. DR’) objected to the admission of the additional evidence filed by the assessee under Rule 29 of the Income-tax (Appellate Tribunal) Rules, 1963. Elaborating on his contention, the Ld. Sr. DR submitted that as the assessee had adopted a casual approach and failed to furnish the aforesaid documents in the course of the proceedings before either of the lower authorities, therefore, he could not now be permitted to furnish the same in the course of the present appellate proceedings. 11. We have given thoughtful consideration to the aforesaid submissions of the learned authorized representatives of both parties. We are of the considered view that as the documents furnished by the assessee by way of additional evidence will have a strong bearing on the adjudication of the present appeal, i.e., as to whether or not the agricultural land sold by the assessee during the year under consideration was a capital asset within the meaning of section 2(14)(iii) of the Act, therefore, taking a liberal view and considering the fact that the assessee is an agriculturist, the said documents, in all fairness and in the interest of justice, are admitted. 12. We find that the assessee has filed before us a printout of the Census of 2011 showing the population of Dundigal village. Apart from that, the assessee has placed on record a printout of Google Maps showing the distance between Dundigal village and the nearest municipality, viz. Quthbullapur, as 22 kilometers. The assessee has also placed on record a printout from Google which reveals that the distance between Dundigal village and the municipal limits of Kukatpally, i.e., which is stated to be the nearest local municipal area, was approximately 10 to 12 kilometers. The assessee, on the basis of the aforesaid documents, has tried to impress upon us that the subject land sold by him during the year under consideration was rural agricultural land which did not fall within the meaning of a capital asset under section 2(14)(iii) of the Act. 13. Elaborating on his aforesaid contention, the learned AR submitted that in January 2018, before Dundigal was upgraded to a municipality, the nearest municipal area to Dundigal village was Kukatpally, which formed part of the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC). It was submitted that the subject land was situated approximately 10 to 12 kilometers away from the municipal limits of Kukatpally. The Ld. AR also drew our attention to Schedule 10 of the Telangana Gazette, Part IV-B (Extraordinary), published on 30.03.2018, which, as claimed by him, revealed that Dundigal was upgraded to a municipality with effect from 30.03.2018. Accordingly, it was submitted that during the relevant period, the subject agricultural land was situated beyond the prescribed distance from the nearest municipality and, therefore, did not fall within the meaning of a capital asset under section 2(14)(iii) of the Act. 14. We have considered the aforesaid submissions of the learned AR and perused the documents filed by the assessee as additional evidence. We find that the determination of the nature of the subject agricultural land, i.e., whether the same falls within the meaning of a capital asset under section 2(14)(iii) of the Act, would require verification of the population of the relevant municipality, the location of the subject land, its distance from the prescribed municipal limits and the applicability of the relevant statutory provisions. We are of the considered view that as the aforesaid documents filed by the assessee by way of additional evidence were not available before the lower authorities, therefore, in all fairness and in the interest of justice, the matter requires to be restored to the file of the AO with a direction to re-adjudicate the issue after considering the aforesaid documents filed by the assessee before us as additional evidence. 15. Before parting, we may herein observe that as we have restored the matter to the file of the AO for fresh adjudication, therefore, we refrain from commenting on the merits of the case, which the AO shall remain at liberty to adjudicate in the course of the set-aside proceedings. Needless to say, the AO shall, in the course of the set-aside proceedings, afford the assessee a reasonable opportunity of being heard. 16. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes in terms of our aforesaid observations. Order pronounced in the open court on 20th November, 2026.
