Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Surinder Kumar Vs ITO (ITAT Amritsar) The Amritsar Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), in Surinder Kumar Vs ITO, held that compensation received on compulsory acquisition of land under the National Highways Act, 1956 was exempt from income tax under Section 96 of the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013 (RFCTLARR Act). The dispute concerned Assessment Year 2016-17, in which the Assessing Officer had assessed income of ₹2,05,93,010 despite the assessee’s revised return declaring ₹26,98,530. The assessee had received compensation of ₹1,95,15,868 and solatium of ₹58,54,760, aggregating to ₹2,53,70,628. The Assessing Officer denied exemption because the acquisition was undertaken under the National Highways Act rather than directly under the RFCTLARR Act. The Tribunal examined the chronology of acquisition, noting that the notification was issued on 4 February 2014, the award was announced on 7 November 2014, and compensation was actually received on 18 May 2015. It considered CBDT Circular No. 36/2016 dated 25 October 2016 and the applicability of Section 96 of the RFCTLARR Act to compensation arising from acquisitions under other enactments. The Tribunal relied on the Supreme Court’s decision in Union of India Vs Tarsem Singh, the Punjab and Haryana High Court’s ruling in NHAI Vs Modan Singh, and the coordinate Bench decision in Ranjit Singh Vs ITO. It also referred to Satish Kumar Vs ITO and Gopa Ram Vs Union of India. The Tribunal found that the assessee’s circumstances were materially similar to those considered in Ranjit Singh, particularly because the compensation had remained unpaid on 31 December 2014. Accordingly, the Tribunal held that the compensation received by the assessee on compulsory acquisition of land was exempt from income tax by virtue of Section 96 of the RFCTLARR Act, 2013, read with CBDT Circular No. 36/2016. It deleted the addition of ₹2,05,93,010 and allowed the assessee’s appeal. However, the separate ground concerning additional compensation of ₹41,142 was dismissed as not pressed, following the assessee’s concession in view of the Punjab and Haryana High Court decisions in Shivaji Roy and Manjit Singh. The order was pronounced on 9 October 2024 in ITA No. 29/Asr/2024. Cases Discussed Ranjit Singh v. ITO and Ranjit Poultry Farm v.
