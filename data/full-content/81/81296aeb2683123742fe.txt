Articles 3 min read SBI ordered to pay Rs 60,000 after delayed cheque clearance in Chhattisgarh; consumer panel rejects bank's unproven server-down defence, awards compensation and costs with interest The District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission in Rajnandgaon found State Bank of India guilty of negligence. Khileshwar Sinha deposited a cheque that was not sent for clearing on time. The bank's claim of a technical fault was not substantiated with evidence, leading to the cheque's dishonour. The Commission mandated the bank to pay the cheque amount, compensation, and legal costs to Sinha. Partha Behera TNN Published On Nov 28, 2026 at 07:55 AM IST var news_msid = 136236765; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook RAIPUR: The District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Rajnandgaon, has held the State Bank of India's local branch guilty of deficiency in service for failing to send a customer's cheque for clearing on time, leading to its dishonour as "out-dated".The bank has been directed to pay the cheque amount of Rs 50,000 along with compensation and costs. The Commission observed that the bank failed to prove the outage. The Commission noted that the bank had mentioned the server failure in its written reply but produced no documentary proof.With banking hours running from 10 am to 5 pm, the burden was on SBI to show that the server was down throughout working hours, and it did not discharge it. Advt In an order on Thursday, the bench of President Prashant Kundu and Member Anand Varghese partly allowed the complaint filed by Khileshwar Sinha (48), a resident of Shanti Nagar, Ward 4, Rajnandgaon district, against the branch manager of SBI's Digvijay Stadium Campus branch on May 14, 2026.Sinha, an SBI account holder, received a Rs 50,000 cheque from Ravi Vaishnav, drawn on Jana Small Finance Bank Ltd's Durg branch and dated Nov 21, 2025. He deposited it at his SBI branch on Feb 18, 2026, within the validity period, and said he had also alerted a bank employee.According to the complaint, the bank did not send the cheque for clearing on Feb 19, a working day, and sent it only on Feb 20, two days later. It was returned on Feb 21 with a memo stamped "instrument out-dated" (stale). Sinha called this "complete negligence" and sought the cheque amount, Rs 10,000 for mental agony and litigation costs, and other relief under Section 35 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.SBI argued that a technical fault, network problem and system outage on Feb 19 prevented the cheque from being sent for clearing. It said the cheque was sent on Feb 20 and returned as stale because 90 days had passed since its issuance, and that the bank was not negligent.It also claimed it had asked Sinha to get a fresh date put on the cheque and redeposit it, but he instead approached the Commission. It sought dismissal of the complaint. The bank's only evidence was the affidavit of its representative, Jadbendra Kapa.The complainant deposited the cheque on the 88th day after its issuance, well within the permitted period, the Commission noted. Advt By not sending the cheque for clearing on time, the bank allowed it to lapse. The bench held that failure to present a cheque on time, leading to its expiry, was clear negligence and a deficiency in service.The order relied on the Supreme Court's ruling in Canara Bank vs Kavita Chaudhary (2026 INSC 363, dated April 15, 2026), as cited in the order.It held that a bank's failure to present or collect a cheque in time, resulting in its expiry, amounts to serious negligence and deficiency in service. The Court also said banks cannot shelter behind internal technical reasons or routine excuses, and are answerable if a customer suffers legal or financial loss. Advt Holding that the customer was entitled to the cheque amount, the Commission recognised that non-realisation of the money would naturally cause mental distress. It awarded Rs 10,000 against the Rs 10,000 claimed for mental agony and costs, split equally.The bank must pay Rs 50,000, the cheque amount, Rs 5,000 as compensation for mental agony, and Rs 5,000 as litigation costs. The Commission also directed the bank to pay interest at 6% per annum from Nov 26, 2026, until payment.If payment is not made within 45 days, the bank will have to pay interest at 9% per annum from the date of the order until payment. Advt The ruling underlines that banks handling cheques for collection carry a duty of timely action, and that a "technical glitch" defence needs documentary backing to stand before a consumer forum.
