Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement Vs Upendra Rai & Shri Narendra Rai (Delhi High Court) Summary: The Delhi High Court, in Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement Vs Shri Upendra Rai and Shri Narendra Rai, allowed two connected appeals filed by the Enforcement Directorate challenging the order dated 16 April 2026 passed by the Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA, Delhi. The appeals, numbered MISC. APPEAL (PMLA) 116/2026 and 118/2026, arose from the Tribunal’s decisions in FPA-PMLA-2105/DLI/2024 concerning Upendra Rai and FPA-PMLA-2104/DLI/2024 concerning Narendra Rai. The Division Bench comprising Justice Navin Chawla and Justice Ravinder Dudeja passed a common order on 25 August 2026. The Court took both appeals on board with the consent of the parties and cancelled the hearing previously scheduled for 6 November 2026. At the outset, the High Court considered applications seeking condonation of a delay of 57 days in filing the respective appeals. Counsel appearing for the respondents did not oppose the applications. Accordingly, the Court condoned the delay and disposed of both applications. The Court thereafter considered the substantive appeals challenging the Tribunal’s order dated 16 April 2026. During the hearing, counsel representing Upendra Rai and Narendra Rai stated, without prejudice to their rights and contentions, that the respondents had no objection to the impugned Tribunal order being set aside and the matters being remanded for fresh adjudication. Taking note of this concession, the High Court allowed both appeals and set aside the order dated 16 April 2026. The Court expressly clarified that it had not expressed any opinion on the merits of either party’s claims. All legal and factual contentions were left open for reconsideration by the Appellate Tribunal, which was directed to decide the appeals afresh without being influenced by the High Court’s order. The High Court also requested the Appellate Tribunal to endeavour to dispose of the remanded appeals within two months from their first listing. The decision is therefore a procedural remand based on the respondents’ consent, rather than a determination of the validity of any attachment or the substantive allegations under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002. The Tribunal must independently reconsider the matters and adjudicate the parties’ competing claims. The order is also relevant to the connected PMLA litigation subsequently considered in Deputy Director Vs Piramal Capital and Housing Finance Ltd. (Delhi High Court), in which the present remand order was specifically referred to. Cases Discussed: No independent judicial precedents are cited or discussed in the original three-page order. The references to Upendra Rai v. Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement, Delhi (FPA-PMLA-2105/DLI/2024) and Narendra Rai v. Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement, Delhi (FPA-PMLA-2104/DLI/2024) identify the underlying appeals before the SAFEMA Appellate Tribunal and do not constitute separate judicial precedents relied upon by the High Court. These applications have been filed seeking condonation of delay of 57 days in filing the respective appeals. 3. The learned counsel for the respondents does not oppose the prayer for condonation of delay. 4. The delay is, accordingly, condoned. MISC. APPEAL(PMLA) 116/2026 & CM APPL. 54932/2026 MISC. APPEAL(PMLA) 118/2026 & CM APPL. 55087/2026 5. These appeals have been filed by the appellant(s), challenging the Order dated 16.04.2026 passed by the learned Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA at Delhi in the batch of appeals, including FPA-PMLA-2105/DLI/2024, titled Upendra Rai v. Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement, Delhi and FPA-PMLA-2104/ DLI/2024, titled Narendra Rai v. Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement, Delhi. 6. The learned counsels for the respondents, without prejudice to the rights and contentions, submit that they have no objection if the Impugned Order is set aside and the appeals are remanded before the learned Appellate Tribunal for a fresh adjudication. 7. Keeping in view the above concession, these appeals are allowed and the Impugned Order dated 16.04.2026 is set aside. The pending applications are disposed of. 8. We, however, make it clear that we have not expressed any opinion on the merits of the claims of the parties and the appeals shall be decided by the learned Appellate Tribunal remaining uninfluenced by the present order. All the contentions of the parties shall remain open to be adjudicated before the learned Appellate Tribunal afresh. 9. We request the learned Appellate Tribunal to try and dispose them of within a period of two months from their first listing.
