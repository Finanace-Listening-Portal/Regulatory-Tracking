ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Nov 6, 2026 at 07:57 AM IST var news_msid = 135590665; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Unusual incidents, including cyberattacks and system outages, must be reported to RBI within six hours and to CERT-In.Boards must strengthen cyber risk oversight, review security policies annually and approve a Cyber Crisis Management Plan.PSOs must conduct annual security testing, round-the-clock threat monitoring and strengthen fraud detection and disaster recovery.New safeguards cover mobile number changes, card transaction limits, customer fraud reporting and annual system audits.The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has tightened cybersecurity requirements for non-bank payment system operators (PSOs), mandating them to report unusual incidents, including cyberattacks, critical system outages, internal fraud and settlement delays, within six hours of detection. Cybersecurity incidents must also be reported to the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In). The new directions require payment operators to strengthen board-level oversight, conduct annual security testing and put in place mechanisms to monitor and respond to cyber threats round the clock.The central bank on Thursday issued the Reserve Bank of India (Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank PSOs) Master Directions, 2026, consolidating earlier instructions on cybersecurity controls for payment operators, system audits and third-party ATM switch application service providers. The consolidated directions incorporate guidelines issued in December 2019 and January 2020. Advt The directions will come into effect from the date they are placed on the RBI's website for large and medium non-bank PSOs. Small non-bank PSOs will have until April 1, 2028, to comply.Under the new framework, the board of each payment operator will be responsible for overseeing information security risks, including cyber risks and resilience. Boards may delegate primary oversight to a sub-committee headed by a member with expertise in information or cybersecurity. The sub-committee must meet at least once every quarter.PSOs will also have to formulate a board-approved information security policy, review it annually and prepare a separate Cyber Crisis Management Plan to detect, contain, respond to and recover from cyber threats and attacks. A senior executive, such as the chief information security officer, will be accountable for implementing the policy and continuously assessing the organisation's information security posture.Stricter security and audit requirementsThe RBI has prescribed a range of baseline security measures covering access management, network security, application development, data protection, vendor management, cloud security and incident response.Payment operators will have to conduct security testing of all applications at least annually, including vulnerability assessments, penetration testing and source-code reviews. They must obtain the source code of critical applications procured from third-party vendors. Where this is not possible, an escrow arrangement will be required to ensure continuity of services. Advt The directions also require multi-factor authentication for privileged accounts and appropriate access controls based on the principle of least privilege. PSOs must establish a Security Operations Centre for centralised monitoring of network and system logs, supported by automated tools to detect suspicious activity and coordinate responses.For vendors involved in critical processes and activities, payment operators will have to obtain independent auditor-certified assurance of their cyber resilience capabilities. They must also ensure that vendors comply with applicable security requirements through contractual arrangements. Advt The RBI has prescribed specific business continuity and disaster recovery requirements. PSOs must maintain disaster recovery facilities in a different seismic zone from their primary data centres and conduct disaster recovery drills at least once every six months. They must also strive to achieve near-zero Recovery Point Objective, which measures the maximum acceptable loss of data following a disruption.Payment operators will have to maintain a real-time or near real-time fraud monitoring system and a manned facility operating round the clock throughout the year to address unauthorised or fraudulent transactions reported by customers and respond to law enforcement agencies. Audit logs must be preserved for at least five years. Advt Additional safeguards for digital paymentsThe directions also prescribe additional security measures for mobile, card and prepaid payment instrument transactions. Mobile payment applications must be bound to the customer's device and SIM, with the process repeated if the application remains unused beyond a specified period.A minimum cooling period of 12 hours must be observed after a change in the registered mobile number or email ID linked to a payment instrument before allowing online transactions.Card networks will have to facilitate transaction limits at the card, bank identification number and issuer levels, with the limits mandatorily implemented at the network switch. They must also operate a round-the-clock alert mechanism to notify card issuers of suspicious incidents.The RBI has also directed payment operators to facilitate mechanisms through which customers can identify or flag fraudulent transactions on their mobile applications or websites, enabling immediate notification to the issuer of the payment instrument.The directions also require payment operators to ensure that transaction alerts identify the merchant rather than the payment gateway or aggregator. Fund transfer notifications must mention the beneficiary's name and the amount debited. Confidential information such as bank account and card numbers must be masked to the extent possible.Annual system auditsAuthorised payment operators will have to submit annual system audit reports within two months of the close of their financial year. The audits must be conducted by CERT-In-empanelled auditors, certified information systems auditors or holders of the ICAI's Diploma in Information System Audit qualification.Audit reports must cover information security governance, access controls, network and data security, business continuity, vendor risk management, incident response and security testing, among other areas. Any open audit observations will require time-bound corrective action, with a maximum period of three months, and must be reported to the RBI. Observations will be closed only after the auditor accepts the corrective action.The directions also consolidate cybersecurity requirements for third-party ATM switch application service providers used by white-label ATM operators. These service providers will have to maintain continuous cyber surveillance, establish cyber security operations centres and provide for incident response, vulnerability assessments and disaster recovery arrangements.
