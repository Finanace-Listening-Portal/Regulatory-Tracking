Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Nov 5, 2026 at 01:41 PM IST var news_msid = 135565014; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook A buy-on-dips strategy appears favourable heading into the festive week, with strict stop-loss placed below the previous week's low of 138,000.As Dhanteras approaches, India's gold market is witnessing a sharp correction after an unprecedented rally, even as the precious metal continues to deliver strong long-term returns. Gold prices have fallen significantly from their January 2026 record highs, with international prices slipping below $4,000 an ounce. However, the metal remains higher than its levels during last year's festive season, keeping its long-term investment appeal intact.According to data from the India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA), gold was priced at Rs 1,30,874 per 10 grams on Dhanteras 2025, compared with around Rs 1,38,000 currently, marking an increase of nearly 10% over the year. Advt The correction comes after gold touched record levels in January 2026. While international prices have declined more than 30% from their peak, domestic prices have also retreated from the record high of around Rs 1.80 lakh per 10 grams on the MCX. The sharp reversal has raised questions about whether the correction has further room to run or whether the current levels could offer an opportunity for festive buyers.Gold delivers 16.5% annualised returns over a decadeDespite the recent volatility, gold has emerged as one of the stronger-performing asset classes over the past decade.According to an analysis by Augmont, domestic gold prices on the MCX have increased nearly 365% between Dhanteras 2016 and November 3, 2026, delivering an annualised return of 16.58%.Gold prices on the MCX rose from Rs 29,989 per 10 grams on Dhanteras 2016 to Rs 1,39,420 on November 3, 2026. An investment of Rs 1 lakh in gold during the period would have grown to approximately Rs 4.65 lakh.The performance has also outpaced international gold prices. Comex gold gained around 212% during the decade, translating into an annualised return of 12.03%.However, the rally has not been linear. Gold prices registered a correction of 5.6% in 2021, while the sharpest gains came in the last two years, with prices rising 30.6% in 2024 and 61.5% in 2025, according to the analysis.Demand outlook remains mixed Advt While the long-term performance has remained strong, elevated prices have started weighing on India's physical gold demand.According to the World Gold Council's latest Gold Demand Trends: India Focus report for the third quarter of 2026, India's gold demand rose 24% sequentially to 162 tonnes. However, it remained 29% lower year-on-year, reflecting affordability concerns amid elevated domestic prices.Jewellery demand increased 33% sequentially to 100 tonnes during the quarter but was still 20% lower year-on-year. Bar and coin demand rose 9% sequentially to 55 tonnes, although it declined 40% compared with the year-ago period. Advt Investment demand, meanwhile, has shown greater resilience. Gold ETF demand increased to 5.3 tonnes in the third quarter from 3.6 tonnes in the preceding quarter. Holdings stood at around 124 tonnes, while ETF demand during the first nine months of 2026 rose 47% year-on-year to 28.8 tonnes.The World Gold Council said seasonal buying is expected to support overall demand in the fourth quarter, although jewellery volumes could remain constrained by elevated prices and investment demand may remain sensitive to price movements.Outlook: Correction or buying opportunity?The near-term outlook for gold remains uncertain, with inflation concerns, crude oil prices, the US dollar and expectations around Federal Reserve interest rates likely to influence the metal's trajectory. Advt Bank of America expects gold to remain under pressure in the near term, with its demand tracker indicating a 16.6% year-on-year decline in purchases by key buyers. However, the bank sees scope for a recovery in 2027, with prices projected at $4,750 an ounce in the first quarter and $5,000 in the second and third quarters.Standard Chartered expects gold to average $4,650 an ounce in the fourth quarter of 2026, before rising to $4,850 in the first quarter of 2027.Goldman Sachs has retained its 2027 price forecast of $5,400 an ounce, even after lowering its end-2026 target to $4,650 in September.For domestic investors, a staggered investment approach could help investors navigate price fluctuations, while those purchasing jewellery for personal use may look at the current correction to meet their requirements.“Gold prices have fallen in recent sessions, with the market closely watching US interest rates, the dollar and inflation. The upcoming US Federal Reserve decision will play a key role in deciding gold’s near-term direction. In India, the price correction may encourage some buying ahead of Dhanteras and Diwali, but high prices are likely to keep consumers cautious. Buyers may prefer smaller, planned purchases, while gold prices are expected to remain volatile due to global economic developments and festive demand,” said Darshan Desai, CEO Aspect Bullion & Refinery."Gifting on auspicious occasion is a special blessing to the loved ones and one may think of gifting something divine or pure. Hence, we generally see the gifts in form of Gold or Silver still in trend over the years. Gold and silver prices have witnessed healthy correction in 2026 compared to the record rally during the same time in previous year. The macro-economic factors like inflation, elevated yields and central bank tightening have dominated for short term over supportive long-term factors. We believe factors deterioration in global growth, diversification in terms of Central banks buying and de-dollarization and debasement risk in terms of US fiscal concerns may continue to provide structural support for long term investment into gold and silver. The macro situation may shift anytime with change in geopolitical factors," said Tapan Patel, Fund Manager – Commodities, Tata Asset Management.The long-term investment case for gold continues to be supported by central bank diversification, geopolitical uncertainty and concerns around currency debasement. However, the extent of any recovery will depend on how inflation and monetary policy evolve in the coming months.
