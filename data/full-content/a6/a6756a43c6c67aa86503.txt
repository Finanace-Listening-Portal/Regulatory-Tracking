(NCLAT Delhi) Summary: The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, Principal Bench, New Delhi, dismissed three connected appeals filed by Arun Gupta and others concerning interlocutory orders in the oppression and mismanagement proceedings involving South Eastern Carriers Pvt. Ltd. Company Appeal (AT) No. 146 of 2024 challenged the NCLT, Kolkata Bench’s order dated 14 March 2024, while Company Appeal (AT) Nos. 155 and 164 of 2024 challenged orders dated 10 April 2024. The Tribunal held that the challenged orders implemented its earlier judgment dated 15 May 2023, which the Supreme Court had affirmed on 6 September 2023. Interference would therefore amount to indirectly reopening a determination that had already attained finality. The dispute arose within a family-controlled transportation company incorporated in 1974. Yogendra Pal Jain and his wife together held 20% of its shareholding; Jain had served as Managing Director, and their son Siddharth Jain as a Whole Time Director. An earlier company petition filed by the appellants was withdrawn following a stated settlement, while the opposing group’s request for transposition as petitioners was rejected with liberty to institute independent proceedings. Company Petition No. 42/KB/2016 subsequently raised allegations of oppression and mismanagement and sought, among other reliefs, regulation of the company’s management, disclosure of records and protection against changes in shareholding and board composition. The appellants, in turn, alleged that the opposing group had established a competing logistics business using the company’s goodwill and infrastructure. An interim order dated 3 May 2016 protected the existing shareholding and prevented implementation of resolutions removing Yogendra Pal Jain and Siddharth Jain. A subsequent order dated 18 August 2016 provided for office access cards, activation of official email accounts and salary payments on the company’s established terms where other directors received salaries. The appellants nevertheless contended that the two directors had failed to attend board meetings and consequently vacated office under Section 167(1)(b) of the Companies Act, 2013. The resulting dispute involved removal filings through Form DIR-12, appointment of additional directors, restoration of directorships, salary claims, asset disclosures and contempt allegations. The NCLAT characterised the invocation of Section 167(1)(b), in the circumstances recorded, as an attempt to override the subsisting interim protections and also criticised the appellants’ concealment of material subsequent proceedings. On 1 October 2019, the NCLT appointed D.C. Agrawal as Special Officer to investigate the company’s affairs, supervise board meetings and ensure implementation of resolutions and the interim orders. The challenge to that appointment was dismissed by the NCLAT on 20 January 2021, followed by dismissal of the corresponding Supreme Court appeal on 9 April 2021. The Supreme Court referred to recourse under Section 422 for expeditious disposal and also suggested mediation allowing one or more shareholder groups to purchase the others’ holdings. The NCLT later dismissed the company petition on 1 April 2022 as not maintainable, also referring to the age-related restriction under Section 196(3). On 15 May 2023, however, the NCLAT set aside that dismissal in its entirety, remanded the petition and connected applications for fresh consideration, and continued the earlier status quo, clarificatory and Special Officer orders. The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal against that judgment on 6 September 2023. Against this background, the NCLAT held that the order dated 14 March 2024 merely carried the binding earlier directions into effect. It rejected the appellants’ argument that reproduction of the earlier judgment’s findings constituted fresh findings prejudicing the merits of the pending company petition. The impugned order supplied no independent new fact or finding warranting appellate interference. Company Appeal (AT) No. 146 of 2024 was accordingly dismissed and its interlocutory applications closed. The two appeals against the consequential orders dated 10 April 2024 were dismissed on the same basis. The decision upheld implementation of the earlier directions; the underlying company petition remained for consideration by the NCLT.
