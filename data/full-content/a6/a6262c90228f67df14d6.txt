Summary: The 57th GST Council meeting held on 8 October 2026 recommended wide-ranging changes to GST administration, including registration and cancellation, return reconciliation, Invoice Management System, tax refunds, input tax credit, adjudication, appeals, prosecution, movement of goods, exports, e-commerce and selected goods and services. CA Rajender Arora groups these proposals into 91 separately identifiable changes across 11 themes. Major recommendations include automated refunds, faster acknowledgements, changes to accumulated-credit refunds, rationalisation of blocked ITC, limits on arrest and prosecution, simplified GST registration, and intelligence-led transit interception. The article compares the existing situation, reasons for change and likely impact, then discusses implementation dates and practical concerns over legal drafting. While the 56th meeting concentrated substantially on rate rationalisation, the 57th meeting has moved decisively towards process reform, automation, credit neutrality, faster refunds, decriminalisation, reduction of litigation and ease of doing business. The recommendations cover registration, cancellation, returns, Invoice Management System (IMS), refunds, input tax credit, exports, adjudication, penalties, arrest, prosecution, movement of goods, e-commerce and several goods-and-services rate issues. Importantly, these are Council recommendations and not yet law merely because they appear in the press release. The official release itself states that legal effect will follow through the relevant amendments, Rules, notifications and circulars. For analytical purposes, I have segregated the recommendations into 91 separately identifiable changes. Since several recommendations contain multiple operational limbs, this is an analytical count rather than an officially announced numerical count. How to read this article The discussion follows the format: existing situation → proposed change → reason for change → likely impact. Each table below carries the change numbers 1 to 91 exactly as analysed. REGISTRATION AND CANCELLATION The Council has sought to substantially reduce officer interface at the registration and cancellation stages. Registration documentation, amendment and cancellation are all proposed to move towards objective and system-driven processing. No. Change Existing situation Reason for change Likely impact 1 Comprehensive registration circular Documentary requirements can vary during processing of REG-01. To establish a uniform national documentary standard. Fewer REG-03 queries, reduced discretion and faster registrations. 2 Structured FORM GST REG-01 Registration involves substantial document uploading and free-form information. To make applications machine-verifiable and predictable. Reduced errors and faster scrutiny. 3 Guided GST portal registration Taxpayers presently navigate forms without sufficient contextual assistance. To reduce incorrect filing through drop-downs, tooltips and guidance. Lower rejection rates and better compliance. 4 Automatic amendment under Rule 19 Core registration amendments generally require officer approval. Routine changes should not remain dependent on manual approval. Near real-time amendment of registration particulars. 5 Automatic PPoB amendment for Rule 14A cases Principal Place of Business amendments normally require approval. Rule 14A taxpayers are already classified as low-risk. Greater automation for low-risk taxpayers. 6 Phase-I automatic REG-16 cancellation Cancellation applications can remain pending for officer action. To permit objective system closure where compliance is complete. Faster exit from GST and reduced officer interface. 7 Cancellation where ₹2.5 lakh ITC threshold was crossed Higher ITC-passing taxpayers face greater scrutiny. Timely GSTR-10 filing provides an additional closure safeguard. Wider eligibility for automatic cancellation. 8 Phase-II universal system cancellation Cancellation is presently substantially officer-driven. Once system controls mature, manual approval adds limited value. Significant reduction in pendency. 9 GSTR-10 details within REG-16 Cancellation application and final return are separate exercises. To integrate business closure reporting. One consolidated compliance journey. 10 Rationalisation of Rule 21 Several defaults can trigger officer-initiated cancellation. Not every technical default warrants cancellation proceedings. More proportionate use of cancellation power. 11 Rules 21A/22 amended; Rule 23A inserted Suspension, cancellation and revocation require multiple officer-driven stages. Objective defaults can be identified and cured electronically. System cancellation followed by system revocation on compliance. 12 New Rule 14B for small ECO sellers Small sellers may need a physical presence/registration footprint in multiple States. To facilitate nationwide e-commerce without creating an office in each State. Major ease-of-business benefit for small online sellers. – What does “all dues are paid” mean? This expression used for automatic cancellation deserves particular attention. Section 29(3) already provides that cancellation does not extinguish liabilities relating to an earlier period even if determined later. Consequently, “all dues” should logically mean crystallised, admitted or system-identifiable dues—self-assessed tax, interest, late fee, confirmed recoverable demand, Section 29(5) liability and similar amounts—not an unquantified future exposure under scrutiny or audit. The final Rules should expressly deal with disputed demands, stayed demands, pending rectification, uncomputed interest and liabilities arising from the final return. II. RETURNS, IMS AND RECONCILIATION The Council proposes a significant legalisation and restructuring of portal-based return controls. The revised mechanism is proposed from the April 2027 return period, after stakeholder consultation. No. Change Existing situation Reason for change Likely impact 13 Enhance GSTR-1/GSTR-1A/IFF Outward liability mismatch with GSTR-3B generates notices. Reconcile liability before final return filing. Fewer mismatch intimations. 14 New Rule 86D—RCM statement RCM liability and corresponding ITC are tracked largely through portal functionality. Give explicit Rule-based framework. Better RCM reconciliation and audit trail. 15 New Rule 61(1A) GSTR-1 liability and GSTR-3B liability can diverge. Provide statutory correction mechanism. Fewer demand notices for reporting mismatches. 16 Invoice details in DRC-03 Voluntary payments may not be invoice-linked. Improve transaction-level identification. Easier adjustment of payment against subsequent proceedings. 17 Rule 60(6A) for IMS IMS functionality exists primarily as portal architecture. Provide explicit legal basis for accept/reject/pending actions. Greater certainty in GSTR-2B generation. 18 New Rule 86C Reversal/reclaim statement exists technologically. Align portal mechanism with Rules. Clearer tracking of temporary ITC reversals. 19 Rule 61(1B) GSTR-3B ITC and GSTR-2B frequently differ. Allow structured correction/rectification. Improved ITC integrity across the supply chain. – Closing the gap between law and portalA notable feature is that Electronic Credit Reversal and Reclaim Statement, RCM statement and IMS functionality preceded their proposed express Rule-based recognition. The reform is therefore also an attempt to close the gap between GST law and GST portal architecture. Technology should implement law; it should not independently create substantive tax disabilities. Same-Line ITC: A Particularly Important Service Reform Change 81 must not be mistaken for full ITC to restaurants, caterers, hotels and gyms. The Council proposes limited ITC of input service in the same line of business, following the model already applicable to passenger transport, tour operators and renting of motor vehicles. Input Position under the recommendation Illustration Same service, subcontracted May become eligible, subject to the final notification An outdoor caterer subcontracts part of an event to another caterer; GST suffered on that catering service General business inputs Not automatically eligible Rent, advertising, kitchen equipment or other general business inputs – The underlying mischief Under a 5%-without-ITC regime, outsourcing the same taxable service creates cascading and therefore economically rewards in-house execution over subcontracting. The proposed exception seeks to restore neutrality without giving full ITC at the concessional output rate. The Overall Direction of the Reforms The common thread across the 91 changes is unmistakable: Officer discretion → System-driven processing Manual refund → Risk-based automation Credit blockage → Greater credit neutrality Broad prosecution → Focused prosecution Routine interception → Intelligence-led enforcement Portal functionality without explicit Rules → Express statutory framework Physical business presence → Digital / e-commerce facilitation The phrase “ease of living and doing business” therefore has a concrete tax meaning. For a business owner, a refund delay, credit blockage, registration cancellation, vehicle detention or arrest proceeding is not merely a compliance inconvenience—it affects liquidity, business continuity and personal time. Implementation: The Most Important Caution These recommendations will not all become effective simultaneously. Some require amendments to the CGST Act or IGST Act; some require changes to the CGST Rules; some need rate/exemption notifications, Forms, circulars, or substantial GSTN development. CGST / IGST Act CGST Rules Rate / exemption notifications Forms and circulars GSTN development The Council has specifically indicated dates such as: Date Matter 1 November 2026 Eligible input-service ITC under the proposed IDS refund reform 1 April 2027 Capital-goods ITC refund eligibility April 2027 return period Revised return correction mechanism 23 October 2017 Retrospective treatment for Rule 96(10) But these dates must ultimately be read with the actual legal instruments giving effect to the recommendations. Conclusion The 57th GST Council meeting may eventually be remembered less for changing tax rates and more for changing the manner in which GST is administered. The most consequential recommendations, in my view, are: 1 Deletion of GST arrest power 2 Rationalisation of prosecution 3 Widening Section 17(5) credit 4 Refund of accumulated input-service and capital-goods ITC 5 System-driven refunds 6 Simplification of registration and cancellation 7 Export-of-services liberalisation 8 Restrictions on transit-State interception 9 Legal recognition of IMS and portal statements 10 Reduced mismatch-driven litigation At the same time, taxpayers should watch the final drafting very carefully. Expressions such as “all dues”, “same line of business”, “risk evaluation”, “system-readable”, and the proposed validation of multi-financial-year notices can acquire consequences much wider than their plain-language description in the press release. The real law The Council has announced the policy. The real law will emerge from the exact words of the amendments, Rules, notifications and circulars that follow. That distinction remains fundamental for every professional advising on the 57th GST Council recommendations. AUTHOR’S VIEW CA Rajender Arora | On what the 57th Council recommendations mean in practice In my view, the 57th meeting is the first Council meeting in some time where the administrative philosophy of GST, rather than its rate structure, has taken centre stage. A taxpayer who loses working capital to a delayed refund, who is denied credit on a genuine business cost, or who faces arrest for an interpretational dispute does not experience GST as a rate table. The taxpayer experiences it as process. That is why I regard these recommendations as more consequential than a rate reduction of equal headline size. The direction is right. My reservations lie almost entirely in execution: a recommendation is policy, and policy becomes law only through precise drafting. My Assessment by Theme Theme Change Nos. My rating Comment Arrest and prosecution 45–49 Strongly welcome Deleting the Section 69 arrest power and narrowing Section 132 mark a fundamental shift in enforcement philosophy. Criminal law is best reserved for fraudulent credit and serious cases. Refunds and automation 20–29 Strongly welcome Automatic cash-ledger refund and 90% provisional refund bring real working-capital relief. The risk is mechanical rejection on minor data differences. ITC and accumulated credit 37–40 Strongly welcome Blocked credit becomes cost and weakens neutrality. The final wording of Section 17(5) must be watched. Exports and zero rating 41–44 Strongly welcome Deleting IGST Section 2(6)(v) and Section 13(3)(a) removes long-standing impediments for service exporters. Same-line ITC and ECO delivery 78, 81 Cautious Limited relief only; it is not full ITC. A 5% rate without ITC carries an embedded credit cost. Validation of multi-FY notices 65 Watch closely Limitation should continue to be tested separately for each financial year. Constitutional litigation is a real possibility. What Professionals and Businesses Should Do Now Treat the press release as a recommendation, not law. Do not alter positions until the Act amendments, Rules, notifications and circulars are issued. Track the stated dates: 1 November 2026, 1 April 2027, the April 2027 return period and the retrospective date of 23 October 2017 for Rule 96(10). Read the final drafting word by word. The real consequences will lie in the exact language. In summary The 57th Council has shifted GST from a regime of discretion towards one of design, where the system, rather than the officer, does the routine work. If the final law honours the spirit of these recommendations, it will be remembered as the meeting that made GST easier to live with. If the drafting falls short, the benefit will be diluted.
