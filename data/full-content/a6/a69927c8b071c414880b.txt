Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Vodafone Digilink Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Mumbai) Summary: The Mumbai Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) adjudicated cross-appeals filed by Vodafone Digilink Limited (formerly Vodafone Essar Digilink Limited) and the Income Tax Department for Assessment Year 2010–11. The appeals arose from the final assessment order dated 27 January 2015, passed following directions of the Dispute Resolution Panel (DRP). The assessee, engaged in cellular mobile telephony services in Haryana, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh, had declared a loss of ₹219.53 crore under the normal provisions and a book loss of approximately ₹1,191.29 crore in its revised return. The assessment involved corporate tax disallowances and transfer pricing adjustments relating to brand royalty and advertisement, marketing and promotion (AMP) expenses. The Tribunal examined the issues in the light of earlier decisions involving the assessee and its associated group entities. In the Revenue’s appeal, the Tribunal upheld the deletion of an ad hoc disallowance of approximately ₹12.93 crore relating to distributor commission expenses. The Assessing Officer had questioned commission payments made to distributors operating under the post-paid telecommunications model and proposed a 10% disallowance despite the assessee furnishing distributor details and Form 16A certificates. The DRP had deleted the adjustment by following the Tribunal’s decision for AY 2009–10 and earlier rulings involving Vodafone Mobile Services Limited. The Tribunal found that the Revenue had not demonstrated any material factual or legal distinction warranting a different conclusion. Accordingly, the Revenue’s first ground was dismissed. A substantial dispute concerned ₹140.33 crore of royalty-WPC expenses payable to the Department of Telecommunications for spectrum and microwave frequency usage. The Assessing Officer had treated these payments as capital expenditure, whereas the assessee argued that they were recurring regulatory charges calculated with reference to revenue and incurred for the actual use of spectrum. The Tribunal distinguished the Supreme Court’s ruling in CIT v. Bharti Hexacom Ltd., which dealt with telecom licence fees, from the separate question of royalty paid for spectrum usage. It also considered the Supreme Court’s subsequent order segregating the WPC royalty issue for independent consideration. Following the earlier decision in Vodafone Digilink’s own case and CIT v. Fascel Ltd., the Tribunal upheld the deduction of WPC royalty as revenue expenditure and dismissed the Revenue’s second ground. The Tribunal also upheld the deduction of expenditure incurred on dealer signboards, granty signs and advertising hoardings. The Assessing Officer had proposed amortising the expenditure over four years on the ground that the promotional material provided an enduring benefit. Vodafone maintained that the signs required frequent replacement because of changing products, schemes and market conditions, and therefore represented ordinary advertising expenditure. The DRP had accepted this explanation and deleted the proposed disallowance. Following the Delhi High Court’s decision in CIT v. Citi Financial Consumer Finance Ltd. and the Tribunal’s decision for the preceding assessment year, the Bench found no reason to interfere with the DRP’s directions. The corresponding Revenue ground was dismissed. The Revenue’s challenge concerning ₹22.48 crore of interest attributed to capital work-in-progress (CWIP) was treated differently. The Assessing Officer had proposed disallowance under Section 36(1)(iii), alleging that borrowed funds were used for acquiring fixed assets. Vodafone argued that the expenditure supported its existing telecommunications operations and that sufficient interest-free funds were available. The Tribunal restored the matter to the Assessing Officer for fresh examination, directing that interest attributable to specific borrowings for CWIP should be disallowed, while no disallowance should arise where the investment was financed from sufficient interest-free shareholders’ funds. The Revenue’s appeal was therefore partly allowed for statistical purposes. On a separate issue, the Tribunal upheld the treatment of transferred passive infrastructure assets under the court-approved demerger arrangement, rejecting the Revenue’s challenge to the transaction’s characterisation as a gift under Sections 47(iii) and 2(47). In Vodafone’s own appeal, the Tribunal considered several corporate tax deductions. On the deduction under Section 80-IA, it directed relief concerning miscellaneous income of ₹5.81 crore, while partly allowing the relevant ground. On annual telecom licence fees, it applied the Supreme Court’s decision in Bharti Hexacom, requiring the Assessing Officer to verify the assessee’s computation and determine the disallowance and consequential amortisation. The Tribunal also directed deletion of the disallowance of domestic roaming charges under Section 40(a)(ia), following the earlier ruling that such payments did not constitute fees for technical services requiring deduction of tax under Section 194J. Further, it deleted the ₹144.46 crore disallowance relating to upfront discounts given to prepaid distributors, following decisions in the assessee’s own cases for subsequent assessment years. The Tribunal granted additional relief regarding ₹2.42 crore paid to the Department of Telecommunications as penalties arising from contractual non-compliance. It held that payments arising from breaches of licence conditions, rather than offences or acts prohibited by law, were not hit by Explanation 1 to Section 37(1). Accordingly, the deduction was allowed. On a separate addition of ₹3.95 crore under Section 68, the Tribunal restored the matter to the Assessing Officer for fresh adjudication, requiring the assessee to establish the genuineness of the transactions. The Tribunal also dealt with other contested deductions and transfer pricing matters, granting relief or directing verification as warranted by the individual grounds. On transfer pricing, the Tribunal deleted the ₹167.83 crore adjustment relating to AMP expenditure, following the decision in Vodafone Digilink’s own case for the preceding assessment year. It accepted that the Department had not established an international transaction involving brand promotion for associated enterprises merely by applying the bright line test. The expenditure was connected with the assessee’s telecommunications operations and subscriber acquisition activities. The Assessing Officer was also directed to verify and grant eligible TDS and MAT credits, while the challenge to interest under Section 234B was treated as consequential. Both the assessee’s appeal, ITA No. 1073/Del/2015, and the Revenue’s appeal, ITA No. 1158/Del/2015, were partly allowed. The order was pronounced on 14 October 2025 by Judicial Member Rahul Chaudhary and Accountant Member Omkareshwar Chidara. Association of Unified Telecom Service Providers of India & Others, (2020) 3 SCC 525 (Supreme Court) — Considered in the discussion of distributor discounts and telecom revenue; distinguished from the question of TDS under Section 194H.
