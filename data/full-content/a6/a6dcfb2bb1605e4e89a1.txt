Summary : DCB Bank Limited’s resident Indian fixed-deposit schedule effective 27 October 2026 lists a 60-month-to-61-month tenure for single deposits below ₹3 crore. The published annual rate is 7.50% for general customers, 8.00% for resident senior citizens aged 60 to below 70, and 8.05% for the bank’s Senior Citizen Plus category aged 70 and above. The official table also displays annualised yields calculated on an assumed quarterly-compounding basis for the highest tenure in the slab; those yields are not the contractual rate and do not apply identically to every payout option or booking date. Premature closure ordinarily uses the rate applicable on the original booking date for the completed tenure, subject to DCB Bank’s penalty policy; non-callable deposits cannot be closed early. Interest remains taxable, and section 393 TDS thresholds for Tax Year 2026-27 are ₹50,000 for non-senior citizens and ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens, subject to conditions. DICGC protection aggregates principal and interest up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity. This guide is neutral and does not recommend or rank the deposit. Choose the Deposit Mandate and Payout Instruction Carefully Callable and Non-Callable Deposits Are Different Products The resident rate table should be read with the application and receipt. A callable fixed deposit can be closed before maturity subject to the bank’s terms and penalty. A non-callable fixed deposit expressly bars premature withdrawal and may have separate amount or rate conditions. The depositor should not assume that a headline slab shown online is available under both variants on identical terms. The deposit advice should state the product, tenure, rate, maturity date and callable status. Payout Frequency Changes Cash Flow and Compounding DCB Bank explains that interest can be paid monthly, quarterly or at maturity, depending on the product. A reinvestment deposit compounds interest quarterly and pays principal plus accumulated interest at maturity. For payout deposits, interest is credited at the selected interval. Monthly payout calculations may differ from a simple annual-rate division because banks apply their operational interest methodology. The customer should retain the application and verify the first credit against the bank advice. Premature Closure Uses the Completed-Tenure Rate and Penalty DCB Bank’s deposit policy states that, when early withdrawal is permitted, interest is based on the rate prevailing on the original deposit date for the period the money actually remained with the bank. The current computation note states a premature-closure penalty of 0.50 percentage point for INR fixed deposits below ₹3 crore and 2.00 percentage points for deposits of ₹3 crore and above. This article’s selected rate slab is specifically for a single deposit below ₹3 crore, so the 0.50-point policy is the relevant published penalty category, subject to the final receipt and booking-date terms. For deposits below ₹3 crore, the computation note states that no interest is payable if the deposit is closed before completing 14 days. Early closure after that minimum does not preserve the original 60–61 month rate. The bank identifies the rate applicable to the completed tenure as at the original booking date and then applies the applicable penalty. Interest already paid under a monthly or quarterly option may be recovered or adjusted in the closure proceeds. In a reinvestment option, quarterly interest is added to principal for the next quarter. Because this tenure crosses several years, the maturity computation may span both leap and non-leap years. A rate multiplied by years is therefore not a reliable maturity calculation. The displayed annualised yield assumes uninterrupted quarterly compounding for the highest tenure in the slab. It does not account for TDS, early closure, payout selection or a booking at a shorter point within the slab. Depositors should use the amount shown on the final deposit advice and reconcile maturity proceeds when credited. Any automatic-renewal instruction should be reviewed because the renewal rate is the rate prevailing for the selected tenure at renewal, not necessarily 7.50%, 8.00% or 8.05%. Tax and TDS for Tax Year 2026–27 Fixed-deposit interest is generally taxable according to the depositor’s applicable law, residential status and method of accounting. TDS is a collection mechanism and does not determine the final tax liability. For interest covered by the banking-company row in section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, the threshold is ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen, subject to the statutory aggregation and other conditions. The threshold applies to the covered interest from the payer, not independently to each receipt or branch under core banking. Form 121 Replaces the Old Form 15G and 15H Architecture For Tax Year 2026-27, an eligible resident with nil estimated tax liability may consider Form 121 under section 393(6) and Rule 211. The declaration should ideally reach the bank before interest is credited or paid. Submitting the form does not exempt the interest; it allows non-deduction only when eligibility is satisfied. The depositor should retain the acknowledgement or UIN and reconcile the bank’s interest certificate with Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement. DICGC Insurance Applies Across All DCB Bank Branches DCB Bank is a banking company within the DICGC deposit-insurance framework. The maximum cover is ₹5 lakh for principal and interest together per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity. Savings, current, recurring and fixed deposits held across different DCB Bank branches in the same ownership capacity are aggregated. Multiple receipts, branches or digital booking channels do not create separate ₹5 lakh limits. Accounts held in genuinely different rights and capacities are treated according to DICGC rules. Joint accounts with the same holders in the same order are aggregated; different ownership capacities can be treated separately when legally valid. DICGC insurance is triggered by specified events such as liquidation, licence cancellation, reconstruction or all-inclusive directions with withdrawal restrictions. It is not a promise of immediate liquidity and does not protect amounts above the insured ceiling. Insurance question Correct reading Are principal and interest each insured for ₹5 lakh? No. The combined maximum is ₹5 lakh. Does each DCB Bank branch receive a separate limit? No. Same-right-and-capacity balances across branches are aggregated. Do several FD receipts multiply insurance? No. Receipt count does not change the depositor-per-bank test. Does insurance guarantee access at any time?
