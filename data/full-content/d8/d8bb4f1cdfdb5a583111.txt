Vs Commissioner of GST and Central Excise (CESTAT Chennai) Summary: The Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), Chennai, in M/s Pallipalayam Spinners Private Ltd. Vs Commissioner of GST and Central Excise, set aside a central excise demand of ₹1,06,18,580, together with consequential interest and penalty, holding that the Department had wrongly invoked the extended period of limitation under Section 11A of the Central Excise Act, 1944. The Tribunal found that the entire demand arose from scrutiny of statutory records and returns maintained by the assessee, and that the relevant facts had already come to the Department’s knowledge during the audit conducted in 2011. Since the show cause notice was issued only on 17 April 2014, the Tribunal held that the proceedings were barred by limitation. It further ruled that once the demand was found to be time-barred, examination of the substantive allegations concerning diversion of raw materials was unnecessary. The appellant, M/s Pallipalayam Spinners Private Limited, manufactured various varieties of yarn, including viscose, polyester, viscose-polyester, polyester-cotton and viscose-flax yarn, falling under Chapter 55 of the Central Excise Tariff. The company followed different procedures for clearance of finished goods depending upon whether the products were cleared domestically, exported under bond or exported under rebate claims. It availed CENVAT credit on a portion of the Viscose Staple Fibre (VSF) used for manufacturing dutiable yarn under Notification No. 29/2004-CE dated 9 July 2004. For another portion of the raw materials, it did not avail CENVAT credit and cleared finished yarn under the exemption provided by Notification No. 30/2004-CE dated 9 July 2004. It also procured VSF without payment of duty under the procedure prescribed by Notification No. 43/2001-CE (NT) dated 26 June 2001 for manufacturing export goods. The assessee maintained separate stock accounts in its Form IV Register under three categories, namely LOCAL, EXPORT and ANNEXURE. The LOCAL account covered raw materials intended for manufacture of yarn cleared domestically without payment of duty. The EXPORT account related to yarn exported under bond, exported under rebate claims or cleared domestically on payment of duty. The ANNEXURE account recorded VSF procured without payment of duty under the prescribed export procedure. During an audit covering April 2010 to August 2011, the Department compared the stock registers with sales invoices and alleged discrepancies in the quantities of VSF used for manufacturing yarn under different categories. According to the audit findings, the LOCAL account recorded issuance of 9,29,344.870 kilograms of VSF, whereas the sales invoices reflected local clearance of yarn and waste containing 14,30,586 kilograms of VSF. After considering opening stock, the Department calculated an excess VSF content of 4,78,637.126 kilograms. Conversely, the EXPORT account showed issuance of 30,24,658.030 kilograms of VSF, while the sales invoices reflected VSF content of 26,83,785.022 kilograms. After adjusting opening stock, the Department arrived at a shortage of 4,18,368.988 kilograms. On this basis, the Revenue alleged that the assessee had diverted duty-paid VSF, on which CENVAT credit had been availed, for manufacturing yarn subsequently cleared without payment of excise duty. The Commissioner issued Show Cause Notice No. 17/2014 dated 17 April 2014, proposing recovery of ₹1,06,18,580 under Section 11A of the Central Excise Act, 1944. The notice also proposed interest under Section 11AA and penalties under Section 11AC and Rule 25 of the Central Excise Rules, 2002. The Department alleged violations of Rules 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 of the Central Excise Rules, 2002, Rule 5 of the Central Excise (Removal of Goods at Concessional Rate of Duty for Manufacture of Excisable Goods) Rules, 2001, and the conditions governing the relevant exemption notifications. It invoked the extended limitation period on the allegation that the assessee had deliberately diverted raw materials and suppressed the relevant facts with an intention to evade excise duty. The assessee denied the allegations and maintained that the Department’s calculations were based on assumptions and incomplete reconciliation of manufacturing records. It argued that the audit had failed to account properly for work-in-process inventory, opening stock and usable waste generated during yarn manufacturing. According to the assessee, usable waste of 4,55,653.80 kilograms was an inherent by-product of the manufacturing process and was subsequently utilised in manufacturing yarn for the domestic market. It also submitted that applicable excise duty of ₹6,75,825 had been paid in relation to such usage. The assessee challenged the Department’s presumed diversion of approximately 60,268 kilograms of VSF and contended that no independent evidence of clandestine clearance had been produced. It further argued that the demand was barred by limitation because the relevant transactions were already reflected in statutory records available to the Department. The adjudicating authority rejected the assessee’s explanations and confirmed the demand. It observed that adequate evidence supporting the work-in-process stock and generation of usable waste had not been furnished. Even after considering the claimed work-in-process stock, the authority calculated a shortage of approximately 4,04,931 kilograms. It also rejected the assessee’s argument that the Department had proceeded on assumptions, observing that the shortages had been identified through a detailed audit of quantitative records. The Commissioner held that the alleged diversion would not have surfaced but for the audit and, therefore, invocation of the extended limitation period was justified. The demand and associated penal consequences were confirmed by Order-in-Original No. 12/2014 dated 26 December 2014. Before the Tribunal, the assessee reiterated that the demand rested entirely on presumptions drawn from stock registers and that there was no independent evidence establishing diversion of duty-paid inputs into exempted finished goods. It submitted that the Department had not properly considered the normal manufacturing process, the generation and reuse of waste fibre or the quantities lying in work in progress. More importantly, the assessee emphasised that the audit had been conducted in 2011 and that it had submitted a reply to the audit observations on 7 November 2011. Therefore, the Department was fully aware of the relevant facts well before the show cause notice was issued in April 2014. The assessee relied on several judicial precedents, including Cosmic Dye Chemical v. Collector of Central Excise, Orissa Bridge & Construction Corporation Ltd. v. CCE and RR Polymers v. Commissioner of GST & Central Excise, to contest the allegation of suppression and invocation of the extended limitation period. The Tribunal framed two issues: whether the extended limitation period could be invoked when the audit had taken place in 2011 but the show cause notice was issued in April 2014, and whether VSF on which credit had been availed and which was intended for export had actually been diverted to domestic clearances under Notification No. 30/2004-CE. Addressing limitation first, the Tribunal found that the Department had calculated the alleged shortages entirely from the assessee’s own statutory stock records and returns. There was no independent evidence unearthed during investigation establishing actual clearance of dutiable goods. The Tribunal also noted that the assessee had regularly filed its returns and that the Department had not alleged any failure to file such returns. In these circumstances, the inability of departmental officers to identify the alleged discrepancy before the audit could not, by itself, establish suppression of facts by the assessee. The Tribunal placed particular emphasis on the assessee’s reply dated 7 November 2011 to the audit observations. This established that the relevant transactions and discrepancies were within the Department’s knowledge long before the show cause notice dated 17 April 2014. The Tribunal held that the Department could not invoke the extended period merely because the alleged irregularity was detected during audit. It observed that scrutiny of returns was a statutory responsibility of the proper officer and that departmental failure to scrutinise available records in time could not be converted into an allegation of deliberate suppression. Relying on the principles laid down in Pushpam Pharmaceuticals Company v. Collector of Central Excise, Anand Nishikawa Co. Ltd. v. Commissioner of Central Excise and Uniworth Textiles Ltd. v. Commissioner of Central Excise, the Tribunal reiterated that suppression must involve deliberate withholding of material information with an intention to evade duty. Mere omission, without the necessary element of deliberate conduct, was insufficient. The Tribunal also referred to the Allahabad High Court’s decisions in CCE, Noida v. Accurate Chemicals Industries and CCE v. Monsanto Manufacturer Pvt. Ltd. The former supported the proposition that regular filing of statutory returns and the availability of relevant information to the Department could defeat allegations of suppression. The latter established that once a demand was held to be barred by limitation, there was no necessity to examine its substantive merits. Following these principles and its own earlier decisions, the Tribunal held that the Department had failed to establish wilful misstatement, suppression of facts or intent to evade duty. Since the show cause notice had been issued beyond the normal limitation period, the extended period under Section 11A(4) was unavailable. Accordingly, the CESTAT Chennai allowed the appeal, holding that the entire demand was barred by limitation. It expressly refrained from examining the merits of the alleged diversion of VSF and set aside the complete demand, interest and penalty, with consequential relief in accordance with law. The decision is significant for manufacturers maintaining statutory stock registers and filing periodic excise returns, as it reinforces the requirement that the Revenue must establish deliberate suppression before invoking the extended limitation period. It also clarifies that discovery of discrepancies during audit does not automatically establish an intention to evade duty, particularly where the relevant facts were already recorded in the assessee’s books and disclosed to the Department. The ruling is confined to limitation and does not constitute a final determination on whether the alleged diversion of inputs actually occurred. Cases Discussed Cosmic Dye Chemical v. Heard both sides and considered the rival submissions including the evidence available on appeal records and the case laws relied upon. 10. The issues that arises for consideration are: – i. Whether the invocation of extended period is sustainable when the audit of the appellants unit took place in September 2011 and Show cause has been issued on 17.04.2014. ii. Whether stock on which credit has been taken and which are meant for purpose of export have been diverted to domestic market under Notification No.30/2004 dt.09.07.2004. 11. On the issue of invoking extended period, it is not disputed that the shortage was quantified on the basis of stock challenge and based on the stock registers maintained by the Appellant during the Audit. We have to observe that duty demand was quantified on the basis of alleged shortages and investigation has not unearthed evidence of clearance of the dutiable goods. Further, it is a settled proposition of law that when facts are culled out from the assessee’s own statutory records and returns, which were duly maintained and produced before the Department, there can be no allegation of suppression or intent to evade duty. 12. Here, the entire basis of the demand emanates from the records and returns filed by the Appellant. It is not the Department’s case that the Appellant failed to file returns. In the present case, the Appellant has regularly filed its returns with the department. The fact that the Department could not detect the alleged short payment until audit only reflects an omission on the part of the Department in timely scrutinizing returns and not suppression by the assessee. 13. In this regard, we find that the Appellant on 07.11.2011, itself, has replied to an Audit Observation establishing that these facts were within the knowledge of the Department. Whereas the show cause notice in the present case has been issued only on 17.04.2014, beyond the normal period of limitation. Therefore, when these facts were clearly within the knowledge of the Department there cannot be any scope for the department to plead that the assessee is guilty of wilful misstatement or suppression of facts with an intent to evade payment of duty. 14. In this connection, it would be appropriate to refer to the decision of the Principal Bench of this Tribunal in the case of Maruti Udyog Ltd. v. CCE reported in 2002 (147) ELT 881 (Tri-Del), wherein the Tribunal followed the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of ITO v. Lakhmani Mewal Das reported in 1996 (103) ITR 437, wherein it was held as under: – “The duty of the assessee in any case does not extend beyond making a true and full disclosure of primary facts. Once he has done that his duty ends. It is for the Income-tax officer to draw the correct inference from the primary facts. It is no responsibility of the assessee to advise the Income-tax Officer with regard to the inference which he should draw from the primary facts.” 15. The scrutiny of returns is not an empty formality; it is a statutory duty of the proper officer to verify returns and seek clarifications if necessary. Having failed to discharge this responsibility, the Department cannot now turn around and allege suppression to invoke extended period. 16. We have consistently expressed such a view in Final Order No.41524/2024 dated 28-11-2024 in the case of M/s. Xomox Sanmar Ltd, Unit II v. Commissioner of CGST & Central Excise, Trichy, Final Order No.40567/2025 dated 30.05.2025 in the case of M/s. Nobel King Purchase Solutions Pvt Ltd v Commissioner of GST and Central Excise, Chennai and in Final Order No.40664/2025 dated 26-06-2025 in the case of M/s. Exide Industries Ltd v Commissioner of GST and Central Excise, Salem. We also note that in the decision reported in Uniworth Textiles v CCE, Nagpur, 2013 (288) ELT 161 (SC), relied upon by the appellant, while considering the ingredients required to invoke the extended period of limitation, the Supreme Court observed that it is a cardinal postulate of law that the burden of proving any form of mala fide lies on the shoulders of the one alleging it. We also note that a similar view has also been taken by this Tribunal in the case of Aarya Construction Vs. Commissioner of GST and Central Excise [Appeal No. 40999/2019 dated 09.07.2025] 17. Accordingly, in the present case, the allegation of suppression with intent to evade duty is wholly unsustainable. Since the demand is founded exclusively on audit verification of disclosed records, invocation of the extended period under Section 11A(4) is barred in law. 18. In continuation to the above, where the Returns have been regularly filed, extended period of limitation is not invokable as per the ratio of the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in the case of CCE, Noida v. Accurate Chemicals Industries [2014 (310) ELT 441 (All.)], wherein it has been held as under: “6. Having considered the judgment of the Tribunal, we see no reason to interfere with the finding of fact that if a scrutiny had been made by the Range Officer of the ER-1 returns, that would have revealed that the assessee had cleared its MS tanks and radiators to the owning company for the manufacture of transformers. This indicated that there was no fraud, collusion, misstatement or suppression of facts. Besides, since the situation was revenue neutral, no intent to evade the payment of duty could be ascribed to the assessee. Once, there was no intent to evade the payment of duty, the Tribunal was justified in coming to the conclusion that the extended period of limitation under the proviso to Section 11A(1) of the Act, would not be attracted. Hence, no substantial question of law arises in the appeal. It is, accordingly, dismissed.” 19. Additionally, the law on invocation of extended period of limitation for the purpose of recovery of tax dues is settled. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Pushpam Pharmaceuticals Company vs. Collector of Central Excise, Bombay [1995 (78) E.L.T. 401 (SC)], held as under: – “2. ……The Department invoked extended period of limitation of five years as according to it the duty was short levied due to suppression of the fact that if the turnover was clubbed then it exceeded Rupees Five lakhs. ….. ….. 4. A perusal of the proviso indicates that it has been used in words company of such strong words as fraud, collusion or willful default. In fact it is the mildest expression used in the proviso. Yet the surroundings in which it has been used it has to be construed strictly. It does not mean any omission. The act must be deliberate. In taxation, it can have only one meaning that the correct information was not disclosed deliberately to escape from payment of duty. Where facts are known to both the parties the omission by one to do what he might have done and not that he must have done, does not render it suppression.” 20. In view of the above, the alleged suppression of facts should be deliberate. The above ratio had subsequently come to be followed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Anand Nishikawa Co. Ltd. vs. Commissioner of Central Excise, Meerut [(2005) 7 SCC 749)], wherein it was held as under: – “27. Relying on the aforesaid observations of this Court in the case of Pushpam Pharmaceuticals Co. v. CCE we find that “suppression of facts” can have only one meaning that the correct information was not disclosed deliberately to evade payment of duty. When facts were known to both the parties, the omission by one to do what he might have done and not that he must have done, would not render it suppression. It is settled law that mere failure to declare does not amount to wilful suppression. There must be some positive act from the side of the assessee to find willful suppression. Therefore, in view of our findings made hereinabove that there was no deliberate intention on the part of the appellant not to disclose the correct information or to evade payment of duty, it was not open to the Central Excise Officer to proceed to recover duties in the manner indicated in the proviso to Section 11-A of the Act. We are, therefore, of the firm opinion that where facts were known to both the parties, as in the instant case, it was not open to CEGAT to come to a conclusion that the appellant was guilty of “suppression of facts.” 21. Furthermore, there are other catena of judgments wherein various High Courts as well as this Tribunal has consistently held that when the assessee is registered and filing returns regularly, the range officer has a duty to scrutinize returns, detect irregularity, and to raise pertinent queries in this regard and that in light of any negligence or failure to do so, the allegation of suppression by the assessee cannot be sustained. 22. In view of the above facts, since audit was conducted in August, 2011 and the Appellant has replied to the same on 07.11.2011, itself, it is clear the facts of the same were within the knowledge of the Department. This being the case, there is no reason as to why the show cause notice issued for the period from April, 2010 to August, 2011 was issued only in the year 2014 (17.04.2014) i.e., beyond the normal period of limitation, thereby the Show Cause Notice issued is barred by limitation. 23. In this context, we find that the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in the case of CCE v. Monsanto Manufacturer Pvt. Ltd. 2014 (35) STR 177 (All.) had held as follows: – “Once that be the position and the Tribunal having came to the conclusion that the extended period of limitation could not have been validly applied, the Tribunal, in our view, acted outside its jurisdiction in entering upon the merits of the dispute on whether the demand for duty should be confirmed. Once it is held that the demand is time barred, there would be no occasion for the Tribunal to enquire into the merits of the issues raised by the Revenue.” 24. Following the above decision this Tribunal in the case of M/s. AAM India Manufacturing Corporation Pvt. Ltd. V. Commissioner of GST & Central Excise reported in 2025 (8) TMI 1655 CESTAT CHENNAI, held that the demand was wholly barred by limitation and accordingly, we had refrained from delving into the merits of the issue in detail. Following the same principle in the instant case, we hold that the demand is barred by limitation and therefore refrain from going into the merits of the matter. Accordingly, the entire demand, interest and penalty is set aside. 25. Thus, the appeal is allowed with consequential relief, if any, as per the law.
