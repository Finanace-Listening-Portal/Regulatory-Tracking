Articles 2 min read Star Health expects 19 pc growth in gross written premium to Rs 24,000 cr in FY27: MD Star Health and Allied Insurance Company anticipates reaching gross written premiums between Rs 23,000 and 24,000 crore by FY27, with an ambitious target of Rs 30,000 crore by FY30. With a strong focus on retail health insurance, they report that 70% of their business stems from Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities. PTI Published On Nov 13, 2026 at 12:58 PM IST var news_msid = 135794035; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook New Delhi, Star Health and Allied Insurance Company expects to close FY27 with gross written premium (GWP) of Rs 23,000-24,000 crore, representing year-on-year growth of around 18-19 per cent, the insurer's MD and CEO Anand Roy said.The standalone health insurer is also targeting GWP of Rs 30,000 crore by FY30, with a focus on expanding its retail health insurance business and increasing insurance penetration in smaller cities, he said.The company recorded GWP of approximately Rs 9,500 crore in the first half of FY27, registering growth of around 18 per cent.New business premium grew 36 per cent to Rs 1,600 crore during the period. Advt The insurer also settled nearly 22 lakh claims, including inpatient and outpatient claims, with payouts amounting to approximately Rs 6,700 crore during the first six months of the financial year.Roy further said the company has witnessed an improvement in its margins over the last four quarters, supported by improvements in claims and combined ratios.On business expansion, he said nearly 70 per cent of Star Health's business comes from Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, which will remain a key focus for increasing health insurance penetration.The company has identified a gap in health insurance adoption, with many individuals postponing purchases because they do not anticipate requiring medical treatment at an early age, he said.To address this, Star Health has introduced Young Star NEO, which offers a 'Time Travel' feature allowing customers to retain the premium-age advantage of purchasing insurance early.Sharing features, he said a customer purchasing the policy at 25 and making the first eligible claim at 35 would have the subsequent renewal premium calculated based on age 26 rather than their actual age, subject to policy terms.Talking about proposed distribution norms, Roy said proposed reforms by the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) are aimed at improving affordability and rationalising distribution commissions. Advt He said the proposed commission caps could help address high distribution costs, particularly in institutional channels such as banks and brokers.The company was supportive of the proposed regulatory changes, which are aimed at making insurance more affordable and accessible to consumers, he added.
