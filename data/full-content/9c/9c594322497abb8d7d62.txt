HDFC Bank – BUY CMP – ₹816.5 FV – ₹1,050 Resistance – ₹830/845 Support – ₹790/770 We maintain ‘Buy’ on HDFC Bank with an unchanged fair value of ₹1,050, supported by a favourable banking-sector backdrop, potential margin recovery, returning foreign institutional investor flows and attractive valuations. While the ongoing leadership transition creates near-term uncertainty, improving earnings drivers provide a constructive risk-reward proposition. The operating environment for banks remains healthy, with system credit growth running in the high teens, supported by demand across retail, SME and corporate segments. Sector asset quality remains strong, while benign slippages and credit costs provide a solid foundation for earnings growth. HDFC Bank is well placed to benefit from these conditions given its strong liability franchise, extensive distribution network and established underwriting capabilities. In Q2FY27, the bank reported 16 per cent y-o-y loan growth and 19 per cent y-o-y deposit growth, indicating continued balance-sheet momentum. Earnings and operating profit increased by approximately 5 per cent y-o-y, while fee income grew 10 per cent y-o-y, led by wholesale banking. Asset quality remained resilient, with slippages at 0.8 per cent and credit costs at 0.5 per cent of loans. RoA stood at 1.7 per cent and RoE at 13 per cent, leaving scope for improvement as margins recover. NIM declined by approximately 10 bps q-o-q to 3.2 per cent, partly reflecting the impact of FCNR deposit mobilisation. However, the rate-hike cycle should create margin tailwinds, as floating-rate loans and other earning assets reprice upward. While higher deposit costs could partly offset this benefit, disciplined liability management and faster asset repricing should support gradual NIM expansion. Combined with strong credit growth and stable asset quality, this should drive better earnings and return ratios. The potential return of FII flows into India represents another important catalyst. Financials carry a relatively high weight in foreign institutional portfolios and benchmark indices, meaning the sector typically receives a disproportionate share of incremental overseas flows. As one of India’s largest and most liquid private-sector banks, HDFC Bank should be a key beneficiary of renewed foreign allocations, potentially supporting both valuation recovery and relative stock-price outperformance. Execution remains the principal monitorable, particularly as Anup Bagchi leads a relatively young management team following Kaizad Bharucha’s departure. Greater visibility on strategic priorities and leadership depth will be necessary for a sustained re-rating. Nevertheless, valuations remain attractive at approximately 2 times FY28E book value and 15 times FY28E earnings, for a franchise capable of delivering around 15 per cent RoE over the medium term.
