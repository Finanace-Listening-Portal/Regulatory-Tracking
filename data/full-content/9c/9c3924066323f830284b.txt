Articles 3 min read LIC flags ULIP volatility, small-ticket policies for persistency dip; says some plans may face commission impact under IRDAI proposal Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) is shifting its sales focus towards higher-ticket policies to address the deterioration in long-term persistency, with market volatility affecting demand for unit-linked insurance plans (ULIPs) and smaller-ticket policies emerging as a key area of concern. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Nov 5, 2026 at 06:39 PM IST var news_msid = 135574895; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook R Doraiswamy, Managing Director and CEO, LIC, answering a question by ETBFSI, said the insurer has been consciously recalibrating its product and sales strategy, particularly in response to challenging market conditions that have affected both ULIP sales and customer confidence in market-linked products."When the markets go through a difficult time, ULIPs tend to get affected. Both the sale of ULIPs as well as the customer's confidence in ULIPs when the markets are going tough, that happens," Doraiswamy said, during its results presser on Thursday.LIC's 61st-month persistency on a premium basis declined to 61.03% in the first half of FY27, compared with 63.81% in the corresponding period last year. On a policy-count basis, the ratio fell more sharply to 48.33% from 51.50%, indicating continued challenges in retaining policyholders over the longer term. Advt Responding to ETBFSI's question on the factors driving the decline and the corrective measures being undertaken, Doraiswamy said persistency concerns are more pronounced among policies with smaller premium tickets, while policies above a certain premium threshold have demonstrated more stable retention."The persistency issues come only in terms of small ticket size policies. So, we are slowly nudging the sales towards policies with above the threshold level," he said.The insurer is also increasing minimum ticket sizes and avoiding pushing certain product lines when market conditions are unfavourable. According to Doraiswamy, this calibrated approach is already showing some traction.LIC is also tracking the number of policies remaining in force, with the management indicating that it has seen encouraging growth in this measure. The strategy signals a shift towards improving the quality and sustainability of business rather than pursuing policy volumes alone.IRDAI commission curbs could push 15-40% of LIC agents out over 2-4 years, deter new recruits, warn industry veteransFormer LIC Managing Director Vipin Anand estimates that 15% of LIC agents could exit over two years, while LIAFI Chairman N Gajapathi Rao warns that attrition could reach 40% over three to four years. Both flag risks to agent livelihoods, recruitment and insurance distribution as IRDAI proposes tighter commission caps and expense limits.Commission framework: LIC awaits draft regulations, says some plans may be impacted Advt On ETBFSI's question on the potential impact of IRDAI's proposed commission framework on LIC's extensive agent network and commission payouts, Doraiswamy said the insurer has submitted its feedback to the regulator and would assess the implications once the draft regulations are issued."This is a consultation paper. We have given our views on that paper to the regulator. We expect our views to be considered and we will see when the draft regulation comes, we will exactly find out what the impact is," he said.However, he indicated that the impact may not be uniform across LIC's product portfolio, with certain plans potentially facing changes under the proposed framework. Advt "Our agent's expense ratio or commission ratios are not high. And we have been very consistently maintaining it within the regulatory limits. But the current hard core limits are on a plan-wise basis. There will be some plans which will be impacted," Doraiswamy said.Asked about the specific feedback submitted to IRDAI, he said LIC had flagged concerns and suggestions relating to different premium-paying terms, lines of business and the commission rates proposed under the consultation paper.While LIC has not yet quantified the potential impact on agent earnings, retention or its overall commission outgo, the eventual provisions in the draft regulations will determine the extent of changes required across its distribution network. Advt LIC's H1 profit rises 21.5%, VNB margin expands to 22.3%LIC reported a 21.52% year-on-year increase in standalone profit after tax to ₹25,568 crore for the first half of FY27, compared with ₹21,040 crore in the corresponding period last year.Total premium income grew 11.64% to ₹2,74,269 crore during the six months ended September 2026. Individual new business premium income increased 14.86% to ₹32,725 crore, while group new business premium income rose 20.63% to ₹1,11,685 crore. Individual renewal premium income grew 4.68% to ₹1,27,950 crore.The insurer's value of new business (VNB) increased 33.95% to ₹6,846 crore, while its net VNB margin expanded by 470 basis points to 22.3%, against 17.6% a year ago. Its overall expense ratio improved by 87 basis points to 10.41%.However, the number of individual policies sold during the first half declined 2.25% to 70.97 lakh from 72.61 lakh in the year-ago period. LIC's assets under management increased 4.16% year-on-year to ₹59,60,941 crore as of September 30, 2026, while its solvency ratio improved to 2.35 from 2.13.
