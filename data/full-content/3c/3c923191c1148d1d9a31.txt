(Calcutta High Court) GST Appeal to Be Heard on Merits Despite Delay Caused by Consultant’s Illness: Calcutta High Court Summary: The Calcutta High Court in Keshab Joshi Vs State of West Bengal & Ors. considered a writ petition challenging GST adjudication and recovery proceedings for April 2020 to March 2021. The petitioner, a senior citizen, alleged that the adjudication order was passed ex parte without effective service of the show-cause notice or a meaningful opportunity to respond. His appeal was dismissed on limitation without examination of the merits. Despite depositing 10% of the disputed tax as pre-deposit under Section 107(6) of the CGST Act, the authorities allegedly attached his bank account on 19 February 2026 and recovered ₹3,59,409 and ₹1,04,574 from his Electronic Credit Ledger. He explained that he depended on his tax consultant for electronic GST compliance and remained unaware of the notices because of the consultant’s prolonged illness. The State disputed these allegations, maintaining that adequate opportunities had been given and the notice was physically served. Considering the petitioner’s circumstances, the High Court held that he should not be left without a remedy and directed the Appellate Authority to reconsider the appeal on merits, provide a personal hearing and pass a reasoned speaking order within six weeks of communication of the judgment. The authority was also directed to independently examine the bank attachment, recovered amounts and pre-deposit. The impugned order dated 21 November 2025 was quashed without deciding the underlying tax liability. Present for the Petitioner: Mr. Himangshu Kumar Ray, Mr. Saptak Sanyal, Mr. Subhasis Podder and Ms. Shiwani Shaw FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CALCUTTA HIGH COURT 1. Affidavit of service filed in Court, is taken on record. 2. The core issue involved in the present case is as to whether the respondent authorities can recover the amount and attach the bank account without giving an opportunity to the petitioner to rebut the allegations mentioned in the show-cause notice by filing a reply to the same. 3. In the present writ petition, the petitioner challenges, inter alia, the validity, legality and sustainability of the order dated 21.11.2025 passed by the respondent no.3 under Section 107 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and West Bengal Rules and Services Tax Act (hereinafter referred to as CGST Act, 2017 and WBGST Act, 2017). 4. The impugned adjudication order was passed exparte, without effective service of show-cause notice and without affording any meaningful opportunity to the petitioner to rebut the allegations contained therein. The appeal preferred thereagainst was dismissed at the threshold on the sole ground of limitation, without any adjudication on merits. 5. Notwithstanding the deposit of 10 percent of the disputed tax as pre-deposit, as mandated under Section 107(6) of the CGST Act, 2017, the respondents proceeded with coercive recovery by issuing garnishee notices, attaching the bank account of the petitioner on 19.2.2026, and also recovered a sum of Rs. 3,59,409/- and 1,04,574/- from the electronic Credit Ledger on 14.1.2026 and 20.2.2026 respectively for the period April 2020 to March 2021, thereby bringing the entire business operations to a standstill. 6. The petitioner, being a senior citizen lacking technical expertise in operating the GST Portal electronically, was wholly dependent upon his tax consultant for compliance. The prolonged illness of the said consultant, who managed the entire business affairs, resulted in non receipt and non-knowledge of the notices. Such delay is neither deliberate nor wilful but is occasioned by a bona fide and sufficient cause. 7. The impugned action, being in gross violation of the principles of natural justice, has caused undue financial hardship and is liable to be set aside. 8. Per contra, learned counsel appearing for the State respondents vehemently opposes the contentions of the petitioner and submits that ample opportunity was afforded to the petitioner to respond the show cause notice, which was also served physically upon the petitioner. 9. It is further contended that the petitioner was liable to pay tax, interest and/or reverse input tax credit, including tax on unreconciled dues in taxable turnover, having failed to produce any relevant documents in support of his claim. 10. Despite repeated reminders, the petitioner failed to adhere to the direction of the authorities, therefore, the appellate authority rightly dismissed the appeal on the ground of limitation. The impugned order, it is argued, suffers from no irregularity or infirmity and is sustainable in the eye of law. 11. After hearing the rival contention of the parties and upon perusing the materials available on record, this court observes that the petitioner was unaware of the notices due to the prolonged illness of the tax consultant who managed the affairs of the business, and the petitioner, being a senior citizen lacking technical knowledge in operating the system electronically, cannot be left remediless on account of unforeseen circumstances. 12. For balancing the equities and for the end of justice, the appellate authority being the respondent No.3 herein is directed to revisit the issue and dispose of the appeal on merits in the light of Section 107 (7) of the CGST Act, 2017 and WBGST Act, 2017, within a period of six weeks from the date of communication of this order by passing a reasoned and a speaking order in accordance with law, after affording an opportunity of personal hearing to the petitioner and shall communicate such decision within a week thereafter. 13. The Appellate Authority shall at the time of adjudication, independently consider the issue of bank attachment, the amount recovered from the Electronic Credit Ledger, including the predeposit and the 10 percent of the tax in dispute deposited for preferring the appeal against the ex parte order of adjudication. 14. It is made clear, that the Appellate Authority shall take an independent decision, uninfluenced by any observations made in this order. The petitioner shall not pray for any unnecessary adjournments before the authority concerned. 15. With the above observations and the directions the order dated 21.11.2025 is hereby quashed and set aside and the writ petition being WPA No. 5907 of 2026 stands disposed of without going into merits of the case. 16.
