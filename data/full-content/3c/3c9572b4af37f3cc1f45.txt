Articles 2 min read RBI retains SBI, HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank in 2026 list of systemically important banks The Reserve Bank of India has retained State Bank of India, HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank in its 2026 list of Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs), with no change in their respective capital surcharge requirements or bucketing structure from the previous year. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Published On Nov 23, 2026 at 05:27 PM IST var news_msid = 136101582; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on Monday retained State Bank of India (SBI), HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank in its 2026 list of Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs), with all three lenders continuing in the same buckets as the previous year.Under the classification, Country’s largest lender SBI remains in Bucket 4 and is required to maintain an additional Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital buffer of 0.80 per cent of its risk-weighted assets (RWAs). Largest private lender HDFC Bank continues in Bucket 2 with an additional requirement of 0.40 per cent, while ICICI Bank remains in Bucket 1 with a requirement of 0.20 per cent. Advt The additional capital requirements are over and above the capital conservation buffer, the RBI said in a press release.The latest list is based on data collected from banks as on March 31, 2026.The RBI releases the list of D-SIBs annually under a framework introduced on July 22, 2014, with disclosures beginning in 2015. Banks are placed in different buckets based on their Systemic Importance Scores (SIS), with higher buckets attracting higher additional capital requirements.The exercise is aimed at addressing risks posed by banks whose failure could have wider implications for the financial system. These banks are often described as being 'too big to fail' because of their interconnectedness and importance to the banking system.The previous year's list, released on December 2, 2025, also included SBI, HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank in the same buckets, with capital requirements of 0.80 per cent, 0.40 per cent and 0.20 per cent of RWAs, respectively. The 2026 update, therefore, makes no changes to the composition or capital requirements of the list.The RBI had first designated SBI and ICICI Bank as D-SIBs in 2015 and 2016, respectively. HDFC Bank was added to the list in 2017.The framework was subsequently updated on December 28, 2023. Under the framework, foreign banks with a presence in India that are identified as Global Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs) are also required to maintain an additional CET1 capital surcharge in India, proportionate to their Indian risk-weighted assets.
