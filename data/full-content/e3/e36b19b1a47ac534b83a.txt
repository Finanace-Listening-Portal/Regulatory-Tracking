Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Updated On Sep 9, 2026 at 07:01 PM IST var news_msid = 133971151; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Third largest private sector lender Axis Bank’s Managing Director & CEO Amitabh Chaudhry said that the large inflows mobilised through FCNR(B) deposits could put pressure on banks to deploy the funds, potentially leading to “abnormal lending”.Chaudhry said so while discussing the outlook for private investment and credit growth, with bank credit currently growing at around 18-19 per cent.“I think this 18 per cent, 19 per cent also has a base effect coming in. Actually, now it could lead to some abnormal lending. We’ll have to deploy this. I hope they don’t do that, but my view is it will end up at some level,” Chaudhry said while speaking at the Global FinTech Fest 2026 in Mumbai. Advt According to provisional data released by the RBI, banks mobilised USD 127.226 billion through FCNR(B) deposits, while overseas foreign currency borrowings (OFCBs) contributed USD 5.26 billion and external commercial borrowings (ECBs) added USD 3.891 billion.FCNR(B) deposits therefore accounted for 93.3 per cent of the total USD 136.377 billion raised through the three routes under the RBI’s special swap facility.On June 23, 2026, the RBI clarified that Indian banks, including their overseas branches, could extend loans to non-residents and issue SBLCs against FCNR(B) deposits mobilised under the swap facility.Axis Bank CEO said his economists expect private investment to grow at around 15-16 per cent for the year, compared with the current pace of around 18-19 per cent.Banks have mobilised sizeable foreign currency deposits under the RBI’s temporary FCNR(B) measures, creating additional liquidity that lenders will need to deploy.For Axis Bank, strengthening the deposit franchise remains a key priority. Chaudhry said the bank wants to “become a whole other franchise” and continue to build its deposit franchise, particularly by making it more premium.“India is, as I said, an extremely competitive market. But I do believe it presents a huge amount of opportunities for us. And so the second priority is to become a whole other franchise and continue to build our deposit franchise, especially the deposit franchise, and make it more premium as we go along,” he said. Advt The Axis Bank CEO also emphasised that scale remains an advantage in India’s banking market, particularly as lenders compete for growth across businesses.“If you are one of the top four or five banks, you get a disproportionate share of profitability. We happen to be one of them, so we are hoping that as competition increases, banks like us have an opportunity to grow and grab market share in various parts of our business,” Chaudhry said.
