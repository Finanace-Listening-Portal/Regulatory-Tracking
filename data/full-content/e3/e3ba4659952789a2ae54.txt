(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India dismissed the Special Leave Petition filed by Brainlink International, Inc. and another against the Delhi High Court order refusing to condone the delay in filing their written statement in a commercial trademark dispute instituted by HT Media Limited and another. The Supreme Court agreed with the High Court’s reasoning, particularly considering the conduct of the defendants and their continued participation in judicial proceedings even during the COVID-19 pandemic. It declined to interfere with the impugned order in exercise of its discretionary jurisdiction under Article 136 of the Constitution of India. Background of Trademark and Domain Name Dispute HT Media Limited and another instituted a commercial suit seeking restraint against the defendants from infringing their trademark and domain name. The plaintiffs also sought an injunction restraining the defendants from pursuing civil proceedings before the United States District Court for the Eastern District of New York, along with rendition of accounts and damages. The Delhi High Court passed an interim order on 28 April 2020. The defendants received information regarding the institution of the suit and the interim directions through email. They subsequently entered appearance through counsel, filed applications and participated in various proceedings. However, their written statement was filed only on 13 July 2021, approximately 441 days after the interim order. Non-Service of Summons and COVID-19 Limitation Extension The defendants sought condonation of delay under Order VIII Rule 1 read with Section 151 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. They contended that formal summons had never been served upon them and that the prescribed period for filing the written statement had consequently not commenced. They maintained that their appearance was pursuant to information furnished under Order XXXIX Rule 3 CPC and that they genuinely believed that a written statement was required only after formal service of summons. The defendants also relied upon the Supreme Court’s orders in Cognizance For Extension of Limitation, In re. and the Delhi High Court decision in Rohit Sharma Vs A.M. Market Place Pvt. Ltd. They argued that the extraordinary circumstances prevailing during the pandemic and the judicial extension of limitation justified acceptance of their written statement. Active Participation Undermined Explanation for Delay The plaintiffs opposed the application by demonstrating that the defendants had continuously participated in the proceedings through their counsel from May 2020 onwards. The defendants had filed an application seeking vacation of the interim injunction, responded to proceedings alleging violation of interim directions and lodged objections before the Trademark Registry. They had also participated in mediation proceedings, which ultimately failed in June 2021. The High Court observed that the prevailing pandemic conditions had not actually prevented the defendants from communicating with their counsel, filing applications or participating in judicial proceedings. The written statement and the application seeking condonation of delay were filed only after the plaintiffs moved an application seeking summary judgment under Order XIII-A CPC. In these circumstances, the High Court held that the defendants could not obtain the benefit of the Supreme Court’s limitation extension orders on the facts of the present case. Formal Service of Summons and Waiver Through Appearance The High Court acknowledged that although issuance of summons had been directed and the plaintiffs had deposited the requisite process fee, the Registry apparently had not issued formal summons. Nevertheless, relying upon Sunil Poddar Vs Union Bank of India and Flight Center Travels Pvt. Ltd. Vs Flight Centre Limited, the Court emphasised that actual knowledge of proceedings, sufficient opportunity to respond and voluntary appearance through counsel were material considerations in determining whether the absence of formal service could justify the defendants’ conduct. The defendants were fully aware of the commercial nature of the litigation and had been represented by counsel throughout. The High Court therefore rejected the contention that the period for filing the written statement had not commenced merely because formal summons had not been served. It dismissed the condonation application and directed that the delayed written statement should not be taken on record. Supreme Court Declines Interference Under Article 136 The Supreme Court agreed with the Delhi High Court’s view, specifically referring to the defendants’ participation in proceedings even during the pandemic. Considering their conduct and the particular facts and circumstances of the dispute, the Supreme Court found no justification for exercising its jurisdiction under Article 136 of the Constitution and dismissed the Special Leave Petition. Consequently, the High Court’s refusal to condone the delay and accept the written statement remained undisturbed. The Supreme Court’s brief order was based on the facts of the case rather than a general declaration withdrawing COVID-19 limitation protection from every litigant who participated in proceedings during the pandemic. Application No. 665/2021 in SMW(C) No. 3/2020; Order dated 23/09/2021 – Relied upon by the defendants to claim the benefit of extension of limitation during the COVID-19 pandemic. Distinguished by the High Court on the facts of the present case. Rohit Sharma Vs A.M. Market Place Pvt. Ltd. (Delhi High Court); 2021 SCC OnLine Del 3092 – Relied upon by the defendants in support of their application for condonation of delay. Love Chauhan Vs Ajay Kumar Kathuria (Delhi High Court); 2021 SCC OnLine Del 4861 – Relied upon by the plaintiffs while opposing the automatic application of the COVID-19 limitation extension orders. Sagufa Ahmed Vs Upper Assam Polywood Products Private Limited and Others (Supreme Court of India); (2021) 2 SCC 317 – Relied upon by the plaintiffs concerning the distinction between extension of limitation and extension of the period within which delay may be condoned. Bharat Kalra Vs Raj Kishan Chabra (Delhi High Court); 2021 SCC OnLine Del 3976 – Relied upon by the plaintiffs while opposing condonation of delay. Flight Center Travels Pvt. Ltd. Vs Flight Centre Limited (Delhi High Court); 2013 SCC OnLine Del 331 – Followed by the High Court for the principle that voluntary appearance through counsel and knowledge of proceedings may amount to waiver of formal service of summons. Siraj Ahmad Siddiqui Vs Prem Nath Kapoor (Supreme Court of India); (1993) 4 SCC 406 – Referred to in connection with waiver of the requirement of formal service of summons. Nath Agrawal Vs Nath (Allahabad High Court); 1981 SCC OnLine All 445 – Referred to concerning waiver of formal service of summons through voluntary appearance before the court. Sunil Poddar Vs Union Bank of India (Supreme Court of India); (2008) 2 SCC 326 – Applied by analogy by the High Court to emphasise actual knowledge of judicial proceedings and sufficient opportunity to answer the plaintiff’s claim notwithstanding irregularity in service of summons.
