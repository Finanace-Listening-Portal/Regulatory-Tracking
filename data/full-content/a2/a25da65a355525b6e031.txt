Summary: Nippon India Small Cap Fund is an open-ended equity scheme predominantly investing in small-cap stocks. Its current Scheme Information Document identifies scheme code NIMF/O/E/SCF/10/07/0044, a minimum 65% allocation to small-cap-company equity and equity-related securities, and the Nifty Smallcap 250 TRI as the AMFI Tier I benchmark. The scheme is actively managed, so its portfolio can differ materially from the index and can hold other equity, debt and money-market instruments, REITs and InvITs within the disclosed ranges. Current official disclosures classify both the scheme and benchmark at Very High risk. Fresh lump-sum and switch-in flows have been restricted since July 2023. A March 2026 addendum revised the fresh SIP, STP and similar-product limit to ₹2 lakh per transaction or instalment and ₹46 lakh per first-holder or guardian PAN per calendar month, subject to stated exceptions. The current exit load is 1% for redemption or switch-out on or before one year from allotment and nil thereafter. Direct and Regular plans share the portfolio but have different expense structures. This article explains mandate, portfolio construction, NAV, subscription gates, redemption, taxation and evidence without recommending the scheme or using historical performance as a forecast. Key Takeaways Source Links Show all 30 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Current Scheme Identity and Core Mandate The current SID names the scheme Nippon India Small Cap Fund and describes it as an open-ended equity scheme predominantly investing in small-cap stocks. Nippon India Mutual Fund is the mutual fund, Nippon Life India Asset Management Limited is the asset management company, and Nippon Life India Trustee Limited is the trustee company. The scheme code is NIMF/O/E/SCF/10/07/0044. The Regular Plan dates from 16 September 2010, while the Direct Plan began on 1 January 2013. The primary investment objective is long-term capital appreciation through predominant investment in equity and equity-related instruments of small-cap companies. A secondary objective is consistent return generation through debt and money-market securities. The objective is not a promise. Small-cap shares can lose value sharply, trade with less liquidity and take longer to sell at a reasonable price than larger-company shares. Small Cap Has a Regulatory Meaning For the scheme mandate, small-cap stocks are companies below the top 250 by full market capitalisation under the applicable mutual-fund classification framework. This is a relative ranking that changes when market capitalisations move and when the industry list is refreshed. A company can migrate between categories. The fund manager must manage the portfolio so that the scheme remains within its fundamental allocation while handling inflows, redemptions and reclassification. A total-return index incorporates the effect of dividends, unlike a price-only index. The benchmark provides a reference for performance and risk disclosure; it does not prescribe every holding or weight in the scheme. The manager can own fewer or more companies, hold non-small-cap equity within the permitted range, maintain cash or debt, and use derivatives as allowed by the SID. Benchmark Difference Creates Both Opportunity and Risk An actively managed small-cap fund can outperform or underperform the benchmark because of stock selection, sector weights, cash levels, transaction timing, expenses and liquidity. A company that appears attractive on fundamental analysis can still fall because earnings disappoint, governance deteriorates, a promoter pledge becomes stressed or market liquidity disappears. Large assets under management can also make incremental deployment and exit execution harder in less-liquid securities. No past return series establishes future results. Official July 2026 product material identified HDFC Bank, Bharat Heavy Electricals, Apar Industries and Karur Vysya Bank among the largest positions, with individual weights below 2% in that snapshot. The list illustrates diversification rather than a permanent portfolio. The AMC publishes monthly portfolios; an investor reviewing the scheme on 21 October 2026 should use the latest disclosed month instead of assuming those July weights remain unchanged. Fresh Lump-Sum Restrictions and the March 2026 SIP Limits The AMC first limited subscriptions with effect from 7 July 2023. The official scheme page continues to flag that restriction. Fresh or additional lump-sum subscriptions and fresh switch-ins are not treated as generally open merely because the SID shows a minimum application amount. Operational addenda control the available entry route. Existing units remain redeemable subject to normal processing, and the scheme remains open-ended. Fresh SIP, STP and Similar Registrations Notice cum Addendum No. 104 dated 24 March 2026 revised the limit from 30 March 2026. Fresh SIP, STP or similar registrations, whether one or multiple applications, are capped at ₹2,00,000 per transaction or instalment. The overall limit is ₹46,00,000 per PAN of the first holder or guardian per calendar month across all frequencies. If the monthly limit is exhausted, remaining instalments for that month are not processed. Funds realised in a later month count in that later month for the cap. The addendum says the revised limit applies exclusively to fresh SIPs, STPs and similar products. It does not affect registrations made before the effective date or IDCW reinvestment. Regulatory employee-alignment and mandatory AMC contributions are also excluded. Intra-scheme switches between plans were permitted from 30 March 2026 because they do not create new inflow. These exceptions should not be widened to a general fresh lump-sum window. The Direct Plan excludes distributor commission and therefore normally has a lower total expense ratio. The Regular Plan includes distribution-related expenses. Their NAVs differ because expenses are deducted from scheme assets each day. A higher or lower NAV between plans is not by itself evidence that one portfolio owns better securities. TER Is Dynamic and Plan-Specific Total expense ratio covers recurring scheme expenses within the regulatory framework. The AMC publishes daily TER and six-month history through its official TER page. The applicable ratio can change with asset size, statutory limits and approved expenses, and the Direct and Regular figures are different. An investor should capture the TER displayed for the chosen plan on the transaction date rather than relying on an old factsheet or third-party screen. Expenses reduce NAV; they are not billed as a separate annual invoice to the investor. Growth and IDCW Are Distribution Choices Growth retains income and realised gains in the scheme until units are redeemed. IDCW permits distribution from distributable surplus when declared. IDCW is neither fixed interest nor an assured dividend. A payout reduces scheme NAV to the extent of the distribution and applicable effects. Reinvestment applies the distribution to new units and creates additional acquisition records. The investor should verify the available payout or reinvestment option and default rule in the current SID and transaction form. NAV, Cut-Off and Redemption Mechanics Mutual-fund units are allotted or redeemed at the applicable NAV under SEBI’s cut-off and funds-realisation rules. Entering an instruction before a displayed platform deadline does not always establish the applicable NAV if cleared funds reach the scheme later or KYC and mandate checks fail. Third-party platform cut-offs can be earlier than the statutory cut-off. The investor should retain the order time, payment debit, funds-realisation status and AMC allotment confirmation. One-Year Exit Load Applies Lot by Lot The current exit load is 1% if units are redeemed or switched out on or before completion of one year from the date of allotment, and nil thereafter. The March 2024 addendum introduced this structure prospectively for subscriptions and systematic registrations covered by its effective-date rules. Each purchase or SIP instalment has its own allotment date, so one redemption can contain units with different load outcomes. Exit load is separate from capital-gains tax and Securities Transaction Tax. Redemption Payment Is Not Guaranteed on the Order Day The SID states that redemption proceeds for a valid request are ordinarily initiated within three working days, subject to permitted exceptional situations. Incorrect bank details, frozen or pledged units, incomplete transmission, lien, death of a holder, KYC mismatch or operational disruption can delay payment. Small-cap liquidity stress may also affect portfolio execution, although the investor transacts with the scheme at NAV rather than selling each underlying share directly. Riskometer, Liquidity and Portfolio Risks Current official material places both the scheme and its benchmark in the Very High risk category. The Riskometer is a regulatory risk label, not a forecast of the next year’s return or a guarantee of maximum loss. It can change when portfolio attributes change. Investors should read the latest Riskometer, portfolio, stress-test and liquidity disclosures together. Small-Cap Liquidity Can Disappear Quickly A quoted market price does not ensure that a large position can be sold at that price. Small-cap order books can be shallow, price limits can prevent execution, and a market decline can cause many funds to seek liquidity simultaneously. The manager may hold cash, larger-company shares or debt within the mandate, but those choices can create benchmark deviation. Subscription restrictions are a capacity-management decision and are not a signal that future returns will be positive. Derivatives, Lending and Debt Add Separate Exposures The SID permits derivatives within the disclosed framework, securities lending up to scheme limits and debt or money-market instruments. Derivatives can be used for hedging, portfolio balancing or permitted investment exposure, but basis, counterparty, margin and liquidity risks remain. Securities lending can create borrower and collateral risks. Debt introduces credit, duration and reinvestment risks. REITs and InvITs add leverage, distribution and asset-cash-flow exposure. Indian Tax on Redemption, Switches and IDCW Nippon India Small Cap Fund is ordinarily treated as an equity-oriented fund when it satisfies the statutory domestic-equity condition. For transfers on or after the current regime’s effective date, units held for not more than 12 months generally produce short-term capital gain. Where the equity-fund and STT conditions are met, that gain is taxed at 20%, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. Holding for more than 12 months generally produces long-term capital gain. Long-Term Gain Uses the Aggregate ₹1.25 Lakh Threshold Qualifying long-term capital gain on equity-oriented fund units is taxed at 12.5% on the aggregate amount exceeding ₹1.25 lakh for the tax year, subject to the statutory conditions, surcharge and cess. The threshold is shared across qualifying listed equity shares, equity-oriented fund units and business-trust units; it is not a separate ₹1.25 lakh exemption for each fund or folio. Cost must be determined lot by lot, and grandfathering can matter for units acquired before 1 February 2018. Switching Plans or Options Is a Tax Transfer A switch from Regular to Direct, Growth to IDCW, or this scheme to another scheme is generally implemented as redemption of old units and purchase of new units. It can therefore trigger capital gain, exit load and STT even when no cash reaches the bank account. IDCW is taxable in the recipient’s hands at the applicable rate; the payer’s withholding rules and threshold must be checked for the current tax year. Reinvested IDCW does not avoid tax merely because it buys new units.
