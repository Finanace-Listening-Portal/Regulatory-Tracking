Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Mohammed Asif Sumar Vs Superintendent of Central Tax & Another (Telangana High Court) Summary: The Telangana High Court disposed of a writ petition filed by Mohammed Asif Sumar challenging an Order-in-Original dated 8 December 2025 and the consequential FORM GST DRC-07 dated 24 December 2025 relating to FY 2021-22. The order confirmed an Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) demand of ₹2,12,897, together with a penalty of ₹21,290 and a late fee of ₹1,050. The petitioner alleged that the demand had been confirmed mechanically, without adequate reasons, and in violation of Articles 14 and 19(1)(g) of the Constitution. He also challenged the unilateral debit of ₹2,12,897 from his electronic credit ledger on 10 April 2026, contending that the action was without jurisdiction and contrary to the principles of natural justice. The petitioner relied on a similar order passed by a Co-ordinate Bench of the Telangana High Court in W.P. No. 16147 of 2026, dated 23 June 2026, in which liberty had been granted to pursue the statutory appellate remedy with an application for condonation of delay. The Revenue’s counsel did not dispute the applicability of that order. Following the same approach, the High Court disposed of the writ petition and permitted the petitioner to file an appeal before the appellate authority within two weeks, accompanied by the statutory pre-deposit and an application for condonation of delay. The appellate authority was directed to consider the delay application in accordance with law, taking into account the period during which the petitioner had pursued the writ remedy. If satisfied with the explanation for delay, the authority must decide the appeal on merits. The Court did not adjudicate the validity of the GST demand or the electronic credit ledger debit and made no order as to costs. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF TELANGANA HIGH COURT Heard Mr.Mohammed Asrar Ahmed, learned counsel for the petitioner and Mr.D.Raghavendar Rao, learned Senior Standing Counsel for CBIC, for respondent No.1. Perused the record. 2. This writ petition is filed seeking the following relief: “…it is prayed that the Hon’ble Court may be pleased to issue a writ, order or direction more particularly one in the nature of Writ of Mandamus: (a) Setting aside the impugned Order-in-Original No.14/2025- 26(GST)(PGR) dt.08.12.2025 along with FORM GST DRC-07 bearing Ref.No.ZD3612250906762 dt.24.12.2025, confirming demand of IGST of Rs.2,12,897/- along with penalty of Rs.21,290/- and late fee of Rs.1,050/- for the period 2021-22 being passed without reasons, mechanically in nature and violative of Articles 14 and 19(1)(g) of the Constitution of India; (b) Declaring the proceedings of the Respondents in unilaterally debiting the Petitioners electronic credit ledger on 10.04.2026 for an amount of Rs.2,12,897/- as being ultra vires the GST Act, 2017, without jurisdiction, illegal, arbitrary, mechanical in nature and in gross violation of principles of natural justice; (c) and pass any such other order or orders as the Hon’ble Court may deem fit and proper in the circumstances of the case.” 3. Learned counsel for the petitioner submits that in similar circumstances, a Co-ordinate Bench of this Court disposed of WP.No.16147 of 2026, dated 23.06.2026. The operative portion of the order reads as under: “…However, upon hearing the learned counsel for the parties, since the petitioner seeks liberty to prefer an appeal, we do not wish to comment on the merits of the contentions raised by the parties. If the petitioner prefers an appeal within a period of two weeks with statutory pre-deposit along with an application for condonation of delay, the learned appellate authority would consider it in accordance with law also keeping into consideration that it has been pursuing the writ remedy before this Court in the meantime as well. The petitioner will be at liberty to take all the grounds in law and on facts before the appellate authority. If the appellate authority is satisfied that the delay has been properly explained, it shall proceed to decide the case on merits. The writ petition is accordingly disposed of with the aforesaid liberty….” 4. Learned Standing Counsel appearing on behalf of respondent No.1 does not dispute the same. 5. In view of the same, for the reasons alike in the aforesaid order, dated 23.06.2026, the present Writ Petition also stands disposed of, with liberty to the petitioner to file an appeal before the appellate authority within a period of two weeks, along with the statutory pre-deposit and an application for condonation of delay. The learned appellate authority shall consider the said application in accordance with law, taking into consideration the fact that the petitioner has been pursuing the writ remedy before this Court in the meantime. The petitioner is at liberty to raise all the grounds, both on facts and in law, before the appellate authority. If the appellate authority is satisfied with the reasons assigned for the delay, it shall proceed to decide the appeal on merits, in accordance with law. No order as to costs. Miscellaneous petitions pending, if any, shall stand closed.
