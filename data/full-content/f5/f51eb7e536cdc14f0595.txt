We note that the assessment proceedings began towards the fag end of the limitation period and were completed in a short time frame. Therefore, there is adequate reason to suggest that the assessee did not have the necessary opportunities to present all the material in support of its contentions during the limited time available to it for assessment. We note that the said additional evidence had been forwarded to the ld. Assessing Officer but the ld. Assessing Officer had contended that the same was not admissible and proceeded to do a cursory examination of the same and send the remand report, without causing a detailed investigation during remand proceedings as were required of him. There is merit in the assessee’s case that while sending the additional evidences to the ld. Assessing Officer, the ld. CIT(A) had requested that the assessee be provided an opportunity before sending of the comments and at that stage, had the ld. Assessing Officer not been satisfied with the additional evidences produced, he had the opportunity to ask the assessee to provide the further clarifications required to satisfy him that the loans stood explained as did the creditworthiness of the lenders. However, this opportunity was not provided and it having not been provided, could not be a ground for the Department to prohibit the assessee to furnish these clarifications to the ld. CIT(A) once a negative remand report had been sent by the ld. Assessing Officer. We are in agreement with the assessee that the ld. CIT(A) cannot be expected to seek the comments of the ld. Assessing Officer on every submission made by the assessee in rebuttal to the remand report, otherwise the proceedings would be stuck in an endless loop. We note that the Department has itself acknowledged Court decisions which hold that the powers of the CIT(A) are coterminous with that of the Assessing Officer. In fact, it is acknowledged that the ld. CIT(A) steps into the shoes of the Assessing Officer. He can do what the AO can do and he can do what the AO did not do. Accordingly, we hold that there was no infirmity on the part of the ld. CIT(A) in entertaining the arguments and facts placed before him by the assessee. While doing so, we note that during the assessment year in question, there was no obligation upon the assessee to explain the source of the funds mobilized by the lenders. To allay any doubts regarding the creditworthiness of its lenders the assessee even undertook to walk that extra mile. We note that the ld. Assessing Officer has not done any meaningful enquiry in the said case. Probably due to the paucity of time, he did not issue any notices to any of the lenders to examine them or their affairs before concluding on the basis of their IT returns, that they did not have the creditworthiness to advance the loans that they had. However, there were no such time constraints imposed upon him in the remand proceedings. Once the matter had been referred back to him by the ld. CIT(A), the ld. Assessing Officer could have undertaken that enquiry to prove this suspicion that the creditworthiness of the lenders was doubtful. However, he did not do so and the ld. Assessing Officer having twice failed to conduct the necessary enquiries, could not thereafter raise questions upon the genuineness of transactions reflected in the lenders bank account to question the creditworthiness of the lenders and thereby the loans itself. As the assessee has demonstrated before the ld. CIT(A) and the ld. CIT(A) has pointed out, not only were the loans repaid but they were repaid alongwith interest and tax was deducted at source on such interest, which was subsequently deposited into Central Government account. Thus, there appears to be no material whatsoever in the hands of the ld. Assessing Officer, whereby he can disprove the elaborate documentation produced by the assessee to demonstrate the genuineness of the transaction and the creditworthiness of the lenders. We also reject the plea of the ld. CIT DR to restore the matter back to the file of the ld. Assessing Officer for examination of these matters, noting that the ld. Assessing Officer had two previous opportunities to do the same, which he did not exercise. Assessment proceedings cannot be allowed to become an endless exercise in which the Department first fails to examine the matter in detail despite having been afforded an opportunity to do so, and thereafter seeks to examine the same matter afresh. After considering his detailed order, we cannot find any infirmity in the decision of the ld. CIT(A) to delete the additions made by the ld. Assessing Officer as the case of each one of the creditors has been examined on the facts and it has been concluded that they had the necessary creditworthiness to make the advances of loans to the assessee. He has also found that the transactions to be genuine as evidenced by their movement through banking channels, the payment of interest on the same and the deduction of tax at source on the same. We note the absence of any cash trail that might suggest otherwise. We, therefore, confirm the action of the ld. CIT(A) in deleting the additions made by the ld. Assessing Officer. However, we note that the ld. CIT(A) has not deleted the addition on account of loans and advances received from Smt. Asha Devi. We observe that the assessee had every opportunity to prove the creditworthiness of the said lender before the ld. CIT(A) and was unable to do so. No fresh material has been brought on record before us that would convince us that the ld. CIT(A) was unjustified in treating the credits received from Smt. Asha Devi to be unexplained in the hands of the assessee on account of the fact that Smt. Asha Devi lacked the creditworthiness to advance the amount of loan that she had. Accordingly, we cannot find any fault in the order of the ld. CIT(A) in sustaining the addition of Rs. 10,00,000/- made on this account and we confirm his actions in this regard. 18. On the legal grounds raised by the assessee in his cross objection, we hold that the assessee has not been able to make out a case of any irregularity in the procedure adopted by the Department while doing assessment of the case of the assessee for the assessment year in question. As has been elaborately brought out by the ld. CIT DR, there was ample time left for the Department to issue a notice under section 143(2) and therefore, the Department did not feel the need to issue notice under section 148 prior to issuing the notice under section 143(2) as an assessable return already existed on its files for assessment. It must be remembered that a notice under section 148 is issued for the purposes of obtaining a return from an assessee whose income has escaped assessment within the meaning of section 147. If there is still time available to issue a notice under section 143(2) and assess the income of an assessee for a particular year, there can be no requirement to first issue a notice under section 148 obtain another return and then issue a notice under section 143(2), if an assessable return already existed on the files of the Department. Furthermore, it has been pointed out by the CIT DR with reference to the orders of the ld. CIT(A), that the notice under section 143(2) was issued with the approval of the PCIT and the orders that were subsequently passed were passed after obtaining the approval of the ld. Addl CIT Range and hence there is no merit in ground no. 1 of the assessee’s cross objection which allege the assessment order to have been passed without following due process of law. Accordingly, it is dismissed. 19. In the result, the Departmental appeal in ITA No.308/LKW/2025 and the assessee’s Cross Objection No. 47/LKW/2025 are both held to be dismissed.
