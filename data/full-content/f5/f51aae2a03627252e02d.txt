Its current official materials identify Nifty 500 TRI as the benchmark, a very-high riskometer, a ₹100 minimum and an exit load of 1% for redemption or switch-out on or before 90 days, then nil. Dated AMC disclosures should be used for AUM, TER and portfolio rather than treating a historical snapshot as permanent. This article explains the scheme’s mandate, plan and option distinctions, costs, transaction evidence, risk and Indian equity-oriented fund taxation without predicting returns or recommending investment. Key Takeaways Show all 24 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Current Scheme Identity, Mandate and Benchmark The current official name is Aditya Birla Sun Life Flexi Cap Fund, managed by Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC Limited. It is described as an open-ended dynamic equity scheme investing across large-cap, mid-cap and small-cap stocks. The investment objective is long-term capital growth through equity and equity-related securities, but the scheme documents do not guarantee achievement. Units are interests in the mutual-fund scheme, not deposits of the AMC, the sponsor or a bank, and principal and returns can fluctuate. Flexi-cap permits allocation changes across market capitalisations The category requires a predominantly equity portfolio, while the fund manager can vary the mix among large, mid and small companies within the scheme’s stated asset-allocation limits and current SEBI categorisation rules. The label does not promise a permanently balanced allocation or a lower-risk portfolio. A high large-cap allocation in one month and a higher mid- or small-cap allocation later can both fit a flexi-cap mandate, so investors should use dated portfolio disclosures when assessing what they actually own. The scheme’s benchmark is Nifty 500 Total Return Index. A total-return index includes dividend reinvestment assumptions and is the more relevant comparison than a price-only index, but benchmark selection is not a guarantee of matching or outperforming it. The fund is actively managed and can differ materially in stock weights, sector exposure, cash and turnover. These are dated disclosures, not fixed contractual numbers. Daily net assets, permitted additional expenses, transaction costs, taxes and regulatory caps can affect the actual total expense ratio. An editorial or investor record should preserve the disclosure date alongside every AUM or TER figure. TER is deducted inside NAV The total expense ratio is charged to the scheme and reflected in the NAV; it is not normally collected as a separate invoice from each unitholder. A higher or lower TER affects NAV over time, but TER alone does not establish suitability or future performance. Direct and regular plans hold the same scheme portfolio yet have different expense structures because the regular plan includes distribution-related costs. The investor’s selected plan is therefore a material fact in every purchase and return calculation. Portfolio data changes after every disclosure date The AMC reported 83 securities in its July 2026 snapshot. That count and individual holdings may change through purchases, sales, corporate actions and cash management. Sector and stock concentration should be read from the latest monthly portfolio file, with derivatives, cash, debt and receivables considered where disclosed. A factsheet is a dated transparency document, not a permanent promise to hold any named company. Minimums can be changed by addendum and may differ for special facilities. A transaction is not complete merely because a payment instruction succeeds; acceptance, valid application, KYC, funds realisation and the applicable cut-off framework determine the allotment and NAV date. Direct and regular are plans; growth and IDCW are options A direct plan is purchased without a distributor commission embedded in plan expenses, while a regular plan uses the distribution route. Growth retains distributable surplus within the option’s NAV. An IDCW option may distribute income at the trustees’ discretion, subject to available distributable surplus and applicable rules, and the NAV ordinarily falls to the extent of the payout and statutory adjustments. IDCW is not interest and is not assured. The holding period is evaluated lot by lot. A SIP creates separate allotment lots, so the exit-load clock for each instalment begins on its own allotment date. A partial redemption normally follows the scheme’s stated unit-allocation method, and investors should verify the folio statement rather than assuming the oldest tax lot or desired lot was selected. A switch is a redemption plus a purchase Switching from one scheme or plan to another generally involves redemption of the source units and purchase of destination units. That can trigger exit load and capital-gains consequences even when no cash reaches the bank account. A regular-to-direct switch within the same named scheme can likewise be a taxable redemption and fresh allotment. The destination NAV follows its applicable transaction and funds-realisation rules. Settlement is subject to validation and banking systems A valid redemption is processed under the scheme and regulatory settlement framework, but liens, incomplete KYC, name or bank mismatches, transmission, frozen folios and exceptional market conditions can delay or restrict payment. Unitholders should keep the confirmation, unit and NAV details, exit-load computation, tax statement and bank credit evidence. A failed payment instruction should be resolved through the AMC or registrar rather than by submitting repeated conflicting requests. Equity, Concentration and Operational Risks The scheme’s very-high riskometer is consistent with material equity-market risk. Large-cap shares can fall, while mid- and small-cap shares may add liquidity, valuation and execution risk. Active market-cap shifts can help or hurt relative performance. Sector concentration, stock selection, style exposure, corporate governance events and cash levels can cause the scheme to differ from its benchmark and category peers. None of these risks is eliminated by a long operating history or high AUM. Fund, benchmark and investor returns are three different numbers Published scheme performance uses prescribed assumptions and a specified plan and date. The benchmark has no expense ratio identical to the scheme and cannot be purchased in the same form. An investor’s realised return depends on transaction dates, plan, option, NAV, cash flows, exit load and tax. Past performance tables should therefore be read as historical disclosure, not as a forecast, target or ranking. Verify the current portfolio and riskometer rather than relying on a dated marketing page. Compare the selected plan and option with the application and folio statement. Track every SIP allotment as a separate lot for load and tax holding periods. Review concentration, liquidity and style exposure alongside headline AUM. Use the AMC or registrar grievance route for transaction and statement mismatches. Indian Tax Treatment and Recordkeeping Where the scheme satisfies the statutory equity-oriented fund conditions for the relevant period, unit transfers are generally taxed under the special equity provisions. For current transactions, short-term gains on qualifying equity-oriented units are generally taxed at 20%, while qualifying long-term gains are generally taxed at 12.5% above the applicable aggregate exemption threshold of ₹1.25 lakh, subject to the Income-tax Act, 2025, transaction date, surcharge, cess and taxpayer facts. A tax professional should confirm the current provisions before filing. Holding period and cost are lot specific Equity-oriented fund units generally become long-term after the applicable twelve-month holding period, but each allotment date must be tested separately. Purchase NAV and stamp duty form part of the evidence; an exit load reduces redemption proceeds through the NAV or transaction computation and should be retained. Switches, systematic transfer plans and plan changes can create redemptions. Set-off and carry-forward of losses depend on the nature of the loss, timely filing and current law. IDCW and TDS questions are separate from capital gains IDCW is generally taxable in the unitholder’s hands under the applicable income provisions, and withholding may apply when statutory conditions and thresholds are met. Resident and non-resident taxation, treaty claims and withholding differ. Investors should preserve the consolidated account statement, AMC capital-gains statement, bank records, Form 26AS or applicable tax statement, and transaction confirmations, then reconcile units and tax credits rather than relying on a single app dashboard. No. It can vary allocations across large-, mid- and small-cap shares within its mandate and current regulatory framework. Dated portfolio disclosures show the actual mix. 2. What is the current benchmark? The official scheme information identifies Nifty 500 Total Return Index as the benchmark. It is a comparison standard, not a guaranteed return. 3. What exit load applies? The current AMC page states 1% for redemption or switch-out on or before 90 days from allotment and nil after 90 days. Each allotment lot is tested separately. 4. Are the July 2026 AUM and TER figures permanent? No. The ₹28,112.33 crore AUM and 1.36% regular/0.71% direct base TER are dated July 2026 snapshots and must not be presented as permanent or current-day values. 5. Does an IDCW option pay assured income? No. IDCW declarations depend on distributable surplus and trustee decisions under applicable rules. A payout generally reduces NAV and is not interest. 6. Is a regular-to-direct switch tax neutral? It generally involves redemption of source units and fresh allotment, so exit load and capital-gains consequences may arise even without a cash payment to the investor. 7. Which records are most important for tax filing? Keep allotment-wise dates, units, plan and option, purchase and redemption NAVs, stamp duty, exit load, switch details, consolidated statements, tax-credit records and bank evidence.
