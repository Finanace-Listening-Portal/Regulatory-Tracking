Assessment was framed by the Assessing Officer, NFAC for the assessment year 2013-14 under Section 147 read with Section 144B of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’) vide order dated 27 March, 2023. 2. The only issue raised in this appeal of the assessee is the order of learned CIT(A) confirming the action of the Assessing Officer in making addition of 16 crores as remaining call money on partly paid up shares of %8 crores to M/s Quadrel Infrastructure Private Limited and %8 crores to M/s Winfield Iron Steel Private Limited by treating the same as unexplained cash credit under Section 68 of the Act. For this, the learned Counsel for the assessee drew our attention to ground No.4 of the appeal, which reads as under:- “4. That the order dated 26.02.2026 passed U/s 250 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)-29, New Delhi is against law and facts on the file in as much as he was not justified to uphold the action of the Learned ACIT, Central Circle-25, New Delhi in making an addition of Rs.16,00,00,000/- (Rs. 8 Crores each) as remaining call money on partly paid up shares to M/s Quadrel Infrastructure Private Limited and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Private Limited by treating the same as, alleged unexplained cash credits u/s 68 of the Act on the ground that the Appellant Company has failed to offer the details requested with substantiating evidence/proofs to the satisfaction of the Ld. Assessing Officer.” 3. We have heard the submissions of both the sides and perused the material placed before us. We noted that original assessment was completed under Section 143(3) of the Act vide order dated 28″ March, 2016 after making an addition of =16 crores under Section 68 of the Act being share capital received by the assessee from Landsky Real Estate (P) Ltd. and Quadrel Infrastructure (P) Ltd. This addition was deleted finally by the Tribunal vide order dated 18″ March, 2021 in ITA No.510/Del/2019 by Bench-C of ITAT, Delhi. Subsequently, a notice under Section 148 of the Act was issued regarding purchase of shares from Quadrel Infrastructure (P) Ltd. and Winfield Iron and Steel Pvt.Ltd. for an amount of %16 crores in aggregate. The relevant reasons recorded under Section 148A(d) of the Act dated 29° July, 2022 read as under:- “1. Facts of the case The assessee has filed his Return of Income on 22.09.2013 for the Assessment Year 2013-14 declaring income of Rs.2,47,053/- further case was assessed u/s 143(3) of the IT Act vide dated 28.03.2016. 2. Information received Information has been received from the office of DDIT (inv.), Unit-1(1), New Delhi through insight portal of the department informing that M/s Bhushaan Steel Limited has received huge fund transfers from various companies namely M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt. M/s Quadrel Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd., M/s Xtended Power Pvt. Ltd., M/s Excellent lacement Services Pvt. Ltd., M/s Kasper Information Technology Pvt. Ltd., M/s Houston Buildwell Pvt. Ltd. and M/s Boost Minerals Mining Pvt. Ltd. It has also been alleged that these companies neither have business activities nor have any income, as they are meant for passing fund through 3? to 4 layers of their group companies. Further it has also alleged that there are 3-5 persons who are directors in all these conduit companies and the ultimate beneficiary of these transactions is Sh. Brij Bhushan Singhal. During AY 2013-14 assessee company purchased share of Rs. 16 crores from M/s Qudrel Infrasturcture Pvt. Ltd. and M/s Winfileld Iron Steel Pvt. Ltd. Rs 8 Cr. from each. How a company having very little or no income, purchased share on huge premium? 2.2 It was also informed that on analyzing the ITRs of above mentioned concerns, it is seen that these entities are investing in M/s LandSky Real States Pvt. Ltd. without there being any apparent sources of funds for such investments, but the bogus share capital generated through issue of unquoted shares at by routing through these bogus entities and thereby facilitating these high value transactions, one such company is the assessee company. Further, M/s LandSky Real State Pvt. has then made investments in M/s Bhusan Steel Limited so the ultimate beneficiary of these investments is M/s Bhusan Steel Limited and Sh. Brij Bhushan Singhal. 2.3 M/s Qudrel Infrasturcture Pvt. Ltd. has purchased shares of M/s LandSky Real States Pvt. Ltd. and M/s Kasper Information Technology Pvt. Ltd. Further, M/s LandSky Real States Pvt. Ltd. has purchased shares of M/s Winfield tron Steel Pvt & Kasper Information Technology Pvt. Ltd. 2.4 On perusal of the audited financials of the companies provided it was seen that all the above share transactions was done at par value (at face value of the share). There was no share premium given in the purchase of these shares. However, on analyzing the ITRs of these entities, it is seen that there is little business of these companies does not justify the high share value of these companies. Further, the uniform amounts in the transactions also point that the same transactions have been done as part of money routing through the entities with insignificant real worth. 3. Further, in compliance to the judgment dated 04.05.2022 of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Union of India & Ors. Vs Ashish Agarwal (Civil Appeal No. 3005/2022) and in accordance with the CBDT’s directions issued vide Instruction No. 01/2022 dated 11.05.2022, the assessee was provided with the information/ material relied upon by this office which suggests that income for the relevant year has escaped assessment, and was given an opportunity to file its response. 4. In accordance with the aforesaid judgment, the assessee was provided with the information/material relied upon, to furnish reply regarding why reassessment u/s 147 of the 1.T. Act shall not be made in the case, on the basis of information which suggests that income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment in this case for the A.Y. 2013-14. 5. In response to opportunity provided to the assessee, the assessee submitted the response which is placed on record. In the reply, the assessee has furnished that: (i) There are no circumstances suggesting that any income chargeable to tax in the case of the assessee has escaped assessment. All transactions are genuine and through banking channel. There is no ground prima facie to prove the transactions as not genuine. (ii) There is no information within meaning of Explanation of section 148 which suggest that income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment and the information received from Investigation Wing cannot be termed as valid information. (iti) No information/material provided to assessee. (iv) Notice is barred by limitation of time. The transaction is not covered under definition of assets as per section 149(1)(b) of the IT Act. (v) Reassessment is based on change of opinion as the case of the assessee has been completed u/s 143(3) of the IT Act vide order dated 28.03.2016 u/s 143(3) of the IT Act. 6. Reply of the assessee has been considered u/s 148A(c) of the Act and is found that (i) & (ii) The assessee’s objection fails on the basic fact that the case of the assessee is proposed to be reopened on basis of prima facie evidence received in form of the information from office of DDIT (inv.), Unit-1(1), New Dethi. In the information, as also communicated to the assessee, it is clearly mentioned and also admitted by the assesse in its reply that during AY 2013-14 assessee company purchased share of Rs. 16 crores from M/s Qudrel Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd, and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt. Ltd. Rs 8 Cr. from each. In view of the same, this argument raised by the assessee is denied and rejected in view of the fact that the assessee has itself admitted to have dealt with the entities M/s Qudrel Infrastructure Pvt.Ltd. and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt.Ltd. during AY 2013-14 assessee company purchased share of Rs.16 crores from M/s Qudre/ Infrastructure Pvt.Ltd. and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt.Ltd. Rs.8 Cr. from each. Even if the assessee has shown the bogus transactions in its financials, it forms the part of modus operandi in such cases where entries/unaccounted income in form of bogus sales and purchases of unquoted shares are done and unaccounted income in form of bogus share capital is generated by rotation of unaccounted monies within the bogus entities for benefit of final beneficiary which in this case is Bhushan Stee/ Ltd. and Sh. Brij Bhushan Singhal. Therefore, the assessee cannot escape the Income Tax provisions attached with such transactions prescribed in the extent provisions of the Act. These details were unearthed by investigation wing of the department in due course. Moreover, the assessee cannot be absolved from these incriminating findings of the investigation wing and this office as the Investigation Wing of the Department has gathered information of the beneficiaries (including assessee), which has made illicit purchases of shares of other bogus entities M/s Qudrel Infrastructure Pvt.Ltd. and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt.Ltd. operating in connivance with each other and accommodated unaccounted Rs.16 crores in their books of accounts. These details are not without any substantial proof, as these were identified on best effort basis through laborious, prolonged and herculean banking and physical enquiries. Therefore, the assessee cannot escape these proceedings by making these vague arguments as it is very well established that the assessee has made bogus purchases of shares of other bogus entities M/s Qudrel Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt. Ltd. (iii) This objection raised by the assessee also fails as it appears that the Assessee has mistaken the opportunity for re-opening of the case with actual assessment proceeding u/s 143(3). Assessee is also not conscious of the facts that the evidences which it has demanded they will be provided during the course of assessment proceeding if, used against the assessee. Assessee has presumed that by demanding these materials it will get some kind of defence which is not tenable. Under IT Act, evidences provided by designated Authority online is acceptable evidences. (iv) Reply filed by the Assessee on limitation of time for issue of notice u/s 148 has been duly considered and found not tenable. The Hon’ble Supreme Court (supra) has upheld the views of Hon’ble High Courts that the benefit of new law shall be made available even in respect of proceedings relating to past A.Ys. Decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court read with the time extension provided by Taxation and Other Laws (Relaxation and amendment of certain provisions) Act, 2020 (TOLA) will allow extended reassessment notices to travel back in time to their original date when such notices were to be issued and then new section 149 of the Act is to be applied at that point. In this case, it is apparent that the assessee was involved in these bogus share transactions as the assessee itself has admitted to have made these transactions which further makes the findings of investigation wing relevant and hence, it is revealed for the present case that income in form of asset as prescribed in definition of “asset” i.e. shares and securities (purchase of share from M/s Qudre/ Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. and M/s Winfileld lron Steel Pvt. Ltd. (Rs 8 Cr. from each) as per section 149(1)(b) of the IT Act has escaped assessment for the year under consideration. Therefore, the objections of the assessee are hereby rejected. (v) The assessee has claimed that the Income Tax assessment has been completed in the case without any adverse inference and action u/s 148 of the IT Act as the same is based on change of opinion is not valid is denied on fact that this information came to fight after investigation in the matter by the Department and the present action being taken has complete merit based on the information in hand in respect of the assessee. In addition to above, it is further submitted that the information on basis of which the case of the assessee is proposed to be reopened is based on prima facie material on record and information in hand. Also, sufficiency or correctness of the facts is not a thing to be considered at this stage, however, in this case it is apparent that the assessee has deliberately held transactions with the bogus entities to adjust its unaccounted income for ultimate beneficiary. Also, the issue based on which the assessment of the case of the assessee for AY 2013-14 has been completed is completely different. The issue on which the addition has been made in order passed u/s 143(3) of the IT Act vide order dated 28.03.2016 is of sale of its shares to LandSky Real Estate (P) Ltd. and Quadre/ Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. for 16 crores (8 crores each). Therefore, it is crystal clear that this amount is nothing but routing of the unaccounted money through these companies in guise of bogus share capital and share premium, which is also evident from the report of investigation wing. However, the present case is reopened on issue of purchase of share from M/s Quadrel Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt. Ltd. (Rs & Cr. from each). Therefore, the assessee’s contention fails and is denied. 7. In view of the above, reply submitted by the assessee has been considered u/s 148A(c) and prima facie found not on merit as per facts and information available with this office (which has already been communicated to the assessee) and it is established that the assessee has no proper explanation regarding transactions as discussed above. Accordingly, it is concluded that this is a fit case for issuing notice u/s 148 of the I.T. Act. 8. Further, the above-mentioned information suggests that the transaction amounting to Rs.16,00,00,000/- in the A.Y. 2013-14,which is more than threshold amount of Rs.50 lacs, has escaped assessment. Further, it is evident that such income is represented in the form of “asset” (shares and securities) as per section 149(1)(b) of the Income Tax Act. This order is being passed with the prior approval of Principal Chief Commissioner of Income Tax, Delhi. Notice u/s 148 of the I.T.Act is issued along with this order.” 4. Consequently, the assessment was framed under Section 147 read with Section 144B of the Act by making addition towards purchase of shares from Quadrel Infrastructure (P) Ltd. and Winfield Iron and Steel Pvt.Ltd. for an aggregate amount of %16 crores under Section 68 of the Act. Learned CIT(A) also confirmed the action of the Assessing Officer by observing in paragraph No.6 as under:- “6. Ground Nos.1 to 3 ; The brief facts of this case are that the appellant filed its return of income declaring total income of Rs.2,47,050/-. Subsequently, information was received by the AO leading to formation of belief that income chargeable to tax had escaped assessment within the meaning of section 147 of the Act. Accordingly, notice u/s 148 was issued and assessment was completed u/s 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act on 27.05.2023 determining total income at Rs.16,02,47,050/-. The AO made addition of Rs.16,00,00,000/- being remaining call money on partly paid-up shares in the names of M/s Quadrel Infrastructure Pvt.Ltd. and M/s Winfield Iron Stee/ Pvt.Ltd. by treating the same as unexplained cash credits u/s 68 of the Act, as the appellant failed to furnish requisite details and documentary evidences to substantiate the nature and source of the credits. 6.1 During the assessment proceedings, the AO issued statutory notices calling for details such as confirmation of parties, financial statements, bank statements and other supporting evidences to establish identity, creditworthiness and genuineness of the transactions. However, the appellant failed to furnish the requisite details to the satisfaction of the AO. Consequently, the AO, after recording reasons and following due procedure u/s 147/148 of the Act, completed the reassessment and made the impugned addition u/s 68 of the Act. 6.2 During the appellate proceedings, in spite of numerous opportunities, no justifications/evidences have been filed by the appellant to controvert the findings and additions made by the AO. It is seen that the AO has given detailed findings in the assessment order which have not been disputed by the appellant by way of any submissions in support of the grounds of appeal. In view of the same, I do not find any reasons to interfere with the assessment order passed by the AO. Accordingly, the addition made by the AO is hereby confirmed. Accordingly, these grounds of appeal are dismissed.” Aggrieved, now the assessee is in appeal before the Tribunal. 5. We noted from the arguments made by the learned Counsel for the assessee that the assessee has not received any credits in its bank accounts or the books of account. Rather, the assessee has made payment i.e., purchase of shares out of its books of account. The Assessing Officer simplicitor applied the provisions of Section 68 of the Act for payments made for purchase of shares as is evident from the assessment order and more so when the Assessing Officer reopened the assessment for that very reason that the assessee has made payment for purchase of shares which is not required to be added under Section 68 of the Act. Learned Counsel for the assessee explained before us that the provisions of Section 68 are very clear which apply for cash credit and not for expenditure for purchase of shares, be it capital or revenue. We noted that the assessee has simplicitor made purchase of shares which is recorded by the Assessing Officer in its reasons reproduced above at paragraph No.6, which is being reproduced again for the sake of clarity and, the relevant portion reads as “….In the information, as also communicated to the assessee, it is clearly mentioned and also admitted by the assessee in its reply that during AY 2013-14 assessee company purchased share of Rs.16 crores from M/s Qudrel Infrastructure Pvt.Ltd. and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt.Ltd. Rs.8 Cr. from each. In view of the same, this argument raised by the assessee is denied and rejected in view of the fact that the assessee has itself admitted to have dealt with the entities M/s Qudrel Infrastructure Pvt.Ltd. and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt.Ltd. during AY 2013-14 assessee company purchased share of Rs.16 crores from M/s Qudrel Infrastructure Pvt.Ltd. and M/s Winfield Iron Steel Pvt.Ltd. Rs. 8 Cr. from each.. Admittedly, the amounts invested in purchase of shares are also explained, whereby the Tribunal in earlier round vide order dated 18″ March, 2021, has already deleted the addition of share capital received from Landsky Real Estate (P) Ltd. and Quadrel Infrastructure (P) Ltd. Hence, nothing survives in this case and accordingly, the addition made by the Assessing Officer and confirmed by the learned CIT(A) is deleted. 6. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed. Decision pronounced in the open Court on 18th November, 2026.
