Summary : The Open Network continues to use the name TON for the blockchain, but its official media materials now identify Gram as the native token and GRAM as the ticker, replacing the earlier Toncoin or TON token name. That distinction matters when reading older wallet screens, exchange records and tax statements. Gram pays network fees, can participate in staking and is transferred through TON messages between accounts. A wallet is a smart contract rather than the account itself, so its code version, sequence number, expiry settings and signing controls affect transaction safety. Validators interact with TON system contracts, and governance can alter configuration; neither staking nor technical activity promises a return. For an Indian taxpayer, a transfer can fall within the virtual digital asset rules: section 115BBH generally taxes income from transfer at 30% plus surcharge and cess, restricts deductions and loss set-off, while section 194S may require 1% TDS above the applicable threshold. The renamed token remains volatile and is not legal tender or an RBI-approved investment. This article explains the current identity, transaction mechanics, operational risks and records needed for tax compliance without recommending purchase, trading or staking. Why the rename must not be treated as a second asset A branding or ticker change does not by itself create a new economic acquisition or disposal. Tax treatment depends on the legal and factual transaction, not the label alone. Holders should retain official rename evidence, exchange notices, wallet addresses, quantities and before-and-after statements. If an intermediary implemented a swap, migration or new deposit address, the exact event must be analysed rather than assumed to be tax-neutral. TON Accounts, Wallet Contracts and Messages TON is message-driven. Accounts hold state and receive messages; transactions record the processing of those messages. A user-facing wallet is a smart contract that checks a signature and other conditions before sending internal messages. Different wallet versions can implement different features. The official wallet documentation describes controls such as a sequence number, a validity deadline and a wallet or subwallet identifier. These fields help prevent replay or misrouting, but they do not protect a user who signs a malicious payload or exposes a private key. Sequence numbers, expiry and wallet identifiers The sequence number, or seqno, normally advances after an accepted external message. The valid-until field limits how long a signed request can remain usable. A wallet identifier helps separate wallet instances derived from related key material. Before signing, a user should confirm the network, wallet contract, recipient, amount, attached message, expected fee and any application-specific call. A rejected or expired external message is not the same as a successful internal transfer. Validators, System Contracts and Staking Exposure TON validators propose and validate blocks while system contracts coordinate network configuration and validator selection. The Elector contract manages validator elections and stakes; the Config contract stores network parameters. Official documentation describes governance through validator voting on configuration proposals, with weighted thresholds. These mechanics are protocol controls, not an assurance that validator participation is decentralised or that a proposal will improve token value. Staking does not create a fixed return A holder may encounter direct validation, pools, liquid-staking arrangements or custodial products. Each has a different contract, operator, commission, liquidity and counterparty structure. Rewards can vary with validator performance, network parameters, pool terms and participation. Lockups, election cycles and withdrawal processing can delay access. A liquid-staking receipt is a separate contract asset and can diverge from the value or redemption availability of underlying Gram. Users should identify who controls validator keys, how slashing or penalties are handled, whether code is upgradeable, how withdrawals are queued and what happens if an operator or interface becomes unavailable. A service describing an annual percentage does not turn a variable protocol outcome into interest guaranteed by an issuer. Custody, Applications and Transaction Evidence Self-custody concentrates responsibility for seed phrases, devices, backups and recovery. Hosted custody adds insolvency, withdrawal, cybersecurity, sanctions and compliance risks. Telegram-linked or embedded interfaces may simplify interaction but can also make phishing, impersonation and approval confusion harder to detect. A verified application name does not prove that every bot, mini app, domain or token contract using that name is authentic. Records an Indian holder should retain Records should show acquisition date, quantity, INR cost, exchange or counterparty, wallet addresses, hashes, network fees, transfers between owned wallets, staking deposits and withdrawals, reward credits, disposals and tax deducted. Historic Toncoin or TON labels should be reconciled to current Gram terminology. The evidence should distinguish internal wallet movements from transfers to another person and separate native Gram from jettons or wrapped assets. Indian VDA Tax and TDS for Gram Transfers A cryptographically secured token represented on a distributed ledger can fall within India’s virtual digital asset definition. Section 115BBH generally taxes income from transfer of a VDA at 30% plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. Only cost of acquisition, if any, is deductible under that special computation; other expenditure is disallowed. A loss from transfer of one VDA cannot generally be set off against other income, including gains from another VDA, and cannot be carried forward under the provision. Section 194S is withholding, not the final tax Section 194S generally requires deduction of tax at 1% from consideration paid to a resident for transfer of a VDA when the applicable annual threshold is crossed. The threshold is generally ₹50,000 for a specified person and ₹10,000 for other payers. Exchange mechanics and consideration in kind can affect who deducts and how the obligation is met. TDS must be reconciled with the final return; it does not substitute for calculating acquisition cost, transfer income and applicable tax. Staking rewards, gifts, airdrops, business inventory, non-resident transactions and token migrations can raise separate questions about timing, character and valuation. The Gram rename should not be used to duplicate cost or conceal a taxable transfer. Technical activity, exchange listing or reporting-entity registration does not mean RBI approval, legal-tender status or investment protection. Frequently Asked Questions Is Gram a different blockchain from TON? No. TON remains the network name. Official materials now call its native coin Gram with ticker GRAM, formerly Toncoin or TON. Does the rename itself create taxable income in India? A label change alone is not necessarily a transfer, but any swap, migration, counterparty exchange or change in beneficial ownership must be analysed from the actual records. Is a TON wallet the same as an account? A wallet is a smart contract operating at an account address. Its code checks signatures and transaction controls before sending messages. Can a sent message be assumed successful? No. Confirm the transaction and resulting message trace on a reliable explorer or node because submission, execution and downstream delivery are distinct. Does staking Gram guarantee an annual return? No. Outcomes depend on validator performance, network parameters, pool contracts, commissions, lockups and counterparty or smart-contract risk. How is transfer income generally taxed? Section 115BBH generally applies a 30% rate plus surcharge and cess, permits only cost of acquisition and restricts loss set-off and carry-forward. When can 1% TDS apply? Section 194S can apply to consideration paid to a resident for a VDA transfer after the relevant ₹50,000 or ₹10,000 annual threshold is crossed.
