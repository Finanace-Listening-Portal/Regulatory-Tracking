Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Vinayaka Carriers Vs Joint Commissioner of Commercial Taxes (Karnataka High Court) Summary: The Karnatahttps://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-appeal-rejec…ended-rule-108-3.htmlka High Court, in Sri Vinayaka Carriers Vs Joint Commissioner of Commercial Taxes, considered whether a GST appeal could be rejected merely because the appellant had not furnished a certified copy of the adjudication order. The petitioner challenged the rejection acknowledgment dated 22 July 2026 and sought restoration of its appeal for adjudication on merits. The dispute concerned compliance with Rule 108(3) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017, and the corresponding Karnataka Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017, following the amendment effective from 26 December 2022. The petitioner contended that rejection for non-submission of a certified copy was inconsistent with the amended procedural requirements and the High Court’s earlier decision in W.P. No. 26493 of 2026 dated 24 August 2026. The High Court noted that, in the earlier writ petition, it had restored an appeal after considering the substituted Rule 108(3). Under the amended provision, the requirement to upload a self-attested copy of the impugned order arises where that order is not automatically populated on the GST portal at the time of filing the appeal. The Court recorded that this legal proposition was not disputed by the respondents. Applying the principle of parity with its earlier order, the High Court held that interference was warranted in the petitioner’s case. Accordingly, it set aside the rejection of the appeal and restored the appellate proceedings for reconsideration. The petitioner was directed to appear before the Appellate Authority on 30 November 2026 without further notice and, to address all contingencies, to furnish a self-attested copy of the adjudication order on the first date of appearance. The ruling reinforces the significance of the amended procedural requirements governing GST appeals and provides relief against rejection based on a certified-copy requirement inconsistent with those provisions. Cases Discussed W.P. No. 26493 of 2026 (Karnataka High Court); Order dated 24/08/2026. The High Court relied upon its earlier order restoring a GST appeal following the substitution of Rule 108(3) with effect from 26 December 2022. The earlier ruling recognised the conditional requirement for uploading a self-attested copy when the impugned order is not automatically populated on the GST portal. The Court applied the same principle on grounds of parity. The names of the parties and the reported citation are not stated in the present order. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT The petitioner’s grievance is with its appeal [Annexure-P] being rejected on the ground that a certified copy has not been furnished. Mr. Sherry Samuel and Mr. Nirmal Mathew, the learned counsels for the petitioner and Ms. Malavika Prasad, a learned High Court Government Pleader, who accepts notice for the respondents, are heard on whether the appeal could have been rejected on the ground that a certified copy has not been furnished in the light of this Court’s Order dated 24.08.2026 in the writ petition in W.P. No.26493/2026. This Court, in the writ petition in W.P. No.26493/2026, has interfered to restore the appeal after hearing a learned Additional Government accepting the canvass that with effect from 26.12.2022 with the substitution of Rule 108[3] of the Central Goods and Services Tax Rules/Karnataka Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017, a self-attested copy of the impugned Order can be uploaded only if the impugned Order is not automatically populated on the portal at the time of filing of this appeal. This proposition is not in dispute. This Court, for reasons of parity, must interfere and restore the appeal for reconsideration. Hence the following: ORDER The petition is allowed, setting aside the rejection of the petitioner’s appeal in terms of Annexure – P restoring the proceedings stipulating that the petitioner shall, without further notice appear before the Appellate Authority on 30.11.2026 and further stipulating that, to meet all exigencies, the petitioner shall also file a self-attested copy of the impugned Adjudication Order on the first date of appearance.
