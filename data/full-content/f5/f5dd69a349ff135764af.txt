CompaniesBank Of Baroda Responds To BR Shetty’s $15 Billion Lawsuit, Says Yet To Receive Court PapersEdited by: Priya RaghuvanshiUpdated: Oct 14, 2026, 12:13 ISTBank of Baroda says it is yet to receive court papers in BR Shetty’s $15 billion lawsuit alleging banking irregularities linked to the collapse of NMC Healthcare.Bank of Baroda (File Photo)follow usfollow usBank of Baroda has responded to reports that UAE-based businessman Bavaguthu Raghuram Shetty has filed a $15 billion lawsuit against the lender and 25 other defendants in the Bombay High Court, stating that it has not yet received the legal documents and is therefore unable to comment on the allegations. In a regulatory filing dated October 14, the public sector bank said it had taken note of reports about the lawsuit, which was reportedly filed on October 13. The lender added that it would contest the matter and take all necessary measures to safeguard its interests if it receives the court papers.The lawsuit reportedly accuses the bank, some of its officials and other individuals of facilitating financial transactions allegedly linked to the collapse of Shetty’s business empire. The claims are yet to be established in court.Shetty, the founder of NMC Healthcare and UAE Exchange, is seeking $15 billion in damages over alleged financial irregularities, disputed banking transactions and reputational losses connected to his former business group.According to the plaint, the compensation sought is divided into three parts: $5 billion for direct damages, $5 billion for consequential losses, including lost business opportunities, and another $5 billion for alleged reputational harm. The first two claims are directed against the defendants collectively, while the reputational damages claim is directed solely at Bank of Baroda.The businessman has also sought annual interest of 18 per cent on the claimed amount.The case reportedly names 26 defendants, including Bank of Baroda, 10 of its officials, former NMC executives Prasanth Manghat and Promoth Manghat, their associates, and Indian companies Avitis Super Speciality Hospitals and SMARD Building Solutions.Lawsuit Alleges Bank Ignored Suspicious Transaction AlertsA key part of the complaint concerns the lender’s alleged handling of transactions involving accounts associated with NMC Group. Shetty claims the bank’s monitoring systems generated more than 100 alerts about suspicious activity but that these warnings did not result in adequate investigations or reports to the relevant authorities.The plaint alleges that Bank of Baroda’s Abu Dhabi branch “advised the Manghat team how to structure and paper those transactions so that they would pass scrutiny”. It also accuses the lender of executing “every leg of the roundtripping” of funds from NMC.The complaint further questions payments of approximately AED 42 million allegedly made to Prasanth Manghat between May 2018 and January 2019 through an account associated with Guide General Contracting and Maintenance.These allegations have not been proven in court. Bank of Baroda has said it will defend the matter if it is formally served with the lawsuit.BR Shetty Questions Accounts And Banking InstructionsThe lawsuit also raises questions about the opening and operation of bank accounts allegedly linked to Shetty without his knowledge or authorisation.According to the plaint, two accounts were opened in his name at the National Bank of Fujairah in December 2017 and March 2019 using documents that allegedly contained forged signatures. Shetty claims the accounts were subsequently used to transfer funds from group companies to the Manghat brothers, their associates and businesses under their control.He has further alleged that Bank of Baroda accepted instructions on his personal account from people who were not authorised to operate it, despite Joseph Pinto being its only authorised signatory. The complaint also questions the lender’s alleged acceptance of cheques bearing forged signatures.The filing cites approximately AED 9.7 billion in payments from Nexgen to NMC accounts between 2012 and 2018, as well as around AED 3.2 billion in payments by Neopharma from 2015 onwards. Shetty has questioned whether these transactions represented genuine supplier payments.$600 Million NMC Settlement Mentioned In The CaseThe plaint also refers to a settlement reached in June 2026 between Bank of Baroda and the joint administrators of NMC Healthcare. The agreement was valued at approximately $600 million, or Rs 5,700 crore, and was disclosed on July 2, according to the filing.Shetty reportedly argues that the settlement relates to the same underlying transactions and questions the bank’s subsequent pursuit of claims against him under personal guarantees whose validity he disputes.The lawsuit also seeks access to and preservation of banking records, details of fund movements and measures to protect certain defendants’ assets in India.The settlement, however, does not independently establish the allegations raised in Shetty’s complaint.How NMC Healthcare’s Collapse Led To Legal DisputesNMC Healthcare, founded by Shetty, grew into one of the UAE’s largest private healthcare providers, while UAE Exchange became a prominent financial services business.NMC came under scrutiny in December 2019 after short-seller Muddy Waters raised questions about its financial statements, debt and cash balances. In 2020, the company disclosed billions of dollars in previously unreported borrowings, with its debt subsequently revised to approximately $6.6 billion.The London-listed parent entered administration in April 2020, followed by NMC’s UAE operating businesses in September. The collapse led to investigations and legal disputes involving lenders, executives and auditors.Bank of Baroda’s latest filing makes clear that it has not received the court papers relating to Shetty’s reported lawsuit. The bank has said it will take the necessary steps to protect its interests once it is formally served.
