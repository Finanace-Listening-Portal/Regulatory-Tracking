CompaniesIDBI Bank Stake Sale: Finance Ministry Cites Delhi HC Ruling To Defend Centre’s PlanEdited by: Priya RaghuvanshiUpdated: Oct 17, 2026, 14:27 ISTThe Finance Ministry cites a 2018 Delhi HC ruling and past Budget speeches to defend the Centre’s IDBI Bank stake sale and disinvestment plan.IDBI Bank stake sale (File Photo)follow usfollow usThe Finance Ministry has defended the Centre’s plan to dilute its stake in IDBI Bank, citing the legislative history of the lender’s restructuring and a 2018 Delhi High Court judgment. In an official statement issued on October 17, the ministry said neither the legislation enacted in 2003 nor the court’s ruling prevented the government from reducing its shareholding.The clarification comes amid the Centre’s proposed strategic disinvestment of IDBI Bank. The ministry reiterated that an amendment seeking to restrict the dilution of the government’s stake was proposed during discussions surrounding the Union Budget in 2003 and the parliamentary debate on the IDBI (Repeal) Act, 2003. However, the amendment was rejected and was not included in the final law.The ministry also referred to the Delhi High Court’s judgment in All India IDBI Officers’ Association v. Union of India & Others, delivered on December 17, 2018. According to the government, the ruling established that statements made during parliamentary debates could not override enacted legislation or prevent the Centre from diluting its stake.The Finance Ministry said the Delhi High Court examined whether statements made in Parliament could be used to prevent the government from reducing its ownership in IDBI Bank.While dismissing the writ petition, the court observed that statements made during a legislative debate could not override the law eventually passed by Parliament. The proposed amendment restricting stake dilution had not made it into the enacted legislation.The judgment stated in paragraph 41, "This Court is of the view that the statements made before the House almost 15 years earlier, in a debate relating to a legislative activity, would not preclude the Central Government from diluting its stake in IDBI Ltd."The court further noted that Parliament had not incorporated the proposed restriction into the final law. It also rejected the argument that the government was bound by statements made during the legislative process under the principle of promissory estoppel.In paragraph 42, the court held, "There is no ambiguity in the language of the IDBI Repeal Act, 2003 and there is no scope to read any provision into the said enactment which proscribes the Government from reducing or divesting its stake in IDBI Ltd."What Is Promissory Estoppel And Why Does It Matter?Promissory estoppel is a legal principle that can, in certain circumstances, prevent a party from going back on a promise when another party has relied on it to their detriment.In the IDBI Bank case, the principle was invoked in challenging the government’s decision to dilute its shareholding.
