Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 9, 2026 at 12:05 PM IST var news_msid = 133956563; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Mumbai wants to become the laboratory for financial AI, Maharashtra CM FadnavisMaharashtra is working towards becoming India’s first “tokenised state” and is putting in place a legal framework to enable blockchain-based tokenisation of land and other immovable assets, Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis said at the Global FinTech Fest 2026 in Mumbai on Wednesday.Fadnavis said the state is developing the Maharashtra Digitization and Exchange of Land Token Asset Act, or DELTA Act, with the objective of creating a legal framework for tokenising land and other immovable assets.“We are now bringing into place the architecture of Maharashtra Digitization and Exchange of Land Token Asset Act, that is the DELTA Act,” Fadnavis said. Advt The proposed framework is aimed at unlocking the value embedded in physical assets, particularly land and real estate, which he estimated could represent an opportunity of around Rs 50 lakh crore.Fadnavis said the state has taken the idea beyond the conference stage and is bringing together expertise from financial market institutions, industry, technology, law and academia. The framework, he said, would need collaboration between the Centre, institutions and the Maharashtra government.“For generations, land has represented wealth in India. But very often, it is wealth without liquidity,” he said, adding that tokenisation could help families and entrepreneurs unlock the economic potential of such assets more efficiently.However, Fadnavis stressed that the state's approach to tokenisation is not intended to encourage speculation.“For us, tokenization is not about speculation. It is about unlocking productive capital. It is about bringing transparency. It is about improving liquidity. It is about making assets more productive,” he said.He said the larger objective was to convert asset wealth into economic opportunity while ensuring legal certainty, consumer protection and regulatory trust.Beyond tokenisation, Fadnavis identified agentic AI and quantum technology as the other major frontiers for India's financial technology ecosystem. Advt On agentic AI, he said the next generation of digital finance should move beyond merely giving people access to financial services towards giving them greater agency. He cited the possibility of AI assistants helping MSME entrepreneurs understand cash flows, anticipate working-capital requirements, identify financing options and make financial decisions.At the same time, he stressed that AI systems operating on behalf of citizens must retain human oversight.“AI may act for the citizen, but the citizen must remain in control,” Fadnavis said, adding that the future of finance would have to combine intelligence with accountability, innovation with consent, and speed with security. Advt He also highlighted quantum computing as a key consideration for the future security architecture of financial systems, given the dependence of billions of financial transactions on cryptography.Fadnavis said Maharashtra wants to position itself as a large-scale testing ground for the next generation of fintech solutions, spanning agriculture, MSMEs, public finance, fraud prevention and citizen services.“Don't look at Maharashtra only as your market. Look at Maharashtra as your laboratory,” he told fintech companies and innovators.He said Mumbai's financial ecosystem, Pune's technology and engineering talent, and Navi Mumbai's emerging infrastructure and data capabilities give the state the ingredients to develop technologies that can eventually be deployed globally. Advt “Maharashtra will become India's largest real-world testbed for the next generation of fintech,” Fadnavis said, adding that the state would work with innovators and institutions to test solutions responsibly, deploy them at scale and take successful solutions to the world.Fadnavis also framed India's next phase of fintech development around the idea of moving from potential to impact, arguing that UPI had demonstrated how sophisticated technology could be made accessible to ordinary citizens.Click here to read ETBFSI’s GFF 2025 coverage By Shrishti Sharma , ETBFSI Published On Sep 9, 2026 at 12:05 PM IST Telegram Facebook Copy Link Comments What are your thoughts?
