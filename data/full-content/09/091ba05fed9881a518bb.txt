Articles 3 min read CGSMFI 2.0 utilisation likely at Rs 8000-9,000 crore of Rs 20,000 crore pool; MFI SRO had sought extension The Rs 20,000 crore credit guarantee scheme for microfinance institutions saw limited traction despite an increase in the borrowing limit for large MFIs and an extension of its validity, with smaller lenders continuing to face funding constraints, according to MFI lenders and self-regulatory organisations. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 20, 2026 at 10:40 PM IST var news_msid = 134372358; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The utilisation of the government’s Credit Guarantee Scheme for Microfinance Institutions 2.0 (CGSMFI 2.0) is estimated to have remained at around Rs 8,000-9,000 crore against the Rs 20,000 crore pool, according to three MFI lenders and self-regulatory organisation ETBFSI spoke with.Sa-Dhan, an RBI-recognised self-regulatory organisation for the microfinance sector, had sought an extension of the scheme to provide additional funding support to smaller MFIs, Sa-Dhan CEO Jiji Mammen told ETBFSI. Muthoot Microfin’s CEO Sadaf Sayeed had also confirmed that the SRO seeked for extension of the scheme. Advt SRO did not specify a particular number of months for the extension.“We had requested the government to extend it at least for the smaller MFIs. We said that because the overall traction for the scheme was very slow. Out of Rs 20,000 crore of guarantee less than Rs 10,000 crore has been made use of,” Sa-Dhan CEO Jiji Mammen said. According to Mammen, the limited utilisation was partly because larger institutions that had obtained sanctions were not necessarily drawing down the funds, while smaller MFIs faced difficulty in accessing the scheme because of the borrowing limits.The scheme, introduced in March 2026, was originally valid until June 30 or until guarantees worth Rs 20,000 crore were issued, whichever was earlier. In June, the government extended its validity to August 31 and simultaneously increased the maximum loan amount available to large sized NBFC-MFIs and MFIs to Rs 1,000 crore from Rs 300 crore, while retaining the overall ceiling at 20 per cent of assets under management (AUM). At the time of the extension, the government said Rs 770 crore had been sanctioned under the scheme.CGSMFI 2.0 provides guarantee cover of 80 per cent for small MFIs, 75 per cent for medium-sized institutions and 70 per cent for large institutions. Under the revised framework, small MFIs with AUM below Rs 500 crore can avail up to 20 per cent of AUM subject to a maximum of Rs 100 crore, medium MFIs with AUM between Rs 500 crore and less than Rs 2,000 crore can avail up to Rs 200 crore, while the maximum for large MFIs with AUM of Rs 2,000 crore or more was raised to Rs 1,000 crore.The government’s June decision to raise the maximum borrowing amount for large MFIs to Rs 1,000 crore from Rs 300 crore was aimed at improving utilisation. However, the overall 20 per cent AUM ceiling continued to apply. Advt According to MFIN’s latest Micrometer for Q1 FY27, the microfinance industry’s portfolio rose 1.1 per cent sequentially to Rs 3,28,708 crore as of June 30, 2026, although it remained 6.9 per cent lower year-on-year. Disbursements increased 8.9 per cent year-on-year to Rs 61,718 crore during the quarter.In terms of portfolio quality, PAR 31-180 days declined to 1.6 per cent as of June 30 from 5.6 per cent a year earlier and 2.1 per cent in the previous quarter, according to MFIN. Funding to MFIN member NBFC-MFIs also rose 91.4 per cent year-on-year during the quarter, with the increase seen across small, medium and large NBFC-MFIs.
