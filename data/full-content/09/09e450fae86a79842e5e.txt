Summary: The allowability of Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) under Section 90 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 has become a frequently litigated issue where taxpayers file Form 67 after the prescribed due date. Resident taxpayers are required to disclose their global income in India, including income earned abroad on which foreign taxes may already have been paid. Relief under Section 90 and applicable Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAAs) is intended to prevent double taxation. However, the Income Tax Department has frequently denied FTC claims because of delays in filing Form 67, even where the foreign taxes were actually paid. Several Income Tax Appellate Tribunals have granted relief, treating delayed filing as a procedural lapse rather than a sufficient ground for denying substantive tax credit. Important decisions include Brinda Rama Krishna v. ITO, 42 Hertz Software India Pvt. Ltd. v. ACIT, and Sonali Verma v. ITO. In Prakash Basappa Savanur Vs ITO (ITA No. 958/Bang/2026, dated 29 October 2026), the Bangalore ITAT directed the Jurisdictional Assessing Officer to accept Form 67 and allow relief under Section 90, rather than merely remanding the matter for verification. These decisions highlight the need for CBDT instructions to prevent avoidable litigation and ensure that eligible taxpayers receive foreign tax credit despite procedural delays. A resident in India is required to declare the Global Income. It is possible that while the assessee earns income a foreign country, the law of that country mandates for payment of tax on income earned in that country. In most of the cases, the tax on foreign income is discharged via Tax Deduction at Source. While in India, the same income is includible in Total income of the tax payer. He will be required to pay tax on the income earned in foreign country also. This leads to double taxation. To avoid the Double Taxation, the countries have entered into agreement for avoidance of double taxation. These agreements are termed as DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements) Under the Income tax law in India, a resident is mandated to include the income earned in a foreign country. If any tax is paid outside India, on income earned outside India, the same is allowable as Relief under section 90 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. However the law imposes certain conditions for availing the said relief under section 90 of the Income tax Act, 1961. Important conditions are 1) Filing of Form 67 before filing the return of income 2) Form 67 is to be filed within the due date for filing the original return. 3) Also the documentary evidence of tax paid outside India is to be provided by the tax payer. In many cases, it is observed that the tax payers are filing their return of income after the due date under section 139(1) of the Act. Also the Form 67 is being filed along with the return of income. The Department is not allowing the relief under section 90 where the Form 67 is filed after the due date for filing the return of income. 2022-23 and later assessment Years: For the assessment years 2022-23 and subsequent assessment years, the due date to file the Form 67 is until the end of the relevant assessment year. But for the Assessment Years prior to 2022-23, the last date to submit the Form 67 is the due date prescribed under section 139(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. A number of assessees for the assessment years upto 2021-22, have claimed the relief under section 90 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 after the due date of filing the return of income. The same is being disallowed by the authorities. Even in First Appellate state also the relief is not being granted to the tax payers. It is only in the Income Tax Tribunals in various parts of the country, the assessees are getting the due credit in respect of foreign tax credit. Remand of the case to JAO: As seen in most of the cases like Brinda Rama Krishna v. ITO (ITAT Bangalore, (2022) 193 ITD 840), 42 Hertz Software India Pvt. Ltd. v. ACIT (ITAT Bangalore, 139 taxmann.com 448), Sonali Verma v. ITO (ITAT Hyderabad, ITA No. 778/Hyd/ 2025 etc., the Hon’ble Tribunals have allowed the Foreign Tax Credit and have directed the Jurisdictional Assessing Officers to verify the records and allow the credit of the foreign tax credit. Prakash Basappa Savanur Vs ITO ((ITAT Bangalore), ITA No. 958/Bang/2026 Dated: 29th October 2026, the Hon’ble Tribunal allowed the appeal and directed the JAO to accept the Form 67 and allow the relief under section 90. The distinction in this case is that unlike other cases, the JAO was not directed to verify the records and allow the credit. JAO is ordered to allow the claim. For getting the relief of foreign tax credit, the assessees are required to travel till the Tribunals in spite of a number of cases being decided in favour of the tax payers. The CBDT should come out with some directions to the JAO/CPC to allow the Foreign Tax credit even in cases where the Form 67 is filed after the due date of filing the return under section 139(1), of course till AY 2021-22.
