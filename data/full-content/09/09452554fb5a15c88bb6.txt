Articles 3 min read IRDAI commission caps unlikely this fiscal; new rules may take effect April 1, 2027: Nilesh Sathe Former IRDAI member Nilesh Sathe expects the proposed commission and expense-management changes to take effect from April 1, 2027, with insurers, brokers and other stakeholders having little room to avoid a structural reset. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Oct 8, 2026 at 08:28 AM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 134778526; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The insurance industry is likely to see the proposed overhaul of commissions and expense-management norms take effect from April 1, 2027, with former IRDAI member Nilesh Sathe saying insurers, brokers and other distribution stakeholders will have to fundamentally realign their businesses with the new framework.Sathe said he does not expect major reversals in the proposals during the consultation process, though some provisions could be tweaked before the final regulations are notified.“By and large, do not expect change in what is proposed. There may be some tweaking here and there. 2.5% may go up to 3% or 3.5%, but there cannot be and there will not be a restoration of what it was in the past. So insurance companies, brokers, all stakeholders will have to realign themselves and recalibrate in view of the regulations which will come in place," Sathe told ETBFSI in an interview. Advt Six-month window for final rulesAccording to Sathe, the regulatory process will have to move quickly if the April 2027 implementation timeline is to be met. Stakeholders have been given time to submit comments, following which IRDAI would examine the responses and come out with draft regulations before the final notification.“The entire process has to be over in the next six months,” Sathe said, explaining that the final regulations would need to pass through the Insurance Advisory Committee and the IRDAI board before being sent to the government for publication in the official gazette.Only after publication would the regulations come into force, he said.Expense caps could force industry-wide cost cutsSathe, however, raised concerns over the proposed tightening of expense-management limits, particularly because the existing framework was introduced only in 2023.Under the proposed glide path, expense of management for general insurers is to move from 30% to 20%, while for life insurers it would decline from 15% to 12.5% over five years.Sathe said the industry would have to cut costs across the board if premiums do not rise sufficiently to absorb the lower limits.“If premium doesn't go up, then your only choice is to cut the expenses. So everywhere you will have to cut the expenses. For general insurers, even more. The cost of motor, health and rural distribution is a lot," he said. Advt Smaller insurers could face greater pressureThe former IRDAI member also flagged the possibility that tighter expense limits could disproportionately favour large, established insurers, which already operate at lower cost ratios because of their scale and large renewal books.He pointed to LIC and SBI Life as examples, saying their operating costs are already below the proposed 12.5% life insurance threshold.For newer insurers, however, building scale takes time, and Sathe said regulatory flexibility would be important during the initial years of operations.He also criticised what he sees as frequent changes to the expense-management framework, arguing that regulatory stability matters for investors and the broader development of the insurance sector. Advt “The gliding path on expense of commission should be withdrawn,” Sathe said, adding that several other aspects of the proposed framework also need modification before finalisation.
