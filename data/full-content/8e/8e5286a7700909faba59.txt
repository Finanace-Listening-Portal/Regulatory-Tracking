Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Bhaiyalal Contractors Vs State of U.P. & 2 Others (Allahabad High Court) Summary: The Allahabad High Court examined a writ petition filed under Article 226 of the Constitution of India challenging the order dated 3 June 2023 and the consequential appellate order dated 23 April 2024. The petitioner’s appeal had been rejected through Form GST APL-02, citing delay in submission of the appeal. The Court observed that the Appellate Authority had rejected the appeal without granting an opportunity of hearing to the petitioner. In support of its contentions, the petitioner relied upon the Allahabad High Court’s decisions in BBCL Infrastructure Vs State of U.P. and 2 Others and R.K. Tripathi Contractors and Suppliers Vs State of U.P. and 2 Others, along with the Rajasthan High Court’s decision in Poonamchand Saran Vs Union of India and Ors.. After considering the facts and applicable judicial precedents, the High Court held that the issue involved in the present case was squarely covered by the aforesaid judgments. Accordingly, the Court quashed and set aside the appellate order dated 23 April 2024 and directed the Appellate Authority to pass a fresh, reasoned order after granting an adequate opportunity of hearing to the petitioner. The entire exercise was directed to be completed within two months from the date of the High Court’s order. The Court thus set aside the rejection of the GST appeal on the ground of delay without granting an opportunity of hearing. However, the original order dated 3 June 2023 was not expressly quashed, nor did the Court itself condone the delay in filing the appeal. With these directions, the writ petition was disposed of. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF ALLAHABAD HIGH COURT 1. Learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner is directed to correct the prayer clause of this writ petition during the course of the day. 2. Heard Sri Ajay Kumar Yadav, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner and Sri Ravi Shanker Pandey, learned Additional Chief Standing Counsel appearing on behalf of the State respondents. 3. This is a writ petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India wherein the writ petitioner is aggrieved by the order dated June 3, 2023 and the appellate order dated April 23, 2024 whereby the appeal of the petitioner was rejected by way of Form GST APL – 02 stating the reason “Delay in submission of Appeal”. 4. From the facts, it is clear that the no opportunity of hearing was granted to the petitioner. Counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner relies on the judgements passed by the coordinate Bench of this Court in BBCL Infrastructure v. State of U.P. and 2 Others, Writ Tax No.1013 of 2022 (decided on December 15, 2022 [Neutral Citation No. – 2022:AHC:217659]) and in R.K. Tripathi Contractors and Suppliers v. State of U.P. and 2 Others, Writ Tax No.1489 of 2022 (decided on January 9, 2023 [Neutral Citation No. – 2023:AHC:4767]) as well as a judgement passed by the Rajasthan High Court in Poonamchand Saran v. Union of India and Ors., Civil Writ Petition No.14521 of 2022 decided on September 29, 2022 to buttress his arguments. 5. The issue involved in the present case is squarely covered by the above judgements, and accordingly, this Court quashes and sets aside the impugned appellate order dated April 23, 2024 and directs the appellate authority to pass a reasoned order after granting an opportunity of hearing to the petitioner. The entire exercise should be completed within a period of two months from date. 6. With the above directions, this writ petition is disposed of.
