What “Predominantly Small Cap” Means for Portfolio Construction SEBI’s mutual-fund categorisation framework generally requires a small-cap fund to invest at least 65 per cent of total assets in equity and equity-related instruments of small-cap companies. “Small cap” is a market-capitalisation classification, not a statement that every holding is an early-stage business. The eligible universe can include established companies outside the top 250 by full market capitalisation, and the list is updated periodically. A company can migrate between large-, mid- and small-cap lists, so portfolio classification is not static. The remaining allocation gives the manager room for other equity, cash, money-market instruments and permitted assets within the SID. That flexibility can support liquidity and portfolio implementation, but it does not remove category risk. Investors should compare the current factsheet with the SID limits and examine the share of the portfolio outside the small-cap bucket, the number of holdings, sector weights, top-ten concentration and cash position. One month’s portfolio is a snapshot rather than proof of a permanent style. Small-cap liquidity can differ sharply from index-level volatility Small-cap shares may trade with lower market depth, wider bid-offer spreads and higher impact cost than large-cap shares. In a broad redemption cycle, the fund may need to sell positions into thinner markets, and market prices can adjust faster than the portfolio can be rebalanced. A daily NAV is calculated under valuation rules, but daily liquidity of the fund does not mean every underlying security could be sold immediately at the last quoted price. This distinction is central when assessing a long-horizon category. Reading the October 2026 Portfolio Without Turning It Into a Forecast The November 2026 factsheet should be used to identify actual holdings and weights as of 31 October 2026, not to extrapolate a future return. A top holding can fall in value or be sold after the reporting date. Sector exposure can create common sensitivity to credit conditions, consumption, industrial cycles, regulation or raw-material costs. A diversified list of company names can still share the same macroeconomic driver. Investors should compare several monthly disclosures to understand turnover and style consistency, while remembering that historical changes do not predict the next allocation. TER, Direct and Regular Plans, and Tracking Investor Cost Total Expense Ratio is charged to the scheme and reflected in the NAV; it is not normally debited as a separate invoice to the unit holder. The direct plan excludes distributor commission and therefore generally has a lower TER than the regular plan. An AMC factsheet dated 31 August 2026 disclosed base expense ratios of 1.54 per cent for the regular plan and 0.43 per cent for the direct plan. These figures are useful historical points, not representations of the rate on 23 November 2026. The current TER page and latest factsheet should be checked because daily or periodic expense disclosures can change within regulatory limits. A lower TER reduces cost drag but does not make a plan suitable, safe or superior on every investor criterion. Direct-plan investors arrange their own advice and execution; regular-plan investors may receive distributor service whose quality and conflicts should be evaluated. Plan comparisons must use the same option and period. A switch from regular to direct is normally treated as a redemption from one plan and purchase into another for tax and exit-load purposes, even though the underlying portfolio is the same scheme. Exit Load and Redemption Mechanics The AMC’s scheme factsheet states an exit load of 1 per cent where units are redeemed or switched out on or before 90 days from allotment, and nil after 90 days. The applicable wording and any exceptions must be checked in the current scheme documents for the transaction date. Exit load is applied to the relevant redemption value and remains distinct from capital-gains tax, securities transaction tax and the economic effect of NAV movement. A switch between plans or options can trigger the load if it falls within the stated period. Mutual-fund units are redeemed at the applicable NAV determined under cut-off and realisation rules. Submission time alone may not establish the NAV if funds or a valid request are not received as required. Large redemptions, folio restrictions, lien, incomplete bank validation or transmission issues can delay processing. Investors should keep the transaction confirmation, allotment date, unit statement, applicable NAV, exit-load calculation and bank credit record. Riskometer, Benchmark and Suitability Boundaries The official material classifies scheme risk as Very High. The riskometer is a standardised indicator, not a loss limit. It cannot capture every liquidity event, governance issue, valuation gap or investor-specific need. The benchmark is a total-return index used for comparison; it is not investible on identical terms and does not deduct the scheme’s TER, taxes or transaction frictions in the same manner. Relative outperformance in a period does not guarantee preservation of capital or future performance. Suitability depends on time horizon, ability to withstand a prolonged drawdown, emergency-liquidity needs, existing equity concentration, tax position and behavioural response to volatility. A small-cap fund should not be treated as a fixed deposit, assured-return plan or short-term parking vehicle. SIP instalments stagger purchase dates but do not prevent loss, eliminate sequence risk or guarantee a profit. Each instalment also has its own holding period for exit-load and capital-gains purposes. Indian Tax on Redemption, Switch and Distribution Equity-oriented capital gains Where the scheme satisfies the statutory equity-oriented fund conditions, units held for not more than 12 months are generally short-term capital assets. Short-term capital gains covered by section 111A are taxed at 20 per cent for transfers on or after 23 July 2024, plus applicable surcharge and cess. Units held for more than 12 months can give rise to long-term capital gains under section 112A; the rate is generally 12.5 per cent on covered aggregate gains exceeding the annual ₹1.25 lakh threshold, plus surcharge and cess. The law, transaction date and taxpayer facts must be verified. A redemption, switch to another scheme, or switch between direct and regular plans can be a taxable transfer. Each purchase or SIP lot has its own acquisition date and cost. Exit load affects proceeds and the capital-gain computation according to the applicable tax treatment. Capital loss set-off and carry-forward require compliance with return-filing rules. Securities transaction tax is generally collected on redemption of equity-oriented fund units, but it does not replace capital-gains tax. Income distribution and reinvestment Amounts distributed under an income-distribution-cum-capital-withdrawal option are generally taxable in the unit holder’s hands under the applicable income-tax rules, and TDS may apply when statutory conditions are met. Reinvestment does not make the distribution tax-free; it creates new units with a new cost and holding period. Investors who do not need distributions should still compare options based on cash-flow and tax needs rather than names such as “dividend.” Document Checklist Before and After a Transaction Read the current SID and KIM with all applicable notice-cum-addenda. Does an SIP remove small-cap risk? No. SIPs stagger purchase dates but do not guarantee returns or prevent loss. Each instalment carries market risk and has a separate holding period. How are long-term gains generally taxed? Can the October 2026 portfolio be used to predict returns? No. It is a dated disclosure. Holdings, weights and market conditions can change, and the factsheet does not promise future performance.
