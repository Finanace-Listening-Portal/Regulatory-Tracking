The Reserve Bank of India emphasized broad-based food inflation pressures, complicating the economic landscape. Retail inflation had already surpassed the RBI's threshold of 6% in October, prompting concern among economists. Market participants are anticipating the upcoming monetary policy decision on December 4. The benchmark Brent crude extended gains in Asian hours to above $95 per barrel. Advt For India, which imports most of its crude oil requirements, a sustained rise in oil prices can widen the current account deficit, put downward pressure on the rupee and feed into domestic inflation."Rise in oil prices is always a bad news for bonds, as inflation worries could very well reverse the entire rally that we have witnessed in bonds through the month," trader with a primary dealership said.Food inflation pressures in India remain broad-based, and data has shown "higher generalisation of price pressures", the Reserve Bank of India said in its monthly bulletin.According to the RBI, high-frequency data available through November 19 indicated that food-price momentum had remained strong and prices of major cereals and pulses continued to rise, underlining that the inflation problem was no longer confined to a small number of items.Earlier this month, Reuters had reported that weak monsoon rains have contributed to tighter food supplies and higher prices, while retail inflation had already climbed above the RBI's upper tolerance threshold of 6% in October.Investors are now closely watching the RBI's policy decision scheduled for December 4. A majority of economists surveyed by Reuters expect a rate hike of 25 basis points, but some are not ruling out a larger action.RATESOvernight index swap rates jumped in line with bond yields, as traders reversed recent receiving trades.The 1-year rate was not yet traded, while the 2-year rate jumped 6 bps to 6.45% and the liquid 5-year rate rose 6 bps to 6.44%.
