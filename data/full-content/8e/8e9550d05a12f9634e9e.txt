Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Sunita Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Additional Evidence Explaining Cash Deposits U/s 69A Cannot Be Rejected Solely for Non-Compliance During Assessment Summary: The Delhi Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held that the CIT(A) cannot reject additional evidence under Rule 46A merely because the assessee failed to furnish it during assessment. The appellate authority must consider the assessee’s circumstances and the relevance of the evidence to the disputed addition. Finding a close link between the assessee’s cash deposits and her husband’s bank withdrawals, the Tribunal admitted the evidence and remanded the matter to the Assessing Officer for limited verification of the source of the deposits. Reassessment Based on Cash Deposits The Assessing Officer received information through the Annual Information Return that the assessee had deposited ₹20,01,100 in her Syndicate Bank account and had filed no return for AY 2010–11. A letter seeking the source of the deposit was returned. The Assessing Officer recorded reasons and issued a notice under Section 148 on 31 March 2017. The assessee’s representative attended on a few occasions, but subsequent notices went unanswered. The Assessing Officer completed the assessment under Section 144 read with Section 147. On examining three Syndicate Bank accounts maintained by the assessee, he treated aggregate cash deposits of ₹58,07,200 as unexplained money under Section 69A. Assessee Traced the Deposits to Her Husband’s Withdrawal The assessee explained that the principal deposits came from cash withdrawn by her husband. She deposited ₹18 lakh on 23 September 2009 and ₹40,02,200 on 24 September 2009. Her husband had withdrawn ₹90 lakh from his State Bank of India account on 23 September 2009. A separate deposit of ₹5,000 had been made on 19 September 2009 when she opened one of the accounts. The bank statements also showed that ₹58 lakh was placed in fixed deposits with Syndicate Bank. The assessee traced her husband’s funds to the sale of agricultural land jointly owned by him and his brother. The land was sold under a registered sale deed dated 4 April 2008 for a total consideration of ₹11 crore. According to the assessee, her husband invested his share in fixed deposits, and the proceeds on encashment were credited to his bank account on 22 September 2009, a day before the cash withdrawal. Why the CIT(A) Rejected the Evidence The assessee furnished the sale deed and gift deed before the CIT(A) as additional evidence. In support of her Rule 46A application, she explained that she was a homemaker from a farming family, had no educational background and lacked access to professional assistance to make the necessary submissions during assessment. The CIT(A) declined to admit the documents because the Assessing Officer had given the assessee several opportunities and the evidence had not been furnished at that stage. Before the Tribunal, the Revenue also questioned the gift deed dated 5 February 2019, arguing that its execution after the assessment order made it an afterthought. Tribunal Admits Evidence and Orders Limited Verification The Tribunal held that earlier non-compliance alone was insufficient to reject the additional evidence. The assessee’s circumstances had to be considered, particularly where the documents bore directly on whether any tax liability arose. The Tribunal found a live link between the deposits in the assessee’s accounts and the transactions in her husband’s account. It also noted that the same Assessing Officer had assessed both spouses and could have examined their bank transactions together. The Tribunal admitted the evidence under Rule 46A and restored the issue to the Assessing Officer for the limited purpose of verifying the cash deposits and their stated source. The officer was directed to consider the husband’s withdrawals and the gift deed and give the assessee a proper opportunity of being heard. The ground challenging the addition was allowed in those terms. The grounds concerning the validity of reassessment, the Assessing Officer’s application of mind and the sanction under Section 151 were not adjudicated, as the assessee argued only the merits. Author’s Comments The ruling is significant where the bank records themselves provide a basis to test the assessee’s explanation. Here, the sequence of the fixed deposit encashment, the husband’s withdrawal and the wife’s deposits gave the Assessing Officer a transaction trail to verify. The date of the gift deed remains relevant to its evidentiary weight. Its admission allows the officer to consider it alongside the contemporaneous bank entries before deciding whether the assessee has established the source. The limited remand also defines the relief precisely. The Tribunal reopened the factual inquiry into the ₹58,07,200 addition, and its ultimate treatment depends on verification. The husband’s withdrawal of 23 September 2009 may explain the deposits on that date and the next day if the link is established. The ₹5,000 deposit on 19 September 2009 preceded that withdrawal and therefore requires a separate explanation. Cases Discussed: CIT Vs Prabhavati S. Shah (Bombay High Court); (1998) 231 ITR 1. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. Income-tax (Appeals)/National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi (NFAC), Delhi [“Ld. CIT (A)”, for short] dated 30.04.2026 for the AY 2010-11. 2. Brief facts of the case are, a notice under section 148 of the Act was issued on 31.03.2017 for the reason that assessee had not filed her return of income for the Assessment Year2010-11 after recording the satisfaction that as per the information available through AIR, the assessee had deposited Rs.20,01,100/- in her Syndicate Bank account and also not filed her return of income. In order to ascertain the source of cash deposit, a letter was issued to the assessee on the available address. However, the same was returned back. Accordingly, the AO formed an opinion that income has escaped assessment. After recording the above satisfaction and obtaining approvals, notice was issued to the assessee under section 148 of the Act and was served by way of affixture through the Inspector. The above notice was also served to Shri Mahavir, husband of the assessee on 23.08.2017. 3. In response, ld. AR of the assessee attended on few occasions and afterwards not complied to the other notices. Since there was no compliance from the assessee side, the AO proceeded to complete the assessment under section 144 read with section 147 based on the material available on record. Further, AO observed that assessee has maintained three bank accounts with Syndicate Bank. Based on the details available with him, he came to the conclusion that assessee had unexplained cash deposit in her bank account to the extent of Rs.58,07,200/-. Accordingly, he assessed the above said amount as total income of the assessee. 4. Aggrieved with the above order, assessee preferred an appeal before the NFAC, Delhi. Before the ld. CIT(A), assessee made submission that the source of cash deposited by the assessee are out of cash withdrawal made by her husband and also submitted copy of the sale deed and also a gift deed as per which husband of the assessee gifted certain amount to the assessee and all these documents were submitted as additional evidence before the ld. CIT(A) and ld. CIT(A) did not admit the additional evidence for the reason these were not submitted during assessment proceedings even though several opportunities were granted to the assessee by the AO. Since assessee/ld. AR of the assessee appeared in several proceedings before the AO but did not file any objection regarding validity of issuance of notice under section 147 at any stage and did not file any details before the Assessing Officer, based on the above observation, ld. CIT(A) proceeded to reject the additional evidence as well as dismissed the appeal filed by the assessee. 5. Aggrieved with the above order, assessee is in appeal before us raising following grounds of appeal:- “1. On facts and circumstances of the case, the authorities below have erred in upholding the reassessment proceedings ignoring the fact that impugned assessment is invalid and without jurisdiction as the said assessment is completed without complying with legal requirements of the provisions of section 147/148/151/149 of the Income Tax Act therefore such assessment is void ab initio and liable to be quashed. 2. On facts and circumstances of the case, the authorities below have erred in upholding the reassessment proceedings ignoring the fact that reassessment proceedings-initiated u/s 147/148 of the IT Act ignoring the contention of appellant that the proceedings have been initiated by the AO without application of independent mind on the material. Therefore, such reassessment is void ab initio and liable to be quashed. 3. On facts and circumstances of the case, the authorities below have erred in upholding the reassessment proceedings ignoring the fact that sanction u/s 151 of IT Act shows mechanical satisfaction by the approving authority. 4. The Ld. CIT(A) has erred both in law and circumstances of the cases in upholding the addition of Rs.58,07,200/- u/s 69A of IT Act ignoring the fact that the source of cash deposit is out of explained sources and therefore, the invocation of provision of see 69A and above addition both are not tenable in law.” 6. At the time of hearing ld. AR of the assessee only made submissions on merit and other grounds raised by the assessee are not adjudicated. On merits, ld. AR of the assessee submitted as under:- “Ground No. 4: Merits of Addition of Rs.58,07,200/- From the reasons recorded and discussion in the assessment order, particularly in para 2.2 at page 2 therein, the reason for addition u/s 69A of IT Act is the deposit of cash in the different bank accounts of Syndicate Bank as per the following details: Date of Deposit Account Number Paper Book Reference Amount (₹) 19.09.2009 90152010422735 3 5,000 23.09.2009 90152010422735 3 18,00,000 24.09.2009 90802010066164 6–7 20,00,000 24.09.2009 90802010066164 6–7 1,100 24.09.2009 90802010066198 4–5 20,00,000 24.09.2009 90802010066198 4–5 1,100 Total 58,07,200 From the above details, it can be noticed that the deposits in the bank accounts were made in two consecutive days, i.e. 23.09.2009 and 24.09.2009, of Rs.18,00,000/- and Rs.40,02,200/- respectively. The other deposit of Rs.5,000/- was on 19.09.2009. The above amounts were utilized, as noted by AO in para 2.2 at page 2 of the AO’s order, for investment in the fixed deposit of Rs.58,00,000/- with Syndicate Bank. This fact is supported by the copies of the bank statements (PB3-7). The appellant opened the bank account with the Syndicate Bank on 19.09.2009 after depositing Rs.5,000/-in account number 90152010422735. The source of the other deposits of Rs.58,02,200/- is the out-of-cash withdrawal of Rs.90,00,000/- on 23.09.2009 by her husband, Sh. Mahabir, from his SBI account No.30314693508 (PB 9).Thereafter, the above account of her husband was closed on 24.09.2009 after making the remaining withdrawals (PB 10). Coming to the source of transferred funds in the hands of the husband of the appellant, Sh. Mahabir, the same is explained to be sale, along with his brother Sh. Suresh (Other joint Co-owner), of agricultural land at Village Bakkarwala with an area of 19 bigha, 4 biswa i.e. 4 acres, for total sale consideration of Rs.11,00,00,000/- through a registered sale deed dt: 04.04.2008 (PB 18-59). The above transaction of sale pertains to AY 2009-10 and the assessment year under appeal is AY 2010-11. The above Sh. Mahabir had received the following amounts as his share of sale consideration as per the following table: Date of Receipt Mode of Payment Paper Book Reference Amount (₹) 31.11.2007 Cheque 3 3,00,000 31.11.2007 Cash 3 20,000 08.12.2007 Cheque 6–7 30,91,900 07.02.2008 Cheque 6–7 5,00,000 07.02.2008 Cheque 4–5 5,00,000 03.04.2008 Cheque 4–5 2,77,11,435 Total 3,21,23,335 The above sale consideration was invested by the husband in the FDR’s and source of credits in accounts of appellant assessee is the encashment of FDR by her husband duly credited on 22.09.2009 (PB 9).It is also extremely relevant to note that the above transaction of sale of property was duly inquired into by Investigation Wing, Unit III(2), New Delhi on 31.07.2009 (PB 16-17) through a summons issued u/s 131(1A) of IT Act of even date. In the summons, the assessee was required to explain the transaction of sale of land at Village Bakkarwala for Rs.11 Crore on 04.04.2008. The above transaction of sale was explained to the satisfaction of the above authority, and the department has initiated assessment proceedings in the case of Sh. Mahabir for AY 2009-10. Copy of the notice dt: 15.04.2013 for AY 2009-10 is enclosed (PB 14-15), which shows that the transaction of sale of property by her husband Sh. Mahabir was within the knowledge of the department. The copy of the gift deed of her husband was filed before the Ld. CIT (A) as additional evidences with the application u/r 46A. The reason given in support of the prayed admission was that the appellant, a house wife with no educational background, belonging to a farmer family, was not aware of the implications of the income tax assessment proceedings. Further, she also did not have access to professional help to make the submissions on the issues involved. In any case, the Ld AO obtained the bank statements of the appellant from the bank, and the same were used by him to give deposit-wise details of bank deposits in para 2.2 of the assessment order. Coming to the explanation regarding the source of the deposits in the bank account of the appellant, which was explained to be out of the amount provided by the husband, Sh. Mahabir, out of his bank withdrawals; undisputedly, the copies of the bank statements of the husband, Sh. Mahabir had also been with the Ld. AO, as the same AO has completed the assessment of the husband on the same date, i.e. 21.12.201, the date on which the appellant’s assessment was completed. It is a settled law that when additional evidence is highly relevant to explaining the source of cash deposits u/s 69A and goes to the root of the matter, the judiciary consistently takes a liberal approach. The ultimate goal of the appellate authority is to ensure a just and fair assessment, rather than penalising a taxpayer for a technical omission. Reliance is placed on the decision of CIT Vs Prabhavati Shah 231 ITR 1 (Bom), CIT Vs Suretech Hospital & Research Centre Ltd 293 ITR 53(Bom), Zia Ur Rehman Vs ITO (2018)96 taxmann.com 482 (Delhi-Trib.) and Mayur Batra v. ACIT, ITA No 7586 /Del 2019 dt.28.11.2022. In view of the explanation showing a direct nexus of the source of cash deposit being amount received from her husband, the action of the AO treating the aggregate deposits as unexplained money u/s 69A of IT Act is not supported by the facts of the case. In view of the above, the above addition needs to be deleted as the source of cash deposit is clearly explained with the help of evidences already on record of the Ld. AO.” 7. On the other hand, ld. DR objected to the above submissions made by the assessee and he submitted that no details were submitted before the AO. Further he submitted that the gift deed is dated 05.2.2019. It is executed subsequent to the date of assessment order and submitted that this is nothing but afterthought. 8. Considered the rival submissions and material placed on record. We observed that as per the information available with the AO, assessee had deposited cash in her bank account and also not filed any return of income. Since there was no compliance from the assessee side, he proceeded to complete the assessment based on the information available with him. However, the assessee had filed details and additional evidence before Ld. CIT(A). Ld CIT(A) rejected the additional evidence for the reason that the assessee had not utilized the various opportunities given at the assessment proceedings. After careful consideration, we observed that the additional evidence cannot be rejected merely because no compliance was made during the assessment proceedings, the relevant situation and position of the assessee also had to be considered. In our view, the tax cannot be levied and collected without there being any actual liability. In the given case, we noticed that there is live link with the cash deposits and transactions made by the husband of the assessee. We noticed that the same AO had assessed the assessment for both the assessee and her husband, he could have analyzed the deposits by combining the data of both the parties. In our view, the additional evidence was rejected in haste and this needs to verify in proper perspective. For the sake of justice, we are inclined to admit the additional evidence under rule 46A and also would like to remit this issue back to the file of AO, with the limited purpose of verification of cash deposit in the bank account maintained by the assessee and the relevant source of cash deposits from the account of the husband of the assessee after considering the relevant cash withdrawals in husband’s bank account and the gift deed submitted by the assessee. This is needless to say that the assessee may be given proper opportunity of being heard. In the result, ground no.4 raised by the assessee is allowed in above terms. 9. In the result, appeal filed by the assessee is allowed as indicated above.
