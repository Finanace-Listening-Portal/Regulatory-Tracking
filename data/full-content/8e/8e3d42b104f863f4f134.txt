Advt The $127.2 billion raised through the 2026 FCNR(B) scheme is several times the amounts mobilised through earlier foreign-currency programmes. The India Development Bond in 1991 raised $3.3 billion, while the Resurgent India Bonds in 1998 mobilised $4.7 billion and the India Millennium Deposit scheme in 2000 raised $5.5 billion.The 2013 FCNR(B) scheme mobilised $26 billion, equivalent to 1.4% of GDP and 40% of annual private remittances. The 2026 scheme, by comparison, raised an amount equivalent to 75.6% of annual private remittances.The scale of the latest mobilisation is also significant relative to the size of the NRI deposit base. Outstanding NRI deposits stood at $165.7 billion in 2026, compared with $67.9 billion in 2013. The Indian diaspora, comprising NRIs and PIOs, was estimated at 37.3 million in 2026, compared with 21.9 million in 2013.Larger diaspora and different rate environmentThe 2026 FCNR(B) scheme offered a coupon of 6%, compared with 4.4% under the 2013 programme. The US two-year yield was around 4% during the 2026 scheme, compared with 0.3% in 2013.The latest mobilisation therefore came against a markedly different interest-rate environment from 2013, while the size of India’s diaspora and outstanding NRI deposit base had also increased substantially.The 2026 scheme raised an amount equivalent to 76.8% of outstanding NRI deposits, compared with 38.3% in 2013. This makes the latest programme the strongest mobilisation among the schemes compared, both relative to GDP and the existing NRI deposit base. India Development Bonds raised 0.6% of GDP in 1991, Resurgent India Bonds 1% in 1998 and India Millennium Deposits 1.2% in 2000.The 2026 FCNR(B) mobilisation at 3.1% of GDP therefore represents a substantial increase over all three earlier programmes. Even against the previous FCNR(B) exercise in 2013, the latest mobilisation was more than twice as large relative to GDP.The comparison underscores the exceptional scale of the 2026 FCNR(B) exercise, with mobilisation reaching a much larger share of India’s economy, annual remittances and outstanding NRI deposits than in previous foreign-currency mobilisation programmes.
