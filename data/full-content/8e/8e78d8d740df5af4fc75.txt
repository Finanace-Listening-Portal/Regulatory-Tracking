Articles 2 min read Bank of India expects to gain nearly Rs 100 crore a quarter from UPI MDR The proposed 0.4 per cent merchant discount rate (MDR) on eligible UPI merchant transactions above Rs 2,000 could open up a new revenue stream for banks, with Bank of India expecting to gain nearly Rs 100 crore from the framework. BoI had more than 11.01 lakh POS terminals and QR codes as of September 30, 2026, according to its investor presentation. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Oct 24, 2026 at 02:08 AM IST var news_msid = 135155400; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook State owned Bank of India expects to gain nearly Rs 100 crore from the proposed merchant discount rate (MDR) on select Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions on a quarterly basis, its Managing Director & CEO Rajneesh Karnatak told ETBFSI.The estimate comes as the government introduces a fee framework for select high-value merchant transactions on UPI, creating a new revenue stream for banks and payment companies. Brokerages estimate that the move could generate an annual revenue pool of Rs 15,000-20,600 crore for the digital payments and banking ecosystem, although the eventual gains will depend on eligible transaction volumes and the role individual institutions play in the payment chain. Advt BoI’s investor presentation showed that the bank processed 2.21 billion transactions through its alternative delivery channels (ADCs) in Q2 FY27, up 25 per cent from 1.77 billion in the year-ago quarter. The value of these transactions rose 44 per cent year-on-year to Rs 12,039 billion from Rs 8,354 billion.The bank had a network of more than 11.01 lakh point-of-sale (POS) terminals and QR codes as of September 30, 2026, according to the presentation. It has also launched a UPI-linked RuPay credit card under the PM SVANidhi scheme for street vendors and is developing its interoperable Banking Connect platform for merchant transactions.The ADC figures cover digital banking channels overall and are not a standalone measure of BoI’s UPI transactions.Under the new framework, a 0.4 per cent MDR applies to specified person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, subject to a cap of Rs 300 per transaction. Certain categories attract concessional rates, while person-to-person payments and most merchant transactions remain outside the charge. The government has said around 96 per cent of merchant transactions will remain unaffected.The MDR will be distributed across participants in the payments ecosystem. Under the reported sharing arrangement, 40 per cent of the fee goes to the customer’s issuing bank, 30 per cent to the merchant-acquiring bank, 20 per cent to the UPI application and 10 per cent to the bank supporting the application. The amount earned by an individual bank will therefore depend on its position in the payment chain and the transactions routed through it. Advt The proposed MDR marks a shift from the zero-MDR model that has governed most UPI payments since 2020.
