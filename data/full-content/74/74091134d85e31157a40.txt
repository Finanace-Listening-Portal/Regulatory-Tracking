NFAC/2013-14/10072600, NFAC/2014-15/10072973, NFAC/2015-16/10055744 and NFAC/2016-17/10058197 dated 01.08.2025 for the AYs 2014-15 to 2017-18 respectively and in Appeal No. NFAC/2017-18/10089791 dated 10.07.2025 for AY 18-19. As these appeals involve common issues, they are disposed off by this common order for the sake of convenience. 2. At the outset, there is a delay of 115 days in the filing of appeal for AY 14-15 to AY 16-17, 164 days for AY 17-18 and 146 for AY 18-19. The appellant has filed affidavit and condonation petition seeking condonation of the delay. Considering the explanation given in the affidavit for the delay, we condone the delay in the filing of these appeals, and the appeals are taken up for adjudication. 3. The brief facts on record is that the AO based on information received through the Investigation wing, with reference to search conducted in the case of M/s Laxmi Remote India Pvt Ltd (LRIPL), that the said entity has made undisclosed sales to various parties, including the appellant herein, for substantial amounts for various years, initiated proceedings under sec. 147 of the Income tax Act. Subsequently, noting that the assessee had made cash purchases from the said entity, and which was not disclosed in the return of the assessee, the Assessing Officer proceeded to make the impugned additions under sec. 69C, to the total income for the subject years as under: AY 2014-15 Rs.1,69,53,826/ AY 2015-16 Rs 1,98,10,566/- AY 2016-17 Rs.1,95,39,113/- AY 2017-18 Rs 1,63,45,272 AY 2018-19 Rs. 57,19,174/- 4. Subsequently, the impugned additions have been upheld by the Ld CIT(A) vide orders referred supra. Aggrieved, the above appeals are filed before us. 5. The Ld AR contended that the impugned additions are without any basis and have been upheld by the Ld CIT(A) in a mechanical manner without application of mind. It was contended that the AO ought to have initiated proceedings under sec. 153C and not under sec. 147, and that the proceedings under sec. 147 is also not valid as there is no live link to establish escapement of income. It was also contended that the assessee was not provided with the material evidence relied on by the Assessing officer in making the impugned additions, and thus violates the principles of natural justice. The Ld AR relied on various decisions in this regard. It was further argued that all the purchases were made in cheque by the appellant and that the impugned addition was based on mere conjecture and liable to be deleted. 6. On the other hand, the DR argued that the initiation of proceedings was valid and based on information received from the Investigation wing. It was also argued that the Ld CIT(A) has confirmed the addition giving reasons and thus pleaded to uphold the order of the Ld CIT (A). 7. We have considered the rival submissions, and also perused the records. From the assessment order it is seen that that a search was conducted in the case of Laxmi Remote India Pvt Ltd on 16-10-2017. The search operation revealed that the said entity had engaged in large scale unaccounted cash sales and such transactions were recorded in its IVMS software, and those transactions disclosed to the government agencies were recorded in ERP software, and that the assessee is one of the parties with whom substantial cash sales have been made by the said company (LRIPL) for all these years, as per its IVMS data. The Assessing officer also refers to the statements given by Vijaya Kumar Sachdeva, director of the said company, under sec. 132(4) conceding to modus operandi in suppression of sales; and also by Pawan Kumar Midha, general manager of the company, who admitted to manipulation in the recording of sales for the purpose of sales suppression. Based on these, the Assessing officer made the impugned additions under sec. 69C of the IT Act. Thus, we find that the Assessing officer had sufficient basis and material information to come to the conclusion that income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment in the assessee’s case, and hence, we uphold the validity of the proceedings initiated under sec. 147 of the Act. Besides, we note that the Assessing officer has rightly initiated proceedings under sec. 147, instead of sec. 153C as argued by the Ld AR, as this would not fall within the express ambit of sec. 153C, for the reason that the documents/assets belonging or pertaining to the assessee have not been seized. We further note that the assessee has not been provided with the material evidence and have not been offered the opportunity to rebut the said evidence. We note that the assessee has raised this plea before the Ld CIT (A) that he has not been provided with the sworn statements and other material evidence, and that opportunity of cross examination was not afforded. However, no specific finding has been given on this issue by the Ld CIT(A). Thus, we are convinced that the appellant has not been given sufficient opportunity to rebut the material evidence relied on by the Department. We further note that the Ld CIT(A) has remarked that it is a clear cut case of accommodation entry without reference to any material. In this factual matrix, we consider it fit, in the interest of justice, to set aside the impugned assessments for the AY 14-15 to AY 18-19, and restore to the file of the Assessing officer for de novo assessments after affording adequate opportunity of hearing in compliance to the principles of natural justice. The Assessing officer shall provide all the materials/information/evidence/statements available with him to the appellant for his rebuttal and explanation. The assessee shall comply to the hearing notices. 8. As a result, the impugned assessments for the AY 2014-15 to AY 2018-19 are set aside to the file of the assessing officer for framing of fresh assessments in compliance to the principles of natural justice. 9. In the result, all the appeals of the assessee are partly allowed for statistical purposes.
