Summary: LIC Jeevan Lakshya, Plan 733 (UIN 512N297V03), is an individual participating, non-linked life insurance savings policy combining protection with a maturity benefit. Its distinguishing feature is the structure of benefits after the insured dies during the policy term: annual income equal to 10% of the basic sum assured may be paid on specified policy anniversaries, while a maturity-linked amount equal to 110% of the basic sum assured and eligible vested bonuses becomes payable at maturity, subject to policy terms. On survival, the basic sum assured and applicable bonuses form the maturity benefit. The premium-paying period is three years shorter than the policy term. LIC’s current product brochure specifies entry and maturity ages, grace periods, surrender conditions and loan limits; illustrations and bonuses must not be mistaken for guaranteed investment returns. From 22 September 2025, individual life insurance premiums are generally exempt from GST under the notified regime, whereas group insurance remains outside that exemption. For Tax Year 2026-27, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies: premium deductions depend on Section 123, Schedule XV and the chosen tax regime, while exemption of proceeds must be tested under Section 11 read with Schedule II, including premium-to-sum-assured and aggregate premium conditions. This article explains these distinctions without endorsing the product. It is not a unit-linked investment product and does not give the policyholder a direct claim on a market portfolio. It combines a stated basic sum assured, a structured family-support feature on death and participation in the insurer’s declared surplus. The distinction matters because the protection amount, payment timing and potential bonus component cannot be reduced to a single headline “return”. The insurer’s English sales brochure applicable from 22 September 2025 is the principal product source for this review. The policy schedule, customer information sheet and terms actually issued to a policyholder remain controlling for an individual contract. An advertisement, premium calculator or sales illustration should not be substituted for the contractual definitions of the life assured, nominee, commencement of risk, in-force status and maturity date. Yearly, half-yearly, quarterly and NACH-based monthly payment modes are available, along with salary deduction where applicable. LIC publishes mode and high-sum-assured rebates, but the final premium depends on age, term, underwriting, riders and mode. The sample premiums in the brochure are illustrations for standard lives, not a quotation for every applicant. Why the last three years still matter The difference between the policy term and premium-paying term should not be interpreted as three years of free insurance. The premium structure is designed into the product from inception. Policyholders should compare the complete premium schedule with the entire expected benefit schedule, and separately assess inflation, liquidity needs and the opportunity cost of a long commitment. The ability to stop premiums without loss of original benefits should never be assumed. Death Benefit: Annual Income and the Maturity-Linked Amount The defining feature of Jeevan Lakshya is the sequence of payments following the life assured’s death while the policy is in force. LIC describes the “sum assured on death” using a comparison between seven times the annualised premium and a benefit package consisting of 110% of basic sum assured payable at the maturity date plus annual income equal to 10% of basic sum assured on the specified anniversaries from death to the anniversary before maturity. The death-benefit definition is also subject to a floor of 105% of total premiums paid up to death. The actual operative comparison and payment schedule must be read together rather than treating every element as immediately payable cash. For example, if the basic sum assured is ₹10 lakh, the 10% annual income reference is ₹1 lakh per eligible policy anniversary, and 110% of the basic sum assured is ₹11 lakh at the maturity-linked payment date. This is a mechanical illustration of two stated percentages only. The number of annual payments depends on when death occurs; the full policy illustration and the seven-times-premium test may affect the benefit calculation. It is not a personalised claim estimate, and no bonus has been assumed. Treatment of participating bonuses The policy participates in LIC’s profits and may accrue simple reversionary bonuses when declared, while a final additional bonus may apply under the insurer’s declared terms. The brochure says vested bonuses and any final additional bonus included in the death benefit are payable at the due date of maturity. Future bonus rates are not guaranteed merely because a historic rate or a 4%/8% illustration appears in a benefit illustration. Once declared, vested bonuses are governed by the insurer’s applicable terms. Survival Benefit, Settlement Options and Riders Where the life assured survives to maturity and the policy remains in force, LIC states that the maturity benefit comprises the basic sum assured plus vested simple reversionary bonuses and any final additional bonus. The brochure also permits eligible settlement choices for receiving all or part of the maturity benefit in instalments over five, ten or fifteen years. The life assured must exercise the maturity settlement option at least three months before the claim date, and minimum instalment amounts apply. An instalment election affects cash-flow timing; it does not transform the underlying policy into a guaranteed high-yield deposit. The current brochure identifies optional accident-related and term-assurance riders, subject to the rider-specific entry, benefit and premium limits. Rider premiums are additional and rider benefits depend on the precise covered event. In particular, accidental death, accidental disability and ordinary death are not interchangeable insured events. The reader should examine the rider wording, exclusions and the point at which rider cover ends instead of assuming all riders automatically continue for the full base-policy term. Missed Premiums, Revival, Surrender and Policy Loans LIC provides a grace period of 30 days for yearly, half-yearly and quarterly premium modes, and 15 days for monthly premiums. If premiums remain unpaid after grace, the policy may lapse. The brochure permits revival within five consecutive complete years from the first unpaid premium and before maturity, subject to arrears, interest and continued insurability. Revival is not an unconditional right to restore full cover without underwriting. Where a policy acquires paid-up status, future benefits may be reduced and future bonus participation may cease. The product can be surrendered after completion of the first policy year if a full year’s premium has been paid. LIC distinguishes guaranteed surrender value, which arises after at least two full years of premiums, from special surrender value available subject to the brochure’s first-year conditions; the payable surrender value is the higher applicable amount. This is not a promise that surrender will return all premiums. A policy loan may become available after the first year within the surrender value, with limits varying by policy status and premiums paid. Outstanding principal and interest can reduce claims, and default can lead to foreclosure under the stated conditions. Free-look and exclusions The brochure gives a 30-day free-look period from receipt of the physical or electronic policy document, whichever is earlier, with specified deductions on cancellation. It also contains suicide exclusions and special claim rules within twelve months of commencement or revival. Those conditions are materially different from ordinary death cover and should be reviewed before concluding that a policy guarantees every possible death-related payment. Accurate disclosure at proposal stage is equally important under the Insurance Act and contract terms. GST on Individual Life Insurance Premiums in October 2026 A significant change for this product category took effect on 22 September 2025. Notification No. 16/2025-Central Tax (Rate), dated 17 September 2025, introduced an exemption for individual life insurance services where the insured is not a group. The Department of Financial Services’ official FAQ confirms that individual life policies are within the exemption, while employer-sponsored and other group life policies continue to attract 18% GST. For instalment premiums, the FAQ explains the application of the exemption by reference to payment timing, subject to the transitional time-of-supply rules where relevant. Accordingly, an ordinary individual Jeevan Lakshya premium paid in October 2026 should not be described using an outdated blanket 18% GST assumption. A brochure’s generic statement that statutory taxes may be charged if applicable does not override the notified exemption. Charges relating to a separate service or an unusual contract arrangement must be assessed on their own facts; a policyholder should check the premium receipt and ask the insurer to explain any GST shown. Income Tax in Tax Year 2026-27: Premium Deduction and Proceeds The Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force on 1 April 2026. For Tax Year 2026-27, Section 123 read with Schedule XV is the relevant successor to the familiar Section 80C framework for eligible life-insurance premium deductions. The overall deduction ceiling is ₹1,50,000 across the qualifying items and remains subject to statutory conditions, including the prescribed relationship between premium and actual capital sum assured. Crucially, the deduction under Section 123 is not available to an assessee computing income under the new concessional tax regime under Section 202, as clarified in the Income Tax Department’s transition FAQ. A person should therefore not claim a deduction merely because the premium receipt bears the word “insurance”. Section 11 read with Schedule II (Table, serial number 2) governs the exclusion of eligible life-insurance receipts from total income under the new Act. For non-ULIP policies issued on or after 1 April 2023, the schedule carries premium-to-sum-assured conditions and an aggregate annual premium ceiling for the relevant policies, with specific exceptions and death-claim treatment. The old Section 10(10D) terminology remains useful for comparing historic policies but is not a substitute for citing the operative 2025 Act for current tax years. If proceeds do not qualify for exclusion, their treatment and any applicable withholding must be examined separately; the whole receipt should not automatically be described as taxable profit. Practical Document Check Before Publication or Purchase The essential verification documents are the insurer’s current brochure, signed policy schedule, customer information sheet, benefit illustration, premium schedule and any rider documents. Confirm the UIN, basic sum assured, life assured’s age, nominee details, premium-paying term, maturity date, annual-income dates and all exclusions. Retain payment receipts and separate old-regime deduction eligibility from maturity-exemption eligibility. An insurer illustration may help compare scenarios, but actual bonuses and surrender values are determined under the policy, not by a general online return calculator. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Is LIC Jeevan Lakshya 733 a ULIP? No. LIC identifies it as a participating, non-linked individual savings life policy. The declared bonus mechanism is different from market-linked units. 2. How long are premiums payable? The brochure states that the premium-paying term equals the selected policy term minus three years. A 20-year term therefore normally has 17 years of scheduled premiums. 3. Is the 10% annual family income guaranteed after every death? It is a stated component of the death-benefit design for eligible in-force claims, but its timing and interaction with the full sum-assured-on-death definition depend on the policy conditions and the death date. 4. Is GST charged on individual Jeevan Lakshya premiums in October 2026? The notified exemption for individual life insurance services generally applies from 22 September 2025. Group contracts and unusual separate charges require their own analysis. 5. Can every policyholder deduct premiums from taxable income? No. Section 123 and Schedule XV impose conditions, and the new concessional tax regime under Section 202 does not permit this deduction. 6. Is every maturity receipt automatically tax-free? No. Section 11 and Schedule II of the Income-tax Act, 2025 contain conditions including relevant premium ratios and aggregate premium limits; death-related receipts have separate protections. 7. Can the policy be surrendered after one year? The brochure permits surrender after the first year where a full year’s premium has been paid, but the guaranteed surrender value has a separate two-year premium condition and the amount may be much lower than premiums paid. Key Takeaways Plan 733 is an individual participating, non-linked policy with family-income and maturity-linked death benefits, not a fixed-return investment. Policy term runs from 13 to 25 years; premiums are scheduled for three years fewer than the selected term. Bonus projections, surrender values and optional rider payments require individual policy verification. Individual life-insurance premium services are generally GST-exempt from 22 September 2025; group insurance differs. For Tax Year 2026-27, apply the Income-tax Act, 2025, especially Sections 11 and 123 and their schedules, with regime-specific conditions.
