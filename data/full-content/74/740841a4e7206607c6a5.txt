Below is her analysis:Gold had a brutal week, with spot gold closing at $3,850/oz yesterday, down 4.54% WoW, while MCX gold settled at Rs 1,34,223/10g, down 4.48%. Spot gold is down 0.64% MTD and 10.88% YTD, putting it on track for its worst annual performance since 2013, while MCX gold is down just 0.90% YTD as rupee weakness cushions domestic prices.Silver fared worse, with spot closing at $47.41/oz, down 8.01% WoW, and MCX silver at Rs 1,69,702/kg, down 7.05%. Spot silver is down 33.85% YTD internationally. Gold briefly touched near $3,790 during the session, marking its third consecutive day of declines. Advt The sell-off is increasingly driven by rising interest-rate expectations, with markets pricing in Fed hikes in December and January and another move by April.The US 10-year Treasury yield at 5.15% and a firm dollar are weighing on bullion by raising the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset and making gold more expensive for overseas buyers. These factors remain the primary headwinds for prices, keeping the near-term trend under pressure.The inflationary impact of the Iran war has also weakened gold’s traditional safe-haven appeal, as higher oil prices fuel inflation concerns and reinforce expectations of monetary tightening.Gold has declined more than 25% since the war began in late February, with equities and Bitcoin also falling amid the broader rates-driven sell-off. While ETFs added 211 tonnes in Q3 and central banks purchased 675 tonnes in the first nine months, slower bar-and-coin demand and a major central bank reaching its 700-tonne buying target could moderate support, leaving ETF flows increasingly important for the next price direction.Focus this week: FOMC October minutes (due today), Fed commentary, the 10-year yield and dollar, oil and US-Iran headlines, and ETF flows.Gold Price Outlook: Gold's near-term outlook remains bearish, with hawkish Fed expectations, Treasury yields holding above 5% and a firm dollar likely to restrict recovery attempts. Advt Markets are increasingly pricing in a December rate hike and further tightening into early 2027, keeping sentiment fragile. Selective buying near key support levels may cushion the decline, while easing crude prices could temper downside by moderating inflation concerns and reducing pressure for further monetary tightening.Brent crude sustaining around $92/bbl remains a key level to watch, a further decline could ease macro pressure on bullion, while renewed oil strength and geopolitical uncertainty may keep gold volatile. Gold is expected to trade in a $3,700–$4,050/oz range internationally and Rs 1,28,000–Rs 1,40,000/10g on MCX, with a break below support potentially extending the correction.
