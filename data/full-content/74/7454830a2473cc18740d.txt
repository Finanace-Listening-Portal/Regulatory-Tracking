However, management said the Q1 number included Rs 284 crore of interest income from a tax refund and that the comparable NIM for Q1 was 3.35%.“Our starting point really should be 3.35 and that should be the comparative for this quarter, which is at 3.23. So, it is a 12 basis point quarter-on-quarter drop. Yes, 7 to 8 basis points, a large chunk of that would be the FCNR impact and then some impact from loan mix, etc.,” CFO Viral Damania said. Advt The bank expects the margin trajectory to improve as it optimises its funding mix and increases retail asset growth.“We do believe we can recoup some of that 12 basis point drop that you saw in NIM. So, yes, directionally, we should see that moving back up over Q3 and Q4,” CFO Damania added.The lender also said the rate hikes already undertaken, along with expectations of further increases, should be positive for NIMs across the banking system and for IndusInd Bank.FCNR(B) funds used to pare high-cost depositsIndusInd Bank mobilised USD 3.51 billion of FCNR(B) deposits under the RBI swap window during the quarter, taking its share of such mobilisation to around 2.75 per cent, against its natural deposit market share of about 1.5 per cent. The bank said the inflows were also supported by a relatively higher share of retail and non-leveraged deposits. The bank has not waited for the entire FCNR(B) pool to be deployed into loans. Instead, it has used part of the long-term funding to reduce expensive domestic deposits.“We have used the opportunity to let go of the high cost CD. So, that is down 10,000 crores already,” MD & CEO Rajiv Anand said.The investor presentation shows that certificates of deposit accounted for 3.3 per cent of total deposits in Q2 FY27, down from 5.9 per cent in Q1 FY27 and 6.4 per cent a year earlier. The cost of deposits stood at 5.96 per cent, while the cost of funds was 5.11 per cent. FCNR(B) money is effectively now part of the bank’s funding pool, with deployment happening through interest-earning assets, investments or balance-sheet optimisation. It also acknowledged that there is a timing lag between mobilisation and deployment. Advt Retail growth to support margin recoveryThe bank expects its retail asset book to grow faster in Q3 and continue the momentum in Q4.Retail loans grew 12 per cent QoQ to Rs 2.10 lakh crore in Q2 FY27, accounting for 58 per cent of the overall loan book. Overall advances grew 11 per cent YoY and 11 per cent QoQ to Rs 3.63 lakh crore. Importantly, management said the growth in advances was not primarily driven by FCNR-linked loans. Wholesale banking grew nearly 10 per cent QoQ, while the retail book excluding FCNR-related advances grew around 4–5 per cent. The bank's overall deposits rose 10 per cent YoY, while average total deposits increased 7 per cent QoQ. Retail and small-business-community deposits stood at Rs 2.02 lakh crore, up 6 per cent QoQ and 12 per cent YoY. Advt MFI growth returns, but bank remains cautious on drought-hit statesIndusInd's microfinance book grew 8 per cent QoQ in Q2, marking the first significant growth in around six to eight quarters. Management said it is reasonably confident of building on the momentum, while remaining cautious in states that have declared drought. On the broader rural book, the bank said it was not seeing any stress yet despite drought declarations in Karnataka, Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh, although it would remain “extremely watchful and calibrated” in these geographies.
