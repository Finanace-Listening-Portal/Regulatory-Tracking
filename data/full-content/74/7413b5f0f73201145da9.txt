Summary: The Reserve Bank of India (RBI), through Notification No. FEMA 23(R)/(2)/2026-RB dated 2 November 2026, has amended the Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026, with effect from 1 October 2026. The amendment simplifies Export Declaration Form (EDF) compliance for service exporters and Import Data Processing and Monitoring System (IDPMS) reporting for service imports. Under amended Regulation 3(2), resident individuals exporting services are deemed to have submitted the EDF when they provide the information required by their Authorised Dealer (AD) Bank for crediting the inward remittance, irrespective of the amount. For exporters other than resident individuals, including companies, LLPs and firms, this facility is available where the inward remittance for the service does not exceed ₹10 lakh or its foreign-currency equivalent. Other cases remain subject to applicable EDF filing requirements. Under amended Regulation 18(1)(d), AD Banks must enter service-import details in IDPMS where the importer is not a resident individual and the invoice or contract value exceeds ₹10 lakh. The prescribed reporting period is five working days after receipt of details. The export threshold is based on inward remittance value, whereas the import threshold is based on invoice or contract value. The changes reduce routine reporting without eliminating underlying FEMA compliance obligations. Questions remain regarding bank-specific information requirements and instalment receipts against invoices exceeding ₹10 lakh. The amendments take effect from 1 October 2026, although the notification was issued later. The changes concern deemed submission of the EDF for service exports, a ₹10 lakh inward-remittance limit for non-individual service exporters, and a revised threshold for IDPMS reporting of service imports. The amendment also defines a resident individual as a person resident in India who is a natural person. Deemed Submission of EDF for Export of Services Resident Individual Exporters: No Monetary Limit Under the inserted proviso (a) to Regulation 3(2), a resident individual exporting services is deemed to have submitted the EDF upon providing the AD Bank with the information required in relation to receipt of payment for the services, for crediting the exporter’s account. No ₹10 lakh ceiling applies to this category. For example, a resident individual consultant receiving ₹15 lakh from an overseas client may qualify for deemed EDF submission upon providing the required information to the AD Bank. This does not dispense with other applicable FEMA requirements, including realisation and receipt of export proceeds. Companies, LLPs and Firms: ₹10 Lakh Remittance Limit Under inserted proviso (b) to Regulation 3(2), exporters of services other than resident individuals are deemed to have submitted the EDF where the inward remittance for the service does not exceed ₹10 lakh or its foreign-currency equivalent, upon furnishing the information required by the AD Bank for crediting the payment. For example, an Indian company receiving ₹8 lakh for exported software services may qualify for deemed EDF submission. Where the inward remittance exceeds ₹10 lakh, this particular deeming facility is unavailable, and the exporter must follow otherwise applicable EDF submission requirements. Under the principal framework, the ordinary EDF submission timeline for relevant service exports is 30 days from the end of the month in which the invoice is raised, subject to applicable provisions and exceptions. IDPMS Reporting for Import of Services Reporting for Non-Individual Importers Above ₹10 Lakh Amended Regulation 18(1)(d) requires the AD Bank to enter service-import details in the Import Data Processing and Monitoring System (IDPMS) where the importer is a person other than a resident individual and the invoice or contract value exceeds ₹10 lakh or its foreign-currency equivalent. The entry must be made within five working days of receiving the relevant details. The specified IDPMS reporting requirement therefore does not apply to resident individual importers or to other importers where the relevant invoice or contract value does not exceed ₹10 lakh. This is a relaxation of the specified reporting requirement, not a general exemption from FEMA rules governing outward remittances and service imports. Matters Requiring Clarification Information Required by AD Banks The amendment refers to information required by the AD Bank in relation to receipt of payment but does not itself prescribe a separate uniform list specifically for the new deemed-submission facility. Exporters should follow the documentation and information requirements communicated by their banks. Multiple Remittances Against One Invoice For non-individual exporters, the ₹10 lakh threshold refers to inward remittance value. A practical question arises where a service-export invoice exceeding ₹10 lakh is realised through multiple instalments, each not exceeding ₹10 lakh. For example, an invoice of ₹18 lakh may be settled through two receipts of ₹9 lakh. The amendment does not expressly resolve the treatment of such arrangements. Exporters should obtain clarification from their AD Bank rather than assume that splitting receipts automatically eliminates separate EDF filing requirements. Compliance Implications Service exporters should identify whether they are resident individuals or other persons, monitor relevant inward remittance values and retain invoices, contracts, remittance particulars and information furnished to AD Banks. Service importers should distinguish IDPMS reporting relief from their continuing FEMA payment and documentation obligations. AD Banks should implement the revised classification and reporting controls while maintaining adequate transaction records. Conclusion The RBI’s Second Amendment Regulations, 2026, simplify compliance for cross-border service transactions from 1 October 2026. Resident individual service exporters benefit from deemed EDF submission without a monetary ceiling, while other exporters receive this treatment where the inward remittance does not exceed ₹10 lakh. On the import side, specified IDPMS reporting applies to non-individual importers where invoice or contract value exceeds ₹10 lakh.
