Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); ACIT Vs Deepak Khattar (ITAT Delhi) Survey Stock Shortage Cannot Be Taxed Again When the Corresponding Cash Sales Are Reconciled and Offered to Tax: ITAT Delhi Survey Revealed a Shortage of Physical Stock A survey under section 133A of the Income-tax Act, 1961 was conducted on 7 September 2016 at Jai Guru Dev Packers, the assessee’s proprietorship concern. The survey team valued the physical stock at ₹1,16,68,441, against ₹4,05,20,040 reflected in the books. The Assessing Officer made an addition of ₹2,88,51,600 for the resulting stock mismatch. During the survey, the assessee had surrendered ₹4 crore, subject to no penal action, and subsequently retracted his statement by a letter dated 13 October 2016. The assessee explained that a temporary shortage of accounts staff had delayed the electronic posting of routine cash sales. Goods had already been sold, while the corresponding sales entries were pending. The cash sales, aggregating to ₹3,14,48,244, were entered before the books were finalised and taken into account in the return. The Assessing Officer considered the subsequent entries an attempt to accommodate unaccounted money. The CIT(A) accepted the reconciliation and deleted the addition. The Revenue appealed. Reconciled Cash Sales Cannot Yield a Second Addition The Tribunal found that the stock shortage corresponded to genuine cash sales which had yet to be posted on the survey date. Those sales were subsequently recorded in the final accounts and offered to tax through the return. A separate addition for the same transactions, described as stock mismatch, would therefore result in double taxation of the same income. The assessee had produced the audited books during scrutiny. The Assessing Officer had accepted them without invoking section 145 and had brought no material to displace the stock reconciliation. The Tribunal distinguished N.K. Proteins Ltd. v. CIT [2017] 250 Taxman 22 (SC), which concerned bogus purchases and accommodation entries. Here, the evidence linked the physical shortage to ordinary sales already reflected in the final accounts. The Tribunal accordingly upheld the deletion of ₹2,88,51,600. Temporary Negative Cash Balance and Section 69A The Assessing Officer also added ₹33,50,000 under section 69A, representing two bank deposits of ₹17,50,000 and ₹16,00,000 made on 18 May 2016. The cash book available during the survey showed negative balances on certain dates, and the entries available for verification did not show sufficient cash to cover the deposits. The Revenue contended that the explanation based on subsequently posted sales was an afterthought. The Tribunal found that the assessee had traced the deposits to regular business cash sales. The incomplete cash book explained the apparent deficit, and the Revenue had produced no evidence that the transactions were fictitious. A temporary negative balance in accounts awaiting routine sales entries could not sustain an addition for unexplained money once the underlying transactions had been verified and entered in the final accounts. The Tribunal upheld the deletion. Uncashed Loan Cheques Do Not Establish Receipt of Money A separate addition of ₹60 lakh under section 69A arose from two cheques issued for a proposed loan. The loan arrangement fell through. The cheques were never presented or cleared, and the Revenue produced no evidence of any corresponding cash payment or bank transfer. Applying the test of human probabilities from Sumati Dayal v. CIT [1995] 214 ITR 801 (SC), the Tribunal treated the expiry of the cheques by the survey date as consistent with the assessee’s explanation. The existence of the instruments established a proposed transaction. The evidence showed that the assessee had received no loan funds. The Tribunal therefore upheld the deletion of ₹60 lakh. Demonetisation Deposit Explained by Recorded Cash in Hand The Revenue also challenged the deletion of ₹7,25,000 under section 68 for a deposit of specified bank notes during demonetisation. The audited accounts showed turnover of ₹13.44 crore, including cash sales of ₹3.14 crore, and opening cash in hand of ₹30,76,220 carried forward from the preceding year. The Tribunal examined whether identifiable cash from recorded sources was available to cover the deposit. It accepted the explanation on the strength of the audited records and distinguished Kale Khan Mohammad Hanif v. CIT [1963] 50 ITR 1 (SC) on the facts. The timing of the deposit during demonetisation did not displace the evidence explaining its source. The Tribunal dismissed the Revenue’s appeal in full. Author’s Comments The decisive evidence was the link between the discrepancy observed during the survey and the transactions reflected in the final accounts. A stock reconciliation explains which sales account for the goods physically missing. A date-wise cash reconciliation explains how those sales or other recorded receipts funded particular bank deposits. In this case, the evidence answered both questions. For a similar dispute, the sales invoices, stock movement records, cash book and bank statements can establish that link and show why a separate addition would tax an amount already accounted for. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI This appeal by the Revenue is arising out of the order of learned CIT(A), Delhi-31 in appeal No.CIT(A), Delhi-5/10519/2019-20 dated 15th May, 2025. Assessment was framed by the DCIT, Circle-13(1), New Delhi for the assessment year 2017-18 under Section 143(3) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’) vide order dated 30th December, 2019. 2. The first issue in this appeal by the Revenue, raised vide ground Nos.1 & 2, relates to the deletion of addition of ₹2,88,51,600/- by the learned CIT(A) on account of stock mismatch. 3. Brief facts of the case are that the assessee is an individual deriving income from salary, business/profession and other sources. He filed the return of income for the relevant assessment year 2017-18 on 30th January, 2018 declaring an income of ₹35,94,630/-. A survey under Section 133A of the Act was carried out by the Rohtak Range, Rohtak, a non-jurisdictional Officer, on 7₹ September, 2016 at the proprietorship concern of the assessee M/s Jai Guru Dev Packers. During the course of survey proceedings, statements of various persons including the assessee were recorded on oath and various incriminating documents were impounded from the premises. Subsequently, the case records were transferred to ITO, Ward 13(1), New Delhi vide letter dated 22nd March, 2018 and subsequently to the DCIT, Circle-13(1) vide transfer memo dated 7″ September, 2018. Notices u/s 143(2) of the Act were issued through ITBA portal to the assessee and, in response to the same, replies were submitted which were examined by the Assessing Officer. Assessment was completed vide order dated 30th December, 2019 wherein the addition of 2,88,51,600/- was made on account of stock mismatch. During the course of survey proceedings, the appellant surrendered a sum of ₹4,00,00,000/- in the capacity of proprietor of M/s Jai Guru Dev Packers subject to no penal action. However, later on, vide letter dated 13 October, 2016, he retracted the contents of the statement recorded during the course of survey conducted under Section 133A of the Act. The Assessing Officer noted that on physical valuation of stock, it was found that the value of the stock was at ₹1,16,68,441/-, whereas, as per the books of account, the stock was valued at ₹4,05,20,040/-. The assessee was asked to explain the difference of ₹2,88,51,600/-. In response, the assessee submitted that the cash sales of ₹3,14,48,244/- have been recorded in the books of account. It was submitted by him that during the period, he was having shortage of manpower and, due to which, the accounting entries could not be completed on regular basis. The mismatch in stock was due to unrecorded sales of ₹3,14,48,244/-, which were recorded by the assessee before finalizing the books. However, the Assessing Officer held that the assessee had inflated the books of account just to accommodate unaccounted money. Accordingly, he added the difference of ₹2,88,51,600/- on account of stock mismatch to the total income of the assessee. Aggrieved, assessee preferred appeal before the learned CIT(A). 4. Learned CIT(A), after examining the facts and the reconciliation, categorically held in paragraph Nos.10 and 11 of his order that the impugned addition represented a double taxation of the same income, once, as stock mismatch at the time of survey under Section 133A of the Act, and again, as cash sales already recorded in the books, and offered to tax in the return of income for the relevant assessment year by the assessee himself. Learned CIT(A), therefore, deleted the addition on this count. Aggrieved, the Revenue has filed appeal before the Tribunal. 5. The learned Senior DR supported the assessment order and submitted that assessee has inflated its books of account to accommodate unaccounted money as certain cash sales were not recorded in the books of account as on the date of survey. Further, there was a difference between physical stock found during the survey and the stock recorded in the books, which the assessee has been unable to counter, at the time of survey, that the difference in the stock was nothing but cash sales not recorded in the books as on that date. 6. On the other hand, the learned Counsel for the assessee submitted that the sole allegation made by the search team was that certain cash sales were not recorded in the books of account as on the date of survey. The said issue was duly and satisfactorily explained during assessment proceedings, wherein it was clarified that due to temporary shortage of staff in the accounts department, the books were not electronically updated on the date of survey. Even in the assessment order, the Assessing Officer merely alleged a difference between physical stock found during the survey and the stock recorded in the books, on which basis alone, the Assessing Officer concluded that the assessee had not disclosed certain cash sales. Further, before the learned CIT(A), it was duly explained that the stock mismatch was purely on account of cash sales which had not been recorded in the books as on the date of survey. However, these sales were subsequently duly recorded before finalization of accounts and offered to tax in the return of income filed under Section 139 of the Act and the tax thereon duly paid. The learned Counsel for the assessee also argued that the stand of the Revenue is misconceived and completely ignores the facts and findings recorded at the assessment stage. The learned CIT(A) has correctly held that sustaining the addition despite the above reconciliation would result in double taxation of the same income, which is impermissible in law. He, therefore, requested that the order of learned CIT(A) may be upheld. 7. We have heard the submissions of both the sides and perused the material placed before us. It is observed that the mismatch in stock had arisen from a delay in electronically updating the books of account due to temporary shortage of staff in the accounts department of the assessee on the date of the survey. Crucially, the missing entries represented regular cash sales. These identical cash sales were subsequently fully recorded in the books of account before finalization of accounts and duly incorporated into the regular return of income filed under Section 139(1) of the Act, and appropriate taxes were fully paid thereon. It is a settled principle of law that the same income cannot be taxed twice. Once the cash sales corresponding to the stock mismatch have been recorded in the books of account and offered to tax in the regular return of income, making a separate addition for the exact same amount under the head “stock mismatch” amounts to double taxation. We find that the reliance placed by the Revenue, in the grounds of appeal, on the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in NK Proteins Ltd. Vs. CIT —- [2017] 250 Taxman 22 (SC) is completely misplaced. The facts involved in that case pertained to deep-rooted structural bogus purchase _ transactions and accommodation entries aimed at artificial profit reduction. In the present case, there is absolutely no allegation by the Revenue at any stage that there were bogus transactions, accommodation entries, or the circulation of unaccounted money. We also noted that the entire cash sales were recorded in the books of account and assessee’s accounts are audited. The books of account were produced before the Assessing Officer during the course of scrutiny assessment but, without rejecting the books of account under Section 145 of the Act, the Assessing Officer straightaway proceeded to make the addition, which is not permissible in law. The dispute here strictly pertains to a physical stock variance that is directly linked to unrecorded cash sales on the date of the survey. Once the books of account are not rejected and even the Revenue has failed to bring any material evidence to rebut the fact that the underlying cash sales were not brought to tax by the assessee, the addition on account of variance in stock cannot be added. In view of the above position and considering the reconciliation placed before the lower authorities, we find no infirmity in the order of learned CIT(A). Accordingly, we uphold the order of learned CIT(A) on this issue and delete the addition of ₹2,88,51,600/-. Grounds Nos.1 and 2 raised by the Revenue are accordingly dismissed. 8. Ground No.3 raised by the Revenue challenges the deletion of an addition of ₹33,50,000/- made by the Assessing Officer under Section 69A of the Act on account of unexplained money arising from alleged bogus cash sales. 9. The learned Senior DR relied upon the assessment order and argued that as on 6″ September, 2016, i.e., the date preceding the date of survey, the assessee’s cash book reflected a negative cash balance of ₹31,00,000/-, with similar negative balances on preceding dates. He submitted that the assessee had deposited cash amounts of ₹17,50,000/- and ₹16,00,000/- totaling to ₹33,50,000/- on 18° May, 2016 in his bank account. He pointed out that on the actual date of these deposits, the assessee lacked a matching cash balance in his primary cash book. When confronted with this discrepancy during survey, the assessee could not provide an immediate explanation. The learned Senior DR submitted that assessee’s subsequent plea that these deposits were sourced from unrecorded cash sales which were finalized later is an afterthought and that the assessee failed to discharge his onus to prove the genuineness of 33.50 lakhs credited to the bank account. He contended that the assessee had made cash sales that were not recorded in the books at the time of survey. Later on, the assessee claimed that he has made unrecorded sales. Hence, the reply of the assessee is not tenable as the assessee has failed to discharge his onus to prove the genuineness of the amount credited in his bank account. 10. On the other hand, learned Counsel for the assessee relied upon the order of learned CIT(A) and contended that the Revenue has proceeded on an erroneous assumption that the addition of ₹33,50,000/- under Section 69A of the Act was made on account of alleged bogus cash sales. At no stage of the assessment proceedings, the Assessing Officer has recorded any finding that the assessee’s sales were bogus and non-genuine. He further contended that the foundation of the addition was not bogus transactions, but an interim and incomplete accounting position during survey proceedings. He submitted that the temporary negative cash balances and the resulting deposits were not rooted in deep bogus transactions, but were on account of an incomplete and non-updated accounting position prevailing at the time of survey. 11. We have heard the rival contentions and perused the material placed before us including the judicial precedents cited. It is observed that the main reason for invocation of Section 69A of the Act by the Assessing Officer was the presence of physical bank deposits alongside temporary negative cash entries in the books of account on the specific dates scrutinized during the survey. It is an undisputed fact that the accounts department of the assessee was running behind in entering routine operational data at the time of the survey. The assessee has subsequently demonstrated that the cash deposited in the Oriental Bank of Commerce accounts was fully derived from regular cash sales generated during the ordinary course of business. Moreover, for an addition to be sustained on the ground of bogus cash sales, the onus is on the Revenue to prove that no goods left the possession of the assessee or that the buyers and transactions were completely simulated. In the present case, the Assessing Officer has failed to bring on record any material to show that the sales were fictitious. A mere delay in maintenance of books of account or inability to instantly reconcile complex cash accounts during the high-pressure environment of a survey cannot turn genuine business sales into unexplained money under Section 69A of the Act, provided the transactions are subsequently verified and entered into the final accounts. 12. Since the addition is made solely on the basis of an incomplete, real-time accounting position during the survey and no tangible evidence was produced that the transactions were bogus, and also considering that the corresponding cash sales were subsequently regularized and documented, we are of the opinion that the invocation of Section 69A of the Act cannot be legally sustained. In that view of the matter, we find no reason to interfere with the order of learned CIT(A) in deleting the addition on this count. Accordingly, ground No.3 raised by the Revenue is dismissed. 13. Ground No. 4 raised by the Revenue relates to the deletion of an addition of ₹60,00,000/- made by the Assessing Officer by treating the proposed loan from Smt. Surender Malik as unexplained cash credit under Section 69A of the Act. 14. Brief facts of the case are that the assessee was issued two cheques of ₹10,50,000/- and ₹49,50,000/- by Smt. Surender Malik stated to be cash loan taken by the assessee. However, since the loan agreement was never finalized, the said cheques were not deposited by the assessee in his bank account and were finally cancelled and the receipt document was never handed over to the lender. The Assessing Officer, however, did not accept the explanation of the assessee and proceeded to make the addition under Section 69A of the Act. 15. Learned Senior DR strongly supported the assessment order and drew our attention to the assessee’s reply to the show cause notice in which the assessee had stated that he has received two cheques from Smt. Surinder Malik for ™60,00,000/-, both dated 15″ May, 2016, but the same did not materialize and the amount was never received. The learned Senior DR submitted that this explanation is merely an afterthought and cannot be accepted. He emphasized that a cheque remains legally valid for a period of only three months from its date of issue. In the present case, the cheques were dated 15″ May, 2016, whereas the survey operation at the assessee’s premises was conducted on 7‘ September, 2016, which is approximately four months after the date of issuance of the cheques. He, therefore, submitted that the transaction was an after-thought to explain an unrecorded transaction. 16. The learned Counsel for the assessee, on the other hand, argued that there was absolutely no receipt of money, either in cash or through banking channels. He explained that the loan from Smt. Surender Malik was always intended to be received through proper banking channels, which is why the two cheques were issued in the name of M/s Jai Guru Dev Packers. However, since the loan agreement was never finalized, the lender specifically instructed the assessee not to deposit the cheques. Consequently, the cheques were never cleared and were ultimately cancelled. The learned Counsel for the assessee further submitted that the Revenue’s reliance on the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Sumati Dayal Vs. CIT – [1995] 214 ITR 801 (SC) is not applicable in the present case as, the existence of uncashed physical cheques, the cancellation of the proposed transaction, and the absence of any corresponding credit or cash entry in the books of account establish that no money ever came into the possession or ownership of the assessee. 17. We have heard the submissions of both the sides and perused the material placed before us. The undisputed fact remains that the two cheques totaling 60,00,000/- issued by Smt. Surender Malik were never deposited into the bank account of the assessee and were never cleared. Therefore, no actual funds were ever transferred from the lender to the assessee. The primary requirement for invoking the provisions of Section 69A of the Act is that the assessee must be found to be the owner or in possession of money, bullion, jewellery, or any other valuable article, and that such asset must not be recorded in the books of account. In the case before us, the Revenue has failed to bring any evidence on record to prove that physical cash or any monetary value actually entered the possession or ownership of the assessee. As regards the argument of the learned Senior DR regarding the three-month validity period of the cheques, it is observed that the cheques had expired by the time of survey, which merely corroborates the assessee’s stand that the transaction had fallen through and the cheques were cancelled. Applying the test of human probabilities as laid down by Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Sumati Dayal (supra), it is logical that if a commercial deal or loan agreement fails to materialize, the instruments issued in anticipation thereof are left uncashed and eventually become invalid by the passage of time. The hypothetical or non-existent inflow of wealth cannot be taxed under Section 69A based on an unexecuted transaction. In that view of the matter, we hold that the invocation of Section 69A of the Act is bad in law and cannot be sustained. We, therefore, find no reason to interfere with the findings of the learned CIT(A) on this issue. Accordingly, the addition of ₹60,00,000/- is deleted, and this ground of the Revenue’s appeal is dismissed. 18. In ground No.5, the Revenue has challenged the addition of ₹7,25,000/- made by the Assessing Officer under Section 68 of the Act on account of Specified Bank Notes (SBNs) deposited during the demonetization period. 19. The learned Senior DR submitted that in response to the show cause notice, the assessee has given details of the cash deposits made in the form of SBN during demonetization period. However, no explanation with regard to the source of the cash deposited was furnished and the assessee has failed to discharge its primary onus to prove the genuineness of the cash deposited during demonetization period. He, therefore, submitted that the addition made under Section 68 of the Act is justified. 20. The learned Counsel for the assessee, on the other hand, argued that the cash deposit of ₹7,25,000/- in the bank account of the proprietorship concern of the assessee M/s Jai Guru Dev Packers was fully sourced from genuine operational business receipts and accumulated cash in hand. He pointed out that the regular books of account for the relevant assessment year were fully audited and were never rejected by the Assessing Officer under Section 145(3) of the Act. The learned Counsel for the assessee drew our attention to the financials of the business of the assessee and submitted that the total turnover for the year under consideration stood at a substantial figure of ₹13,43,88,893/-, out of which cash sales alone constituted ₹3,14,48,244/-. Furthermore, the audited financial statements for the preceding year ended March, 2016 reflected an opening cash balance of ₹30,76,220/-. Thus, considering the audited cash sales and the opening balance, the availability of cash for deposit of ₹7,25,000/- stands explained. He further stated that the reliance placed by the Revenue on the decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Kale Khan Mohammad Hanif v. CIT [1963] 50 ITR 1 (SC) is completely misplaced under these facts. 21. We have considered the rival contentions and perused the material placed before us. It is observed that the addition has been made under Section 68 of the Act, which requires the assessee to explain the nature and source of any sum found credited in his books. In the context of cash deposits during the demonetization period, the primary test is whether the assessee possessed sufficient, identifiable cash-in-hand generated from regular and recorded sources to cover the physical deposits made into the bank. In the present case, the Revenue does not dispute the fact that the assessee’s books of account are audited and have been fully accepted without invocation of Section 145(3) of the Act. Once the total turnover of ₹13.43 crores and the corresponding cash sales of ₹3.14 crores are accepted as genuine trading results, the availability of cash cannot be questioned in a piecemeal manner simply because the deposits were made during the demonetization period. Moreover, the opening cash balance of ₹30,76,220/-, carried forward from the duly audited balance sheet of the preceding year, explains the source for the subsequent deposit of ₹7,25,000/-. The reliance placed by the Revenue on the decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Kale Khan Mohammad Hanif (supra) is misplaced, as that judgment applies to cases where the cash credit is completely unrecorded or flows from an unexplained and anonymous source. In the present case, the availability of cash is proved as a direct component of the recorded cash-in-hand flowing through normal business operations. The Assessing Officer cannot treat a portion of the closing cash balance as unexplained under Section 68 of the Act when the underlying sales transactions that generated the said cash have been fully accepted as part of the total business turnover. We, therefore, find no reason to interfere with the order of learned CIT(A) on this issue. The same is upheld and this ground of appeal of the Revenue is dismissed. 22. Inthe result, the appeal filed by the Revenue is dismissed. Decision pronounced in the open Court on 18th November, 2026.
