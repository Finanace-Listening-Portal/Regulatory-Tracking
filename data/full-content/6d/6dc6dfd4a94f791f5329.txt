The RBI is expected to go for another 50 basis points of rate hikes over the next two policy meetings, taking the terminal repo rate to 6 per cent, although persistent food and energy shocks could extend the hiking cycle beyond its current expectations, according to a report by Kotak Institutional Equities. Advt Underwriting discipline to contain credit risksThe current tightening cycle poses limited risks to banks, given healthy balance sheets, tighter underwriting standards in recent years and stronger borrower fundamentals. Historically, rate tightening episodes driven by global inflation and liquidity dynamics have had a smaller bearing on credit costs than cycles preceded by prolonged domestic credit excesses.Banks have already tightened underwriting standards following regulatory concerns around unsecured lending during FY2023-24. The brokerage sees limited evidence of any build-up in credit risk and believes most portfolios are well positioned to absorb higher rates. Incremental credit growth in recent quarters has also been led by corporates, whose balance sheets remain significantly stronger than in previous cycles.While loan growth is expected to moderate, the slowdown is likely to be driven less by higher interest rates and more by the normalisation of elevated credit demand seen after the Middle East crisis, which had supported short-term financing requirements across segments.Fixed-rate deposits to support marginsThe structural shift in bank balance sheets, with increasingly pro-cyclical loan yields supported by fixed-rate deposits, should drive NIM expansion, particularly for large private banks, even if the expected rate cycle is relatively shallow.The brokerage said its estimates do not yet fully reflect the impact of FCNR deposit repricing, leaving scope for positive earnings surprises as margin tailwinds play out. Resilient asset quality and healthy growth prospects further reinforce its preference for frontline private banks as the best way to position for the next phase of the banking cycle.
