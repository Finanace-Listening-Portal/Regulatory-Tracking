The pricing change could prompt merchants and payment providers to look more closely at alternative digital payment rails, but it does not automatically make CBDC cheaper than UPI. About 96 per cent of P2M transactions will remain unaffected, while consumers will not pay the MDR. The charge will be distributed among payment ecosystem participants rather than collected by the government. Advt The pricing change could prompt merchants and payment providers to look more closely at alternative digital payment rails, but it does not automatically make CBDC cheaper than UPI. Digital rupee payments made using interoperable UPI QR codes are subject to existing NPCI switching fees, according to the central bank's interoperability framework.Programmable subsidiesThe bigger opportunity for CBDC lies in government payments, where its programmability allows funds to be restricted to a specified purpose. The government in August launched CBDC based direct benefit transfers under the Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana in Chandigarh and Dadra & Nagar Haveli, following pilots in Gujarat and Puducherry.Under the scheme, food subsidies are transferred directly into beneficiaries' digital rupee wallets and can be used to purchase foodgrains from authorised merchants. The government said the system is designed to provide traceability and restrict the use of the subsidy to its intended purpose.RBI has said it plans to expand CBDC pilots to additional direct benefit transfer schemes and domestic retail applications in FY27. During 2025-26, it launched multiple CBDC pilots under central and state government DBT schemes using the programmability feature of the digital currency.That gives the e-rupee a potential role beyond conventional payments. A government agency can programme a digital rupee transfer around a defined use, while the beneficiary can spend the funds at designated merchants. Advt MDR opens a windowThe new UPI pricing structure could provide an additional reason for merchants to consider different payment channels, particularly for larger transactions. A Rs 50,000 eligible UPI payment would attract Rs 200 in MDR, while the charge would be capped at Rs 300 for payments of Rs 75,000 and above.But the scope for MDR alone to drive a large migration to CBDC is limited. More than 95 per cent of P2M UPI transactions are below Rs 2,000, and the government estimates that only about 4 per cent of merchant transactions will attract the new MDR.CBDC also already has access to UPI's extensive merchant acceptance infrastructure. NPCI's interoperability framework allows digital rupee users to make merchant payments using existing UPI QR codes, although the underlying CBDC transaction continues to operate within a separate digital currency framework.Adoption remains the challenge Advt The e-rupee remains far smaller than UPI in terms of usage. UPI processed 2,366 crore transactions worth Rs 29.9 lakh crore in July 2026 alone, according to the Finance Ministry, while RBI's digital rupee programme remains focused on expanding pilots and testing new applications.The government and RBI are therefore building CBDC around use cases where it offers capabilities beyond ordinary account-to-account payments. Programmable subsidies are already being deployed, while RBI is also examining additional retail and cross-border applications.The new UPI MDR could add to that momentum by introducing a cost consideration for a small segment of merchant payments. But the more significant driver of e-rupee adoption is likely to be whether the government and RBI can scale use cases such as programmable subsidies and make CBDC sufficiently convenient for merchants and consumers. Advt For now, the MDR change is therefore better seen as a potential catalyst for CBDC experimentation rather than a direct trigger for a shift from UPI to the digital rupee.
