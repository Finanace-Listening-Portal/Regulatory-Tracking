Summary: Axis Direct is the retail brand used by Axis Securities Limited, a SEBI-registered stock broker with registration number INZ000161633 and NSE member code 14816. Its current website offers currency-derivatives activation to Indian residents for trading on the National Stock Exchange of India. This is exchange-traded access to permitted futures and options, not an RBI-authorised overseas forex portal, spot-FX dealer or CFD platform. The provider states that its currency service is enabled only on NSE and publishes a standard currency brokerage of 0.03% for futures, subject to a ₹10 minimum per order, and ₹20 per lot for options, while warning that brokerage plans may change. Other exchange, statutory, GST, margin and delayed-payment charges can apply. Indian residents must separately comply with RBI’s permitted-product and purpose rules: INR-involving exchange-traded forex derivatives are for hedging contracted exposure, even though positions up to the regulatory threshold can be taken without producing exposure evidence upfront. This profile explains the entity, service scope, KYC, costs, settlement, grievance routes and legal limits without treating broker registration as approval for every forex product. Entity separation matters because a bank’s authorised-dealer status does not automatically attach to a group company’s broking service. The registration is for securities-market intermediation. Current RBI materials identify USD-INR, EUR-INR, GBP-INR, JPY-INR, EUR-USD, GBP-USD and USD-JPY as permitted pairs, subject to exchange availability and contract specifications. Axis Securities does not thereby promise that every permitted pair, expiry or option series will be continuously available on its platform. INR pairs retain a hedging purpose requirement For exchange-traded contracts involving INR, RBI states that the purpose is hedging contracted exposure. A user may take positions up to the prescribed single limit without having to establish the exposure at the outset, but must ensure that a valid underlying contracted exposure exists, has not been hedged elsewhere and can be established if required. The operational documentation concession is not permission for unrestricted leveraged speculation. Positions beyond the threshold require additional monitoring arrangements under the directions. Cross-currency pairs follow a different purpose rule RBI permits recognised exchanges to offer non-INR currency-pair derivatives without a restriction in terms of purpose, subject to product, exchange, eligibility, position-limit and broker rules. That does not convert an NSE contract into overseas leveraged forex. It also does not authorise a resident to remit money to an unauthorised foreign platform. Electronic forex transactions must occur through an RBI-authorised ETP or a recognised Indian exchange under the applicable conditions. Orders remain subject to margins and position limits Currency futures and short option positions create margin obligations. Axis Securities may collect initial, exposure, extreme-loss, mark-to-market or other exchange-prescribed margins and apply additional broker risk controls. Client-wise open-interest limits apply across trading members on a PAN basis. The broker can reduce limits, restrict new orders or square off positions under its risk policy. Availability of collateral does not guarantee acceptance of every order or prevent a loss greater than the amount initially deposited. Brokerage Is Only One Part of the Transaction Cost Axis Direct’s current FAQ publishes standard currency brokerage of 0.03% for futures, with a stated minimum of ₹10 per order, and ₹20 per lot for options. The same material says the slab can be reviewed and changed. The account’s actual subscribed plan and contract note govern. A client should not treat a search result, promotional page or old tariff as a permanent quote. Other costs can include exchange transaction charges, SEBI turnover fees, GST on brokerage and eligible charges, stamp duty, clearing or platform components and penalties for margin shortfall. Axis Direct also states that delayed-payment charges may be imposed on unrecovered mark-to-market losses, with GST on the charge. The taxable transaction result is separate from GST on brokerage services. Before trading, the client should obtain the current schedule and use a sample contract note to identify every line item. The provider states that currency futures expire two working days before the last business day of the expiry month, but the current NSE contract calendar controls each contract. A digital contract note records the exchange, contract, side, quantity, price, brokerage and statutory charges. It should be matched to the order log, margin statement and ledger. Funds withdrawal is subject to settlement, available cash, margin blocking, running-account authorisation and broker timelines; a profitable screen position does not equal immediately withdrawable money. Open positions should not be left to expiry without understanding exchange settlement and broker square-off policy. Refunds are not the same as trade reversals A valid exchange trade ordinarily cannot be cancelled merely because the price moved unfavourably. A rejected order may release blocked margin, while an excess ledger balance may be withdrawable after obligations are satisfied. Technical incidents, erroneous orders and exchange annulments follow distinct rules. Clients should raise an issue promptly with timestamps, order IDs, screenshots, contract notes and ledger evidence instead of describing every disputed outcome as a refund. Grievances Start with the Broker and Escalate Through Market Channels Axis Securities publishes helpdesk and grievance contacts and identifies its compliance function. A complaint should state the UCC or client ID, order and trade numbers, date, contract, amount, disputed charge or margin event, and requested resolution. Supporting evidence should include the order log, contract note, margin statement, bank entry and communications. Passwords, one-time codes and full private credentials should never be sent in a complaint. If the broker does not resolve a securities-market complaint, the client can use the exchange investor-grievance route and SEBI’s SCORES system, followed where appropriate by the online dispute-resolution framework. These mechanisms review service and market disputes; they do not convert an impermissible forex purpose into a lawful transaction or protect against ordinary trading loss. Cyber fraud may require immediate bank, broker, cybercrime and police reporting in parallel. Tax Treatment Depends on the Trader’s Facts and Records Exchange-traded derivatives carried out on a recognised stock exchange and satisfying statutory conditions are generally excluded from the definition of a speculative transaction under section 43(5). Profit or loss may therefore be treated as non-speculative business income where the activity is a business, but classification depends on frequency, intention, books and taxpayer facts. Turnover computation, eligible expenses, tax audit thresholds and loss set-off require professional analysis. There is no securities transaction tax on currency derivatives merely by analogy with equity derivatives; clients should rely on the actual contract note and current law. GST on brokerage and service charges is not an income-tax payment and is not proof of deductibility. A resident should preserve contract notes, ledgers, margin statements, bank entries and year-end reports. The underlying commercial exposure used for INR contracts should also be retained for RBI compliance.
