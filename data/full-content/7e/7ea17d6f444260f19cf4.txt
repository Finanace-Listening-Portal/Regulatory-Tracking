Economists believe that signs of an economic recovery are beginning to emerge. Smriti Jain TIMESOFINDIA.COM Published On Nov 17, 2026 at 08:15 AM IST var news_msid = 135908101; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Even as industry and investors seek relief on long-term and short-term capital gains tax on equities, economists have struck a different and cautious note.As the government prepares the Union Budget for 2027-28, leading economists on Monday urged Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman to retain the capital gains tax on equities, pushing back against demands from industry bodies and overseas investors for its removal.Sitharaman chaired the first round of pre-budget consultations with economists to seek their views ahead of the upcoming Budget.What economists told FM on capital gains tax on equities"In the meeting, most economists were of the view that there should not be any reduction in Long Term Capital Gains tax (LTCG)," a source told PTI. Advt Economists believe that signs of an economic recovery are beginning to emerge, with early improvements starting to translate into results and private investment beginning to come in, the source said.Economists expect inflation to ease further, citing a favourable geopolitical environment and the likelihood of a decline in oil prices.The recommendation comes as industry chambers have already approached the Finance Ministry, calling for the abolition of long-term capital gains tax.Currently, gains exceeding Rs 1.25 lakh from listed equities and equity mutual funds attract LTCG tax at 12.5%, along with applicable surcharge and cess.For investments to qualify as long-term capital assets, they must be held for more than 12 months from the date of acquisition. If these assets are sold within 12 months of purchase, the resulting short-term capital gains (STCG) are taxed at 20%.Stakeholders have also proposed separate tax treatment for foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) investing in equities, pointing out that several emerging markets exempt such investors from LTCG tax on equity gains.Another demand is to revise the method used to calculate capital gains for FPIs, with gains to be computed in US dollar terms.Separately, Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) Chairman Ravi Agrawal said on Monday that the Finance Ministry was reviewing various aspects of capital gains taxation on equities. Advt The source said the economists also lauded the Modi government’s increased spending on infrastructure, noting that the higher outlay had benefited both public sector entities and private businesses."They also gave recommendation to boost MSME and labour intensive sector to create more employment opportunities," the source said.Sitharaman is expected to present the Union Budget on February 1.The finance minister also conducted a separate pre-Budget consultation with experts representing the agriculture and rural development sectors.
