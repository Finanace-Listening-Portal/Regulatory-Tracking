} } Listen to This ArticleShriram Finance, one of India’s largest retail non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), is targeting a gold loan portfolio of around ₹20,000 crore over the next two years and expects its electric vehicle (EV) financing business to expand significantly as it strengthens both segments. Umesh Revankar, executive vice-chairman of Shriram Finance, told Business Standard that gold loans currently account for around 2.5 per cent of the company’s overall loan book. The lender aims to increase their share to 5 per cent of its assets under management (AUM) over the next two years. “We are growing the book at around 30 per cent year-on-year. Our target is to reach a significant portfolio size of around 5 per cent or around ₹20,000 crore in the next two years,” Revankar said. Monthly disbursements under the company’s EV financing business have risen to around ₹350 crore, from ₹30-40 crore when it began lending to the segment. “We expect it to reach ₹5,000 crore soon,” he said. Automobile-related financing accounts for about 75 per cent of Shriram Finance’s total AUM.
