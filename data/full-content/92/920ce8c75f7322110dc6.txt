In November so far, the rupee has appreciated over 2 per cent against the dollar.Anjali Kumari Mumbai 3 min read Last Updated : Nov 28 2026 | 12:11 AM IST @media (min-width:1100px) and (max-width:1280px) { .storymeta .meta-info { flex: 0 0 45%; } } Listen to This ArticleThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has decided to restore the minimum 90 per cent daily maintenance of the cash reserve ratio (CRR) by banks, effective December 1, 2026, amid tight financial conditions as system liquidity turned into a deficit and led to the hardening of short-term rates. The daily requirement was increased to 99 per cent with effect from October 16. The reduction will release ₹50,000 crore in the banking system, treasury officials said. “On the basis of an assessment of the current and evolving macroeconomic situation and liquidity conditions, it has been decided to reduce the minimum daily maintenance of the CRR from 99 per cent of the requirement to 90 per cent effective from the fortnight beginning December 1, 2026,” the central bank said. The liquidity deficit in the banking system—as measured by the amount banks borrowed from the RBI’s liquidity adjustment facility (LAF) window—was ₹47,641 crore on Thursday. The weighted average call rate (WACR) is hovering near the upper band of the rate corridor, that is, the marginal standing facility (MSF) rate of 5.75 per cent. The MSF rate is 25 basis points (bps) above the repo rate and the standing facility rate; the lower band of the corridor is at 5.25 per cent, with repo rate at 5.5 per cent. Ideally, the WACR should align with the policy repo rate. At the weekly government bond auction, the RBI accepted less than half the notified amount in one of the four government securities, as investors sought higher yields. The central bank accepted bids worth ₹3,677.495 crore in the 6.20 per cent GS 2029 against the notified ₹9,000 crore, setting the cutoff yield at 6.9960 per cent. There was no devolvement on primary dealers in any of the four securities.
