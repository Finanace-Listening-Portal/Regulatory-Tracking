Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Trendset Jayabheri Elevate Flat Owners Maintenance Mutually Aided Cooperative Society Limited (GST AAR Telangana) Summary: The Telangana State Authority for Advance Ruling (AAR), Hyderabad, examined an application filed by Trendset Jayabheri Elevate Flat Owners Maintenance Mutually Aided Cooperative Society Limited concerning the applicability of Goods and Services Tax to maintenance contributions and common utility reimbursements collected from residential apartment owners. The applicant, a Residents Welfare Association registered under the Telangana Mutually Aided Co-operative Societies Act, 1995, sought clarification on two issues. First, whether monthly maintenance collections not exceeding ₹7,500 per member remained exempt when the society’s aggregate annual turnover exceeded ₹20 lakh. Second, whether electricity and water charges recovered separately from members at actual cost, without any markup, could be excluded from taxable value under the pure-agent provisions of Rule 33 of the CGST Rules, 2017. The applicant maintained the common areas and facilities of the Trendset Jayabheri Elevate residential complex at Kondapur, Hyderabad. Its responsibilities included site management, gardens, clubhouse facilities, diesel generators, lifts and other common amenities. The society charged residents ₹4 per square foot per month based on the super built-up area of each apartment, with collections made quarterly. The arrangement was approved through Circular No. 1/2025 following decisions taken at the General Body Meeting on 4 May 2025 and the Board of Directors’ Meeting on 24 May 2025. The charges became effective from 1 July 2025, when the community was handed over to the applicant society. The society also recovered common-area electricity and water expenses from residents and imposed delayed-payment charges of ₹200 for the first month after the prescribed due date and ₹1,000 per month thereafter. Its aggregate turnover exceeded ₹20 lakh during the relevant financial year, and it was registered under the GST legislation. The applicant stated that it had been paying GST on the entire maintenance charges collected whenever the monthly amount exceeded ₹7,500. It therefore requested an advance ruling on whether collections below that threshold continued to enjoy exemption despite the society crossing the aggregate turnover threshold. The Authority examined Serial No. 77 of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), as amended by Notification No. 2/2018-Central Tax (Rate), dated 25 January 2018. The relevant entry provides an exemption for specified services supplied by an unincorporated body or non-profit entity to its own members for sourcing goods or services from third parties for common use in a housing society or residential complex, subject to the prescribed monthly monetary limit. The Authority explained that the ₹7,500 exemption is determined with reference to the amount collected per member per month. Accordingly, the society’s aggregate turnover exceeding ₹20 lakh does not, by itself, make qualifying maintenance contributions below the monthly exemption threshold taxable. The Authority further held that where the RWA’s aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh and monthly maintenance charges collected from a member exceed ₹7,500, GST at 18% is payable on the entire maintenance contribution rather than merely the amount exceeding the exemption limit. It illustrated this position by referring to maintenance charges of ₹9,000 per month per member, on which GST would be calculated on the full ₹9,000 rather than only the excess ₹1,500. Conversely, where monthly charges remain below ₹7,500 per member, the Authority stated that the association need not pay GST on those qualifying collections even where its annual turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh. On the second question, the Authority considered the tax treatment of common-area electricity and water charges recovered from apartment owners. It referred to Serial No. 104 of Notification No. 2/2017-Central Tax (Rate), dated 28 June 2017, concerning exemption for electrical energy, and Serial No. 99 of the same notification concerning water. The applicant’s position was that these utility charges were merely reimbursements of actual expenditure incurred for the common benefit of residents and were recovered separately from maintenance charges without any profit margin. The Authority therefore examined whether the applicant could be regarded as acting as a pure agent of its members while making payments for such utilities. Referring to Rule 33 of the CGST Rules, the Authority explained that expenditure incurred by a supplier as a pure agent of the recipient may be excluded from the value of supply only where the prescribed conditions are satisfied. These include authorisation from the recipient to make payments to third parties, separate disclosure of such expenditure in invoices and procurement of goods or services in addition to those supplied on the supplier’s own account. The explanation to Rule 33 also requires a contractual arrangement, absence of ownership or title in the goods or services procured, non-use of those supplies for the supplier’s own interest and recovery of only the actual expenditure incurred. Applying these conditions, the Authority concluded that common-area electricity charges could be excluded from the applicant’s taxable value where the association genuinely acted as a pure agent of its members. The electricity expenses must be separately indicated in the invoices issued to residents and recovered strictly at actual cost, without creating any surplus or deficit for the society. The Authority further held that analogous treatment would apply to qualifying water charges covered under Serial No. 99 of Notification No. 2/2017-Central Tax (Rate). However, the exclusion was conditional upon compliance with Rule 33 and was not an automatic exemption merely because the amounts were described as reimbursements. The Telangana AAR ultimately clarified that qualifying maintenance contributions below ₹7,500 per member per month remain exempt notwithstanding aggregate annual turnover exceeding ₹20 lakh, while contributions exceeding that threshold attract GST on the entire amount in the circumstances described in the ruling. It also recognised the exclusion of separately recovered common-area electricity and water charges where the pure-agent conditions are fulfilled. The ruling highlights the importance of distinguishing maintenance services from actual utility reimbursements and maintaining appropriate documentation, separate invoices and evidence of payments made on behalf of members. The decision is an advance ruling concerning the applicant’s stated arrangements and does not establish an unconditional exemption for all RWA utility collections. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF AUTHORITY FOR ADVANCE RULING, TELANGANA 1. Trendset Jayabheri Elevate Flat Owners Maintenance Mutually Aided Cooperative Society Limited, Hyderabad – 500084, Telangana (GSTIN: 36AANAT2642P1ZS) has filed an application in FORM GST ARA-01 under Section 97(1) of TGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 104 of CGST/TGST Rules. 2. At the outset, it is made clear that the provisions of both the CGST Act and the TGST Act are the same except for certain provisions. Therefore, unless a mention is specifically made to any dissimilar provisions, a reference to the CGST Act would also mean a reference to the same provision under the TGST Act. Further, for the purposes of this Advance Ruling, the expression ‘GST Act’ would be a common reference to both CGST and TGST Act. 3. It is observed that the queries raised by the applicant fall within the ambit of Section 97 of the GST Act. The Applicant enclosed copies of challans as proof of payment of Rs. 5,000/- under SGST and Rs.5,000/- under CGST towards the fee for Advance Ruling. The Applicant has declared that the questions raised in the application have neither been decided nor are pending before any authority under any provisions of the CGST/TGST Act’2017. The application is, therefore, admitted after examining it and the records called for and after hearing the applicant as per section 98(2) of TGST Act’2017. 4. BRIEF FACTS OF THE CASE: 4.1 Trendset Jayabheri Elevate Flat Owners Maintenance Mutually Aided Cooperative Society Limited, Hyderabad having GSTIN: 36AANAT2642P1ZS, Sy.No. 5, Trendset Jayabheri Elevate, Hitech City Road, Kondapur, Hyderabad – 500 084. 4.2 Trendset Jayabheri Elevate Flat Owners Maintenance Mutually Aided Cooperative Society Limited is a Resident Welfare Association (RWA) registered under the Telangana mutually Aided Co-operative Societies Act, 1995, with Registration No. TS/RRD/MACS/2023-77/FOW & M. 4.3 The Applicant is an association of the owners of flats in the residential complex “Trendset Jayabheri Elevate” and is responsible for the upkeep and maintenance of the common areas and for providing services and amenities to its members. 4.4 To carry out these functions, the Applicant charges the residents an amount of Rs.4/- per sft per month but collects the said amount on quarterly basis. Area is calculated on the basis of the super built-up area of each of the residential apartment. The above arrangement of Rs.4/- per sft was decided as per the Circular No. 1/2025 issued by the Applicant Society as per the decisions taken in the GBM dated 04.05.2025 and Board of Directors Meeting held on 24.05.2025. The said charges are collected with effect from 1st July, 2025 as the community was handed over to the applicant society on that date. 4.5 These charges are collected towards the maintenance charges like: site management, garden, club house, other facilities, DG maintenance, lift maintenance, etc., and also towards reimbursement of common electricity and water charges. It was also decided to collect penal charges at the rate of Rs.200/- for the first month after the due date which is 10th of the month of invoice and Rs.1000/- per month from the 11th of the subsequent month following the month for default. It is submitted that the applicant has been paying GST on the entire charges collected as above whatever they exceed Rs.7,500/- per month and pays GST on the same every month. 4.6 The aggregate turnover of the Applicant during the current financial year exceeded the threshold limit of Rs. 20 lakhs and therefore, the Applicant is registered under the CGST/TGST Act, 2017. 5. QUESTION RAISED: 5.1 Is GST applicable on monthly collection not exceeding Rs. 7,500 per member given that the total collection of the society is more than Rs.20lakhs a year? 5.2 Whether the applicant is liable to pay GST on the amounts collected from members towards common area electricity and water charges, where these charges are collected as a reimbursement at actuals, without any markup, and are billed separately from the maintenance charges, thereby qualifying the Applicant as a “pure agent” under Rule 33 of the CGST Rules, 2017? 6. PERSONAL HEARING: The authorized representative Sri K. Radha Krishna Rao, appeared before the AAR on 30.09.2026 and reiterated their averments in the application submitted. 7. DISCUSSION & FINDINGS: 7.1 The Serial No. 77 of Notification No. 12/2017 as amended vide Notification No. 02/2018, dt: 25.01.2018 states that service by an unincorporated body or a non-profit entity to its own members is exempt upto an amount of Rs. 7500 per member for sourcing goods or services from a 3rd person for the common use of its members in a housing society or a residential complex. Therefore where the aggregate turnover of a Residents Welfare Association (RWA) exceeds Rs.20 lakhs in a financial year and the amount collected for maintenance per member exceeds Rs. 7500/- then the entire amount is chargeable to GST at the rate of 18%. For example: if the maintenance charges are Rs.9000 per month per member, GST @18% shall be payable on the entire amount and not on the amount in excess of Rs.7,500/-. Further even if the annual turnover of the RWA is greater than Rs.20 lakhs but the monthly maintenance charged per person is less than Rs. 7500/-, then such RWA need not pay tax on the amounts so collected. 7.2 Notification No.02/2017 dated:28.06.2017 provides for exemption from tax under CGST Act, 2017 on Electrical energy at Serial No. 104 with chapter heading ‘2716 00 00’. As seen from the notification there are no conditions specified for this exemption. Similarly water is exempt as per notification No. 2/2017, dated. 28.06.2017 at Sl.No. 99 vide HSN code 2201. 7.3 Rule 33 of CGST Rules 2017, Reads as under: Value of supply of services in case of pure agent Notwithstanding anything contained in the provisions of this Chapter, the expenditure or costs incurred by a supplier as a pure agent of the recipient of supply shall be excluded from the value of supply, if all the following conditions are satisfied, namely; i. The supplier acts as a pure agent of the recipient of the supply, when he makes the payment to the third party on authorization by such recipient; ii. The payment made by the pure agent on behalf of the recipient of supply has been separately indicated in the invoice issued by the pure agent to the recipient of service and iii. The supplies procured by the pure agent from the third party as a pure agent of the recipient of supply are in addition to the services he supplies on his own account. Explanation:- For the purposes of this rule, the expression “pure agent” means a person who- a. Enters into a contractual agreement with the recipient of supply to act as his pure agent to incur expenditure or costs in the course of supply of goods or services or both; b. Neither intends to hold nor holds any title to the goods or service or both so procured or supplied as pure agent of the recipient of supply; c. Does not use for his own interest such goods or services so procured and d. Receives only the actual amount incurred to procure such goods or services in addition to the amount received for supply he provides on his own account. The rule 33 of the CGST Rules excludes the expenditure or costs incurred by a supplier as a pure agent of the recipient of the supply subject to the conditions prescribed in the rule. The rule also has an illustration for describing a pure agent: “Illustration to rule 33 of the CGST rules: Corporate services firm A is engaged to handle the legal work pertaining to the incorporation of Company B. Other than its service fees, A recovers from B, registration fee and approval fee for the name of the company paid to the Registrar of Companies. The fees charged by the Registrar Companies for the registration and approval of the name are compulsorily level on B. A is merely acting as a pure agent in the payment of those fees. Therefore A’s recovery of such expenses is a disbursement and not part of the value supply made by A to B”. A combined reading of the above notification and the Rule reveals that the cost of electrical energy supplied by the applicant to its members as a pure agent is not taxable at the hands of the applicant, if and only if the common area electricity charges are collected from the members on actual basis. In order to qualify as pure agent, the association has to indicate the common area electricity charges separately in the invoice and charge to the Members only on actual basis without making any surplus or deficit to the association. 8. In view of the foregoing, we rule as follows: In view of the above discussion, the questions raised by the applicant is clarified as below: Question Ruling 1. Is GST applicable on monthly collection not exceeding Rs. 7,500 per member given that the total collection of the society is more than Rs.20lakhs a year? As mentioned at para 7.1 2. Whether the applicant is liable to pay GST on the amounts collected from members towards common area electricity and water charges, where these charges are collected as a reimbursement at actuals, without any markup, and are billed separately from the maintenance charges, thereby qualifying the Applicant as a “pure agent” under Rule 33 of the CGST Rules, 2017? The applicant is eligible to claim exemption to the extent of collection made from its members towards common area electricity charges, if it acts in accordance with the provisions governing “pure agent” and the common area electricity charges are collected from members on actual basis. The applicant has to indicate the common area electricity charges separately in the invoice and charged to the members only on the actual basis without making any surplus or deficit to the applicant. Further, the above analogy applies to exemption for supply of water covered in Sl.No.99 of notification No. 2/2017, dated. 28.06.2017.
