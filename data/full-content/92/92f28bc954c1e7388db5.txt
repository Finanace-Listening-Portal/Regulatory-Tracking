Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Guardian Nutrition & Healthcare Private Limited (NCLT Mumbai) Summary: The National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai, Court-IV sanctioned the Composite Scheme of Arrangement amongst Guardian Nutrition & Healthcare Private Limited, Guardian Healthcare Services Private Limited and Idam Natural Wellness Private Limited under Sections 230 to 232 read with Section 66 of the Companies Act, 2013. The scheme provides for cancellation of Guardian Nutrition’s equity shares held by shareholders other than Idam and the demerger of Guardian Healthcare’s Nutrition Products Division into Idam. The companies explained that the restructuring would improve operational and capital efficiency, consolidate the nutrition business and allow separate strategies for the remaining pharmacy retail business. The proposed consideration for cancellation of 11,16,974 equity shares was Rs. 59 per share, aggregating to Rs. 6,59,01,466, based on a registered valuer’s report dated 28 January 2026. The source states that no shares would be issued as consideration for transfer of the demerged undertaking. The requisite shareholder and unsecured creditor meetings were held on 10 July 2026 pursuant to the Tribunal’s earlier directions, and the chairpersons’ reports recorded the prescribed approvals. The Regional Director raised observations concerning accounting standards, appointed dates, statutory approvals and notices, income-tax and GST compliance, FEMA requirements, significant beneficial ownership disclosures, capital reduction payments, securities premium and matters appearing in the companies’ financial statements. In response, the companies gave undertakings covering those matters, including compliance with General Circular No. 09/2019 dated 21 August 2019, payment through banking channels, applicable tax withholding and protection of creditors and employees. They also furnished particulars of Forms BEN-2 filed in August 2026. The Registrar of Companies’ observations included that only 17 of 40 shareholders attended the first company’s meeting and that shareholders holding 1.55% of the shares proposed for reduction had neither participated nor furnished consent letters. The company responded that the statutory majority under Section 230(6) had been achieved and that the same valuation-based consideration would be paid uniformly to every shareholder whose shares were cancelled, including those who did not attend or vote. Other responses addressed the pending registration of an acquired trademark, the accounting presentation of an earlier scheme involving Idam and Elegare Beauty Private Limited, and the demerged company’s preparation of financial statements on a going concern basis despite negative net worth. These explanations were the companies’ submissions; the Tribunal recorded that the Regional Director found their undertakings satisfactory and had no further objection. Finding the scheme fair and reasonable, compliant with the requisite statutory requirements and neither unlawful nor contrary to public policy, the Tribunal allowed the petition. It fixed 31 December 2025 as the appointed date for Guardian Nutrition’s capital reduction and 1 January 2026 for Guardian Healthcare’s demerger. The companies were directed to file the certified order and scheme with the Registrar of Companies, electronically with e-Form INC-28 and physically, within 30 days of receipt, and to submit them for stamp-duty adjudication within 60 days of receipt of the certified order. All undertakings and consequential statutory requirements must be fulfilled. The Tribunal expressly preserved the Income Tax Department’s power to examine tax payable and take action if the scheme results in tax avoidance, and clarified that sanction would not prevent regulatory proceedings, investigations or prosecution against the companies. This Composite Scheme involves the following: a) Reduction of the share capital of Shareholders (Other than Idam) by GNHPL; and b) Demerger of the identified Demerged Undertaking of GHSPL into Idam. II. It has become imperative for GNHPL to re-align and re-organize its share capital structure by cancelling the shares held by the Shareholders (Other than Idam) following the process of capital reduction. The extinguishment of shares of Shareholders (Other than Idam) would result into cancellation of shares of GNHPL held by these Shareholders (Other than Idam) in order to achieve the objective of enhancing operational and capital efficiency, unlocking shareholder value and enabling focused growth, independent strategy, greater flexibility, and attract strategic partners. III. The proposed capital reduction would be beneficial for the remaining shareholder of GNHPL as it would improve earnings per share and also enhance the shareholders’ value. IV. In view of the above, the Board has decided to reduce the paid-up equity share capital of GNHPL by cancelling and extinguishing the Equity Shares as held by the Shareholders (Other than Idam) as on the Appointed Date 1 of this Composite Scheme. V. Furthermore, Board has also considered that the proposed capital reduction would not have any adverse effect on the creditors of GNHPL and the Company would honour its commitments and meet its obligations in the ordinary course of business and there is cash consideration paid to the Shareholders (Other than Idam) pursuant to the proposed capital reduction. Following the reduction of capital, GNHPL will become a wholly owned subsidiary of Idam. As a result, Guardian Healthcare Services Private Limited will become a step-down wholly owned subsidiary of Idam. VI. GHSPL has 2 (two) distinct business segments/divisions viz. (i) Nutrition Products Division (which includes business of health, wellness, personal care, and nutrition supplements under the brand name GNC); and (ii) Pharmacy Retail Division. VII. The nature of risk, competition, challenges, opportunities and business methods for the Nutrition Products Division (‘NPD’) i.e. the Demerged Business is separate and distinct from Pharmacy Retail Division (‘PRD’) i.e. the Remaining Business carried out by the Demerged Company. Further, the way and manner in which the Nutrition Products Division is required to be handled and managed is not similar to that of the Pharmacy Retail Division. VIII. The benefits of the Composite Scheme, inter alia, would include the following: a) The consolidation of the identified Demerged Undertaking with Resulting Company will result in integration, synergies, enable better supervision of the business, efficient utilization of the infrastructure facilities and optimum utilization of the available resources. b) The synergies arising out of the consolidation will lead to enhancement of combined earnings, market penetration and cash flow of the business and consequently enhance the shareholder’s value. c) The segregation of the identified Demerged Undertaking and Remaining Business will allow the management to devise, implement and pursue independent business strategies which will enable a wider scope for independent collaborations, investment opportunities and expansion for the Remaining Business Undertaking. d) Cost savings are expected to flow from more focused operational efforts and simplification of business processes.
