Summary: Indian Bank’s IB Tax Saver Scheme is a named five-year deposit offered in fixed-deposit or money-multiplier form. The bank publishes a ₹1,000 minimum, deposits in multiples of ₹100, a ₹1,50,000 maximum, no loan or ordinary foreclosure, nomination, and an additional 0.50 percentage point for eligible senior citizens. Indian Bank’s domestic retail rate card effective 23 October 2026 shows 6.00% per annum for the five-year bucket and states that the card rates also apply to the IB Tax Saver Scheme, producing 6.50% for an eligible senior citizen under the published addition. For deposits made in tax year 2026-27, deduction analysis should refer to section 123 and Schedule XV of the Income-tax Act, 2025 rather than mechanically relying on the product page’s legacy section 80C wording. Interest remains taxable, TDS is only withholding, and DICGC cover is aggregated per depositor per bank in the same right and capacity. Its official product page states that the scheme is designed for tax benefit, is available in fixed-deposit and money-multiplier-deposit forms, has a five-year period and offers interest quarterly, on a discounted monthly basis or at maturity according to the selected option. A depositor should keep adequate emergency funds outside this deposit because ordinary premature access cannot be assumed merely by accepting an interest penalty. The lock-in changes liquidity, credit and succession planning Indian Bank publishes no foreclosure and no loan against the IB Tax Saver Scheme. These restrictions distinguish it from its ordinary fixed deposit page, which generally mentions pre-closure and loan facilities. The specific product terms govern the tax-saver certificate. A customer should not import the liquidity features of another Indian Bank FD or assume that an online loan-against-deposit screen overrides the tax-saver restriction. Nomination should be completed and verified because the certificate can remain outstanding for five years. The bank’s current operating documents should be consulted for death claims, joint-holder operation and legal-heir requirements; the public product page does not justify inventing a routine premature-closure rule. The receipt, nomination reference, PAN, holder order and maturity instructions should be stored together. Renewal should be reviewed at maturity because a renewed ordinary deposit may not constitute a fresh qualifying tax-saver deposit without satisfying the then-current scheme. Deposit form determines when interest is received In an FD payout option, interest can be paid at the chosen interval under bank rules; in a money-multiplier form, interest is retained and compounded for maturity. Tax recognition and TDS do not necessarily wait until cash reaches the savings account. The customer should obtain an annual interest certificate and reconcile it with Form 26AS and AIS, especially for a cumulative deposit. Use section 123 and Schedule XV for tax year 2026-27 The product page’s section 80C label is legacy wording Indian Bank’s product page describes the scheme by reference to section 80C of the Income-tax Act, 1961. For deposits made from 1 April 2026, the operative statute is the Income-tax Act, 2025. Section 123 allows an individual or Hindu undivided family a deduction for eligible payments and deposits enumerated in Schedule XV, subject to an aggregate ceiling of ₹1,50,000 and the Schedule’s conditions. Tax files for 2026-27 should cite the current Act while retaining the product documentation. The ₹1,50,000 limit is shared across the eligible items in section 123 and Schedule XV; it is not an additional deduction dedicated to this deposit. Provident-fund contributions, life-insurance premiums and other eligible payments can use part or all of the same ceiling. The depositor must also consider the chosen tax regime. Section 123’s deduction is generally not available when total income is charged under the concessional regime in section 202, subject to the Act’s precise terms. No; tax-saving principal treatment does not exempt interest Interest certificate, AIS and Form 26AS Interest is taxable even when principal qualifies for deduction The scheme’s tax-saving label concerns the eligible deposit amount; it does not convert interest into exempt income. Interest must be included in taxable income under the applicable method and head. The bank may deduct tax at source based on the statutory threshold and aggregate interest information available for the customer. TDS is a collection mechanism, not the final computation of liability. Under the current Income-tax Act, 2025 framework, bank time-deposit interest thresholds commonly relevant for section 393 withholding are ₹50,000 for a non-senior depositor and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen, subject to current law, PAN, aggregation, status and any valid declaration. Crossing or not crossing the TDS threshold does not decide whether the interest is taxable. A depositor with several Indian Bank branches or deposits should reconcile bank-wide interest and withholding rather than reviewing this one certificate alone. Where the depositor believes total tax is nil, any declaration or lower-withholding position should be evaluated under the current statute and forms. An invalid declaration can cause under-withholding but does not change the income’s character. Conversely, excess TDS can be claimed only after matching the credit and reporting the corresponding income correctly. DICGC insurance is aggregated across Indian Bank deposits Eligible bank deposits, including fixed deposits, are covered within the DICGC scheme up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in the same right and same capacity, inclusive of principal and accrued interest. The limit is not ₹5 lakh for each certificate or branch. Savings, current, recurring and term deposits at Indian Bank in the same capacity are aggregated for the insurance calculation. The tax-saver cap reduces the principal placed in this particular scheme but does not establish unused insurance cover because the depositor may hold other Indian Bank balances. Joint accounts and different legal capacities follow DICGC’s rules and should not be structured artificially. Insurance protection is not the same as instant liquidity or a guarantee of interest beyond the covered aggregate.
