Articles 3 min read Moneyview IPO: Profitability trajectory prompts capital reset, fresh issue cut to ₹750 crore, says Puneet Agarwal Moneyview had initially proposed raising ₹1,500 crore through the fresh issue, but has now halved the capital raise to ₹750 crore, with management saying its profitability trajectory has changed its assessment of funding requirements. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Sep 21, 2026 at 03:17 PM IST var news_msid = 134386142; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Puneet Agarwal, CEO, MoneyviewMoneyview co-founder, MD & CEO Puneet Agarwal said the fintech has cut its proposed fresh issue from ₹1,500 crore to ₹750 crore after reassessing its capital requirements in light of the company’s growth and profitability trajectory.Responding to an ETBFSI question on why the company had made such a sharp reduction in its fresh capital requirement, Agarwal said Moneyview now believes the revised ₹750-crore issue is sufficient to support its growth plans.“Given the profitability trajectory that we are on, we made a reassessment to the board. And what we realised is we don't need ₹1,500 crore. ₹750 crore is sufficient for us to stay on track for the plans that we have out there and continue the strong growth and profitability,” Agarwal said. Advt The response puts Moneyview’s profitability trajectory at the centre of its decision to reduce the fresh capital component of the IPO, rather than presenting the reduction simply as a change in the size of the public issue.Why should IPO investors fund DLG-backed lending?The revised IPO plan also raised a separate question around Moneyview’s use of the fresh capital.Of the ₹750 crore fresh issue, ₹325 crore has been earmarked for driving growth in loan disbursals under Default Loss Guarantee (DLG) arrangements. ETBFSI asked the management why public-market investors should effectively fund credit risk through a DLG structure rather than Moneyview deploying more capital directly on its own balance sheet.Moneyview has increasingly built its lending business around a partner-led, off-balance-sheet model. In its presentation, the company said around 75% of its business is conducted through lending partners’ balance sheets, with the remaining business routed through its own NBFC balance sheet. Under the lending service provider model, Moneyview acquires and services customers while the lending partner provides the loan capital.The management explained the ₹325-crore DLG allocation in the context of this growing off-balance-sheet lending model. However, the response in the press conference did not fully spell out the maximum potential credit-loss exposure under these arrangements or the economics Moneyview earns for assuming that risk. Advt Credit losses down as Moneyview scales unsecured lendingThis comes against the backdrop of Moneyview’s continued dependence on consumer credit, particularly personal loans. Agarwal spoke on asset quality and delinquencies as the macro environment remains challenging for unsecured consumer lending.Agarwal said Moneyview has been in the personal-loan business for around nine years and has navigated multiple credit cycles. According to him, annualised losses that were earlier in the 7.5-8% range have come down to below 7% over the past two to three years.“Our strength is identifying good customers,” Agarwal said, pointing to the company’s use of data and technology to assess individual income, expenses and repayment affordability. Advt He said the company adjusts its products based on the customer’s ability to repay, arguing that “designing the right product for the right individual” has helped Moneyview maintain credit performance even as the broader macro environment changes.The company’s argument for the reduced capital requirement rests on a combination of continued credit-led growth, increasing profitability and the use of partner balance sheets to scale lending without funding the entire loan book directly.Moneyview IPO: Price band and issue structureMoneyview has fixed the price band for its IPO at ₹32-34 per share. The ₹1,091.61-crore issue will open for subscription on September 24 and close on September 28, with the anchor book opening on September 23. At the upper end of the price band, the company will command an implied market capitalisation of about ₹5,985 crore. Advt The IPO comprises a fresh issue of ₹750 crore and an offer-for-sale of up to 10.04 crore equity shares by promoters and existing investors, including Puneet Agarwal, Sanjay Aggarwal, Accel, Tiger Global, Ribbit Capital, DI Investment and Crimson Winter. The minimum bid is 441 shares, while 50% of the issue has been reserved for qualified institutional buyers, 15% for non-institutional investors and 35% for retail investors.
