Summary: Flow is a proof-of-stake application network whose native FLOW token is used for fees, storage capacity and network staking. Its account model separates addresses from keys, supports weighted multi-key control, and stores Cadence contracts and resource assets inside accounts. This article explains those mechanics without treating them as an investment case, maps staking and custody risks, and identifies the transaction-level evidence an Indian taxpayer should preserve for VDA income, transfer and TDS reporting. Key Takeaways Show all 23 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Flow Network and FLOW Token: Verified Technical Identity Flow is a layer-1 proof-of-stake network designed for applications and digital assets. FLOW is the native fungible token used to pay transaction fees, support account storage and participate in staking. A token balance is not a share in Dapper Labs, the Flow Foundation or an application operating on the network. It does not create a contractual redemption right, dividend or guaranteed yield. Users should distinguish the protocol asset from branded collectibles, application tokens, bridged assets and exchange-created representations that may carry different issuer, custody and recovery risks. Specialised node roles do not remove validator risk Official staking documentation identifies Collection, Consensus, Execution, Verification and Access node roles. The separation assigns different work to different participants, while the protocol’s identity table determines the authorised node set for an epoch. This architecture does not make transactions reversible or eliminate software, networking, governance or concentration risk. A wallet user ordinarily relies on access infrastructure to submit or query transactions, but final state changes are governed by the network protocol rather than by the wallet interface. An account can hold multiple public keys, and a key can be revoked or replaced. Each key carries a weight; a transaction needs total signing weight of at least 1,000 to authorise account access. This supports multi-signature and recovery designs, but only when the owner understands which keys, weights and devices are active. A compromised combination reaching the threshold can authorise transactions, while loss of every usable threshold combination can make assets inaccessible. Resources are stored in accounts Cadence introduces resource types intended to represent assets that exist in one location at a time. The account stores resources and may publish capabilities that grant defined access. This differs from assuming every asset is merely a number recorded in a contract mapping. A user should verify the exact contract address, resource type and capability being authorised. A malicious transaction may request broader access than a screen label suggests, and a legitimate contract can still contain defects or administrative controls. The payer may be the user or a wallet or service that subsidises the fee, so the signer, proposer, authoriser and payer should not automatically be treated as the same economic person. Flow accounts also need a minimum FLOW reservation for storage. Official documentation states a 0.001 FLOW minimum and explains that a transaction fails and reverts if it would exceed storage capacity or reduce the balance below the required reservation. Wallet displays should therefore be reconciled to on-chain events rather than treated as complete accounting records. A transaction record should identify more than the token amount For each acquisition, disposal, transfer, staking action or reward, preserve the UTC timestamp, transaction ID, addresses, asset and contract identity, quantity, fee payer, FLOW fee, exchange or wallet statement and INR valuation method. Internal transfers between wallets controlled by the same taxpayer may not be a sale, but the taxpayer must be able to prove common ownership and separate the network fee from the transferred principal. Bridging or wrapping can create a different token representation and should be documented as a distinct technical event before deciding its tax character. Export exchange trade and deposit-withdrawal histories before an account closes. Save explorer evidence and wallet confirmations for self-custody transactions. Record the source and timestamp of the INR conversion used for each taxable event. Separate protocol rewards, application incentives, airdrops and reimbursements. Keep evidence of failed transactions and refunded amounts instead of deleting them. Flow Staking, Delegation and Exit Timing Node operators stake FLOW and delegators may assign stake to an operator without running the node role themselves. Epochs are roughly one week. Stake or unstake instructions are processed at epoch boundaries, and official documentation states that an unstaking participant must wait an additional epoch before the unlocked tokens are withdrawable. Staking operations can also be unavailable during epoch setup and commit phases. A displayed request is therefore not equivalent to immediately spendable FLOW. Rewards and slashing are conditional protocol outcomes Rewards depend on protocol rules, participation and node performance; misconduct can be slashable. A delegation service may also impose commission, custody or interface terms. None of these mechanics creates a fixed INR return. The user should identify whether the arrangement is native delegation, custodial exchange staking, liquid-staking exposure or an application deposit, because control, withdrawal and counterparty risks differ. Reward records should show gross units, deductions, receipt or credit time, wallet and later disposal rather than only the net balance. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 framework applicable from 1 April 2026, special VDA provisions continue the separate treatment of transfer income and the restricted deduction approach associated with VDA transfers. Acquisition cost evidence is therefore critical. Network fees, staking income, airdrops and business activity should not be assigned a tax result by analogy without examining their facts. TDS is not a final tax calculation Section 194S-style withholding rules reflected in the 2025 Act require attention when consideration for a VDA transfer is paid and threshold, payer and transaction conditions are met. Exchange withholding, buyer withholding and in-kind consideration can require different operational steps. TDS credit does not establish the acquisition cost, extinguish the return-filing obligation or prove that every wallet transfer was a taxable sale. Reconcile Form 26AS or the applicable tax statement with exchange reports and on-chain evidence, and investigate mismatches before filing. Staking receipts need event-specific analysis A staking reward may raise an income-recognition question when the taxpayer receives control, followed by a separate VDA-transfer question when the rewarded FLOW is later sold or exchanged. The article does not prescribe one outcome for every delegation or custodial programme. Maintain reward timestamps, vesting or withdrawal restrictions, operator deductions, fair-value evidence and disposal records so a tax professional can apply the current statute and guidance to the actual arrangement. Custody, Contract and Compliance Review Before Using FLOW Self-custody places key security, recovery and transaction review on the holder. Exchange custody introduces insolvency, withdrawal, internal-ledger and account-freeze risks. A Flow account’s weighted keys can support safer separation of duties, but an incorrect weight configuration can also lock out the owner or leave one device with full control. Hardware-wallet support, recovery procedures and key rotation should be tested with small amounts and documented before a material transfer. Identity, venue and legal permissions remain separate questions The existence of Flow mainnet, an official wallet or a token listing does not establish that a particular Indian platform is registered, that a foreign service can lawfully solicit Indian users, or that a token transaction meets tax and exchange-control requirements. Verify the service provider, contracting entity, terms, custody model and applicable Indian compliance status independently. Do not send FLOW to an address merely because its ticker or logo matches; confirm the network and contract or resource identity and use a controlled test transaction where appropriate. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Is FLOW the same as an ownership interest in Dapper Labs or the Flow Foundation? No. FLOW is the network’s native token. Holding it does not by itself create shares, dividends, redemption rights or a guaranteed claim against a company or foundation. 2. Why can a Flow account have several keys? Flow separates the account address from public keys and supports weighted keys. This enables multi-signature, rotation and recovery designs, but the configured signing threshold must be understood and protected. 3. Can unstaked FLOW be withdrawn immediately? Not necessarily. Official documentation says unstaking is processed at an epoch boundary and an additional epoch generally passes before the unstaked tokens become withdrawable. 4. Does a subsidised transaction mean the wallet owns the asset? No. A service may pay the transaction fee while another account authorises the asset movement. Accounting should identify the authorisers, payer and beneficial owner separately. 5. Is moving FLOW between my own wallets automatically a taxable sale? A genuine transfer between wallets under the same beneficial ownership may differ from a disposal, but ownership, quantities, fees and addresses must be proved. Obtain advice for the specific facts. 6. Does TDS deducted by an exchange settle the final tax on FLOW? No. Withholding is a credit and compliance mechanism. The taxpayer must still determine income, acquisition cost, transfer value and return disclosure under the law for the relevant year. 7. What should be saved for staking rewards? Keep the operator or service terms, reward date and quantity, wallet or exchange statement, deductions, INR valuation evidence, withdrawal status and the later disposal trail. Key Takeaways FLOW is used for fees, storage and staking; it is not corporate equity or a guaranteed-return product. Flow accounts support weighted multiple keys, while Cadence resources and capabilities require transaction-level review. Staking and unstaking follow epoch timing and may involve an additional withdrawal wait. Indian VDA reporting depends on the actual acquisition, reward, transfer and payment facts; TDS is not final tax.
