ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Published On Sep 7, 2026 at 08:22 AM IST var news_msid = 133860670; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The one-year median marginal cost of funds-based lending rate (MCLR) of scheduled commercial banks moved above its year-ago level in August for the first time in 14 months, despite the repo rate remaining unchanged, pointing to some persistence in funding-cost pressures. The increase, however, was not broad-based and was driven entirely by foreign banks, while MCLR for public sector and private sector banks remained unchanged.The one-year median MCLR rose to 8.70% in August from 8.60% in July, compared with 8.60% a year earlier. The repo rate has remained unchanged at 5.25%, making the increase notable even as banking system liquidity improved sharply during the period. Advt Surplus liquidity in the banking system rose to around Rs 6.7 lakh crore by August 31 from Rs 2.5 lakh crore at the end of July and Rs 1.4 lakh crore at the end of June. The improvement was largely supported by the conversion of Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits into rupees, which eased near-term funding pressures for banks.The increase in MCLR was concentrated among foreign banks, whose one-year median MCLR rose to 7.33% in August from 7.20% in July. The corresponding rates for public sector and private sector banks remained unchanged at 8.80% and 9.35%, respectively.Liquidity boostThe improvement in liquidity also helped banks' fresh spreads recover in July. The spread for scheduled commercial banks widened by 8 basis points month-on-month to 2.62%, as fresh deposit rates fell 9 basis points to 5.90%, while fresh lending rates remained largely stable at 8.52%.The improvement was more pronounced among private banks, whose fresh spreads widened 22 basis points to 3.13% as fresh deposit rates fell 25 basis points to 5.96%. In comparison, fresh spreads for public sector banks narrowed by 5 basis points to 1.58%.The easing in funding conditions was also reflected in banks' reduced reliance on certificates of deposit. CD issuance moderated to around Rs 95,000 crore in July from Rs 1.71 lakh crore in June, while CD and commercial paper rates softened during the second half of July and August as system liquidity improved. Advt The credit-deposit growth gap also narrowed sharply. Bank credit grew 18.3% year-on-year as of August 15, compared with 14.7% growth in deposits, leaving a gap of 360 basis points. The gap had been around 500 basis points a month earlier, when credit and deposit growth stood at 17.7% and 12.7%, respectively.However, the improvement in funding conditions could prove temporary as the special FCNR(B) deposit mobilisation window closed on August 31, with associated swaps available until September 11. The easing in deposit rates could therefore moderate as the impact of FCNR(B) inflows fades and banks resume competing for deposits. Advt Fresh lending rates have remained relatively sticky, with the weighted average lending rate on fresh loans of scheduled commercial banks easing only marginally to 8.52% in July. Cumulative transmission to fresh lending rates since February 2025 stood at 80 basis points, compared with 125 basis points of repo-rate cuts.Outstanding lending rates have also remained broadly stable because changes in funding and lending rates are transmitting gradually to existing loan and deposit books. The weighted average lending rate on outstanding rupee loans edged up to 8.97% in July, while the corresponding outstanding deposit rate remained unchanged at 6.58%. Advt The divergence between private and public sector banks is also linked to differences in their loan benchmark structures. Private banks have a larger share of floating-rate loans linked to external benchmarks, limiting the scope for further repricing while the repo rate remains unchanged. Public sector banks, with a larger share of MCLR-linked portfolios, remain more sensitive to changes in funding costs.The 10-year government security yield also rose to 6.95% at end-August from 6.74% at end-June, reflecting renewed pressure on the bond market amid higher crude prices, geopolitical risks and elevated US Treasury yields.With credit demand remaining healthy, competition for deposits is likely to persist even as surplus liquidity provides near-term support to bank funding conditions. The recent improvement in spreads may therefore prove difficult to sustain once the effect of FCNR(B) inflows fades, keeping margin expansion gradual and uneven across banks.
