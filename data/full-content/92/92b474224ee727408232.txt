Articles 5 min read LIC’s bancassurance business could get a ‘substantial boost’; mis-selling crackdown needs wider review: Vipin Anand Former LIC Managing Director Vipin Anand expects tighter restrictions on distribution incentives to make LIC policies more competitive within bank networks. He also calls for a separate review of mis-selling, an additional allowance for agent incentives and marketing expenses, and a gradual reduction in commission payouts. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Oct 22, 2026 at 02:14 PM IST var news_msid = 135113211; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India’s (IRDAI) proposed commission curbs could improve Life Insurance Corporation of India’s (LIC) competitive position in bancassurance, with former LIC Managing Director Vipin Anand expecting business to shift towards the state-owned insurer as restrictions on distributor incentives narrow the advantage enjoyed by private insurers.Anand said LIC has partnerships with a wider network of bank branches but has not fully leveraged them for insurance sales, as banks have often directed business towards private insurers offering more attractive incentives. If the proposed framework reduces these differences, LIC could gain business through existing bank partnerships and potentially improve its market share. Advt “I personally feel that LIC’s bancassurance business could improve substantially because banks have more branches partnered with LIC, but they are doing very little bancassurance business. What was happening was that banks were passing business to private companies that were giving them more incentives,” Anand told ETBFSI in an interview.“If the incentive gets pared, selling LIC policies becomes easier. Some of the business that was going to private companies from the same banks may start coming to LIC because a lot of customers prefer LIC,” he said.Anand also questioned the pace of insurance penetration by private insurers, arguing that the industry needs to focus more on expanding coverage. He estimated that LIC accounts for more than 55% of the market, with its share of the number of policies at around 65%.“Even after 25 years of opening up the sector, why does LIC still have such a large share of the number of policies? Out of 24 companies, LIC alone is selling more than half the policies,” he said.Commission cuts must account for incentives and selling costsAnand cautioned that the proposed commission limits could have a sharper impact than headline rates suggest because they also take into account non-commission incentives, including gifts, travel and other benefits. He said agents also bear office, travel and customer-acquisition costs that need to be factored into the framework.“The impact is not on commission. The impact is going to fall very heavily on the incentives because the figures given in the paper include both commission and non-commission incentives. The commission may look very healthy at 20% or 25%, but these figures include all kinds of incentives, which were substantial,” Anand said. Advt He recommended allowing insurers an additional 10% of premium for incentives and marketing expenses, over and above the proposed commission limits. He said selling life insurance often requires repeated visits and follow-ups, making distribution a costly and time-intensive process.Anand also called for a phased reduction in payouts instead of a sharp cut. “In place of bringing it from 70 to 20, initially bring it from 70 to 30 and then revise it after another three years. Bringing it down too sharply at one go is too much,” he said.IRDAI has proposed bringing life insurers’ expenses of management (EoM) down to 15% of gross direct premium income within two years and 12.5% within five years. Anand said commission reductions would themselves help lower expenses, as commissions account for nearly 30% of life insurers’ total EoM, according to his estimate. Advt He said LIC’s cost ratio was around 11%, while SBI Life’s was below 10%, arguing that several insurers were already operating within the proposed limits before the 2023 regulatory changes.“I would not say the 2023 regulation was a regulatory mistake. Maybe the regulator had too much faith in the industry, hoping that the industry would self-regulate itself,” he said.Anand suggested that insurers less than 10 years old be given some additional flexibility because they face higher initial costs in setting up operations and have yet to build renewal income. However, he said established insurers that continue to have high costs should reassess their business models or consider consolidation. Advt Credit-linked payouts and mis-selling need a closer lookAnand questioned high payouts on certain credit-linked insurance products, arguing that commissions should reflect the work involved in distribution and the value provided to customers. He said insurance bundled with loans also protects lenders, making it important to examine whether the distribution payouts are justified.On mis-selling, Anand said commission clawbacks and restrictions on bank employee incentives would not be enough to address the problem. He argued that the regulator must also examine insurer conduct, particularly where complaints are concentrated among a small number of companies.“Clawback is too little, too late, and it does not work by itself. Whom are they going to recover the commission from at the bank, and what about the employee who sold the policy? What is there to deter him if he sells the policy and moves on?” Anand said.He cautioned that removing incentives altogether could also reduce insurance sales and customer access. Instead, he called for targeted action against insurers and intermediaries where evidence points to persistent mis-selling, rather than placing the burden primarily on individual agents or bank employees.“If 90% of mis-selling complaints are coming from four companies out of 24, why do you have to look only at the agent, bank or broker? You need to examine the companies that are a major part of the problem and take action where the evidence points,” he said.Anand called for a separate, comprehensive review of mis-selling involving people with direct experience of insurance distribution. He said the issue was too complex to be addressed through commission rules alone and required stronger accountability across the industry.“Mis-selling cannot be addressed in a couple of pages in a regulatory paper; it is a book in itself. The problem is much more serious, and we are only touching the surface,” he said.The challenge for IRDAI will be to reduce excessive distribution costs and protect policyholders without undermining the economics of insurance sales. For LIC, Anand believes the changes could create a competitive opportunity in bancassurance, while the final framework will determine how insurers balance commissions, incentives and consumer protection.
