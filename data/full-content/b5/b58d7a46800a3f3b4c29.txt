Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re SARG Media Holding Private Limited (NCLT Mumbai) Summary: The National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai Bench, Court V, sanctioned the scheme of arrangement involving the merger by absorption of SARG Media Holding Private Limited into ARG Outlier Media Private Limited under Sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act, 2013. The transferee company operates Republic Bangla, Republic Bharat and Republic TV. The petitioners submitted that the merger would simplify the group’s ownership structure, eliminate administrative duplication, pool resources and address restrictions on layers of subsidiaries, without changing the transferee company’s control or management. The scheme had received the requisite approvals of shareholders and creditors. Following changes in the transferee company’s share capital, the equity share exchange ratio was revised from 0.6416 to 0.6419 equity shares for every equity share held in the transferor company, while the preference share exchange ratio remained 0.0870 for every preference share. A material issue concerned Clause 18.2, which contemplated consolidation of authorised share capital but inadvertently reproduced only the transferee company’s authorised capital in the proposed memorandum clause. The petitioners proposed a corrected combined authorised capital of ₹57.50 crore. After the Tribunal initially questioned whether the proposed correction would alter the petition behind shareholders’ and creditors’ backs, the companies placed additional affidavits and board resolutions dated 14 August 2026 on record. These approved correction of the capital clause and extension of the scheme’s validity from three to four years. The Tribunal found the error inadvertent, its rectification permissible under the scheme’s amendment clause, and the amendments non-prejudicial to creditors and stakeholders. The Regional Director ultimately expressed satisfaction with the petitioners’ responses, and the Official Liquidator reported no adverse observations. The petitioners also addressed loan repayment defaults, statutory filings, accounting treatment, beneficial ownership disclosures and sectoral requirements. They explained that the transferor company’s shareholder-related loans would pass to the operating transferee company, and undertook compliance with Section 232(3)(i), applicable accounting standards and directions of tax and other regulators. The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting had advised compliance with its policy guidelines, including requirements concerning shareholding, directors and security clearance. The transferee company represented that ultimate beneficial ownership and effective control would remain unchanged. The appointed date, originally stated as 1 April 2023, was revised to 1 April 2026. Finding the scheme fair, reasonable, lawful and consistent with public policy, the Tribunal approved it with 1 April 2026 as the appointed date and directed dissolution of the transferor company without winding up. The sanction does not grant exemption from stamp duty, taxes, charges or other statutory requirements, and does not prevent action for legal violations. Tax liabilities and related obligations of the transferor company pass to the transferee company, while the Income Tax Department retains its powers under the Income Tax Act. Pending proceedings will continue against or by the transferee company, and employees will transfer without interruption of service on terms no less favourable. The companies must file the certified order and revised scheme with the Registrar of Companies in e-form INC-28 within 30 days of receipt and lodge the documents with the Superintendent of Stamps within 60 days for adjudication of stamp duty, if any.
