Articles 2 min read Tata Sons-Noel Rift Widens Over Succession Calls Appointments at Trent, Voltas and Tata International reignite oversight concerns within the group kala vijayraghavan and sagar malviya Published On Nov 12, 2026 at 07:59 AM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 135752672; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Mumbai: Tata Sons has been blindsided by leadership succession decisions at some Noel Tata-led group companies, which have appointed new chairpersons without seeking the holding company's customary approval, people close to the matter said.The appointments, made after Noel Tata stepped down as chairman after turning 70 earlier this month, have brought into sharper focus the rift between Tata, who heads Tata Trusts, and Tata Sons, led by chairman N Chandrasekaran, over authority and the holding company's role in succession decisions at group companies.While the people cited above said Tata Sons' approval has always been sought for such appointments, legal experts said such approval would be legally mandatory only if the company's articles of association specifically provide for it. The Companies Act, 2013, does not require a promoter or holding company to approve the appointment of a chairperson. Advt The appointments of Bahram Vakil as chairman of Trent and Tata Investment Corporation, Sunil D'Souza as chairman of Voltas, and TV Narendran as chairman of Tata International were made without prior approval from Tata Sons, sources said.The holding company is likely to take up the matter at its next meeting, they said, adding that the emergence of parallel power centres within the conglomerate is a concern. Tata Sons and Noel Tata declined to comment.Tata was in control of strategic decisions and business plans at the group companies he chaired, officials said. However, appointments of chairmen and CEOs at these companies have always been referred to Tata Sons for approval, they said. "These are practices always followed by the Tata group. The chairman of Tata Sons has the choice of being the chairman of any group companies he wants to. So, approval of chairman or board members of all group companies have been taken to the Tata Sons chairman for several years," a group insider explained. "That has not been followed by Tata this year." All these companies are subsidiaries of Tata Sons and must keep Tata Sons updated on board developments, the person said."Tata International is a 100% subsidiary of Tata Sons and any top appointments on other companies, including Voltas, as a practice, have always been taken up to the chairman of Tata Sons," another official said. The developments have raised questions within the holding company over the extent to which operating companies are beginning to function independently of the group's central leadership and oversight framework.Group company CEOs are also conflicted about showing allegiance and loyalty on such matters, insiders said. As a matter of corporate law, a company's board chooses its chairperson from among its directors, subject to its articles of association and applicable regulations. "The dispute, therefore, appears to be about group authority and convention, not the legal validity of the appointments," said Harshal Anjaria, founder of Shreeyam Advisors.
