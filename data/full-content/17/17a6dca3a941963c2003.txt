Summary: CBIC Circular No. 257/03/2026-GST dated 26 October 2026 provides detailed guidance on GST registration procedures, prescribed documentation, verification requirements, departmental queries and processing timelines, with the objective of reducing unnecessary delays while preventing fraudulent registrations. Issued under Section 168(1) of the CGST Act, the Circular clarifies the responsibilities of applicants and proper officers regarding identity verification, constitution of business, authorised signatories and proof of principal place of business. It explains acceptable documents for owned, rented, inherited, consent-based and co-working premises and discourages officers from demanding documents beyond prescribed requirements, raising presumptive objections or rejecting applications merely because premises are residential. The guidance also covers Aadhaar authentication, biometric verification, physical verification, Forms GST REG-01, REG-03, REG-04 and REG-05, deemed approval and grievance redressal. Particular attention is given to Rule 14A, which provides a simplified three-working-day registration route for eligible applicants whose monthly output tax on supplies to registered persons does not exceed ₹2.5 lakh, subject to prescribed conditions. The article further explains registration timelines, treatment of previously cancelled GSTINs, bank-account reporting under Rule 10A and the distinction between the Circular and amendments introduced through Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax. For practical compliance, it recommends attaching a voluntary one-page fact sheet explaining the applicant’s constitution and premises arrangements, maintaining consistent supporting documents, monitoring statutory deadlines and preserving a complete registration record. Eight practical tables, illustrative calculations and a comprehensive checklist help taxpayers, Chartered Accountants and GST practitioners prepare accurate applications, respond effectively to departmental queries and minimise avoidable registration disputes. First, what kind of document is this? Issued under section 168(1) of the CGST Act, the circular is administrative guidance. It explains the law; it does not amend it. It builds on Instruction No. 03/2025-GST of 17 April 2025, which told officers to stick to the prescribed document list and avoid presumptive queries. One caution: on the same date, Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax substituted REG-01 and added Rule 14B. Those changes come from the notification, with their own commencement dates. Keep the two apart. Your deadline, not the officer’s Apply within 30 days of becoming liable to register. A casual or non-resident taxable person must apply at least five days before starting business. The officer’s processing timelines are a separate matter, covered in Section 6. 2. Name, people and authority Part A: the identity line Part A generates the TRN. For a proprietorship, the legal name is the proprietor’s name; the shop name goes in the trade name field. For a company, the name must match the PAN and the MCA incorporation certificate. If PAN and MCA records differ, correct them at source rather than typing whichever version clears validation. Use the applicant’s own PAN-linked email and mobile. A TRN is not an ARN; only the ARN shows a complete application. The address: where most files get stuck The principal place of business must point to the actual unit: shop or plot number, street, locality, district, PIN and, where relevant, khasra or khata. A bare village name may cover a dozen buildings. Put the map pin on the real entrance, check Centre and State jurisdiction, and describe the premises honestly. If documents carry an old street name, explain the link with evidence. The family property trap The circular says plainly that property of parents, spouse, siblings, partners or directors is not automatically the applicant’s property. A company running from its director’s house needs consent or a rent arrangement. A proprietorship is different: proprietor and business are one person in law, so the proprietor’s own house is owned premises. The spouse’s house is not. Rent agreements and the “no agreement” route The circular does not make every lease registrable, and an “eleven-month agreement” label settles nothing under local law. A valid registered agreement needs no extra ownership bundle. The no-agreement route is a concession on evidence; it does not make unlawful occupation lawful. Consent, co-working and additional places A consent letter should name owner, applicant, relationship and space. For co-working, a receipt or brochure is not evidence; check the chain before the client signs up. Each additional place needs its own proof; towers and ATMs need not be declared. In Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Assam and Madhya Pradesh, electricity board and consumer number fields are mandatory, so check the live State requirements. 5. Always attach a fact sheet An officer opening an application sees a form and a stack of PDFs, and must work out for himself who the applicant is and why the bill is in someone else’s name. Most REG-03 queries are born in that gap. My advice is simple: with every application, attach a one-page fact sheet signed by the authorised signatory. It should answer the two questions that cause the most trouble: what is the constitution of the business, and what is the principal place of business and on what basis is it occupied. Upload it along with the supporting documents wherever the form allows. Table 5 Fact sheet template Point to cover What to write A. Mismatches that would trigger a query (a bill in the landlord’s name, a director’s house, an old street name) are explained before anyone asks, and a visiting inspector knows which door to knock on. Two cautions The fact sheet is not a document prescribed by the circular. It supports the prescribed documents; it never replaces them. And since it is signed, every line must be true and must match the form. 6. How long should it take? “GSTIN in three days” is only partly true, and adding thirty days to every case is equally wrong. The clock depends on the route, verification and any valid query. Where centre verification applies, the application is complete only after it succeeds, so keep every acknowledgement. Counting from the TRN leads to mistaken complaints of delay. The officer’s report must give a clear finding on existence; a “non-existent” finding must record the efforts made, with GPS-tagged photographs. Deemed approval needs a clean chronology Deemed approval (para 5.7) applies stage by stage; pending authentication on your side is not officer inaction. Keep a dated record of every step. 7. Rule 14A: a fast lane with a speed limit Rule 14A gives automatic registration within three working days to eligible applicants. The test is monthly output tax on supplies to registered persons not exceeding Rs. 2.5 lakh, counting CGST, SGST or UTGST, IGST and compensation cess together. It is not turnover, and it is not the net cash paid after ITC. Since the rule says “not exceeding”, a figure of exactly Rs. 2.5 lakh still qualifies. Meeting the money test alone does not make an applicant eligible. Only one Rule 14A registration is allowed per PAN in a State, with Aadhaar authentication. To withdraw (from 1 April 2026), at least one tax period’s returns and all due returns must be filed; withdrawal runs through REG-32/REG-33 with REG-01 documents (para 6.1). Higher B2B tax can be reported only from the month after the order; filing the request does not lift the cap. Rule 9A (also three working days) is different: the system picks those cases on risk; it cannot be demanded. 8. If a demand goes beyond the prescribed list, say so politely and cite the paragraph, but reply anyway. Ignoring even an unjustified REG-03 can end in rejection. If REG-05 follows, read its reasons before refiling or appealing. A GSTIN is not a municipal or fire permission, and such permissions cannot be demanded in place of prescribed GST documents. 9. Old baggage and the bank account Paragraph 4 deals with applicants whose earlier GSTIN on the same PAN was suspended under Rule 21A or cancelled under Rule 22. Pending returns and dues, including GSTR-10, must be addressed; a fresh application is no escape. Collect the cancellation order, grounds and liability position, and never certify compliance that has not happened. Bank details are not needed at the application stage. Under Rule 10A, they must be furnished within 30 days of grant or before filing GSTR-1 or IFF, whichever is earlier. So an early GSTR-1 brings the deadline forward. The account must be in the applicant’s own name and PAN; a passbook page, statement or cancelled cheque is enough. If no bank tab appears initially, add it by amendment within time. 10. If things still go wrong: the grievance route Paragraph 8 allows a grievance about delay or unwarranted queries to the jurisdictional Chief Commissioner of Central Tax or Commissioner of State Tax, on the special email notified for that Zone or State. There is no national address. A grievance does not stop the clock for an appeal. 11. Fine print worth a second look Cross-reference slips. Para 5.4(ii) cites Annexure IV for the address list, which sits in Annexure III. Annexure III’s inherited and no-agreement rows also differ from paras 3.8.2.3 and FAQ 25. Follow the substantive route and explain your evidence. Rule 14B from 1 November 2026. A separate route for suppliers using an e-commerce operator’s warehouse in another State, from the notification, not the circular. 12. For professionals, registration is not an upload exercise. Most queries will then never be raised, and the few that are can be answered with confidence.
