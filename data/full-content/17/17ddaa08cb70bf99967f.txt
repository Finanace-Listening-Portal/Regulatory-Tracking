Summary: Karur Vysya Bank’s Samriddhi 400 is a named 400-day retail term deposit with bank-published rates of 7.20% for the general public and NRIs and 7.70% for senior citizens, subject to eligibility and the terms in force when the deposit is booked. The official product page states a minimum deposit of ₹1,000, a maximum below ₹3 crore, payout and cumulative choices, auto-renewal at the rate prevailing on maturity in the absence of instructions, and premature withdrawal with a 1% rate reduction against the applicable rate for the actual run period. Interest is taxable, while section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 currently specifies bank-interest TDS thresholds of ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen. DICGC cover is not product-wise: eligible deposits with the same insured bank are aggregated in the same right and capacity, with principal and interest covered together up to ₹5 lakh. Who the bank says can open the deposit The product page lists individuals, joint individuals, minors, Hindu undivided families, sole proprietorship concerns, trusts, associations, clubs and societies, partnership firms, joint-stock companies and other entities not prohibited by the Reserve Bank of India. That broad list does not mean every entity receives the same rate, tax treatment, senior-citizen benefit or digital-channel access. Entity documents, KYC, beneficial ownership, authority to invest and FEMA status must be checked separately. NRI eligibility does not make every interest receipt tax-free The bank page includes NRIs alongside the 7.20% display, but the account type and applicable FEMA and income-tax conditions matter. NRE, NRO and resident deposits have different legal and tax consequences. A person who changes residential status should inform the bank and obtain advice rather than assume that the original account label can continue unchanged. Payout and cumulative choices change cash-flow timing KVB states that interest-payout and cumulative options are available. Monthly, quarterly and half-yearly interest payments are stated to use a simple rate of interest; the bank’s general fixed-deposit page additionally notes that monthly interest is paid at a discounted rate. A cumulative option retains interest within the deposit until maturity under the bank’s calculation method. Depositors should compare the deposit receipt and maturity instruction, not only the headline annual rate. Do not calculate maturity value with an assumed compounding convention A 400-day period does not equal a calendar year, and the product page states an actual/actual interest-calculation basis. The exact maturity amount can depend on the booked principal, value date, payout or cumulative selection, compounding or discount method, rounding and tax deduction. Use the bank-generated advice or calculator and retain the final confirmation. A manually estimated amount should be treated only as a cross-check. Premature closure applies the run-period rate and a 1% reduction Premature withdrawal is allowed. The product page states that interest will be paid for the period the money actually remained with the bank at the applicable rate, subject to a pre-closure penalty. It specifies a 1% penalty, expressed as payment of interest at 1.00% less than the rate applicable on the deposit-opening date for the actual run period. This means the original 400-day headline rate should not be used automatically when a deposit is closed earlier. Auto-renewal can change the rate and the liquidity date If the bank has no contrary maturity instruction, the page says the deposit will be auto-renewed for the same period at the rate prevailing on the maturity date. The renewed rate may differ from 7.20% or 7.70%, and the next maturity will move by another 400-day period. A depositor who needs proceeds on a particular date should lodge and preserve an instruction before maturity and verify that the bank has recorded it. Renewal is a new rate event, not continuation of the old headline The original receipt proves the first booking. After renewal, obtain a new receipt showing principal carried forward, interest treatment, renewed tenure, new rate, nominee and lien status. If tax has been deducted or payout interest was already received, reconcile those amounts before treating the renewed principal as the original principal plus all accrued interest. Interest is taxable even when no TDS is deducted Interest on a bank term deposit is ordinarily taxable according to the depositor’s applicable income-tax position. Tax deduction at source is only a collection mechanism; crossing or not crossing a threshold does not determine final taxability. Under section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, the current threshold for interest other than interest on securities paid by a banking company is ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen and ₹50,000 for a person other than a senior citizen. The section applies aggregation rules, and core-banking interest should not be tested branch by branch. Use the current statutory thresholds, not a stale product-page FAQ One KVB promotional page still refers to older ₹40,000 and ₹50,000 limits. Those figures should not be used for TY 2026-27. The current official Income Tax Department text of section 393 specifies ₹50,000 and ₹1,00,000 respectively. A declaration seeking non-deduction, where legally available, does not make interest exempt; eligibility, estimated total income and the current form requirements must be checked before submission. DICGC insurance is aggregated across deposits at the bank Karur Vysya Bank is a bank deposit product, so DICGC principles are relevant. DICGC states that eligible deposits such as savings, fixed, current and recurring deposits are insured, but the maximum is ₹5 lakh for principal and interest together for each depositor at the same bank in the same right and capacity. Multiple branches and multiple account numbers do not create separate limits when the ownership capacity is the same. Insurance cover is not a guarantee of immediate access or every rupee DICGC liability arises in specified events such as liquidation, cancellation of the bank’s licence, an applicable reconstruction or amalgamation scheme, or qualifying all-inclusive directions with withdrawal restrictions. Balances may be aggregated and bank dues may be set off. A deposit above the insured limit remains a claim against the bank under the applicable process.
