Articles 5 min read Banks, SFBs raise IRDAI insurance commission concerns through IBA, ASFBI; seek transition time Banks and small finance banks are engaging with the Indian Banks’ Association (IBA) and the Association of Small Finance Banks in India (ASFBI) over IRDAI’s proposed insurance distribution reforms, with lenders seeking time to adjust to any changes. JM Financial estimates SFBs are the most exposed banking segment, with insurance income accounting for 13–25 basis points of assets. A mid-sized private bank CEO told ETBFSI that the bank has already sent its representation to IBA, while the Association of Small Finance Banks in India (ASFBI) is also preparing a representation. The deadline for submitting feedback has been extended to October 31st, 2026. Advt ETBFSI had earlier reported on October 21 that FIDC was preparing a representation to IRDAI after aggregating feedback from NBFCs. FIDC has now asked the regulator for a five-year glide path for distributors to reduce insurance commissions, similar to the transition proposed for insurers under the EoM framework, while seeking recognition of the infrastructure, manpower, technology, compliance, servicing and credit-related costs incurred by lenders.Exclusive: FIDC to represent NBFCs' case to IRDAI for gradual reset of insurance commissionsNon-bank lenders are seeking a phased reduction in insurance distribution commissions proposed by IRDAI, saying that the sharp reduction could make credit-linked insurance distribution unviable and disrupt the network through which insurance reaches customers. Five industry sources told ET BFSI that the SRO body FIDC is aggregating feedback from NBFCs and will submit its representation to IRDAI by October 24.One of the most exposed private banks on insurance income, IndusInd Bank MD & CEO Rajiv Anand said the insurance ecosystem, including manufacturers, distributors and agents, was engaging with regulators and SROs, with banks also part of the ongoing discussions.“We are very much part of the ecosystem. There are multiple conversations that are going on within the insurance ecosystem. We are certainly part of many of these conversations,” Anand told ETBFSI.Banks assess impact, seek time to adjustAxis Bank Executive Director Subrat Mohanty said industry participants have already sent their views to the regulator and that the bank expects there could be some time before the final framework comes into effect, including the possibility of a transition period.“We also possibly believe there might be a few areas where there might be a specific amount of time given or a glide path given for us or for the insurance players to actually manage the change,” Mohanty said. Advt Axis has already assessed the potential impact and is looking at mitigating it through a combination of volumes, product mix and other sources of fee income.“We've done some of the work from our side in terms of what potential impact could be and how we could mitigate through a combination of volumes, product mix, etcetera,” Mohanty said, adding that the bank could get about six to nine months before the changes come through.The potential impact is particularly relevant for banks that have seen insurance distribution income rise sharply since the removal of commission caps under the 2023 Expense of Management (EOM) regime. Advt According to JM Financial, insurance distribution income across its banking coverage rose from 3.5 per cent of PBT in FY23 to 5.1 per cent in FY26, while insurance income as a proportion of average assets increased from 4 basis points to 7 basis points. Life insurance accounts for 84% of total insurance income, making proposed changes to life insurance commissions particularly relevant for banks.Private lenders may turn to other fee pools as IRDAI proposes insurance commission cutsPrivate lenders are engaging with the regulator on IRDAI’s proposed insurance distribution norms, but are also preparing for the possibility of lower commissions. While the impact will vary across lenders, banks say they could see pressure on insurance fee income and are looking to offset it through higher volumes, other fee pools, product-mix changes and efficiency gains.Among large private banks, Axis Bank is the most exposed, with insurance income accounting for 12.5% of FY26 PBT and 18 basis points of average assets, according to JM Financial. HDFC Bank's insurance income accounted for 7.3% of PBT, while Kotak Mahindra Bank stood at 5.8%. ICICI Bank was the least exposed among the large private banks, at 0.6% of PBT. Advt The exposure is substantially higher among some mid-sized private lenders. JM Financial puts insurance income at 30.8% of FY26 PBT for Bandhan Bank, 21% for DCB Bank, 28.5% for RBL Bank and 48.8% for IDFC First Bank.SFBs face sharper balance-sheet impactUjjivan Small Finance Bank Interim CEO Carol Furtado told analysts in their Q2 FY27 call that the lender remains engaged with its insurance partners and through IBA channels, while also giving feedback to IBA and the Small Finance Bank Association.“IRDAI's notification is a consultation paper, and we await the final guidelines on the matter. We remain engaged with our insurance partners as well as through IBA channels,” Furtado said.Small finance banks could face a more pronounced impact relative to their balance sheets, with JM Financial estimating insurance income at 13–25 basis points of average assets, compared with 1–3 basis points for PSU banks. Insurance income accounted for 14–71% of PBT for SFBs in FY26, according to the brokerage.Among individual SFBs, Equitas Small Finance Bank had the highest dependence on insurance income at 70.7% of FY26 PBT, followed by Ujjivan SFB at 19.2% and AU Small Finance Bank at 14.3%. As a proportion of average assets, Ujjivan stood at 0.25%, AU SFB at 0.22% and Equitas at 0.13%.For SFBs, the exposure is not only about the absolute level of commission but also the role insurance plays in the economics of lending, particularly for credit-linked products.A senior banking executive said the industry is seeking recognition of the infrastructure and manpower costs associated with insurance distribution, arguing that a sharp reduction in income could make the business unviable.“See, you have got so much infrastructure cost and manpower cost. So, if you have to incur all these costs, you need income. Otherwise, it will completely become an unviable business for all banks,” the executive said.The executive said banks are responding both individually to IRDAI and through the IBA, while SFBs are also sending their own feedback to the regulator.The executive also argued that credit-linked insurance should not be viewed simply as a low-effort bundled product, pointing to the protection it provides borrowers and their families in the event of death or other adverse events.“Credit shield is a very important proposition. Without credit shield during COVID, many families would have got destroyed,” the banker quoted above said, referring to its role across microfinance, mortgages and other lending segments.Axis's Mohanty said the bank is already looking at mitigating measures, including changes in product mix and volumes, while exploring other sources of fee income.
