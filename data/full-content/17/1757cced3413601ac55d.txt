} } Listen to This ArticleMajor lenders have started raising interest rates on select fixed deposit (FD) tenures, following the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) 25-basis points (bps) repo rate hike in October. The revisions come amid expectations of further monetary tightening, with retail inflation breaching the central bank's upper tolerance limit of 6 per cent. On Thursday, state-owned Bank of Baroda (BoB) raised interest rates on its special 555-day retail term deposit scheme by 10 bps, taking the maximum rate offered to 7.5 per cent, effective November 12. The revised rates under the bob Golden Goal Deposit Scheme apply to retail term deposits below ₹3 crore across customer categories. Rates on deposits across other tenures remain unchanged. General customers will earn 6.85 per cent on callable deposits and 6.90 per cent on non-callable deposits. Resident senior citizens are eligible for rates of up to 7.40 per cent, while super senior citizens (aged 80 years and above) can earn up to 7.50 per cent. Federal Bank has also increased the additional interest benefit offered to senior citizens on term deposits to 0.60 per cent from 0.50 per cent, taking the maximum rate on select tenures to 7.50 per cent. The revised rates, effective November 12, apply to deposits with tenures of 18 months and two years to less than three years. Senior citizens will earn 7.40 per cent on deposits with a premature withdrawal facility and 7.50 per cent on deposits of ₹1 crore and above without the facility. The bank said the effective annualised yield on these deposits could go up to 8.18 per cent and 8.30 per cent, respectively, for a 35 month tenure. General customers, including non-resident Indians (NRIs), will earn 6.80 per cent and 6.90 per cent, respectively, on the two options. The revised rates apply to single term deposits below ₹3 crore.
