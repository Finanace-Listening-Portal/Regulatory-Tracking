The gross non-performing asset (GNPA) ratio of scheduled commercial banks (SCBs) in agriculture could rise to 5.3%-5.6% by March 2028 from an estimated 4.6%-4.8% by March 2027, as the impact of a weak monsoon feeds into loan repayments. Four states that have declared drought account for about a third of the banking system's agricultural credit.The impact of the poor monsoon is expected to push up bad loans mainly in FY28, as some crop loans remain overdue and turn into non-performing assets. CareEdge Ratings expects the agricultural GNPA ratio to first ease to 4.6%-4.8% by March 2027 before rising to 5.3%-5.6% by March 2028. Advt The southwest monsoon ended at 87% of the long-period average, its weakest performance since 2015. Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana have declared drought, with 305 of Maharashtra's 358 talukas and 217 of Karnataka's 240 taluks affected. The four states together account for about a third of scheduled commercial banks' agricultural credit.The GNPA ratio of agriculture loans at 12 public sector banks (PSBs) fell 157 basis points year on year to 5.14% in Q2FY27, from 6.71% a year earlier. It also declined 52 basis points sequentially from 5.65% in Q1FY27. The improvement came even as the monsoon was weak, with growth in agricultural lending accounting for a substantial part of the decline in the ratio.The outstanding agricultural GNPAs of these banks stood at Rs 0.99 lakh crore in September 2026, down 8.5% year on year and 3.8% quarter on quarter. Agricultural advances, however, grew 19.5% year on year to Rs 19.43 lakh crore.Around two-thirds of the 157-basis-point decline in the agricultural GNPA ratio came from growth in the loan book, while the remaining one-third was due to a reduction in bad loans. Agricultural GNPAs continued to rise year on year at four of the 12 PSBs, indicating that the improvement was not uniform across lenders.The divergence between the movement in the bad-loan stock and the ratio suggests that credit growth has played an important role in improving reported asset quality. The increase in agricultural advances has expanded the denominator against which GNPAs are measured, helping bring down the ratio even as the underlying stock of bad loans has declined. Advt Asset quality in focusThe broader banking system has also reported an improvement in asset quality. The GNPA ratio of 30 SCBs declined 53 basis points year on year to 1.54% in Q2FY27, while the net NPA ratio fell to a record low of 0.34%. However, faster loan growth accounted for much of the improvement in the system-wide GNPA ratio as non-food bank credit expanded 19.1% year on year as of September 30, 2026, compared with 11.2% a year earlier.The agricultural loan outlook could therefore become an important test of whether the improvement in banks' asset quality can be sustained. The weak monsoon's effects are expected to become visible as crop loans remain overdue and subsequently migrate into the NPA category, with the impact likely to be concentrated in FY28. Advt The rating agency has identified agriculture, unsecured retail and small-ticket MSME lending as areas that will require closer monitoring as the benefits of the legacy NPA clean-up fade. Under the expected credit loss framework, banks will also have to provide for early signs of stress before loans turn into NPAs.
