Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Navitas Eduservices Private Limited Vs Commissioner of CGST (GSTAT Delhi) Summary: The GST Appellate Tribunal, Principal Bench, New Delhi, disposed of five appeals filed by Navitas Eduservices Private Limited against rejection of refunds of unutilised input tax credit and one appeal filed by the Revenue concerning the first appellate authority’s power to remand. The taxpayer’s five appeals concerned refund amounts of ₹50,77,028, ₹32,62,600, ₹3,80,506, ₹4,24,248 and ₹49,64,984 for periods between January 2018 and March 2023. The principal dispute was whether marketing, promotion, business support and IT-enabled services supplied to Navitas Australia constituted export of services or intermediary services under the IGST Act. Navitas India, a wholly-owned subsidiary of Navitas Australia, supplied services under a Master Services Agreement dated 7 December 2016 and received remuneration in Australian dollars on a cost-plus-16% basis. The agreements described the parties as independent contractors and excluded an agency relationship. Navitas Australia separately contracted with recruitment advisors, who interacted with prospective students and received enrolment-linked commissions. Navitas India was neither a party to those advisor agreements nor a supplier or recipient of services under them. Its services included marketing strategies, advisor support, admission-document verification, relationship management, web development, digital design and other IT-enabled functions. The taxpayer argued that it performed substantive services on its own account as an independent subcontractor, satisfied the conditions for export under section 2(6) of the IGST Act, and fell outside the intermediary definition in section 2(13). It also contested the application of section 13(3)(b), the allegation of unjust enrichment and discrepancies arising from changes in the contracting companies’ names. The Revenue relied on the recruitment-related activities, asserted that Navitas facilitated education services involving foreign institutions and Indian students or advisors, and cited advance rulings supporting intermediary classification. The Tribunal rejected the documentary-discrepancy objection, observing that the company’s reconstitution and change of name were not seriously disputed. Applying Genpact India, Columbia Sportswear, Blackberry India, its earlier decision in Dow Chemical International and Fateh Education Consulting, it held that Navitas supplied services on its own account and did not merely arrange or facilitate another supply. It also applied CBIC Circular No. 159/15/2021-GST, including the exclusion for subcontracting. The Tribunal preferred the High Court judgments over the advance rulings cited by the Revenue and concluded that Navitas’s services qualified as exports under section 2(6). It set aside the impugned appellate orders in the taxpayer’s appeals and directed refund of the specified unutilised ITC with interest, using the wording “six percent and nine percent, respectively,” from the dates listed in paragraph 37. On the Revenue’s appeal, the Tribunal held that section 107(11) permits the first appellate authority to confirm, modify or annul an order but expressly prohibits referring the case back to the adjudicating authority. An order annulling the original decision while directing reconsideration could not circumvent that prohibition. The Revenue’s appeal was allowed to that extent, and the matter was remanded to the first appellate authority under the Tribunal’s separate power under section 113(1). That authority was directed to hear both parties and reconsider the matter on merits in accordance with the Tribunal’s principles, preferably within six weeks of taking cognizance of the order. The Navitas Eduservices Private Limited (formerly known as Learning Information Systems (India) Private Limited) is a private limited company incorporated under the Companies Act, 1956, and is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Navitas Pty Limited, Australia (formerly known as Navitas Limited). Navitas Australia is a global education provider offering pre-university, managed campus, and university pathway programs in collaboration with various Partner Universities across Australia, Canada, the United Kingdom, etc. Navitas Australia’s business involves two primary functions: – (i) delivering and administering educational programs for Partner Universities, and (ii) engaging local recruitment advisors (Advisors) to interact with prospective students, provide course information, and assist with admission and visa procedures. 7. Navitas Australia entered into bilateral services agreements dated 15.05.2018 with Edith Cowan College Pty Ltd with each Partner University. Under these agreements, Navitas Australia is the sole contracting service provider and is expressly contemplated to procure or sub-contract the underlying services through its Indian subsidiary, i.e., the Appellant. The agreement explicitly states that Navitas Australia and its subsidiary (LIS/Appellant) are engaged as independent contractors and not as employees, agents, partners, or joint ventures of the principal (Partner University). Clause 5.1 and 5.2 of the Sample University Agreement expressly negate any agency relationship and prohibit Navitas Australia or the Appellant from incurring obligations on behalf of the Partner University. 8. Pursuant to the University Agreements, Navitas Australia entered into a Master Services Agreement dated 07.12.2016 (Subject Agreement) with the Appellant to sub-contract the supply of marketing, promotion, and business support services. The scope of services under Schedule I of the Subject Agreement includes assisting in managing relationships with the principal’s business partners in India, including recruitment agents for the recruitment of students. The Appellant is remunerated on a cost-plus basis (cost + 16% markup) in convertible foreign exchange (Australian Dollars). Clause 5.1 of the Subject Agreement states that the Appellant is engaged as an independent contractor and not as an employee, agent, partner, or joint venture of the principal. Clause 5.2 restricts the Appellant from incurring any obligation on behalf of Navitas Australia without prior written instruction. 9. Separately, Navitas Australia entered into an Authorised Advisor Agreement with local recruitment advisors/agents in India. Under this agreement, the Advisors are responsible for physically interacting with prospective students, providing information about courses, and assisting with admission procedures. The Advisors are remunerated by Navitas Australia on a commission basis, linked to successful student enrolments. The Appellant is neither a party to this agreement nor does it provide or receive any services to or from the Advisors. 10. As per the Transfer Pricing Report (TP Report) and Form 3CEB filed with the Income Tax authorities, the Appellant provides a bouquet of Marketing Support Services and IT Enabled Services to Navitas Australia. These include: Marketing Support Services: Planning and implementing strategies for increasing enrolment via Advisors, supporting Advisors in achieving enrolment targets, participating in agent-run exhibitions, assisting Advisors in promoting parent universities, giving presentations/counselling to students, vetting student admission applications, assisting in counselling and checking eligibility, verifying documents for visa and admission processes, managing day-to-day affairs of the team, liaising between Navitas Australia and recruitment agents, and hosting university partners on its payroll for marketing and promotion. IT Enabled Services: Web development and digital design, marketing automation, social media campaigns, updating the Study Link portal, quality analysis, and mobile application and content development. 11. It has no contractual privity with the Partner Universities or the Advisors and does not raise invoices on them. For proper appreciation, we quote the relevant provisions which reads as follows: – 2(13)- “intermediary” means a broker, an agent or any other person, by whatever name called, who arranges or facilitates the supply of goods or services or both, or securities, between two or more persons, but does not include a person who supplies such goods or services or both or securities on his own account; Section 2(86) of the CGST Act, 2017, reads as, “Place of supply” means the place of supply as referred to in Chapter V of the “Integrated Goods and Services Tax Act; Chapter V of the IGST Act deals with place of supply of goods and services or both. Section 10 of IGST Act, 2017, provides for “Place of supply” of goods other than supply of goods imported into, or exported from India. Section 11 of the IGST Act, 2017, provides, “place of supply” of goods imported into, or exported from India. Section 12 of the IGST Act, 2017, provides, “place of supply” of services where location of supplier and recipient is in India. Section 13 of the IGST Act, 2017, provides, “place of supply” of services where location of supplier or location of recipient is outside India and for our purpose, sub section (8) of Section 13 is relevant which provides the place of supply of the following services shall be the location of the supplier of services, namely: – clause (b) – intermediary services. [this clause has been omitted by Finance Act, 2026, w.e.f. 23.03.2026.] 29. In the said notification CBIC clarifies that the concept of ‘intermediary’ was borrowed in GST from the Service Tax Regime. The definition of ‘intermediary’ in the Service Tax law as given in Rule 2(f) of Place of Provision of Services Rules, 2012 issued vide notification No. 28/2012-ST, dated 20-6-2012 was as follows: – “intermediary” means a broker, an agent or any other person, by whatever name called, who arranges or facilitates a provision of a service (hereinafter called the ‘main’ service) or a supply of goods, between two or more persons, but does not include a person who provides the main service or supplies the goods on his account;” 33. The CBIC circular further elucidates that upon perusal of the definition of the “intermediary” under IGST Act as well as under Service Tax Law, it is evident that there is broadly no change in the scope of intermediary services in the GST regime vis-à-vis the Service Tax regime, except addition of supply of securities in the definition of intermediary in the GST Law. The concept of intermediary services, requires basic requisites, which are discussed below: – i. Minimum of Three Parties: an intermediary is someone who arranges or facilitates the supply of goods or services or securities between two or more persons. It is, thus, a natural corollary that the arrangement requires a minimum of three parties, two of them transacting in the supply of goods or services or securities (the main supply) and one arranging or facilitating (the ancillary supply) the said main supply. An activity between only two parties can, therefore, NOT be considered as an intermediary service. It essentially “arranges or facilitates” another supply (the “main supply”) between two or more other persons and does not himself provide the main supply. ii. Two distinct supplies: there are two distinct supplies in case of provision of intermediary services; 1. Main supply, between the two principals, which can be a supply of goods or services or securities; 2. Ancillary supply, which is the service of facilitating or arranging the main supply between the two principals. This ancillary supply is supply of intermediary service and is clearly identifiable and distinguished from the main supply. Thus, a person involved in the main supply on principal-to-principal basis to another person cannot be considered as a supplier of intermediary service. iii. The definition of “intermediary” itself provides that intermediary service provider means a broker, an agent or any other person, by whatever name called….”. Thus, the role of intermediary is only supportive. In this case, admittedly, the Appellant is not as a broker or an agent. There is no dispute that he cannot act as broker or as an agent as he doesn’t have authority to bind the principal i.e. Navitas Australia. The only question that remains to be seen is whether he may be ‘any other person by whatever name called’. In this connection we have already taken a view in Dow Chemicals that interpretation of ‘any other person’ has to governed by principles of ‘ejusdem generis’ and it cannot be applied to any person or party who does not have the characteristics of a broker or an agent, having the authority to buying its principal and to act on behalf of the principal. iv. “Intermediary” does not include a person who supplies such goods or services or both or securities on his own account”. (12)x 39.
