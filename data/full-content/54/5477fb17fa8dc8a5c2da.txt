Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); P. Manmatha Raja Vs Secretary to Government of Tamil Nadu & Ors. (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madras High Court, in P. Manmatha Raja Vs Secretary to Government of Tamil Nadu & Ors., examined whether road tax exemption available to persons with disabilities could be extended to vehicles that were not specially designed or adapted for their use. The petitioner, a person with disability, challenged the Regional Transport Officer’s order dated 23 June 2026 rejecting exemption for his Maruti Suzuki Ertiga ZXI Plus Automatic Transmission car. He relied on communications issued by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways dated 13 November 2020 and 3 February 2023, recognising that automatic transmission vehicles may be suitable for certain persons with disabilities. The State maintained that the exemption under G.O. Ms. No. 3352 dated 29 December 1976 was restricted to specially designed or adapted vehicles, although a clarification dated 12 March 2025 extended the benefit to adapted vehicles used for the benefit of persons with disabilities. The High Court examined Section 41(2) of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016, which requires the appropriate Government to develop schemes promoting affordable personal mobility through incentives, concessions, vehicle retrofitting and mobility assistance. It also considered Sections 3 and 20 of the Tamil Nadu Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1974. The Court held that Section 20 empowers the Government to grant exemptions or reductions in motor vehicle tax to specified persons or classes of persons, or in respect of specified vehicles or classes of vehicles. Importantly, the provision does not statutorily restrict exemption to specially designed or adapted vehicles. Consequently, extending road tax exemption to unadapted vehicles registered in the names of persons with disabilities would not require legislative amendment. The existing restriction arose from the terms of the exemption notification rather than the parent statute. At the same time, the Court recognised the possibility of misuse if exemption were granted without safeguards. Vehicles could be registered in the names of persons with disabilities but used by others, or transferred shortly after purchase. Referring to earlier guidelines governing GST concession certificates for persons with orthopaedic disabilities, the Court identified possible safeguards relating to vehicle specifications, disability certification, income tax returns, declarations concerning previous concessions and restrictions on disposal of vehicles. Although the judgment noted that the GST concession appeared to have been withdrawn following reductions in GST rates, it considered those earlier conditions useful as guidance for reviewing the State’s road tax exemption framework. Accordingly, the High Court disposed of the writ petition by directing the Transport Commissioner to consider issuance of a fresh exemption notification under Section 20(1) of the Tamil Nadu Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1974, taking account of the Court’s observations, within three months of receiving the order. Subject to the extension of exemption to unadapted vehicles, the petitioner was permitted to submit a fresh application, which the Regional Transport Officer must decide within one month. The earlier rejection would not obstruct reconsideration, and the petitioner’s temporary registration certificate was extended until disposal of the fresh application. The judgment did not itself grant an unconditional road tax exemption or immediately invalidate the existing exemption notification. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF MADRAS HIGH COURT This writ petition was filed by a person with disability. The petitioner’s request for exemption from payment of road tax was rejected under the order impugned in this writ petition. 2. Learned Counsel for the petitioner contends that the rejection of the request for exemption is contrary to communications issued by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH). Communications dated 13.11.2020 and 03.02.2023 are relied upon in this regard. In particular, it is submitted that said communications record that vehicles with automatic gears are considered suitable for driving by some of the Divyangjan. Several orders and judgments of this Court are cited in support of the claim for exemption. 3. On behalf of the respondents, it is submitted that the exemption is granted pursuant to Notification bearing G.O. Ms. No. 3352, Home (Tr-I) Department, dated 29.12.1976. It is also pointed out that such exemption is restricted to motor vehicles that are specially designed or adapted for the use of persons with disability. By virtue of the clarification dated 12.03.2025, the respondents state that the exemption benefit would also apply if the adapted vehicles are used for the benefit of the person with disability. 4. The principal statute governing the rights of persons with disabilities is the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016. Section 41 of the said statute deals with access to transport. Subsection (2) thereof is as under: “(2) The appropriate Government shall develop schemes programmes to promote the personal mobility of persons with disabilities at affordable cost to provide for,— (a) incentives and concessions; (b) retrofitting of vehicles; and (c) personal mobility assistance.” As can be seen from the above, a statutory obligation is imposed on the appropriate government to develop schemes and programmes to promote the personal mobility of persons with disabilities by inter alia providing for incentives and concessions. The expression “appropriate government” covers the State Government. 5. Taxes are levied on every motor vehicle used or kept for use in the State of Tamil Nadu in terms of Section 3 of the Tamil Nadu Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1974. The said statute provides for exemptions under Section 20 thereof, which reads as under: “Section – 20. Exemptions. The Government may, by notification and subject to such conditions, if any, as the Government, may, specify in such notification, (1) make an exemption, reduction in rate or other modification in regard to the tax payable. (a) by any person or class of persons, or (b) in respect of any motor vehicle or class of motor vehicles or motor vehicles running in any particular area; and (2) cancel or vary such exemption, reduction or other modification. A notification with respect to matters specified in Clause (1) may be issued so as to have retrospective effect from a date not earlier than the 1st April 1974.” From the text of Section 20, it follows that the Government is empowered to exempt or reduce the rate of tax or make any other modification with regard to tax payable by any person or class of persons or in respect of any motor vehicle or class of motor vehicles. Thus, Section 20 does not impose any restrictions with regard to the extension of exemption to a motor vehicle that is not adapted or specially designed for use by a person with disability. In other words, no statutory modification is necessary for purposes of extending the benefit of exemption to persons with disability in respect of unadapted vehicles registered in their name. The said restriction flows solely from the nature of the exemption notification. 6. A possible reason underlying the nature of the exemption notification is the possibility of abuse if exemption were to be granted for vehicles that are not specially designed or adapted for use by persons with disability. Put differently, in such event, it becomes possible for a person without disability to avail of the benefit of exemption after registering the vehicle in the name of a person with disability. It is also possible that the vehicle may be transferred by the person with disability shortly after purchasing the same. Some safeguards may be necessary in this regard. 7. The Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises issued revised guidelines for GST concession certificates for persons with orthopaedic disability. Among the several conditions set out therein are the following conditions: “5. This concession can be availed for following motor vehicles of length not exceeding 4000mm only, namely:- a. Petrol, Liquified petroleum gases (LPG) or compressed natural gas (CNG) driven vehicles of engine capacity not exceeding 1200cc; and b. Diesel driven vehicles of engine capacity not exceeding 1500 cc 6. For availing the concessional duty, an eligible applicant with Orthopedic physical Disability (=>40% disability) shall a. make an application to Department of Heavy Industry (DHI) in the format prescribed at Annexure A. b. submit a Medical certificate duly signed and countersigned in prescribed format placed at Annexure B. c. is also required to submit the copy of Income Tax Return filed for the last three vears. d. is required to submit a Self-declaration stating that he has not availed this concession in the last five years and he will not dispose of the vehicle after availing the GST concession for a period of 5 years from the date of purchase in prescribed format placed at Annexure C.” 8. Although GST concession for persons with disability appears to have been withdrawn subsequently, after GST rates were reduced, the above conditions may be referred to as a guideline by the respondents while reconsidering the exemption notification. 9. For reasons set out above, this writ petition is disposed of on the following terms: (i) The second respondent/Transport Commissioner is directed to consider issuance of a fresh exemption notification under Section 20(1) of the Tamil Nadu Motor Vehicles Taxation Act 1974, after duly taking note of the observations set out in this order. Said fresh notification shall be issued within three months from the date of receipt of a copy of this order. (ii) Subject to the extension of exemption to unadapted vehicles, the petitioner is granted leave to reapply for exemption once the fresh exemption notification is received. (iii) Any such application shall be considered and disposed of by the Regional Transport Officer within one month from the date of receipt thereof. (iv) The impugned order herein shall not stand in the way of such reconsideration. (v) The validity of the temporary registration certificate issued to the petitioner shall stand extended until the fresh application of the petitioner is considered and disposed of.
