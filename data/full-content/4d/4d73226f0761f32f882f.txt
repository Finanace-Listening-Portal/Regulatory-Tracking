Diversification of NBFCs' funding profile is imperative as their reliance on bank borrowings increases, it said.The borrowing profile of NBFCs has undergone a shift in recent years, driven by increasing reliance on banks. The share of debentures, traditionally the largest source of finance, declined steadily from 40 per cent at end-March 2022 to 34.1 per cent at end-March 2026. Borrowings from banks almost reached 40 per cent at end-March 2026, up from 35.3 per cent in 2022. Advt On the liabilities side, growth in borrowings from banks accelerated while funding through market sources such as debentures and commercial papers showed deceleration. Total borrowings grew 17.9 per cent in FY26. Bank borrowings increased 24.6 per cent, while debentures and commercial papers grew 13.2 per cent and 21.8 per cent, respectively.Bank borrowings dominate across most segments, especially microfinance institutions (NBFC-MFIs) and investment and credit companies (NBFC-ICCs), indicating strong dependence on banks by retail and microfinance-oriented NBFCs. At end-March 2026, the share of bank borrowings in total borrowings of ICCs and MFIs was 46.6 per cent and 58.2 per cent, respectively, it said.Other sourcesIn contrast, infrastructure-focused NBFCs such as infrastructure finance companies (NBFC-IFCs) and infrastructure debt funds (NBFC-IDFs) rely more on debentures. At end-March 2026, the share of debentures in total borrowings of IDFs and IFCs was 95.3 per cent and 51.5 per cent, respectively.Among other sources, borrowings from abroad saw a notable increase over recent years, suggesting greater access to global funding markets for NBFCs and benefits from gradual liberalisation of the external commercial borrowing framework. Their share in total borrowings rose from 6.9 per cent in FY22 to 11 per cent in FY26, after reaching 11.3 per cent in FY25. Advt NBFCs have assumed an increasingly important role in credit intermediation in India. Their share in the combined credit extended by scheduled commercial banks, excluding regional rural banks, and NBFCs increased from around 19 per cent at end-March 2019 to 20.5 per cent at end-March 2026.The consolidated balance sheet of NBFCs grew 15.9 per cent in FY26 to Rs 70.12 lakh crore. Loans and advances grew 17.6 per cent to Rs 56.92 lakh crore.At an aggregate level, NBFCs continued to demonstrate improvement in asset quality, as measured by gross and net non-performing asset ratios, and remained well capitalised. The authors conclude that rising dependence of NBFCs on bank borrowings needs to be monitored on an ongoing basis in view of possible systemic concerns, making diversification of their funding profile imperative.
