Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Vijay Sharma Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) Credit Card Expenditure Cannot Be Disallowed When No Deduction Was Claimed and the Source of Payment Was Explained Tribunal Deletes Addition of ₹15,67,409 The Delhi ITAT deleted an addition of ₹15,67,409 arising from transactions on the assessee’s SBI credit card. The assessee explained that his wife had used the card and settled the dues from her own funds. She confirmed the payments directly to the Assessing Officer, and the assessee furnished a source-wise explanation for the full amount. The Tribunal also found that the assessee had claimed no deduction for the disputed expenditure in his return. There was therefore no expense claim to disallow in his assessment. Credit Card Payments Triggered Scrutiny The assessee filed his return for AY 2018-19 on 30 August 2018, declaring total income of ₹10,51,500. The return was selected for scrutiny because of substantial credit card payments. The Assessing Officer identified transactions totalling ₹22,85,144 across three credit cards. These comprised ₹1,12,985 on the HDFC Bank card, ₹15,67,409 on the SBI card and ₹6,04,750 on the Standard Chartered Bank card. The effective dispute before the Tribunal concerned the SBI card alone. The assessment order recorded that the assessee had provided the card details, a note on the activities and particulars of the payments. The assessee stated that his wife, Jyoti Sharma, had used his card in connection with her garment activities and made the payments. He also furnished her written confirmation. Assessing Officer Questioned the Wife’s Business and Payment Capacity The Assessing Officer issued a notice under section 133(6) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 to Jyoti Sharma. She responded directly and furnished her income-tax return acknowledgements for AYs 2016-17, 2017-18 and 2018-19. The Assessing Officer noted that she had filed ITR-1, rather than ITR-3 or ITR-4, and inferred that she was not engaged in business. He consequently doubted her ability to settle the SBI card dues from the stated sources. The Assessing Officer also treated the transactions as personal expenditure unrelated to the assessee’s business. He disallowed the full amount in the assessment completed on 20 April 2021 under section 143(3) read with section 144B. The CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, upheld the addition by order dated 29 April 2026. Source-Wise Explanation Covered the Full Amount The assessee explained that his wife had used the SBI and Standard Chartered Bank cards for her own transactions. For the disputed SBI card amount, he furnished a breakdown identifying ₹6,00,797 paid by cheque out of income and savings, ₹1,62,850 from cash available as on 31 March 2017, and ₹8,03,762 from cash sales of garments and other items. The last amount was stated to have come from total cash sales of ₹9,27,200. The three components reconciled to ₹15,67,409, the full amount in dispute. The Tribunal considered the breakdown alongside the wife’s confirmation and her direct response to the statutory notice. On the record before it, the Tribunal accepted that the assessee had explained the source of the credit card payments. No Deduction Had Been Claimed in the Assessee’s Return The Tribunal examined how the disputed amount had been treated in the assessee’s return. The assessee had categorically stated that the transactions pertained to his wife and clarified that the SBI card expenditure had never been claimed as a deduction. The Assessing Officer had treated the spending as unrelated to the assessee’s business, but that finding did not identify an expense deducted in computing his taxable income. The Tribunal thus had two factual findings before it. The assessee had explained who paid the card dues and from what sources, and his return contained no deduction for the disputed expenditure. It directed deletion of the entire addition of ₹15,67,409 and allowed the appeal. Author’s Comments A credit card statement identifies the cardholder and the transactions charged to the account. Where the cardholder explains that someone else used the card and settled the dues, the relevant evidence includes that person’s confirmation, the payment details and the explanation of funds. Here, the wife’s direct response under section 133(6) and the reconciliation of the full amount gave the Tribunal a basis to examine the explanation despite the Assessing Officer’s inference from the form of return she had filed. A comparable issue concerning credit cards used by a family member and payments made from explained sources has also arisen before the Pune ITAT. The computation issue was equally important. A business expenditure disallowance ordinarily reverses an amount deducted in computing income. In this case, the disputed transactions had never reduced the assessee’s returned income. Reading the return computation alongside the credit card and payment records therefore resolved both issues that had led to the addition. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. This appeal in ITA No. 6831/Del/2026 for A.Y. 2018-19, arises out of the order of the ld. National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [hereinafter referred to as ‘ld. CIT(A), in short] dated 29.04.2026 against the order of the assessment passed u/s 143(3) r.w.s. 144B of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereafter referred to as ‘the Act’) dated 20.04.2021 by the Assessing Officer, Income Tax Department, National e- Assessment Centre, Delhi (hereinafter referred to as the ‘ld. AO). 2. Though the assessee has raised several grounds of appeal before me, the only effective issue to be decided is as to whether the learned CITA was justified in confirming the addition made in the sum of ₹15,67,409 on account of SBI credit card expenses in the facts and circumstances of the instant case. 3. I have heard the rival submissions and perused the materials available on record. The return of income for the assessment year 2018-19 was filed by the assessee on 30-8-2018 declaring total income of ₹10,51,500. The case of the assessee was selected for scrutiny on the ground that huge payments were made for credit card purchases during the year under consideration. On verification of its details, it was found that assessee had made transaction of ₹22,85,144 through credit cards as under:- a) ₹1,12,985 in HDFC Bank credit card b) ₹15,67,409 in SBI credit card c) ₹6,04,750 in Standard Chartered Bank credit card. 4. The learned AO in Page number 2 of the assessment order noted that assessee had complied by submitting all the relevant details of credit cards together with the brief note of the activities and details of payments made on account towards credit cards. It was also submitted by the assessee that amount spent on SBI credit card of ₹15,67,409, the payment for the same was made by his wife Mrs Jyoti Sharma who had separate business and also submitted confirmation from her for making payment on behalf of the assessee. The learned AO on verification of the same found that expenses of ₹15,67,409 spent in SBI credit card were personal in nature and not related to the business of the assessee. The learned AO noted that assessee’s wife Mrs Jyoti Sharma is a women entrepreneur during doing freelance transactions of garments and had also used assessee’s credit card for purchases. The notice under section 133(6) of the Act was issued to Mrs Jyoti Sharma which was duly responded by her directly before the learned AO by furnishing the income tax returns for assessment years 2016-17, 2017-18 and 2018-19. The learned AO noted from the ITR acknowledgement that Mrs Jyoti Sharma had filed the returns in Form No. 1 and not in Form No. 3 or 4 which goes to prove that she is not engaged in any type of business. Accordingly, the learned AO disbelieved the availability of sources for making payments to SBI credit card in the total sum of ₹15,67,409 by Mrs Jyoti Sharma on behalf of the assessee and disallowed the same in the assessment of the assessee as not meant for the purpose of business. 5. The assessee clarified that the SBI credit card and Standard Chartered Bank credit card were used by his wife Mrs Jyoti Sharma for her business purposes and that the amount of ₹15,67,409 spent in SBI credit card pertains to the transactions carried out by his wife and the same does not pertain to the assessee at all. The assessee submitted the complete details of sources for making payments of ₹15,67,409 by his wife Mrs Jyoti Sharma as under:- a) Payments made by cheque by Mrs Jyoti Sharma out of her income and savings –₹6,00,797 b) Payments made by Mrs Jyoti Sharma out of available cash balance as on 31/3/2017 – ₹1,62,850 c) Payments made by Mrs Jyoti Sharma out of cash sales of ₹9,27,200 on sale of garments and other items – ₹8,03,762 Total payments – ₹15,67,409 6. The aforesaid explanation given by the assessee was not appreciated by the learned CITA and the learned CITA upheld the action of the learned AO. 7. At the outset, I find that assessee had furnished the confirmation from his wife Mrs Jyoti Sharma stating the payments for SBI credit cards were made by her from her disclosed sources of income. She has also responded to notice issued in the section 133(6) of the Act directly before the learned AO by furnishing the requisite details together with the ITR acknowledgements thereon for 3 assessment years. I find that assessee had also furnished the entire details of payments made by Mrs Jyoti Sharma towards SBI credit card in the total sum of ₹15,67,409. Further I find that assessee had categorically stated that the amounts spent in SBI credit card does not pertain to the assessee at all and that those transactions relate to his wife’s business. Further it was duly clarified that no deduction has been claimed by the assessee as expenditure in his return of income in the sum of ₹15,67,409 being the amount spent on SBI credit card. Hence there is no question of making any disallowance of the said expenditure in the assessment of the assessee when there was no claim per se made by the assessee in the return. The assessee on his part had duly explained the source of making payment to SBI credit card in the total sum of ₹15,67,409 by his wife Mrs Jyoti Sharma. Hence the addition made by the learned AO and confirmed by the learned CITA deserve to be deleted as it would have no legs to stand in the eyes of law in view of the aforesaid facts. The grounds raised by the assessee are allowed. 8. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed.
