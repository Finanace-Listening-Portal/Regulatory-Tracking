Vs Arvind Jagannath Bagul (NCLAT Delhi) Summary: The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, Principal Bench, New Delhi, set aside the NCLT Mumbai’s order dated 16 December 2025 directing a share purchase in proceedings under Sections 241–242 of the Companies Act. The dispute concerned Sanjit Instruments Private Limited, its shareholders Sunil Jagannath Bagul and Megha Sunil Bagul, and promoter-director Arvind Jagannath Bagul, who held 31.79% of the equity. The appellants alleged that the respondent and his son had supported a competing business, diverted customers and misused the company’s resources, causing an alleged loss of approximately Rs.50 lakh. Following changes to the respondent’s management role and his removal, he instituted the company petition seeking, among other reliefs, restoration as executive director, recovery of allegedly diverted monies and division of the business between shareholder groups. During the proceedings on 16 September 2019, the respondent expressed willingness to exit at a fair valuation, and the NCLT appointed a valuer. The report submitted on 16 June 2021 valued the company’s equity at Rs.3,82,68,000 as on 31 March 2018. It was subsequently resubmitted on 10 February 2025. The appellants did not file objections to that report. The respondent therefore argued that their participation in the valuation process, payment of the valuer’s fees and failure to object precluded them from challenging the valuation or resisting the buy-out mechanism. The appellants contended that no definite finding of oppression or mismanagement had been recorded, that Section 242(2)(b) could not operate independently of the statutory prerequisites, and that a valuation based on the financial position in March 2018 could not justify a purchase direction made in December 2025. The NCLAT held that relief under Section 242(2) depends on the requisite findings and opinion under Sections 241 and 242(1). The NCLT could not dispense with those findings merely because the jurisdiction was preventive, remedial or equitable, or because the parties had participated in valuation. Referring to Upper India Steel Manufacturing and Engineering Co. Ltd. Vs Gurlal Singh Grewal & Ors., it reiterated that a direction to sell or purchase shares cannot bypass the statutory foundation. It also found that the impugned order lacked findings on matters including irreconcilability, breakdown of trust, the parties’ ability to run the company and deadlock. The Tribunal further held that non-filing of objections did not relieve the NCLT of its duty to scrutinise the valuation report independently. Resubmission in February 2025 did not convert the June 2021 report, based on March 2018 figures, into a fresh valuation. The NCLAT found that the NCLT had incorrectly treated the report as one dated 10 February 2025. Drawing on the Bombay High Court decisions in Nafan B.V. Vs Saf Yeast Company Pvt. Ltd. and Prudence Maynard and Anr. Vs Mundhra Container Freight Station Pvt. Ltd., it emphasised valuation reflecting the position at the time of the purchase direction or reasonably close to it. The order was quashed, and the matter was remitted to the NCLT to obtain a fresh valuation report and decide the petition afresh on its merits, applying the interplay between Sections 241 and 242. We are of the view that, under Section 241 and 242 of the Companies Act, which contemplates to provide an avenue for filing of an application before the Tribunal, it caste a mandatory duty, as has been assigned under its Sub-Section (1), its that, only upon the applicant i.e. the respondent herein who has to establish a specified act of misconduct and oppression as contemplated or perceived of under Sub-Section (1) (a) and (b) and before initiation of proceedings under Sub-Section (1), there has had to be a satisfaction and a determination based on records pertaining to existence of an act of oppression and mismanagement. The argument of the learned Counsel for the respondent has been opposed by the appellant apart from it on perusal of the pleading contending thereof that if the observation and finding recorded by the learned Tribunal are taken into consideration, in fact, there is no definite opinion, which has been drawn based upon appreciation of evidence, to logically conclude that, there was an establishment of an act of oppression and mismanagement. For the aforesaid purpose, the learned Counsel before the Tribunal has taken us to the judgment, which records a description of parties, submission of petitioner, submission of respondent and lastly the finding. 22. The finding recorded by the Ld. Tribunal, in the order under challenge before us. The finding commences from para – 5. In para – 5 and 6, there is no whisper nor any finding, which has been recorded by the learned Tribunal after considering the evidence on record for bringing the case of the respondent/petitioner to be at all falling within the ambit of Section 241 of the Companies Act, as there is not even a single finding recorded by the learned Tribunal about any act of oppression and mismanagement. If we further proceed to scrutinize the judgment, immediately where the judgement after referring to the basic facts, considers the implication of Section 242 of the Companies Act. The learned Tribunal, while issuing the directions as given in para – 12 of the impugned Judgement, has made a peculiar observation contending that since the provision contained under Sections 241 and 242 are preventive and remedial in nature, the Tribunal has to form an opinion only in order to bring it within a just and equitable act. The learned Tribunal has recorded as under:- “8. The Hon’ble Supreme Court has consistently held that proceedings under Sections 241–242 are preventive and remedial in nature, and not punitive. Once the Tribunal forms an opinion that facts justify winding up on “Just and equitable” grounds, but winding up would unfairly prejudice shareholders, it is empowered to grant alternative reliefs including purchase of shares. The Tribunal is not bound to record a detailed finding of oppression if the conduct of parties and circumstances warrant equitable relief under Section 242. Consent to valuation and buy-out is a strong indicator that mutual trust has irretrievably broken down, justifying separation of shareholding principle consistently followed in India).” 23. The inference drawn by the Ld. Tribunal, as extracted herein above, and the findings recorded by the Tribunal, to the effect that the Tribunal is not bound to record any reasons, for any act of oppression and mismanagement which could be established only after assimilation of facts, is not necessary, if the circumstances warrant attraction of Section 242, it could still be done under the concept of equitable relief. This finding recorded by the learned Tribunal observing thereof that if the situation contemplates attraction of Section 242 of the Companies Act, no finding of oppression and mismanagement is required to be recorded, is not acceptable by us for the reason, being that if provisions contained under Section 242 itself is taken into consideration and particularly, that as contained under Sub-Section (1) which is extracted hereunder: – “242 – Powers of Tribunal. (1) If, on any application made under section 241, the Tribunal is of the opinion— (a) that the company‘s affairs have been or are being conducted in a manner prejudicial or oppressive to any member or members or prejudicial to public interest or in a manner prejudicial to the interests of the company; and (b) that to wind up the company would unfairly prejudice such member or members, but that otherwise the facts would justify the making of a winding-up order on the ground that it was just and equitable that the company should be wound up, the Tribunal may, with a view to bringing to an end the matters complained of, make such order as it thinks fit. (2) Without prejudice to the generality of powers under Sub-Section (1), an order under that sub-section may provide:- (a) the regulation of conduct of affairs of the company in future; (b) the purchase of shares or interests of any members of the company by other members thereof or by the company; (c) in the case of a purchase of its shares by the company as aforesaid, the consequent reduction of its share capital; (d) restrictions on the transfer or allotment of the shares of the company; (e) the termination, setting aside or modification, of any agreement, howsoever arrived at, between the company and the managing director, any other director or manager, upon such terms and conditions as may, in the opinion of the Tribunal, be just and equitable in the circumstances of the case; (f) the termination, setting aside or modification of any agreement between the company and any person other than those referred to in clause (e): Provided that no such agreement shall be terminated, set aside or modified except after due notice and after obtaining the consent of the party concerned; (g) the setting aside of any transfer, delivery of goods, payment, execution or other act relating to property made or done by or against the company within three months before the date of the application under this section, which would, if made or done by or against an individual, be deemed in his insolvency to be a fraudulent preference; (h) removal of the managing director, manager or any of the directors of the company; (i) recovery of undue gains made by any managing director, manager or director during the period of his appointment as such and the manner of utilisation of the recovery including transfer to Investor Education and Protection Fund or repayment to identifiable victims; (j) the manner in which the managing director or manager of the company may be appointed subsequent to an order removing the existing managing director or manager of the company made under clause (h); (k) appointment of such number of persons as directors, who may be required by the Tribunal to report to the Tribunal on such matters as the Tribunal may direct; (l) imposition of costs as may be deemed fit by the Tribunal; (m) any other matter for which, in the opinion of the Tribunal, it is just and equitable that provision should be made.” 24. It observes that its only upon an application preferred under Section 241 when the conclusion is drawn, there has had to be a definite opinion to be recorded by the learned Tribunal about the affairs of the company, which is mandated under the provision, is analysis of facts whether, the conduct of the company, the prejudicial acts of the company and its members, the public interest involved therein and henceforth are established, there has to be determination by the Tribunal. Meaning thereby, Section 242(1), which precedes the provisions contained under Section 242(2), becomes mandatory to be satisfied. Before the Tribunal barges upon to attract adoption of Section 242 of the Companies Act, the Tribunal will have to arrive at a conclusive finding about the act to be brought to be falling within the ambit of Section 241 first and its after that conclusion and findings recorded under “that Sub-Section” i.e. Section 241(1) only, and its upon the satisfaction being recorded about the act complained of to be falling under Section 241, then only Section 242 could be attracted thus the finding under Section 241(1) would be the basis and attract 242(1) of the act. Hence, the inference drawn by recording the fact in para – 8 of the judgement as extracted above, will itself not take away the responsibility of the learned Tribunal, to escape to record the finding of oppression and mismanagement because irrespective of attraction of Section 242, a prior recording of satisfaction and finding of Section 241 is mandatory in the light of the provisions contained under Sub-Section (1) of Section 242 to be read with Sub-Section (2) of Section 242 of the Act. 25. This logic is because of the reason that the alternative actions under Section 242(1), which could be taken by the Tribunal, are prescribed under Sub-Section (2) of Section 242, are only upon the conclusion drawn based upon the finding recorded under Sub-Section (1) of Section 242 meaning thereby the attraction of Section 242(2) is not exclusive in nature, but rather its applicability is based upon the reasoning and rationale recorded under Sub-Section (1) of Section 242 of the Act, which in turn is based upon the finding which were required to be recorded based on material under Section 241 of the Companies Act. Thus, if we take into consideration the provision of Section 242(2) itself, the same cannot be directly attracted to be made applicable in the absence of their being any prior opinion drawn qua the actions falling within the ambit of Section 241 of the Act, and recording its satisfaction and drawing of opinion by the Ld. Tribunal, under sub-section (1) of Section 242 of the act. 26. If we go through the judgement in its entirety, there is no finding of satisfaction recorded pertaining to parameters required to be established under Section 241 and there is not even a satisfaction recorder further with regards to satisfying the conditions of Sub-Section (1) of Section 242 because it is thereafter only Sub-Section (2) of Section 242 could be attracted. Thus, the manner in which the Tribunal has proceeded, is judicially not sustainable for the reason being that if Sub-Section (2) of Section 242 of the Companies Act, is taken into consideration, it had got close nexus and connectivity with Sub-Section (1) of Section 242, which in turn has got a connectivity and an interplay with Section 241 of Companies Act, and if we see the reverse process then primarily, we are of the view that it is mandatory that there has to be a prior satisfaction of the condition about recording of the finding on the act of oppression and mismanagement for bringing it to be falling under Section 241, its then only the tribunal will have to draw an opinion based on the finding as contemplated under Section 242(1) and upon that “opinion only”, then the provisions of Sub -Section (2) of Section 242 could be applied. Otherwise, there cannot be a reverse process of applying Sub-Section (2) of Section 242 in the absence of their being any prior opinion under Sub-Section (1) of Section 242 and Section 241 and, more particularly, in the absence of their being any finding, justifying the attraction of 242(2)(b) of the Companies Act, hence applying the same would be bad in the eyes of law and cannot be sustained. 27. In the Judgement passed by this Tribunal, in the matter of “Upper India Steel Manufacturing and Engineering Co. Ltd. vs Gurlal Singh Grewal & Ors. [CA (AT) No.150 of 2017], it is observed as under: – “31. From bare perusal of Section 242 of the Companies Act, 2013, it is clear that the Tribunal if it comes to a definite conclusion that the Company’s affairs have been or are being conducted in a manner prejudicial or oppressive to any member or members or prejudicial to public interest or in a manner prejudicial to the interests of the company and that to wind up the company would unfairly prejudice such member or members, then only with a view to bring to an end the matters complained of, may make such order as it thinks fit including the order in terms of sub-section (2) of Section 242. 35. Even if it is accepted that the parties have agreed to sell out their shares, in absence of any power vested with Tribunal, after its specific finding there was no ‘Oppression and Mismanagement’, the Tribunal had no jurisdiction to direct any party to sell or buyout any share. Further finding about, the date of valuation/ buyout as the date closest to the filing of the petition i.e. 31st March, 2007 being perverse and contrary to the offer made by parties in the year 2008, and the order dated 25th February 2009, passed by Tribunal such order cannot be upheld. 36. For the reasons aforesaid, while we uphold the findings of the Tribunal in so far as it relates to failure of petitioners to prove ‘Oppression and Mismanagement’, the last part of the order and direction to the extent of sale of shares, date of valuation/ buyout and the order regarding payment of interest and the findings that the minority group have systematically whittled Company Appeals (AT) Nos. 150 and 189 of 2017 due to non-payment of dividend, and reduction in the number of directors being perverse, such portion of the impugned order are set aside.” 28. It had been argued by the learned Counsel for the respondent/petitioner that the report of the valuer since was not opposed by the appellant, it was bound to be accepted. Mere non-filing of an objection cannot be a reason to accept the report irrespective of its contents and its analysis by an adjudicating authority ceased with the matter. We are of the view that since the valuer’s report is determining the major controversy of purchase of shares by the petitioners, then the genuineness and sanctity of the report was all the more required to be judicially scrutinized by the learned Tribunal itself on its own merits, irrespective of whether the same has been objected or not. And the aforesaid reason is backed by that the reciprocal buying and selling mechanism itself is a separate statutory remedy provided under Section 242(2)(b) and if that is to be based upon the valuation report, then the valuation report in itself is required to be tested on its anvil of its judiciousness pertaining to the valuation assigned by the valuer. 29. In the instant case, in fact, the inclination of the Ld. Tribunal right from the inception of the proceedings when the Tribunal proceeds to record its finding, the Tribunal itself was found to be bent upon to adopt an alternative recourse provided under Section 242, rather than coming to its conclusion after recording the finding of oppression and mismanagement. In the absence of their being any finding, (i) on oppression and mismanagement, (ii) that the difference between the parties were irreconcilable, (iii) in the absence of binding on irretrievable break down of trust and confidence, and (iv) in the absence of their being any finding about the capability of either of the sides of running the company, (iv) in the absence of there being any finding upon the deadlock and its efforts to resolve, and then to arrive at a conclusion without analysis and then taking positive steps was mandatorily required to have been taken by the learned Tribunal before passing of an Order directing the buying out of shares, by making an effort to resolve the dispute, rather than carving out an exception of buying out and that too by an irrational applicability of Section 242(2)(b) of the Companies Act. 30. We have been consistently observing in the preceding paragraph that the valuer’s report was called by the learned Tribunal, by passing an Order on 16.09.2019 and the report thereof too was submitted also, before the learned Tribunal on 16.06.2021. However, it was merely a resubmission of the same valuer’s report that was made before the Tribunal on 10.02.2025, which was, in fact, the valuation of 31.03.2018 as thereafter, no fresh valuation report was ever called for. The fact remains that, there happens to be one valuation report on record by Mr. V. S. Nagulapati Rao, which was submitted on 16.06.2021, it may be that the report was resubmitted before the learned Tribunal on 10.02.2025, but the fact remains that it was, in fact, the only valuation of 31.03.2018 that has been referred as to be the report of 10.02.2025. If we go through the finding recorded by the learned Tribunal invariably whenever the reference has been made with regards to the report, the same has been mentioned by the learned Tribunal as to be that of 10.02.2025, though as a matter of fact, there is no such report of 10.02.2025 on record, rather it was the report of 16.06.2021, which was only resubmitted on 10.02.2025 pertaining to the valuation as it existed on 31.03.2018. Thus, observing thereof that the report happens to be of 10.02.2025 itself shows that the Tribunal has not correctly appreciated the evidence and documents on record while recording its finding in the impugned Order. 31. What we could also see that, even let us presume that there happens to be a report of 16.06.2021 placed on the record of the Tribunal on 10.02.2025, what sanctity did the report of 16.06.2021 had as regards to portraying the correct valuation of the shareholding on the date of rendering of the Judgement, that would have been one of the vital issues, that was required to be considered, particularly, when the Judgement aimed at observing that respondent Nos.2 and 3 would, being the majority shareholders, shall purchase the shares held by the petitioner. If that be the situation, the actual transaction of purchase of shares under buy back situation was upon the orders of Tribunal, it should have been on the basis of the actual face value, when the direction is issued under Section 242(2)(b) of the Companies Act. Issuing a direction of buying back of the shares on the basis of valuation of 16.06.2021 report, as held by the petitioner/respondent herein has had to be on the basis of the current valuation of the shares as on the date of the Judgement or at least should have a close proximity to the valuation with the date of determination. It will not be safe and justified too, to rely upon the valuation report, which projects the financial position and the valuation as conducted on 31.03.2018, and that too particularly when the learned Tribunal has read the report and dealt with it as to be that of 10.02.2025. 32. The process of buying is more or less akin to any business deal or transaction, it has had to be upon consideration of present economic condition of the company, and actual prevailing current market rate of the share, it would be unfair, its an equitable jurisdiction of Section 241 and 242 of Companies Act, to direct buying out of share on the date of judgement, based on the earlier valuation, which may as of now project the correct value. The correct value has to commensurate to date on which the process has to take or at least in close proximity. That has been laid by Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the matter of “Nafan B.V. vs Saf Yeast Company Pvt. Ltd.” [(2016) 1 Comp LJ36 (Bom)] as under: “147. For the purpose of valuation of shares, it will be most appropriate that today’s date is taken as a reference. The order could thus be in two steps. Part I will be the forward competitive bid for which Muthu Group will have to withdraw the civil suit they have filed and not take any steps based on MOU henceforth and convey its acceptance within a particular period. If such willingness is not shown in the stipulated period, then Part II of the order regarding buy-out will come into effect.” It has also been laid by the same High court in the matter of “Prudence Maynard and Anr. Vs. Mundhra Container Freight Station Pvt. Ltd., [(2017) 202 Comp Cas 635] as under: – “66. Learned counsel for the appellant placed reliance on the judgment of this Court in the case of Nafan B.V. Vs. Saf Yeast Company (P) Ltd. & Ors., (2016) 1 Comp LJ 36 (Bom) and in particular paragraph 147 in support of her submission that for the purpose of the valuation of the shares, it would be most appropriate to consider the date on which the judgment is delivered as a reference.” 33. Hence, there happens to be an absolute perversity in the judgement so far it relates to the reading of the evidence on record. Hence, based on the aforesaid, we are of a confirmed opinion that in the light of the interplay of Sections 241 and 242 of the Companies Act, in its totality, a finding of oppression and mismanagement has to be preceded with before resorting to any steps under Section 242(2) of the Companies Act. 34. If the Tribunal was issuing the directions for the purchase of shares by the petitioner/respondent herein, it has had to be on the basis of the current value of the shares, and logically not on the basis of the stale valuation which was done as back as on 31.03.2018 i.e. about 8 years back, which may as to now with passage of time reflect the true value. Since the judgement itself happens to be perverse without a rational finding on establishment of any act of oppression and mismanagement. The same cannot be sustained, is accordingly hereby quashed the order of 16.12.2025 as rendered in CP No. 1105(CA)/(MB)2018. The matter is remitted back to the learned Tribunal to call for a fresh valuation report and then to proceed to decide the matter on its own merits afresh. Considering the implications of interplay of Section 242 along with 241, as to under what circumstances it could be made to be attracted to be applied.
