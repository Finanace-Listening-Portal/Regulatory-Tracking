Summary: Litecoin (LTC) is a proof-of-work cryptocurrency launched in 2011, with a protocol supply ceiling of 84 million units, a target block interval of about 2.5 minutes and a block-subsidy halving after every 840,000 blocks. Litecoin is not a proof-of-stake token and does not pay protocol staking rewards. Its Mimblewimble Extension Blocks (MWEB) functionality introduces optional confidentiality features but does not eliminate exchange KYC, transaction tracing or Indian legal obligations. For tax year 2026-27, LTC ordinarily falls within the virtual digital asset (VDA) definition under the Income-tax Act, 2025. Section 194, Table Sl. No. 4 generally taxes income from transfer of VDAs at 30%, plus applicable surcharge and cess, allowing cost of acquisition but disallowing ordinary expenses and VDA loss set-off/carry-forward. Section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 8(vi) provides the 1% TDS architecture on qualifying VDA transfer consideration, with thresholds and reporting obligations. FIU-IND registration and PMLA duties attach to covered service providers rather than automatically to every personal wallet holder. GST treatment of exchange or other services, FEMA implications of cross-border transfers, accounting classification, and evidence of transaction value require independent fact-specific review. The position below is verified against official Litecoin, CBDT, FIU-IND and RBI material as at 14 October 2026. Key Takeaways Show all 19 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); What Litecoin Is and What It Is Not Litecoin is a peer-to-peer blockchain network with its own native unit, LTC. Its principal design is transfer and settlement of units over a distributed ledger, with blocks produced by proof-of-work miners. The network is not issued by an Indian bank, the Reserve Bank of India or a statutory deposit-taking institution. An LTC balance is neither a bank deposit nor a claim to repayment at a fixed rupee value. The fact that wallets and exchanges can display LTC in rupees does not turn it into Indian legal tender. The Litecoin project’s published learning material identifies a maximum supply of 84 million LTC, a target block interval of approximately two-and-a-half minutes, and a halving schedule every 840,000 blocks. Miners receive newly issued coins under the block-subsidy schedule as well as transaction fees. The protocol is based on Scrypt proof-of-work. Unlike proof-of-stake chains, LTC does not confer native validator staking rights merely by holding tokens. MWEB and transaction confidentiality Mimblewimble Extension Blocks, or MWEB, are an optional extension-block design used to support confidential transfer features. A holder must distinguish the ordinary transparent chain from transfers involving the extension mechanism. Confidentiality is not equivalent to a lawful exemption from tax reporting or KYC. It also does not guarantee that a regulated service provider will accept deposits or withdrawals involving every type of wallet or transfer route. The exchange’s published operational rules and the taxpayer’s records matter. Section 194, Table Sl. No. 4, carries forward the special VDA tax architecture previously contained in section 115BBH of the 1961 Act. A taxable gain on transfer of LTC is ordinarily charged at 30% plus applicable surcharge and 4% health and education cess. The law generally allows only the cost of acquisition, if any, against transfer consideration; it does not permit the normal range of brokerage, financing, electricity or other deductions against such VDA transfer income. The legal classification of a particular receipt as transfer income or a separate business receipt must be examined rather than assumed. A disposal can arise when LTC is sold for INR, exchanged for another crypto-asset or used as consideration for goods or services, subject to the legal character of the transaction. A transfer need not produce a bank credit. A swap of LTC for another VDA may have two valuations and potentially withholding issues, because the transaction is not economically the same as continuing to hold the original coin. Merely transferring the same beneficially owned LTC between self-controlled wallets is ordinarily different from a disposal to another person; documentary evidence of ownership and wallet addresses is essential. Why losses cannot simply be netted The special regime prohibits set-off of loss from transfer of a VDA against income computed under other provisions and does not permit that VDA loss to be carried forward to succeeding tax years. Accordingly, a ledger showing a ₹90,000 gain on one LTC sale and a ₹70,000 loss on another cannot simply declare ₹20,000 as the taxable VDA gain. Each disposal needs its own computation; the positive transfer income is reported according to the return schema, while the loss restriction is applied. This can produce income tax even when the overall trading account shows a small profit or an economic loss. No. 8(vi), deals with deduction at source on consideration for transfer of a VDA to a resident. The general rate is 1% of consideration, not 1% of profit. The familiar thresholds carried forward from the earlier VDA withholding framework are ₹50,000 for a specified individual/HUF category and ₹10,000 for other relevant cases, subject to the statutory definition and aggregation. A taxpayer must determine who is responsible for deduction in an exchange-facilitated, peer-to-peer, or consideration-in-kind transaction; a platform’s marketing statement is not a substitute for the law. The Income Tax Department confirms that Form 141 consolidates several PAN-based challan-cum-statement transactions under the new Act. Schedule D addresses qualifying VDA transfers by individual/HUF deductors; other deductors use the applicable TAN-based reporting architecture. Where consideration is wholly or partly in kind and cash is insufficient for withholding, statutory payment-before-release safeguards require special attention. The tax withheld is creditable against final liability where correctly reported; it is not the final 30% tax on gain. For the reporting transition, see TaxGuru’s verified discussion of Form 141 under the Income-tax Act, 2025; the Income Tax Department’s Form 141 user guidance is the controlling operational reference. Mining Receipts, Rewards and GST Are Separate Questions LTC is mined rather than staked. Receipt of LTC from mining, payment for mining services, pooled mining proceeds, and a subsequent sale of mined LTC are not necessarily one tax event. The income-tax head, cost basis and timing require an examination of the mining arrangement and facts. The 30% VDA-transfer provision does not by itself answer every question about the initial receipt or valuation of a mining reward. Mining expenses cannot automatically be netted against a later VDA transfer gain merely because the miner incurred electricity or equipment costs. For GST, distinguish the asset transfer from separately identifiable supplies: exchange facilitation, brokerage, custodial services, software access, advisory services, mining services or other contractual consideration. Registration, place of supply, export-of-service status, value and rate depend on the precise supply and notifications. It would be incorrect to assert that every personal LTC sale is necessarily subject to GST at a universal rate or that all crypto-related services are outside GST. Businesses must maintain invoices and input-tax-credit documentation where the statutory conditions are satisfied. PMLA, FIU-IND and Customer Due Diligence The Ministry of Finance notification of 7 March 2023 brought specified VDA-related business activities within the PMLA reporting-entity framework. FIU-IND’s 4 July 2023 registration circular identifies fiat-to-VDA exchange, VDA-to-VDA exchange, transfer, custody/administration and financial services linked to an issuer’s offer and sale when carried on for or on behalf of another person in the course of business. Such service providers can be required to register with FIU-IND, appoint compliance personnel, conduct KYC and beneficial-owner due diligence, preserve records and report suspicious transactions. A person who simply owns LTC in a private wallet is not automatically a registered VDA service provider. Conversely, describing a business as “decentralised” or operating outside India does not necessarily avoid the applicability assessment when Indian customers are served. For compliance, distinguish wallet self-custody, platform custody, OTC facilitation, P2P matching and professional intermediation. MWEB use does not displace lawful requests for source-of-funds or beneficial-ownership evidence. FEMA, Cross-border Platforms and Payment Risks FEMA legality is a separate question from income-tax liability. Paying tax on an LTC gain does not validate an otherwise impermissible foreign-exchange transaction. RBI guidance on the Liberalised Remittance Scheme expressly excludes remittances for trading in foreign exchange abroad and margin/margin calls to overseas exchanges. Those forex restrictions should not be mechanically relabelled as a blanket statutory classification of every crypto transfer; the actual fiat payment route, resident status, overseas asset, transaction purpose and applicable FEMA regulations must be analysed. Foreign exchanges, overseas bank accounts and custody arrangements create documentation risks: the location of the platform is not conclusive evidence of the situs or beneficial ownership of an asset. Resident and ordinarily resident individuals should separately examine Schedule FA and foreign-source income disclosure requirements for reportable overseas accounts or interests. A self-custodied LTC wallet is not automatically a foreign bank account, but a foreign custodial or financial account may create reporting obligations. NRIs must apply the correct residential-status tests rather than using resident reporting rules without review. Accounting and Audit Trail Indian accounting classification depends on the reporting entity and actual business model. Where LTC is held for sale in the ordinary course of business, inventory principles under AS 2/Ind AS 2 may be relevant; otherwise an intangible-asset analysis under AS 26/Ind AS 38 may be considered. Neither treatment should be imposed without examining control, measurement basis, recognition conditions, accounting-policy consistency and disclosures. Fair-value claims need to be checked against the applicable standard rather than presumed from exchange quotations. An auditable register should capture trade identifier, timestamp and time zone, LTC quantity, INR-equivalent consideration, fee, wallet and exchange addresses, beneficial owner, source of funds, cost lot, transaction nature, counterparty evidence, TDS certificate/challan and reconciliation to bank statements. Private-key loss, smart-contract or custodian failure and price volatility should be evaluated for impairment, provisions or disclosures as relevant. Businesses should not treat exchange dashboard balances as independently verified assets without confirmations and reconciliation. Common Errors and Control Checklist The principal errors are: using the wrong law year; calculating 1% TDS on profit rather than consideration; offsetting a VDA loss against another gain; treating an internal wallet movement as a sale without examining beneficial ownership; treating mining proceeds as protocol staking; assuming an MWEB transfer is tax-invisible; treating FIU registration of a platform as RBI authorisation of an investment; and reporting only INR withdrawals instead of all taxable transfers. Each error can lead to inaccurate returns or an incomplete audit trail. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Does Litecoin pay staking rewards? No. Litecoin uses proof-of-work mining. A third-party platform may market a lending or yield product, but that is not native Litecoin protocol staking. 2. What is the maximum LTC supply? Official Litecoin material states a protocol ceiling of 84 million LTC and a target block interval of approximately 2.5 minutes. 3. What is the LTC transfer tax rate in India for 2026-27? Income from transfer of a qualifying VDA is generally subject to the special 30% basic rate under section 194, Table Sl. No. 4 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, plus applicable surcharge and cess. 4. Is 1% TDS calculated on LTC gain? No. Section 393(1) withholding is generally calculated on qualifying transfer consideration, subject to thresholds and transaction-specific responsibility. 5. Can a loss on one LTC trade offset a gain on another? The special VDA loss set-off restriction prevents simply netting losses against other taxable VDA transfer income. Each transfer must be computed and reported under the statutory rules. 6. Must every private LTC holder register with FIU-IND? No. FIU registration concerns specified VDA service-provider activities carried on as business for or on behalf of others; personal holding alone does not establish that status. 7. Does MWEB make a Litecoin transfer exempt from reporting? No. Optional confidentiality features do not override applicable tax, KYC, anti-money-laundering or accounting duties. Key Takeaways Litecoin is a proof-of-work cryptocurrency with a stated 84-million-unit cap; it does not provide native staking. MWEB is an optional technical feature, not a legal exemption. For tax year 2026-27, section 194 Table Sl. No. 4 generally imposes 30% basic tax on VDA transfer income. One-percent TDS on qualifying transfer consideration is governed by section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 8(vi). VDA transfer losses cannot be routinely netted or carried forward under the special regime. FIU-IND/PMLA obligations attach to covered VDA service providers; GST and FEMA require transaction-specific analysis. Maintain a transaction-level, wallet-level and INR-valued audit trail.
