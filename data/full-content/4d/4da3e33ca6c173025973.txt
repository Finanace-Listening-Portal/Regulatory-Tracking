Bajaj Finance, Poonawalla Fincorp, Sundaram Finance, Muthoot Capital Services and Jio Credit were among the largest borrowers during the month. Overall corporate bond fundraising through private placements declined nearly 37% month on month to Rs 80,500 crore, although it remained 19% higher year on year.NBFCs raised Rs 24,457 crore through private placements in October, compared with Rs 30,034 crore in September. Bajaj Finance, Poonawalla Fincorp, Sundaram Finance, Muthoot Capital Services and Jio Credit were among the largest borrowers during the month. Advt The slowdown followed a surge in corporate bond issuances in September, when companies raised Rs 1.27 lakh crore through 367 issuances. Borrowers had stepped up fundraising to secure funds at relatively lower rates ahead of the Reserve Bank of India's 25-basis-point repo rate hike on October 7. In October, companies raised Rs 80,500 crore through 265 issuances, according to data compiled by Informist.The funding environment became more challenging as government and corporate bond yields rose amid uncertainty over the prolonged West Asia conflict, elevated crude oil prices and expectations of further monetary tightening. The yield on the 10-year benchmark 6.94%, 2036 government bond rose to 7.4983% on Tuesday, its highest level since October 2022. Brent crude prices climbed above $115 a barrel and approached $117, adding to inflation and interest rate concerns.Corporate bond yields also moved higher. The yield on three-year National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development bonds rose to 8.30-8.33% at the end of October from 7.88-7.90% at the end of September. The five-year yield increased to 8.23-8.27% from 7.85-7.87%, while the 10-year yield rose to 8.13-8.15% from 7.90-7.92%.Volatility made it difficult for issuers to time their borrowings. Temporary declines in yields were often followed by increases in response to global developments, discouraging companies from announcing issuance plans. Investors also remained selective, particularly towards longer-tenor corporate bonds, amid uncertainty over the RBI's policy trajectory, inflation and liquidity.NBFCs face higher funding costs Advt The pressure on bond yields comes at a time when NBFCs need funds to support loan growth. With borrowing costs rising across corporate bonds, bank loans and short-term money markets, lenders face a more challenging funding environment. Investors are also demanding higher yields to compensate for macroeconomic and liquidity risks.Housing finance companies moderated their fundraising as well. They raised Rs 3,399 crore in October, down from Rs 5,150 crore in September. Aditya Birla Housing Finance was the largest issuer, raising Rs 2,300 crore through two bonds, followed by PNB Housing Finance, which raised Rs 700 crore. Advt The broader corporate bond market remained dominated by highly rated issuers. Companies rated AAA raised Rs 54,629 crore in October, accounting for nearly 68% of total fundraising. Lower-rated issuers raised around Rs 5,489 crore, or about 7% of the total. In September, AAA-rated companies accounted for more than 52% of fundraising, while lower-rated categories accounted for nearly 31%.Banking system liquidity also tightened during the month. The surplus moved towards near-neutral levels following liquidity-draining measures by the RBI and its decision to raise the repo rate and shift its policy stance to calibrated tightening on October 7. The RBI's net liquidity absorption, a proxy for the banking system surplus, fell to Rs 542 crore on October 21 from Rs 11.16 lakh crore in September, partly reflecting inflows through the central bank's special window for FCNR(B) deposits. Tighter liquidity made banks and other institutional investors more selective in deploying funds, adding to pressure on corporate borrowing costs.Fundraising outlook Advt Corporate bond issuances in the coming months will depend on government bond yields, global developments and banking system liquidity. Borrowing requirements from NBFCs and infrastructure-related public sector undertakings could support issuances as lenders seek to meet credit demand and finance capital expenditure. However, sustained volatility could prompt issuers to stagger fundraising or defer longer-tenor borrowings.Borrowers will also compare the all-in cost of raising funds through domestic bonds, bank loans and overseas markets. The choice will depend on pricing, maturity, hedging costs, availability of funds and execution certainty.Some public sector undertakings and banks may continue to explore offshore borrowing while swap concessions remain available until December 2026. Private companies, however, may find overseas funding expensive without such concessions, although entities with natural foreign currency hedges could continue to access these markets.With expectations of another repo rate increase in December and banking system liquidity remaining tight, bank loan pricing and money market rates could rise further. This may make domestic corporate bonds relatively competitive for large borrowers, even as higher yields continue to constrain fundraising.
