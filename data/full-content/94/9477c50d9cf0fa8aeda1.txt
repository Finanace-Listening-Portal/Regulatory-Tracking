The company effectively creates a dollar liability without borrowing directly from an overseas lender. Discussions are under way with several corporates for three to five year borrowings, although no deal had been executed when the structure was reported. Advt The economics have become more attractive as RBI's dollar rupee sell buy swaps and other foreign exchange operations have pushed up forward premiums. Reuters reported that two, three and five year rupee dollar swap pricing had risen by 90-110 basis points, making the currency swap route more competitive with direct dollar borrowing.The gainsThe advantage is particularly relevant for companies that already earn dollars or have overseas operations, as their dollar revenues can provide a natural hedge for the resulting liability. Under the structure, a company can raise rupee debt domestically and use a cross currency swap to exchange the rupee interest and principal payments for dollar payments.Direct dollar borrowing has also become more expensive as US Treasury yields have risen and markets have priced in further US rate increases. Mint reported an indicative example in which a AAA rated company could borrow domestically at about 8-8.25 per cent for five years and, after the swap, bring the effective dollar cost to around 4.75-5 per cent, compared with about 6-6.20 per cent for direct dollar borrowing. The actual saving would vary with the company's credit spread, tenor and swap pricing.The change in swap economics is linked to RBI's broader effort to absorb surplus rupee liquidity. The central bank has used dollar rupee sell buy swaps, spot dollar sales, bond sales and variable rate reverse repos, helping reduce the banking system's liquidity surplus from a September peak of Rs11.16 lakh crore to roughly half that level. The operations have also pushed up dollar forward premiums, altering the relative economics of rupee and dollar funding. Advt The opportunity, however, is not a risk free arbitrage. The value of the currency swap can move with interest rates and currency markets, while companies may face mark to market volatility in their profit and loss accounts if the transaction does not qualify for hedge accounting. Banks are therefore likely to target companies with suitable dollar cash flows and the ability to manage the associated risks.
