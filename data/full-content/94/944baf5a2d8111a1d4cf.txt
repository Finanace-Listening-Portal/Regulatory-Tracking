NFAC/2019-20/10507386 by the ld. Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi [hereinafter referred to as the “CIT(A)] u/s. 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as the “Act”) for the A.Y. 2020-21, wherein ld. CIT(A) has dismissed assessee’s appeal. 2. Brief facts of the case: The assessee is registered as a Co-operative Society and engaged in the trading and marketing of agriculture produce and PDS Fertilizers, seeds etc. for the farmers. The AO noted that the assessee did not furnish the return of income for A.Y. 2020-21 and an order u/s. 148A(d) and notice u/s. 148 of the Act was issued on 18.03.2024. The assessment was completed u/s. 147 r.w.s. 144B of the Act at a total income of Rs.40,566/-. The said addition was made as per the provisions of section 80A(5) and 80AC of the Act by denying the deduction u/s. 80P of the Act on the ground that the assessee did not file its return of income u/s. 139(1) of the Act within the prescribed due date and filed its return on 05.06.2024 in response to notice u/s. 148 of the Act dated 18.03.2024. 2.1 The AO also noted that in the instant case, the assessee was liable to furnish tax audit report in statutory form as required u/s. 44AB of the Act within the due date of furnishing of income tax return u/s. 139(1) of the Act, which was filed on 04.06.2024, i.e., beyond the due date of furnishing audit report for the A.Y. 2020-21. Accordingly, the AO initiated penalty proceedings u/s. 271B of the Act. 2.2 During the penalty proceedings, the assessee submitted that the assessee is a registered co-operative society as per the provision of Madhya Pradesh Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 and hence, regular audit was conducted by the internal auditor from controlling authority of the Co-operative Society under Madhya Pradesh Co-operative Societies Act, 1960. Further, it was submitted that the assessee has never undergone any other audit for the financial books other than the provision of the Co-operative Societies act 1960. Further, it was submitted that the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in the case of CIT v. District Co-operative Bank [2013] 36 taxmann.com 318/217 Taxman 145 (Mag.) (All) held that where assessee, a Co-operative Society, in a view of provision of U.P. Co-operative Societies Act, 1963 got its accounts audited by its internal auditors, levy of penalty under section 271B for not getting its accounts audited by any chartered accountant was not justified. 2.3 The AO, however, did not accept the above explanation of the assessee and noted that the AO made addition of Rs.40,566/- and the turnover of the assessee was Rs.2,78,57,647/- for the year under consideration, which was much more than Rs. one crore for the period under consideration. Accordingly, the AO held that the assessee was liable for penalty u/s. 271B of the Act and levied a penalty of Rs.1,39,288/-. 2.4 Aggrieved with the said order, the assessee filed an appeal before the learned CIT(A), who dismissed the appeal of the assessee. The Ld. CIT (A) held that mere fact that audit of Appellant was conducted under provisions of Co-operative Societies Act, would not be sufficient reason for non-compliance under section 44AB, unless report of audit is furnished in prescribed form accompanied with a further report by an accountant in prescribed form. 3. Aggrieved with the said order, the assessee is in appeal before us on grounds Nos. 1 to 16. All the grounds of appeal relate to imposition of penalty u/s. 271B of the Act. Grounds No. 2 of the appeal, on the basis of which this appeal can be decided, is reproduced as under: “GROUND NO, 2: Reasonable cause under section 273B not appreciated. That the Ld. CIT(A) erred in confirming the penalty without appreciating that the Appellant’s failure, if any, to obtain a tax audit report under section 44AB within the due date under section 139(1) was on account of a bona fide belief that the audit conducted by the audit authority of the Department of Co-operative Societies under the Madhya Pradesh Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 was sufficient compliance, and such bona fide belief constitutes “reasonable cause” within the meaning of section 273B of the Act, requiring the penalty to be deleted in its entirety.” 4. At the time of hearing, the Ld. AR filed a written submission and reiterated the facts stated before the AO and the Ld. CIT (A). The Ld. AR submitted that the appellant is a 65 years old small tehsil- level co-operative society, marketing agricultural produced and distributing PDS fertilisers and kerosene to farmers. It was submitted by the Ld. AR that the bona fide belief of the assessee that its accounts were in fact being audited under the governing State legislation as per the co-operative societies by the Registrar of Co-operative Society, coupled with its full claim of exemption u/s 80P of the Act sufficed the purpose of audit requirement under any other law and hence separate audit u/s 44AB as per the third proviso was not required, squarely fell within “reasonable cause” u/s 273B of the Act and therefore, the penalty levied by the AO and confirmed by the Ld. CIT(A) may be deleted. 5. On the other hand, the ld. Sr. DR supported the orders of the authorities below. 6. We have heard both the parties and perused the material on record. We note that the appellant is a 65 years old small tehsil- level co-operative society, marketing agricultural produce and distributing PDS fertilisers and kerosene to farmers. The AO imposed penalty u/s 271B of the Act on the ground that the turnover of the assessee was Rs.2,78,57,647/- for the year under consideration, which was much more than Rs. one crore for the period under consideration. However, under the provisions of section 273B of the Act, inter alia, no penalty u/s. 271B of the Act will be imposable on the assessee if the assessee proves that there was a reasonable cause for the said failure. In this regard, we are satisfied by the explanation of the assessee that the appellant’s failure to obtain a tax audit report under section 44AB within the due date under section 139(1) was on account of its bona fide belief that the audit conducted by the audit authority of the Department of Co-operative Societies under the Madhya Pradesh Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 was sufficient compliance of the provisions of section 44AB of the Act. The above explanation constitutes “reasonable cause” within the meaning of section 273B of the Act. Accordingly, on careful consideration, we are satisfied that there was a reasonable cause for the failure on the part of the assessee to get its accounts audited as per the provision of section 44AB of the Act. Accordingly, we delete the penalty of Rs. 1,39,288/- in this case. 7. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on 04/11/2026.
