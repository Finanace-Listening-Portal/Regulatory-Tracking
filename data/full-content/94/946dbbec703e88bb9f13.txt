Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Axis Bank Limited Vs PCIT (ITAT Ahmedabad) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Ahmedabad “A” Bench, allowed the appeal of Axis Bank Limited for Assessment Year 2020-21 and quashed the revisionary order dated 6 March 2026 passed by the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax, Ahmedabad-1, under Section 263 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The revision involved two issues: disallowance under Section 14A read with Rule 8D and deduction under Section 80G for donations linked to corporate social responsibility (CSR) expenditure. The bank had filed its original return on 25 November 2020 and revised return on 31 March 2021. Assessment was completed on 29 September 2023 under Section 143(3) read with Section 144B. The Assessing Officer made a disallowance of ₹20,11,72,067 under Section 14A read with Rule 8D in addition to ₹1,18,49,966 voluntarily disallowed by the bank. The CIT(A) confirmed the disallowance on 13 January 2025, but the Ahmedabad Tribunal subsequently deleted it on 20 May 2026 in ITA No. 611/Ahd/2025. On the Section 14A issue, the bank argued that the original assessment order had merged with the appellate order, so Explanation 1(c) to Section 263 prevented the PCIT from revising a matter already considered and decided in appeal. The Tribunal accepted this submission, observing that the CIT(A) had adjudicated the same issue before the PCIT issued the revision notice on 29 October 2025. The later Tribunal order deleting the disallowance further undermined the revision. Accordingly, the revisionary order was quashed on this ground. The second dispute concerned a Section 80G deduction of ₹31,68,03,055 claimed for donations forming part of CSR expenditure. The bank had added back its CSR and donation expenses in computing business income but claimed the separate Chapter VI-A deduction. The PCIT initially questioned whether mandatory CSR spending could qualify as a donation and, in the revision order, faulted the Assessing Officer for not independently verifying donation receipts, donee approvals, bank statements and other evidence. The Tribunal accordingly quashed the Section 263 revision on the Section 80G issue as well and allowed the bank’s appeal in full. The ruling emphasises the limits of revisionary jurisdiction over matters already decided in appeal, the need to provide an adequate opportunity where a new factual objection is raised, and the distinction between disallowance of CSR expenditure under Section 37(1) and eligibility of qualifying donations under Section 80G. Cited on CSR-linked donations and revision under Section 263. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT AHMEDABAD The present appeal has been filed by the Assessee against the order of the Ld. Principal Commissioner of Income Tax Ahmedabad-1, (hereinafter referred to as “PCIT”), dated 06.03.2026 passed under Section 263 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as the “Act”) and relates to Assessment Year (A.Y.) 2020-21. 1. Incorrect exercise of revisionary jurisdiction u/s 263 with respect to additional disallowance u/s 14A r.w. Rule 8D of the Income-tax Rules, 1962 (Tax effect – Rs. 4,31,97,130) 1.1 The learned Principal Commissioner of Income Tax (“Ld. PCIT”) erred in not appreciating the fact that the assessment order is not ‘erroneous’ as the Assessing Officer (“AO”) had duly examined and applied his mind to issue of disallowance u/s 14A of the Income-tax Act (“the Act”) r.w. Rule 8D. During the assessment proceedings, the Appellant had filed detailed submissions, including all relevant supporting documentation and AO categorically rejected the suo- motu disallowance computed by the Appellant and had proceeded to compute the disallowance as per the formula prescribed under Rule 8D. Further, the issue was also adjudicated during the first appellate proceedings and Hon’ble CIT(A) upheld the methodology adopted by AO while adjudicating the appeal for the year under consideration. 1.2. The Ld. PCIT erred in not appreciating that the assessment order is not ‘prejudicial to the interest of revenue’ as AO’s computation of disallowance u/s 14A r.w. Rule 8D of the Rules is in accordance with the provisions of the Act, restricting the disallowance computed under Rule 8D(2)(ii) in the ratio of taxable and exempt income following the decision in the case of Maxopp Investments Ltd. (402 ITR 640 (SC)) and in accordance with consistent practice followed by the AO in the recent years and upheld by the CIT(A).. Further, the underlying issue of disallowance u/s 14A read with Rule 8D has been recently decided in favour of the Appellant pursuant to recent order of by Hon’ble Tribunal in the quantum proceedings for the year under consideration, wherein it is upheld that there should not be 14A disallowance over and above the suo-moto disallowance made by the appellant and hence the question of treating assessment order as prejudicial to the interest of revenue does not arise. 1.3 The Ld. PCIT erred in assuming jurisdiction u/s 263 of the Act and in setting aside the assessment order passed u/s 143(3) r.w.s. 144B of the Act dated 29.09.2023 for A.Y. 2020- 21, without satisfying the twin pre-conditions of the assessment order being erroneous and the assessment order being prejudicial to the interest of revenue, prerequisites for initiation of revisionary proceedings with respect as mandated by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Malabar Industrial Co. Ltd. v. CIT [2000] 243 ITR 83 (SC), CIT v. Kwality Steel Suppliers Complex [2017] 84 taxmann.com 234 (SC) and others. 1.4 The Ld. PCIT erred in not appreciating that there are no employees in the investment portfolio division of the Appellant who are exclusively employed for generating tax-free income and no expenditure is directly incurred exclusively for earning exempt income. The Bank’s core business is lending and borrowing, and the employees engaged in investment activities manage both taxable and exempt investments. Accordingly, the salary cost of such employees cannot be characterized as ‘direct expenditure’ under Rule 8D(2)(i). 1.5 The Ld. PCIT erred in not appreciating that the suo-moto disallowance of Rs. 1,18,49,966 made by the Appellant in the return of income represents a reasonable estimation of indirect administrative expenses and other expenses (including the relevant indirect salary cost) that can be allocated to earning of tax-free income and is not a direct expenditure exclusively relating to income which does not form part of total income within the meaning of Rule 8D(2)(i). Accordingly, the said amount cannot be treated as ‘direct expenditure’ under Rule 8D(2)(i). 1.6 The Ld. PCIT erred in not appreciating that the AO had categorically rejected the suo motu disallowance computed by the Appellant and had proceeded to compute the disallowance as per the formula prescribed under Rule 8D. Having rejected the Appellant’s working and invoked Rule 8D, it is impermissible to simultaneously treat the suo-moto disallowance as a separate disallowance under Rule 8D(2)(i), as that would result in double disallowance, which is not permissible under law. 1.7 The Ld. PCIT erred in not appreciating that the investment portfolio of the Appellant yields both taxable income (including business income from sale of shares and interest income from bonds/PTC/debentures/NCDs amounting to Rs. 23,47,02,70,797) and exempt income (amounting to Rs. 1,04,93,50,134), with exempt income constituting only 4.28% of total investment income. The disallowance u/s 14A must be restricted to expenditure relatable to exempt income in accordance section 14A and the principle of apportionment, as affirmed by the Supreme Court in Maxopp Investment Ltd. (supra) and by the Delhi High Court in Principal Commissioner of Income-tax, Central v. Alchemist Ltd. [2024] 167 taxmann.com 284 (Del.) 1.8.The PCIT erred in not appreciating the fact that significant amount of investments made by the Appellant are in the nature of stock in trade and accordingly, the provisions of section 14A shall not be applicable to such investments in line with judicial precedents and in not appreciating that the Appellant’s submission with respect to proportionate disallowance in the ratio of exempt and taxable amount was on without prejudice basis. 1.9 The Ld. PCIT erred in relying upon the decision in BSES Rajdhani Power Ltd. v. Pr. CIT [2023] 152 taxmann.com 139 (SC) to hold that non-consideration of the entire amount under section 14A constitutes an error, without appreciating that in the present case the AO had duly considered the entire quantum of investment income, had applied the principle of apportionment in accordance with a legal view upheld by the CIT(A) and thus the issue does not involve a case of the AO ‘not going into the whole amount’. Further, the Hon’ble Tribunal has deleted the entire addition made by AO in case of the appellant. 2. Incorrect exercise of revisionary jurisdiction u/s 263 with respect to deduction u/s 80G of the Act on eligible Corporate Social Responsibility (“CSR”) expenditure (Tax effect – Rs. 7,97,32,993) 2.1 The Ld. PCIT erred in holding that the assessment order was erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of revenue on the ground that the AO had allowed deduction u/s 80G of the Act amounting to Rs. 31,68,03,055 in respect of donations forming part of CSR expenditure without appreciating the fact that complete party-wise details of donations, along with PAN of the donees and relevant disclosures were duly disclosed in the return of income and were also examined and reported by the tax auditors in Form 3CD, disclosure was also made in the computation of income and other submissions made during course of the assessment proceedings , which constituted sufficient material for verification of the nature and eligibility of such donations. 2.2 The Ld. PCIT erred in not appreciating that section 80G of the Act is an independent deduction from gross total income under Chapter VI-A. Explanation 2 to section 37(1), inserted by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2014, only restricts the allowance of CSR expenditure as business deduction under the head ‘Profits and Gains of Business or Profession’, and imposes no corresponding bar or restriction under Chapter VI-A or section 80G of the Act. 2.3 The Ld. PCIT erred in not appreciating that the Section 80G specifically excludes CSR related donations only in respect of the Swachh Bharat Kosh and the Clean Ganga Fund, by way of the express exclusions in sections 80G(2)(iiihk) and 80G(2)(iiihl) of the Act. This specific and limited exclusion implies that Parliament intended to allow deduction u/s 80G in respect of donations to all other eligible institutions, even where such donations form part of CSR expenditure. The Appellant’s donations to Axis Bank Foundation, Ispirit Foundation and Indian Cancer Society are not covered by the said exclusions. 2.4 The Ld. PCIT erred in not considering below judicial precedents Hon’ble Ahmedabad Tribunal, which have upheld the allowability of deduction u/s 80G on CSR donations where the applicable conditions of section 80G are satisfied and thus the assessment order is not “prejudicial to the interests of revenue”: Vimal Coal (P.) Ltd. v. DCIT [2025] 175 taxmann.com 694 (Ahmedabad Trib.); Gujarat Mineral Development Corporation Ltd. v. PCIT-1 [2025] 176 taxmann.com 227 (Ahmedabad Trib.); Gujarat State Financial Services Ltd. v. DCIT [2025] 174 taxmann.com 461 (Ahmedabad Trib.); PCIT v. AIA Engineering Ltd. [ITA Nos. 309 & 310/Ahd/2024]. The Appellant craves leave to add, to amend, alter, delete and/or modify the above grounds of appeal on or before the final date of hearing.” 3. The facts, in brief, are as under: The assessee, Axis Bank Limited, filed its return of income for AY 2020-21 on 25.11.2020, declaring total income of Rs.111,59,73,25,620/-. A revised return was subsequently filed on 31.03.2021, declaring total income of Rs. 111,59,71,80,280/-. The revised return was processed under section 143(1), and thereafter the case was selected for scrutiny assessment under CASS. The assessment was completed under section 143(3) read with section 144B on 29.09.2023, determining total income at Rs.123,07,49,17,375/- after making aggregate additions of Rs. 444,72,64,855//-. 4. The A.O. had made a disallowance of Rs.20,11,72,067/- under section 14A read with Rule 8D in addition to the sum of Rs.1,18,49,966/- disallowed suo moto by the assessee in its return on this account. The A.O. had apportioned the amount worked out under Rule 8D(2)(ii) on the basis that the tax-free income from investment activity formed 57.14% of the total gains from that activity and determined the disallowable sum of Rs.20,11,72,067/- on that basis. 5. The A.O. had made a disallowance of Rs.20,11,72,067/- under section 14A read with Rule 8D in addition to the sum of Rs.1,18,49,966/- disallowed suo moto by the assessee in its return on this account. The A.O. had apportioned the amount worked out under Rule 8D(2)(ii) on the basis that the tax-free income from investment activity formed 57.14% of the total gains from that activity and determined the disallowable sum of Rs.20,11,72,067/- on that basis. deduction claimed under section 80G in respect of donations; and disallowance under section 14A read with Rule 8D 5.1. In respect of section 80G, the assessee had debited an amount of Rs. 53,91,53,985/-towards CSR expenditure and Rs. 48,34,25,825/- towards donations in its Profit & Loss Account. Both the aforesaid amounts were disallowed and added back while computing the taxable income. However, as per the details furnished in the return of income under Schedule 80G and Chapter VI-A, the assessee had claimed deduction of Rs. 31,68,03,055/- under section 80G of the Act. The PCIT observed in the notice u/s 263 of the Act, that the said deduction was claimed in respect of donations forming part of the CSR expenditure and, on the premise that CSR expenditure constituted a statutory obligation rather than a voluntary donation, held that the deduction under section 80G was not allowable. According to the PCIT, although the CSR expenditure had already been disallowed while computing the taxable income, the deduction of Rs.31,68,03,055/- under section 80G had been allowed by the Assessing Officer, resulting in alleged underassessment of income and consequential prejudice to the interests of the Revenue. 6. We have examined the documents submitted and heard the learned A.R. Shri Tushar Hemani, Sr. Advocate and the Ld. CIT-DR Shri Kiran Unavekar. At the outset, we need to examine the argument regarding the Doctrine of Merger as raised by Shri Tushar Hemani. In the synopsis filed by the Ld. Counsel it is concisely submitted as under: “At the outset, it is submitted that the very issue of disallowance u/s 14A r.w,r 8D, which has been made the subject matter of revision under section 263 of the Act; has already been considered in the appellate proceedings arising from the original assessment order dated 29.09.2023. The said computation was challenged by the appellant before the Ld. CIT(A), who, after considering the issue, upheld the computation made by the Assessing Officer vide appellate order dated 13.01.2025 (relevant finding at Para 5 Pg no. 197-199 of PB). The appellant thereafter carried the matter in further appeal before the Hon’ble ITAT, and the Hon’ble Tribunal, vide its order dated 20.05.2026 in ITA No. 611/Ahd/2025, has adjudicated the issue and deleted the impugned disallowance made by the AO. The order of Hon’ble ITAT is attached at Pg no. 193-232 of PB @ Pg no. 199-203. Section 263 itself contains a statutory restriction in Explanation 1(c), under which the revisionary power of the PCIT extends only to matters which “had not been considered and decided in such appeal.” Therefore, once the very addition/disallowance which, according to the Ld. PCIT, resulted in underassessment of income has already passed through the appellate hierarchy and finally been deleted by the Hon’ble Tribunal, there remains no surviving prejudice to the interests of the Revenue on the basis of which the jurisdiction under section 263 could validly be exercised. Reliance is place on Aishwarya Rai Bachchan vs. Principal Commissioner of Income-tax-8 [2022] 135 taxmann.com 335 (Mumbai-Trib.).” 6.1. We have carefully considered this argument and find ourselves in agreement with the same. At the time of initiation of the revision proceedings by issue of notice u/s 263 dated 29-10-2025, the issue of disallowance u/s 14A read with Rule 8D had already been considered and adjudicated by the CIT(A) vide order dated 13-01-2025. The assessment order had merged, in respect of this issue of disallowance u/s 14A read with Rule 8D, in the order of the CIT(A) and the PCIT could not seek to assume jurisdiction even by invoking clause (c) of Explanation 1 to section 263. The fact that the ITAT Ahmedabad had deleted the disallowance made by the A.O., which was confirmed by the CIT(A), in the assessee’s own case for the same assessment year, i.e. 2020-21, goes further to make the revision order on this issue an exercise in futility. The impugned order u/s 263 is quashed on this issue and the assessee’s ground of appeal no. 1 (including the sub-parts thereof) is allowed. Ground No. 2: Revision in respect of 80G deduction: 7. The PCIT, in the impugned order u/s 263, held the assessment order to be “erroneous in so far as it is prejudicial to the interest of the revenue” on the ground that the A.O. failed to verify if the donations were actually made and were eligible for deduction under the Act. The entire finding of the PCIT in this regard is reproduced below: “The assessee’s contention regarding allowability of deduction u/s 80G donations is not acceptable. It is observed that though the assessee has claimed deduction of Rs. 31,68,03,055 u/s 80G on donations aggregating to Rs.63,36,06,110/-the case records do not demonstrate that the Assessing Officer has carried out any verification with respect to the allowability of such claim. The AO has neither called for nor examined the basic documentary evidences such as donation receipts, approval/registration certificates of the donee institutions u/s 80G(5), mode of payment, bank statements reflecting the payments, confirmations from donees, or utilisation details to establish that the donations were actually made and were eligible for deduction under the Act. Further, no independent verification or cross-checking of the donations with the respective donee institutions was carried out to ascertain the genuineness and eligibility of the claim. The assessee has also failed to furnish the requisite supporting evidences even during the present proceedings. In absence of such verification, the claim of deduction u/s 80G cannot be said to have been examined by the AO in accordance with law. Therefore, the assessment order allowing the deduction without proper inquiry and verification is erroneous insofar as it is prejudicial to the interest of revenue.” 8. Shri Tushar Hemani Ld. Sr. Advocate submitted that the fact that the donations were indeed made and were eligible for deduction u/s 80G was duly examined by the Tax Auditors. He drew our attention to the Tax Audit Report submitted in Form 3CD wherein the Tax Auditors have clearly certified at clause 33 Rs. 31,68,03,055/- as the sum eligible for deduction under section 80G of the Act. He also stated that in the show-cause notice the PCIT raised the issue of eligibility of an expenditure linked to CSR for deduction under section 80G, and the assessee had made elaborate submissions on this legal issue. However, in the impugned order the PCIT raised the issue of verification for the first time, giving no opportunity to the assessee to submit evidence. 9. On the issue raised in the show cause notice i.e., if a CSR linked expenditure could be allowed as deduction u/s 80G, the Ld. A.R. has relied upon the following decisions of ITAT Ahmedabad Benches wherein the proposition as suggested by the PCIT had been rejected by the Co-ordinate Benches. Gujarat Tea Processors & Packers Ltd. vs. Principal Commissioner of Income-tax [2026] 182 taxmann.com 232 (Ahmedabad – Trib.)[06-01-2026/ Vimal Coal (P.) Ltd. v. DCIT [2025] 175 taxmann.com 694 (Ahd. Trib.); Gujarat Mineral Development Corporation Ltd. v. Pr. CIT-1 [2025] 176 taxmann.com 227 (Ahd. Trib.); Gujarat State Financial Services Ltd. v. DCIT [2025] 174 taxmann.com 461 (Ahd. Trib.); and Pr. CIT v. AIA Engineering Ltd., ITA Nos. 309 & 310/Ahd/2024. 10. The Ld. CIT-DR relied upon the order of the PCIT. 11. We have carefully considered the contentions and examined the impugned order, the underlying assessment order and the documents submitted by the assessee. The PCIT has indeed initiated the revisionary proceedings in respect of the deduction u/s 80G on a legal issue, i.e., whether the assessee had received an undue advantage by claiming deduction u/s 80G in respect of CSR expenditure which is specifically disallowable in view of Explanation 2 to section 37(1). In the impugned order, while recording his reasoning and conclusion, the Ld. PCIT has simply referred to the failure of the A.O. to call for and verify donation receipts etc., as the reason for which the A.O’s order could be regarded as erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of the revenue. 12. As far as this aspect is concerned, we are inclined to agree with the Ld. A.R. that the PCIT did not raise this verification aspect in the show cause notice and hence the assessee had no opportunity to submit all details regarding the donations. He ought to have called for the details to satisfy himself before issuing direction to the A.O. and forcing the assessee to go through a time consuming and costly process of assessment once again, and the A.O. to carry out the process under a vague direction. Besides, the fact that the Tax Auditors have certified the eligibility of the sum of Rs.31,68,03,055/- for deduction u/s 80G raises a very strong presumption about the validity of the claim. 13. The legal issue raised in the notice has been dealt with by the Co-ordinate Benches in several cases cited by the Ld. A.R. andas reproduced supra. A company is required to spend a specified percentage of its profits for social activities that qualify to be defined as the Corporate Social responsibility (CSR) expenditure. The legislature in its wisdom has sought to make such expenditure ineligible for being claimed as business expenditure. The assessee has complied with the same and disallowed the CSR expenditure u/s 37(1). Section 80G operates on a different footing. Eligibility of the claim for such deduction depends on eligibility of the Donee and also on the availability of sufficient gross total income of the Doner (any donation in excess of 10% of gross total income is ignored for this deduction). Besides, those cases where donation made under CSR category is not eligible for deduction u/s 80G have been specified in section 80G(2)(iiihk) (Swachh Bharat Kosh) and 80(2)(iiihl) (Clean Ganga Fund). The assessee had donated to Axis Bank Foundation, Ispirt Foundation and Indian Cancer Society, none of which are specified in section 80G for ineligibility on account of CSR eligible contribution. 14. In view of the above findings we have no hesitation in quashing the order u/s 263of this issue, too. 15. In the result, appeal of the assessee is allowed.
