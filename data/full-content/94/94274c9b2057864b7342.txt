The rate page separately identifies additional-interest categories, so a resident senior citizen who satisfies the bank’s conditions should obtain the exact applicable rate on the deposit advice rather than relying on arithmetic alone. This article distinguishes the currently listed 444-day tenor from an older promotional UCO 444 page whose displayed validity ended on 30 June 2026, and explains payout, premature closure, TDS, income inclusion and DICGC aggregation without recommending the deposit. This article therefore covers an ordinary UCO Bank domestic term deposit booked specifically for 444 days under the live rate schedule, not an extension of the expired promotional product and not an NRE, FCNR, tax-saver, recurring, non-callable bulk or senior-citizen-only scheme. A standard resident senior-citizen addition of 0.50 percentage point above the public rate would arithmetically take the 444-day nominal rate from 6.45% to 6.95% per annum. That arithmetic is not a substitute for eligibility. The bank must confirm the customer’s residential status, age, deposit amount, account constitution, first-holder rule where relevant, booking channel and whether the particular premium may be combined with any staff or ex-staff benefit. A joint account containing a senior citizen does not automatically prove that the senior rate applies, and an NRO deposit should not be treated as a resident senior deposit merely because NRO deposits appear in the same base-rate table. The final deposit receipt should state the contracted rate, booking date, principal, tenor, maturity date and maturity or payout instructions. Any mismatch between the displayed quote and the receipt should be resolved before funds remain locked for the full 444 days. The 6.83 percent figure is a yield indicator not a second interest rate The official table places 6.45% beside an annualised-yield figure of 6.83% for 444 days. The first figure is the nominal annual rate used under the bank’s interest-calculation method. The yield figure reflects compounding over the relevant tenor and should not be read as a separate selectable rate. A cumulative deposit, a monthly-interest option and a quarterly-interest option can produce different cash-flow timing even when they begin from the same nominal rate. Monthly payouts may involve discounting, while cumulative interest remains invested according to the bank’s method. The customer should rely on the maturity value printed in the deposit advice or generated by the bank’s official system. Multiplying principal by 6.45% and then by 444/365 produces only a simple-interest estimate; it can differ from the contractual result because of quarterly compounding, broken-period calculation, day-count convention, rounding, payout choice, TDS timing and any premature closure. The 6.83% column also should not be multiplied by principal as though it were the interest payable for 444 days. Callability and premature closure need the deposit specific rule The RBI’s Master Direction on Interest Rate on Deposits, 2025 provides the regulatory framework within which banks formulate deposit rates and premature-withdrawal policies. It requires banks to disclose the applicable penalty and recognises that interest on premature withdrawal is determined under the bank’s policy rather than automatically at the original contracted rate. UCO Bank’s customer charter states that premature withdrawal is allowed unless a deposit is specified otherwise. That qualification matters because non-callable and special deposits can restrict closure. For the ordinary 444-day domestic term deposit covered here, the customer should obtain the callable or non-callable status and the current premature-closure formula in writing at booking. If closed early, the bank may apply the rate that was available on the original booking date for the actual completed period, reduce it by the disclosed penalty, or apply another permitted policy. The 444-day contracted rate should not be assumed to survive early closure. No interest may be payable if the deposit is withdrawn before the bank’s minimum interest-bearing period. A loan or overdraft against the deposit is a separate credit facility with interest, margin and lien terms; it is not automatically cheaper than closure. For a depositor whose accounting facts place it under income from other sources, the gross interest must be reported under that head; business entities may have a different character where the deposit is demonstrably connected with business. The absence of tax deduction at source does not make the interest exempt, and TDS is only a credit against the final tax liability. A 444-day deposit is not a five-year tax-saver deposit and does not become eligible for a principal deduction merely because it is called a fixed deposit. Tax Year 2026-27 is governed by the Income-tax Act, 2025 and the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Depositors should therefore not mechanically copy section numbers, threshold statements or declaration forms from an article dealing with FY 2025-26 under the Income-tax Act, 1961. The bank applies the current TDS provision to interest credited or paid, subject to the payee category, aggregate threshold, PAN status, rate, declarations and any lower-deduction certificate. The Income Tax portal identifies Form 121 under the 2026 Rules as the declaration route for eligible taxpayers seeking non-deduction on specified receipts where the statutory conditions, including nil estimated tax liability, are satisfied. The depositor should reconcile the bank’s annual interest certificate, Form 16A where issued, AIS and the tax return. Cumulative deposits can create annual tax reporting even though cash is received only at maturity, depending on credit and accrual. If total estimated tax after TDS remains payable, advance-tax obligations may arise. Resident senior citizens and other taxpayers should separately test any deduction permitted by the current Act and their chosen tax regime; a deduction, when available, is not the same as exemption of the bank interest. DICGC cover aggregates UCO Bank deposits in the same capacity DICGC insures eligible deposits with an insured bank up to Rs 5 lakh per depositor in the same right and same capacity, including principal and interest. The ceiling is not Rs 5 lakh for each UCO Bank branch, each fixed-deposit receipt or each account. Savings, current, recurring and fixed-deposit balances across branches are aggregated where they are held in the same right and capacity. If principal itself is Rs 5 lakh, accrued interest does not sit above the ceiling merely because it is interest. Accounts held in genuinely different capacities or rights can be treated separately under DICGC rules, while joint accounts with the same names in the same order are aggregated together. Changing branches does not create fresh cover. Depositors should use the DICGC illustrations and legal account constitution rather than splitting receipts mechanically. Deposit insurance protects within the statutory scheme if an insured-bank failure or covered restriction triggers payment; it is not a promise of immediate liquidity, protection against early-withdrawal loss, compensation for a wrong rate, or a guarantee of all balances.
