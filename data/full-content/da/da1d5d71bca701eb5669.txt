Summary: SBI Contra Fund is an open-ended equity scheme of SBI Mutual Fund following a contrarian investment strategy. The filed Scheme Information Document identifies SBI Funds Management Limited as the asset management company and SBI Mutual Fund Trustee Company Private Limited as trustee. Its objective is long-term capital appreciation through a diversified portfolio of equity and equity-related securities selected under a contrarian strategy, without assurance that the objective will be achieved. The stated first-tier benchmark is the S&P BSE 500 Total Return Index. The mandate permits 65% to 100% in equity and equity-related instruments of companies following the contrarian theme, while other equity, debt, money-market securities, REITs/InvITs, derivatives, securities lending and foreign securities can be used within disclosed limits. This breadth makes the scheme an actively managed, high-equity-risk product rather than a fixed replication of the benchmark. This article explains what “contra” does and does not mean, how direct and regular plans share one portfolio but have different expenses, the current disclosed exit-load structure, NAV and cash-flow mechanics, portfolio and style risks, investor-service controls, and Indian tax treatment for equity-oriented fund units. It does not use returns as a recommendation or present dynamic AUM, NAV or TER figures as permanent. Key Takeaways Show all 28 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); SBI Contra Fund’s Legal and Scheme Identity SBI Contra Fund is a scheme of SBI Mutual Fund. The scheme document names SBI Funds Management Limited as the AMC and SBI Mutual Fund Trustee Company Private Limited as trustee. SBI Mutual Fund’s public registration number is MF/009/93/3. The scheme opened in June 1999 and is available for ongoing purchase and redemption at applicable NAV, subject to the current scheme documents, cut-off and realisation rules. Its longevity does not reduce equity-market risk or make future outcomes predictable. An Open-Ended Equity Contra Scheme The scheme category is not a broad label chosen by a distributor. The SID describes an open-ended equity scheme following a contrarian investment strategy. “Open-ended” means units are normally available from the fund at NAV-related prices on business days; it does not guarantee instant liquidity in every contingency. A high volume of redemptions, market disruption, illiquid holdings or an exceptional regulatory event can affect portfolio execution or the permitted redemption process. It may include out-of-favour companies, sectors facing temporary pressure or businesses in a recovery phase. The process is still fundamental security selection. It is not a rule that every falling share is attractive, nor does the category require the fund to oppose the market on every position. Minimum Thematic Equity and Supporting Flexibility The filed mandate sets 65% to 100% for equity and equity-related instruments of companies following the contrarian theme, including derivatives. It allows up to 35% in other equity and equity-related instruments, up to 35% in debt, up to 35% in money-market instruments and up to 10% in REITs and InvITs. The SID also permits derivatives exposure up to 50% of net assets, securities lending up to 20%, and foreign securities within a 35% ceiling, subject to regulations and operational availability. These are limits, not a current portfolio statement. Why the BSE 500 TRI Is a Reference, Not a Portfolio The S&P BSE 500 TRI covers a broad listed-equity universe and includes reinvested dividends in the benchmark return. SBI Contra Fund does not have to hold each constituent at index weight. Active sector, market-cap and company decisions can produce long periods of divergence. The benchmark helps evaluate an active result over a suitable period, but benchmark comparison does not remove the need to examine risk, expenses, cash flows and the actual dated portfolio. The important structural difference is expenses: the Direct Plan excludes distributor commission and generally carries a lower expense ratio, while the Regular Plan includes distribution-related costs. Their NAVs therefore differ even when the underlying holdings are common. The right plan depends on the route used and the investor’s service arrangement; this article does not select one for the reader. Growth Retains Income; IDCW Distributes From Scheme Value Under Growth, income and realised gains remain reflected in NAV until units are redeemed. IDCW is not interest and is not guaranteed. A declaration depends on distributable surplus and reduces the scheme’s NAV to the extent of the distribution and applicable effects. Reinvestment is a distribution followed by acquisition of additional units and can have tax reporting consequences. The scheme documents and transaction form should be checked for the available IDCW frequencies, defaults and payout rules at the time of application. NAV, Cut-Off, Expense Ratio and Exit Load A purchase is allotted at the applicable NAV determined under SEBI’s cut-off and funds-realisation framework, not necessarily the NAV visible when an instruction is keyed in. Redemption value uses the applicable NAV less exit load, if any, and statutory charges. Weekends, bank holidays, incomplete KYC, rejected payments, lien-marked units and transaction-platform cut-offs can affect processing. TER Must Be Read as a Dated Disclosure The total expense ratio is charged to scheme assets and reduces NAV over time. SBI Mutual Fund publishes current and historical TER disclosures separately. The Direct and Regular ratios can change prospectively within regulatory limits; a factsheet value is not permanent. A sound editorial or investor record should capture the plan, effective date and official TER disclosure rather than copying an undated figure from an aggregator. Current Filed Exit-Load Structure The current filed SID states an exit load of 1% for exit within one year from the date of allotment and nil after one year. The AMC may change the load prospectively through an addendum. Each purchase or SIP instalment has its own allotment date, so a partial redemption can contain units with different load outcomes. A switch is generally a redemption from one scheme or plan and a purchase into another, with load and tax implications where applicable. Portfolio Facts Require a Reference Date Holdings, sector weights, cash, AUM, NAV, fund managers, Riskometer and benchmark Riskometer are dynamic disclosures. SBI Mutual Fund provides monthly factsheets and portfolio files, while NAVs are published on the AMC and AMFI channels. A current assessment should pair the latest month-end portfolio with the effective TER notice and any SID or KIM addenda. This article does not freeze a dynamic AUM, TER or top-ten list into a permanent scheme description. Questions for Reading the Latest Portfolio How much of the portfolio is concentrated in the largest holdings, sectors and business groups? Are the positions genuinely out of favour, or has the portfolio shifted toward established market leaders? How much exposure is in mid- and small-cap securities with lower trading liquidity? What cash, debt, derivative and overseas positions affect effective equity exposure? Did any month-on-month change arise from trades, price movement, corporate action or investor flows? Risks Specific to an Active Contra Portfolio A contrarian thesis can remain unpopular longer than expected or be wrong because the business is structurally impaired rather than temporarily mispriced. Value traps can combine weak governance, leverage, technological disruption and poor capital allocation. A broad benchmark can rise while out-of-favour positions lag. Turnaround holdings may also have lower liquidity, making entry or exit more expensive during stress. Style, Manager and Liquidity Risk Results depend on research quality, position sizing and the manager’s decision about when a thesis is validated or broken. A change in fund manager or process can alter implementation without changing the scheme name. Derivatives can hedge, manage flows or create market exposure, but introduce basis, counterparty and operational risk. Foreign securities add currency and market-access uncertainty. Securities lending adds borrower and collateral-management risk. None of these tools makes capital preservation certain. Riskometer Is a Current Disclosure, Not a Warranty The scheme and benchmark Riskometers must be checked from the latest AMC disclosure because they can change with portfolio characteristics and the applicable methodology. A label such as “Very High” is a regulatory communication of risk, not a probability forecast. Investors must still consider horizon, liquidity need, ability to tolerate loss, other holdings and tax position. Indian Tax Treatment of SBI Contra Fund Units Where the scheme qualifies as an equity-oriented fund and the statutory STT conditions are met, a redemption or taxable switch within twelve months generally produces short-term capital gain taxable at 20%, plus applicable surcharge and cess. A qualifying transfer after more than twelve months generally produces long-term capital gain taxable at 12.5% on the aggregate eligible gain exceeding ₹1.25 lakh for the tax year. Costs, losses, exemptions, residential status and special taxpayer provisions can change the computation. IDCW and Capital Gains Are Different Income Events IDCW is generally taxable in the unitholder’s hands at the applicable rate and may be subject to withholding when statutory conditions and thresholds are met. Reinvestment does not erase the distribution event. Redemption, SIP instalments, STP and SWP require lot-level records because holding period and cost are determined for the units actually transferred. STT, exit load and tax are separate deductions or liabilities and should not be combined into one percentage. If unresolved, the investor can use the escalation and SEBI complaint mechanisms applicable at that time. Frequently Asked Questions 1. Is SBI Contra Fund an index fund? No. It is an actively managed contra equity scheme. The S&P BSE 500 TRI is a benchmark, not a portfolio to be replicated. 2. Must every holding be an unpopular stock? No. The mandate requires substantial contrarian-theme equity exposure but allows other equity and supporting asset classes within disclosed limits. 3. Do Direct and Regular plans hold different securities? They share the scheme portfolio. Expenses and distributor involvement differ, which leads to different NAVs. 4. Is IDCW a guaranteed monthly income? No. IDCW depends on distributable surplus and trustee declaration and reduces NAV when paid. 5. What exit load is currently stated in the filed SID? One per cent for exit within one year from allotment and nil after one year, subject to any current prospective addendum. 6. Does an SIP avoid equity risk or exit load? No. Each instalment buys units at the applicable NAV and has its own market exposure, holding period and potential exit load. 7. How are qualifying redemptions taxed in 2026? Subject to equity-oriented fund and STT conditions, the general rates are 20% for qualifying short-term gains and 12.5% for eligible long-term gains above the aggregate ₹1.25 lakh threshold, plus surcharge and cess. 8. Why are AUM and TER not stated as permanent facts? They change. The current month-end factsheet, portfolio and effective TER disclosure should be cited with their reference date. Key Takeaways SBI Contra Fund is an active open-ended contra equity scheme, not an index tracker or bank deposit. At least 65% of the disclosed mandate is in contrarian-theme equity and related instruments. Direct and Regular plans share a portfolio but differ in expenses and therefore NAV. The filed exit load is 1% within one year and nil later, subject to current addenda.
