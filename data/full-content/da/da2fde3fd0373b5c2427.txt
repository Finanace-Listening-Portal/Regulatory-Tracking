Summary: SEBI’s circular dated 2 November 2026 introduces a framework for issuing Depository Receipts (DRs) backed by Infrastructure Investment Trust (InvIT) units, providing eligible InvITs with a mechanism to access overseas investors through international exchanges in India’s International Financial Services Centre (IFSC). Implementation requires compliance teams to address regulatory approvals, issue structuring, investor eligibility, minimum public unitholding, ownership concentration, custody arrangements, disclosures and ongoing investor servicing. The framework permits DR issuance against fresh or existing units, subject to applicable conditions and unitholder approvals. InvITs must establish contractual arrangements involving investment managers, foreign depositories, domestic custodians, Indian depositories, merchant bankers and participating unitholders. Particular attention is required to the 25% public unitholding threshold, investment limits under Regulation 22(5C), restrictions applicable to privately placed InvITs and the exclusion of persons resident in India from holding DRs. Operational procedures should address issuance, surrender, cancellation, fungibility, reconciliation of underlying units, distribution payments, voting instructions, beneficial ownership verification and regulatory reporting. Compliance teams must also coordinate IFSC listing obligations with domestic exchange disclosures, including the 24-hour reporting requirement. Existing corporate Depository Receipt practices may provide useful precedents, but they must be adapted to the InvIT regulatory framework. The article presents a practical pre-issue checklist for implementing these requirements and maintaining effective compliance after listing. Settle the structure before seeking approvals The first step is to decide how the issue of DRs will be structured. This will determine the approvals, parties and documents required for its implementation. The first step is to Review the trust deed and investment management agreement for authority and necessary trustee involvement. keep the trustee informed about the developments and necessary approvals required from him before beginning the actual process. Thereafter, identify whether the proposal involves new units, existing units, or both. New units raise capital for the InvIT; existing-unit transfers provide liquidity to participating unitholders. Prepare a transaction note covering the DR-to-unit ratio, size, pricing, costs, proceeds and responsibilities. since this is a phased introduction, The permitted exchanges are India International Exchange and NSE International Exchange in IFSC. hence it is advisable to Engage a advisory or compliance firm having expertise in IFSC. 2. issue of DRs to be in compliance with INVIT regulations A DR issue must fit within the InvIT’s existing regulatory obligations. Assess its effect on unitholding, transfer restrictions and investor participation before finalising the proposal. Calculate post-issue public unitholding: units on which DRs are issued, are considered under non-public holding. hence minimum 25%public holding post issue of DRs should be calculated excluding the units against which DRs are issued. Check sponsor holdings, lock-ins and encumbrances; conversion should not release restricted units. The compliance team should first identify whether the proposal involves a new DR offering backed by fresh units, or an offering backed by existing unitholders’ units. Prior unitholder approval by at least 75% of votes cast is generally required in both these cases, subject to the circular’s exceptions. A subsequent offering within the overall limit already approved does not require fresh approval. Where an offering involves units tendered by existing unitholders, the team should document the invitation, participation period and allocation process to ensure that all existing unitholders have an opportunity to participate. 3. Agreements to be entered into and key clauses to include The agreements must explain how the parties will perform their responsibilities throughout the programme. Clear provisions are particularly necessary where the InvIT depends on intermediaries for information or action. The compliance team of InvITs can Use company deposit agreements as precedents, with InvIT-specific changes. They should Settle the following documents, separately or through coordinated agreements: Agreement or arrangement Key contents Deposit agreement between the Investment Manager acting for the InvIT and Foreign Depository, with binding holder terms DR ratio; issue, surrender and cancellation; distributions; voting instructions; eligibility and holding-limit undertakings; information access; fees; grievances; liability; amendments; termination and governing law. custody and depository arrangements involving the relevant intermediaries Accounts; receipt and release of units; reconciliation; transaction reporting; conversion headroom; settlement failures; corporate actions; data sharing and business continuity. Merchant banker engagement Compliance oversight, due diligence, filings, deliverables and compliance report for the Investment Manager’s Board. Selling-unitholder and placement or subscription agreements, where applicable Title and transferability; participation; allocation; settlement; proceeds; representations; indemnities and expenses. The compliance team should Allocate responsibility for timely flow of updates relating to regulatory information and discrepancies. Compliance with clauses of IFSC listing agreement should also be factored in while finalizing contractual obligations. 4. Make investor eligibility and holding limits monitorable Compliance with investor restrictions requires information beyond domestic unitholder records. Establish how this information will be obtained, updated and checked. The compliance team should put arrangements in place to obtain information on who holds the DRs, their beneficial-owner details, residency declarations, domestic holdings and persons-acting-in-concert information. Agree response deadlines, periodic confirmations and escalation procedures. This can be done by including clauses in relevant agreements. Teams need to ensure that the DRs are not issued to any Indian resident, neither any beneficial owner of any DR holder is an Indian resident. As per regulation 22(5C), investors other then sponsors, their related parties and associates cannot invest in more then 25% of total outstanding units without unitholders approval. The compliance team needs to be mindful of the fact that this 25% includes the units underlying DRs. Therefore while issuing DRs, care should be taken that this limit of investing in not more then 25% of outstanding units is not breached. In addition, privately placed InvITs need to keep in mind some additional restrictions. for example, investment in DRs issued by such InvITs may be made only by institutional investors or body corporates. Also there have to be restrictions on minimum lot size for DRs issued by such InvIT. 5. Put processes in place for fungibility of DRs Before the issue, the compliance team should coordinate with the Domestic Custodian, Foreign Depository and Indian Depository to establish how underlying units will be deposited for issuing DRs, how DR holders can surrender their DRs to receive units, and how units can subsequently be deposited to issue DRs again, subject to applicable limits. The process should ensure that the Foreign Depository issues DRs only after the Domestic Custodian confirms receipt of the underlying units. It should also specify how surrendered DRs will be cancelled and the corresponding units released to the holder. The compliance team should agree with these intermediaries how often their records will be compared to confirm that the number of outstanding DRs matches the underlying units held in custody. They should also decide who will report and resolve any mismatch and who will check the available limit before further units are accepted for issuing DRs. A trial run before launch can help confirm that these arrangements work. Distinguish the DR-to-unit ratio from market pricing and currency conversion. Confirm domestic account and investor requirements before promising delivery on cancellation. 6. Structure for distributions to, and voting by, DR holders DR holders receive distributions and exercise voting rights through the depository structure. Preserve their entitlements while accommodating payment and meeting timelines. Adapt company dividend processes to InvIT distribution components, but only after considering the timelines prescribed for payment of distributions by INVIT regulations. Allocate distribution-breakup reporting, tax documentation, withholding, currency conversion and remittance. Disclose charges and treatment of unpaid amounts. Coordinate record dates, holidays and cut-offs. Confirm the legal payment deadline and its satisfaction point; do not assume payment to an intermediary completes compliance. Set voting-material and instruction deadlines. Permit underlying votes only on holder instructions; discretionary voting clauses used by companies may be referred for guidance in this regard. Since at present DRs are to be issued and listed on IFSC exchanges only, arrangements are required to be made keeping in mind the requirements of IFSC listing regulations. 7. Prepare disclosures and continuing support Listing DRs creates continuing responsibilities across two exchange systems. Establish coordinated disclosures, regulatory reporting and investor support before opening the issue. Prepare a filing calendar covering applicable IFSC requirements. Company DR rules require simultaneous English disclosure of home-market filings; confirm their application to the InvIT with the selected exchange. Public IFSC disclosures must reach domestic exchanges within 24 hours under SEBI’s circular; shorter domestic deadlines remain relevant. Obtain the merchant banker’s report and confirm approvals, pricing compliance and FEMA reporting with the authorised dealer bank. For simultaneous initial listings, issue or transfer underlying units only after domestic trading approval. Publish grievance contacts, response periods and escalation routes. Conclusion The preparations above should be assessed together before launch. Their effectiveness will determine how smoothly the InvIT can meet its obligations and service DR holders. The consultation paper envisages wider foreign investor access. Company DR experience can shorten preparation for custody, distributions and voting, but each precedent must fit the InvIT structure.
