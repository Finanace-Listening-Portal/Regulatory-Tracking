The scheme provides interest subsidies of 5%, 6% or 7% on term loans for purchasing new machinery and equipment, depending on the geographical classification of the manufacturing unit, with annual ceilings ranging from ₹20 lakh to ₹25 lakh. Eligible special-category entrepreneurs and joint ventures with foreign investors receive an additional 1% subsidy. Manufacturing MSE exporters can claim interest subvention of 3% on pre-shipment and post-shipment export credit, increased to 4% for specified categories, subject to an annual ceiling of ₹10 lakh. Both benefits require a minimum ZED Silver certification. Eligibility depends on enterprise classification, loan sanction or disbursement dates, operational status, regulatory approvals, ownership conditions and satisfactory loan repayment records. Applications must be submitted annually through the Invest Haryana Single Window Portal within the prescribed period, with 30 June 2027 being the first annual claim deadline for FY 2026-27. Chartered Accountants must carefully verify financial particulars, government subsidies, investment, turnover and commercial production details before issuing the prescribed certificate, which carries a personal liability clause. The article explains geographical classifications, subsidy rates, eligibility conditions, documentation, scrutiny, appeal procedures and differences from the earlier HEEP 2020 scheme. It also examines potential gaps in loan eligibility dates, other incentives under the new MSME policy and practical measures that manufacturing enterprises should undertake to maximise eligible benefits while ensuring statutory compliance. Haryana Interest Subsidy Scheme 2026 for Manufacturing MSMEs Haryana has started putting money behind its new MSME policy. The Haryana Progressive MSME & Export Promotion Policy 2026 was notified on 29 July 2026. On 16 November 2026 the State notified the first scheme under it that most of our manufacturing clients can actually claim: the Haryana Interest Subsidy Scheme. The scheme gives two benefits: an interest subsidy of 5% to 7% on term loans taken to buy new machinery; a 3% interest subvention on export credit. Both are limited to manufacturing micro and small units. Both apply only to loans sanctioned or disbursed on or after 29 July 2026. Both need a ZED Silver certificate. That last condition will decide who actually benefits. The policy itself is only a framework. A benefit announced in it can be claimed only after the Directorate of MSME notifies scheme guidelines for that benefit. This note therefore concentrates on what has been notified, and touches on the rest only briefly. Who Can Claim Haryana MSME Interest Subsidy? Eligible Manufacturing Micro and Small Enterprises The scheme is for manufacturing micro and small enterprises in Haryana, whether new or already running. Medium enterprises, traders and service units are outside it. Almost any form of business can apply: an individual or proprietor; a partnership firm or LLP; a public or private company or a corporation; a cooperative, a self-help group, a trust or a farmer producer organisation. MSME Classification Based on Investment and Turnover Size is tested on the latest Udyam certificate: Micro: investment up to ₹2.5 crore and turnover up to ₹10 crore. Small: investment up to ₹25 crore and turnover up to ₹100 crore. Eligible Loan Sanction Period and Expansion at New Locations Timing matters. The term loan or export credit must be sanctioned or disbursed between 29 July 2026 and 28 July 2031. An existing unit that expands at a new location is treated as a new unit, provided that location is added to its GST and Udyam registrations. Interest Subsidy Rates and Export Credit Subvention Benefits 1. Interest Subsidy on Term Loans for New Machinery The State pays part of the interest on a term loan taken to buy new plant, machinery or equipment. The rate depends on where the unit is located: Area category Interest subsidy Ceiling per year Core Area 5% ₹20 lakh Intermediate Area 6% ₹20 lakh Sub-Prime and Prime/Focus Area 7% ₹25 lakh Units get 1% more if they are owned by women, SC, Divyang or transgender entrepreneurs, ex-servicemen or Agniveers. Joint ventures with a foreign investor also get the extra 1%. The subsidy runs for three years from the start of commercial operations. A unit that takes this subsidy cannot claim a similar interest benefit under any other State scheme. 2. Interest Subvention on Export Credit Manufacturing MSE exporters get 3% on their pre-shipment and post-shipment export credit. The ceiling is ₹10 lakh a year, for any three years within the policy period. The rate goes up to 4% for: first-time exporters, meaning units whose IEC was issued on or after 29 July 2026; units owned by the special categories listed above; exporters in the State’s identified export focus categories. An existing exporter must earn at least 25% of its turnover from exports. This benefit comes on top of the Government of India’s interest subvention under the Export Promotion Mission. How Geographical Area Categories Determine Interest Subsidy The old A/B/C/D block system is gone. The category now depends on the exact location of the unit, and the Single Window Portal shows the category for each site. Category Broadly covers Prime/Focus Notified Industrial Investment Promotion Zones Sub-Prime Industrial zones in Development Plans; vacant or unallotted plots in HSIIDC estates, IMTs and licensed industrial parks; new industrial colony licences; areas outside Controlled Area limits Intermediate Plots already allotted in HSIIDC estates, IMTs and private industrial parks; areas between municipal limits and Controlled Area limits Core Areas within municipal limits not covered above If the project land spans two categories, the category with the larger share of the land applies. Subsidy Difference Between Existing and Fresh Industrial Plots One result is easy to miss. A unit on an already allotted plot in an IMT is Intermediate (6%). The same unit on a fresh, unallotted plot in that IMT is Sub-Prime (7%, with a higher ceiling). Mandatory Eligibility Conditions for Haryana Interest Subsidy ZED Silver Certification Requirement ZED Silver certificate. The applicant must hold ZED certification of at least Silver level when it applies, for either benefit. Few of our clients hold it today. Getting it started now is the single most useful step. Financial, Operational and Regulatory Compliance Conditions The other conditions are familiar, but each one can hold up a claim: Clean record: neither the unit nor its promoters may be wilful defaulters under RBI rules, blacklisted, or on the State’s restrictive list. Running unit: the unit must be in regular operation when the money is released. A closed unit gets nothing. Land and approvals: a sale deed, a registered lease of at least 15 years, or an HSIIDC/IMT Regular Letter of Allotment; CLU or NOC; HSPCB Consent to Establish and Consent to Operate. Bank certificate: the bank must confirm that repayments are regular and that no penal interest has been charged. Start date: the date of commercial production is the date of the first sale invoice for an eligible product. The DETC verifies it. Trial sales count; trading and scrap sales do not. Special-category ownership: at least 51% must be held by the woman, SC or other category owner. SC, Divyang, transgender, ex-servicemen and Agniveer owners must be Haryana residents, verified through the Parivar Pehchan Patra or service documents. Overall limit: everything received under this policy plus any Government of India scheme for the same project cannot exceed 100% of fixed capital investment. How to Apply for Haryana Interest Subsidy Scheme 2026 Annual Claim Filing Procedure and Deadline The claim is made once a year on the Invest Haryana Single Window portal. It must be filed within three months of the end of the financial year being claimed. For FY 2026-27, the deadline is 30 June 2027. Until the new portal is working, the Directorate may accept claims by e-mail. Documents Required for Interest Subsidy and Export Credit Claims The documents needed are: the application form (Annexure I); the loan or export credit sanction letter; the IEC, for exporters; registration documents of the applicant; land documents, CLU and HSPCB consents; Udyam and GST certificates; the ZED certificate; a CA certificate (Annexure II); a bank certificate (Annexure IIIA for term loans, IIIB for export credit); a notarised affidavit on ₹100 stamp paper (Annexure IV); a board resolution or power of attorney. Application Scrutiny, Rectification and Sanction The Directorate raises all its objections in one go, within 10 working days. The applicant then has 30 working days, and only one chance, to fix them. A verification agency may visit the site to check the machinery. The Additional Director, MSME sanctions the claim. Appeal Procedure and Condonation of Delay Appeals go first to the Director General, MSME and then to the Administrative Secretary, each within 30 days. Delays in filing can be condoned: up to 3 months by the DG, and up to 6 months by the Administrative Secretary. Recovery, Compound Interest and Debarment for False Claims A false claim is costly. The full amount must be refunded with 12% compound interest, and the unit can be barred from State incentives and government purchases. CA Certificate Requirements Under Haryana Interest Subsidy Scheme The certificate (Annexure II) goes on our letterhead with the membership number, FRN and UDIN. For a company, the statutory auditor must sign it. Financial and Operational Particulars to Be Certified It covers: the loan drawn and principal and interest repaid during the year; interest worked out at the applicable rate; investment in plant and machinery, and turnover; for existing exporters, the export share of turnover; the date of commercial production, and confirmation that the unit is still operating; confirmation that every government subsidy received or applied for has been disclosed. Personal Liability of the Certifying Chartered Accountant The certificate carries a personal liability clause. We should insist on complete supporting papers before signing. Comparison of HEEP 2020 and Haryana Interest Subsidy Scheme 2026 The last general interest subsidy came under HEEP 2020. It covered loans sanctioned from 1 January 2021 to 31 December 2025. Particulars HEEP 2020 Interest Subsidy New scheme (2026) Where B, C and D category blocks only Whole State, by area category, including city areas Rate 5% to 8% (start-ups 8%) 5%, 6% or 7%, plus 1% for special categories Ceiling ₹20 lakh a year ₹20 lakh a year (₹25 lakh in Prime and Sub-Prime) Period 3 to 7 years depending on category 3 years Existing units Only for a one-time expansion of at least 50% in plant and machinery Any loan for new machinery Quality condition None ZED Silver mandatory Export credit Not covered 3% (4% for special categories), up to ₹10 lakh a year In short, the new scheme reaches far more units, including existing units in urban areas. In exchange it pays for a shorter period and asks for ZED Silver. Possible Gap in Loan Eligibility Dates and PADMA Interest Subsidy Possible gap in loan dates. Loans sanctioned between 1 January and 28 July 2026 seem to fall in a gap between the two schemes. For such clients, the PADMA Interest Subsidy was an alternative. It provided 6% interest subsidy (ceiling ₹20 lakh in the cluster, ₹10 lakh outside) and was previously scheduled to remain open until 16 August 2028. However, the Haryana Government, through its corrigendum dated 18 November 2026, has closed the PADMA Interest Subsidy Scheme for receipt of fresh applications with immediate effect. Clients with loans falling within the above period should therefore examine whether any transitional relief is available. The no-double-benefit rule still applies. Other Schemes Under Haryana Progressive MSME & Export Promotion Policy 2026 Two more schemes notified under the same policy on 16 November 2026 include: an Adoption of AI Solutions/Labs Scheme, which reimburses part of the cost of AI tools or an in-house AI lab; a Haryana MSE Insurance Scheme, which reimburses part of the premium on insuring factory assets. The State Budget 2026-27 had already promised an insurance scheme for small traders and MSMEs. We have not yet seen the gazette copies of these two schemes, and will send a separate note once we have. Capital Investment, Freight, Rooftop Solar and Employment Subsidies The Directorate of MSME has also listed separately notified schemes covering other benefits announced under the policy. These include the capital subsidy, freight assistance, rooftop solar assistance under the Greening & Sustainable Development Scheme and the employment incentive. Claims under these schemes must be examined with reference to their respective notified guidelines. Open Questions and Next Steps for Haryana Manufacturing MSEs Interpretational Issues Requiring Clarification From the Directorate A few points in the gazette need clarification from the Directorate: whether the 5%, 6% or 7% is limited to the interest actually charged by the bank; how the three-year period “from commercial operations” works for an existing unit that only buys new machinery; from which date the special-category ownership must be in place. Practical Action Checklist for Manufacturing MSMEs and Exporters For clients, the practical steps are: 1. Start the ZED Silver certification now. 2. Check the area category of each factory on the Single Window Portal. 3. Ask the bank to state “purchase of new plant and machinery” clearly in the sanction letter. 4. Exporters should keep monthly export turnover records to support the 25% test. 5. Diarise 30 June 2027 for the first claim.
