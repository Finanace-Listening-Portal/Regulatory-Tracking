Articles 1 min read Government may link motor insurance premiums to traffic violations The road transport ministry is gearing up to introduce initiatives to lower the incidence of uninsured vehicles on Indian roads. Key proposals include establishing a unified insurance premium for different vehicle types. Furthermore, traffic violations recorded against vehicle owners could potentially affect their insurance costs. A trial of the 'no insurance, no fuel' strategy will be initiated at select fuel stations. The proposed changes are aimed at encouraging more vehicle owners to maintain valid insurance coverage and improving compliance with the Motor Vehicles Act. Advt The ministry is also examining options to revise the base insurance rates for specified vehicle categories, including electric vehicles, as part of a broader review of the existing premium structure.Insurance data show that nearly 25 crore active vehicles have valid insurance, which is around 81 per cent of the vehicles that are currently running across the country. While over 90 per cent of heavy commercial vehicles have valid insurance, it's around 88 per cent in the case of cars. Compliance is lowest at around 57 per cent in the case of three-wheelers, and it's around 80 per cent in the case of two-wheelers.
