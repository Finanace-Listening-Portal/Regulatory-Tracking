Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Ram Chandra Choudhary Vs ITO (ITAT Jaipur) Bona Fide Unawareness of Appellate Order Justifies Condonation: ₹90 Lakh Property Addition Restored for Fresh Hearing The Legal Principle The Jaipur Tribunal held that bona fide unawareness of an appellate order constituted sufficient cause for condoning delay, where earlier hearing notices had been communicated to the counsel’s email address but the final order was not received through that channel. It condoned the 53-day delay and separately restored the appeal concerning a ₹90,04,708 addition under section 69 to the CIT(A) for fresh adjudication. The relief was procedural. Neither the property-investment addition nor the reopening proceedings were finally invalidated on merits. Background of the Property Addition The assessee, a senior citizen and agriculturist belonging to a rural area, had declared income of ₹3,36,670 for Assessment Year 2016–17. Information available on the Department’s Insight Portal indicated that he had purchased immovable property worth ₹84,55,108 during the year. The Assessing Officer considered the investment unexplained and initiated reassessment proceedings. The eventual addition was ₹90,04,708, including registration charges, stamp fees and other amounts forming part of the property investment. According to the assessment record, the assessee did not comply with the notices issued during reassessment. The source of investment consequently remained unexplained, and the amount was added under section 69. The CIT(A) sustained the assessment, leading to the Tribunal appeal. Why the Tribunal Appeal Was Delayed The assessee’s appeal before the Tribunal was filed on 25 March 2026, with a delay of 53 days. In the condonation application, he explained that hearing notices during the first appellate proceedings had been sent to his counsel’s email address. He and his counsel therefore believed that further communications, including the appellate order, would arrive through the same channel. However, the CIT(A)’s order dated 24 November 2025 was not received on that email address. The assessee stated that he was unaware of the order until his counsel checked the Income Tax portal in the second week of March 2026. After discovering the adverse decision, the appeal was prepared and filed promptly. The explanation was thus based on delayed knowledge of the order and action taken after discovery, rather than an intentional decision to allow limitation to expire. Revenue Alleged Laxity; Tribunal Accepted Sufficient Cause The Departmental Representative opposed condonation, contending that the explanation showed laxity in pursuing the appellate remedy. The Tribunal examined the submissions and material and accepted that the delay resulted from bona fide non-awareness of the order. It also took account of the assessee’s responses to notices during the appellate proceedings when assessing the credibility of the delay explanation. Following Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag v. Mst. Katiji & Others [1987] 167 ITR 471 (SC), the Tribunal emphasised that “sufficient cause” should receive a liberal construction advancing substantial justice. It noted that delay should not automatically be presumed deliberate or attributable to culpable negligence, and that ordinarily a litigant gains nothing by filing an appeal late. The 53-day delay was accordingly condoned. Responding to Notices Was Distinct From Arguing the Merits After admitting the appeal, the Tribunal examined how the substantive dispute had been dealt with. The CIT(A) had decided the matter on the existing record because the assessee had not furnished written submissions addressing the merits. His response had essentially sought restoration of the matter. This distinction matters. The condonation discussion records responses to appellate notices, while the merits discussion records the absence of substantive explanations supporting the case. The Tribunal also noted non-compliance before the Assessing Officer. Thus, the source of the property investment had not been effectively explained at either stage. Counsel requested another opportunity to present the case before the CIT(A). Fresh Hearing Granted Without Deciding the Addition Considering that the assessee’s case had remained substantively unheard, the Tribunal restored the matter to the CIT(A) in the interest of justice. The first appellate authority was directed to adjudicate the appeal afresh after providing a due opportunity of hearing and then pass an order in accordance with law. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. In seeking condonation, the assessee had also asserted that he was only a 50% joint owner of the property. However, the Tribunal did not determine that claim or reduce the addition on that basis. Similarly, the grounds challenging reopening remained for adjudication in the restored proceedings. Author’s Comments Delayed awareness and failure to substantiate an appeal are separate questions. The Tribunal accepted the explanation for late filing while recognising that the underlying investment still required examination. The remand gives the assessee an opportunity to establish ownership share, actual investment and its source through relevant records. It does not establish that the entire addition was excessive or that only half could lawfully be assessed. The decision also highlights the practical importance of monitoring appellate communications. Reliance on an established email channel explained the delay here, but the substantive defence must still be presented once the opportunity is restored. A fresh hearing provides the forum for evidence; it does not replace that evidence. Cases Discussed Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag & Anr. Vs Mst. Katiji & Ors. (Supreme Court)– (1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC); (1987) 2 SCC 107. We have heard both the parties. 6. The law governing condonation of delay is now well settled. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag & Anr. v. Mst. Katiji & Ors.(1987) 167 ITR 471 (SC) laid down that the expression “sufficient cause” occurring in the Limitation Act should receive a liberal construction so as to advance substantial justice. The Court held that when substantial justice and technical considerations are pitted against each other, the cause of substantial justice deserves to be preferred. The Court further observed that there is no presumption that delay is deliberate or occasioned on account of culpable negligence or mala fides and that ordinarily a litigant does not stand to benefit by lodging an appeal belatedly. 7. We have considered the submissions and the material available on record. Considering that the assessee had duly complied with all the notices issued during the appellate proceedings and that the delay occurred on account of bona fide non-awareness of the impugned order, we are satisfied that there was sufficient cause for the delay. We accordingly condone the delay of 53 days in the filing of the present appeal before us. Order was pronounced in the Open Court. 8. Taking up now the appeal for hearing. 9. The facts of the case are that, for the impugned year, the assessee’s case was reopened for assessment by issuing notice u/s 148 of the Act, since there was information in the INSIGHT portal under the CRIU/VRU Category that the assessee had purchased an immovable property worth Rs.84,55,108/- during the year, which transaction was not disclosed in the return filed for the impugned year, in which income of Rs.3,36,670/- had been declared by the assessee. The Assessing Officer formed reason to believe that investment in property was made by the AO from unexplained sources and income to the said extent has escaped assessment. Accordingly, he assumed jurisdiction to reopen the case of the assessee by issuing notice u/s 148 of the Act. Subsequently, order was passed u/s 147 of the Act making addition of Rs.90,04,708/- on account of investment in immovable property inclusive of registration charges, stamp fees etc., the source of which remained unexplained. 13. CIT(A), the assessee has made no submissions on the merits of the case. 14. In the light of the above, since the assessee has remained unheard all along, in the interest of justice, we consider it fit to restore the issue back to the file of the Ld. CIT(A) with the direction to adjudicate the appeal of the assessee afresh after giving due opportunity of hearing and thereafter to pass an order in accordance with law. 15. In effect, appeal of the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes.
