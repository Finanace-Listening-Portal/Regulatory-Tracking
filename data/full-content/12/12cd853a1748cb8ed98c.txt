Most companies expect revenue growth above 5% Business sentiment remains positive, with 60% of companies expecting revenue growth of more than 5% in 2026-27, according to the survey. However, only 37% anticipate double-digit growth. At the same time, 62% of companies are budgeting salary increases above 9%, indicating that pay expectations remain resilient. The survey also estimates real wage growth — salary increases adjusted for inflation — at 5.1% in 2027, compared with 4.3% in 2026. While headline salary increases remain close to 9%, inflation and rupee depreciation could affect the real value of these increases, particularly when compared with earnings in other markets. AI skills and productivity reshape workforce spending Employers are increasingly directing workforce investment towards skills and productivity as artificial intelligence becomes more widely used in the workplace. Established adoption of AI productivity tools increased to 56% in 2026 from 46.1% in 2025, with usage rising across every job cluster measured by the survey. Around 60% of employers are prioritising employee upskilling. Meanwhile, talent premiums for roles in artificial intelligence, machine learning and data science have increased by approximately 10% from 2025, reflecting demand for specialised capabilities. “The rapid adoption of AI signals a shift from experimentation to enterprise-wide application. The next priority is translating that momentum into measurable productivity and growth,” Chaudhary said. Wage Code compliance could add to costs Employers are also preparing for the cost implications of the Wage Code. According to Aon, 65.4% of employers are already compliant, while another 20.9% expect to be ready within six months. However, 42.8% anticipate payroll cost increases of at least 1%. Aon said employers need to assess the impact on workforce costs, align changes to cost to company with their rewards philosophy, and communicate the transition clearly to employees.
