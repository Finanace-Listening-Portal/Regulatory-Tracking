Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Orient Color Art Printers Private Limited Vs Commissioner of GST & Central Excise (Appeals) (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madras High Court disposed of a batch of writ petitions concerning denial or reversal of input tax credit (ITC) arising from differences between Form GSTR-2A and Form GSTR-3B. The central issue was whether mismatches alone justified disallowance and, particularly, whether tax officers complied with the reconciliation procedure prescribed in CBIC Circular Nos. 183/15/2022-GST and 193/05/2023-GST. The Court did not decide the constitutional validity of Section 16(2)(c), Section 16(2)(aa), or the applicable rules, as these provisions were not challenged. For the representative petition, W.P.(MD) No. 5198 of 2024, an audit for July 2017 to March 2018 disclosed an ITC mismatch of ₹8,09,334. A show-cause notice dated 17 February 2021 led to an order dated 17 June 2022 reversing the disputed credit. In appeal, the taxpayer produced Chartered Accountant certificates supporting ₹1,64,722, which the appellate authority accepted, but upheld reversal of the remaining ₹6,44,612. As the appellate tribunal was not functioning, the High Court entertained the writ petition notwithstanding the statutory appellate remedy. The petitioners argued that the original GST framework contemplated automated invoice matching under Sections 37, 38 and 42 and Rules 69–72, while the prescribed matching machinery was never fully operationalised. They contended that provisional self-assessed credit under the former Section 41 could not be defeated simply because the system did not permit verification of the supplier’s tax payment. The Revenue maintained that ITC is subject to statutory eligibility conditions, including possession of tax invoices, receipt of supplies, filing of returns and actual payment of tax to the Government. On the first issue, the Court applied the Supreme Court ruling in Union of India v. Bharti Airtel Ltd., (2022) 4 SCC 328. Taxpayers are required to maintain primary books and documents and self-assess their ITC eligibility; GSTR-2A is a facilitator, not a substitute for statutory records. Non-operation of the originally envisaged return-matching system did not invalidate the statutory framework or confer an unconditional entitlement to credit. The Court also referred to a Madras High Court Division Bench decision dated 9 May 2025 concerning Rule 36(4). On the second issue, the Court examined CBIC Circular No. 183/15/2022-GST dated 27 December 2022 and Circular No. 193/05/2023-GST dated 17 July 2023. These prescribe documentary verification of certain historical GSTR-2A/GSTR-3B differences, subject to the relevant period and Rule 36(4) limits. Under Circular 183, where the annual difference for a supplier exceeds ₹5 lakh, the proper officer must ask for a prescribed Chartered Accountant certificate; for a difference up to ₹5 lakh, the officer must ask for a supplier certificate. The Court emphasised that the expression “shall ask” places an affirmative procedural obligation on the proper officer to inform taxpayers about the required reconciliation route and evidence. The authorities had not separately and expressly communicated the certificate-based mechanism and prescribed certificate requirements to the petitioners. The Court held that this failure breached binding CBIC instructions and amounted to procedural arbitrariness. It therefore set aside the impugned assessment and adjudication orders, allowing the authorities to reconsider the ITC claims in accordance with the circulars and the amendments to Section 16, after providing a reasonable hearing. Reconsideration was directed within four months from uploading of the web copy. No costs were awarded, and connected miscellaneous petitions were closed. The ruling grants procedural relief and fresh consideration; it does not finally allow every disputed ITC claim. Cases Discussed Union of India v. A reading of the above circulars would show that whenever there is a discrepancy in the Input Tax Credit claim between GSTR-3B and GSTR-2A the same ought to be resolved in accordance with the method and manner provided inCircular no. 183/15/2022-GST and 193/05/2023-GST. 22. With the insertion of clause (aa), (ba) and amendment to clause (c) to subsection (2) to Section 16 a taxable person would have no right to claim Input Tax Credit until and unless it complies with clause (aa) to subsection (2) to Section 16. 23. However, an examination of the Circulars 183 & 193 also reveal that there are lapses on the part of the Respondent authorities to adhere to the procedure prescribed by the Board in exercise of the powers conferred under Section 168(1) of the Act. a. Clause 4.1.1 of the Circular No.183 referred supra provides that in case where difference between the ITC claimed in Form GSTR-3B and available in Form GSTR-2A of the registered person in respect of a supplier for the said financial year exceeds Rs.5 Lakhs, the proper officer shall ask the registered person to produce a certificate from the Chartered Accountant of the Supplier as prescribed therein. b. Clause 4.1.2 of the Circular provides that where the difference between the ITC claimed in Form GSTR-3B and that available in Form GSTR-2A of the registered person in respect of a supplier for the said financial year is upto 5 lakhs, the proper office shall ask the claimant to produce a certificate from the concerned supplier as prescribed therein. (c ) Therefore, the Circular places the onus squarely on the proper officer in the process of verification of Section 16(2)(C) to first intimate the taxpayer of the discrepancy between Form GSTR-2A and Form GSTR-3B and then, more importantly, to ask the registered person to produce certificates under the Circulars either from the CA or from the supplier depending on the prescribed monetary criteria. d. It is the proper officer who has been obliged to intimate the assessee of the process of resolution through production of certificates and to aid the assessee in such resolution. e. The proper officer must expressly intimate the assessee and the mandate imposed on the proper officer is clear by use of the term “shall ask”. f. Since in the impugned assessment proceedings there has not been any separate and express intimation to the petitioners highlighting the mechanism of reconciliation through production of certificates and the form/stipulations which such certificates must satisfy, it amounts to violation of the procedures as prescribed by the Board and binding on the proper officers. Consequently, the impugned orders are liable to be set aside on the ground of procedural arbitrariness. 24. The present Writ Petitions are disposed of setting aside the impugned orders of assessment/adjudication and granting liberty to the respondent authority to reconsider the claim of input tax credit by the petitioners in accordance withCircular no. 183/15/2022-GSTand 193/05/2023-GST and the amendments made to Section 16 of the Act after affording the petitioners a reasonable opportunity of hearing within a period of 4 months from the date of uploading of web copy without waiting for the receipt of certified copy. No costs. Consequently, connected miscellaneous petitions are closed.
