Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Delhi Hardware Stores Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) 270A Penalty Cannot Rest Solely on Filing a Return U/s 148 Where the Explanation Is Bona Fide &amp; All Material Facts Are Disclosed Summary: The ITAT Delhi, in Delhi Hardware Stores Vs ITO, deleted a penalty of ₹9,29,928 levied under section 270A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, for alleged under-reporting of income in consequence of misreporting. The Tribunal held that the assessee’s explanation for not filing its return within the prescribed time was bona fide and supported by evidence of disputes among the partners. The firm’s rental income had suffered TDS, its return filed in response to a reassessment notice was accepted without any addition, and the material particulars were disclosed to the Assessing Officer. Reassessment Completed at the Returned Income Delhi Hardware Stores, a partnership firm, did not file its return within the time prescribed under section 139(1) for AY 2019–20. The Assessing Officer received information that the firm had earned rent of ₹24,43,990 and initiated reassessment proceedings. After an order under section 148A(d) dated 23 March 2023 and the issue of notice under section 148, the firm filed its return on 12 April 2023, declaring total income of ₹14,89,910. During reassessment, the AO examined the bank statement, the lease agreement with Punjab and Sind Bank, and the computation of income. These showed actual rental receipts of ₹21,28,440. The firm had disclosed the rent and claimed the 30% standard deduction. The AO accepted the returned income in full and completed the reassessment without making any addition. Why the AO Levied Penalty at 200% The AO invoked section 270A(2)(b) because the firm had first furnished its return for the year in response to a section 148 notice and the assessed income exceeded the maximum amount not chargeable to tax. He treated the original non-filing as suppression of facts, characterised the default as under-reporting in consequence of misreporting, and levied a penalty of ₹9,29,928, calculated at 200% of the tax on the alleged under-reported income. The firm explained that disputes among the partners had prevented timely filing and furnished a court order to support its explanation. The AO rejected the explanation and held that the firm did not qualify for the exclusion under section 270A(6). He also observed that the reasonable-cause protection in section 273B did not extend to section 270A. The CIT(A), NFAC, confirmed the penalty. Partner Dispute Supported by Court Records Before the Tribunal, the firm submitted that its business activity had come to a standstill amid disputes among the partners over the division of the dissolved firm’s assets. The rent arose under an existing lease, and the tenant bank had deducted tax at source and reported the payment. The firm also relied on its history of regularly filing returns and explained that the omission concerned the year under appeal. The Revenue argued that the income would have escaped assessment had the AO not issued the section 148 notice. The Tribunal examined the record of the partnership dispute, including the Delhi High Court order dated 2 November 2023 and the appointment of a sole arbitrator. It accepted that the dispute explained the failure to file the return on time. It also noted that the firm had filed the return promptly after receiving the notice and that the AO had accepted the income and the particulars furnished in support of it. Bona Fide Explanation and Full Disclosure Under Section 270A(6)(a) The Tribunal examined section 270A(6)(a), which excludes from under-reported income an amount for which the assessee offers a bona fide explanation and discloses all material facts to substantiate it. The Tribunal found both elements present. The court record supported the reason for the missed filing, and the reassessment record showed that the disclosed income and supporting particulars had been accepted. The Tribunal also took into account the firm’s past compliance and viewed the omission as an isolated default. Although the lower authorities relied on the filing of the return only after notice under section 148, the Tribunal held that this circumstance, by itself, did not trigger the penalty on the facts before it. It deleted the entire penalty under section 270A and allowed the appeal. Author’s Comments The distinction between section 270A(2)(b) and section 270A(6)(a) matters in cases where a return is first furnished under section 148. Under clause (2)(b), the starting comparison is between the assessed income and the maximum amount not chargeable to tax. Therefore, acceptance of the returned income without an addition does not, by itself, answer the penalty question. In this case, that acceptance supported the finding of full disclosure, while the court record supplied independent evidence for the explanation. The 200% rate also makes the charge of misreporting significant. The AO inferred suppression from the original filing omission. The Tribunal’s findings showed that the rent had already been reported by the tenant through TDS, the firm had furnished its lease and banking particulars, and the AO had accepted the resulting computation. For a similar penalty appeal, the most persuasive record would connect the documented cause of non-filing with the disclosures made during reassessment. Here, the two sets of evidence together supported relief under section 270A(6)(a). FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. This appeal is filed by the assessee against the order of ld. Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals)/National Faceless Appeal Centre, Delhi (NFAC), Delhi [“Ld. CIT (A)”, for short] dated 03.02.2026 for the AY 2019-20. 2. Brief facts of the case are, as per the information available with the Assessing Officer, assessee has received rent amounting to Rs.24,43,990/- during the assessment year 2019-20. But assessee has not filed any return of income. Accordingly, the case of the assessee was reopened by issue of notice under section 148A and B of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (for short ‘the Act’). Subsequently, an order under section 148A(d) was passed on 23.03.2023. Subsequently notice under section 148 was issued and served on the assessee. In response, assessee filed its return of income on 12.04.2023 declaring total income of Rs.14,89,910/-. The assessment was completed based on the information submitted by the assessee and accordingly, returned income was admitted and the assessment was completed. Since the assessee has not filed the return of income and only filed upon issued and served of notice under section 148, the AO initiated penalty proceedings under section 270A of the Act. During penalty proceedings, assessee filed two submissions dated 13.02.2024 and 22.04.2024. The latest submission of the assessee dated 22.04.2024 is reproduced below:- “There is dispute among the partners and matter regarding the firm is pending in the Courts, so the return could not be fled in time and after receiving notice u/s 148 the return has been fled and it has also been assessed as per return filed vide order dated 24.0.12024. Photocopy of Assessment Order and Court Order are enclosed for your record & reference. That due to the above facts the penalty notice may kindly be dropped.” 3. After considering the above submissions, the AO rejected the submissions of the assessee by observing as under :- “3.7.1 As per Information received from insight portal assessee had received rent amounting to Rs.24,43,990/- during the AY 2019-20, while, no tax return for said A.Y had filed by the assessee. Therefore, the income from above transactions remained undisclosed. During the assessment proceedings, on perusal of copy of bank statement, lease agreement with Punjab and Sind Bank and computation of total income furnished by the assessee, it was noticed that the assessee had received rent income from Punjab and Sind Bank of Rs. 21,28,440/- only. And said income disclosed in its return of income filed in response to notice u/s 148 of the I T Act after claiming 30% standard deduction of Rs. 6,38,532/-. Therefore, the AO had accepted return income of Rs.14,89,910/- as assessed income. 3.7.2 Vide the show cause notices as per para-2, the assessee was requested to furnish its explanation. In response, the assessee has furnished its replies which have also been reproduced above. On perusal of the said replies, the same are found to be not acceptable as per the discussion made in the para 3.5 of this order. It is also pertinent to mention that the assessee has not filed any appeal before the Ld.CIT(A) which shows that the assessee has accepted the assessment order. 3.7.3 In view of the above, it can be seen that despite having substantial income during the year, the assessee failed to file return of income without reasonable cause. It show that the assessee has suppressed the facts by not filing return of income related to its income which had resulted into under-reporting of its income in consequence of misreporting of income. Therefore, it is a fit case for levy of penalty u/s 270A of the Act. 3.7.4 As per section 270A(2)(b) of the I T Act, the assessee has under reported its income, as the income assessed is greater than the maximum amount not chargeable to tax. Section 270A(2)(b) is reproduce hereunder: “(2) A person shall be considered to have under-reported his income, if- 1. the income assessed is greater than the income determined in the return processed under clause (a) of sub-section (1) of section 143; 2. b) the income assessed is greater than the maximum amount not chargeable to tax, where no return of income has been furnished or where return has been furnished for the first time under section 148; 3.7.5 The assessee has not having any shelter of exclusion as per section 270A(6) of the I T Act. Section 270A(6) is reproduce hereunder: “(6) The under-reported income. for the purposes of this section, shall not include the following, namely:- (a) the amount of income in respect of which the assessee offers an explanation and the Assessing Officer or the Commissioner (Appeals) or the Commissioner or the Principal Commissioner, as the case may be, is satisfied that the explanation is bona fide and the assessee has disclosed all the material facts to substantiate the explanation offered; (b) the amount of under-reported income determined on the basis of an estimate, if the accounts are correct and complete to the satisfaction of the Assessing Officer or the Commissioner (Appeals) or the Commissioner or the Principal Commissioner, as the case may be, but the method employed is such that the income cannot properly be deduced therefrom; (c) the amount of under-reported income determined on the basis of an estimate, if the assessee has, on his own, estimated a lower amount of addition or disallowance on the same issue, has included such amount in the computation of his income and has disclosed all the facts material to the addition or disallowance; (d) the amount of under-reported income represented by any addition made in conformity with the arm’s length price determined by the Transfer Pricing Officer, where the assessee had maintained information and documents as prescribed under section 92D, declared the international transaction under Chapter X, and, disclosed all the material facts relating to the transaction; and (e) the amount of undisclosed income referred to in section 271AAB.” 3.7.6 The shelter of reasonable cause u/s 273B of the I T Act, is not available for penalty u/s 270A of the I T Act. Section 273B is reproducing hereunder: “Notwithstanding anything contained in the provisions of clause (b) of sub-section (1) of section 271, section 271A, section 271AA, section 271B, section 271BA, section 271BB, section 271C, section 271CA, section 271D, section 271E, section 271F, section 271FA, section 271 FB, section 271 G, clause (c) or clause (d) of sub-section (1) or sub-section (2) of section 272A, sub-section (1) of section 272AA or section 272B or sub-section (1) or sub-section (1A) of section 272BB or sub-section (1) of section 272BBB or clause (b) of sub-section (1) or clause (b) or clause (c) of sub-section (2) of section 273, no penalty shall be imposable on the person or the assessee, as the case may be, for any failure referred to in the said provisions if he proves that there was reasonable cause for the said failure.” 3.7.7 In view of the above facts and circumstances, I am satisfied that the assessee has committed default of under reporting its income in consequence of misreporting of income. I am also satisfied that, this is fit case for levy of penalty u/s 270A of the I T Act. The penalty is leviable in respect of undisclosed house property income of Rs.14,89,910/- @ 200% of the tax sought to be evaded is worked out at Rs.9,29,928/-. Therefore, I levy penalty of Rs.9,29,928/- u/s 270A of the I T Act for underreporting of income in consequence of misreporting of income, on the assessee for the said default.” 4. Aggrieved with the above order, assessee preferred an appeal before the NFAC Delhi. After considering the detailed submissions of the assessee, ld. CIT(A) sustained the penalty. 5. Aggrieved with the above order, assessee is in appeal before us raising following ground of appeal :- “On the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law, the ld. CIT(A) of NFAC, Delhi erred in confirming the penalty imposed by the Assessment Unit of the National Faceless Assessment Centre u/s 270A of the Act.” 6. At the time of hearing, ld. AR of the assessee submitted as under :- “6. It may also to be specifically noted that the appellant firm is not a regular defaulter. In the impugned penalty order the AU expressly concedes that the returns for A.Y. 2022-23 and 2023-24 were filed by the firm. In that way, the failure to file the return for the subject year, is one time and is purely accidental which is forced due to exceptional circumstances. 7. It needs appreciation and recognition that the sole income of the year arose out of contractual agreement for letting out of premises to the bank. The income did not accrue or arise out of any positive act or effort of the partners to earn such income during the ear. There was a total lull in the business activities of the firm. And it is such a situation, that in the face of the inter se disputes between the parties, the task of filing the return was missed out. Indeed the AU also does not allege the carrying on of any business during the subject assessment year. The complete absence of business income is the best proof of dispute amongst the partners and the consequent lull in activities of the firm. 8. The AU levied the penalty on the charge that the income from rent was not brought to the notice of the Department by filing of a return of income. That is a lame complaint. The Income complained of during the year is from rent. Admittedly the rent has suffered TDS. The tenant bank has deducted TDS. It has also filed the regular follow up forms after making such deduction by intimating the name of the party from where rent, the TDS has been paid to the exchequer. In such scenario the AU could not have complained of the income of the appellant assessee not having been brought to its notice formally through the filing of a return of income by the appellant firm. 9. The penalty has been invoked and levied by the AU under the deeming provisions of clause (b) of sub-section (2) of section 270A. Such a deeming provision, existing in the Act, is to sub-serve the interest of Revenue in the event of a failure by the assessee to pay tax on the income after the requisition of a return. That is not the situation in the present case. The object of section 270A, as the title clearly suggests, is to levy penalty for under reporting and mis-reporting of income. Appellant has done none such. 10. Under-reporting of income is defined in sub-section (3) of section 270A. The exceptions to under-reporting of income is cited in sub-section (6) of that section. Mis-reporting of income is defined in sub-section (9). 11. Returning to sub-section (3) and particularly to sub-clause (b) of clause (i) there is seemingly no difference between the amount assessed and the amount returned. The AU concedes this in the last sub-para of para 4, where it notes as under: “Considering the above-mentioned/acts of the case and on the basis of submissions filed by the assessee, the reply submitted by the assessee is found acceptable. Hence, no addition made to the total income of the assessee.” 12. Apparently the Revenue has not suffered any loss due to the lack of the return at the prescribed time. Also nothing by way of tax remains unpaid or unremitted. 13. Sub-section (6) deals with exceptions to underreporting. Clause (a) provides for the bona fide explanation for exemption from the charge. The fact of the dispute between the partners; the anxiety and tension consequent thereto resulting in the failure to file the return in time, the forgetness caused due to the business itself lying in a stalemate all do constituent bona fide cause. 14. Mis-reporting as per sub-clause (9) includes misrepresentation or suppression of facts; or a failure to record investments; or mis claim of expenditure; or false entry in the books; or failure to record any receipt in the books of account; or failure to report any international transaction. Appellant’s case does not fall in any of the aforesaid categories. The AU also does not allege any such. It is thus not a case of misreporting as alleged by the AU. 15. The contention of the AU that there is no equivalent clause to section 273B in 270A is not correct. Sub-section (6) of Section 270A is the equivalent section of Section 273B of the Act. 16. Thus, on the ground that the income as assessed in the hands of the appellant firm was not an income which was un-known to the department or on which tax was not collected and for the further fact that there was a dispute between the partners closely followed by the Covid minace causing the omission to file the return are cogent reasons. They are all beyond the control of the partners of the firm resulting in omission as complained of by the AU. 17. Under the aforesaid circumstances the charge of the AU of underreporting and misreporting his fallacious both on facts and in law. The NFAC has ignored to consider and failed to pass an appropriate order. The position both on facts and law have been either misread or omitted to be read properly and correctly by the NFAC. It pleaded that the order of the NFAC confirming penalty as levied by AU under section 270A be quashed.” 7. On the other hand, ld. DR of the Revenue objected to the submissions of the assessee and submitted that the assessee has filed its return of income only upon issue of notice under section 148 of the Act. Otherwise the income was so declared by the assessee would not have been assessed. Therefore, the present case is squarely falls under-reporting of income. Therefore, penalty levied under section 270A is justified. 8. Considered the rival submissions and material placed on record. We observed that assessee has filed its return of income upon receipt of notice under section 148 of the Act and it also filed on record that the income declared by the assessee was assessed to tax without any deviation. It is brought to our notice that due to certain disputes among the partners, the return of income was not filed within the time limit specified under section 139(1) of the Act. During the hearing, ld. AR of the assessee brought to our notice that due to certain dispute on the aspect of division of the assets of the dissolved firm and the disputes are resolved with the appointment of sole arbitrator for adjudication of disputes between the parties which had arisen in the context of partnership deed dated 17.01.2019. Apart from that, we also noticed that assessee is a regular filer of return of income and further observed that the major income declared by the assessee in the return of income is rental income which admittedly suffered TDS and all the details of the deductee are already placed on record. Apparently it looks that the assessee has under-reported the income. However, the facts available on record indicate that the reason for not filing the return is due to certain disputes among the partners. It clearly shows that there is reasonable cause for not filing the return of income on time. Upon issue of notice, assessee has promptly filed return of income and declared the total income and majority of the income which was already suffered TDS. Since there is no deviation from income declared by the assessee which was duly accepted by the Revenue, we hold that initiation of penalty proceedings only on the basis of return filed by the assessee under section 148, the return which was submitted by the assessee was duly accepted and no deviations were made. We noticed that the lower authorities have rejected the submissions of the assessee on the basis that the income assessed is greater than the maximum amount not chargeable to tax. It is fact that assessee has not filed return of income for the year under consideration only but assessee was regularly filing its return of income in the past. As per clause 270A(2)(b) of the Act, the assessee has under-reported its income, as the income assessed is greater than the maximum amount not chargeable to tax, there were two conditions specified : (a) where no income has been furnished; or (b) where income has been furnished for the first time under section 148 of the Act. This clause clearly indicates that the penalty can be levied only for those assessees who are habitually non-filers of the return of income or who never filed return of income or filed the return of income first time upon issue of notice under section 148. In the present case, the assessee was regularly filing return of income. 9. Further, we observed that assessee has filed settlement order dated 02.11.2023 passed by Hon’ble Delhi High Court in the case of the assessee. It clearly demonstrates that there is a dispute among the partners which was ultimately settled by the Hon’ble High Court. Since the dispute was arisen between the partners, the relevant return of income was not filed for the year under consideration. It clearly shows that there is reasonable cause for the assessee for not filing the return of income on time. We further observed that ld. CIT(A) rejected the plea of the assessee which does not fall under section 270A sub-section (6) of the Act. On careful reading of the above sub-section, it indicates that there is an exception mentioned at clause (6)(a) that an amount of income which the assessee offers an explanation and the Assessing Officer/CIT(A) is satisfied that explanation is bonafide and assessee has disclosed all the material facts to substantiate the explanation offered. From the explanation submitted by the assessee, we noticed that all amount of income declared by the assessee in response to notice issued under section 148 was accepted (majority of income was already suffered TDS) by the lower authorities and all the material facts disclosed by the assessee to substantiate above said income was also accepted by the AO. Since the assessee has not filed the return of income under section 139(1) but filed the return of income only upon notice issued under section 148 only for the year under consideration, this itself will not trigger the penalty for under-reporting of income. Since the claim under sub-section (6)(a) of section 270A gives liberty to Assessing Officer/CIT(A) to accept or reject the explanation offered by the assessee as bonafide and disclosure of material facts, in this case, the above aspect was accepted by them but they are not satisfied only for the reason of non-filing of return of income u/s 139(1) but filed only upon issue of notice u/s 148 of the Act, they proceeded to levy the penalty. In our view, the explanation and reasonable cause submitted are acceptable. Therefore, the explanation offered by the assessee for not filing the return within the period specified under section 139(1) is reasonable. Therefore, we are inclined to delete the penalty levied under section 270A of the Act. 10. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced in the open court on this 18TH day of November, 2026.
