Execution of Capital Punishment-Unorthdox to Hanging-Legal Medicine Summary: The method of executing capital punishment in India raises important constitutional, legal and medical questions concerning human dignity, physical suffering and the limits of permissible punishment. The Supreme Court considered these issues in Rishi Malhotra v. Union of India, 2026 SCC OnLine SC 1602, where the petitioners challenged the constitutionality of execution by hanging and sought consideration of alternative methods. The Court declined to invalidate the existing statutory method, finding insufficient grounds to reconsider the earlier ruling upholding hanging, while leaving open the possibility of legislative or expert review. The article examines the statutory framework governing the death penalty under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, and Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, alongside the constitutional principles established in Jagmohan Singh, Bachan Singh and Machhi Singh. These judgments address the validity of capital punishment, sentencing safeguards, aggravating and mitigating circumstances, and the rarest-of-rare doctrine. From the perspective of legal medicine, the authors discuss alternative execution methods involving inert gases and examine international litigation concerning nitrogen-based executions, including Smith v. Hamm and Jeffery Lee v. Greg Lovelace. The discussion also considers helium-related oxygen deprivation and the serious medical consequences of paraquat poisoning, with reference to the Sharon Raj murder case in Kerala. The central concern is whether any method of execution can reliably eliminate pain, suffering and uncertainty while satisfying the constitutional protection of human dignity. The authors ultimately question the assumption that an alternative method would necessarily constitute a painless execution and emphasise the need for careful examination of legal, medical and ethical considerations before changing the existing method.
