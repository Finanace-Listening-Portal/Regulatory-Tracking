Articles 3 min read Bank of Baroda raises FY27 credit growth guidance to 13-15%; FCNR(B) to support H2 Bank of Baroda has raised its FY27 credit growth guidance to 13–15% from 12–14%, while increasing deposit growth guidance to 11–13%. The bank said strong H1 growth and liquidity mobilised through FCNR(B) deposits give it confidence on stronger H2 growth. The bank retained its net interest margin guidance at 2.75–2.95%, while indicating an upward bias in margins in the third and fourth quarters.“On the credit growth from 12% to 14% guidance, we are upsizing that to 13% to 15%. Similarly, the deposit guidance, we are given 10% to 12%, we are upsizing that to 11% to 13%,” MD & CEO Debadatta Chand told media during the bank’s Q2 FY27 earnings call on Friday. Advt Chand said the upgrade was supported by strong performance in the first two quarters, with the bank seeing advances growth of more than 15% in the June quarter and around 18% in the September quarter, while deposits grew around 16.5%.The bank also expects the December and March quarters to support growth, as these are typically its stronger quarters.FCNR(B) liquidity to support H2 advancesA key factor supporting the bank's confidence is the liquidity mobilised through the Reserve Bank of India's FCNR(B) deposit scheme.BoB has mobilised USD 8.08 billion through FCNR(B) deposits, with around 70% of the amount linked to leverage/advances under the scheme, Chand said.The bank has described the liquidity generated through the FCNR(B) mobilisation as “stored liquidity”, which it plans to use to support advances, particularly in December and March.“We have stored deposits, suppose I put it in that word, stored liquidity rather in that way. We used to fund the advances both in December and March,” Chand said.The bank had earlier said the FCNR(B) mobilisation had given it significant liquidity, and that it had not yet fully utilised the funds. Chand said the availability of this liquidity means funding is not expected to be a constraint as the bank looks to grow its loan book. Advt BoB has also said the FCNR(B) funds have been converted into rupees through the swap mechanism. On the USD 8 billion mobilisation, the bank estimated an annual interest obligation of around USD 350–400 million, while the interest earned on the leverage component would offset a substantial portion of that cost.Corporate pipelineThe bank's corporate loan pipeline currently stands at more than Rs 60,000 crore, comprising applications under processing and sanctions, management said.Domestic corporate advances grew around 6.4% year-on-year, but the bank said the relatively lower growth was partly a result of its pricing discipline. Its core corporate, excluding PSU exposure, grew around 12%, while mid-corporate advances grew 26%. Advt BoB expects its corporate loan book to grow by more than 10% in FY27.Retail and MSME growth also remained strong, with retail advances growing around 18.5% and MSME advances around 21% during the quarter. Management said demand across corporate, mid-corporate and MSME segments remained strong and was expected to continue through December and March.The bank has retained its credit-cost guidance at below 0.6% and slippage guidance at 1–1.25%. Its NIM guidance remains at 2.75–2.95%, with management expecting an upward bias in Q3 and Q4.BoB's global advances grew 18.3% year-on-year to Rs 15.12 lakh crore in Q2 FY27, while global deposits grew 16.4% to Rs 17.45 lakh crore.
