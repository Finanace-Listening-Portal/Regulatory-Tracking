Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Pawan Kumar Vs Initiating Officer (Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA, New Delhi) Benami Attachment Upheld as Retracted Statement Was Corroborated by Property Transfers and Financial Records Introduction The Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA, New Delhi, upheld the attachment of three immovable properties under the Prohibition of Benami Property Transactions Act, 1988, dismissing appeals filed by Pawan Kumar, his mother Urmila Devi and his wife Sonam. The Tribunal relied on the alleged benamidar’s admission, his financial profile, bank records and subsequent transfers to the appellants’ family. It held that the later retraction of the admission did not displace the corroborative material supporting the benami arrangement. Background of the Case The dispute concerned three agricultural properties in Farrukhabad, Uttar Pradesh, initially purchased in the name of Yashveer Singh. The Department alleged that he was merely the ostensible owner, while Pawan Kumar and his family were the beneficial owners. Pawan Kumar practised as an advocate and deed writer at Tehsil Kaimganj. The investigation examined his involvement in the property transactions, including the participation of his clerk as a witness. Following inquiry, the Initiating Officer passed an attachment order dated 19 March 2024 under Section 24(4)(a)(i). The Adjudicating Authority confirmed the attachment by its order dated 26 March 2025 under Section 26(3). The three appellants challenged that confirmation. Appellants’ Arguments The appellants contended that the Department had failed to establish that they provided consideration for the original purchases. They argued that allegations involving Yashveer Singh’s transactions with other persons could not establish a benami arrangement concerning these properties. According to them, Yashveer Singh possessed ancestral agricultural land and earned sufficient agricultural income. They relied on cultivation records, his wife’s statement and claimed receipts from ancestral land sales aggregating to ₹6.83 lakh. They also submitted that registered deeds and statements of persons connected with the purchases supported Yashveer Singh’s ownership. Although Yashveer Singh had implicated the appellants in a statement dated 4 September 2023, he subsequently retracted it through replies and affidavits. The appellants maintained that the adverse statement was false and had been made under pressure. Department’s Stand The Department argued that the transactions satisfied Section 2(9)(A) because the properties stood in Yashveer Singh’s name while the appellants provided consideration and enjoyed the beneficial interest. It relied on his sworn admission, alleged lack of independent financial capacity, the transaction pattern and subsequent transfers to the appellants’ family. The Department also alleged that the arrangement sought to circumvent restrictions on transfers of Scheduled Caste land under the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006. Yashveer Singh belonged to the Scheduled Caste category, and the lands originally belonged to Scheduled Caste persons. Subsequent Transfers Supported the Benami Finding The Tribunal examined the purchase and transfer history of the three properties. One property purchased in Yashveer Singh’s name for ₹1.30 lakh in October 2020 was subsequently transferred to Urmila Devi for ₹2 lakh in October 2023. Another property purchased for ₹2.50 lakh in March 2021 was transferred to Sonam and Pawan Kumar for ₹1.50 lakh in October 2021. The Tribunal found that no plausible explanation had been offered for this reduction in consideration. The third property, purchased for ₹1 lakh in May 2022, continued in Yashveer Singh’s name. The Tribunal viewed these transactions collectively as supporting the Department’s case that the apparent ownership did not reflect the actual arrangement. Bank Records Did Not Corroborate Independent Funding The Tribunal examined Yashveer Singh’s State Bank of India account for the relevant period. It found no entries corroborating the claim that he had funded the purchases independently. It specifically observed that neither debit nor credit entries substantiated the asserted purchase and subsequent sale of the properties from his own financial resources. The Tribunal also relied on the Adjudicating Authority’s findings concerning the mismatch between his financial profile and property dealings. The claimed sources of funds were not accepted as adequately substantiated in the circumstances. Retraction Did Not Displace Corroborated Admission The Tribunal rejected reliance on Yashveer Singh’s subsequent denial. It found that the appellants had failed to explain convincingly why the retraction should be accepted or demonstrate circumstances establishing that the earlier adverse statement was false. The admission was supported by the property transfers, financial records and surrounding circumstances. Consequently, the finding did not depend on an uncorroborated statement alone. The Tribunal also considered the land-transfer restrictions as relevant context supporting its inference that the arrangement was designed to circumvent those restrictions. Conclusion The Tribunal dismissed all three appeals, leaving the attachment confirmation undisturbed. The decision highlights the evidentiary significance of financial capacity, transaction history, subsequent family transfers and corroborated admissions in benami proceedings. On these facts, registered ownership and a later retraction were insufficient to rebut the material indicating that Yashveer Singh held the properties for the appellants’ benefit. Ld. Counsel for the Respondent submitted that Ld. AA held that the Initiating Officer had invoked Section 2(9)(A) of the Prohibition of Benami Property Transactions Act, 1988, which defines a benami transaction as one where a property is transferred to or held by a person, but the consideration for such property has been provided by another person, and the property is held for the immediate or future benefit of the person providing such consideration. The essential ingredients of this definition are (i) the transfer or holding of property by the benamidar for the benefit of the beneficial owner, and (ii) the flow of consideration from the beneficial owner to the original seller. In the instant case, both these conditions stand satisfied since the property is held in the name of Shri Yashveer Singh, while the consideration was provided by the Appellant Shri Pawan Kumar, Smt. Urmila Devi and Smt. Sonam, for their immediate or future benefit. Accordingly, the properties constitute benami property, the transactions in question amount to benami transactions, Shri Yashveer Singh is the benamidar, and the Appellants are the beneficial owners. Accordingly, Ld. AA confirmed the PAO dated 19.03.2024 under Section 24(4) of the PBPTA of the I.O. vide its Order dated 26.03.2025 under Section 26(3) of PBPTA, holding the property to be Benami Property, title holder thereof i.e., Shri Yashveer Singh to be Benamidar and the Appellants to be Beneficial Owners and further directed that the property shall not be transferred or otherwise dealt with, in any manner, except with the prior permission. The Ld. Ld. Counsel for the Respondent submitted that the statements of third parties (Sh. Raj Kumar, Smt. Parwati, Sh. Ishwar Dayal, etc.) about the financial status of Shri Yashveer Singh though described as hearsay by the Appellant, are relevant circumstantial evidence under the PBPTA. The statement of Shri Geeta Nand clearly establishes that the transaction was routed through an intermediary (Suraj) and that consideration was received in cash. The use of such intermediaries is a common feature in benami arrangements to conceal the true source of funds. The statement of Sh. Geeta Nand, seller of property, confirming receipt of consideration from the Appellant (possibly via Suraj), does not exclude the possibility that the Appellant (Beneficial Owner) provided the funds indirectly. Circumstantial evidence, coupled with Appellant’s bank records and financial profile, supports that the Appellant provided the funds, reinforcing the Benami nature of the transaction. Absence of adverse statements against the Appellants family members does not undermine the evidence against Shri Yashveer Singh. The Respondent’s financial incapacity, admissions, and circumstantial evidence collectively demonstrate that the impugned properties were purchased with funds provided by the Appellant, confirming the Benami arrangement. The statements of witnesses regarding payments in other references are merely corroborative of the pattern of Benami transactions, and Shri Yashveer Singh’s own admissions, coupled with documentary and financial evidence, clearly demonstrate that the Appellant (Beneficial Owner) provided the consideration for the purchase of the impugned properties. Ld. Counsel for the Respondent submitted that the statement dated 04.09.2023 of Shri Yashveer Singh recorded under Section 19 of PBPTA, wherein he admitted that the impugned properties were purchased by the Appellant and his family members in his name, was given on oath before the Initiating Officer carries high evidentiary value, whereas the subsequent replies and affidavit dated 07.03.2024 and 20.03.2024 retracting the said statement are clearly afterthoughts without any contemporaneous complaint of coercion and thus lack credibility. The alleged contradictions about transfer of property are immaterial since the fact remains that the Appellant himself submitted that properties stood in his name and later transferred to family members of the Appellant which only fortify the benami arrangement, particularly when Section 6 of PBPTA prohibits retransfer of benami property. Further the plea that the Appellant purchased the properties out of agricultural income, ancestral land sale, or soil sale proceeds is wholly unsubstantiated as no cogent documentary evidence such as bank statements, sale deeds of ancestral land, or income tax returns were ever produced to prove financial capacity, and mere oral assertions cannot outweigh the sworn statement dated 04.09.2023 corroborated by the statements of several independent witnesses who confirmed that Shri Yashveer Singh and his associates routinely purchased properties in his name. Therefore, the retraction has no evidentiary value and the cumulative material on record conclusively establishes that the impugned properties are benami in nature, beneficially owned by the Appellant and his family members. The Respondent pleaded to dismiss the Appeals. 13. We have considered the rival submissions and the material on record. The IO has provisionally attached on 19.03.2024 under Section 24(4)(a)(i) of PBPTA, the three properties as listed in the Table in Paragraph 1 of this Order. The inquiry conducted by the IO into the financial profile of Sh. Yashveer Singh and his statement recorded on 04.09.2023 under Section 19 of PBPTA reveal that the said three properties had been purchased in the name of Shri Yashveer Singh. The first of these properties of 0.04562 hectares was sold by Shri Sanjay Kumar and others for the consideration of Rs. 1,30,000/-, for which registration was done on 09.10.2020. The second of these properties measuring 0.041 hectares was sold by Shri Geeta Nand for the consideration of Rs. 1,00,000/- for which registration was done on 10.05.2022. The third of these properties measuring 0.0956 hectares was sold by Shri Gaya Prasad and others for the consideration of Rs. 2,50,000/- for which registration was done on 06.03.2021. The IO found that Shri Yashveer Singh did not have financial resources to fund such purchases. The statement dated 30.08.2022 of Shri Geeta Nand reveal that for the registration of the property measuring 0.041 hectares the deed writer and the witness to the deed was Shri Pawan Kumar, the Appellant herein. For the two properties sold by Shri Geeta Nand and Shri Sanjay Kumar, the witness to the registration deeds of both the properties was Shri Suraj Singh a clerk of Shri Pawan Kumar, the Appellant herein. The IO having reasons to believe had issued Show Cause Notice (SCN) under Section 24(1) and Section 24 (2) of PBPTA on 26.12.2023 and subsequently issued the PAO under Section 24 (3) of the PBPTA on 28.12.2023. In view of the inquires conducted including examination of financial profile and recording of statements, the IO passed the PAO under Section 24 (4)(a)(i) of PBPTA on 19.03.2024. Shri Yashveer Singh revealed that though these properties were in his name, these were actually owned by the Appellants Shri Pawan Kumar, his wife Smt. Sonam and his mother Smt. Urmila Devi. We further observe from the Table in Paragraph 1 of this Order that the two properties which were purchased in the name of Shri Yashveer Singh were subsequently sold to the family members of Shri Pawan Kumar Appellant herein. The property measuring 0.04562 hectares having been shown as purchased by Shri Yashveer Singh on 09.10.2020 for Rs. 1,30,000/- was found to have been bought by Smt. Urmila Devi, mother of Shri Pawan Kumar on 20.10.2023 for consideration of Rs. 2,00,000/-. The property measuring 0.0956 hectares which was bought in the name of Shri Yashveer Singh on 06.03.2021 for consideration of Rs. 2,50,000/- was found to be sold to Smt. Sonam and Shri Pawan Kumar on 20.10.2021 for consideration of Rs. 1,50,000/-. It is only the third property measuring 0.041 hectares which bought in the name of Shri Yashveer Singh for Rs. 1,00,000/- on 10.05.2022 that remained with Shri Yashveer Singh. The aforementioned transactions in themselves show their dubious nature. We observe that the property measuring 0.0956 hectares lost its value from Rs. 2,50,000/- to Rs. 1,50,000/- for which no plausible explanation has been offered. The mere transfer in the names of Smt. Sonam, wife of Shri Pawan Kumar, and in the name of Shri Pawan Kumar with such decline in value could have only been plausible if the property was already in the name of the said persons. The Sale Deed which is part of the record shows the transfer. It is also noteworthy that the property measuring 0.041 hectares remained in the name of Shri Yashveer Singh. We have also examined the copies of the bank account for the relevant period of Shri Yashveer Singh relating to his account in State Bank of India and we do not find any entry in the said account which can corroborate the said transactions having been funded by Shri Yashveer Singh. 14. In this regard, we cite the following critical findings made by the Ld. Competent Authority in Paragraphs 5.5.2 and 5.5.3.1 of the Impugned Order: “5.5.2 The crux of the submissions of the defendant D-1 (Shri Yashveer Singh) is that the transaction under question is a fair transaction, whereas D-1 has no financial capacity. On perusal of the property transactions, it emanated that D-1 works as a labourer. His residence was also allotted by the Govt. under “Sarkari Awaas Yojna”. This goes a long way to establish that D-1 never had the financial wherewithal to purchase the Benami Property, besides being a non-filer of ITR. D-1 has although claimed that he had income of about Rs. 8 lakh per annum by his own admission and about Rs. 4 to 4.5 lakh per annum as per his wife’s statement but he never filed ITR claiming that there was no need to file ITR as his income was below the threshold for filing ITR. However, contention of D-1 is found not to be correct since threshold income for filing ITR for year under reference was as Rs. 2,50,000/-. Hence, D-1 was required to file ITR, while he failed to do. xxxxx 5.5.3.1 With regard to financial creditworthiness of D- 1, it is seen that he is a non-filer of income tax and has no financial capacity. On perusal of the property transactions, it emanated that D-1 identifies himself as a small farmer with an annual income of 7-8 lakhs rupees and claims not to file ITR. However, he has been involved in significant land transactions, which are disproportionate to his declared income and financial capacity. This discrepancy raises questions about the actual source of funds for these transactions. During the course of departmental proceedings before the IO, D-1 has accepted that the impugned properties, initially registered under his name, were effectively under the control or later transferred to Shri Pawan Kumar and his family members i.e. D-2 to D-4. Shri Yashveer Singh admits that the financial considerations and obligations related to the properties’ purchase and registration were managed by Shri Pawan Kumar and his family members i.e. D-2 to D-4. This admission is a key feature of benami transactions, where the BD is the titular owner, but the BOs are responsible for the financial aspects. These admissions by D-1 proved a complex network of property transactions where he served as the ostensible owner while the actual ownership and financial responsibilities lay with the others. The involvement of coercion and the lack of the genuine consent in these transactions raise serious concerns under the PBPT Act. D-1 has categorically stated that, despite the properties being registered under his name he is not the bona fide owner. D-1 has accentuated that his name and caste have been utilized in the property deeds. In this case, the BD acted as per the directions of the BO and BD acted as real owner in front of third parties including the registration authorities. It is a fact established beyond doubt that the BD never really wished to purchase the property but he was influenced by the BO in this case. Accordingly, when, IO had issued Show Cause Notice dated 26.12.2023, D-1 framed a new story to avoid the proceedings of Benami and retracted from his own statement recorded on oath. However, D-1 had already stated in his statement recorded on oath by IO on 04.09.2023 that Pawan Kumar and his family (D-2 to D-4) are the real possessors of the properties registered under his name. Further, the fact that properties in question initially purchased in the name of D-1 were transferred to Pawan Kumar’s wife and mother, coupled with one property still being in the name of D-1, clearly proved a pattern of transactions that are characteristic of benami arrangements. The D-1 has also failed to substantiate the source of income for purchase of impugned lands with supporting documentary evidences. However, he has been involved in significant land transactions, which are disproportionate to his declared income and financial capacity. Further, wife of D-1 has also described his modest lifestyle, with a monthly expenditure of about Rs. 10,000/- and no significant assets other than a motor cycle……….” On perusal of these findings made by the Ld. Competent Authority, we find that our inference about the bank accounts not showing the entries as to explain the purchase of the three Impugned Properties from the financial resources of Shri Yashveer Singh are supported and corroborated. To be specific there are neither debit entries nor credit entries as to substantiate the contention that the properties were purchased and then sold by Shri Yashveer Singh from his own financial resources. 15. We take note of the fact that the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006 did not permit the transfer or sale of immovable property belonging to persons of Scheduled Caste to persons of other categories. It therefore follows that in the face of such prohibition a well thought strategy needed to be formulated on the part of the Appellants to circumvent the provisions of the Code in Order to acquire the impugned properties. Subsequently, denial of his earlier statement by Shri Yashveer Singh will not come to his rescue because of lack of any explanation and circumstances as to establish the falsehood of his initial statements. We also note that the pleadings of the Appellants are almost entirely couched in terms of the subsequent denials by Shri Yashveer Singh. However, the Appellants have miserably failed to demonstrate the reasons that why the denial by Shri Yashveer Singh should be accepted. In fact, as already discussed that initial statements of Shri Yashveer Singh are well corroborated as to infer that Sh. Yashveer Singh had truthfully stated that he acted at the behest of Shri Pawan Kumar. 16. In view of the aforementioned discussions and analysis, we dismiss the Appeals Nos. FPA-PBPT-197/KNP/2025 filed by Shri Pawan Kumar, FPA-PBPT-198/KNP/2025 filed by Smt. Urmila Devi and FPA-PBPT-199/KNP/2025 filed by Smt. Sonam. Applications pending, if any, are disposed of accordingly.
