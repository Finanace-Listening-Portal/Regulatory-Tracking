Vs State of Maharashtra (Bombay High Court) Summary: The Bombay High Court allowed three connected writ petitions filed by Roadway Solutions India Infra. Ltd. challenging recovery of disputed GST interest aggregating to Rs.22,12,15,180 for FYs 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26. The petitioner, engaged in construction, maintenance and repair of highways and internal roads under contracts awarded by NHAI, MSRDC, PWD and others, had paid the entire underlying tax. Following examination of its GSTR-3B returns in January 2026, the authorities demanded interest for delayed tax payment. In the proceeding discussed in detail, a notice dated 22 January 2026 proposed interest of Rs.7,05,15,986 for April 2023–March 2024; the adjudication order dated 16 April 2026 confirmed the demand, and the first appellate order dated 13 July 2026 upheld it. Appeals against the interest demands were pending before the Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal. Nevertheless, the department issued recovery communications dated 4 and 23 September 2026 and five bank notices dated 29 September 2026 under Section 79(1)(c). The petitioner argued that the percentage-based pre-deposit provisions in Sections 107(6) and 112(8) do not require a deposit against disputed interest alone once the entire tax has been paid, and that recovery stood stayed under Sections 107(7) and 112(9). It also referred to the three-month recovery period under Section 78. On the underlying liability, it submitted that customers had withheld contract payments and tax because of disputed services, that related matters were sub judice, and that payments made amid banking restrictions did not constitute acceptance of liability. It relied on Evergreen Construction, Tejas Arecanut Traders, Magnum Estates and Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation. The State maintained that interest under Section 50 arose automatically from belated payment of self-assessed tax; subsequent payment did not extinguish that liability. It distinguished outward-supply reporting in GSTR-1 from discharge of tax through GSTR-3B, invoked Sections 75(9) and 75(12), and contended that the appeals against interest were not maintainable without payment. The Court confined the writ proceedings to enforcement of statutory protection against recovery, leaving the merits of the interest demands to GSTAT. It recognised the recovery mechanism for unpaid self-assessed tax and interest under Section 75(12), but held that the percentage-based pre-deposit prescribed by Section 112(8)(b) does not extend to disputed interest alone, particularly where the entire tax has already been paid. The first appellate authority had not insisted on an interest pre-deposit, and GSTAT had accepted the appeals and issued acknowledgments without one. Upon compliance with the applicable requirements of Section 112(8), recovery of the balance amounts covered by those appeals stood stayed under Section 112(9) until their disposal. Accordingly, the Court quashed the recovery communications and consequential bank notices insofar as they sought recovery of the interest demands covered by the pending GSTAT appeals contrary to Section 112(9). It directed withdrawal of the bank notices, immediate communication to the banks and lifting of debit restrictions imposed solely under those notices. The Court expressed no opinion on the interest tax base, payment dates or computation, or on the petitioner’s claim to a refund of around Rs.80 crore and submissions concerning payments of Rs.35,66,61,186 under protest. Those contentions remained open before the appropriate forum. The petitions were disposed of without costs. Cases Discussed Magnum Estates Pvt. Ltd. Further submission of the learned Additional Government Pleader is as under :- (A) Interest liability under the GST Act is considered admitted if it arises from self-assessed tax liability disclosed in returns (GSTR-3B) that was paid late, allowing for recovery without adjudication. Accepted liability under the GST Act generally refers to the tax, interest, or penalty dues that a taxpayer has admitted to or self-assessed in their returns, which are subsequently recorded in the Electronic Liability Register. Self-Assessed Liability includes liabilities reported by the taxpayer in their regular returns such as GSTR-3B, GSTR-4 etc., which are finalized upon filing. (B) Section 37 of the CGST/MGST Act, 2017 mandates every registered person to furnish details of outward supplies of goods or services or both in such form, manner and within such time as may be prescribed. The said provision deals with reporting and disclosure of outward supply details by the registered person. Section 37 of the CGST/MGST Act mandates furnishing the details of outward supplies of services effected during the period on or before the tenth day of the month succeeding the said tax period, and such details [shall, subject to such conditions and restrictions, within such time and in such manner as may be prescribed, be communicated to the recipient of the said supplies] if the invoices are issued. Form GSTR-1 is a statement containing details of outward supplies furnished in accordance with Section 37 of the Act. The purpose of GSTR-1 is disclosure of invoice-wise particulars of outward supplies and communication of such details to recipients for further compliance under the GST framework. Form GSTR-1 is merely a statement containing details of outward supplies furnished under Section 37. The purpose of GSTR-1 is disclosure of invoice-wise particulars so that corresponding input tax credit may flow to the recipient subject to fulfilment of statutory conditions. On the other hand, the actual discharge of tax liability is effected through the return furnished under Section 39, namely Form GSTR-3B. The self-assessed tax liability declared therein is discharged by utilisation of the electronic cash ledger and/or electronic credit ledger in accordance with Section 49 of the CGST/MGST Act, 2017, namely Form GSTR-3B. It is through this return that self-assessed tax becomes payable and is discharged by debiting the electronic cash ledger and electronic credit ledger maintained under Section 49. (C) Thus, the statutory scheme clearly distinguishes between : (i) furnishing details of outward supplies under Section 37; and (ii) discharge of tax liability through returns and payment mechanisın under Sections 39 and 49. Both statutory compliances operate in different fields, though they are interconnected under the GST framework. (D) Therefore, delayed reporting or subsequent declaration of invoices in Form GSTR-1 cannot postpone, defer, or alter the statutory liability to pay tax along with applicable interest thereon, where the taxable supply has already taken place and the time of supply has occurred under the provisions of the Act. Despite being granted ample opportunities to discharge the interest liability arising from short payment of tax, the petitioner failed to discharge the self-assessed liability of interest under Section 50 of the MGST Act, 2017 within the prescribed time. 23. Section 75(12) recognises that once a tax liability is self-assessed by a registered person in the return furnished under Section 39, such liability represents an admitted liability of the petitioner. 24. Where the self-assessed tax liability has been admitted and discharged belatedly, the consequential liability to interest under Section 50 arises automatically by operation of law. The petitioner cannot avoid such interest merely by disputing the liability after having accepted the underlying tax and the fact of delayed payment. 25. It is submitted that Section 75(12) of the GST Act, 2017, provides that where any amount of self-assessed tax in accordance with a return furnished under Section 39 remains unpaid, either wholly or partly, or any amount of interest payable on such tax remains unpaid, the same shall be recoverable under Section 79 of the Act. 26. The sub-section (12) of Section 75 of the MGST Act, 2017 is reproduced as under : “Notwithstanding anything contained in Section 73 or Section 74, where any amount of self-assessed tax in accordance with a return furnished under Section 39 remains unpaid, either wholly or partly, or any amount of interest payable on such tax remains unpaid, the same shall be recovered under the provisions of Section 79. Section 75(12) recognises that once a tax liability is self-assessed by a registered person in the return furnished under Section 39, such liability represents an admitted liability of the taxpayer. Consequently, unpaid self-assessed tax and interest payable thereon are treated differently from disputed tax demands requiring determination under Sections 73 or 74 of the Act. The provision therefore enables recovery of such admitted liability in accordance with Section 79 without requiring a fresh determination of the liability already admitted by the taxpayer.” 27. The relevance of Section 75(12) in the present case is that the petitioner has ultimately discharged the tax liability through returns furnished under Section 39, thereby accepting the underlying tax liability. Once such admitted liability was discharged beyond the prescribed due date, interest under Section 50 became payable as a statutory consequence for the period of delay. Thus, the present proceedings essentially concern recovery of statutory interest arising from delayed payment of admitted tax and not determination of any disputed tax liability. 28. The provision is justified because GST is a self-assessment-based tax system. If a person files a return declaring tax liability, the law expects payment of that admitted amount within the prescribed time. If not, the revenue must have a direct recovery path, otherwise the self-assessment system would become ineffective. In such cases, the department issues a communication asking the taxpayer to pay the unpaid self-assessed tax or explain the shortfall. If the taxpayer does not pay, recovery action under Section 79 may follow. 29. In the present matter, the petitioner does not dispute either the underlying taxable supplies or the corresponding tax liability. The record reveals that the petitioner ultimately discharged the tax through returns furnished under Section 39 after considerable delay. Once the tax liability stood admitted and discharged belatedly by the taxpayer, the corresponding liability towards interest under Section 50 became a statutory consequence. Read conjointly, Sections 50, 75(9) and 75(12) clearly indicate that statutory interest on delayed payment of admitted tax neither depends upon a fresh adjudication of tax liability nor ceases merely because the tax has subsequently been paid. 30. Further, every registered person other than ISD or NRTP or person paying tax under Section 10 or 51 or 52 shall furnish a return under sub-section 1 of Section 39. For every calendar month or part thereof, furnish a return, electronically, of inward and outward supplies of goods or services or both, input tax credit availed, tax payable, tax paid and such other particulars, in such form and manner, and within such time, as may be prescribed. The petitioner has paid tax as disclosed in outward supply furnished under Section 37. 31. Thus, from the records, it is evident that the petitioner has discharged the tax liability belatedly through Form GSTR-3B as discussed in the annexure to the impugned order. However, the applicable interest on such delayed payment was not discharged by the petitioner. Considering the provisions of Section 50 read with Sections 75(9) and 75(12), Appellate Authority has held that the adjudicating authority i.e. this office has correctly confirmed the liability towards statutory interest. The contention that delayed reporting of invoices in Form GSTR-1 postpones the liability to pay tax is contrary to the statutory scheme of the Act and is therefore rejected by the Appellate Authority. 32. It is further observed that the liability to pay interest under Section 50 is not dependent upon initiation of recovery proceedings or establishment of intention to evade payment of tax. Interest is compensatory in character and arises due to delayed availability of tax revenue to the Government. Therefore, subsequent payment of tax may discharge the principal liability but does not erase the statutory consequence arising due to delay. 33. It is not in dispute that the petitioner has discharged the entire tax liability. The petitioner is not admitting the tax liability but says that during the course of investigation, has discharged the tax liability under protest. It is therefore the case of the petitioner that upon compliance with Section 112(8), recovery of the balance amount covered by the appeals is deemed stayed under Section 112(9) until disposal of the appeals before the Tribunal. 38. The petitioner is seeking enforcement of the statutory protection, while the merits of the interest demands remain for adjudication before the Tribunal. It is the submission of the petitioner that the impugned recovery proceedings in respect of the demands covered by its appeals before the Tribunal and the statutory protection under Section 112(9), are contrary to law. 39. For the facility of convenience, Section 50, Section 75(9) and Section 75(12) of the CGST/MGST Act are reproduced which read thus :- “50. Interest on delayed payment of tax (1) Every person who is liable to pay tax in accordance with the provisions of this Act or the rules made thereunder, but fails to pay the tax or any part thereof to the Government within the period prescribed, shall for the period for which the tax or any part thereof remains unpaid, pay, on his own, interest at such rate, not exceeding eighteen per cent., as may be notified by the Government on the recommendations of the Council : Provided that the interest on tax payable in respect of supplies made during a tax period and declared in the return for the said period furnished after the due date in accordance with the provisions of section 39, except where such return is furnished after commencement of any proceedings under section 73 or section 74 [or section 74A] in respect of the said period, shall be payable on that portion of the tax which is paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger. (2) The interest under sub-section (1) shall be calculated, in such manner as may be prescribed, from the day succeeding the day on which such tax was due to be paid. (3) Where the input tax credit has been wrongly availed and utilised, the registered person shall pay interest on such input tax credit wrongly availed and utilised, at such rate not exceeding twenty-four per cent. as may be notified by the Government, on the recommendations of the Council, and the interest shall be calculated, in such manner as may be prescribed. 75. General provisions relating to determination of tax (9) The interest on the tax short paid or not paid shall be payable whether or not specified in the order determining the tax liability. (12) Notwithstanding anything contained in Section 73 or Section 74 [or Section 74A], where any amount of self-assessed tax in accordance with a return furnished under Section 39 remains unpaid, either wholly or partly, or any amount of interest payable on such tax remains unpaid, the same shall be recovered under the provisions of Section 79.” 40. Section 75(12) recognises that once a tax liability is self-assessed by a registered person in the return furnished under Section 39, such liability represents an admitted liability of the taxpayer. Consequently, unpaid self-assessed tax and interest payable thereon are treated differently from disputed tax demands requiring determination under Sections 73 or 74 of the Act. The provision therefore enables recovery of such admitted liability in accordance with Section 79 without requiring a fresh determination of the liability already admitted by the taxpayer. 41. The relevant provisions indicate that for filing an appeal before First Appellate Authority, under Section 107 of CGST/MGST Act, 2017 or, for that matter, before the Second Appellate Authority viz. the Tribunal under Section 112 of the Act, the petitioner is required to make specified amount of pre-deposit, as specified therein. 42. Significant in the context of the present case would be a reference to sub-section (6) of Section 107 of CGST/MGST Act for the purpose of filing appeal before the First Appellate Authority. Section 107(1) and 107(6) is reproduced hereunder for the facility of convenience :- “107. Appeals to Appellate Authority (1) Any person aggrieved by any decision or order passed under this Act or the State Goods and Services Tax Act or the Union Territory Goods and Services Tax Act by an Adjudicating Authority may appeal to such Appellate Authority as may be prescribed within three months from the date on which the said decision or order is communicated to such person. (6) No appeal shall be filed under sub-section (1), unless the appellant has paid – (a) in full, such part of the amount of tax, interest, fine, fee and penalty arising from the impugned order, as is admitted by him; and (b) a sum equal to ten per cent. of the remaining amount of tax in dispute arising from the said order, [subject to a maximum of [twenty] crore rupees,] in relation to which the appeal has been filed: Provided that in case of any order demanding penalty without involving demand of any tax, no appeal shall be filed against such order unless a sum equal to ten per cent. of the said penalty has been paid by the appellant.” 43. A reading of Section 107(6) of CGST/MGST Act indicates that the petitioner is required to pre-deposit entire undisputed amount of tax, interest, penalty etc. and 10% of the disputed tax, arising out of the order appealed against, if tax, interest and penalty are under challenge or 10% of the penalty, if only penalty is in dispute. There is no mention of deposit of any amount towards interest, if interest alone is under challenge. Likewise, for filing an appeal before the Tribunal under Section 112(8) of the CGST/MGST Act, similar provisions exist i.e. entire undisputed amount of tax, interest and penalty are required to be deposited and the sum equal to 10% of the remaining amount of tax in dispute, in addition to the amount paid/deposited under Section 107(6), while filing the appeal before the First Appellate Authority. 44. Perusal of Section 107(6) and Section 112(8) of the CGST/MGST Act make it clear that there is no requirement of pre-deposit of interest, when entire amount of tax has been paid. Factually, the First Appellate Authority, i.e. Deputy Commissioner of State Tax Appeals-3, did not insist for any pre-deposit of interest, obviously since the entire amount of tax was paid by the petitioner. Likewise, the appeal before the Tribunal also has been accepted and acknowledgment to that effect has been issued by the Tribunal, without making any pre-deposit of interest, considering the undisputed fact that entire amount of tax has already been deposited. 45. At this juncture it would be pertinent to extract the provisions of Section 107(7) of the CGST/MGST Act which reads thus :- “107. Appeals to Appellate Authority (7) Where the appellant has paid the amount under sub-section (6), the recovery proceedings for the balance amount shall be deemed to be stayed. 46. A perusal of Section 107(7) of the CGST/MGST Act indicates that once an appeal has been filed, after making the statutorily required pre-deposit as applicable before the First Appellate Authority, recovery proceedings for the balance amount is deemed to have been stayed. Likewise, after filing an appeal before the Tribunal, when the petitioner has made the statutorily required pre-deposit under Section 112(8) of the CGST/MGST Act, recovery proceedings for the balance amount are deemed to have been stayed. In this context, for the facility of convenience, Section 112(8) and 112(9) of the CGST/MGST Act is extracted which reads thus :- “112. Appeals to Appellate Tribunal (8) No appeal shall be filed under sub-section (1), unless the appellant has paid- (a) in full, such part of the amount of tax, interest, fine, fee and penalty arising from the impugned order, as is admitted by him, and (b) a sum equal to [ten per cent.] of the remaining amount of tax in dispute, in addition to the amount paid under sub-section (6) of section 107, arising from the said order, [subject to a maximum of [twenty crore rupees],] in relation to which the appeal has been filed: Provided that in case of any order demanding penalty without involving demand of any tax, no appeal shall be filed against such order unless a sum equal to ten per cent. of the said penalty, in addition to the amount payable under the proviso to sub-section (6) of section 107 has been paid by the appellant. (9) Where the appellant has paid the amount as per sub-section (8), the recovery proceedings for the balance amount shall be deemed to be stayed till the disposal of the appeal.” 47. It is not in dispute that the entire tax underlying the impugned interest demands has been discharged by the petitioner. The remaining demands challenged before the Tribunal are demands for disputed interest. The petitioner relies upon compliance with the applicable requirements of Section 112(8) and the consequent protection under Section 112(9). We therefore have no hesitation in holding that the percentage-based pre-deposit prescribed by Section 112(8)(b) does not extend to disputed interest alone, especially when the entire tax has been paid. The merits of the interest demands, including the applicable tax base, payment dates and computation, are the subject of the pending appeals before the Tribunal. 48. The petitioner does not dispute that Section 79(1)(c) permits notices to persons holding money for or on account of a taxable person, including banks. The challenge is to the exercise of that power for recovery of the identified interest demands notwithstanding the statutory protection claimed under Section 112(9). 49. It is the submission of the petitioner that it made substantial payments in the circumstances of repeated banking restrictions, while its objections and reservations were communicated to the respondents. The representations dated 30th January 2026, 6th February 2026, 30th March 2026, 4th April 2026 and 22nd May 2026 record the project disputes, financial constraints, payment arrangements and proposed consequential accounting and tax adjustments. According to the petitioner, the payments made to restore or preserve banking operations cannot, by themselves, be treated as acceptance of the separately disputed interest computation. The petitioner is claiming refund. The petitioner says that it is entitled to a refund of around Rs.80,00,00,000/-, as submitted in the representation dated 30th January 2026. All these contentions are kept open. We express no opinion on this aspect. 50. Learned Senior Advocate submitted that the petitioner has made payments to the tune of Rs.35,66,61,186/- notwithstanding the petitioner’s recorded objections and financial constraints. It is his submission that from the reservation expressed by the petitioner in the representations, it is obvious that this may not amount to acceptance of the tax liability or abandonment of statutory remedies. It is therefore submitted that the tax liability has not been discharged as an admitted tax liability but the petitioner has disputed the liability to pay tax and thus the petitioner has discharged the entire tax liability which according to the petitioner is under protest. These contentions obviously may be raised before the appropriate forum. We express no opinion. 51. We therefore have no hesitation in holding that the recovery initiated is in the teeth of the provisions of Section 112(9) of the CGST/MGST Act. Consequently, the writ petitions are allowed in terms of prayer clauses (a), (b) and (c) which read thus :- (a) issue an appropriate writ, order or direction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India quashing and setting aside the impugned recovery communications dated 04.09.2026 and 23.09.2026 (Exhibits “A” and “B”) and the consequential notices dated 29.09.2026 issued to the banks under Section 79(1)(c) (Exhibits “C” to “G”), insofar as they seek recovery, contrary to the protection under Section 112(9) of the Act, in respect of the interest demands covered by the Petitioner’s pending GSTAT appeals; (b) declare that, upon compliance with the applicable requirements of Section 112(8) of the Act in respect of the appeals covering the impugned interest demands, recovery of the balance amounts stands stayed under Section 112(9) until disposal of those appeals, and direct the Respondents to give effect to that statutory protection; (c) direct Respondent No.3 to withdraw the impugned bank notices dated 29.09.2026 (Exhibits “C” to “G”), communicate the withdrawal forthwith to the respective banks, and cause all debit restrictions imposed solely pursuant to those notices to be lifted so as to restore ordinary banking operations.” 52.
