Summary: The research publication “171 Legal Compliances and Legal Updates for November 2026 in India”, authored by CA Satish Agarwal under the Darshan Mala Series (DMS), presents a comprehensive compilation of statutory filing requirements, regulatory obligations, compliance deadlines and recent legal developments relevant to businesses, companies, professionals, financial institutions, exporters, investors and other regulated entities operating in India. The publication brings together important monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, annual and event-based compliances across nine principal regulatory areas, including the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999; Income-tax Act, 1961; Goods and Services Tax (GST) laws; Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) regulations; Companies Act, 2013; Special Economic Zones (SEZ) framework; Software Technology Parks of India (STPI) requirements; labour laws; and Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act (RERA), 2016. Under FEMA, the calendar covers reporting obligations relating to the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), External Commercial Borrowings (ECB-2), NBFC regulatory returns, statutory auditors’ certificates, foreign direct investment, foreign capital instruments, depository receipts and LLP investment transactions, including reporting through Forms FC-GPR, FC-TRS, LLP-I and LLP-II. The income-tax section addresses tax deduction at source (TDS), tax collection at source (TCS), challan payments, quarterly certificates, property transaction reporting, income-tax return filing, foreign tax credit declarations, tax audit-related obligations and applications for concessional tax regimes. The publication identifies 21 November 2026 as an important date for specified income-tax filings relating to Assessment Year 2026-27, citing extensions of certain original deadlines, while separately identifying 30 November 2026 for transfer-pricing-related returns and other specified compliances. It also summarises changes in TDS and TCS thresholds, provisions governing payments to partners under Section 194T, professional fees, rent, commission, interest, dividends, insurance commission, lottery winnings, compensation payments and remittances under the LRS. The GST compliance calendar addresses monthly and quarterly returns in Forms GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-5, GSTR-5A, GSTR-6, GSTR-7, GSTR-8, GSTR-11, GST PMT-06 and other applicable forms, together with requirements concerning composition taxpayers, the QRMP scheme, input service distributors, reverse charge transactions, e-invoicing, invoice reporting restrictions and GST registration. The compilation also identifies changes affecting transactions involving used cooking oil, plastic, tyre scrap, electronic waste and metal scrap, including proposed or notified treatment under GST TDS, reverse charge and registration provisions. For listed companies, it provides an extensive overview of SEBI disclosure requirements covering financial results, utilisation of issue proceeds, related-party transactions, board meeting intimations, material events, shareholding patterns, promoter reclassification, annual reports, corporate governance disclosures, record dates, non-convertible securities, securities transfers and transmission, auditor resignations and independent director resignations. The Companies Act section covers ROC and MCA filings, including PAS-6, MGT-7, MGT-7A, ADT-1, DIR-12, MGT-14, PAS-3, CRA-2, CRA-4, NFRA-related filings, CSR registration, beneficial ownership reporting and other event-based corporate compliance requirements. Additional chapters address SEZ and STPI reporting, SOFTEX submissions, employee provident fund contributions, Employees’ State Insurance payments, professional tax obligations, gratuity-related requirements and real estate regulatory compliance. Overall, the publication is structured as a practical reference calendar to assist taxpayers, compliance officers, chartered accountants, company secretaries and management teams in identifying applicable obligations, reviewing statutory timelines, assigning compliance responsibilities and monitoring recurring as well as transaction-triggered reporting requirements during November 2026. In addition to its extensive compliance calendar, the publication presents a consolidated review of important legal and regulatory updates relating to income tax, GST and international taxation, drawing upon notifications, circulars, regulatory advisories, government announcements and other publicly available developments. The income-tax updates address changes affecting return filing, tax audit reporting, withholding tax obligations, penalty and immunity procedures, documentation requirements and compliance under the income-tax framework. Among the developments highlighted are a CBDT notification introducing revised Form 161 for penalty-waiver-related procedures, clarifications concerning misreporting categories and taxpayer relief, an extension of specified tax audit and corporate income-tax return deadlines, and changes concerning withholding tax on purchases of immovable property from non-residents and PAN-based reporting requirements. The publication refers to CBDT Notification No. 134/2026 dated 8 October 2026, Circular No. 7/2026 dated 28 September 2026, and Notification No. 121/2026 dated 22 September 2026 in connection with these developments. The GST updates discuss the revenue collection figures reported for October 2026, including a reported gross GST collection of approximately Rs.2.31 lakh crore, along with GSTN advisories relating to recipient-generated self-invoices for reverse charge transactions involving supplies from unregistered domestic suppliers and imports of services. The compilation also covers the preparation and filing of annual returns and reconciliation statements in Forms GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C for Financial Year 2025-26, the recommendations of the 57th GST Council Meeting held on 8 October 2026, and developments concerning the multistate GST registration facility intended to improve registration-related administration. In international taxation, it highlights the amendment to the India–Mauritius Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement protocol, particularly the introduction of the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) as an anti-treaty-abuse measure, referring to a Ministry of Finance notification dated 13 October 2026 and an effective date identified in the publication as 10 August 2026. These updates are presented alongside the broader statutory calendar to help readers connect changes in law and administrative procedures with their practical consequences for tax deduction, return filing, reporting, registration, financial disclosures and cross-border transactions. The publication emphasises the importance of timely compliance, maintaining proper statutory records, reconciling financial and tax information, monitoring changes in regulatory instructions and taking corrective action before deadlines expire. It is particularly relevant to organisations operating across multiple regulatory frameworks because a single business transaction may simultaneously generate obligations under income-tax law, GST, FEMA, corporate law, SEBI regulations or sector-specific reporting requirements. By collecting numerous requirements in one publication, the research seeks to reduce the risk of missed filings, late fees, interest, penalties, procedural defaults and regulatory non-compliance while improving awareness of legislative and administrative developments. At the same time, the applicability of each requirement depends upon the legal status, turnover, transaction characteristics, registration category, reporting period and specific statutory provisions governing the entity concerned. Accordingly, the listed dates, notifications, forms and legal changes should be independently checked against the applicable official statutory provisions, government notifications, circulars and regulatory portals before being relied upon for actual compliance. The publication forms part of CA Satish Agarwal’s continuing Darshan Mala Series, which aims to make legal and regulatory research accessible to professionals, businesses, researchers, students and the general public through periodic compilations of compliance obligations and legal developments in India.
