Here's when you should sound alarmOne of the biggest challenges with data breaches is that customers may not realise their information has been compromised until someone tries to misuse it. There may be no immediate warning or suspicious transaction to indicate that their details have fallen into the wrong hands.Singh told TOI, “Customers may discover breaches through bank notifications, security alerts or suspicious transactions. Fraudsters combine stolen information with impersonation, using genuine personal details to gain trust before requesting OTPs, payment approvals or account access.”He added, “The data leak creates the opportunity. The fraud often happens through the conversation that follows.”5 steps to take after bank details are hackedMarrè advised customers to watch for breach notifications, unexpected password-reset requests, unfamiliar account-access alerts and unrecognised transactions.“When a phishing attempt references recent transactions, account details or personal information, it is often far more effective at convincing a victim to disclose additional information or authorize fraudulent transactions,” Marrè added.Beyond immediate financial fraud, exposed information can also be used for identity theft or account takeovers, meaning the risks may persist even when no money is stolen immediately after a breach.Caught in a data breach? Don't wait, act When it comes to banking fraud, waiting to see what happens can prove costly. Whether your personal information has been exposed or you spot an unauthorised transaction, acting quickly is important. Here are the steps customers should take to protect their money and limit further damage:Contact your bank immediately: Report any suspected data breach or unauthorised activity without delay. If your account has been compromised, ask the bank to block affected cards or payment instruments.Change your passwords: If personal information has been exposed, update passwords for potentially affected accounts. Also enable multi-factor authentication for an additional layer of security.Secure your email account: Criminals may use your email to reset passwords or intercept important banking alerts. Change its password and enable multi-factor authentication.Monitor your accounts: Regularly check transactions, login attempts and account changes. Do not ignore even small unfamiliar charges, as criminals may use them to test an account before attempting larger transfers.Report financial cyber fraud: If money has been stolen, call the cyber fraud helpline at 1930 or report the incident at cybercrime.gov.in.Keep a record of complaints: Singh advises customers to file a written complaint and preserve transaction records and acknowledgements. Keep records of all communications with the bank in case the matter needs to be escalated.Approach the RBI Ombudsman if needed: If the bank rejects the complaint, fails to resolve it adequately or does not respond within 30 days, customers can approach the RBI Ombudsman.Continue monitoring your accounts: Reporting an incident does not mean the risk is over. Stolen information can circulate among criminal groups for months or even years, making it important to keep checking bank accounts and credit reports.Money recovered from bank fraudsHow much money can customers get back?When money is stolen from a bank account, getting it back is not always guaranteed. The chances of recovery depend on how quickly the fraud is reported, the payment method used and whether the funds can be frozen before criminals move them through other accounts or overseas. Reporting the fraud at the earliest can improve the chances of stopping the money from moving further.However, recovering stolen funds and getting compensation from the bank are two different matters.Singh says there is no universal recovery percentage, as the outcome depends on the circumstances of the fraud and who was responsible for the loss. Under the RBI's existing framework, customers generally have zero liability if the loss is caused by bank negligence.Qualifying third-party breaches reported within three working days also attract zero liability, while reporting within four to seven working days can result in limited liability. Customer negligence, however, can change the outcome.“Recovering stolen funds and receiving reimbursement under RBI's liability rules are different matters.”The compensation framework is also set to evolve.From January 2027, revised RBI rules will introduce conditional compensation for certain small-value frauds, subject to eligibility requirements.Separately, the Supreme Court has called for financial institutions to compensate victims by at least the amount lost when an investigation establishes that the fraud resulted from a bank's failure of due diligence.
