Summary: Listing equity shares completes the Initial Public Offering (IPO) process but marks the beginning of continuing compliance obligations under securities laws. Newly listed companies must establish exchange filing access, make initial disclosures, maintain investor grievance facilities and implement regulatory controls. On the listing date, important activities include activating NSE and BSE filing platforms, disclosing authorised key managerial personnel under Regulation 30(5) of the SEBI LODR Regulations, submitting the Code of Fair Disclosure under the PIT Regulations and ensuring website readiness under Regulation 46. Following listing, companies must update relevant corporate records, obtain applicable insider-trading disclosures, verify promoter information and assess market-capitalisation-based requirements. The first financial reporting cycle requires particular attention to Regulation 33(3)(j), which provides a specific reporting framework for companies listed through an IPO. Other obligations include publication of financial results, Integrated Filing requirements, trading-window restrictions and automated PAN freezing. Quarterly compliance covers shareholding-pattern submissions, reconciliation of share capital, depository certifications and monitoring the utilisation of IPO proceeds. The applicability and deadlines of these obligations depend on the listing date, reporting period, nature of the disclosure and relevant regulatory provisions. A stage-wise compliance calendar identifying each filing, responsible person, approval requirement, electronic platform and statutory deadline can help companies avoid omissions and establish an effective post-listing compliance framework. While the IPO process focuses on approvals, offer-document disclosures and allotment, the post-listing phase requires ongoing regulatory filings, corporate governance compliance and timely communication of material information to stock exchanges and investors. These obligations arise at different stages. Some disclosures are required on listing, certain formalities must follow immediately, and others become due with the first financial results or later reporting periods. The post-listing compliance process can be examined across four stages. Stage 1: On the Listing Date – Completing Exchange Formalities and Initial Disclosures 1. Stock-Exchange Credentials and Filing Access A company listed on NSE or BSE must submit regulatory filings through the prescribed electronic platforms. It should therefore obtain and activate access to the NSE Electronic Application Processing System (NEAPS) and BSE Listing Centre, as applicable. For NSE, the designated user should confirm access to the compliance and corporate-announcement modules, including facilities for uploading prescribed PDF documents and XBRL returns. Access to BSE Listing Centre should likewise be verified for corporate announcements and compliance filings. The company should verify that the authorised user’s email address and mobile number, the Company Secretary’s particulars, and all other registration details are accurate. Any discrepancy should be resolved through the relevant exchange’s registration or support process. Exchange records should also contain the correct GSTIN for billing. The company should review and update its GSTIN master and billing details wherever necessary. These are administrative exchange requirements and are distinct from disclosures mandated by the LODR Regulations. NSE and BSE also operate an API-based Single Filing System. For disclosures covered by this facility, a filing made on one exchange is transmitted to the other. The company should select the correct filing category, submit the required document and confirm successful dissemination, rather than assume that every filing is automatically covered. 2. Disclosure of Authorised KMP under Regulation 30(5) SEBI LODR Regulations [“LODR”] Immediately after listing, the company should disclose the authorized personnel’s names and contact details to the exchanges and publish them on its website. The Board must authorise one or more key managerial personnel for determining the materiality of events or information and making disclosures to the stock exchanges under Regulation 30(5). 3. Disclosure of Code of Fair Disclosure under PIT Regulations [“PIT”] The company should submit its Code of Fair Disclosure through the applicable corporate-announcement facility and make the code available on its website. Regulation 8(2) of the PIT requires a company whose securities are listed to promptly intimate its Code of Practices and Procedures for Fair Disclosure of UPSI, including amendments, to the stock exchanges. 4. Website and Investor Grievance Facilities Reg. 46 (2) of LODR require listed entities to have a separate section on website for displaying information as is required by Reg. 46. Accordingly listed entity should activate an investor-relations section on its website carrying the content as per regulation 46. 5. Investor Grievance Mail ID The designated investor grievance email address should be operational, and access to SCORES and the applicable SMART ODR framework should be arranged in accordance with SEBI’s prescribed onboarding requirements. Complaints received through the website, email, RTA or regulatory platforms should be centrally recorded and assigned for resolution. Stage 2: After Listing – Updating Records and Implementing Regulatory Controls 1. Updating the CIN and MCA Master Data After listing, the company should submit a Change Request Form (‘CRF’) on the MCA website to correct its CIN. A resolution of board of directors in this regard is mandatory for making an application of change request for CIN and MCA master data. Company should simultaneously update the CIN on its letterheads, website, communications and other documents. 2. Post-Listing Declarations and Disclosures Upon listing, the Company should obtain requisite initial disclosures of securities holdings under Regulation 7(1)(b) of PIT, and disclosures of subsequent transactions under Regulation 7(2), wherever applicable.[i] 3. Designated-Person Information and Declarations Under Clause 14 of Schedule B of the PIT, designated persons are required to furnish prescribed information, including the names and PAN or other permitted identifiers of immediate relatives and persons with whom they share a material financial relationship, along with specified contact information. SAST Disclosures and Promoter Information SEBI, through its circular dated 07th March 2022, automated disclosures under Regulations 29 and 31 of the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011, through a mechanism involving depositories and stock exchanges, dispensing with separate manual filings for transactions covered by the framework. Accordingly, upon listing, the company should ensure that the PAN details of its promoters and promoter-group members are correctly mapped with the designated depository and that subsequent changes are updated in accordance with the prescribed procedure. The company should also verify the system-generated disclosures disseminated by the stock exchanges and promptly report discrepancies. However, manual disclosures continue to be required in specified cases, including disclosure triggers arising from aggregate holdings of persons acting in concert or transactions not covered by the automated mechanism. 4. Identify Size-Based Obligations It is recommended that the Companies should monitor market-capitalisation-based LODR requirements under Regulation 3(2) and examine pre-IPO employee benefit schemes under Regulation 12 of the SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations, 2021, including conformity and shareholder ratification before fresh grants. Stage 3: First Financial Reporting Cycle – Completing the First Periodic Filings 1. Determining the First Financial Reporting Period Regulation 33(3)(j) of the LODR Regulations contains a specific relaxation for companies listed through an IPO. The first financial results must cover the quarter or financial year immediately following the period for which financial statements were disclosed in the offer document. The filing deadline is the applicable period under Regulation 33(3)(a) or (d), or 21 days from listing, whichever is later. The company should therefore examine the financial statements included in its offer document, identify the next reporting period and calculate its first reporting deadline accordingly. e.g. if a company got listed on October 28, 2026, then company will have to submit financial results for quarter ended September 2026 and any prior period within 21 days from listing or within 45 days from end of September 2026 (as results would be published for period of September 2026 and earlier if any). So, results will have to be published by November 18, 2026. 2. Publication of Financial Results Regulation 47 requires publication of the prescribed financial-results advertisement in an English national daily newspaper and a regional-language newspaper, subject to the applicable exemptions. Under the amended framework, the company must publish the prescribed advertisement with a QR code and details of the webpage hosting the complete financial results. Publishing the detailed results in the newspaper is optional. The company should arrange the advertisement after approval of the results, ensure compliance with the applicable publication timeline and retain the newspaper copies and proof of publication. 3. Integrated Filing – Governance and Financial Integrated Filing (Governance) includes the corporate governance compliance report, statement of investor grievances and the additional disclosures prescribed under the integrated format. It is generally due within 30 days after the quarter-end. Integrated Filing (Financial) covers financial results, statements of deviation or variation in the use of issue proceeds, applicable related-party transaction disclosures, and other prescribed financial information. It is generally due within 45 days after the quarter-end and within 60 days for the last quarter or financial year, subject to specific provisions, including Regulation 33(3)(j). Related-party transaction disclosures remain half-yearly in frequency even though they form part of the Integrated Filing (Financial) framework. If a company is listed during a quarter but before the quarterly compliance filings are submitted, the applicable filings must be completed for the quarter in which the listing occurs. 4. Trading Restrictions and Automated PAN Freezing SEBI’s circular dated 19 July 2023 extended automated PAN freezing at the security level to all listed companies in phases. From 1 October 2025, the framework applies to equity and equity-linked instruments of all listed entities. SEBI’s circular dated 21 April 2025 further extended automated freezing for financial-results closures to the immediate relatives of designated persons. For a newly listed company, automated freezing begins on the first day of the second quarter following the quarter of listing. This operational timeline does not defer the underlying insider-trading restrictions, which apply from listing. The mandatory automated PAN–ISIN freezing framework applies only to trading-window closure arising from declaration of financial results. 5. Quarterly Compliance – If an Entity Gets Listed at the End of Quarter Regulation 31(1)(b) requires submission of the quarterly shareholding pattern within 21 days from the end of each quarter. The company should obtain the quarter-end beneficial-position and shareholding records from its RTA and depositories, reconcile the issued capital with dematerialised and physical holdings, verify shareholder classifications and submit the prescribed shareholding pattern. Separately, Regulation 76 of the SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018 requires reconciliation of share capital by a qualified practising professional. The report is required within 30 days from the quarter-end and should reconcile issued capital, listed capital and shares held in dematerialised and physical form, together with the prescribed particulars. Company should also file certification under reg. 74(5) of SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018 within the prescribed time stating demat of securities, if any done during the quarter. 6. Monitoring IPO Proceeds The company must track the amount raised, utilisation against each object stated in the offer document, the unutilised balance, and any deviation or variation in use. Under Regulation 32 of LODR, the prescribed deviation or variation statement should be reviewed by the Audit Committee and submitted through the applicable Integrated Filing framework. Where a monitoring agency has been appointed under the ICDR Regulations, the company must obtain its reports at the prescribed intervals, place them before the Audit Committee and make the required stock-exchange disclosures. Conclusion Post-listing compliance begins with immediate operational and disclosure requirements: activating exchange filing systems, making initial regulatory disclosures, and implementing trading restrictions. It then progresses to the first financial reporting cycle, followed by quarterly, annual and event-based obligations under securities laws. A newly listed company must clearly identify each filing, the person responsible for approving or submitting it, the filing platform, and the applicable deadline. A stage-wise compliance calendar covering these points can prevent omissions as the company transitions from an IPO issuer to a continuously regulated listed entity.
