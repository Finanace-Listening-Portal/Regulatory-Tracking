Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Upadhyay Padia and Associates Vs ITO (ITAT Rajkot) On-Money Additions Restricted to 10% on the Facts and Taxable at Normal Rates Without Invoking Section 115BBE Tribunal Grants Partial Relief on Alleged On-Money Payments The Rajkot ITAT restricted additions for alleged on-money paid on the purchase of commercial property to 10% of the disputed amounts. The Assessing Officer had added ₹9,00,000 and ₹98,530 under section 69 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Tribunal sustained ₹90,000 and ₹9,853, respectively, and directed that these amounts be taxed at normal income-tax rates, without applying section 115BBE. It expressly confined the determination to the appeals before it and stated that the order should not be treated as a precedent for preceding or succeeding assessment years. Search of Developer’s Group Yielded a Digital Ledger The assessee was a partnership firm engaged in accounting, bookkeeping and taxation consultancy. For AY 2019–20, taken as the lead case, it filed its original return on 10 September 2019, declaring total income of ₹31,650. A search on 24 August 2021 covered various premises of the R.K. Group, including the residence of its accountant, Girish Vanjani. The Department recovered files maintained in Miracle accounting software, including a ledger titled “RKP 0816”. Vanjani stated that the data had been maintained on the instructions of Sarvanand Sonwani, a key person of the group. The Department recorded statements from both individuals. The assessment proceeded on the inference that the ledger entries had been backdated by ten years and that two zeros had been omitted from the figures. Using that interpretation, the Assessing Officer attributed an alleged cash payment of ₹9 lakh to the firm for the purchase of Office No. 816 in the R.K. Prime project. The reassessment for the lead year was completed under section 147 on 30 March 2024. Registered Deed and Bank Payments Formed the Assessee’s Defence The firm denied paying any on-money. It relied on a registered purchase deed dated 27 February 2020, recording consideration of ₹12,36,000, and stated that the entire consideration had been paid through banking channels. It also submitted that the developer’s ledger did not reconcile even with the bank payments and that the Department’s interpretation depended on assumptions about both the dates and the amounts recorded. The firm pointed out that the ledger had been recovered from a third party, did not name the firm and bore no signature or approval of its partners. It also challenged the reliance placed on the statements without an opportunity for cross-examination. The Assessing Officer rejected the explanation and made the addition under section 69. The CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, confirmed the additions in both appeals. Earlier Decisions From the Same Search Supported Partial Relief Before the Tribunal, the firm referred to three Rajkot Bench decisions concerning buyers affected by the same search and similar seized material. These were Nitaben Kirtikumar Parekh, ITA No. 506/Rjt/2026, Payal Piyushbhai Jethva, ITA No. 425/Rjt/2026, and Manisha Sureshchandra Rajdev, ITA No. 254/Rjt/2026. The firm submitted that the Tribunal had restricted similar additions to 10% of the alleged on-money in those cases and requested relief on the same basis. The Departmental Representative supported the Assessing Officer’s findings. Tribunal Sustains an Estimated Addition at Normal Rates The Tribunal considered some of the documents furnished before the lower authorities self-serving and found that the inconsistencies in the evidence warranted an estimate. It concluded that partial relief was justified and adopted what it described as a 10% net profit rate on the amounts in dispute. This reduced the additions from ₹9,00,000 to ₹90,000 and from ₹98,530 to ₹9,853. The Tribunal specifically directed the Assessing Officer to apply normal income-tax rates to the amounts sustained, excluding section 115BBE. Both appeals were partly allowed. The second year is identified here as AY 2021–22, as shown in the cause title and the assessee’s written submissions. The opening paragraph of the order refers to AY 2020–21. Author’s Comments The practical significance of the order lies in the relief on both quantum and rate of tax. The Department’s computation depended on interpreting a third party’s digital ledger and reconstructing the recorded dates and figures. The assessee disputed that interpretation and, before the Tribunal, sought the same proportionate relief granted to other buyers affected by the search. The Bench retained an estimated amount to account for discrepancies it found in the record and expressly directed ordinary taxation of that amount. The 10% estimate must be read with the Tribunal’s express restriction on its use in other years. For the two appeals actually decided, the operative directions provide the figures to be assessed and the rate treatment to be applied. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT RAJKOT Captioned two appeals filed by the same assessee, pertaining to different Assessment Years, 2019-20 & 2020-21, are directed against the separate orders passed under section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as “the Act”) by Commissioner of Income-tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC) Delhi, dated 30.09.2026, which in turn arises out of separate assessment orders passed by the Assessing Officer u/s 147 of the Act. 2. The grounds of appeal raised by the assessee in ITA No.1407/Rjt/2026 are as follows: “1. The assessment order u/s. 147 of the Act is bad in law. 2. The reopening of the assessment is bad in law. 3. The learned Assessing Officer has erred in law as well as on facts in making an addition of Rs. 9,00,000/- u/s. 69 of the Act on the ground of alleged unexplained investment. The ld. CIT(A) has erred in law as well as on facts in confirming the same.” 3. The grounds of appeal raised by the assessee in ITA No.1408/Rjt/2026 are as follows: “The learned Assessing Officer has erred in law as well as on facts in making an addition of Rs.98,530/- on the ground of alleged unexplained investment under section 69 of the Income tax Act, 1961. The ld. CIT(A) has erred in law as well as on facts in confirming the same”. 4. In these both appeals, similar and identical issues are involved therefore both these appeals have been clubbed and heard together and a consolidated order is being passed for the sake of convenience. To narrate the facts, I have taken lead case in ITA No.1407/Rjt/2026 for the A.Y. 2019-20. 5. Brief facts of the case are that the present appeal arises from the assessment order u/s. 147 dated 30.03.2024 passed by the Income Tax Officer. The assessee is a partnership- firm engaged in rendering professional services of accounting, bookkeeping, and taxation consultancy services during the year under appeal. The assessee, e-filed its original income tax return on 10.09.2019, declaring a total income of Rs. 31,650/-. There was a search action carried out on various premises of R.K. Group on 24.08.2021. As a part of the same, the residential premises of Shri Girish Vanjani were also covered, who is the accountant of the R.K. Group. From his possession, certain Miracle files were discovered on which he stated that the data was maintained as per the instructions of Shri Sarvanand Sadhuram Sonwani who is the key person of the R.K. Group and the statement of Shri Girish Vanjani and Shri Sarvanand Sonwani were also recorded. In the seized miracles files, a ledger named “RKP 0816” was found for the period of 01.04.1999 to 31.03.2099. It was assumed that the data in this ledger was backdated by 10 years and the figures were manipulated by eliminating two zeros. Based on this, it was inferred that “on- money” amounting to Rs. 9,00,000/- was paid by the assessee firm during the year under appeal for the purchase of commercial property bearing office No. 816 in “R.K. Prime” project developed by R.K. Group. Consequently, the learned A.O. issued a show cause notice proposing the addition of Rs. 9,00,000/- u/s 69 of the Act. In response, the assessee- firm clearly denied the allegation of making any “on money” payment for the purchase of the property. The assessee- firm also stated that they purchased a commercial property bearing office No. 816 in “R.K. Prime” building developed by R.K. Group vide a registered purchase deed dated 27.02.2020 for a total consideration of Rs. 12,36,000/-. All the payments in respect of the purchase transactions were made through the banking channel and no cash payments were involved. Moreover, the registered purchase deed is always an overwhelming evidence compared to unilaterally created ledgers. The assessee -firm also submitted that the miracle ledger reproduced in the SCN is erroneous and unreliable as much as the bank payments are also not matching. Further, the assessee- firm explained that the seized ledger has neither been found in their possession nor does it contain the signature or approval of any of their partners. The seized ledger was found in the possession of a third party and does not even contain the name of the assessee- firm. Moreover, the data related to dates and figures in the ledger are mentioned in an ambiguous manner, and assumptions are made to correlate them with the dates of the year under appeal. Further, in the case of unilaterally created digital data and general statements from witnesses, cross-examination becomes an inevitable requirement of law to ascertain the truth. 6. However, the assessing officer rejected the contention of the assessee and made addition of Rs.9,00,000/- under section 69 of the Act. 7. Being aggrieved by the said order of the Assessing Officer, the assessee filed an appeal before the Ld.CIT(A), but remained unsuccessful. Therefore, assessee is left with no other alternative but to knock on the doors of the Tribunal with this appeal praying for justice. 8. The Ld. Counsel for the assessee, argued that assessee did not pay the “on- money”. The Ld. Counsel for the assessee, also submitted written submission submitted before us, which is reproduced as under: “1. The assessee is a partnership firm engaged in rendering professional services of accounting, bookkeeping, and taxation consultancy services during the year under appeal. 2. In the assessee’s case, the Id. AO has made an addition of ₹9,00,000/- for A.Y. 2019-20 and 98,530/- for A.Y. 2021-22 on the allegation that the assessee had made on-money payments for the purchase of commercial properties bearing office No. 816 at “R.K. Prime” developed by M/s. Naresh Organiser Pvt Ltd (R. K. Group entity). The above addition was made on the basis of the statement of Shri Girish Vanjani and Shri Sarvanand Sonwani (key persons of R. K. Group) and the digital data in the form of a Miracle ledger found during the search. 3. In this regard, it is submitted that the Hon’ble Rajkot ITAT, in the recent decision in the case of the following buyers arising out of the same search proceedings in the case of R.K. Group and involving identical seized material, restricted the similar addition at 10% of the alleged on-money payments. Nitaben Kirtikumar Parekh (ITA No. 506/RJT/2026 decision dated 01.06.2026). – [Page No.1-5] Payal Piyushbhai Jethva (ITA No. 425/RJT/2026 decision dated 06.07.2026). – [Page No. 6-11] Manisha Sureshchandra Rajdev (ITA No. 254/RJT/2026 decision dated 30.06.2026). – [Page No. 12-14] This decision is directly applicable in the present case. 4. Therefore, without burdening the record with detailed submissions explaining that the AO cannot make addition based on unclear, unilateral and ambiguous data found from the possession of a third party in the absence of any corroborative evidence, simply relying on retracted statement and without providing an opportunity of the cross-examination, we request that the addition may kindly be restricted in line with the ratio laid down by the Hon’ble Rajkot ITAT in the above cases and oblige. 5. It is further requested that if the Hon’ble bench is inclined to take a different view in assessee’s case from that adopted in the aforesaid decisions, an opportunity may please be granted to file a detailed paperbook and make further submissions and oblige.” 9. On the other hand, Ld. DR for the revenue relied on findings of the assessing officer. 10. I have heard, both the parties and carefully gone through the submission put forth on behalf of the assessee along with the documents furnished and the case laws relied upon, and perused the fact of the case including the findings of the Ld. CIT(A) and other materials brought on record. I find that some of the documents and evidences are self-servicing, documents and evidences, filed before the lower authorities, on which reliance cannot be placed, therefore, to protect the interest of the revenue, and considering the smallness of the amount an ad hoc, estimated addition should be made in the hands of the assessee. Therefore, I find some merit in the contention of the ld. Counsel for the assessee. Hence, I find that while the case of the assessee merits some relief, at the same time entire relief cannot be permitted to the assessee. Therefore, in the appeals of assessee in ITA No.1407/Rjt/2026 and in ITA No. 1408/Rjt/2026, in my view the ends of justice would be met, if a net profit rate of @ 10% of Rs.9,00,000/- and 10% of Rs.98,530/-, which come to Rs. 90,000/- and Rs.9,853/-, respectively are adopted, since the same would take care of the inconsistencies, in the various documents and evidences submitted before the lower authorities. Therefore, in order to plug the leakage of revenue, I direct the assessing officer to make addition of Rs. 90,000/- and Rs.9,853/- respectively, by applying the normal rate of income tax (not under section 115BBE of the Act). It is also made clear that instant adjudications shall not be treated as a precedent in any preceding or succeeding assessment year. 11. In the result, both the appeals of the assessee are partly allowed, in above terms.
