Vs Commissioner of Customs (CESTAT Mumbai) Summary : The Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), Mumbai, allowed the appeal filed by Fortune Gourmet Specialities Pvt. Ltd. against the classification of imported Starbucks Frappuccino coffee beverages as caffeinated beverages attracting 28% Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST). The dispute concerned ready-to-drink, milk-based Starbucks beverages imported in Coffee, Mocha and Caramel flavours. The importer had filed Bill of Entry No. 8757295 dated 20 May 2022, declaring the goods under Customs Tariff Item (CTI) 22029930, applicable to beverages containing milk and attracting 12% IGST. However, during faceless assessment, the customs authorities treated the imported coffee beverage as a caffeinated beverage falling under CTI 22029990 and applied 28% IGST. The Commissioner (Appeals) upheld that assessment, leading to the appeal before the Tribunal. The importer contended that the beverages were manufactured using milk as their principal base ingredient and contained natural coffee extract rather than added synthetic caffeine. It relied upon the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) regulations, particularly the standards applicable to flavoured milk and caffeinated beverages, to demonstrate that the two categories were fundamentally different. According to the importer, flavoured milk products were milk-based, whereas caffeinated beverages regulated under FSSAI Regulation 2.10.6(2) were water-based formulations. The importer also relied upon an earlier FSSAI review petition order dated 27 February 2018, manufacturer’s declarations, certificates of analysis and product specifications supporting classification as milk-based beverages. The financial dispute arose because the customs assessment imposed IGST of ₹97,517.60 at 28%, whereas the importer maintained that the applicable liability was ₹41,793.27 at 12%. The importer consequently claimed that excess IGST of ₹55,724.36 had been paid. It sought correction of the assessment so that the excess amount could subsequently be claimed as a refund. The importer also submitted that provisional clearance against bond and security under Section 18 of the Customs Act, 1962, had not been permitted. The Revenue defended the assessment by relying upon the presence of caffeine in the product and the reasoning adopted by the Commissioner (Appeals). The Tribunal examined the product packaging, ingredient declarations, applicable tariff entries, Harmonised System explanatory notes and FSSAI regulatory classifications. It observed that the imported Starbucks product was a milk-based coffee beverage, while conventional caffeinated energy drinks were water-based products subject to separate regulatory requirements. The ingredients included milk, water, sugar, coffee, cocoa powder, potassium carbonate and natural coffee flavouring. The Tribunal distinguished caffeine naturally present in coffee extracts from synthetic caffeine added to formulated energy drinks. It also examined the differences in product labelling, including the high-caffeine declarations and consumption warnings applicable to regulated caffeinated beverages. The Tribunal further examined the FSSAI Food Category System and the product characteristics of Starbucks Frappuccino in comparison with commercially available caffeinated beverages, including Red Bull, Hell Energy and Cloud 9. It observed that milk-based coffee beverages could fall under the applicable coffee preparation or dairy-based drink classifications, depending upon the characterising ingredients. In contrast, the separate regulatory category for caffeinated beverages governed specified water-based formulations. The Tribunal considered the product’s milk base, naturally occurring coffee caffeine and packaging disclosures decisive in distinguishing it from beverages covered by the higher IGST rate. On tariff classification, the Tribunal held that CTI 22029930 specifically covered beverages containing milk, whereas CTI 22029990 represented the residual category for other beverages. Relying upon the Supreme Court’s decision in Hamdard (Waqf) Laboratories, which referred to Dunlop India Ltd. v. Union of India, the Tribunal reiterated that goods reasonably covered by a specific tariff entry should not be placed under a residuary entry. Since the imported Starbucks Frappuccino beverages were undisputedly milk-based, the specific classification under CTI 22029930 prevailed over the general classification under CTI 22029990. The Tribunal concluded that the imported goods did not fall within the description of caffeinated beverages covered by Serial No. 12A of Notification No. 14/2019-Integrated Tax (Rate). Accordingly, the applicable classification was CTI 22029930, attracting 12% IGST rather than 28%. Finding no merit in the Commissioner (Appeals)’ order, the Tribunal allowed the appeal. The decision establishes the importance of product composition, applicable regulatory standards, packaging characteristics and the specific-over-residuary classification principle when determining the IGST treatment of imported beverages. The order allowed the classification appeal; it did not separately adjudicate or expressly sanction the claimed refund. By the impugned order Commissioner (Appeal) has upheld assessment made by the Deputy Commissioner Customs GR 1B, JNCH Nhava Sheva on Bill of Entry No.8757295 dated 20.05.2022, by classifying the imported goods under CTH 2202 99 90 subjected to IGST @ 28%. 2.1 Appellant, they are importers of Starbucks Frappucino, in 3 flavours, viz. Coffee, Mocha and Caramel, which is a ready to drink milk based beverage for sale in the domestic market. Bill of Entry was assessed, under faceless assessment, with IGST @ 28% for BE item Sr. No. 1, viz. 49820-DK-4506-EC SBUX FRAP COFFEE (8 X 250ML) (MILK BASED BEVERAGES) having a total quantity of 1020.60 LTR with Assessable Value INR 261862.61. This resulted in levy of IGST of INR 97517.60 @ 28% 2.3 Subsequently, appellant realized that the there goods were to be classified under CTH 2202 99 30 attracting IGST of INR 41793.27 @ 12%. The appellants were denied the option of provisional clearance against bond and security under Section 18 of the Customs Act, 1962 and were directed to proceed with clearance. 2.4 Being aggrieved by the above impugned assessment order, the appellant filed the present appeal on various grounds. The appeal was dismissed as per the impugned order referred in para 1 above. 2.5 Aggrieved appellant have filed this appeal. 3.1 We have heard Shri Anirudh Nansi, Advocate for the appellant and Shri C S Vinod, Authorized Representative for the revenue. 3.2 Arguing for the appellant learned Counsel submitted that: > Imported goods are milk based flavoured beverages and classified under FSSAI standards 2.1.3 and 1.1.2(5), i.e. Flavoured Milk. > It is already established by FSSAI Delhi that this product is covered under the aforesaid FSSAI Flavoured Milk category through the review petition order dated 27th February 2018 granted by FSSAI Delhi for the initial import consignment vide Bill of Entry 4869830 Dt 19/01/2018 under FSSAI consignment ID no.: ICA20180100002171. > the declaration from the manufacturer and the COA which establishes that the said products are Milk based Beverages with natural coffee extract. > As per FSSAI Standards and Additives Regulations, 2011, the standards for “Dairy based drinks Flavoured Milk” are defined under FSSAI category 2.1.3 and Additives as per 1.1.2. Whereas, the standards for “Caffeinated Beverage” are defined under FSSAI category 2.10.6(2). Therefore, both items are recognised by FSSAI as being completely independent and separate categories from each other. > Refer sr.2(a) under FSSAI standard of Flavoured Milk, which requires that the raw material of the product, i.e. the base, is MILK. Further in sr.2(b), the permitted ingredients include Coffee, thereby making it a Coffee flavoured milk based beverage. > Refer sr. (1) under FSSAI standard of Caffeinated Beverage, which clearly states that it is a WATER based beverage and the water should conform to the standards of packaged drinking water as per 2.10.08. Therefore, under no circumstances can a Caffeinated Beverage include a beverage which is Milk based and for a product to qualify as a Caffeinated Beverage, it essential for it to be water based only. > Imported goods are correctly classifiable under HS Code 2202 9930, i.e. Beverages containing Milk and IGST @ 12% is correctly filed by the appellant. > The 28% IGST for caffeinated beverages will not apply for the subject products. Under the rules outlined by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI), a prepared, milk-based Starbucks coffee drink (such as a Latte, Macchiato, or Cappuccino) are evaluated under a mix of product-specific standards, labeling mandates, and generic category systems. Under the Food Safety and Standards Act (FSSAI) regulations, packed energy drinks and packed coffee drinks cross into completely distinct regulatory vertical lines. FSSAI statutory enforcement directives have drawn a strict boundary between products that use synthetic/added caffeine versus those containing naturally derived agricultural caffeine. 1. Prescription II.1.1.2 & II.2.1.3.2 (b): Food Category System (FCS) These references correspond to the specialized sub-sections under the FSSAI Food Category System (FCS) hierarchy, which mandates how products are tracked for licenses and additive compliance. > Primary Classification: A milk-based Starbucks coffee falls under FCS 14.1.5: Coffee, coffee substitutes, tea, herbal infusions, and other hot cereal and grain beverages, excluding cocoa. > Sub-classification under II.2.1.3.2 (b): This specifically indexes “Ready-to-drink coffee and tea beverages” or “Fluid coffee/tea preparations.” Because a milk-based coffee blends a dairy component with a coffee extract base, it is licensed under the beverage hierarchy rather than dairy products (which sit under FCS 01.0). 2. Prescription 2.10.6: Non-Alcoholic Beverages (Water-Based/ Caffeinated) Under the Food Safety and Standards (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations, sub-regulation 2.10.6 governs Non-Alcoholic Beverages. > The “Caffeinated Beverage” Rule (2.10.6(2)): This clause regulates formulated, water-based energy drinks containing between 145 mg/L and 300 mg/L (or 320 mg/L) of total caffeine. > Exclusionary Application: A standard milk-based Starbucks coffee is exempt from the tight synthetic formulations governed by 2.10.6(2) because its caffeine is naturally derived from the agricultural bean substrate (coffee extract) rather than added synthetic caffeine anhydrous. Instead, its coffee core is checked against standard Coffee Identity Specifications (Regulation 2.4). 3. Prescription II.2.(I): Labelling, Display, and Restaurant Disclosures If a beverage contains caffeine, it automatically triggers specific declaration clauses under Schedule II of the FSS (Labelling and Display) Regulations. > Menu Labelling Mandate: Because Starbucks operates as a retail food service establishment, it is governed under the FSS (Labelling and Display) Regulations. It must state nutritional info (Energy/Calories, Allergen declarations, and Caffeine thresholds) directly on its menu boards or point-of-sale materials. > Warning Declarations: If a drink crosses typical safety parameters for vulnerable groups, the prescription requires a clear notice at the counter stating: “Contains Caffeine. Not recommended for children, pregnant or lactating women.” 4.5 A packaged Starbucks Frappuccino coffee drink is fundamentally classified under Food Category System (FCS) 14.1.5 (Coffee, coffee substitutes, tea, herbal infusions) or FCS 1.1.2 (Dairy-based Drinks) depending on whether the dairy or water-extract component serves as the characterising base formulation. It is governed by Regulation 2.4 (Cereals, Legumes, and Coffee Standards) and standard proprietary food protocols rather than the caffeinated beverage rules. Compliance Metrics & Packaging Prescriptions: > Exempt from Regulation 2.10.6(2): Even though a Frappuccino contains more than 145 mg/L of caffeine, it is exempt from the “Caffeinated Beverage” category. This is because its caffeine is 100% naturally inherent to the agricultural raw material (coffee beans), rather than added synthetic caffeine anhydrous. > Labeling Identity: It does not carry the “Caffeinated Beverage” label title. It uses standard descriptive identity tags such as “Packaged Coffee-Milk Drink” or “Milk Beverage with Coffee Extract”. Menu/Package Disclosure: It does not require the strict “500 ml per day maximum” text block required for energy drinks. However, under general Food Safety and Standards (Labelling and Display) Regulations, it must cleanly display standard nutritional declaration boxes, declaration of milk/dairy allergens, and the standard shelf-stable processing certifications. 4.6 Thus on the basis of the prescriptions, specifications and label we draw the comparative classification matrix as per FSSAI in table below: Direct Comparative Classification Matrix as per FSSAI Product Matrix Legal Product Designation FSSAI Food Category System (FCS) Core Governing Regulation Critical Package Declaration Red Bull Caffeinated Beverage (Carbonated) FCS 14.1.4.1 Regulation 2.10.6(2) “High Caffeine”, Max 500 ml per day warning, “Energy Drink” term barred. No discussion is available in the impugned order in this respect. 4.6 Correct HSN Tariff Classification for IGST: As per the HSN classification table available on https://services.gst.gov.in/services/searchhsnsac, the classification for the levy of IGST has to be determined amongst the headings of Chapter 2202.99. The descriptions and classification as per the said headings are reproduced below: 220299 OTHER: 22029910 Soya milk drinks, whether or not sweetened or flavoured 22029920 Fruit Pulp Or Fruit Juice Based Drink 22029930 Beverages containing milk 22029990 Other As the impugned imported goods undisputedly are milk based or are beverages containing milk they would be appropriately be classified under heading 22029930 which is more specific to the product rather than than the residuary entry a i.e. 22029990. It is basic principle of the classification that classification of the good is to be determined in terms of description of the tariff entries and the entry giving more specific description should be preferred over the residuary entry. In the case of Hamdard (Waqf) Laboratories [2026 (396) E.L.T. 3 (S.C.)] Hon’ble Supreme Court has held as follows: 30. It is equally settled that where a commodity reasonably falls within a specific enumerated entry, recourse to the residuary entry is impermissible. In Dunlop India Ltd. v. Union of India [(1976) 2 SCC 241 =1983 (13) E.L.T. 1566 (S.C.)] this Court cautioned against consigning goods to the “orphanage of the residuary clause” when they bear a reasonable claim to a specific entry. It was held thus : “35. When an article has, by all standards, a reasonable claim to beclassified under an enumerated item in the Tariff Schedule, it will be against the very principle of classification to deny it the parentage and consign it to an orphanage of the residuary clause.” 4.7 Further we determine the classification of the impugned imported goods after determining the following which align our classification with the prescription in FSSAI; > Dairy Substrate Base: The primary characterizing ingredient of the product is milk/dairy components, not carbonated or plain water. > Naturally Inherent Caffeine: The caffeine present in Starbucks Frappuccino is 100% naturally inherent to the agricultural raw material substrate—namely, roasted Arabica coffee bean extract. There is zero added synthetic caffeine anhydrous in the product formulation. > FSSAI Categorization: The product is licensed and monitored under FCS 14.1.5 (Coffee preparations) or FCS 1.1.2 (Dairy-based Drinks). It does not carry—nor is it legally allowed to carry—the mandatory warning labels prescribed for synthetic energy drinks under Regulation 2.10.6(2). 4.8 Thus we find that IGST rate under S. No. 12A of Notification No. 14/2019-Integrated Tax (Rate) and FSSAI classifications, the impugned imported goods are structurally isolated from the 28% luxury/sin tax brackets using its physical packaging characteristics and ingredient profile. S. No. 12A of Notification No. 14/2019-IT (Rate) explicitly defines the description of goods as “Caffeinated Beverages” classifiable under head 22029990. Since we find that the impugned goods are “Beverages Containing Milk” they are appropriately classifiable under heading 22029930 and will be outside the purview of entry 12A of the Notification No 14/2019-IT. 4.9 We do not find any merits in the impugned order. 5.1 Appeal is allowed.
