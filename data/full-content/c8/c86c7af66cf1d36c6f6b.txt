Articles 4 min read Tata Sons: Holding companies reshape businesses as RBI regulatory net tightens Tata is not the only holding company proposed to seek a way out of the Reserve Bank of India’s NBFC regulatory framework. Shanghvi Finance surrendered its NBFC licence in 2023 after repaying Rs 879 crore of debt, while J Thomas Finance changed the composition of its balance sheet, reducing financial assets to 0.81% of total assets, to fall below the principal business threshold. The cases show that repayment of borrowings, changes in asset composition and changes in the nature of the business can all affect whether a company continues to meet the criteria for NBFC regulation.Tata Trusts has proposed merging Tata Electronics Systems and Tata Consulting Engineers into Tata Sons, which would give the holding company substantial operating revenue and change the proportion of its financial income. The proposal still requires Tata Sons board approval and would subsequently have to be considered by RBI. Advt The issue has gained prominence after RBI rejected Tata Sons’ application to surrender its registration as an upper layer NBFC. Tata Sons had repaid Rs21,813 crore of loans and applied for deregistration in March 2024, but RBI rejected the request in September 2026. Tata Sons now faces a separate restructuring proposal under which Tata Electronics Systems and Tata Consulting Engineers would be merged into it, increasing its operating business and revenue.An NBFC is identified under the principal business criteria when its financial assets exceed 50% of total assets, net of intangible assets, and income from financial assets exceeds 50% of gross income. Both conditions have to be satisfied. This means a company can cease to meet the definition by changing the composition of its assets or income, rather than simply shutting down its business.The casesJ Thomas Finance provides the clearest court documented example of such a restructuring. The company, which received its NBFC registration in 1998, decided in 2024 that continuing the NBFC business was no longer commercially feasible because it was unable to make adequate profits and was incurring substantial statutory compliance costs. It subsequently sought to surrender its registration.RBI initially rejected the application because J Thomas Finance continued to satisfy the principal business criteria. During proceedings before the Calcutta High Court, RBI said that reducing the PBC below 50% would not violate the conditions of the registration and could be achieved by liquidating financial assets and deploying the proceeds into a non financial business, increasing total assets while reducing the proportion of financial assets, or reducing financial income to below 50% of total income. Advt J Thomas Finance subsequently reduced its financial assets to 0.81% of total assets. Its financial income to gross income, however, remained 99.99%. Because both conditions have to be met for the PBC test, the company no longer satisfied the twin criteria after the reduction in financial assets. The High Court on April 20, 2026 set aside RBI's earlier rejection and directed the regulator to reconsider the surrender request after giving the company an opportunity of hearing.Shanghvi Finance provides a different precedent. The promoter entity, fully owned by Dilip Shanghvi and his wife, was classified as an upper layer NBFC investment and credit company by RBI in September 2022. It applied to surrender its licence on February 9, 2023 after repaying Rs879 crore of debt, including Rs341 crore of short term loans and Rs538 crore of promoter loans, according to its corporate filings. RBI allowed the surrender on May 17, 2023. Advt Following the surrender, Shanghvi Finance became an unregistered core investment company. RBI rules allow a CIC with assets of Rs100 crore or more to remain unregistered if it does not access public funds, while smaller CICs below Rs100 crore can also remain outside registration subject to the applicable conditions.The Shanghvi case differs from Tata Sons in another important respect. Shanghvi Finance's public borrowing was substantially smaller, while Tata Sons had Rs21,813 crore of loans before it became debt free. Tata Sons also has a much larger asset base. Its standalone assets were Rs2.01 lakh crore as of March 31, 2026, according to Mint, putting it above the Rs1 lakh crore asset threshold used in RBI's latest upper layer classification. Advt The three cases therefore illustrate different routes through the regulatory framework. Shanghvi Finance exited after repaying its borrowings and receiving RBI approval. J Thomas Finance changed the composition of its assets sufficiently to fall below the principal business threshold and obtained a court direction for RBI to reconsider its surrender request. Tata Sons, despite repaying its borrowings and seeking deregistration, remains an upper layer NBFC after RBI rejected its application.
