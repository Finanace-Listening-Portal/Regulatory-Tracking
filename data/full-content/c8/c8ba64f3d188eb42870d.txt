Articles 3 min read Fairfax's IDBI takeover puts CSB Bank at crossroads: Stake sale or merger? Canadian billionaire Prem Watsa's Fairfax will have two options to resolve its existing CSB Bank holding — sell the stake or merge CSB with IDBI Bank — after acquiring 60.72 per cent of IDBI for Rs 52,878 crore. Fairfax currently owns 40 per cent of CSB, with the eventual structure subject to RBI and SEBI regulations. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Updated On Oct 14, 2026 at 07:51 PM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 134943038; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Union Cabinet on Wednesday approved Fairfax-backed Canada Inc as the strategic buyer for IDBI Bank, with the investor acquiring a 60.72 per cent stake for Rs 52,878 crore. The Government of India will sell 30.48 per cent and LIC 30.24 per cent, leaving them with 15 per cent and 19 per cent, respectively, in IDBI Bank.As per the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) regulations, Fairfax will be expected to either merge or exit its CSB Bank holding within two years of signing the share purchase agreement (SPA).The issue stems from Fairfax's existing ownership of CSB Bank. Its subsidiary FIH Mauritius Investments Ltd had reduced its holding in CSB to 40 per cent in June 2024 by selling a 9.7 per cent stake to comply with the Reserve Bank of India's dilution requirements. Advt Fairfax had acquired control of CSB Bank in 2018, when the lender was seeking fresh capital. The 40 per cent holding therefore represents an established banking interest that now needs to be reconciled with Fairfax's proposed control of IDBI Bank.Under RBI's ownership framework, prior RBI approval is required for acquisition of a major shareholding in a banking company, with major shareholders required to remain fit and proper. The framework also provides for a 26 per cent promoter shareholding limit after 15 years, while higher promoter holdings can be permitted earlier as part of an RBI-approved dilution plan.For Fairfax, therefore, the two-bank ownership issue is not simply a question of portfolio preference but also one of regulatory compliance.Shriram Subramanian, founder and managing director, InGovern Research Services, said Fairfax could potentially get some regulatory leeway to first consolidate IDBI Bank before deciding on the eventual structure.“The new promoter would bring in a new board and management team. The new promoter may also get some regulatory leeway for a few years to first stabilise IDBI Bank and then only think about merging, if at all, or sell off their shareholding in CSB,” Subramanian told ET BFSI. Advt He said a merger could also be considered from a business perspective given the different geographic strengths of the two lenders.“CSB has a strong presence in southern India, and would likely have complementary strengths with IDBI Bank. Banking is a question of size, and a larger bank will have pricing power on both the asset and liability sides. Hence a merger cannot be ruled out,” he said.However, Vivek Iyer, Partner and Financial Services Risk Advisory Leader, Grant Thornton Bharat, said he did not see a merger between the two banks as the likely outcome, citing limited synergies.“A lot of people in the market are suggesting that potentially a merger of CSB and IDBI may be an option. I don't see that as a possibility, because there have to be effective synergies and you can't have significant overlaps,” he said. Advt According to Iyer, CSB Bank's regional presence and niche business profile could make a separate ownership structure more valuable than a merger with IDBI Bank.“CSB is a more regional bank with a very specific niche market focus and a strong NRI angle. I believe CSB individually is a great investment, with significant upside because it is a strong brand, a strongly governed bank and a robust institution,” Iyer said.“When Fairfax sells it, they should get good value, and anybody who comes into the organisation will get a great opportunity. CSB has its own journey for growth and, with the right kind of investment, it can follow its own path instead of being integrated with something far larger and losing its significance,” he said. Advt Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw, responding to questions on the structure of the transaction and Fairfax's existing banking interests, said the deal would be governed by the applicable RBI and SEBI regulations.The government has also said that Fairfax-backed Canada Inc will now present its credentials to RBI for a fit-and-proper assessment and submit a roadmap for compliance with regulatory conditions.IDBI Bank opened at Rs 84.32 on the BSE and closed at Rs 90.44, gaining 9.07 per cent from its previous close of Rs 82.92.
