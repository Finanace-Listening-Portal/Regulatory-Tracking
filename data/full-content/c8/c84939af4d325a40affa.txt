Articles 2 min read Indian-origin leaders head global financial services firms worth $1 trillion: Report Financial Services emerges as the second-largest sector in the HSBC Hurun Global Indians List 2026, with 31 companies and a combined valuation of $1 trillion. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Nov 12, 2026 at 02:52 PM IST var news_msid = 135763201; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Financial Services has emerged as the second-largest sector represented in the HSBC Hurun Global Indians List 2026, with 31 companies led by individuals of Indian origin and a combined valuation of $1 trillion, accounting for 6.7% of the total value of companies featured in the list.The list features 236 individuals leading 200 companies headquartered outside India. The combined value of these companies stands at $15 trillion.Software & Services leads the industry rankings with 53 companies, followed by Financial Services with 31 and Healthcare with 21. While software companies account for the largest share of the overall valuation at $8.7 trillion, Financial Services continues to hold a significant position in the global business landscape shaped by Indian-origin leadership. Advt Among the prominent names from the financial services sector is Gunjan Kedia, President and CEO of U.S. Bancorp, whose company is valued at $95 billion. She ranks among the leading women executives featured in the list. Other financial services leaders include Rajeev Mittal of Eastspring Investments, whose company is valued at $24 billion.The report also highlighted the profitability of financial services businesses, with the sector accounting for three of the ten most profitable companies on the list, underlining its contribution to global corporate earnings.Overall, companies featured in the ranking generated combined revenue of $2.5 trillion in 2025 and reported net profits of $416 billion. Of the 200 companies, 127 are publicly listed and 73 are privately held.The findings also pointed to the growing influence of Indian-origin professionals in global corporate leadership. Professionals account for 53% of the individuals featured, and the companies they lead represent 92% of the total list value, at $14.1 trillion.The report further noted that 58% of the leaders completed their undergraduate education in India, highlighting the contribution of Indian educational institutions to the global talent pipeline.
