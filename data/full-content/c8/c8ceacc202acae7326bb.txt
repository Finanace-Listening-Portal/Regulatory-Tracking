Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Lagisetty Viswanatham Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) Section 69A Addition Cannot Rest on Staggered Demonetisation Deposits When Records Establish the Source of Cash The Hyderabad Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held that cash deposited on different dates during demonetisation could not be treated as unexplained money under section 69A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, where the taxpayer’s cash book, debtor ledgers and confirmations established the source of the deposits. The Assessing Officer considered it improbable that a person holding sufficient cash on 8 November 2016 would deposit it in instalments. The Tribunal examined the records and found that the available cash explained the deposits and that the banking pattern showed no abnormal deviation from the rest of the financial year. Facts of the Case The assessee carried on wholesale trading in pharmaceuticals under the name Visweswara Medical & Surgical Agencies and was also engaged in money-lending. He filed his return for AY 2017–18 declaring total income of ₹12,50,890. During scrutiny, the Assessing Officer noticed cash deposits aggregating to ₹42,55,000, made between 15 November and 28 December 2016, and called upon the assessee to explain their source. The assessee explained that his books showed a cash balance of ₹43,45,781 as on 8 November 2016, accumulated from collections against earlier sales of pharmaceutical products. He attributed the deposits to that balance and furnished his cash book and the ledger accounts of the sundry debtors from whom the collections had been made. Why the Assessing Officer Made the Addition The Assessing Officer reasoned that if the assessee already held the claimed cash balance on 8 November, he would ordinarily have deposited the amount on a single day. The deposits spread across November and December were therefore considered improbable. The Assessing Officer invoked the test of human probabilities discussed by the Supreme Court in CIT v. Durga Prasad More [1971] 82 ITR 540 (SC) and Sumati Dayal v. CIT [1995] 214 ITR 801 (SC). The Assessing Officer accepted the explanation to the extent of ₹6,70,000, attributing that amount to income of the previous year, and added the balance of ₹35,85,000 under section 69A. The CIT(A), National Faceless Appeal Centre, sustained the addition, taking the view that the assessee had failed to furnish satisfactory evidence of the source and genuineness of the deposits. How the Tribunal Tested the Explanation Before the Tribunal, the assessee relied on the bank statement, cash book and confirmations from sundry debtors, along with evidence of their identity. He also submitted that the deposits during demonetisation were broadly consistent with his banking pattern across the year. The Revenue questioned the size of the cash balance in light of the nature of the business and cash held in earlier months, and maintained that the debtor collections had been inadequately substantiated. The Tribunal examined the cash book and debtor ledgers and found that cash receipts against sales were broadly uniform from April 2016 to March 2017. It also examined the year’s comparative figures for total sales, cash sales and bank deposits. The deposits in November and December did not show any abnormal deviation when compared with April to October 2016 and January to March 2017. This comparison supported the assessee’s explanation that cash already available on 8 November funded the later deposits. The Tribunal found that the Assessing Officer’s doubts about the debtor collections ran contrary to the evidence on record. Coordinate Bench Decision Followed The Tribunal followed Devraju Gajji v. ITO, ITA No. 1051/Hyd/2025, order dated 20 February 2026. In that case, the Hyderabad Bench accepted the cash book as evidence of sufficient cash in hand on 8 November 2016. It recognised that the taxpayer could deposit the cash on different dates within the period permitted under the applicable Government and RBI directions. The distinction mattered because a later bank deposit did not, by itself, establish that the underlying cash had been received after demonetisation. Applying that reasoning to the records before it, the Tribunal set aside the CIT(A)’s order, directed deletion of the ₹35,85,000 addition and allowed the assessee’s appeal. Author’s Comments The ruling illustrates how the test of human probabilities should be applied when the taxpayer produces records that can be checked against actual business activity. The dates of deposit may justify asking how the cash was accumulated and why it was banked in instalments. In this case, however, the cash book established availability, the debtor ledgers and confirmations supported the underlying collections, and the full-year comparison put the November and December deposits in context. Together, those records answered the inference drawn from the timing of the deposits. For a similar section 69A dispute, the most persuasive reconciliation would trace the cash balance on 8 November 2016 to the underlying sales or debtor collections and then match the subsequent deposits against that balance. A month-wise comparison of cash sales, collections and deposits can help test any allegation of an unusual spike. Here, the Tribunal’s conclusion depended on that evidence and the consistency it showed across the year. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF ITAT HYDERABAD This appeal filed by the assessee is directed against the order of the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC), Delhi, dated 16.09.2025, and pertains to assessment year 2017- 18. 2. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee an individual engaged in the business of wholesale trading in pharmaceuticals under the name and style of M/s. Visweswara Medical & Surgical Agencies and also in money- lending business, filed his Return of Income for AY 2017-18 on 09.09.2017 declaring total income of Rs.12,50,890/-. The case was selected for scrutiny and during the course of assessment proceedings, the AO noticed that the assessee has made cash deposits of Rs.42,55,000/- into his bank account during demonetization period. The AO called upon the assessee to explain the source. In response, the assessee submitted that, he was having opening cash balance of Rs.43,45,781/- on 08.11.2016, which was accumulated out of receipts from sundry debtors for sale of pharmaceutical products and out of opening balance, he had deposited sum of Rs.42,55,000/- starting from 15.11.2016 to 28.12.2016. To support his arguments, filed necessary cash book and also ledger account of the parties from whom the assessee has received cash. The AO after considering relevant submissions of the assessee and also taken note of certain judicial precedents observed that although, the assessee claims to have had opening cash balance of Rs.43,45,781/-, but going by the cash deposits pattern, it is highly improbable that the assessee had deposited cash in the month of November, 2016 & December, 2016 and further, if the arguments of the assessee are true that he has opening balance as on 08.11.2016, the assessee would have deposited cash in one day. Since the arguments of the assessee is contrary to theory of human probability in view of the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of CIT v. Durga Prasad More reported in [1971] 82 ITR 540 (SC) and also in the case of Sumati Dayal v. CIT reported in [1995] 214 ITR 801 (SC), rejected the explanation of the assessee for the entire source out of opening balance and accepted explanation to the extent of Rs.6,70,000/- out of previous year income and balance amount of Rs.35,85,000/- has been added u/s.69A of the Income Tax Act, 1961 [in short “the Act”] 3. Aggrieved by the assessment order, the assessee preferred an appeal before the Ld.CIT(A). Before the Ld.CIT(A), the assessee has reiterated his submissions made before the AO and argued that, he was having sufficient opening cash balance as on 08.11.2016, which is the source for cash deposits into bank account during demonetization period. The Ld.CIT(A) after considering relevant submissions of the assessee and also taken note of relevant reasons given by the AO for making addition u/s.69A of the Act, observed that the explanation of the assessee in light of business activity of wholesale trading in pharmaceutical products, which is highly cash incentives, is highly improbable going by the theory of human probability, because, the assessee claims to have received cash from the sundry debtors, however, failed to file relevant supporting evidences to establish the source and genuineness of the large cash deposits. Therefore, rejected explanation of the assessee and upheld the addition made by the AO. 4. Aggrieved by the order of the Ld.CIT(A), the assessee is now in appeal before this Tribunal. 5. The Ld. Counsel for the assessee, Sri K. A. Sai Prasad, referring to various evidences, including relevant bank account statement and cash book submitted that, the AO never disputed that the assessee having opening cash balance of Rs.43,45,781/- as on 08.11.2016, but the AO denied the explanation only on the ground that the explanation of the assessee is contrary to the theory of human probability, even though, the assessee has substantiated the explanation with supporting evidences like confirmation from the sundry debtors along with their identity. Further, the AO has not made out any case of abnormal increase in cash deposits during demonetization period when compared to earlier period and in fact there is no change in cash deposits for the entire Financial Year, which is almost equal right from April, 2016 to March, 2017. Since there is no abnormal deviation in cash deposits during demonetization period, the AO ought to have accepted the explanation of the assessee out of opening balance. Therefore, he submitted that the addition made by the AO should be deleted. In this regard, he relied upon the decision of the ITAT Hyderabad Bench in the case of Devraju Gajji v. ITO in ITA No.1051/Hyd/2025 order dated 20.02.2026. 6. The Ld.Sr.AR for the Revenue, on the other hand, supporting the order of the AO & the Ld.CIT(A) submitted that, the opening cash balance shown by the assessee as on 08.11.2016 is highly improbable going by the nature of business and the cash balance held in the previous months. Further, the assessee has failed to substantiate cash receipts from sundry debtors with supporting evidence. In the absence of relevant supporting evidence, the AO has rightly treated the cash deposits into bank account as unexplained money of the assessee. The Ld.CIT(A) after considering relevant submissions, has rightly sustained the addition made by the AO. Therefore, he submitted that the order of the Ld.CIT(A) should be upheld and addition made by the AO should be sustained 7. We have heard both the parties, perused the materials available on record and had gone through orders of the authorities below. There is no dispute with regard to amount of cash deposits during demonetization period and explanation of the assessee out of opening cash balance as on 08.11.2016. In fact, the assessee has made cash deposits of Rs.42,55,000/- into his bank account from 15.11.2016 to 28.12.2016 and explained the source for said cash deposits out of opening cash balance of Rs.43,45,781/- on 08.11.2016. Further, to support the cash balance, the assessee has filed ledger account of various sundry debtors and also confirmation from them. Upon perusal of relevant evidences, including cash book and ledger account of debtors, we find that there is enough cash receipts during demonetization period and further, the cash receipts against sales are uniform throughout the Financial Year staring from April, 2016 to March, 2017. Therefore, in our considered view, the doubt expressed by the AO on the genuineness of cash receipts from sundry debtors is contrary to the evidences available on record. Further, upon perusal of relevant abstract of total sales, cash sales and cash deposits into bank account for the Financial Year under consideration, we find that, there is no abnormal deviation in cash deposits during the month of November & December, 2016 and when compared to April to October, 2016 & January to March, 2017. In fact, the cash sales, cash deposits into bank account is almost uniform for throughout the year and if you go by the explanation of the assessee, in our considered view, it appears that the source for cash deposits into bank account was out of opening cash balance available as on 08.11.2016. Since the assessee has explained the source out of opening cash balance, in our considered view, the AO ought not to have made addition towards cash deposits u/s.69A of the Act. 8. The assessee has relied upon the decision of the ITAT Hyderabad Bench in the case of Devraju Gajji v. ITO (supra), where the coordinate Bench of the Tribunal, under identical set of facts, deleted the addition made by the AO towards cash deposits into bank account. The relevant findings of the Tribunal are as under: 8. We have heard both the parties, perused the material on record and gone through the orders of the authorities below. There is no dispute with regard to the fact that the assessee has made cash deposit in SBNs on various dates starting from 10.11.2016 to 30.12.2016, totalling to Rs.26,33,500/-, The appellant explained the source for cash deposit into bank account during demonetisation period out of realization from sundry debtors. The A.O. had accepted the explanation of the assessee with regard to source to the extent of Rs.4,10,000/-, being the amount of cash deposited into bank account on 10.11.2016 and in respect of remaining cash deposits, starting from 11.11.2016 and upto 30.12.2016 for Rs.22,23,500/-. The A.O. has not accepted the explanation of the assessee on the ground that, the appellant cannot accept SBNs from 08.11.2016 onwards, either against sales or from sundry debtors. Except this observation, the A.O. has not made any observation with regard to books of accounts submitted by the assessee, including cash book extract for the month of November, 2016, statement showing cash sales, cash receipts, cash deposits and closing cash balance submitted by the assessee for the entire financial year. The A.O. was only on the point that the appellant claims to have realized from sundry debtors, which is the source for cash deposit into the bank account and the same cannot be accepted, because the appellant is not supposed to accept SBNs after 08.11.2016. We find that nowhere, the appellant stated either before the A.O. or before the CIT(A) that he has received cash from sundry debtors after 08.11.2016. It is only the A.O. and CIT(A) presumed that the appellant has received cash in SBNs after 08.11.2016. Otherwise, evidences submitted by the assessee including relevant cash book extract for the month of November, 2016 clearly shows that the appellant was having sufficient cash in hand of Rs.30,09,578/- on 08.11.2016, which was carried forward from 01.11.2016, which is the source for cash deposited into bank account in SBNs on different dates. In our considered view, once the appellant has filed relevant evidences to prove that, he was having sufficient cash in hand before the date of demontisation, then, the appellant is free to deposit said cash in hand available in SBNs after 09.11.2016 any date and up to 31.12.2016 as per the guidelines or notifications issued by the Government and RBI. Since the appellant has filed relevant evidences to prove availability of cash in hand as on 08.11.2016, which is sufficient to explain cash deposit of Rs.26,33,500/- into bank account during demonetization period, in our considered view, the A.O. ought not to have made additions of Rs.22,23,500/- u/s 69A of the Act. The Ld.CIT(A) without considering relevant facts simply sustained the additions made by the A.O. Thus, we set aside the order of the Ld.CIT(A) and direct the A.O. to delete the addition of Rs.22,23,500/- made towards cash deposit into the bank account during the demonetization period u/s 69A of the Act. 9. In this view of the matter and considering the facts and circumstances of the case and also by following the decision of the ITAT Hyderabad Bench in the case of Devraju Gajji v. ITO (supra), we are of the considered view that the assessee was able to explain source for cash deposits into bank account out of opening cash balance as on 08.11.2016. The AO & the Ld.CIT(A) without appreciating the relevant facts simply made addition towards cash deposits u/s.69A of the Act. Thus, we set aside the order of the Ld.CIT(A) and direct the AO to delete addition made towards cash deposits of Rs.35,85,000/- u/s.69A of the Act. 10. In the result, appeal filed by the assessee is allowed. Order pronounced on the 18th day of November, 2026.
