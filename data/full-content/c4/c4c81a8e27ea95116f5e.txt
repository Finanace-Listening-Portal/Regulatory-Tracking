Summary: The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) has introduced significant GST changes effective from 1 November 2026 through Notifications 2/2026 to 10/2026-Central Tax (Rate), alongside amendments to registration, tax deduction at source, e-invoicing and the CGST Rules. The changes include reduction of GST on retreaded tractor tyres from 18% to 5%, exemption for psyllium seeds, revised input tax credit conditions for hotels, restaurants, catering and electric vehicle transport, and additional exemptions for specified services. The reverse charge mechanism has been extended to supplies of used cooking oil, plastic waste, tyre scrap and electronic waste by unregistered suppliers to registered recipients. The registration and GST TDS framework previously applicable to metal scrap has also been extended to these additional categories. Registered buyers must examine their liability to deduct 2% GST TDS on qualifying purchases exceeding ₹2.5 lakh per contract, while purchases from unregistered suppliers may attract reverse charge. Other important amendments address e-commerce delivery services, cab rentals, toll rights, post-supply discounts, refunds and special procedures for Toll-Operate-Transfer concessionaires. Businesses must update their tax classifications, assess input tax credit eligibility, verify suppliers’ registration status and apply the transitional provisions of Section 14 of the CGST Act to supplies occurring around the effective date. What buyers and sellers of scrap must do Registered buyers Buying from an unregistered seller: pay the GST under reverse charge. Issue a self-invoice (section 31(3)(f)) and a payment voucher (section 31(3)(g)), pay the tax in cash, and claim the credit afterwards. Buying from a registered seller, contract value above ₹2.5 lakh (excluding GST): deduct 2% from the payment. Register as a tax deductor under section 24(vi) if not already registered (FORM GST REG-07), file GSTR-7 by the 10th of the next month, and let the seller see the TDS certificate. Check every supplier’s status at the time of purchase. The rule that applies turns on whether the seller is registered. Plan for e-invoicing: from 1 January 2027, buyers who must issue e-invoices also report inward supplies taxed under reverse charge from unregistered persons (4/2026-CT). Sellers Register once aggregate turnover crosses the threshold, even if every buyer pays the tax under reverse charge (6/2026-CT). Once registered, charge GST on the invoice. Expect 2% TDS on contracts above ₹2.5 lakh and claim it in the electronic cash ledger after the buyer files GSTR-7. Supplies that straddle 1 November Section 14 of the CGST Act decides the rate when the supply, the invoice and the payment fall on different sides of a rate change. In short: of those three events, the two on the same side of 1 November decide the rate. Read the section and its proviso on bank credits before advising on a live case. What did not change, and a checklist Hotel rooms up to ₹7,500 a day and restaurants outside specified premises stay at 5%; only their credit condition eased. No change was made to late fees or interest. Who must issue e-invoices is unchanged (aggregate turnover above ₹5 crore). A checklist for the next return cycle: Update item masters: retreaded tractor tyres (4012) at 5%, psyllium seeds (1211 90 13) at Nil. Restaurant, hotel and catering clients at 5%: review which same-line input services now qualify for credit from 1 November. Scrap and e-waste buyers: list unregistered suppliers, set up self-invoices and payment vouchers, and get a TDS registration if needed. Scrap and e-waste sellers: register once turnover crosses the threshold. Delivery partners selling through apps: the e-commerce operator now pays the tax on all delivery other than postal and courier (9/2026-CT(R)). Companies hiring cabs with fuel: reverse charge applies unless the supplier’s invoice shows CGST at 9% (8/2026-CT(R)). Post-supply discounts: from 1 November a credit note with the buyer’s credit reversal is enough; no prior agreement is needed (section 15(3)(b), 3/2026-CT). Non-resident taxable persons: GSTR-5 is now due on the 13th (rule 63, 5/2026-CT).
