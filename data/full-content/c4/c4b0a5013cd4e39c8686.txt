Summary: Punjab National Bank’s official product page identifies its tax-saving term deposit as PNB Tax Shield – Fixed Deposit Scheme. The scheme is for eligible individuals and Hindu undivided families, has a five-year tenure, accepts deposits from ₹100 in multiples of ₹100 up to ₹1.50 lakh per financial year, and attributes the tax deduction in a joint deposit only to the first holder. PNB’s official rate page, checked on 24 November 2026, states that the PNB Tax Saver Fixed Deposit Scheme rate effective 1 February 2026 is 6.10% per annum for the public and 6.60% for eligible senior citizens for five years; that specific product table is distinct from PNB’s ordinary retail term-deposit rates revised from 1 November 2026. For tax year 2026-27, eligibility should be tested under section 123 and Schedule XV of the Income-tax Act, 2025, and the deduction is generally unavailable under the section 202 concessional regime. Interest remains taxable and may face TDS. DICGC protection aggregates eligible principal and interest across all PNB branches in the same right and capacity up to ₹5 lakh. The official features describe a five-year term deposit designed to satisfy the notified tax-saver conditions, not an ordinary flexible fixed deposit. A customer intending to claim the statutory deduction should ensure the application and receipt identify the tax-saver product rather than relying only on a five-year maturity date. The published eligibility includes individuals, including specified customers requiring assisted operation, joint accounts and Hindu undivided families. A joint receipt may be issued to two adults or to an adult and minor under the product conditions, but the bank states that the deduction is available only to the first holder. Holder order must therefore be finalised before booking; changing the economic understanding between joint holders does not rewrite the statutory certificate. The five-year lock-in is a liquidity constraint, not a rate feature A notified tax-saving bank term deposit is designed around a statutory five-year lock-in. The depositor should not assume the premature-closure or loan facility available for an ordinary PNB fixed deposit. The product page and the notified scheme conditions should be read with the application, and the branch or digital channel should confirm the operational restrictions before funds are placed. Emergency and succession planning should precede booking Because the deposit is intended to remain locked, emergency cash, upcoming liabilities and debt repayments should be funded separately. Nomination and joint-holder instructions should be completed and preserved. Death-claim or exceptional-payment rules should be verified from current PNB procedures; they should not be reconstructed from a different bank’s tax-saver product or from ordinary-deposit terms. At maturity, automatic renewal requires attention. Renewal into an ordinary deposit is not automatically a new qualifying tax-saver contribution, and a fresh deduction cannot be assumed without a new eligible deposit satisfying current law. The maturity mandate, destination account and renewal instructions should be reviewed before the due date. Section 123 and Schedule XV govern tax-year 2026-27 deduction analysis From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 governs tax year 2026-27. Section 123 allows an individual or HUF a deduction for eligible payments and deposits listed in Schedule XV, up to an aggregate ₹1,50,000, subject to the Schedule’s conditions. The current Act preserves the substance of the earlier section 80C framework but uses new numbering. Files for deposits made in 2026-27 should therefore record section 123 and Schedule XV rather than relying solely on legacy wording displayed on a bank page. The ₹1.50 lakh amount is shared across the eligible items in section 123. Provident-fund contributions, qualifying insurance premiums, tuition fees and other listed payments can consume the same ceiling. The product’s own maximum does not create a separate ₹1.50 lakh benefit. Prepare one consolidated schedule of eligible payments and retain the PNB receipt as evidence for the deposit component. The concessional regime normally excludes the deduction The Income Tax Department’s transition guidance confirms that section 123 is not available when income is charged under the new concessional regime in section 202. A deposit may still be opened for its contractual terms, but the tax deduction depends on the taxpayer’s applicable regime and valid option. Tax benefit should be computed from the complete return position, not assumed from the product name. Interest credited or paid by PNB remains taxable under the applicable provisions and accounting method. A cumulative option may defer cash receipt but does not automatically defer every tax recognition event. Obtain the annual interest certificate and reconcile it to the Annual Information Statement and Form 26AS. For tax year 2026-27, section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies to resident interest withholding. The official table shows a ₹50,000 threshold for a person other than a senior citizen and ₹1,00,000 for a senior citizen when interest other than interest on securities is paid by a banking company, subject to the provision’s conditions. The published TDS rate is generally 10%, but PAN, status, declarations and other law can affect withholding. TDS is not the final tax calculation Interest can be taxable even when it remains below the TDS threshold, and TDS can exceed or fall short of final liability. Aggregate PNB interest across branches and deposits instead of reviewing only this certificate. Any permitted non-deduction declaration or lower-deduction position should be examined using the current form and eligibility rules; an invalid declaration does not exempt the interest. DICGC protection is bank-wide and capacity-specific DICGC states that eligible deposits such as savings, current, recurring and fixed deposits are insured up to ₹5,00,000 for each depositor in a bank in the same right and same capacity. Principal and accrued interest share the ceiling. Deposits held in different PNB branches are aggregated, so each tax-saver receipt does not receive a separate ₹5 lakh cover. Joint accounts and accounts held in different legal capacities are treated under DICGC rules. A depositor should calculate total eligible PNB balances, including accrued interest, and not infer additional cover from account numbers or branches. Deposit insurance is a statutory protection on the covered aggregate in specified events; it is not a guarantee of instant access during the five-year lock-in or of returns above the insured limit. Frequently Asked Questions on PNB Tax Shield What is the official product name? Punjab National Bank’s page identifies it as PNB Tax Shield – Fixed Deposit Scheme; the rate table calls it PNB Tax Saver Fixed Deposit Scheme. What is the five-year rate checked on 24 November 2026? The official product-specific table effective 1 February 2026 shows 6.10% per annum for the public and 6.60% for an eligible senior citizen. Why not use PNB’s ordinary five-year rate revised in November? The bank publishes a separate tax-saver table. The ordinary domestic FD rate belongs to a different product and should not replace the tax-saver rate. How much can be deposited? The product page states a ₹100 minimum, deposits in multiples of ₹100 and a maximum of ₹1.50 lakh per financial year. Who claims the deduction in a joint deposit? PNB states that only the first holder is eligible for the deduction, subject to applicable tax law. Is the interest exempt from tax? No. The qualifying principal deduction does not exempt interest, and TDS is only a withholding mechanism. Is every PNB tax-saver FD insured up to ₹5 lakh separately? No. DICGC aggregates eligible deposits across PNB for the same depositor in the same right and capacity, including principal and accrued interest.
