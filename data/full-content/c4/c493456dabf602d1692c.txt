Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Nov 17, 2026 at 08:01 AM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 135907718; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook India's insurance regulator, the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI), pursued an expansive regulatory agenda in 2026, with reforms spanning foreign investment, capital management, financial reporting, distribution economics and policyholder protection. The year also saw the regulator move towards a more risk-sensitive solvency regime, tighten oversight of insurance intermediaries and initiate changes aimed at making insurance products and distribution more transparent.As the sector prepares for a new phase of growth, several of these decisions could have a lasting bearing on insurers' business models, capital strategies and customer relationships. Advt 100% FDI regime opens the door to foreign capitalOne of the biggest structural developments was the operationalisation of the Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025, which came into effect on February 5, 2026.The legislation raised the foreign direct investment (FDI) limit in Indian insurance companies from 74% to 100%, aimed at attracting long-term capital, facilitating technology transfer and deepening insurance penetration.It also reduced the minimum net-owned fund requirement for foreign reinsurance branches from Rs 5,000 crore to Rs 1,000 crore, potentially making India more accessible to overseas reinsurers.Policyholder protection gets a dedicated institutional mechanismIn July, IRDAI notified the Policyholders' Education and Protection Fund Regulations, 2026, operationalising the fund created under the amended insurance laws.The fund is intended to support insurance awareness and literacy, strengthen grievance redressal, improve technology-driven policyholder services and facilitate the tracing and settlement of unclaimed insurance money.The move marked an effort to create a more institutionalised approach towards consumer protection, beyond individual grievance-handling mechanisms.Ind AS implementation changes financial reporting Advt Another significant regulatory shift came with IRDAI's March 30 amendment to the regulations governing actuarial, finance and investment functions of insurers.The amendment mandated financial statements prepared in accordance with applicable Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS), with the new requirements taking effect from April 1, 2026.The transition is expected to bring greater consistency and comparability in insurers' financial reporting, particularly in the recognition and measurement of insurance contracts, assets and liabilities.IRDAI also constituted a Joint Expert Group in April to address implementation challenges and issued further clarifications during the year. The transition has implications for insurers' reporting systems, actuarial assumptions and financial disclosures. Advt IRDAI pushes for stronger cyber security governanceWith increasing digitisation of insurance distribution and servicing, cyber security emerged as another regulatory priority.In April, IRDAI issued revised Information and Cyber Security Guidelines, 2026, replacing the earlier framework. The guidelines strengthened board-level oversight, accountability and security governance across insurers and insurance intermediaries.Among the changes was a requirement for the Information Security Risk Management Committee to meet at least quarterly, compared with twice a year under the earlier framework.The revised requirements also strengthened the role of chief information security officers and incident-response planning, reflecting the growing importance of cyber resilience across the insurance ecosystem. Advt Greater flexibility in capital and investment normsAt its 137th Authority Meeting in July, IRDAI approved a series of measures aimed at providing insurers greater flexibility in capital formation, investments and corporate restructuring.The decisions included amendments to regulations governing actuarial, finance and investment functions, as well as registration, capital structure, transfer of shares and amalgamation of insurers.The changes were intended to facilitate capital infusion, streamline ownership transfers and corporate restructuring, and improve insurers' financial resilience while retaining regulatory safeguards.Earlier in February, IRDAI had also clarified investment provisions relating to Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs), including investments involving excuse rights and exposure limits. This was aimed at providing greater clarity and flexibility in insurers' investment activities.Intermediary regulation shifts towards accountabilityIRDAI's July amendments to the Insurance Intermediaries Regulations marked an important change in the way distribution entities are registered and supervised.The earlier periodic renewal-based registration framework was replaced by continuous registration, subject to payment of annual fees and ongoing compliance.The regulator also mandated the tagging of an authorised salesperson to every insurance proposal, policy and certificate of insurance. This creates greater traceability of individual sales and could help establish accountability in cases of mis-selling and customer grievances.The amendments also strengthened disclosure, governance and reporting requirements for intermediaries, signalling a shift towards more continuous regulatory supervision.Commission and distribution reforms put industry economics under scrutinyIn September, IRDAI released a major consultation paper titled 'Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution', proposing a significant overhaul of the industry's distribution model.The regulator proposed a five-year glide path to bring down Expenses of Management (EoM) limits for life insurers to 12.5% and general insurers to 20%.It also proposed product- and channel-specific commission limits, greater scrutiny of distributor remuneration and measures to curb mis-selling, including restrictions on compulsory bundling of insurance with loans.The proposals seek to shift the focus from premium acquisition towards persistency, affordability, quality of advice and policyholder outcomes.The consultation has potentially significant implications for bancassurance, corporate agents, brokers and other distribution channels. However, these remain proposals and are subject to the regulatory consultation and finalisation process.Risk-Based Capital framework moves closer to developmentTowards November, IRDAI took another significant step towards introducing a Risk-Based Capital (RBC) framework.On November 10, the regulator constituted two expert working groups, covering life and non-life insurers, to develop the proposed framework in alignment with Ind AS.The groups have been given one month to prepare recommendations, an exposure draft of the proposed regulations and an accompanying approach paper.The proposed framework aims to move insurers away from the existing factor-based solvency regime towards capital requirements that more closely reflect the risks undertaken by individual companies.The exercise could influence how insurers assess solvency, allocate capital and manage underwriting and investment risks. The final methodology and implementation timeline, however, are yet to be determined.Motor insurance moves towards a four-layer structureOne of the significant developments towards the end of the year was IRDAI's November directive introducing a four-layer motor insurance policy structure for private cars and two-wheelers.Issued in compliance with a Supreme Court direction, the framework separates motor insurance into third-party liability, legal liability to occupants, personal accident and own-damage cover.Third-party liability remains mandatory, while the other components are subject to the applicable mandatory and optional cover requirements.Insurers will also have to provide uniform policy wordings, a customer option form and a Customer Information Sheet to improve transparency around coverage and premiums.The framework is scheduled to come into force from December 15, 2026, with insurers also required to integrate the structure with Bima Sugam.The road aheadTaken together, IRDAI's regulatory actions in 2026 reflect a broader attempt to balance the insurance industry's growth ambitions with stronger financial discipline and policyholder protection.While the opening up of foreign investment and greater flexibility in capital and investment norms could support expansion, proposed commission reforms and the move towards risk-based capital could fundamentally change how insurers operate.The coming year will be important in determining how these proposals translate into binding regulations, particularly in distribution economics, solvency requirements and the implementation of Ind AS.
