Second sale of Apartment by Landowner is not liable to tax under GST Under JDA the builder has to sell the constructed area to every prospective buyer including the landowner. The landowner is one of the buyer under Joint development agreement who got the construction area from the builder. So the builder who executed the construction can only transfer such constructed are by way of sale deed. Unless the constructed area registered in favour of landowner, the land owner cannot get legal rights on such property. Hence the second sale of constructed area by the landowner is considered to be sale of building falling under S. No (5) Schedule III sales of land and building neither sale of goods nor sales of services under GST Act. Under JDA contracts. the time of supply from builder to landowner as per notification 4/2018 Dt 25/01/2018 shall arise at the time when the said developer, builder, transfers possession or the right in the constructed complex, building or civil structure, to the person supplying the development rights by entering into a conveyance deed or similar instrument (for example allotment letter). Eventually the landowner gets his share of completed building from the builder. Therefore, the sale of his share by the landowner becomes second sale of building by the landlord not liable under serial number 5 of schedule III. With regards to grant of right to develop the property cannot be a supply of service, since the landowner keeps his right with him till the end of the project is being sold. The same opinion has been upheld by Hon’ble Karnataka High Court judgment in M/S ROHAN CORPORATION INDIA PVT LTD Vs UNION OF INDIA the Hon’ble adjudged that, when the constructed immovable property whether fully constructed or partially constructed is sold, as such, without providing any construction service subsequently, the same would not attract Paragraph 5(b) of Schedule II and Section 7 of the Act, since there is no supply of goods or services or both in the said transaction and consequently, the question of whether the building has received completion certification or not would be irrelevant in such cases. The fact that Paragraph 5(b) of Schedule II and Section 7, contemplates only construction service provided by a builder/promoter to a service recipient is further evident from the description of service entries provided under “construction services” in the Notification No.11/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017 prescribed the rate of GST. Sale of land is treated under the Act as neither supply of goods nor a supply of service. As far as sale of building is concerned, if the same is not coming within the ambit of Paragraph 5(b) of Schedule II, then the same would be treated neither as supply of goods nor a supply of service. In the present case, as stated supra, the subject transaction would not fall within the ambit of Paragraph 5(b) of Schedule II, as there was no construction service being contemplated between the parties and consequently, in terms of Paragraph 5 of Schedule III, the subject sale transaction between the Petitioner and Respondent No.4 is neither a supply of goods nor a supply of service as wrongly held in the impugned order which deserves to be quashed.
