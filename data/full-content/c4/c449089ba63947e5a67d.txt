Summary: For Assessment Year 2026-27, section 139(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 fixes 30 November 2026 as the return-filing due date for an assessee—including the specified partner or spouse—where section 92E applies. The September 2026 extension for the non-section-92E audit class did not replace this separate date. Section 92E applies where a person entered into an international transaction or specified domestic transaction and requires an accountant’s report in the prescribed form. Form 3CEB and the income-tax return are connected but separate compliance products: the return must reconcile to the final financial statements, tax audit, Form 3CEB, transfer-pricing computation, withholding records and tax credits. This guide converts the statutory date into an evidence-based filing process, explains how to identify the correct taxpayer population, resolve Form 3CEB-to-ITR differences, pay residual tax, complete valid verification and preserve a close-out file. It does not treat the 30 November date as an extension for every company or audited taxpayer. Serial number 1 specifies 30 November of the assessment year for an assessee, including the partners of the firm or the spouse of such partner where section 5A applies, when section 92E applies. The Income Tax Department’s current return-of-income guidance uses the same classification and illustrates the 30 November 2026 date for financial year 2025-26. CBDT Circular No. 7/2026 moved the non-section-92E audit-case return date from 31 October to 21 November 2026. Its stated class is serial number 2, not the section 92E class in serial number 1. A transfer-pricing filer should therefore retain the enacted due-date provision and the circular, record why 30 November applies, and check for any later official order before submission rather than extending the date by analogy. The filing team should reconcile the legal-entity list to related-party ledgers, cross-border invoices, inter-company agreements, guarantees, loans, cost allocations, royalty arrangements, business restructurings and permanent-establishment dealings. A nil or immaterial accounting balance does not by itself answer whether a transaction exists under the statute. Specified domestic transactions need their own threshold and provision test Domestic related-party entries should not be added merely because they are related-party disclosures under company law or accounting standards. The tax definition and threshold for specified domestic transactions control. The file should identify the precise statutory limb, aggregate the relevant transactions consistently and retain the computation. Conversely, a domestic transaction cannot be omitted solely because both parties are Indian entities. Partners and a spouse covered by section 5A require a separate return-date review. Their inclusion in the due-date table does not mean the firm’s Form 3CEB automatically supplies every fact required for their individual returns. Maintain a taxpayer-by-taxpayer matrix showing PAN, status, transaction trigger, Form 3CEB acknowledgement, return form, signatory, tax-payment status and verification method. Form 3CEB and the ITR must be reconciled without treating either as a substitute Form 3CEB reports prescribed transaction particulars and the accountant’s opinion; it is not the income-tax return. The return reports income, deductions, losses, taxes and disclosures under the applicable ITR schema. A filed Form 3CEB does not complete section 139 compliance, while a return upload does not cure a missing or inaccurate report. The final filing pack should contain both acknowledgements and a controlled bridge between them. The bridge should state the reason, quantify the difference and identify the supporting document. An unexplained plug creates risk even when the two totals appear to match. If a material error is identified in a filed Form 3CEB, the taxpayer and accountant should assess the permitted correction or revised-report process using current portal functionality and law. The return should not silently contradict the report. Preserve the original, revised version, reason, approval and acknowledgements so the chronology remains defensible. Compute taxable income and residual tax from the final controlled numbers The return computation should begin with the audited financial statements and incorporate tax depreciation, disallowances, exemptions, loss set-off, transfer-pricing adjustments, withholding consequences and other applicable provisions. Each change after the audit sign-off should be traced to an approved journal, tax adjustment or disclosure. The return preparer should not copy a draft trial balance merely because the Form 3CEB was prepared from it. Transfer-pricing adjustments affect more than one schedule An arm’s-length adjustment may affect business income, book-to-tax reconciliation, minimum-tax calculations where relevant, interest, losses and related disclosures. A primary adjustment can also require separate analysis of repatriation or secondary-adjustment consequences. The return file should identify the exact adjustment, the transaction, the computation base and every schedule affected; this article does not assume one universal treatment. Recompute interest, fee and self-assessment tax immediately before payment. Match every challan by PAN, assessment year, payment type, amount and bank status before claiming it. A payment with the wrong assessment year or minor head does not become valid merely because money left the bank. Where correction is needed, use the official process and retain evidence rather than inflating an unverified credit in the return. Complete upload and verification as two controlled filing events Before upload, validate the return with the current utility or schema, inspect the final preview and obtain taxpayer approval. The reviewer should confirm the correct return form, filing section, due-date class, audit-report details, Form 3CEB acknowledgement, bank accounts, losses, foreign-asset schedules where applicable and tax payable. Portal validation proves only that a file passes system checks; it does not prove legal or factual correctness. Verification must be valid and timely The Income Tax Department requires a return to be verified by an eligible method. Depending on the taxpayer and signatory, this may involve a registered Digital Signature Certificate or another permitted electronic method. The Department’s current guidance applies a 30-day verification period from upload. Where the return is verified within that period, the upload date remains the filing date; late verification can shift the filing date and produce late-filing consequences. The close-out file should contain the due-date memo, financial statements, tax audit report, Form 3CEB and transfer-pricing study, computation, tax-credit reconciliation, challans, signed approvals, upload acknowledgement and verification proof. Record who prepared and reviewed each bridge and when. Run a final exception report for entities with pending verification, unmatched credits, revised reports, unresolved related-party differences, foreign-asset disclosures or portal grievances. A helpdesk ticket is evidence of an issue, not an automatic extension. Where the 30 November date or section 92E scope is uncertain, obtain advice before filing or omitting a taxpayer. The goal is a timely and internally consistent return, not merely an acknowledgement generated before midnight. Freeze the legal-entity population and due-date basis before final review. Tie each Form 3CEB transaction class to ledgers and the relevant ITR schedules. Quantify and approve every Form 3CEB-to-financial-statement difference. Verify tax payments, upload status and electronic verification independently. Preserve a versioned evidence index so later corrections do not erase history. Frequently Asked Questions on the 30 November Section 92E Return Who is covered by the 30 November 2026 date? An assessee, including the specified partner or spouse where applicable, to whom section 92E applies for AY 2026-27. Scope must be established from the transaction facts and law. Did Circular No. 7/2026 move this date to 21 November? No. That circular addressed the separate non-section-92E audit class in serial number 2 of the due-date table. Does filing Form 3CEB complete the income-tax return? No. Form 3CEB and the ITR are separate filings and both require their own acknowledgements and reconciliation. Must Form 3CEB values exactly equal financial-statement lines? Not always. Presentation, currency and gross-versus-net differences can exist, but every material difference should be quantified and supported. Which Act governs AY 2026-27 return filing? The Income-tax Act, 1961 governs the FY 2025-26 return for AY 2026-27; the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies to tax year 2026-27 income from 1 April 2026. Does upload alone make the return valid? No. The return must also be validly verified through an eligible method within the applicable period. What should be retained after filing? Keep the filed return, Form 3CEB, audit reports, computation, reconciliations, challans, approvals, upload acknowledgement and verification evidence. Key Takeaways 30 November 2026 is the enacted AY 2026-27 return date for the section 92E class. The 21 November audit-case extension does not replace that separate classification. Form 3CEB and the ITR require a quantified, documented bridge and separate proof of filing. Use final audited data, matched credits and approved transfer-pricing adjustments.
