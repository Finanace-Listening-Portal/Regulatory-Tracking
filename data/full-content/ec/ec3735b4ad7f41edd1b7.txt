Articles 3 min read Private banks to see faster rate transmission as 91% of floating rate loans linked to EBLR Private sector banks are likely to see faster transmission of the Reserve Bank of India’s 25 basis point repo rate hike to 5.5 per cent, given their significantly higher share of externally benchmarked loans compared with public-sector banks, though the eventual earnings impact will also depend on deposit repricing. For scheduled commercial banks as a whole, the EBLR (External Benchmark Linked Rate) linked share stood at 68.24 per cent.The distinction is important because loans linked to external benchmarks such as the repo rate typically reset faster following a policy rate change, while loans linked to the marginal cost of funds-based lending rate (MCLR) are repriced according to their respective reset periods. Advt “The transmission for private banks will happen faster,” Bhalerao said, pointing to the higher share of EBLR-linked loans at private-sector banks. While public-sector banks will also raise lending rates, the larger MCLR component of their loan books means the transmission will take more time.The RBI’s June data shows that the difference between the two bank groups has widened significantly over the past few years. For private-sector banks, the EBLR share of outstanding floating-rate rupee loans has risen from 4.65 per cent in September 2019 to 90.75 per cent in June 2026. For public-sector banks, the corresponding share has risen from 0.41 per cent to 53.62 per cent over the same period.This means that the 25 basis point increase in the repo rate is likely to affect a larger portion of private banks’ floating-rate loan books relatively quickly.A banking analyst told ETBFSI that roughly 50-60 per cent of retail loans could be linked to EBLR and that such loans would largely get repriced within a quarter. Loans linked to MCLR would reprice as their respective reset dates come up, while a smaller portion of fixed-rate loans would not immediately see any impact.Jefferies’ analysis also points to the importance of loan-pricing benchmarks in determining the impact of the rate cycle. The global investment bank said Indian banks, particularly large private banks, have a higher share of policy-rate-linked loans that reprice over one to three months. It expects banks with higher EBLR-linked loan shares and reasonable domestic loan-to-deposit ratios to see some positive earnings impact over the next three to six months. Advt However, faster transmission on the asset side does not automatically make private banks larger beneficiaries of the rate hike.The liability side will be equally important. Deposits also reprice as existing term deposits mature and banks compete for incremental deposits. Banks that are able to keep deposit rates lower for longer could retain more of the benefit from faster loan repricing.“Banks which are able to hold on to their deposit rates on the lower side will have a higher benefit,” Bhalerao said, adding that determining which banks can do so is more difficult and requires examining their deposit-rate structures. Advt Jefferies has similarly flagged loan-to-deposit ratios as an important variable. It said banks with manageable LDRs and higher EBLR-linked loan shares stand to benefit, while banks with higher LDRs or greater reliance on wholesale deposits may see a relatively different impact.
