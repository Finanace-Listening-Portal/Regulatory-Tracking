Articles 5 min read GFF 2026: AI alone won’t fix India’s insurance gap; ‘Missing Middle’ needs collaborative solution: NITI Aayog’s Sanjeet Singh Artificial intelligence can help insurers scale distribution and improve underwriting, claims and personalisation, but technology alone cannot bridge India’s insurance gap, NITI Aayog and industry leaders said at The Global FinTech Fest 2026. The panel flagged the country’s ‘missing middle’, inadequate coverage, low risk literacy and consumer trust around data as critical challenges to achieving the goal of insurance for all. Advt The government is working towards increasing insurance penetration from around 3.7% to 4.7%, while pursuing the broader goal of insurance for all, he said. However, Singh flagged accessibility, awareness, affordability and trust as key challenges that need to be addressed alongside technology.“AI and insurance, AI and national goals, AI and whatever, doesn't have to be discussed as separate subjects. AI is a tool,” Singh said.He also cautioned against creating a new form of exclusion through technology, particularly for consumers without internet access.“Where are the AI professionals who can really, really take over?” Singh said, pointing to the capacity constraints that could emerge as the insurance industry increasingly adopts AI.‘Missing middle’ needs collaborative solutionThe panel identified the ‘missing middle’, consumers who fall between government-supported coverage and the customer base traditionally served by private insurers, as one of the biggest gaps in India’s insurance landscape.Singh said NITI Aayog has already held discussions with private-sector players on developing a collaborative solution for this segment.He said the government’s role is to encourage and enable the private sector rather than expect it to solve the entire insurance coverage challenge on its own.“The private sector has to thrive. You have to make money,” Singh said, while emphasising that the industry also needs to recognise its role as a partner in achieving national objectives. Advt He invited insurers and other private-sector players to work with the government on solutions for consumers who remain outside adequate insurance coverage.“This is not a problem that the government has to solve... This is an opportunity also,” Singh said.SBI General Insurance MD & CEO Naveen Chandra Jha said scale would be critical to increasing insurance coverage, adding that AI could help insurers combine scale, convenience and intelligence.He said SBI General and the wider SBI ecosystem had covered around 2.39 crore policies during the previous year, highlighting the need to bring more people, businesses and properties under insurance. Advt Insurance adequacy matters more than premium growthPB Fintech Joint Group CEO Sarbvir Singh argued that India also needs to rethink the way it measures insurance penetration.He said the traditional metric of insurance premium as a percentage of GDP does not adequately capture whether consumers are actually protected.For life insurance, the industry should track sum assured per person, while health insurance should be assessed through sum insured per family, he said.“If we measure the right number, we work towards it,” Singh said.He pointed out that the industry’s average life insurance cover is around Rs 4 lakh per policy, while the average sum assured for Policybazaar customers is around Rs 81 lakh. Advt The distinction, he said, is important because having an insurance policy does not necessarily mean that a consumer has adequate protection.Singh also said the private sector alone cannot be expected to cover the bottom of the pyramid, calling for greater clarity around the respective roles of the government and private sector.“The private sector cannot cover the bottom of the pyramid. It is not possible,” he said.Trust becomes key as insurers deploy AIAs insurers increasingly use consumer data for underwriting and personalisation, the panel also highlighted the importance of data protection and consent.Jha said consumer research showed that more than 80% of customers were concerned about protecting their data, while around two-thirds were willing to share some information for personalisation.“Trust is a precondition to personalisation,” he said.He said insurers should distinguish between core data, which is essential for underwriting, and optional data, such as wellness and lifestyle information that can be used for personalisation with consumer consent.Jha said optional data should be governed through a consent framework and consumers should receive tangible benefits when they choose to share such information.For instance, wellness information such as step counts could be used to provide benefits to customers, while those benefits could be withdrawn if the customer subsequently withdraws consent.Prudential Health India MD & CEO (Designate) Amit Dave said new-age insurers have an opportunity to build the insurance proposition around both higher penetration and adequate coverage.He said the COVID-19 pandemic had increased consumer awareness about health insurance, while rising per-capita income had improved consumers’ ability to purchase insurance.Dave said technology would be used across the insurance value chain — from customer onboarding and underwriting to risk assessment, pre-authorisation and claims settlement — rather than being treated simply as a technology layer.The panel also called for greater insurance and risk literacy, with Jha noting that consumers in India often understand the value of insurance only when they face an actual financial or health shock.For India to close its insurance gap, the discussion pointed to a combination of AI-led scale, adequate coverage, stronger consumer trust, better risk literacy and a clearer public-private framework, rather than relying on technology or digital distribution alone.Click here to read our coverage of the Global FinTech Fest 2026.
