(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India disposed of the civil appeals in Commissioner of Service Tax Vs Jain Housing and Construction Ltd. after recording the submission of Mr. N. Venkatraman, learned Additional Solicitor General appearing for the appellant, that the issues arising in the appeals were covered by the Supreme Court’s judgment in Total Environment Building Systems Pvt. Ltd. Vs Deputy Commissioner of Commercial Taxes and Others, (2022) SCC OnLine SC 953. The Court condoned the delay and observed that the earlier judgment applied on all fours to the appeals. Accordingly, the civil appeals were disposed of, along with any pending applications. The Supreme Court’s short order did not independently examine the merits, reproduce the reasoning in Total Environment, or expressly set aside or affirm any particular service tax demand. Background of the Underlying Service Tax Dispute The accompanying tribunal material concerns Jain Housing and Construction Ltd., a developer engaged in residential projects through composite contracts with purchasers. The Commissioner of Service Tax, Chennai, had confirmed service tax demands under Section 73(1) read with Section 73(2) of the Finance Act, 1994, together with applicable interest and penalties, through common Order-in-Original Nos. 54, 55 and 56/2012 dated 28 March 2012. Three show cause notices concerned construction activities undertaken during periods extending from April 2006 to June 2010. The demands involved the classifications of Construction of Residential Complex Service and Works Contract Service. Before the tribunal, the assessee argued that the classification and taxability of composite construction contracts had already been addressed in Commissioner of Central Excise and Customs, Kerala Vs Larsen & Toubro Ltd., 2015 (39) STR 913 (SC), and Real Value Promoters Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner of GST and Central Excise, CESTAT Chennai. The tribunal accepted that indivisible composite works contracts could not be subjected to service tax under the construction service categories invoked in the notices merely because they involved construction activities. It distinguished the legal treatment of periods before and after 1 June 2007 and applied the relevant precedents in favour of the assessee. A separate issue concerned miscellaneous income treated by the department as consideration for construction services. The assessee maintained that certain amounts represented payments towards the purchase of land from Norne Homes Pvt. Ltd., rather than consideration received for taxable services. Relying on decisions including Reynolds Petro Chem Ltd. and Synergy Audio Visual Workshop Pvt. Ltd., the tribunal held that service tax liability could not be established merely from income-tax returns, TDS records or Form 26AS without the necessary basis for identifying taxable services. The Revenue could not distinguish the authorities relied upon by the assessee. Consequently, the tribunal set aside the impugned adjudication order and the service tax demands, allowing the appeals with consequential benefits, if any, in accordance with law. Its order was pronounced on 24 February 2023. The subsequent Supreme Court order must be read separately: it records disposal of the civil appeals on the basis of Total Environment, without setting out any additional findings on the underlying construction contracts, miscellaneous receipts, interest or penalties. Cases Discussed Total Environment Building Systems Pvt. Ltd. Vs Deputy Commissioner of Commercial Taxes and Others, (2022) SCC OnLine SC 953 — Supreme Court precedent expressly applied in disposing of the civil appeals. Commissioner of Central Excise and Customs, Kerala Vs Larsen & Toubro Ltd., 2015 (39) STR 913 (SC) — Relied upon in the tribunal proceedings concerning composite works contracts. Real Value Promoters Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner of GST and Central Excise, Final Order Nos. 42436–42438/2018, CESTAT Chennai — Applied in determining the classification of composite construction contracts. Reynolds Petro Chem Ltd. Vs Commissioner of Central Excise and Service Tax, 2023 (68) GSTL 292 (Tri.–Ahmd.) — Relied upon regarding service tax demands based on TDS/Form 26AS. Synergy Audio Visual Workshop Pvt. Ltd. Vs Commissioner of Service Tax, 2008 (10) STR 578 (Tri.–Bang.) — Cited concerning demands founded on income-tax records. Ved Security Vs CCE, Ranchi-III, 2019 (6) TMI 383 (CESTAT Kolkata) — Discussed in the quoted tribunal precedent concerning Form 26AS. Commissioner of Central Excise, Jaipur-I Vs Tahal Consulting Engineers Ltd., 2016 (44) STR 671 (Tri.–Del.) — Discussed regarding income-tax returns as a basis for service tax demands. Calvin Wooding Consulting Ltd. Vs Commissioner of Central Excise, Indore, 2007 (7) STR 411 (Tri.–Del.) — Discussed concerning the distinction between income-tax deduction obligations and service tax liability. Mr. N. Venkatraman, learned ASG appearing for the appellant submitted that the issues which arise in these appeals are covered by the judgment of this Court in Total Environment Building Systems Pvt. Ltd. vs. Deputy Commissioner of Commercial Taxes and Others [(2022) SCC Online SC 953] and therefore, these appeals may be accordingly disposed. Since the aforesaid judgment applies on all fours to these appeals, the Civil Appeals stand, accordingly, disposed of. Pending application(s), if any, shall stand disposed of.
