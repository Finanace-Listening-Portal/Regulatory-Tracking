Summary: Notification No. 06/2026-Central Tax (Rate), dated 27 October 2026 and effective from 1 November 2026, introduces significant changes to GST treatment of electrically operated vehicles, hospitality services, subcontracted services and delivery services supplied through e-commerce operators. The notification amends Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate) and expressly recognises electric passenger transport and renting of electric vehicles, including E-bicycles, under specified concessional GST rate entries. Where battery charging cost forms part of the consideration and prescribed conditions are satisfied, eligible services may attract GST at 5%, with restricted input tax credit, while specified alternative entries allow taxation at 18%. The notification also clarifies the treatment of input tax credit on services procured from other suppliers in the same line of business. For specified hotel accommodation, outdoor catering and electric vehicle services, eligible credit is restricted to the equivalent of 2.5% central tax where the input supplier charges a higher rate, subject to the corresponding State or Union Territory tax provisions. These restrictions create practical implications for vendor pricing, subcontracting arrangements, GST accounting and profitability. The amendment also introduces a 5% GST treatment without ITC for specified delivery services connected with e-commerce operators. Businesses must examine the interaction between the revised rate notification and Notification No. 09/2026-Central Tax (Rate), which governs the operator’s tax liability under Section 9(5) of the CGST Act. Notification No. 06/2026-Central Tax (Rate) explicitly resolves these ambiguities. Effective 1st November 2026, CBIC has provided formal statutory definitions for EVs, streamlined same-line ITC across hospitality and transport sectors, and introduced statutory capping rules for subcontracted inputs. Key Provisions & Practical Breakdown 1. Electric Vehicles (EVs): Tax Rates & Broadened Scope The notification explicitly extends concessional tax treatment to electrically operated vehicles, treating “battery charging costs” on par with traditional fuel costs. Statutory Additions: Passenger Transport by EV (Entry 8(vib)): 5% GST (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) where battery charging cost is included in the fare. Renting of Passenger EVs (Entry 10(ib)): 5% GST with restricted ITC, OR 18% GST with full ITC. Renting of Goods Transport EVs (Entry 10(ic)): 5% GST with restricted ITC, OR 18% GST with full ITC. Formal Definition of EV: Explanation 4 defines an “Electrically operated vehicle” as any road vehicle running solely on electrical energy derived from an external source or fitted batteries, explicitly including E-bicycles. Statutory Capping: ITC on same-line input services is allowed, but strictly capped at 2.5% of the input invoice value if the output service is billed at 5%. ECO Delivery Services Ambiguity regarding delivery partners operating via E-Commerce Operators (ECOs). Unregistered delivery partners operating through ECOs attract 5% GST without ITC, payable by the ECO under Section 9(5). COMPLIANCE SHIFT: Compliance burden shifts from small delivery partners directly to the E-Commerce platform. 2. The Same-Line Business ITC Mechanism: How the Capping Works Under the 5% concessional GST scheme, general ITC on capital goods, rent, and overheads remains blocked. However, taxpayers are permitted to take ITC on “services in the same line of business” (subcontracted services). The New Mathematical Rule: If a primary provider bills their client at 5% GST (2.5% CGST), but procures the subcontracted service from a vendor who bills at a higher rate (e.g., 18% GST / 9% CGST), ITC is strictly capped at 2.5% of the input invoice value. The excess tax charged by the vendor cannot be claimed and becomes a cost. Step-by-Step Practical Examples (Statutory Illustrations) Example 1: EV Fleet Aggregator Subcontracting Scenario: Corporate Client A contracts Fleet Operator B for employee EV transport for ₹1,000. Operator B subcontracts the trip to Cab Supplier C for ₹800. Vendor (C) Billing: Charges 18% GST (9% CGST = ₹72). Operator (B) Billing: Charges 5% GST (2.5% CGST = ₹25). ITC Determination for B: Under Entry 8(vib), Operator B cannot claim the full ₹72 CGST. B‘s credit is capped at 5% of the input value (2.5% of ₹800 = ₹20). Financial Impact: The unclaimable balance of ₹52 (₹72 – ₹20) must be absorbed as an operational cost. Example 2: Event Manager Subcontracting Outdoor Catering Scenario: Client A hires Event Planner B for a wedding package including catering for ₹1,00,000. B subcontracts the catering portion to Specialist Caterer C for ₹60,000. Caterer (C) Billing: Charges 18% GST under full ITC route (9% CGST = ₹5,400). Event Planner (B) Billing: Charges 5% GST on catering (2.5% CGST = ₹2,500). ITC Determination for B: B cannot claim ₹5,400. Credit is capped at 5% of ₹60,000 = ₹1,500. The remaining ₹3,900 is unclaimable. 3. Delivery Services via E-Commerce Operators (ECOs) Entry 12(iii) has been substituted to clarify delivery service taxation: Delivery services (other than courier/postal) supplied through an ECO by unregistered delivery partners (below ₹20 Lakh threshold) attract 5% GST without ITC. Section 9(5) Alignment: Combined with Notification No. 09/2026-CTR, the tax liability on these delivery services shifts directly to the E-Commerce Platform. Action Plan for CAs, CFOs & Business Owners 1. Vendor Pricing Renegotiation (Crucial): If your business bills clients at 5% GST (e.g., hotel aggregators, caterers, EV fleet managers), do not accept 18% invoices from vendors. Require vendors to bill under the 5% concessional scheme so that input tax matches the 2.5% CGST credit cap, preventing tax loss. 2. ERP & ERP-GSTR 2B Reconciliation: Configure accounting software to auto-cap same-line ITC at 2.5% of the input invoice value whenever output supplies are coded under 5% GST entries. Automatically route the unclaimable balance (e.g., the 6.5% CGST difference) to expense/cost heads rather than the Electronic Credit Ledger. 3. EV Billing Documentation: Fleet operators must ensure invoices explicitly state: “Supply of transport/renting services using electrically operated vehicles where battery charging cost is included in the consideration” to claim the 5% rate seamlessly.
