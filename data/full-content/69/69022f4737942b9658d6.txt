The dispute concerns two issuances of AT-1 bonds — ₹3,000 crore carrying a coupon rate of 9.5 per cent, issued in December 2016, and ₹5,415 crore carrying a 9 per cent coupon, issued in October 2017. AT-1 bonds are a form of bank debt designed to absorb losses when a lender runs into financial trouble. Yes Bank had written down the instruments in March 2020 following the RBI’s reconstruction of the lender, which was grappling with mounting bad loans, governance concerns and deterioration in its financial position. Axis Trustee Services, representing the bondholders, approached the Bombay HC in May 2020 against the writedown. The high court ruled in favour of the bondholders in January 2023, a decision subsequently upheld by the Supreme Court on October 15 this year. The Supreme Court’s judgment also addressed the payment of annual coupons on the bonds. It held that these would remain unaffected by the outcome of the appeals and have to be paid in accordance with the respective information memorandums. Following the judgment, Axis Trustee Services sought restoration of the bonds and their associated rights. The trustee also sought the restoration of the instruments in the relevant depository and exchange records. It indicated that it could initiate contempt proceedings over alleged non-compliance. The ruling has implications for Yes Bank’s capital position. The lender had earlier indicated that reinstatement of the principal amount could impact its Common Equity Tier-1 (CET1) capital ratio by around 250 basis points (bps), based on the June 2026 position.
