Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Uchchadungi Sadhan Sahakari Samiti Limited Vs ITO (ITAT Delhi) ITAT Can Remand a Time-Barred First Appeal Directly to the AO Where a Section 80P Claim Requires Fresh Fact-Finding Background of the Case The Delhi ITAT gave a cooperative society a fresh opportunity to explain substantial bank credits and substantiate its deduction under section 80P after its first appeal was dismissed on limitation. The Tribunal considered the approach to condonation unduly narrow and set aside the appellate order. It restored the assessment issues directly to the Assessing Officer, taking into account the factual verification required for both the additions and the deduction claim. The Assessing Officer had made two additions. The first was ₹1,10,12,676 towards unexplained credits in the bank account. The second was ₹1,24,59,303 towards interest income. The resulting demand of ₹2,60,37,690 led the society to file a stay application before the Tribunal. Two Delays at Two Appellate Stages The society filed its first appeal with a delay of 35 days. The Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), through the National Faceless Appeal Centre, declined to condone the delay and dismissed the appeal at the threshold by an order dated 17 July 2026 under section 250 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Consequently, the additions and the society’s grounds against them remained unexamined at the first appellate stage. The appeal before the Tribunal itself was delayed by 41 days. In explaining that delay, the society referred to staff changes, the existence of two PANs, and a communication that had landed in spam. The Tribunal accepted the explanation and admitted the appeal. When the stay application came up, the society asked for either a stay of recovery or an immediate hearing of the appeal. The Departmental Representative had no objection to the regular appeal being heard immediately. Why the Section 80P Claim Required Examination The society’s representative submitted that a section 80P claim had been raised during assessment. The Assessing Officer had declined it because the society had omitted the deduction from its return and had furnished no supporting documents to establish eligibility. The society explained that it had originally filed a return declaring a loss and had therefore omitted the claim. It sought an opportunity to establish its entitlement after the assessment brought the interest income to tax. The dispute thus involved both the assessment of the interest and its eligibility for deduction under section 80P. The society’s explanation addressed why the claim was absent from the return. Establishing entitlement still required examination of the relevant facts and documents. The separate addition for unexplained bank credits also called for factual verification. Why the Tribunal Remanded the Matter Directly to the AO The Tribunal held that the CIT(A) had taken a rather narrow view in declining to condone the delay and that the society deserved another opportunity to present its case. It expressly recognised that where a first appeal has been dismissed at the threshold, the ordinary course would be to send the matter back to the first appellate authority. The Bench considered a direct remand to the Assessing Officer appropriate in the circumstances. The dispute was fact-dependent, and the society was seeking examination of a deduction that it had omitted from its loss return. The Tribunal therefore set aside the impugned order and restored the matter to the Assessing Officer for fresh adjudication, expressly including the question whether the interest income qualified for deduction under section 80P. The appeal was partly allowed for statistical purposes. With the disposal of the main appeal, the stay application ceased to survive. The additions and the deduction claim are to be decided in the fresh proceedings. Author’s Comments The most significant aspect of the order is the choice of authority on remand. Sending the matter to the CIT(A) would ordinarily reopen the first appeal. Here, the Tribunal considered that the controversy called for an examination of the underlying evidence at the assessment stage itself. The remand gives the society an opportunity to explain the bank entries and produce material in support of its deduction claim in the same proceedings. The filing of a loss return explains the society’s position, but the order leaves the Assessing Officer to examine the claim under the applicable statutory provisions. In particular, section 80A(5) expressly addresses the omission of a Chapter VI-A deduction from the return. The Tribunal’s order contains no ruling on the application of that provision. The decision can therefore be cited for the procedural relief actually granted, while the availability of the deduction has to be argued on its own facts and statutory footing. For practitioners, the order illustrates why a remand request should identify the specific issues requiring verification. A reconciliation of the bank credits addresses one addition. Evidence identifying the source and character of the interest, together with the precise limb of section 80P invoked, addresses the deduction claim. Those are the matters on which the society’s substantive relief will turn. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT DELHI 1. This appeal has been filed with a delay of 41 days. The said delay has been requested to be condoned as under: “Respected Sir, It is hereby submitted that CIT (Appeal) order for the AY 2019-20 was served online on dated 17.07.2026, the appeal against the CIT(A) order should have been submitted within Sixty days from the end of the month in which impugned order has been passed by the Ld. CIT(A). However, the appeal was submitted on dated 10.11.2026. Hence, there was a delay of 41 days in filing of appeal. In connection of above it is submitted that delay in filing of appeal was due to the fact that due to change in staffs as well as the samite has two PAN, therefore, the PAN in which order has been passed is not in use & the assessment order was on spam mail hence could not see (Copy of old PAN & New PAN is attached). Sir, the delay is submitting the appeal due to the above reason, it is therefore, requested to condone the delay of 41 days in filing of appeal and allow the appeal for hearing for which act of kindness the appellant remains obliged.” 1.1 Considering the reasons given for the said delay, the same is hereby condoned and this appeal is admitted for adjudication. 2.0 This matter came up for hearing as a petition requesting stay on recovery of demand of Rs. 2,60,37,690. The Ld. AR prayed that the recovery of demand may be stayed or the case may be heard on merit. The Ld. AR briefed on the facts and stated that the assessee has suffered the additions due to an administrative difficulty and attempts to present the facts at first appellate stage could not succeed because of the inadvertent delay in filing of first appeal. 2.1 Considering the averments of Ld. AR, it is felt that this case can be disposed of. The Ld. DR also had no objection in case the regular hearing was to be conducted immediately. 3. This appeal arises from order dated 17.07.2026, passed u/s 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereafter as “the Act”), by NFAC. In this case, the Ld. AO has made two additions as under: – i. Addition of Rs.110,12,676/- on account of unexplained credits in the bank account; & ii. Addition of Rs.124,59,303/- on account of interest income received by the assessee. It is seen that there was a delay of 35 days in the filing of appeal before the Ld. CIT(A), which was not condoned and the appeal was dismissed inlimine. 3.1 The aggrieved assessee has approached the ITAT with grounds challenging the addition on merits and also on denial of opportunity. 4. The Ld. AR pointed out that a claim was tendered regarding deduction u/s 80P of the Act before the Ld. AO but the same was denied on the ground that no such deduction was claimed in the return of income and neither any relevant documents were submitted to justify any claim of exemption u/s 80P of the Act. It was the submission that since the assessee had filed a return of loss hence such claim had not been tendered but the assessee was a cooperative society deserving of benefit u/s 80P of the Act. It was also the submission that the delayed filing before the Ld. CIT(A) was inadvertent, deserving to be condoned. 4.1 The Ld. DR relied on the orders of the authorities below. 5. We have considered the rival submissions and have gone through the records before us. We find that the Ld. CIT(A) has taken a rather narrow view in not condoning the delay. We feel that the assessee deserves another chance to present his case before the authorities below and for that purpose, we set aside the impugned order and remit this matter back to the file of Ld. AO for fresh adjudication. We are conscious of the fact that the dismissal of appeal inlimine by the Ld. CIT(A) would normally merit a remanding back to that appellate authority only. However, this is a facts-based case and the assessee is also claiming, in hindsight, deduction u/s 80P of the Act. For a consideration of all these matters, including the issue of interest income being allowed as a deduction u/s 80P of the Act, we deem it fit to remand this matter back to the file of Ld. AO. 6. In the result, the appeal is partly allowed for statistical purposes 6.1 The Stay Application no longer survives with the disposal of the main appeal.
