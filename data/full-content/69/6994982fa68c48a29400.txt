Summary: Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017 restricts Input Tax Credit (ITC) on specified expenses despite their business use. This article examines statutory provisions, judicial interpretations, administrative clarifications and proposed relaxations concerning blocked ITC. Key restrictions cover motor vehicles, food and beverages, employee benefits, works contracts, construction of immovable property, CSR expenditure, personal consumption, free samples and goods lost or destroyed. It also discusses statutory exceptions, eligibility for demonstration vehicles and mandatory employee welfare facilities. The article highlights proposed relaxations attributed to the 57th GST Council Meeting concerning outdoor catering, insurance, telecommunication towers, pipelines, free samples and expired goods. However, GST Council recommendations become enforceable only upon implementation through appropriate legislative or statutory measures. Businesses must ensure invoice-level verification, documentary compliance and examination of statutory exceptions to avoid ineligible ITC claims. Conclusion Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Brief Input Tax Credit (ITC) is a fundamental feature of the GST framework, intended to eliminate cascading taxation. However, Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017 specifically restricts credit on certain goods and services, even where they are used in the course or furtherance of business. This article examines the statutory framework governing blocked credits important judicial interpretations, the impact of the Finance Act, 2025 amendment concerning plant and machinery, and the significant relaxations recommended by the 57th GST Council meeting. 1. Introduction: Is ITC a Right or a Concession? One of the most frequently misunderstood principles under GST is that every business expenditure carrying GST automatically qualifies for input tax credit. Section 16(1) of the CGST Act provides the general entitlement to ITC on goods or services used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business. However, this entitlement is subject to prescribed conditions and restrictions. Section 17(5), beginning with a non-obstante clause, overrides the general entitlement under Sections 16(1) and 18(1) for specified categories of inward supplies. Further, Section 155 places the burden of proving eligibility for ITC on the person claiming such credit. The principle that ITC is a statutory benefit rather than an unconditional right has also been recognised in judicial pronouncements, including TVS Motor Company Ltd. v. State of Tamil Nadu and Thirumalakonda Plywoods v. Assistant Commissioner. Therefore, the mere fact that an expenditure is incurred for business purposes is insufficient. The taxpayer must establish that the credit is permitted under the specific provisions of the Act. 2. Understanding the Categories of Blocked Credits Section 17(5) broadly covers he following categories: A. Motor Vehicles, Vessels and Aircraft – Section 17(5)(a), (aa) and (ab) ITC is generally blocked on motor vehicles used for transportation of persons having an approved seating capacity of not more than 13 persons, including the driver. Exceptions are available where such vehicles are used for: i. Further supply of such motor vehicles. ii. Transportation of passengers. iii. Imparting training on driving such vehicles. Separate restrictions and exceptions apply to vessels, aircraft and specified related services, including insurance, servicing, repair and maintenance. An important clarification is that the purpose for which the vehicle is designed or approved is relevant. Merely using a passenger vehicle for transporting goods does not automatically make ITC eligible. Recent development – Demo vehicles InPlatinum Motocorp LLP, In re (AAAR Haryana, 2022), ITC on demonstration vehicles and related expenses was denied. However, CBIC Circular No. 262/08/2026-GSTdated 26 October 2026 has withdrawn the earlier restrictive clarification under Circular No. 231/25/2024-GST for the dealer-agency model. ITC on demo vehicles is now clarified as not being blocked under Section 17(5)(a) in that model as well. B. Employee Benefits and Personal Expenses – Section 17(5)(b) The following inward supplies are generally restricted: i. Food and beverages and outdoor catering. ii. Beauty treatment, health services and cosmetic or plastic surgery, subject to statutory exceptions. iii. Rent-a-cab services. iv. Life and health insurance. v. Membership of clubs, health and fitness centers. vi. Travel benefits extended to employees on vacation, such as leave travel concession. However, the proviso provides an important exception where it is obligatory for an employer to provide such goods or services to employees under any law. CBIC Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST dated 6 July 2022 clarifies that this exception applies to all three categories covered by clauses (i), (ii) and (iii) of Section 17(5)(b). For example, where a factory is legally required to maintain a canteen, ITC may be available subject to the applicable statutory conditions. In Adithya Auto Products & Engineering India Pvt. Ltd., Karnataka AAR (2026), the authority examined canteen services provided to regular employees and contract workers. Credit was allowed for the employer-borne portion relating to regular employees, while the claim relating to contract workers was not accepted on the facts of the case. D. Works Contracts and Construction – Section 17(5)(c) and (d) Clause (c) restricts ITC on works contract servicessupplied for construction of immovable property, except where the inward works contract service is used for further supply of works contract service. Clause (d) restricts ITC on goods or services received for construction of immovable property on one’s own account, even when used in the course or furtherance of business. The explanation to Section 17 provides that construction includes reconstruction, renovation, additions, alterations and repairs to the extent of capitalization. Impact of Finance Act, 2025 In Chief Commissioner of CGST v. Safari Retreats Pvt. Ltd., the Supreme Court examined whether a shopping mall constructed for letting out commercial premises could qualify as a plant under the then-existing wording of Section 17(5)(d). The Court considered the functionality test and observed that the issue required examination on the facts of each case. Subsequently, Section 124 of the Finance Act, 2025 retrospectively substituted the words “plant or machinery” with “plant and machinery” in Section 17(5)(d), with effect from 1 July 2017. The amendment also introduced a deeming explanation. The definition of plant and machinery excludes land, buildings and civil structures, telecommunication towers and pipelines laid outside factory premises. Consequently, the earlier interpretation in Safari Retreats must be read alongside this retrospective legislative amendment. D. Other Blocked Credits – Section 17(5)(e) to (i) Other important restrictions include: i. Goods or services on which tax has been paid under the composition scheme. ii. Inward supplies received by a non-resident taxable person, except imported goods. iii. Goods or services used for activities relating to Corporate Social Responsibility obligations under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013. iv. Goods or services used for personal consumption. v. Goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off or disposed of by way of gift or free samples. vi. Tax paid under Section 74 in respect of periods up to FY 2023-24. These provisions require careful examination of the nature, purpose and accounting treatment of the expenditure. 3. The 57th GST Council: Proposed Rationalization of Blocked Credits One of the significant developments in the area of ITC is the recommendation made during the 57th GST Council meeting held on 8 October 2026. The Council recommended amendments to Section 17(5) to remove restrictions on ITC relating to: i. Outdoor catering. ii. Health and life insurance. iii. Telecommunication towers. iv. Pipelines laid outside factory premises. v. Free samples. vi. Goods destroyed or written off on expiry of shelf life, as required by law. These proposed relaxations are expected to benefit employers, manufacturers, pharmaceutical companies, FMCG businesses and telecom operators. However, it is important to distinguish between a GST Council recommendation and an amendment becoming legally effective. Taxpayers should continue following the existing statutory provisions until the necessary legislative amendments and applicable notifications come into force. 4. Practical Takeaways for Businesses Businesses should periodically review their ITC positions, particularly in relation to: i. Vehicle purchases, demonstration vehicles and related expenditure. ii. Employee welfare expenses and statutory canteen facilities. iii. Construction of factories, warehouses, commercial buildings and other immovable properties. iv. CSR expenditure. v. Free samples, expired inventory and goods written off. vi. Credits relating to composition suppliers and specified tax demands. A proper ITC review should involve invoice-level verification, identification ofblocked credits examination of statutory exceptions and maintenance of supporting documentation. 5.Memory Technique – We have read and understoodblocked credits for multiple times since its introduction. Conclusion Section 17(5) remains one of the most important provisions affecting the availability of ITC under GST. While the general principle under Section 16 encourages credit on business-related inward supplies, the specific restrictions under Section 17(5) must be independently examined. Judicial interpretations, statutory amendments and administrative clarifications continue to shape the practical application of these provisions. The recommendations of the 57th GST Council indicate a move towards rationalizing certainblocked credits and reducing cascading taxation. However, until the proposed amendments are legally implemented, businesses must exercise caution before availing credits currently restricted under the Act. Ultimately, the key to effective GST compliance is not merely identifying business expenditure but establishing its eligibility under the statute, supported by proper documentation and a sound understanding of judicial and legislative developments.
