Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Gangasani Ameeta Reddy Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) Summary: The Hyderabad A-Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), comprising Judicial Member Ravish Sood and Accountant Member Madhusudan Sawdia, allowed the appeal filed by Gangasani Ameeta Reddy for Assessment Year 2015-16 and quashed the reassessment involving an addition of Rs. 85,06,916 under Section 69A of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Tribunal also held that where the CIT(A) decides an appeal on merits despite expressly declining to condone the delay in filing it, such delay stands impliedly condoned. The reassessment was ultimately quashed because the notice issued under Section 148 on 19 April 2022 was beyond the applicable six-year limitation period. The Assessing Officer initiated reassessment proceedings based on information flagged under the Risk Management Strategy formulated by the CBDT through the Insight and ITBA systems under the category of non-filers. The information indicated that the assessee had made cash deposits aggregating to Rs. 70,06,850, comprising Rs. 23,72,850 with the State Bank of Hyderabad, Gunfoundry Branch, Rs. 4,20,000 with Canara Bank, Himayatnagar, and Rs. 42,14,000 with the State Bank of Hyderabad. Since the assessee had not filed her return of income for the relevant assessment year, a show-cause notice under Section 148A(b) was issued on 23 March 2022. The initial show-cause notice was returned unserved, and the time for furnishing a response was subsequently extended to 18 April 2022. As no reply was furnished, the Assessing Officer passed an order under Section 148A(d) on 19 April 2022 and issued the consequential notice under Section 148 on the same date. During the reassessment proceedings, the Assessing Officer concluded that the assessee had failed to explain the nature and source of cash deposits aggregating to Rs. 85,06,916, comprising Rs. 65,86,850 deposited with the State Bank of India and Rs. 19,20,066 deposited with Canara Bank. Accordingly, the entire amount was treated as unexplained money under Section 69A through the assessment order dated 17 January 2024. The assessee challenged the addition, contending that she had no independent source of income other than salary income below the taxable limit and that the deposits in the joint bank accounts were attributable to the business activities of her spouse. She submitted that bank statements and the return of income of her spouse had been furnished to substantiate the transactions. She further argued that merely holding a joint bank account did not automatically establish ownership of the deposits by both account holders. However, the CIT(A), vide order dated 12 January 2026, dismissed the appeal both on the ground of limitation, by refusing to condone the delay of 211 days, and on the merits of the case. Before the Tribunal, the assessee raised additional legal grounds challenging the validity of the reassessment notice dated 19 April 2022. Before adjudicating the jurisdictional challenge, the Tribunal examined the effect of the CIT(A)’s decision to dismiss the appeal both on limitation and on merits. The Tribunal observed that although the CIT(A) had expressly declined to condone the delay, he had nevertheless examined the substantive grounds raised by the assessee and decided the appeal on merits. Relying upon the Madras High Court judgment in Vijayeswari Textiles Ltd. Vs CIT, reported in (2002) 256 ITR 560 (Mad.), the Tribunal held that when an appellate authority examines and decides the merits of a case, the delay in filing the appeal stands impliedly condoned. Accordingly, despite the express refusal of the CIT(A) to condone the delay of 211 days, the Tribunal concluded that the delay had been impliedly condoned by virtue of the adjudication on merits. On the jurisdictional issue, the assessee submitted that the six-year limitation period prescribed under the erstwhile Section 149(1)(b) for AY 2015-16 had expired on 31 March 2022. Consequently, the reassessment notice issued on 19 April 2022 was prohibited by the first proviso to the amended Section 149(1). The assessee further contended that the fifth and sixth provisos to Section 149(1), which provide for exclusion and extension of specified periods, could not be invoked to extend the restrictive limitation prescribed under the first proviso. Reliance was placed upon the jurisdictional Telangana High Court judgment in Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Private Limited Vs DCIT, Writ Petition No. 25121 of 2024, dated 17 November 2025. The Revenue relied upon the orders of the authorities below. The Tribunal accepted the assessee’s contention and observed that the notice under Section 148 had admittedly been issued beyond the limitation period prescribed under the erstwhile statutory provisions. Following the jurisdictional High Court judgment, the Tribunal held that the fifth and sixth provisos to the amended Section 149 qualify the substantive limitation periods prescribed under that section and cannot be applied to extend the restriction imposed by its first proviso. The reproduced judicial findings also discussed the Supreme Court judgment in Union of India Vs Rajeev Bansal and the High Court decisions in Godrej Industries Ltd., Shree Cement Ltd. and Hexaware Technologies Ltd. regarding the operation of the amended reassessment provisions. Accordingly, the Tribunal held that the reassessment notice issued under Section 148 on 19 April 2022 was barred by limitation and that the consequential assessment order dated 17 January 2024, passed under Section 147 read with Sections 144 and 144B, was invalid for want of a valid assumption of jurisdiction. Since the reassessment itself was quashed, the Tribunal refrained from adjudicating the remaining grounds concerning ownership of the cash deposits in the joint bank accounts and the sustainability of the addition under Section 69A. These issues were left open, and the assessee’s appeal was allowed. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT HYDERABAD The present appeal filed by the assessee is directed against the order passed by the CIT(Appeals), NFAC, Delhi (for short, “CIT(A)”) dated 12/01/2026, which in turn arises from the assessment order passed by the Assessing Officer (for short, “AO”) under Section 147 r.w.s 144 r.w.s 144B of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (for short, “the Act”), dated 17/01/2024, for A.Y. 2015-16. The assessee has assailed the impugned order on the following grounds of appeal before us. “1) The order of the Ld. CIT(A) in upholding the order of the Ld. AO u.s 147 r.w.s 144 of the IT Act is arbitrary and erroneous in law as well as in facts of the case 2) The Ld. CIT(A) erred in upholding the addition made by the Ld.AO by invoking provisions of sec.69A on account of cash deposits in joint bank account, which is bad in law 3) The Ld. CIT(A) failed to appreciate the fact that the appellant has no independent source of income except salary income below the taxable limit, and there is no basis to treat the cash deposits as unexplained income of the appellant 4) The Ld. CIT(A) ought to have observed the fact that the Appellant has placed bank statements and ROI of the spouse to prove the identity, genuineness and creditworthiness of such transactions. 5) The Ld. CIT(A) failed to appreciate the fact that the AO has erred in considering the settled legal position that mere holding of a joint bank account does not lead to automatic ownership of deposits by both account holders. Hence, the addition of Rs. 85,06,916/- is liable to be deleted 6) The Ld. CIT(A) ought to observed that the AO has erred in considering the fact that the business of the Appellants spouse is involved with substantial cash transactions and the same has been deposited in their bank account which are also evident and explained from the submission made before the AO. 7) The learned CIT(A)erred upholding the addition made by the Assessing Officer, despite the appellant having clearly explained that the sources of cash deposits were relatable to the business activities of her spouse and did not belong to the appellant, and therefore the addition sustained is contrary to the facts on record and settled principles of law. 8) The Appellant craves to add, alter, modify, leave any other grounds at the time of hearing.” Apart from that, the assessee has raised the following additional grounds of appeal which reads as under: “10. The notice issued u/s. 148 dated 19.04.2022 by the Ld. AO, is barred by limitation in view of the First Proviso to Sec. 149 of The Income Tax Act, 1961. Hence the notice issued u/s. 148 is void and liable to be quashed. 11. The reassessment proceedings u/s. 147 stands vitiated as the notice u/s. 148 is barred by limitation in view of first proviso to Sec. 149. Hence the entire reassessment proceedings shall be treated as null and void.” 2. Succinctly stated, the AO based on specific information flagged by Risk Management Strategy formulated by the CBDT through Insight and ITBA software under the head ‘NMS cases’ gathered that the assessee during the year under consideration had made cash deposits aggregating to Rs.70,06,850/- in his bank accounts, viz., (i) State Bank of Hyderabad, Gunfoundry Branch: Rs.23,72,850/-; (ii) Canara Bank, Himayatnagar: Rs.4,20,000/-; and (iii) State Bank of Hyderabad: Rs.42,14,000/- but had not filed her return of income for the subject year, initiated proceedings under section 147 of the Act, therefore, the AO issued notice under section 148A(b) of the Act, dated 23/03/2022, wherein the assessee was called upon to explain on or before 30/03/2022 as to why a notice under section 148 of the Act should not be issued on the basis of the aforesaid information. As is discernible from the record, the aforesaid notice under section 148A(b) of the Act, dated 23/03/2022 which was forwarded through speed post was returned unserved and was again forwarded to the latest available address through speed post on 11/04/2024. Accordingly, the time period allowed to the assessee to furnish the reply to the notice issued under section 148A(b) of the Act was extended to 18/04/2022. However, as no reply was furnished by the assessee, the AO passed an order under section 148A(d) of the Act, dated 19/04/2022. Notice under section 148 of the Act, dated 19/04/2022, was issued by the AO. 3. During the course of the assessment proceedings, the AO observed that the assessee had failed to explain the nature and source of the cash deposits made in his bank accounts aggregating to Rs.85,06,916/-, viz., (i) cash deposits in bank account No.00000062184297454 held with State Bank of India: Rs.65,86,850/-; and (ii) cash deposits in Canara Bank account No.30631180000427: Rs.19,20,066/-. Accordingly, the AO after treating the aforementioned cash deposits as having been sourced out of the assessee’s unexplained money under section 69A of the Act, determined the income of the assessee at Rs.85,06,916/-. 4. Aggrieved, the assessee carried the matter in appeal before the CIT(A). However, as the assessee had failed to participate in the appellate proceedings, therefore, the CIT(A) proceeded with and disposed of the appeal considering the material available on record. As the assessee had delayed the filing of the appeal before the CIT(A) by 211 days, therefore, he had dismissed the same both on the ground of limitation, ie., non-condonation of delay as well as on the merits of the case. 5. The assessee, aggrieved with the order of the CIT(A) has carried the matter in appeal before us. 6. We have heard the Learned Authorized Representatives of both parties, perused the orders of the authorities below and the material available on record. 7. Shri C Maheswar Reddy, CA, Learned Authorized Representative (for short, “Ld. AR”) for the assessee, at the threshold of hearing of the appeal assailed the jurisdiction assumed by the AO to frame the assessment vide notice issued under section 148 of the Act, dated 19/04/2022 on the ground that the same as per the “first proviso” to section 149 of the Act is barred by limitation. Apart from that, the Ld. AR submitted that the AO had grossly erred in law and facts of the case in assuming jurisdiction for framing the impugned assessment vide his order passed under section 147 r.w.s. 144 r.w.s 144B of the Act, dated 17/01/2024. Elaborating on his contention, the Ld. AR submitted that as the AO as per “first proviso” to section 149(1) of the Act (post amended) as was applicable at the time of issuing the notice under section 148 of the Act, dated 19/04/2022 was divested of his jurisdiction to issue any such notice seeking to reopen the case of the assessee for the subject year AY 2015-16 beyond 31/03/2022, i.e., beyond the time limit specified under the clause (b) of sub-section (1) of section 149 of the Act as was available on the statute before commencement of the Finance Act, 2021, therefore, the initiation of the impugned proceedings and the consequential framing of the assessment by him based on the said notice cannot be sustained and is liable to be struck down on account of invalid assumption of jurisdiction. The Ld. AR to buttress his contention had drawn our attention to section 149(1) of the Act – “first proviso” (as was made available on the statute by the Finance Act, 2021, w.e.f. 01/04/2021). 8. Carrying his contention further, the Ld. AR submitted that though the fifth and sixth provisos of the post amended section 149 of the Act provide for excluding certain periods while computing the period of limitation, viz., (i) the time or extended time allowed to the assessee, as per show cause notice (SCN) issued under clause (b) of section 148A of the Act or the period during which the proceedings under section 148A is stayed by an order or injunction of any Court (as per “fifth proviso”); and (ii) that where immediately after the exclusion (period referred to in “fifth proviso”) of the period of limitation available to the AO for passing an order under clause (d) of section 148A of the Act does not exceed seven days, such remaining period shall be extended to seven days and the period of limitation under sub-section (1) of section 149 shall be deemed to be extended accordingly (as per “sixth proviso”), but both the aforementioned provisos, i.e., “fifth proviso” and “sixth proviso” qualify the substantive amended section 149 of the Act and do not relate to the un-amended section 149 of the Act, which in turn is taken care of exclusively by the “first proviso” to section 149(1) of the Act. Accordingly, the Ld. AR based on his aforesaid contentions submitted that the time spent from the issuance of notice under section 148A(b) of the Act upto the passing of the order under section 148A(d) of the Act in terms of “fifth proviso” and “sixth proviso” cannot be excluded for reckoning the limitation period for issuance of notice under section 148 of the Act. Elaborating on his contention, the Ld. AR submitted that as the notice under section 148 of the Act, dated 19/04/2022 for the AY 2015-16 had been issued beyond the time limit specified under the provisions of clause (b) of sub-section (1) of section 149 of the Act as was available on the statute before the commencement of the Finance Act, 2021, which expired as on 31/03/2022 (six years from the end of the assessment year, i.e., AY 2015-16), therefore, the same cannot be sustained and is liable to be quashed. The Ld. AR to buttress his contention had relied upon the judgment of the Hon’ble High Court of Telangana in the case of Cyberabad Citizens Health Services Private Limited vs. DCIT, Circle-1(1), Writ Petition No.25121 of 2024, dated 17/11/2025. 9. Per contra, Shri Mohan Babu, Learned Senior Departmental Representative (for short, “Ld. Sr-DR”) relied upon the orders of the authorities below. 10. Before proceeding further, we deem it apposite to observe that a material aspect involved in the present appeal, i.e., dismissal of the appeal by the CIT(A), inter alia, for the reason that he had declined to condone the delay of 211 days requires to be addressed before we advert to the contention advanced by the Ld. AR regarding the validity of the jurisdiction assumed by the AO for issuing the notice under section 148 of the Act, dated 19/04/2022. 11. As is discernible from the CIT(A) order, we find that he had, taking cognizance of the fact that the appeal filed by the assessee firm on 14/09/2024 involved a delay of 211 days, rejected the assessee’s explanation regarding the reason leading to the same, and dismissed the appeal by declining to condone the same. However, the CIT(A) after declining to condone the delay had proceeded with and dealt with the merits of the case, and after necessary deliberations on the specific issues based on which the impugned order of assessment was assailed before him also dismissed the appeal on merits. 12. We have given thoughtful consideration and are of a firm conviction that though the CIT(A) had declined to condone the delay, he nevertheless proceeded to decide the appeal on merits. In our view, as it is a settled principle of law that when an appellate authority examines the merits of the case, the delay in filing the appeal stands impliedly condoned by him. Our aforesaid view is supported by the judgment of the Hon’ble High Court of Madras in the case of Vijayeswari Textiles Ltd. Vs. CIT (2002) 256 ITR 560 (Mad). We thus, in the backdrop of the fact that the CIT(A) had though declined to condone the delay involved in the appeal filed by the assessee before him, at the same time dealt with the specific grounds based on which the impugned order of the assessment was assailed before him and dismissed the appeal on merits also, therefore, it can safely be concluded that he had impliedly condoned the delay involved in the appeal filed before him. 13. Coming back to the core issue involved in the present case, i.e., sustainability of the impugned order of assessment passed by the AO under section 147 r.w.s 144 r.w.s 144B of the Act, dated 17/01/2024, which in turn is based on the notice issued under section 148 of the Act, dated 19/04/2022, for the AY 2015-16. 14. Admittedly, it is a matter of fact borne from record that the impugned notice under section 148 of the Act, dated 19/04/2022 has been issued beyond the time limit specified under the provisions of clause (b) of sub-section (1) of section 149 of the Act as they stood immediately before the commencement of the Finance Act, 2021. We say so, for the reason that as per the pre-amended section 149(1)(b) of the Act a notice under section 148 of the Act for the AY 2015-16 could have been issued by the AO latest by 31/03/2022. 15. Considering the aforesaid factual position, we find substance in the Ld. AR’s contention that as the notice under section 148 of the Act, dated 19/04/2022 had been issued beyond the time period specified under the provisions of clause (b) of sub-section (1) of section 149 of the Act, as was available on the statute prior to the commencement of the Finance Act, 2021, therefore, the same could not have been issued as per the clear mandate of the “first proviso” to section 149 of the Act as had been made available on the statute by the Finance Act, 2021. Also, we concur with the Ld. AR that the period sought to be excluded for the purpose of computing the period of limitation as contemplated in the “fifth proviso” of section 149(1) of the Act (post amended) and also the extension of time limit to seven days in a case where after the exclusion of the time limit contemplated in the “fifth proviso” to seven days (as per the “sixth proviso”) of section 149(1) of the Act cannot be read into for the purpose of computing the period of limitation for issuance of notice under section 148 of the Act as contemplated in the “first proviso” of post amended section 149(1) of the Act. Apparently, the fifth and sixth provisos of the amended Section 149 of the Act extracted hereinabove provide for excluding certain periods while computing the period of limitation as per the amended Section. It prescribes the time or extended time allowed to the assessee as per the show cause notice under clause (b) of Section 148 of the Act or the period during which the proceeding under Section 148A of the Act is stayed shall be excluded. The sixth proviso to the amended Section 149 of the Act also deals with exclusion of the period referred to in the fifth proviso i.e., the period of limitation available to the Assessing Officer for passing an order under clause (d) of Section 148A of the Act if it does not exceed seven days. In that event, such remaining period shall be extended to seven days and the period of limitation under this sub-section shall be deemed to be extended. Accordingly, both the fifth and sixth provisos in the first place do not amount to clarification of the first proviso. These two provisos qualify the substantive amended Section 149 of the Act and do not relate to the un-amended Section 149 of the Act for which the first proviso takes care of. The contention of the learned counsel for the Revenue that the time spent from the issuance of notice under Section 148A(b) of the Act up to the passing of the order under Section 148A(d) of the Act in terms of the fifth and sixth provisos stands excluded for reckoning the limitation period for issuance of notice under Section 148 of the Act is not worth acceptance. Section 148A of the Act lays down the procedure for issuance of notice under Section 148 of the Act whereas Section 149 of the Act prescribes strict time limit within which notice under Section 148 of the Act can be issued in the prescribed circumstances. The Revenue is therefore obliged to adhere to the timeline prescribed under Section 149 of the Act for issuance of such notice and undertake the procedure before issuance of notice under Section 148A of the Act. 13. In this regard, it is apposite to refer to opinion of the Delhi High Court. Paragraphs 15 and 16 of Godrej Industries Ltd., (supra) are extracted hereunder: “15. The validity of a notice must be judged on the basis of the law existing as on the date on which the notice is issued under Section 148 of the Act, which in the present case is 31 July 2022, by which time the Finance Act, 2021 is already on the statute and in terms thereof, no notice under Section 148 of the Act for AY 2014-15 could be issued on or after 1” April 2021 based on the first proviso to Section 149 of the Act. Therefore, the fifth proviso cannot apply in a case where the first proviso applies because, if a notice under Section 148 of the Act could not be issued beyond the time period provided in the first proviso, then the fifth proviso could not save such notices. The fifth proviso can only apply where one has to determine whether the time limit of three years and ten years in Section 149(1) of the Act are breached. 16. The sixth proviso to Section 149 of the Act has no impact as it only provides a situation where after exclusion of the time period referred to in the fifth proviso, the time available with the Assessing Officer for passing an order under Section 148A(d) of the Act is less than 7 days, then the remaining time frame shall be extended to 7 days and limitation also stands extended by 7 days.” 14. Paragraph 12 of Shree Cement Ltd., (supra) is also extracted hereunder. “12. In this case, as it pertains to Assessment Year 2017-18, six years period would have expired on 31” March 2024. Whereas notice under Section 148 of the Act itself came to be issued on 1” May 2024. Mr. Siddharth Bapna, counsel for Revenue, made an attempt to argue that fifth and sixth provisos to Section 149(1)(b) of the Act would save the period of limitation for issuing notice under Section 148 of the Act. We are afraid we do not agree with him. Same argument was raised in Hexaware Technologies Ltd. (supra) and was rejected. The Court held, with respect to applicability of fifth and sixth provisos to Section 149(1)(b) of the Act for extension of limitation for issuing notice under Section 148 of the Act, fifth and sixth provisos are only applicable with respect to the period of limitation prescribed under Section 149(1) of the Act ie, three years or ten years, as the case may be. The Court also held that fifth and sixth provisos extend limitation for issuing notice under Section 149 of the Act, however, first proviso is an exception to the period of limitation and provides for a restriction on the notices under Section 148 of the Act being issued for assessment years up to 2021-22 (in this case, it is Assessment Year 2017-18) beyond a certain date. Therefore, the way the section would operate, is to fira decide whether a notice issued under Section 148 of the Vet is within the period of limitation under Section 149(1)(2) or (b) of the Act. To decide whether the notice is within the period of limitation under Section 149(1)(a) or (b) of the Act, the extension of time as prescribed in fifth and/or sixth proviso would be considered. The Court further held once. The notice is otherwise within the period of limitation, thereafter one has to see whether the said limit is within the prescribed restriction provided in first proviso or not. If the notice is beyond the restriction period, the notice is invalid, and the fifth and/or the sixth proviso cannot apply at this stage to extend the period of restriction as per first proviso. Hence, if a notice is not within the time prescribed under first proviso to Section 149(1) of the Act, then such period cannot be extended by fifth or sixth proviso. In Hexaware Technologies Ltd. (supra), the Court had relied upon another judgment of Bombay High Court in Godrej Industries Ltd. V. Assistant Commissioner of Income-tax [2024] 160 taxmann.com 13 (Bombay)/(2024) 338 CTR (Bom) 25, which was also authored by one of us (the Chief Justice), where paragraph No.15 reads as under: “15. The validity of a notice must be judged on the basis of the law existing as on the date on which the notice is issued under Section 148 of the Act, which in the present case is 31” July 2022, by which time the Finance Act, 2021 is already on the statute and in terms thereof, no notice under Section 148 of the Act for AY 2014-15 could be issued on or after 1 April 2021 based on the first proviso to Section 149 of the Act. Therefore, the fifth proviso cannot apply in a case where the first proviso applies because, if a notice under Section 148 of the Act could not be issued beyond the time period provided in the first proviso, then the fifth proviso could not save such notices. The fifth proviso can only apply where one has to determine whether the time limit of three years and ten years in Section 149(1) of the Act are breached.” 15. The reliance placed by the Revenue on the decision rendered by Patna High Court in the case of Chandra Shekhar (supra) is distinguishable as it relates to the Assessment Year 2020-21 in respect of which the notice under Section 148A(b) of the Act was issued on 28.03.2024. The petitioner therein had assailed the notice on the ground that the Assessing Officer had no jurisdiction to undertake the assessment for the Assessment Year 2020-21 after 31.03.2024 with reference to the second notice issued on 22.04.2024 as it was beyond the time limit stipulated under Section 149(1)(a) of the Act. In the aforesaid facts, the learned Court held that the combined reading of the fifth and sixth provisos meant that the first notice dated 28.03.2024 was issued well within the time limit stipulated. Therefore, the Assessment Officer has jurisdiction. 16. In view of the above discussion, the initiation of reopening of assessment by the impugned notice dated 22.04.2024 is barred by limitation being beyond the period of six (6) years reckoned from the relevant Assessment Year 2017-18 as per the un-amended Section 149 of the Act read with the first proviso thereof brought into effect from 01.04.2021.” 16. We thus, in terms of our aforesaid deliberations find substance in the Ld. AR’s contention that as the notice issued by the AO under section 148 of the Act, dated 19/04/2022 is barred by limitation as per the mandate of the “first proviso” to section 149 of the Act as had been made available on the statute by the Finance Act, 2021, therefore, the same cannot be sustained and also, the consequential assessment order passed by the AO on the basis of the same vide his order under section 147 r.w.s 144 r.w.s 144B of the Act, dated 17/01/2024 has to meet the same fate and is liable to be quashed. 17. We thus, in terms of our aforesaid deliberations, quash the assessment order passed under section 147 r.w.s 144 r.w.s 144B of the Act, dated 17/01/2024 for want of a valid assumption of jurisdiction. 18. As we have quashed the assessment order passed by the AO under section 147 r.w.s 144 r.w.s 144B of the Act, dated 17/01/2024, therefore, we refrain from adverting to the other contentions based on which the impugned order of assessment has been assailed by the assessee before us, which, thus, are left open. 19. In the result, appeal filed by the assessee is allowed in terms of our aforesaid observations. Order pronounced in the open court on 11th September, 2026.
