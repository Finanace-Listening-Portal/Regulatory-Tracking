Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Vs Samanvay Sparsh (ITAT Ahmedabad) Only Profit Embedded in Real Estate On-Money Receipts Is Taxable as Business Income: ITAT Ahmedabad Summary: The Ahmedabad Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held that only the profit embedded in on-money received from the sale of real estate units can be brought to tax. The Tribunal upheld the CIT(A)’s estimate of net profit at 15% and allowed the profit to be set off against the ₹4.76 crore excess stock disclosure already offered to tax. It also held that Section 115BBE did not apply because the receipts arose from the assessee’s construction business. Facts of the Case During a survey under Section 133A, the Department impounded an Excel sheet recording on-money received by the assessee on the sale of units. For AY 2019–20, the Assessing Officer (AO) quantified the receipts at ₹14,23,19,510 and added the entire amount under Section 69A, applying the special tax rate under Section 115BBE. The CIT(A) estimated the profit at 15% of the receipts, which worked out to ₹2,13,47,927, and allowed a set-off against the excess stock disclosure. The Revenue challenged the profit estimate, the set-off and the finding that Section 115BBE did not apply. It argued that the assessee had already debited all project expenses to its profit and loss account and had failed to explain the source of the excess stock. Only the Profit Element in On-Money Receipts Can Be Added The Tribunal relied on the Gujarat High Court decisions in PCIT v. Anupam Organiser and DCIT v. Panna Corporation, which held that the taxable amount is the profit embedded in the unaccounted receipts. It also referred to Jay Builder v. ACIT, where the Gujarat High Court upheld an addition at 15% of the on-money received. On the facts of the present case, the Tribunal upheld the CIT(A)’s 15% net profit estimate. On-Money Profit Set Off Against Excess Stock Already Offered to Tax During the survey, a partner disclosed ₹4.76 crore on account of excess stock, and the assessee offered that amount to tax in its return. The Tribunal upheld the CIT(A)’s finding that the estimated on-money profit was applied towards the excess stock. The Revenue did not dispute that the stock disclosure had been offered to tax or contend that the estimated profit exceeded that disclosure. The Tribunal accordingly held that a separate addition of the on-money profit would tax the same income twice, once when it was earned and again when it was reflected in the excess stock. Section 115BBE Inapplicable to Receipts From the Construction Business The Tribunal found that the on-money was intrinsically connected with the sale of units in the assessee’s construction business and formed part of its business receipts. It therefore upheld the CIT(A)’s finding that Section 115BBE was inapplicable. The Tribunal applied the same reasoning to AY 2020–21 and dismissed both Revenue appeals. Cases Discussed DCIT Vs Panna Corporation (Gujarat High Court); Tax Appeal No. 323 of 2000; 16/06/2012; AY. The Gujarat High Court held that the taxable component of on-money or unaccounted cash receipts is the profit embedded in those receipts, rather than the gross amount. The Tribunal reproduced paragraph 15 of this judgment. Jay Builder Vs Assistant Commissioner of Income-tax (Gujarat High Court); [2013] 33 taxmann.com 62. The Tribunal referred to this decision as supporting the adoption of a 15% profit rate on on-money receipts. PCIT Vs Anupam Organiser (Gujarat High Court); Tax Appeal No. 168 of 2020; 10/09/2020; AY: 2013-14. Relied upon for the principle that the entire on-money receipt cannot be added as income and that only the profit component is taxable. PCIT Vs Surya Agrotech (Delhi High Court); [2023] 154 taxmann.com 156. Referred to in the CIT(A)’s reasoning on the set-off of undisclosed income against its application in excess stock. The Tribunal referred to the coordinate Bench’s adoption of a 13% profit rate on on-money receipts as support for estimating taxable profits rather than taxing gross receipts. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT AHMEDABAD These two appeals are filed by the Revenue against the separate orders passed by the Learned Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)-12, Ahmedabad, (in short “the Ld. CIT(A)”) both dated 03.01.2025 for the Assessment Years 2019-20 & 2020-21 respectively. The facts involved in these two appeals are identical, hence both the matters were heard together and are being disposed of vide this common order for the sake of convenience. We will take up appeal bearing ITA No.874/Ahd/2025 for AY 2019-20 as lead case for the purpose of adjudication. 2. At the outset, we find that there was a delay of 24 days in filing both the appeals. A condonation application has been filed by the AO explaining the reason for the delay. It has been submitted that the delay was due to certain technical glitches on the e-filing portal. Considering the explanation of the AO, the delay in filing of both the appeals is condoned. ITA No.874/Ahd/2025 for AY 2019-20 3. The brief facts of the case are that assessee had filed its return of income for AY 2019-20 on 02.10.2019, declaring Nil income under normal provision and income of Rs.11,25,00,585/ under AMT/MAT provisions. The assessee is engaged in the business of construction. A survey action u/s.133A of the Act was conducted in the case of the assessee on 30.05.2019. In the course of survey, a digital data in the form of excel sheet was impounded which contained details of on-money received by the assessee on sale of units. The case of the assessee was reopened by issue of notice u/s.148 of the Act on 19.04.2023. In the course of assessment proceeding, the AO had analysed the impounded excel sheet from which it transpired that the assessee had received total on-money of Rs.14,23,19,510/- during the year. In the assessment, Assessing Officer treated the entire on-money of Rs.14,23,19,510/- as income of the assessee, which was subjected to tax at special rate as per the section 115BBE of the Act. The assessment was completed u/s.147 of the Act on 21.07.2023 at a total income of Rs.14,23,19,510/- 4. Aggrieved with the order of the AO, the assessee had filed an appeal before the First Appellate Authority which was decided by the Ld. CIT(A) vide the impugned order and the appeal of the assessee was partly allowed. 5. Now the Revenue is in appeal before us. The following grounds have been taken in this appeal: 1. On the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in restricting the addition u/s 69A of the I.T. Act from Rs. ₹14,23,19,510/- to ₹2,13,47,927/- that was made on account of ‘on Money’ receipts despite the fact that the same had been made on the basis of impounded materials recovered during the course of survey proceedings. 2 On the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in restricting the addition of 14,23,19,510/- to ₹2,13,47,927/- made by the Assessing Officer u/s 69A of the I.T. Act being ‘On Money’ receipts estimating the profit 15% of ‘On Money’ despite the fact that the entire expenses in relation to the project of the assessee were debited in the Profit and Loss account of the assessee. 3. On the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in restricting the addition u/s 69A of the I.T. Act from 14,23,19,510/-to 2,13,47,927/- that was made on account of ‘On Money’ by giving set-off against excess stock of Rs.4.76 Crore disclosed by the assessee in survey even though the assessee failed to satisfactorily explain the source of such excess stock. 4. On the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law the Ld. CIT(A) has erred in allowing the ground in respect of invoking the provisions of section 115BBE of the I.T. Act despite the fact that the addition was made u/s 69A of the I.T. Act and the provisions of section 115BBE are applicable in this case, 5. The appellant craves to add, amend, alter, substitute, modify the above ground of appeal, raise any new ground of appeal, if necessary, either before or during the course of the hearing of the appeal on the basis of submissions to be made. 6. Shri R V Aroon Prasad, CIT-DR, explained that the excel sheet was found and impounded from the premises of the assessee and the fact that the entries appearing in the excel sheet pertained to the assessee, was not disputed. He had drawn our attention to the assessment order, wherein the entries in the excel sheet were analysed in detail by the AO. The Ld. CIT-DR explained that as per the entries appearing in the excel sheet, the total on-money received by the assessee during the year was Rs.14,23,19,510/-. He submitted that Ld. CIT(A) was not correct in estimating the income out of on-money receipt @ 15% and restricting the addition to the extent of Rs.2,13,47,927/- only. The Ld. CIT.DR contended that the entire expenses in relation to the project of the assessee was already debited to the profits & loss account. Therefore, Ld. CIT(A) was not correct in allowing further set off of expenses from the on-money receipt and estimating the profit by applying net profit rate of 15% in respect of on-money receipt. The Ld. CIT.DR further submitted that the Ld. CIT(A) had allowed set-off of on-money income of Rs.2,13,47,927/- with the excess stock of Rs.4.76 crores disclosed by the assessee in the course of survey. He submitted that the addition for excess stock was made for the reason that the assessee had failed to satisfactorily explain the source of excess stock, which was an altogether different issue. The Ld. CIT.DR also challenged the finding of Ld.CIT(A) in not invoking the provisions of section 115BBE of the Act. 7. Per Contra, Shri Biren Shah, the Ld. AR submitted that the AO was not correct in treating the entire on-money receipts as per excel sheet, as income of the assessee. He submitted that only the profit imbedded in the on-money receipt could have been subjected to tax and the Ld. CIT(A) had rightly estimated the profit by applying net profit rate of 15%. The Ld. AR also supported the decision of the Ld. CIT(A) in allowing set-off of the income out of on-money receipt with the disclosure made by the assessee on account of excessive stock. He explained that the source of excess stock was application of income earned by the assessee by way of on-money receipt. The Ld. AR further contended that the on-money receipts were part of business receipts and, therefore, the Ld. CIT(A) had rightly held that the provisions of section 115BBE of the Act was not applicable in the facts of the present case. 8. We have carefully considered the rival submission. The precise question to be answered in this case is whether the entire on-money receipt as per the impounded excel sheet could have been considered as income of the assessee. It is a settled position of law that only income embedded in the on-money receipts can be subjected to tax. In the business of real estate, many expenses are incurred in cash and it is for that reason that part of the sale consideration is received in cash as on-money. The Hon’ble Jurisdictional High Court in the case of PCIT vs. Anupam Organiser (Tax Appeal No.168 of 2020) dated 10.09.2020 has held that not the entire receipt of on-money but only profit embedded therein can be added. Similarly, in the case of DCIT vs. Panna Corporation – Tax Appeal 323 of 2000 (Guj) the Hon’ble jurisdictional High Court had held as under: “15. It can, thus, be seen that consistently, this Court and some other Courts have been following the principle that even upon detection of on money receipt or unaccounted cash receipt, what can be brought to tax is the profit embedded in such receipts and not the entire receipts themselves. If that be the legal position, what should be estimated as a reasonable profit out of such receipts, must bear an element of estimation”. It is thus a well settled legal proposition that the entire unaccounted on-money receipts cannot considered as income. Rather, only the profit element embedded therein can be added in the hands of the assessee. The Ld. CIT(A) had, therefore, rightly held that only profit embedded in the on-money receipt could have been taxed. Accordingly, he has estimated the profit at the rate of 15% on the on-money. This Tribunal has consistently worked out profit of on-money by applying net profit margin. In the case of Sankalp INN decided by the Co-ordinate Bench of this Tribunal in IT(SS)A No.45 to 48/Ahd/2022 and others, dated 31.01.2025, profit rate of 13% was upheld in respect of on-money receipts. In the case of Jay Builder v. Assistant Commissioner of Income-tax, [2013] 33 taxmann.com 62 (Gujarat) Hon’ble Gujarat High Court had upheld the addition at rate of 15 per cent of on money received by assessee. In our considered opinion, the Ld. CIT(A) has rightly estimated the profit of on-money receipts by applying net profit rate of 15%. We do not find any infirmity in the order of the Ld. CIT(A) on this issue. Accordingly, the estimation of profit out of on-money by applying net profit rate of 15% is upheld. The ground of appeal taken by the Revenue in this respect is dismissed. 9. The next objection of the Revenue is against allowing set off the profit earned out of on-money receipt with the disclosure made by the assessee on account of excess stock. In the course of survey Mr. Jayesh Rao, a partner of the assessee, had admitted additional income on account of excess stock for the current year. The disclosure of Rs.4.76 crores made on account of excess stock was honoured by the assessee and offered to tax in return of income and this fact has not been controverted by the Revenue. The Ld. CIT(A) has rightly held that the profit embedded in on-money receipt was subsumed in the application in the form of disclosure for excess stock as made by the assessee and, therefore, no separate addition for the unaccounted profit earned out of the on-money receipts was called for. The Ld. CIT(A) has relied upon the decision of Hon’ble Delhi High Court in the case of PCIT vs Surya Agrotech [2023] 154 taxmann.com 156(Delhi) in this regard. It is not the case of the Revenue that the assessee did not offer the disclosure of Rs.4.76 crores made on account of excess stock in the return of income. It is also not the case that the income worked out in respect of on-money receipt exceeded the disclosure for excess stock. As rightly held by the Ld. CIT(A), the assessee could not have been burdened with to tax on earning of undisclosed income and also on application of the said income, in the form of excess stock. The set-off of income out of on-money receipt with the excess stock was based on sound accounting principle and was rightly allowed by the Ld. CIT(A). Therefore, the ground taken by the Revenue in this regard, is dismissed. 10. The next ground of the Revenue regarding application of provision of section of 115BBE of the Act, is also found to be without merit. The on-money receipt was intrinsically connected with the construction business of the assessee and, therefore, was a part of the business receipt. Under the circumstances, the provision of section 115BBE of the Act was not attracted. The decision of the Ld. CIT(A) on this issue is upheld and the ground of the Revenue is dismissed. 11. In the result, the appeal of the Revenue in ITA No.874/Ahd/2025 for A.Y. 2019-20 is dismissed. ITA No.875/Ahd/2025 for AY 2020-21 12. The facts involved and the grounds taken by the Revenue in this appeal are identical to ITA No.874/Ahd/2025 for A.Y. 2019-20, which we have decided in favour of the assessee and against the Revenue in the preceding paragraphs. Therefore, the decision as taken in ITA No.874/Ahd/2025 for AY 2019-20 will apply mutatis mutandis in ITA No.875/Ahd/2025 for AY 2020-21 as well. Hence, this appeal of the Revenue is also dismissed. 13. In the combined result, both the appeals of the Revenue are dismissed. Order pronounced in the Court on 18/11/2026 at Ahmedabad.
