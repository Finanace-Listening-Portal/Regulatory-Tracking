Workmen’s Compensation, generally called Employees’ Compensation in current legislation, concerns compensation payable when an employee suffers a qualifying injury or occupational disease arising from employment. Depending on the circumstances, the employer may be liable for compensation relating to disablement or death. An employee compensation insurance policy can help employers meet covered legal liabilities. It differs from marine insurance, which may cover goods during transit. For a logistics business, cargo damage and injuries suffered by workers are separate concerns. Compensation is assessed under applicable law, while insurer payments depend on policy terms. Employers should also maintain accident reports and relevant medical records. What Is Employees’ State Insurance? ESI is a social security scheme administered by the Employees’ State Insurance Corporation (ESIC). It supports insured employees and, for certain benefits, eligible family members. Unlike employee compensation, its benefits are not limited to incidents occurring at work. Depending on the prescribed conditions, ESI can provide: Medical treatment for insured employees and eligible dependants. Sickness benefits when qualifying illness prevents work. Maternity benefits for eligible insured women. Disablement and dependants’ benefits following employment injuries. Employers covered by the scheme must complete the required registrations, maintain records and pay applicable contributions. Can Employees Claim Both for One Injury? Workmen’s Compensation and ESI do not normally provide duplicate benefits for the same employment injury. Under the Code on Social Security, 2020, an employee entitled to ESI disablement benefits, or a dependant entitled to benefits, cannot also claim Employees’ Compensation from the employer for that injury. Eligibility depends on the worker’s circumstances and the legal provisions. Employers should confirm which scheme applies before explaining compensation options or processing a claim. What Should Small Businesses Review? Small firms should review employee benefit obligations alongside business risks. An MSME insurance policy may cover premises, equipment or business assets, depending on its terms, but it does not replace statutory employee benefits. Check whether ESI applies to the establishment and staff, and assess compensation responsibilities for workers outside the scheme. When contractors provide labour, clarify registration and reporting duties. Accurate records and clear responsibility for incidents support claims handling. Final Thoughts Workmen’s Compensation focuses on an employer’s legal liability for qualifying workplace injuries, while ESI provides wider medical and financial benefits to eligible employees. Both are addressed under India’s Code on Social Security, 2020, but their purposes and eligibility requirements differ. Employers should understand these differences, maintain proper employee records and review their legal responsibilities. As requirements depend on several factors, businesses should check applicable rules to ensure appropriate employee support and statutory compliance.
