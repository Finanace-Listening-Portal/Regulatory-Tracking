Summary: ICICI Prudential Bluechip Fund is now named ICICI Prudential Large Cap Fund; it is the same underlying scheme, not a new fund created for the rename. The AMC describes it as an open-ended equity scheme predominantly investing in large-cap stocks, benchmarked to the NIFTY 100 TRI and carrying a Very High Riskometer. Current dated disclosures should be used for NAV, assets under management, portfolio and total expense ratio because those figures change. As at 30 September 2026, the reported scheme AUM was about ₹76,352 crore; investors should confirm this against the official monthly factsheet before publication or transaction. The current disclosed exit-load structure is 1% on redemption or switch-out within one month from allotment and nil thereafter. This article explains the mandate, benchmark, active-management risk, Direct and Regular plans, Growth and IDCW options, costs, redemption, KYC and tax. It does not rank or recommend the scheme and does not use past performance as a prediction. The historical name in an old statement, folio, platform or ledger should therefore be mapped to the current name rather than treated as a second scheme. Plan and option variants remain interests in the same underlying scheme, although NAV and expense structure differ. The AMC describes the product as an open-ended equity scheme predominantly investing in large-cap stocks. Under SEBI’s mutual-fund categorisation framework, a large-cap scheme must maintain the prescribed minimum exposure to large-cap companies. The category label narrows the investment universe but does not provide capital protection or a fixed return. Such companies may have established businesses and relatively deeper trading liquidity, but they can still suffer earnings shocks, governance failures, valuation compression and prolonged market declines. Large-cap does not mean low-risk, and the scheme’s Very High Riskometer should not be diluted by informal descriptions such as conservative equity. The NIFTY 100 TRI is the primary benchmark. TRI means Total Returns Index and assumes reinvestment of dividends, making it a more complete comparator than a price-only index. The scheme is actively managed rather than a tracker. Its portfolio, sector weights and cash position can differ from the benchmark, so returns can be higher or lower over any period. Tracking the benchmark in a table does not create an obligation to match it. Monthly portfolio disclosures should be read across several periods. A single snapshot can overstate the permanence of a sector weight or top holding. Relevant questions include concentration in the ten largest holdings, financial-sector weight, exposure outside the large-cap bucket within regulatory limits, turnover, cash and derivatives, and whether the investment process has changed. Portfolio data should be cited with an explicit date. Scheme AUM, NAV and Expense Ratio: Reference Dates Assets under management measure the market value managed in the scheme; they are not an insurance reserve or guarantee. The September 2026 reported AUM of about ₹76,352 crore is a dated figure and may change with subscriptions, redemptions and market movements. NAV is calculated for each plan and option after expenses and portfolio valuation. Different NAVs do not by themselves mean that a lower-NAV plan is cheaper or has more upside. The total expense ratio is deducted in computing NAV and may change within regulatory limits after disclosure. Direct and Regular plans hold the same portfolio, but the Regular plan includes distribution-related expense that the Direct plan excludes. Consequently, their NAVs and returns diverge over time. Investors must quote the plan-specific current TER from the official AMC disclosure rather than applying a single percentage to both plans. The fund’s market spread is not directly visible as a separate investor charge in an open-ended scheme, but trading costs and portfolio expenses affect NAV. Taxes, exit load and platform or advisory fees may also affect an investor’s outcome. A factual profile should keep TER, exit load and tax separate instead of presenting one combined number as the cost of ownership. Direct and Regular Plans, Options and Redemption Conditions Direct and Regular are plan choices; Growth and Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal are option choices. Under Growth, distributable surplus remains reflected in NAV. Under IDCW, the trustee may declare a distribution from distributable surplus, and NAV normally falls to reflect the payout and related effects. IDCW is not assured interest and may include capital withdrawal; its label should not be interpreted as a guaranteed dividend. The current exit-load rule is applied lot by lot. Redemption or switch-out within one month from the allotment date attracts 1%; after that period the stated load is nil. Each SIP instalment has a separate allotment date. A switch from Regular to Direct, Growth to IDCW or this scheme to another scheme is generally processed as a redemption and fresh purchase, creating possible load and tax consequences even when cash does not reach the bank. Applicable NAV depends on a valid transaction request, cut-off rules and, for purchases, realisation of funds. Redemption proceeds are normally paid under SEBI and scheme timelines subject to valid bank details, exceptional market conditions and statutory holds. Investors should escalate a delay through the AMC or registrar, then the prescribed grievance mechanisms with folio and transaction evidence. Mutual Fund KYC, Purchase and Redemption Procedures An investor ordinarily completes mutual-fund KYC using PAN, identity, address, bank, contact and beneficial-owner information. FATCA and CRS declarations may be required, and nomination or a valid opt-out must be recorded. The investor selects the plan, option, investment amount and mode, and should verify units in the statement of account or demat holding after allotment. A distributor’s ARN identifies distribution involvement; it does not change the AMC or trustee responsible for the scheme. SIP is an instruction for periodic purchases, not a separate product or promise of return. A missed debit, rejected mandate or insufficient balance can interrupt purchases. The investor should reconcile bank debits, allotment dates, units and NAV. Consolidated account statements, capital-gains statements and folio-level transaction reports should be preserved for tax and succession purposes. Indian Tax on Large-Cap Equity Fund Investments Where the scheme satisfies the statutory definition of an equity-oriented fund and the relevant Securities Transaction Tax conditions are met, units held for not more than 12 months are generally short-term capital assets. Qualifying short-term capital gains are taxed at 20%, plus applicable surcharge and cess. Units held for more than 12 months can produce qualifying long-term capital gains taxed at 12.5% on the aggregate covered gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh, plus surcharge and cess. The ₹1.25 lakh amount is an aggregate threshold for the covered long-term equity gains of the taxpayer, not a fresh threshold for each fund, folio or redemption. Grandfathering can remain relevant for qualifying units acquired before the statutory cut-off. Lot selection, cost, STT, exit load and residence status affect the working. NRIs can face withholding at source that differs from their final liability and may need treaty or return analysis. A switch is usually a taxable redemption followed by a purchase. IDCW is generally taxable in the investor’s hands at the applicable slab rate, with TDS where statutory conditions are met. TDS is a credit and should be reconciled with AIS and Form 26AS. Securities Transaction Tax may apply on redemption of equity-oriented fund units as prescribed. Tax rules should be checked for the actual transaction date.
