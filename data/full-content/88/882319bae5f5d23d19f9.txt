The majority opinion of Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud, joined by Justices J.B. Pardiwala and Manoj Misra, held that equality applies from the appointment stage, that a party cannot unilaterally appoint a sole arbitrator, and that a public sector undertaking cannot compel its counterparty to choose its nominee from a panel exclusively curated by that undertaking. Justices Hrishikesh Roy and P.S. Narasimha delivered separate opinions differing on material aspects of the reasoning and permissible appointment arrangements.The reference arose from conflicting decisions on the extent of party autonomy under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. In Voestalpine Schienen GmbH v. Delhi Metro Rail Corporation Ltd., the Court had addressed appointment from a government entity’s panel and required a broad-based panel. In TRF Ltd. v. Energo Engineering Projects Ltd., it held that a managing director who was statutorily ineligible to act as arbitrator could not nominate another arbitrator. Perkins Eastman Architects DPC v. HSCC (India) Ltd. extended the protection against an interested party’s control over appointment of a sole arbitrator.The earlier CORE decision concerned Clause 64(3)(b) of the Railways’ General Conditions of Contract. Railways would forward at least four retired railway officers’ names; the contractor would suggest at least two; and the General Manager would choose at least one as the contractor’s nominee, appoint the remaining arbitrators and identify the presiding arbitrator. That decision treated the contractor’s limited choice as a counterbalance to the General Manager’s powers. In Union of India v. Tantia Constructions Limited, a three-Judge Bench expressed prima facie disagreement and sought consideration by a larger Bench.
