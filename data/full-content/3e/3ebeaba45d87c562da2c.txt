Kadhar Basha & Another Vs Union, dismissed a criminal original petition filed under Section 528 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, read with Section 482 Cr.P.C., challenging the judicial remand order dated 27 July 2023 passed by the Judicial Magistrate-II, Ponneri. The petitioners, E. Kadhar Basha and R. Senthil Kumar, were arrayed as accused Nos.1 and 2 in R.R.No.23 of 2023, involving alleged offences under Sections 8(c) read with 20(b)(ii)(C), 27A, 28 and 29 of the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985. The Narcotics Control Bureau alleged that the petitioners were transporting approximately 432.700 kg of ganja in a vehicle bearing registration No.TN 31 BD 6347, intercepted near Karanodai Toll Plaza, Chennai, on 24 July 2023. The petitioners challenged the legality of their arrest and subsequent remand, alleging prolonged unlawful detention, physical and mental torture, failure to produce them before a Magistrate within 24 hours and non-communication of their arrest to relatives or nominated persons. The petitioners contended that although they were intercepted on 24 July 2023, they were produced for judicial remand only on 27 July 2023. According to them, the intervening detention violated Section 57 and Section 167 of the Code of Criminal Procedure and the constitutional safeguards under Article 22. They further alleged that their friends, relatives and family members were not informed of their arrest. Relying on the Supreme Court judgment in Vihaan Kumar Vs State of Haryana and Another, dated 7 February 2025, they argued that an arrested person must be informed of the grounds of arrest and that statutory requirements concerning arrest intimation must be strictly observed. They submitted that non-compliance with these safeguards rendered their custody and the remand order illegal. The High Court examined the arrest and investigation records. It noted that, acting on confidential information, NCB officers intercepted the vehicle driven by the first petitioner at approximately 18.10 hours on 24 July 2023. The accused allegedly disclosed that the vehicle contained 220 packets of ganja packed in 11 gunny bags, concealed beneath a green plastic tarpaulin and intended for delivery at Ramanathapuram. The officers conducted a search in the presence of witnesses, recovered the contraband, drew samples and seized the vehicle. Personal searches were also conducted after following the procedure prescribed under Section 50 of the NDPS Act. The petitioners were subsequently issued summons under Section 67 of the NDPS Act on 25 July 2023 and made statements on 25 and 26 July 2023. The Court recorded that arrest memos were issued on 26 July 2023 at approximately 19.00 hours and that the petitioners were thereafter produced before the Judicial Magistrate-II, Ponneri. On examining the arrest-intimation records, the High Court found that the grounds of arrest had been recorded and communicated. The first petitioner had endorsed the arrest intimation dated 26 July 2023, acknowledging that his wife had been informed of his arrest through a telephone call made from the respondent’s office. Similarly, the second petitioner had endorsed that his mother had been informed by telephone. The Court also referred to the Magistrate’s remand proceedings, which recorded the absence of external injuries and complaints, and noted that arrest intimation had been given to the persons nominated by the petitioners. The Magistrate had accepted the remand and committed the petitioners to judicial custody until 4 August 2023. On these facts, the High Court concluded that the respondent had complied with the applicable constitutional and statutory procedures concerning arrest and remand. The High Court distinguished the Supreme Court decision relied upon by the petitioners, holding that the factual records in the present case established communication of the grounds of arrest and intimation to family members. It further observed that the petitioners had challenged the remand order dated 27 July 2023 nearly two years later, after they had not obtained bail. Finding no infirmity or illegality in the remand order, Justice G.K. Ilanthiraiyan dismissed Crl.O.P.No.5321 of 2025 by order dated 26 February 2025. The decision turns on the Court’s assessment of the arrest records and acknowledgments in this particular case, rather than any relaxation of the constitutional safeguards applicable to arrested persons. Cases Discussed 1. Vihaan Kumar Vs State of Haryana and Another (Supreme Court); SLP (Crl.) No.13320 of 2024; 07/02/2025 Principle: The petitioners relied on this decision concerning the constitutional requirement to inform an arrested person of the grounds of arrest and the statutory safeguards requiring arrest intimation to relatives, friends or nominated persons. Treatment: Distinguished on facts. The Madras High Court found that the arrest records and petitioners’ endorsements established compliance with the relevant safeguards. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF MADRAS HIGH COURT This petition has been filed challenging the order of remand passed as against the petitioner by the Judicial Magistrate-II, Ponneri in R.R.No.23 of 2023, dated 27.07.2023. 2. Heard the learned counsel on either side and perused the materials placed on record. 3. The petitioners are arrayed as A1 and A2 in R.R.No.23 of 2023 under Sections 8(c) read with 20(b)(ii)(C), 27A, 28 and 29 of NDPS Act, 1985 on the file of the respondent, alleging that the petitioners were in possession and transported Ganja weighing about 432.700 kgs and intercepted a vehicle which was driven by the first accused bearing Registration No.TN 31 BD 6347 at Karanodai Toll Plaza, Chennai on 24/25.07.2023. However, they were remanded to judicial custody on 27.07.2023. In the meanwhile, they were kept in the illegal custody and they were tortured by physically and mentally for almost three days. 4. After a period of three days, they were produced for remand on 27.07.2023. Immediately, after their arrest, they were not produced before the nearest Magistrate. That apart, their arrest were not intimated to any of the petitioners’ friends, relatives or family members. The respondent violated the mandatory procedure as contemplated under Section 167 Cr.P.C. Further, the respondent failed to produce the petitioners within 24 hours from the time of arrest/detention as contemplated under Section 57 of Cr.P.C or not transmitted to the nearest Judicial Magistrate. Therefore, it is a clear violation of Article 22 of Constitution of India. 5. In support of contention, the learned counsel for the petitioners relied upon the Judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India in SLP (Crl.)No.13320 of 2024, in the case of Vihaan Kumar Vs State of Haryana and another dated 07.02.2025, in which the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India held that as per clause 1 of Article 22 of Constitution of India that no person who is arrested shall be detained in custody without being informed, as soon as may be, of the grounds for such arrest. Further as per Section 50 of Cr.P.C, a person arrested to be informed of grounds of arrest and of right to bail. 6. Article 22(5) of Constitution of India is regarding communication of the grounds of arrest or detention in writing to the relatives, family members or friends of the accused. Therefore, Article 22(1) and 22(5) of Constitution of India cannot be breached under any situation. Non compliance of the constitutional requirements and statutory mandate would lead to the custody or the detention being rendered illegal, as the case may be. 7. It was further held that the purpose of inserting Section 50A of Cr.P.C, making it obligatory on the person making arrest to inform about the arrest to the friends, relatives or persons nominated by the arrested person, is to ensure that they would able to take immediate and prompt actions to secure the release of the arrested person as permissible under the law. The arrested person, because of his detention, may not have immediate and easy access to the legal process for securing his release, which would otherwise be available to the friends, relatives and such nominated persons by way of engaging lawyers, briefing them to secure release of the detained person on bail at the earliest. Therefore, communicating the grounds of detenue and in addition to his relatives as mentioned above is not merely a formality but to enable the detained person to know the reasons for his arrest but also to provide the necessary opportunity to him through his relatives, friends or nominated persons. 8. A perusal of records revealed that on secret information, the respondent intercepted the vehicle which was driven by the first accused along with second accused at about 18.10 hrs, on 24.07.2023 and the accused were questioned about the transportation of ganja. The accused replied that they were carrying 220 ganja packets packed in 11 gunny bags concealed in the truck back side and covered with green colour plastic tarpaulin. Those packets have to be delivered at Ramanathapuram, Tamil Nadu. Thereafter, they were served with summons to conduct search. In the presence of two witnesses, search was made and it was found that they were in possession of Ganja in 11 gunny bags. Each gunny bag had 20 brown colour taped packets. After taking samples, sealed remaining packets. Thereafter, personal search was also conducted on the petitioners, after following the provisions under Section 50 of NDPS Act. The vehicle was also seized and prepared mahazer. 9. Thereafter, on 25.07.2023, the petitioners were issued with summons under Section 67 of NDPS Act for enquiry and the same were duly received by them. They made statements on 25.07.2023 and 26.07.2023. After recording their statements, on 26.07.2023, they were issued with arrest memo at about 19.00 hrs. Thereafter, they were produced before the learned Magistrate II, Ponneri, Thiruvallur. 10. On 27.07.2023, at about 09.45 pm, the petitioners were served with arrest memo and the reasons for the arrest were also duly explained to them. The learned Judicial Magistrate II, Ponneri, recorded that no external injury found and no complaint against the petitioners. The arrest intimation was also given to the persons nominated by the petitioners. Thereafter, the learned Magistrate accepted the remand and remanded them to judicial custody till 04.08.2023. 11. A perusal of the arrest intimation of the petitioners dated 26.07.2023 categorically stated about the grounds of arrest and also intimated to the wife of the first petitioner. In fact, the first petitioner also endorsed in the arrest intimation dated 26.07.2023 that the arrest of the first petitioner was duly informed by him through the phone from the office of the respondent. Likewise, the second petitioner also endorsed that the arrest intimation was informed to his mother through the phone from the office of the respondent. 12. Therefore, the respondent followed the procedures as contemplated under Article 22(1) and 22(5) of the Constitution of India. They also followed the procedure as contemplated under Section 57 of Cr.P.C and Section 50 of NDPS Act, while arresting the petitioners and also while remanding them to the judicial custody. Therefore the above Judgment is not applicable to the case on hand. That apart, the petitioners challenged the order of remand dated 27.07.2023 after a period of nearly two years, since they were not granted bail. Therefore, they have challenged the order of remand as if the respondent failed to follow the procedures while arresting them and while remanding them to judicial custody. 13. In view of the above, this Court finds no infirmity or illegality in the order of remand as against the petitioners by the Judicial Magistrate-II, Ponneri in R.R.No.23 of 2023, dated 27.07.2023 to interfere by this Court. 14. Accordingly, this Criminal Original Petition is dismissed.
