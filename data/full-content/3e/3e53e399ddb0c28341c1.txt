Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Royal Infrastructure Vs ITO (ITAT Surat) Section 68 Addition Cannot Rest on a Lender’s Alleged Entry-Provider Status When Loan Evidence Stands Unrebutted Unsecured Loans and Interest Disputed Across Four Years Royal Infrastructure, a partnership firm engaged in construction and development, challenged additions under section 68 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 for unsecured loans of ₹2.13 crore, ₹1.85 crore and ₹20 lakh for AYs 2015–16, 2016–17 and 2017–18, respectively. The dispute also covered interest on the loans across four assessment years, including an enhancement of ₹11.30 lakh by the CIT(A) for AY 2015–16. For AY 2018–19, the principal dispute concerned interest of ₹17.97 lakh. The firm furnished lender confirmations, PAN details, income-tax return acknowledgements and bank statements. For AY 2015–16, the lenders also responded directly to notices issued under section 133(6). The firm showed that the loans had been repaid through banking channels and that interest had been paid after deduction of tax at source. Low Returned Income and Cash Deposits Warranted Enquiry The Revenue relied on cash deposits immediately preceding the issue of loan cheques in the accounts of two individual lenders. It also pointed to the low returned income of certain corporate lenders and to common addresses and directors. The Tribunal acknowledged that these circumstances warranted enquiry into the lenders’ capacity and the movement of funds. The Tribunal held that creditworthiness must be assessed from the available financial and banking records. A lender’s returned income for a particular year gives only a partial picture of the funds available for advancing a loan. In this case, the Assessing Officer identified no cash trail from the assessee to the lenders and produced no independent evidence that the loans represented the assessee’s own undisclosed funds. The Tribunal weighed the warning signs against the confirmations, direct replies and documented movement of funds. Third-Party Statement Required a Link to the Assessee’s Loans The Revenue relied on a statement of Pranaw Kumar Modi concerning companies alleged to provide accommodation entries. The assessee requested cross-examination, which was not granted. The Department argued that the statement merely corroborated the documentary evidence. The Tribunal held that even if a lender had provided accommodation entries to some beneficiaries, the Revenue still had to connect the specific transactions under assessment with that arrangement. The record contained no material identifying Royal Infrastructure as a beneficiary or tracing funds from it to an alleged entry operator. In those circumstances, the untested statement could not independently sustain the addition. The Tribunal applied the same reasoning to the statements relied upon to characterise Dolphin Commerce Pvt. Ltd. as an entry provider. Documented Repayment Was Relevant to Genuineness The Tribunal followed the Gujarat High Court decisions in Ayachi Chandrashekhar Narsangji and Ambe Tradecorp Pvt. Ltd. In the former, the High Court declined to interfere with deletion of an addition where the Department had accepted repayment of the loan in the following year. In the latter, it recognised that where the lender’s identity and repayment were established, the original credit had to be considered alongside the corresponding repayment entry. Applying those decisions, the Tribunal treated repayment as one part of the evidence, alongside the lender particulars, confirmations, bank records and payment of interest after TDS. The Revenue did not dispute that the loans had been repaid. The Tribunal also referred to the Surat Bench decision in DCIT v. Gordhanbhai Ranchhodbhai Asodaria, ITA No. 254/SRT/2025, which considered repayment in the context of an alleged accommodation-entry loan. Supreme Court Tests Under Section 68 Continue to Apply The Tribunal accepted the principles in PCIT v. NRA Iron & Steel Pvt. Ltd. (2019) 412 ITR 161 (SC) and Sumati Dayal v. CIT (1995) 214 ITR 801 (SC). The assessee must establish the identity of the creditor, creditworthiness and genuineness of the transaction, and the surrounding circumstances remain relevant. The Bench distinguished the Revenue’s reliance on Seema Realcon Pvt. Ltd. and J.K. Global, where the evidence demonstrated a direct nexus with the alleged accommodation-entry arrangement. On the evidence as a whole, the Tribunal deleted the principal additions under section 68. It also deleted the consequential interest disallowances and enhancements. All four appeals were allowed, and the remaining grounds were treated as academic. Author’s Comments The ruling shows why the evidence under section 68 should be examined lender by lender. A confirmation addresses who advanced the loan. Financial and banking records show the lender’s capacity and the movement of money. Repayment records show how the liability was discharged. Their combined effect carried weight here because the Department’s material raised questions about the lenders generally while leaving the specific loan transactions unsupported by evidence of an accommodation arrangement. Where a third-party statement is relied upon, the most consequential question is what the statement and the corroborating material actually establish about the assessee’s own borrowing.
