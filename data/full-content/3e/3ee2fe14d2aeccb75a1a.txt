Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Sweekar Vs ITO (ITAT Nagpur) Delayed Form 10B Cannot Defeat Section 11 Exemption When Filed Before Processing: ITAT The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Nagpur Bench, held that exemption under section 11 cannot be denied merely for delayed filing of Form 10B where the audit report was available with the Department before processing the return under section 143(1). Allowing the appeals of Sweekar for assessment years 2019-20 and 2020-21, the Tribunal directed the Assessing Officer to grant the exemption claimed. The decision emphasises the distinction between substantive compliance with audit requirements and a procedural lapse concerning the stage of furnishing the audit report. The Tribunal followed judicial precedents holding that delayed submission should not, by itself, defeat an otherwise admissible exemption. Facts of the Case The assessee was a charitable trust registered under section 12A of the Income-tax Act, 1961. For assessment year 2019-20, it filed its return on 31 October 2019, declaring nil total income after claiming exemption under section 11 amounting to ₹15,38,925. The order records that the return was filed within the extended due date under section 139(1). The assessee also electronically furnished its audit report in Form 10B on 31 October 2019. However, while processing the return under section 143(1), the Department treated the audit report as not having been furnished within the prescribed time. Consequently, the exemption was denied and total income was determined at ₹15,38,925 through an intimation dated 11 May 2020. The assessee sought rectification under section 154, but its application was rejected by an order dated 20 January 2025. The first appellate authority subsequently upheld the denial of exemption. A similar dispute arose for assessment year 2020-21. Both appeals were therefore heard together, with assessment year 2019-20 treated as the lead case. Assessee’s Submissions The assessee argued that the appellate authority had sustained the denial of exemption without properly considering the filing particulars and supporting submissions. Its principal contention was that Form 10B had already been electronically filed before the return was processed. The report was furnished on 31 October 2019, whereas the intimation under section 143(1) was issued on 11 May 2020. Accordingly, the audit report was available with the Department when it examined the return. The assessee relied on its paper book and judicial decisions to contend that an alleged delay in furnishing the report should not result in denial of the exemption. The Revenue supported the order of the first appellate authority. Tribunal’s Observations Judicial Member Pavan Kumar Gadale considered the submissions and the material on record. The Tribunal identified the central question as whether the exemption could be denied for delayed furnishing of Form 10B when the report was available before processing under section 143(1). The Tribunal relied on the Jaipur Bench’s decision in Kinki v. ITO (Exemption), ITA No. 1185/JPR/2025, dated 24 December 2025. That decision concerned delayed filing of Form 10BB and examined several authorities addressing the procedural nature of furnishing audit reports. The precedents discussed included the Gujarat High Court’s decisions in CIT (Exemption) v. Laxmanarayan Dev Shrishan Seva Khendra, Parul Mahila Pragati Mandal v. ITO (Exemption) and Association of Indian Panel Board Manufacturers v. DCIT. These authorities distinguished the requirement of an audit report from the procedural question of when it was furnished. They supported the position that a procedural omission should not automatically extinguish the substantive right to exemption. Audit Report Available Before Intimation The Tribunal also considered decisions of the Ahmedabad Bench discussed in the Jaipur order, including Shree Vardhman Stanakvasi Jain Shravak Trust v. ITO and Shree Bhakt Samaj Vikas Education Trust v. ACIT (Exemption). The cited decisions recognised that delayed filing of Form 10B constituted a procedural default and should not, by itself, disentitle a trust to exemption where the applicable substantive conditions were satisfied. In the present case, the decisive factual circumstance was that Form 10B was filed on 31 October 2019, well before the return was processed on 11 May 2020. The Department therefore had the audit report on record before issuing the intimation denying exemption. Following the judicial precedents, the Tribunal held that the denial could not be sustained on the stated ground. Decision and Conclusion For assessment year 2019-20, the Tribunal set aside the appellate order and directed the Assessing Officer to grant the benefit of exemption under section 11 claimed by the assessee. For assessment year 2020-21, it recorded that the facts and circumstances were identical, apart from differences in figures. The same reasoning was applied, and that appeal was also allowed. The ruling reinforces that delayed furnishing of Form 10B alone is insufficient to deny exemption on the facts considered, particularly where the report was available before processing. It does not dispense with the audit requirement or the other substantive conditions governing section 11 exemption.
