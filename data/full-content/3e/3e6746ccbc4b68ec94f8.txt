Despite Tata Sons' debt repayment efforts, the RBI's regulations still apply.Tata Sons follow usfollow usThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Governor Sanjay Malhotra rejected Tata Sons’ bid to shed its non-bank lender status leaving little doubt that the Tata Group holding company must list publicly as long as it remains classified as an upper-layer (UL) non-banking financial company (NBFC) and core investment company (CIC). Governor's remarks appear to settle a debate sparked by Noel Tata, chair of Tata Trusts, Tata Sons’ largest shareholder, who had argued that RBI’s Sept 11 order, which rejected the holding company’s request to deregister as a CIC and directed it to comply with UL-NBFC regulations, did not explicitly mandate a listing.The governor declined to be drawn into the question of Noel Tata’s proposal to merge two Tata Sons subsidiaries with the holding company, a move that could help it sidestep a stock market listing.“NBFCs and CICs, like for example Tata Sons and others, are required to be registered with us because of the systemic risks,” he said.“As long as it is an NBFC within our definition, it’s a CIC and has access to funds direct or indirect, it’s required to be registered and if it is in the upper layer, it is required to list,” he said at NDTV’s World Summit conference.Malhotra said CICs sit atop group companies and can leverage funds through them, exposing money raised from banks, corporate bonds and other sources to risk.In September 2022, the RBI classified Tata Sons, the holding company of the Tata Group, as an upper-layer NBFC under its scale-based regulatory framework.In 2024, Tata Sons repaid approximately Rs 21,813 crore in debt and applied to the RBI to surrender its certificate of registration as a core investment company (CIC).Becoming debt-free did not automatically remove Tata Sons from the RBI's regulatory framework.
