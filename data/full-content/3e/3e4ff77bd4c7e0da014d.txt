Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); SEL Resources Ltd Vs ITO (ITAT Hyderabad) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Hyderabad, partly allowed for statistical purposes the appeal of SEL Resources Ltd for Assessment Year 2017-18, sustaining the property valuation adopted for computing short-term capital gains while restoring a limited acquisition-cost issue to the Assessing Officer. The appeal arose from the CIT(A), NFAC, Delhi’s order dated 18 February 2025 concerning reassessment completed on 29 March 2022. The Tribunal had earlier disposed of the appeal in ITA No.679/Hyd/2025 on 20 August 2025. That order was recalled on 6 May 2026 in the Revenue’s miscellaneous application because the earlier decision proceeded on the premise that the valuation reference was under Section 142A, whereas it had actually been made under Section 50C read with Section 55A. The company had sold its office in Dalamal Tower, Nariman Point, Mumbai, on 24 November 2016 for Rs.2,14,73,862 but had not filed its return of income. The AO initiated reassessment and proposed adopting the stamp-duty value of Rs.3,20,20,000. The company disputed that value, stating that the property had remained closed for several years, was in poor condition and had been sold in distress. At its request, the AO referred the property for valuation. However, without awaiting the report, he completed the assessment by adopting the stamp-duty value and computed short-term capital gains of Rs.1,82,40,172, observing that the assessment could subsequently be rectified when the valuation report arrived. The CIT(A) dismissed the appeal ex parte after the company failed to participate despite opportunities. Before the Tribunal, the assessee argued that a later DVO report could not be implemented under either Section 155(15) or Section 154, and that completing the assessment before receiving the report was impermissible. The Tribunal accepted the assessee’s interpretation of Section 155(15). It explained that the provision concerns a subsequent revision of stamp-duty valuation in an appeal, revision or reference of the kind contemplated by Section 50C(2)(b); it does not cover a valuation report obtained by the AO from the DVO. However, the Tribunal rejected the separate contention that Section 154 could never give effect to a later DVO report. In its view, a difference between the DVO’s valuation and the stamp-duty value adopted in the assessment could constitute a mistake apparent from the record, permitting rectification. On the facts, the DVO’s report dated 4 December 2023 valued the property at Rs.3,20,20,000, exactly matching the value adopted by the AO. Consequently, there was no valuation difference requiring rectification. The ground concerning Section 155(15) was allowed for statistical purposes, while the objections to rectification under Section 154 and completion of the assessment without awaiting the report were rejected. The Tribunal also rejected the contention that the DVO had denied adequate opportunity. The record showed an initial notice dated 28 March 2022, subsequent reminders and an opportunity to object to the preliminary valuation by 30 November 2023. The company neither supplied the requested documents nor submitted objections. The DVO therefore finalised the valuation using the information available, including the property’s location and other relevant factors. The additional ground seeking consideration of newly filed financial statements, ledger accounts, stamp-duty challans and supporting documents was not admitted because its adjudication required examination of facts not already on record. The authorities cited in the assessee’s grounds concerning distress sales, valuation references and admission of legal grounds were submissions relied upon by the assessee; the present order did not separately adopt every proposition advanced through those citations. A separate dispute concerned stamp-duty expenditure forming part of the property’s acquisition cost. The assessee claimed that it had paid Rs.9,91,000 on 14 June 2013, but the purchase deed could not then be executed, compelling it to pay another Rs.9,91,000 on 4 December 2013 before registration on 6 December 2013. Against the total claim of Rs.19,82,000, the AO had allowed only Rs.9,91,000. Referring to Section 52B of the Maharashtra Stamp Act, 1958, the Tribunal observed that the first stamp papers appeared to remain within the statutory six-month validity period when the deed was executed. Nevertheless, since the AO had recorded but failed to adjudicate the specific claim, the Tribunal remanded only this issue for verification and a speaking order after providing sufficient opportunity. It did not itself allow the additional stamp-duty expenditure. The appeal was accordingly partly allowed for statistical purposes. Before proceeding with the subject issue, we deem it apposite to observe that the present appeal was earlier disposed of by the Tribunal vide its order passed in ITA No.679/Hyd/2025, dated 20/08/2025. However, the aforesaid matter was recalled on a Miscellaneous Application filed by the Revenue in ITO v. SEL Resources Ltd. [MA No. 4 (Hyd) of 2026, dated 6-5-2026], for the reason that though the Tribunal had disposed of the appeal based on the premise that the AO had while framing the assessment made a reference to the Valuation Cell under section 142A of the Act, but the fact remained that the said reference was made under section 50C r.w.s 55A of the Act. Accordingly, the order passed by the Tribunal in ITA No.679/Hyd/2025, dated 20/08/2025 was recalled for fresh adjudication in accordance with law, after considering the correct statutory provisions and affording due opportunity to both parties. 3. Coming to the facts of the case, we find that the AO, observing that the assessee company had during the subject year sold immovable property for a consideration of Rs. 2,14,73,862/-, but had failed to file its return of income, initiated proceedings under Section 147 of the Act. Notice under Section 148 of the Act, dated 30.03.2021, was issued to the assessee company. 4. During the assessment proceedings, the A.O. observed that the assessee company had not carried out any commercial activities in the subject year. The A.O. observed that the assessee company had, during the year under consideration, sold an immovable property, i.e., Office No. 1410 on the 14th floor in Dalamal Tower, situated at 211, Nariman Point, Mumbai, on 24.11.2016, for a consideration of Rs. 2,14,73,862/-. On a perusal of the record, the A.O. observed that the said property was purchased by the assessee company on 06.12.2013. 5. The A.O. observed that the fair market value (FMV) of the subject property as on the date of transfer was Rs. 3,20,20,000/-. The assessee company, on being confronted with the aforesaid fact, submitted vide its reply dated 17.03.2022 that as the subject property had remained closed and was in a very bad condition at the time of sale, therefore, the stamp duty value fixed by the Valuation Authority for the said property was very high as compared to its actual realizable market price. The assessee company requested the A.O. to refer the matter to the Valuation Officer to determine the fair market value of the subject property, keeping in view its condition. 6. The AO acted upon the aforesaid request of the assessee company and made a reference to the Technical Unit (TU) for determining the fair market value of the subject property on the date of sale, i.e., on 24-11-2016. 7. As the valuation report from the valuation cell was not received by the A.O. prior to culmination of the assessment proceedings, therefore, he, as per Section 50C of the Act, adopted the stamp duty value of the subject property of Rs. 3.20 crore (supra), as the deemed sale consideration with a specific observation that as and when the valuation report is received after the completion of the assessment proceedings, then the value so reported would be revised as per the amendment/rectification/revision provisions of Section 154 of the Act. Accordingly, the A.O., based on his aforesaid observation, after inter alia, taking the deemed sale consideration of the subject property at Rs. 3.20 crore (as against the actual consideration of Rs.2,14,73,862/-), determined the short-term capital gain on the transfer of the same at Rs. 1,82,40,172/-. 8. Aggrieved, the assessee company carried the matter in appeal before the CIT(A). However, as the assessee company, despite having been afforded sufficient opportunity, failed to participate in the proceedings before the CIT(A), the latter, finding no infirmity in the view taken by the A.O., dismissed the appeal vide an ex parte order. 9. The assessee company, being aggrieved with the order of the CIT(A), has carried the matter in appeal before us. 10. We have heard the learned Authorized Representatives of both parties, perused the orders of authorities below and the material available on record. 11. Shri Phaneendra Nag, CA, Learned Authorized Representative (for short, “Ld. AR”) for the assessee company, at the threshold of hearing of the appeal, submitted that as section 155(15) of the Act does not encompass rectification/amendment of the assessment order based on the DVO’s report received subsequent to culmination of the assessment proceedings, therefore, the DVO’s report thereafter cannot be given effect under the said statutory provision. Apart from that, it was submitted that the DVO’s report received after completion of the assessment proceedings cannot trigger an action under section 154 of the Act. Accordingly, the Ld. AR based on his aforesaid contention submitted that the AO in the present case had erred in framing the assessment without waiting for the valuation report after having made a reference to the Valuation Officer. 12. We have given thoughtful consideration to the aforesaid contentions of the Learned Authorized Representative. Although we principally concur with the Ld. AR that the provisions of sub-section (15) of section 155 of the Act contemplates that where the AO had computed the capital gains arising from the transfer of a capital asset being land or building or both, by taking the value adopted or assessed by any authority of a State Government for the purpose of payment of stamp duty, as the full value of the consideration received or accruing as a result of transfer in accordance with sub-section (1) of section 50C of the Act, and subsequently such value is revised in any appeal or revision or reference before any authority, Court or the High Court as referred in Clause (b) of sub-section (2) of section 50C of he Act, the AO shall amend the order of the assessment so as to compute the capital gains by taking the full value of the consideration to be the value as so revised in any such appeal or revision or reference, and the provisions of section 154 shall so far as may be applied thereto. We concur with the Ld. AR that section 155(15) of the Act can only be triggered by the AO for revising the full value of the consideration that was adopted based on the value assessed by any authority of a State Government for the purpose of payment of stamp duty, which, thereafter, has been revised in any appeal or revision or a reference by any authority, Court of High Court. Admittedly, the aforesaid amendment does not take within its meaning any such modification in the value based on the report received by the AO from a Valuation Officer. We say so, for the reason that section 50(2)(b) of the Act envisages a situation where the assessee is divested from seeking a reference of the value of the capital asset to a Valuation Officer in a case where he had either filed an appeal or revision or reference before any authority, Court or High Court. In our view, the very fact that the AO cannot make a reference to the Valuation Officer if the assessee has assailed the value adopted by the stamp valuation authority by way of an appeal or revision or reference before any authority, Court or High Court, in itself would be rendered as meaningless and otiose if the reference before any authority is allowed to take within its sweep a reference made by the AO to the valuation cell. We may herein clarify that as the conditions contemplated under section 50(2)(b) of the Act in itself preclude the AO from making a reference to the valuation cell, then it would be incomprehensible that the same could in any manner also include reference to the valuation officer. 13. Be that as it may, we are of the view that though the effect of the DVO’s report received after culmination of the assessment proceedings cannot be given under section 155(15) of the Act, we are unable to persuade ourselves to subscribe to the Ld. AR’s contention that the same cannot be done under section 154 of the Act. In our view, if the valuation determined by the DVO as per the valuation report received after the framing of the assessment is not in parity with the value adopted by the stamp valuation authority for payment of stamp duty, which value was adopted by the AO for computing the capital gains at the time of framing of the assessment, then the same would render the order passed by the AO as suffering from a mistake apparent from record, as a result whereof he would be statutorily bound to take the value determined by the Valuation Officer as the deemed sale consideration. We thus are of the view that the AO, on receipt of the report from the Valuation Officer, remains well within his jurisdiction to rectify his order and bring the same in parity with the value determined by the Valuation Officer, and thus, adopt the said value as the deemed sale consideration for determining the capital gain on transfer of the subject property. 14. Coming to the facts involved in the present case, we are of the view that as the DVO vide his report, dated 04/12/2023, had determined the value of the subject property at Rs.3,20,20,000/- as was adopted by the AO for determining the capital gains based on the stamp duty value, thus, as there is no variance in the value adopted by the AO as the deemed sale consideration for computing the capital gain and that determined by the DVO, there would be no need or occasion for the AO to rectify his order under section 154 of the Act. We have perused the record and are unable to concur with the aforesaid claim raised before us. We say so, for the reason that the AO had made reference to the Valuation Officer on 24/03/2022, and pursuant thereto, the DVO-1, Mumbai had issued notice, dated 28/03/2022 wherein the assessee company was called upon to furnish certain documents and arrange the inspection of the subject property. We further find that the DVO taking cognizance of the fact that the assessee company had failed to respond and provide the requisite details, had therein proceeded and based on the details submitted by the AO, enquiry done for inspection, relevant information gathered by him and after giving due consideration for the factors affecting the value of the subject property such as site, location, situation determined the fair market value (FMV) of the said property, viz., “Office No.1410, 14th Floor, Dalamal Towers, Plot No.211, Nariman Point, Mumbai” as on 24/11/2016 at Rs.3,20,20,000/-. We have given thoughtful consideration and are of the view that though the DVO-1, Mumbai had afforded sufficient opportunity to the assessee company vide his notice, dated 28/03/2022 followed by reminders, dated 26/04/2022, 08/08/2022, 26/08/2022 and 21/11/2022, but the assessee company had neither responded nor furnished the requisite details as were specifically called for by the Valuation Officer. Also, as observed herein above, the DVO vide his preliminary valuation report had sought to determine the fair market value of the subject property at Rs.3,20,20,000/-, and had specifically called upon the assessee company to state his objections on or before 30/11/2023, if any, but the assessee company for the reasons best known to it had had failed to file any objections, as a result whereof the valuation officer had proceeded with and determined the final valuation at the same figure as was proposed by him vide his preliminary valuation. In the backdrop of the aforesaid facts, we find no substance in the Ld. AR’s contention that the DVO had failed to afford the assessee company sufficient opportunity to put forth its case and object to the valuation as was sought to be adopted by him for the property under consideration as on 24/11/2016 at Rs.3,20,20,000/-. 20. We shall now deal with the Ld. AR’s contention that both the authorities below had erred in not allowing the claim regarding the cost of acquisition of the subject property which was purchased vide a registered deed, dated 06/12/2013 by the assessee company from M/s. Sankalp Energy Limited, inter alia, to the extent of its claim of having incurred the stamp duty charges of Rs.9,91,000/-twice, viz., (i) on 14/06/2013: Rs.9,91,000/-; and (ii) on 04/12/2013: Rs.9,91,000/-. Elaborating on his contention, the Ld. AR submitted that the assessee company had initially incurred the stamp duty expenses of Rs.9,91,000/-on 14/06/2013 for executing the registered purchase deed of the subject property, but thereafter, as the sale deed could not be executed, therefore, he was on 04/12/2013 constrained to once again pay the registration charges/stamp duty of Rs.9,91,000/- and the sale deed was executed on 06/12/2013. The Ld. AR had drawn our attention to the assessment order wherein, at Page-6/para-3.8, the aforesaid contention was raised before the AO. The Ld. AR submitted that the AO had grossly erred in law and facts of the case in not addressing the aforesaid issue. 21. The Ld. AR submitted that considering the fact that the assessee company for purchasing/acquiring the subject property had incurred stamp duty charges of Rs.19,82,000/- (supra), the AO ought to have allowed the same instead of restricting the claim to an amount of Rs.9,91,000/-. 22. We have given thoughtful consideration to the aforesaid contention raised by the Ld. AR before us. Admittedly, it is a matter of fact borne from the assessment order that the assessee company had submitted before the AO that it had incurred the stamp duty charges towards acquisition of the subject property aggregating to Rs.19,82,000/-, viz., (i) on 14/06/2013: Rs.9,91,000/-; and (iii) on 04/12/2013: Rs.9,91,000/-. As observed hereinabove, it is the Ld. AR’s claim that though the assessee company had incurred the stamp duty charges of Rs.9,91,000/- on 14/06/2013, the sale deed could not be executed, therefore, the assessee company was compelled to incur the stamp duty expenses of Rs.9,91,000/- on 04/12/2013 for executing the registered sale deed for purchase of the subject property on 06/12/2013. 21. We have given thoughtful consideration and are of the view that as per Section 52B of the Maharashtra Stamp Act, 1958, stamps purchased on or after the relevant commencement date which remained unused for six months from the date of purchase are rendered invalid. However, we find that as it is the claim of the Ld. AR that the assessee company had initially incurred stamp duty charges on 14/06/2013; thus, as the registered deed was executed on 06/12/2013, the aforesaid stamp papers purchased on 14/06/2013, being within the statutory sixmonth period, were apparently valid. 22.Be that as it may, we are of the view that as the AO had failed to address the aforesaid specific claim of the assessee company despite referring to the same in the assessment order, therefore, the matter requires to be set aside to his file for the limited purpose of verifying the veracity of the said claim as was raised by the assessee company before him and adjudicate the same based on a speaking order. Needless to say, the AO shall afford a sufficient opportunity to the assessee company to substantiate its aforesaid claim, and the latter shall produce the necessary documentary evidence in the course of the set aside proceedings to the satisfaction of the AO. 23. Considering the aforesaid facts, we herein set aside the aforesaid matter to the file of the AO for the limited purpose of verifying the claim of the assessee company that it had incurred stamp duty of Rs.19.82 lakhs as against that allowed by the AO at Rs.9,91,000/- incurred on 04/12/2013. 24. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee company is partly allowed for statistical purposes in terms of our aforesaid observations. Order pronounced in the open court on 11th September, 2026.
