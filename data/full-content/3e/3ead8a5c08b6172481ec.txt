Articles 1 min read Standalone primary dealers must hasten forex market making plans: RBI DG Rohit Jain The Reserve Bank of India has asked standalone primary dealers (SPDs) to hasten preparations to offer foreign exchange market-making facilities, noting that the initial uptake of the permission has been slow. The move is aimed at broadening the pool of market makers in the forex market and strengthening support for government securities market activities. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Nov 24, 2026 at 01:42 PM IST var news_msid = 136123943; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has asked standalone primary dealers (SPDs) to hasten preparations to offer foreign exchange market-making facilities, noting that the initial uptake of the permission has been slow.In an address at the Primary Dealers' Association of India (PDAI) Seminar 2026 in Mumbai on November 23, RBI Deputy Governor Rohit Jain said SPDs are expected to speed up the process of setting up the necessary infrastructure, risk management systems and developing the required skill sets to actively participate in the market.“With a view to strengthening the role of Standalone Primary Dealers (SPDs) as market makers, on par with the banks operating PD business, SPDs are permitted to offer all foreign exchange market-making facilities as currently permitted to Category-I Authorized Dealers, subject to prudential guidelines,” Jain said. Advt He said the measure broadens the spectrum of available market-makers in the forex market and improves the ability of SPDs to support primary issuance and secondary market activities in government securities.Role of primary dealers in the G-Sec marketThe RBI introduced the primary dealer system in 1996 with three objectives: strengthening government securities market infrastructure, widening the investor base and deepening secondary market liquidity.Currently, 21 primary dealers are authorised by the RBI, comprising seven standalone primary dealers and 14 bank primary dealers operating departmentally within banks.Primary dealers participate in government securities auctions, undertake underwriting commitments and support secondary market liquidity through market-making activities.They account for around 60 per cent of the overall secondary market turnover in dated Central Government securities, 45 per cent in Treasury Bills and about 50 per cent in State Government securities, according to the RBI.
