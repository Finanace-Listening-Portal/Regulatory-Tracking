Articles 6 min read Edelweiss Life sees bancassurance growth accelerating in two years, targets 75%+ persistency: Sumit Rai The insurer's 13th-month persistency is around 70%; it is targeting 72–73% this year and over 75% next year. It also expects the bancassurance channel to remain an important growth driver despite uncertainty around the impact of IRDAI's proposed distribution reforms. Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Nov 3, 2026 at 07:00 AM IST var news_msid = 135460905; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Edelweiss Life Insurance expects bancassurance to remain a key growth driver over the next two years, with the insurer anticipating a stronger contribution from the channel as the distribution landscape evolves amid proposed regulatory changes. While the company sees limited immediate disruption from IRDAI's proposed commission reset, it expects the impact on distribution models to play out over the next 12–18 months, Sumit Rai, CEO, Edelweiss Life Insurance, told ETBFSI in an interview.The insurer is also looking to improve persistency, with its 13th-month persistency currently at around 70%. Rai attributed some of the pressure to higher surrender values and said the company is targeting 72–73% persistency by the end of the current fiscal year and more than 75% in the following year. He also discussed the insurer's product mix, capital requirements and its path to profitability. Advt Here are the edited excerpts from the interview:Q. Edelweiss Life has targeted profitability in FY27. How close are you to achieving that?Sumit Rai: We will break even this year. We will be profitable this year.Break-even is a function of two things. It is a function of revenue growth, how much you are growing revenue, and it is also a function of better cost management. If you look at our performance over the last four years, our cost expansion has been around 4%, whereas our revenue expansion has been around 9–10%. So, we have been on that journey for a while.This year will be a similar trajectory. We should be closer to around ₹700 crore of premium income for the year, give or take. Some of it is also dependent on when the IRDAI deadline comes into play. Our expenses should be in line with the previous year, a little bit more. So, we will be profitable this year for certain.Q. Will you need to raise capital to support this growth?Sumit Rai: We will raise some capital this year. But that is to support growth, not for reserving. We are very comfortable on reserving and do not necessarily need capital for that. We do need some degree of growth capital, so we will raise a small amount. It is not very significant.For the last two years, we have raised around ₹200 crore each year, in the ballpark of ₹175–200 crore. This year, it should be a little lower. It should probably be around ₹150 crore.Q. How do you see IRDAI’s proposed commission changes affecting your distribution channels? Advt Sumit Rai: I honestly do not see too much of an impact on our distribution channel. There are certain distribution channels, such as brokers, which may get a little more impacted than others. But we are not very heavily into the broking channel as such.There might be a period of readjustment. It could take between 12 to 18 months for things to completely settle down. But post that, even from our perspective, I think it is a very healthy move. We are fairly balanced in our distribution between banks, third parties and our own proprietary channels.I do not think anybody is going to be set back in any way. I think it will act as a prod for growing the business further rather than be a setback. For some of the proprietary channels, it will be a big boost. This could also help increase penetration in Tier-3 and Tier-4 towns over a period of time. Advt Q. How do you expect bancassurance to impact from the proposed commission reset?Sumit Rai: I do not think bancassurance will be hindered. My belief is that it will continue to be a significant channel and will continue to grow. If you ask me about the medium to long term, say two years from today, I expect it to be significantly better.There is an immediate short term where there is not as much visibility. Things will take time to settle down and reset. But two years out from here, I think growth will be faster.Q. Persistency has been a sticky issue in the industry. Your FY26 13th-month persistency stood at 70%. Why are nearly three in ten policies not persisting to the 13th month, and what is your improvement target? Advt Sumit Rai: Persistency is a function of many things. Of late, I think we have seen a persistency adjustment, partly because of surrender value. Since surrender values are slightly more attractive than they were, customers may have a better ability to choose not to continue.Across the industry, we have seen some degree of drop in persistency in the last year. A large part of it has to do with things like surrender values. But I would not worry about it too much, frankly, because I think in a couple of years we will be back and maybe ahead of where we are today.It takes a little time to learn and reset and decide which is the right way to go about it. I am not seriously concerned because I believe the trajectory is right and we will be on the right upward trajectory.This year, we should hopefully end between 72% and 73%, and then go to north of 75% the year after.Q. Edelweiss Life was found to have exceeded its expense-of-management limits for FY25. What went wrong, and how are you bringing expenses within the prescribed limits?Sumit Rai: Nothing went wrong. We are already within the limit as of FY25–26. We were at around 98.6%. So, nothing went wrong.Ours was as per the glide path. The penalty is for FY24–25. In FY24–25, we were marginally off. We had given IRDAI a glide path under which we were supposed to meet the limit last year, and we have met it. So, we are all okay. There is nothing to report there.Q. With new regulations and compliance requirements coming in, how much pressure will these put on insurers’ cost structures?Sumit Rai: If you implement IFRS, it is going to cost you extra. When you implement DPDP, it is going to cost you extra. There are multiple things happening right now that are going to cost extra and will have an impact on our cost structures.There are lots of one-time costs that are going on right now, which will wear out over the next two to three years. That has to be accounted for. There might be something else that becomes a one-time cost two years from now, so we will see that.Cybersecurity, DPDP, IFRS and risk-based capital will involve costs. Some of these costs will be incurred. They are part of the natural cost of doing business. There is no getting away from it. Hopefully, we will get some credit for it in EoM.Q. When will Edelweiss Life be ready for Ind AS implementation, and how will the transition affect costs?Sumit Rai: Ind AS has to be implemented on April 1, 2027. I do not have a choice. I have to do it on April 1, 2027. We are well on the path to that.The starting year was 2026, with the ability to ask for a deferment of one year. Most of the industry has gone for a deferment of one year. As things stand, our implementation date will be April 1, 2027, and we will be ready for it.Q. With executive remuneration increasingly linked to customer-centric key performance indicators, which metrics are becoming most important for the company?Sumit Rai: I think it is a fairly spread-out bucket between grievances and persistency, because persistency is ultimately the longest-term measure. Customers voting with their feet is the biggest evidence of whether they are happy or not, and also, claim settlement.
