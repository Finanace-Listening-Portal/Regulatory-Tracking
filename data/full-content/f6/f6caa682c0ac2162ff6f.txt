Articles 2 min read SEBI to look into broker, AMC concerns over UPI MDR: Tuhin Kanta Pandey SEBI chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said the regulator will look into concerns raised by brokers and AMCs over UPI MDR and assess how they can be eased, following the government’s recent framework introducing charges on specified merchant transactions. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Sep 17, 2026 at 01:06 PM IST var news_msid = 134305069; console.log(news_msid); Select ETBFSI as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Markets regulator Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said the regulator will look into concerns raised by brokers and asset management companies over the new UPI merchant discount rate (MDR) framework and see how these concerns can be eased.“There are some important issues there. We will certainly look into it and see how we can ease them,” Pandey said when asked what SEBI could do to ease concerns around MDR faced by brokers and AMCs.The comments come after the government introduced a new framework for UPI MDR, following an amendment to the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. Advt The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026, introduced in Parliament on August 4, sought to amend Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, which had prohibited banks and system providers from imposing charges on certain electronic modes of payment. The Bill was passed by the Lok Sabha on August 6 and the Rajya Sabha on August 10.The government had on August 8 clarified that the amendment was an enabling provision to ensure the long-term sustainability of UPI and did not by itself impose MDR. It said any MDR, if introduced, would be decided by the UPI and Services Steering Committee headed by NPCI.The framework was subsequently announced on September 15, with MDR of 0.4 per cent on specified person-to-merchant (P2M) transactions above Rs 2,000. The charge will be capped at Rs 300 for transactions of Rs 75,000 and above.For capital-market transactions, including payments relating to mutual funds, securities, stockbrokers and dealers, the MDR has been set at 0.02 per cent, capped at Rs 300 per transaction. Transactions above Rs 2,000 in specified essential and thin-margin sectors, including railways, telecommunications, insurance, fuel and agricultural inputs, will attract a flat Rs 5 MDR. Advt The MDR is a charge within the payments ecosystem and is distributed among ecosystem participants, including banks, payment service providers and UPI application providers. The government has said customers will not be charged MDR and merchants should not pass the cost on to customers.Pandey did not specify what regulatory changes SEBI could consider.
