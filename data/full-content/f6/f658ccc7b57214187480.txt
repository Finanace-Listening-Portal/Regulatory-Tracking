Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Naresh Jhanwar Vs DCIT (ITAT Jaipur) Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Jaipur Bench, considered two appeals filed by Shri Naresh Jhanwar against the common order of the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals)-4, Jaipur, dated 28 February 2022, for Assessment Years 2016-17 and 2017-18. The assessments were completed under Section 143(3) read with Section 153A of the Income Tax Act, 1961, following a search and seizure operation conducted on 20 December 2016 in the case of M.B. & Sons Group, Sikar. The principal controversy concerned additions under Section 68 of the Income Tax Act representing the entire amount of money transfer transactions recorded in diaries and loose papers seized from the assessee’s premises. The assessee was engaged in the business of transferring money on a commission basis through his concern, M/s V.N. Impex. During the search proceedings, the assessee explained in his statement recorded under Section 132(4) that he arranged cash transfers between different persons and locations and received commission ranging from ₹100 to ₹200 per ₹1 lakh transferred. He did not maintain regular books of account for this business but recorded transaction details in handwritten diaries, notepads and loose papers. The assessee consistently maintained that the funds belonged to his customers and that his income was restricted to the commission earned for arranging such transfers. During the search, cash amounting to ₹14,00,120 was found at the assessee’s office, apart from cash and jewellery found at his residential premises and jewellery in a bank locker. The assessee disclosed undisclosed commission income of ₹48 lakh for Financial Years 2010-11 to 2016-17. Subsequently, he filed a declaration on 3 April 2017 under the Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Yojana, 2016 (PMGKY), for which a certificate under Section 199C of the Finance Act, 2016, was issued on 25 April 2017. The disclosure included ₹7 lakh for AY 2016-17 and ₹14 lakh for AY 2017-18. During the assessment proceedings, the assessee furnished explanations regarding the nature of transactions recorded in the seized documents, along with available names and contact numbers of various parties. The date-wise reconciliation for the period from 17 October 2015 to 19 December 2016 showed credit transactions of approximately ₹127.22 crore, debit transactions of approximately ₹127.31 crore and commission income of ₹11,92,300. The assessee submitted that the near correspondence between the debit and credit entries demonstrated that he was merely facilitating money transfers and that taxation of the entire credit entries by ignoring corresponding debit entries would not reflect his actual income. The Assessing Officer accepted that the assessee was engaged in cash transfer activities but concluded that he had failed to establish the complete identities of the persons involved in the transactions. Accordingly, the AO invoked Section 68 and made an addition of ₹4,52,59,300 for AY 2016-17 and approximately ₹122.69 crore for AY 2017-18, applying the special taxation provisions of Section 115BBE. The CIT(A) substantially upheld these additions on the ground that the assessee had failed to establish the identity, creditworthiness and genuineness of the parties. However, limited telescoping relief of ₹7 lakh was granted for AY 2017-18 against the income disclosed under PMGKY. Before the Tribunal, the assessee contended that Section 68 could be invoked only where an unexplained sum was found credited in the books of account maintained by an assessee. Relying upon the definition of books of account under Section 2(12A), he argued that rough diaries, loose papers and memorandum records could not automatically be treated as regular books of account. The assessee also relied on the statutory presumption under Section 292C concerning seized documents, arguing that the Revenue could not selectively rely upon the receipt entries while disregarding corresponding payment entries and commission details contained in the same documents. The Tribunal examined the seized materials, statements recorded during the search and explanations furnished during the assessment proceedings. It observed that the assessee had consistently explained the nature of his money transfer business and that the Revenue itself accepted the existence of such business. The available documentary evidence supported the assessee’s contention that he was acting as an intermediary. The Tribunal noted that despite the availability of names, mobile numbers and WhatsApp communications relating to certain transactions, the Department had not effectively examined the concerned parties or brought contrary evidence establishing that the entire money belonged to the assessee. The Tribunal emphasised the importance of corroborative evidence in search assessments. The Tribunal further observed that the Assessing Officer was required to determine the correct taxable income of the assessee rather than adjudicate the legality of his business activities under FEMA or the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002. Whether an activity was legal or illegal did not alter the principle that income actually earned by the assessee was liable to taxation. The Tribunal held that the absence of complete particulars of every customer could not, on the facts of the case, establish that the assessee owned all the funds transferred through him. Consequently, only commission income earned by an intermediary, rather than the entire value of transactions facilitated, could be assessed in his hands. Relying upon judicial precedents concerning the meaning of books of account and the evidentiary value of seized documents, the Tribunal held that the diaries maintained by the assessee were merely memorandum records of money transfer transactions. Since the assessee did not maintain regular books of account for this business and the seized diaries did not constitute such books, the Tribunal concluded that the addition under Section 68 based on loose papers and diary entries was unsustainable. It also held that the Revenue could not disregard the surrounding circumstances, corresponding debit entries, assets actually found during the search and explanations regarding the ownership of the funds. Accordingly, the ITAT vacated the addition of ₹4,52,59,300 for AY 2016-17 and directed the Assessing Officer to recompute the actual commission income earned on money transfer transactions, considering the commission rate of ₹100 to ₹200 per ₹1 lakh transferred and the income already disclosed by the assessee. Only the correct commission income not covered by the earlier disclosure was directed to be taxed, after providing sufficient opportunity of hearing. The Tribunal extended the same findings and directions to AY 2017-18, mutatis mutandis. Consequently, both appeals filed by the assessee were partly allowed.
