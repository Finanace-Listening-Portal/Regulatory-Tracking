Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Hemant Goswami Vs ITO (ITAT Agra) Fresh Leave Encashment Claim Allowed in Appeal: ITAT Grants SBI Retiree Additional Rs. 7.56 Lakh Exemption Summary: The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Agra Bench, in Hemant Goswami v. ITO, ITA No. 426/Agr/2026, allowed an SBI retiree’s claim for an additional Rs. 7,56,590 exemption under Section 10(10AA)(ii) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The assessee had claimed only Rs. 3 lakh in his return and sought exemption for the balance during appellate proceedings. The order, pronounced on 22 October 2026, concerned Assessment Year 2022–23. The Bench comprising Shri Sunil Kumar Singh, Judicial Member, and Shri Brajesh Kumar Singh, Accountant Member, held that appellate authorities were not barred from admitting a fresh claim and directed verification of a revised computation for consequential relief. Background of the Leave Encashment Receipt The assessee retired from the State Bank of India and received leave encashment of Rs. 10,56,590, besides other retirement benefits. In his return for Assessment Year 2022–23, he claimed exemption of Rs. 3,00,000 under Section 10(10AA)(ii). The remaining Rs. 7,56,590 was included in taxable income. The return was processed by the Centralised Processing Centre under Section 143(1) through an intimation dated 27 July 2022. CPC accepted the income declared in the return without making an adjustment concerning the disputed leave encashment. The assessee subsequently sought exemption for the balance amount, relying on Tribunal decisions that had granted relief concerning leave encashment beyond Rs. 3 lakh. Why the First Appellate Authority Rejected Relief The assessee approached the Additional/Joint Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), Kochi, seeking admission of the fresh claim and refund of excess tax. The appellate authority noted that exemptions aggregating Rs. 23 lakh under Section 10, as claimed in the return, had been allowed in full. CPC had processed total income at Rs. 15,93,800, matching the income admitted by the assessee. Accordingly, the authority found no mistake in the processing intimation. It observed that an omission could have been addressed through a revised return or by seeking condonation from the appropriate authority. The appeal was dismissed by an order dated 12 May 2026, prompting the assessee’s further appeal. Assessee’s Argument on Fresh Appellate Claims Before the Tribunal, the assessee contended that the first appellate authority could entertain an additional exemption claim even though it had not been included in the return. He relied on Goetze (India) Ltd. v. CIT, CIT v. Pruthvi Brokers & Shareholders Pvt. Ltd., and Tribunal decisions concerning similar claims. He also referred to the Agra Tribunal’s decision in Vijay Kumar Jain v. ITO, where an additional leave encashment claim had been accepted. The relief sought was specifically an additional Rs. 7,56,590, representing the difference between total leave encashment received and the exemption already allowed. The Departmental Representative supported the first appellate authority’s order. Earlier Leave Encashment Decisions Supported Entitlement The Tribunal observed that the claim for further exemption was supported by decisions of coordinate Benches. It reproduced the Ahmedabad Tribunal’s reasoning in Goverdhan Bhambhani, ITA No. 289/Ahd/2025, concerning Assessment Year 2020–21. That decision followed Govind Chhatwani v. CIT(Appeals) and referred to CBDT Notification No. 31/2023 dated 24 May 2023, which enhanced the specified leave encashment exemption ceiling to Rs. 25 lakh. The cited rulings had allowed relief in earlier assessment-year disputes by applying the enhanced ceiling. The present Bench relied on that judicial reasoning in recognising the assessee’s entitlement. Its conclusion was therefore based on the precedents discussed, rather than a separate examination of the notification’s temporal operation. Distinction Between an Existing Claim and a Fresh Claim The Tribunal recognised an important factual distinction. In Goverdhan Bhambhani, the entire leave encashment exemption had been claimed in the return, but CPC restricted the relief. Here, the assessee himself had claimed only Rs. 3 lakh and first sought the additional amount before the CIT(A). The Tribunal nevertheless agreed that appellate authorities were not barred from admitting fresh claims during appellate proceedings. For this procedural principle, it considered decisions involving BSNL employees who initially claimed limited retirement compensation exemption and later sought greater relief before appellate authorities. BSNL Decisions Applied for the Procedural Principle The Tribunal referred to Prathibha Jagdish Unawane v. ITO and connected appeals decided by the Pune Tribunal on 29 April 2026. Those proceedings concerned fresh claims under Section 10(10B) for BSNL retirement compensation. Their relevance here lay in accepting additional claims during appellate proceedings and permitting revised computations for verification. The SBI retiree’s substantive exemption continued to arise under Section 10(10AA)(ii). The Tribunal followed the coordinate Bench reasoning to grant the additional leave encashment exemption. Final Decision and Refund Directions The Tribunal held that the assessee was entitled to Rs. 7,56,590 additional exemption, over and above Rs. 3 lakh already allowed. It directed him to submit a revised computation of income before the jurisdictional Assessing Officer. The Revenue must verify that computation, recompute the tax liability and grant any refund lawfully due. The CIT(A)’s adverse finding was set aside, and the assessee’s appeal was allowed. Thereafter, the said employees made a claim for exemption of the entire above received u/s 10(10B) of the Act for the first time before the ld. CIT (A) who rejected such claims. To us, the takeaway from this judgment seems to be that it is not the form but the substance that matters so far definition of retrenchment compensation is concerned. Right now we are dealing with an employee who is giving up his source of livelihood under the threat of dislocation, and the hyper technical interpretations based on technicalities about the wordings in the settlement deed, signed by him under these compelling circumstances, is being taken as the understanding about assessee’s actual conduct; that is too pedantic an approach and it cannot meet our approval. Let us also not forget that while taking calls on these issues, which deal with employees in the lower rung of hierarchy, we must not be too pedantic or hyper technical in approach. We have to be pragmatic in approach and we must give full effect to the true intent of the public welfare provisions. To us, the arrangement in question is nothing but a termination of employment with the offer of compensation. Viewed thus, the payment in question cannot be anything but retrenchment compensation. 10. Accordingly, we direct the assessee to place revised computation of income before the jurisdictional Assessing Officer claiming further exemption of Rs. 7,56,590/- u/s 10(10AA)(ii) of the Act and thereafter the Revenue authorities shall compute the tax liability and grant the refund (if any) entitled to the assessee after due verification of such revised computation of income. The impugned finding of the ld. CIT(A) is set aside and the issues raised in the grounds of the appeal by the assessee are allowed. Accordingly, the appeal stands allowed. 7. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is allowed in the terms indicated above. Order pronounced in the open court on 22/10/2026.
