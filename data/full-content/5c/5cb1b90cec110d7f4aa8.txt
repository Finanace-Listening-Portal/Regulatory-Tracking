Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Bharat Explosives Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Agra) Section 68 Addition Does Not Automatically Justify Penalty Where the Explanation Is Bona Fide The controversy The Agra Tribunal deleted a penalty of ₹16,50,000 under section 271(1)(c) arising from additions concerning public fixed deposits under section 68. Although the company’s evidence had not been accepted as sufficient to explain the credits in the quantum proceedings, the Tribunal found that its explanation could not be regarded as wholly unacceptable or lacking bona fides. The company had furnished depositors’ names, addresses and supporting details, and claimed that some entries represented renewal of earlier deposits. The Tribunal distinguished an addition under a deeming provision from the separate question of whether the facts warranted a penalty. Public deposits and the section 68 addition The assessee was a public limited company that filed its return declaring nil income and reported a loss of approximately ₹7.96 crore in its profit and loss account. Its books reflected fixed deposits aggregating to ₹2,64,34,000. During assessment, confirmations were sought from the depositors. Certain deposits remained unconfirmed, and the Assessing Officer added ₹56,35,000 under section 68, citing deficiencies in proof of the creditors’ creditworthiness and the genuineness of the transactions. The disputed list contained 234 fixed-deposit entries, with individual amounts ranging from ₹3,000 to ₹50,000. Some persons had made deposits on more than one occasion. The first appellate authority granted relief of ₹2,53,000. In the quantum appeal, the Tribunal confirmed ₹23,98,000 and restored ₹29,84,000 to the Assessing Officer to verify the claim that these were renewals of old deposits involving no fresh receipt during the relevant year. The Assessing Officer subsequently added the restored amount again, holding that the renewal claim had not been established despite opportunities. Penalty followed the sustained additions The Assessing Officer levied ₹16,50,000 as penalty on the surviving deposits aggregating to ₹53,82,000. The penalty dispute had an extended appellate history. In an earlier round, the Tribunal directed consideration of the assessee’s jurisdictional objection concerning absence of the requisite satisfaction for initiating penalty. In the order now challenged, the CIT(A) rejected that objection and maintained the penalty. The CIT(A) also declined to reconsider other grounds, stating that they had already been decided in the earlier appellate order. Before the Tribunal, the assessee challenged both the jurisdictional basis and the substantive justification for penalty. What the company had disclosed The company explained that the deposits were accepted from the public at large under a fixed-deposit scheme governed by the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 1975. It had furnished particulars including depositors’ names and addresses, FDR numbers, deposit amounts, maturity dates and maturity amounts, together with application forms and other supporting records. The assessee submitted that the depositors were not personally known to it. It also relied on the receipt and repayment of deposits through account-payee cheques, with interest payments made through the same banking mode. Its case was that complete facts and evidence had been placed on record, while the dispute concerned whether that evidence met the degree of proof required by the Assessing Officer. The Tribunal examined these circumstances separately from the outcome of the quantum proceedings. Inadequate proof did not establish concealed income The Tribunal acknowledged that the explanations had not been accepted as sufficient under section 68 and that the additions had survived the relevant proceedings. However, it noted that acceptance of the deposits from the public under the company’s deposit scheme was not disputed by the Assessing Officer. More importantly, there was no other information from an external source or enquiry by the Assessing Officer showing that the company had introduced its own undisclosed income through these deposits. The Tribunal also examined particulars supplied concerning fresh deposits and renewals. It noticed sample entries recording renewal dates in FY 2004-05, preceding the year under appeal. These particulars supported its conclusion that the explanation concerning earlier deposits could not simply be dismissed as entirely unacceptable or non-bona fide. Reliance Petroproducts applied The Tribunal relied on CIT v. Reliance Petroproducts (P.) Ltd., 322 ITR 158 (SC) in holding that the penalty was unsustainable on the facts before it. It directed deletion of the entire ₹16,50,000 penalty and allowed the assessee’s appeal. Since relief had been granted on the substantive penalty ground, the other grounds—including the objections concerning satisfaction and the penalty notices—became academic and were left open. Thus, the Tribunal did not cancel the penalty on the defective-notice argument. Author’s comments Failure to establish a credit conclusively in assessment does not make penalty an automatic consequence. This decision turns on the company’s disclosures, supporting particulars and the Tribunal’s finding that its explanation retained bona fides. The absence of confirmations was relevant to the section 68 addition. For penalty, however, the Tribunal also considered whether the available evidence justified treating the explanation as false or lacking good faith. The ruling should not be presented as a blanket exemption from penalty whenever an addition is made under section 68. Nor did the use of banking channels independently resolve the dispute. The cumulative facts and the acceptability of the explanation were decisive. Its practical value lies in preparing a separate penalty defence rather than merely repeating the quantum arguments. The disclosures made, supporting records and circumstances explaining evidentiary gaps must be identified. The addition survived, but the penalty did not—because an unsuccessful explanation was not, on these facts, a dishonest one. Cases Discussed CIT v. Reliance Petroproducts (P.) It is evident from record that the AO has initiated penalty proceedings u/s 271(1)(c) vide the assessment order dated 24.12.2008. It is evident from the assessment order that the penalty has been initiated for the addition of Rs 56,35,000/- made on account of unexplained cash credits u/s 68 of the Act. Section 271 nowhere mandates that the recording of satisfaction is a must in specific terms and words. In absence of such specific provision if penalty proceeding is initiated by the AO without specifically recording his satisfaction, it cannot be said to be invalid or illegal, or improper exercise of jurisdiction. It is established law that satisfaction of the Assessing Officer must reflect from the order either with the expressed words recorded by the AO himself or by his overt act and action. In the appellant’s case, there is no confusion whatsoever why the penalty proceedings u/s 271(1)(c) have been initiated. Therefore, I am of the opinion that the appellant’s contention that the penalty notice is invalid is not correct. The argument that the penalty notice is invalid was never made before the AO. There is plethora of judicial decisions wherein it is held that the penalty notice cannot be held as invalid for the reason that the grounds on which penalty is proposed to be levied was not specified in the notice. In the case of Sundaram Finance Ltd vs ACIT (2018) 403 ITR 407 / 170 DTR 8/304 CTR 846/93, the Hon’ble Madras High Court held that when the taxpayer has understood the import and purport of the penalty notice and has participated in the proceedings, then the levy of penalty is valid. The Hon’ble Court also held that where assessee not been put to any prejudice due to the defect in the notice then not showing the nature of default in the penalty notice is protected u/s 2928 of the Act. Similarly, the Hon’ble Madras High Court in Amtex Software Solutions Pvt Ltd v. ACIT (2019) 418 ITR 99 (Mad) (HC) has held that the penalty notice, not specifying the charge could be rectified and was not invalid. The writ to quash the notice was held to be not maintainable. The Hon’ble Karnataka High Court in CIT vs Sri Durga Enterprises (2014) 44 taxmann.com 442 / [2015] 231 Taxman 886 (Kar) effectively reviewed its earlier decision and held that where taxpayer responded to the penalty notice issued by the AO in letter and spirit and participated in the proceedings then in light of the provisions of Section 292B, notice issued under section 148 was held to be valid. It was held that where a notice issued or purported to have been issued in pursuance of any provisions of this Act, shall not be deemed to be invalid mainly by reason of any mistake, defect or omission in such notice, if such notice is in substance and effect in conformity with or according to the intent and purpose of this Act. The Hon’ble Delhi High Court in the case of CIT v. M/s Sudev Industries Limited [2018] 94 taxmann.com 373/256 Taxman 317 (Delhi) held that any notice, order, proceedings, etc, will not be invalid on account of any mistake, defect or omission if in substance and effect it is in conformity with and in accordance with the intent and purpose of the Act. The Hon’ble Delhi High Court has laid down that a Defective notice/summon, if it serves the intent and purpose of the Act, ie. to inform the assessee and when there is no confusion in his mind about initiation of proceedings under Section 147/148 of the Act, the defective notice is protected under Section 292B. The Hon’ble Bombay HC in CIT v. Smt. Kaushalya & Others [1994] 75 Taxman 549/ [1995] 216 ITR 660 (Bom), held that the issuance of notice is an administrative device for informing the assessee about the proposal to levy penalty in order to enable him to explain as to why it should not be done and mere mistake in the language used or mere non-striking of the inapplicable portion cannot by itself invalidate the notice. In CIT v. Mithila Motors [1984] 16 Taxman 224/149 ITR 751 (Pat), relying on the decision in Kantamani Venkata Narayana & Sons v. Addl. ITO [1967] 63 ITR 638 (SC), wherein it was clarified that a mistake in the notice does not invalidate the penalty proceedings, it was held that even granting that the notice u/s. 274 was defective or bad in law, the penalty proceedings would not fail as no prejudice had been caused to the assessee and that all that is required is that the assessee should be given an opportunity of show cause, and no statutory notice has been prescribed in this behalf. Hence, it is sufficient if the assessee was aware of the charges he had to meet and was given an opportunity of being heard. A mistake in the notice would not invalidate penalty proceedings. The Hon’ble Bombay High Court in the case of M/s Maharaj Garage & Company, v. CIT [2017] 85 taxmann.com 86/ [2018] 400 ITR 292 (Bombay) held that- “ The requirement of Section 274 of the Income Tax Act for granting reasonable opportunity of being heard in the matter cannot be stretched to the extent of framing a specific charge or asking the assessee an explanation in respect of the quantum of penalty proposed to be imposed, as has been urged. The assessee was supplied with the findings recorded in the order of re-assessment, which was passed on the same date on which the notice under Section 271(1)(c) was issued, initiating the proceedings of imposing the penalty. The assessee had sufficient notice of the action of imposing penalty. It was thus concluded, that there was no jurisdictional error or unjust exercise of power by the authority.” Therefore, after considering the facts of the case and the ratio of decisions on the issue as discussed above, I am of the opinion that the objections raised by the appellant are baseless. Therefore, the objections raised by the appellant in ground no. 04 in the appeal no. CIT(Appeal) 2, Agra/10591/2018-19 are frivolous and baseless and are therefore rejected. Thus, the said ground no. 4 is hereby dismissed. 6. Other grounds of appeal no. 1, 2, 3 & 5 were already adjudicated by the earlier CIT(A), Agra vide order dated 28.10.2016. All these grounds were dismissed by the Hon’ble CIT(A), Agra. Therefore, fresh adjudication of these grounds is not required. 7. In the Result, the Appeal is dismissed.” 3. However, as discussed above, the same was not considered adequate and the addition of Rs. 23,98,000/- was confirmed by this Tribunal vide order dated 09.07.2013 in ITA no. 385/Agr/2012 and further the balance addition of Rs. 29,84,000/- was restored by this Tribunal to verify the claim of the assessee that the balance deposit of Rs. 29,82,000/- were renewal of old deposits. However, the said sum of Rs. 29,82,000/- was again added by the AO vide order dated 05.02.2015 u/s 254/143(3) of the Act, where in the AO noted that the assessee despite several opportunities given, the assessee failed to establish that the balance deposit of Rs. 29,82,000/- were renewal of old deposits as claimed by the assessee. However, the issue in this appeal is as to whether on the submission of the said details which was not found to be satisfactory by the AO and addition was made as discussed above then whether the assessee would be liable to levy of penalty u/s 271(1)(c) of the Act or not on the said addition. On this we take note of the fact that the deposits of Rs 53,82,000/- on which the AO has levied penalty was accepted by the assessee from public at large under the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 1975 which has not been disputed by the AO. However, in absence of confirmation from the said depositors the amount of fixed deposits were added and confirmed as discussed above u/s 68 of the Act where the assessee failed to explain the credit entries by way of the receipts on account of FDR received by the assessee as per the provisions of section 68 of the Act, which is a deeming provision. However, there is no other information on record either on account of any external source or by any enquiry done by the AO which shows that the assessee had introduced its undisclosed income by way of the fixed deposits of Rs. 53,82,000/-. Further, we also note that the assessee vide letter dated 08.08.2016 before the ld. CIT (A) to Agra had filed the details wherein, a bifurcation of deposits received during the year and those received in year prior to year under consideration but renewed during the year, which was submitted by the assessee before the AO on 22.11.2013 and according to the assessee that as directed by the Tribunal, the AO was required to examine the said renewal and to allow the requisite relief which was not carried by the AO. The said letter and the relevant details are placed on page no. 68 to 78 of the paper book. In this regard, the relevant extract of the said letter (placed on page no. 68 of the paper book) and page no. 1 (placed on page no. 69 of the paper book) of the said details on a sample basis is reproduced below which shows that in the FDRs at Serial. No. 4,7 and 17 the date of renewal is indicated 04.11.2004 which pertains to FY. 2004-05 relevant to AY. 2005-06 whereas the present appeal is for AY. 2006-07. — 6.4 Therefore, in the given facts of the case, the submission of the assessee that it furnished the complete name and address of the depositors and other details in this regard to substantiate its claim and that some of the deposits were renewal of old deposits not relating to AY. 2006­07 cannot be said to be totally unacceptable and not bona fide. 6.5 Therefore, considering the above facts the levy of penalty u/s 271(1)(c) of the Act levied by the AO is not tenable in this case. In this regard, reliance is placed on the decision of the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of CIT Vs Reliance Petroproducts (P) Ltd: 322 ITR 158 (SC) wherein on similar facts the Hon’ble Court held that penalty u/s 271 (1)( c) of the Act was not leviable. Accordingly, the levy of penalty of Rs. 16,50,000/- by the AO u/s 271 (1)(c) of the Act is deleted. Ground no. 1 of the appeal is allowed. In view of ground no. 1 of the appeal allowed, the other grounds of appeal become academic and are left open in this case. 7. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed.
