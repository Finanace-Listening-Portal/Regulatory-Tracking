Summary : A non-resident taxable person is a person who occasionally undertakes taxable transactions in India but has no fixed place of business or residence in India. The CGST framework gives this class a special, time-limited registration route. Registration is compulsory when the statutory definition and supply facts are met; the normal turnover threshold is not the controlling test. The applicant uses FORM GST REG-09, acts through an authorised signatory resident in India, applies at least five days before beginning business, and deposits an amount equal to the estimated tax liability for the requested period. The certificate ordinarily lasts for the shorter of the requested period and ninety days from its effective date. One extension of up to a further ninety days may be sought in FORM GST REG-11 before expiry, with an additional advance-tax deposit. For tax periods from October 2022 onward, the GST portal states that GSTR-5 is due by the thirteenth day after the tax period, or within seven days after the last date of registration validity, whichever is earlier. This guide converts those rules into a dated compliance file and distinguishes an NRTP from an overseas OIDAR supplier, an Indian fixed establishment and a casual taxable person. Key Takeaways Show all 19 sections Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Who Fits the Non-Resident Taxable-Person Category The statutory definition focuses on three connected facts: the person occasionally undertakes transactions involving supply of goods, services or both; the transactions occur in the course or furtherance of business; and the person has no fixed place of business or residence in India. Legal form is not decisive. An individual, company or other foreign entity may fall within the category if the operational facts satisfy the definition. A foreign person with an Indian fixed establishment should not automatically use the NRTP route because the existence and role of that establishment can change the registration analysis. The category is also different from a casual taxable person. A casual taxable person has no fixed place of business in the State or Union territory where the occasional supply is made, whereas an NRTP has no fixed place of business or residence anywhere in India. It is also different from the special registration and return system for a person outside India supplying online information and database access or retrieval services to specified Indian recipients. Classifying the activity correctly comes before form selection. Where the applicant has no PAN, the passport and the tax-identification or unique identification number issued by the home jurisdiction become central. The form also calls for an authorised signatory who is resident in India. Corporate applicants should align the authorisation, board or equivalent approval, identity documents and digital-signature arrangements early; the five-day rule is not a safe project-management lead time for a first-time foreign applicant. State-wise supply planning GST registration is State or Union-territory specific. Before filing, the applicant should map the place of supply, location from which the activity is conducted, event or project sites, warehousing, import arrangements and customer invoicing. A single REG-09 application should not be assumed to cover unrelated activity in multiple States. The factual map should explain where taxable supplies are made and which registration will report each invoice. Advance Tax Is a Registration Gate, Not a Final Assessment Section 27 requires an advance deposit equal to the estimated tax liability for the registration period. The portal generates a temporary reference number for payment, and the amount is credited to the electronic cash ledger after registration. The estimate should be supported by forecast supplies, applicable GST rates, inter-State or intra-State character, reverse-charge items, available credits and the requested duration. A deliberately low estimate can create an immediate cash shortfall and compliance risk when actual liability arises. The advance deposit does not replace invoice-level tax determination, return filing or payment of the actual liability. The finance file should reconcile the estimate to actual outward supplies and explain material variances. Any balance remaining in the cash ledger is not simply remitted on request; the return cycle and refund conditions must be completed. Contract managers should therefore distinguish the cash deposit from an expense and keep the ledger balance visible until closure. Validity Ends on the Earlier Statutory Date The registration certificate is valid for the period specified in the application or ninety days from the effective date, whichever is earlier. Taxable supplies may be made only after the certificate is issued. The compliance calendar should therefore show at least four dates: proposed commencement, certificate effective date, requested end date and the statutory ninety-day cap. Invoicing or supplying after expiry is not cured merely because an extension application was contemplated. REG-11 extension before expiry The proper officer may extend the initial period once, on sufficient cause, by up to a further ninety days. Rule 15 requires FORM GST REG-11 before the existing validity ends. The extension request is acknowledged only after the additional amount equivalent to estimated tax liability for the extended period has been paid. The file should contain the reason, revised turnover and liability estimate, payment reference, application acknowledgement and the approved extended validity. If operations are becoming continuous, management should reassess whether the temporary NRTP model remains factually and legally appropriate. The alternative limb remains important for a short registration: filing is required within seven days after the last date of validity, whichever date arrives earlier. Older summaries showing the twentieth day should not be used for a 2026 tax period. The responsible team should calculate both limbs for every period, record the earlier date and verify the portal status rather than rely on a generic monthly calendar. GSTR-5 contains outward supplies, inward supplies, tax, interest and other amounts. The return file should reconcile invoice series, amendments, debit and credit notes, import documentation, recipient GSTINs, place-of-supply treatment and electronic ledgers. The final short period up to expiry is easy to miss because it may not align with a full calendar month. A nil result should be confirmed from records rather than assumed from inactivity. Input Tax Credit Is Deliberately Narrow The special restriction in section 17(5) denies an NRTP input tax credit except on goods imported by that person. This is substantially narrower than the ordinary registered-person framework. Domestic hotel, venue, professional, logistics and other invoices should not be treated as creditable merely because they bear the NRTP GSTIN. Import bills, bills of entry, ownership of goods and linkage to the registered activity require separate verification. The advance cash deposit should not be reduced in a forecast by assumed domestic-service credits that the law blocks. A Defensible NRTP Closing File At the end of the Indian activity, management should freeze the supply register, confirm that no transaction falls after expiry, complete the final GSTR-5, pay all tax and statutory amounts, and reconcile the electronic cash ledger. The document set should preserve registration and extension approvals, authorised-signatory records, source invoices, import documents, contracts, tax computations, returns and acknowledgements. If cancellation, refund or another portal action is necessary, it should be supported by the completed return record and the current portal procedure. Frequently Asked Questions Does the normal GST turnover threshold protect an NRTP from registration? No. The compulsory-registration framework applies to a person who is properly classified as a non-resident taxable person, subject to the Act and any specific exemption that genuinely covers the supply. Which form is used for initial NRTP registration? FORM GST REG-09 is used. It should be filed electronically at least five days before commencement of business. How long can the first certificate remain valid? It lasts for the period requested or ninety days from the effective date, whichever is earlier. Can the registration be extended? Yes. On sufficient cause, one extension not exceeding a further ninety days may be granted. REG-11 must be filed before expiry and the additional estimated tax must be deposited. What is the GSTR-5 due date for a 2026 tax period? For periods from October 2022 onward, the portal implements the thirteenth day after the tax period, or seven days after the last day of registration validity, whichever is earlier. Can an NRTP claim credit on Indian professional or hotel invoices? The statute restricts NRTP credit to goods imported by the person. Domestic service invoices should not be assumed creditable. Is the advance deposit the final GST liability? No. It funds the electronic cash ledger. Actual supplies, return liability, interest and other dues must still be determined and reconciled. Key Takeaways Classify the foreign person and the Indian activity before selecting the NRTP route. File REG-09 before business begins and support the estimate used for the advance-tax deposit. Track the shorter of the requested period and ninety days, and file REG-11 before expiry if an extension is required. For 2026 periods, calculate both GSTR-5 deadline limbs and use the earlier date. The distinction between an NRTP, a casual taxable person, an OIDAR supplier and an ordinarily registered person must be made on evidence. Portal fields, filing processes, form versions, due-date extensions and administrative practices may change, and an individual registration certificate may contain dates that control the actual compliance calendar. The advance-tax estimate, input-tax-credit position, refund entitlement and return liability require transaction-level review; the examples and controls above are not calculations for any taxpayer.
