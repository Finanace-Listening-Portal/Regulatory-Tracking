Summary: SEBI’s adjudication order dated August 7, 2026, in the matter of Ultracab (India) Limited highlights the continuing obligation of promoters and persons acting in concert (PACs) to make manual disclosures under Regulation 29 of the SEBI SAST Regulations, 2011, notwithstanding the introduction of System Driven Disclosures (SDD). The matter involved a decline in promoter and promoter-group shareholding from 62.12% in June 2023 to 27.90% in December 2023, with twelve instances of disclosure threshold breaches. SEBI rejected the noticees’ reliance on automated disclosures, availability of individual transaction details in the public domain and absence of personal trading by certain promoter-group entities. The order emphasises that disclosure thresholds must be assessed with reference to the aggregate shareholding of an acquirer and PACs rather than merely individual transactions. Promoters continuing to be classified as PACs may remain subject to disclosure obligations even where they have not personally traded. However, entities whose cessation of shareholding and promoter-group status was established were exonerated. SEBI imposed a joint and several penalty of Rs 5 lakh on sixteen noticees. Read alongside SEBI’s circular dated March 7, 2022, and FAQ 80, the order reinforces the distinction between individual disclosure triggers covered by SDD and aggregate PAC-based triggers requiring manual disclosures. Listed companies and promoters should accordingly reconcile promoter-group records, monitor aggregate shareholding changes, verify manual filing obligations and maintain documentary evidence supporting changes in promoter classification. With the introduction of the System Driven Disclosure (“SDD”) mechanism for disclosures under SAST and PIT since April 2021, a question that arises in practice is whether manual disclosures under the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 (“SAST Regulations”) is still required under which circumstances? SEBI Adjudication Order in the Matter of Ultracab (India) Limited This question issue assumes significance in light of SEBI’s adjudication order dated August 7, 2026, in the matter of Ultracab (India) Limited[3]. SEBI examined a sharp decline in promoter and promoter group shareholding from 62.12% as of June 2023 to 27.90% by December 2023. Twelve instances of breach of the 2% threshold under Reg 29(2) of SAST were identified with no disclosures on the exchange. Twenty promoter group entities were proceeded against. Key defences from the Noticees were – (i) reliance on System Driven Disclosure (SDD) mechanism, (ii) individual trades being in public domain, and (iii) no personal trade by some entities. These all were all rejected by SEBI. SEBI’s Findings on Aggregate Shareholding and Manual Disclosure Obligations SEBI held that : (a) Reg 29(2) is triggered on aggregate holding of acquirer + PACs, not individual; (b) per circular dated March 7, 2022, manual disclosure continues to be mandatory where trigger is on PAC basis, SDD does not replace it; (c) passive promoters who continue to be classified as promoter/PAC (Noticees 8, 11, 13 – reflected in FY24 Annual Report and not disputed) remain liable even if they did not personally trade; (d) Noticees 16-19 who had nil holding since FY22/FY23 and were absent from FY24 promoter list were exonerated. Penalty of Rs 5 lakh jointly and severally was imposed on 16 noticees. While the order is straightforward on facts, it revives an interpretational issue which merit deeper examination – (i) whether SDD can ever replace manual disclosure under Reg 29(2). We examine these below. Manual Disclosures vs SDD Disclosures SEBI FAQ 80: Individual and Aggregate PAC-Based Disclosure Triggers SEBI’s Frequently Asked Questions on the SAST Regulations, 2011, under Question 80, address this situation directly. The FAQ states that transactions triggering disclosure thresholds on an individual basis are covered by SDD and do not require manual disclosures; however, manual disclosures continue to be required where the trigger arises from acquisition or disposal by an acquirer/seller together with persons acting in concert. By way of illustration, it states that where X and Y are PACs and each holds or acquires 3%, resulting in aggregate shareholding of 6%, manual disclosure is required under Regulation 29(1). It further explains that where PACs cumulatively hold 5% or more and a change triggering Regulation 29(2) occurs, manual disclosure is required.3 The FAQ therefore reinforces the distinction relevant to the Ultracab matter. SDD for individual transactions does not dispense with manual disclosure where the threshold is triggered on an aggregate acquirer/PAC basis. The FAQ also states that it is intended as a simplified explanation and should not be treated as a binding interpretation of law or guidance from SEBI. Takeaway for Listed Companies and Promoters From a practical standpoint, listed companies and their promoters should consider the following: Promoter and promoter-group records should be periodically reconciled with annual reports, shareholding patterns and supporting records. Disclosure monitoring should assess the applicable aggregate shareholding and not only the individual transaction of each promoter or member of the promoter group. Where a disclosure trigger arises from transactions by an acquirer together with PACs, the applicability of manual filing under the SAST Regulations should be specifically checked, notwithstanding the SDD mechanism. Historical transfers, cessation of shareholding and changes in promoter-group status should be supported by documentary evidence and appropriately reflected in the relevant disclosures. Conclusion Post the introduction of System Driven Disclosures, market practice has increasingly proceeded on the assumption that depository-driven reporting covers all SAST disclosure obligations. As this order reiterates, read with SEBI FAQ 80 and Circular dated March 7, 2022, that assumption is incorrect.
