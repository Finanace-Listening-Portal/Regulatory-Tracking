Articles 2 min read YES Bank files review petition in Supreme Court over Rs 8,415 crore AT1 bond write-down YES Bank has approached the Supreme Court seeking a review of its October 15 judgment setting aside the Rs 8,415 crore AT1 bond write-down, after disclosing the potential capital impact, its position on coupon payments and exchanges with the bond trustee. Shrishti Sharma ETBFSI Published On Nov 13, 2026 at 11:50 PM IST Read by 100 Professionals var news_msid = 135813906; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Private lender YES Bank on Friday filed a review petition before the Supreme Court, seeking a review of its October 15, 2026 judgment and order concerning the write-down of Additional Tier-1 (AT1) bonds aggregating Rs 8,415 crore.The dispute dates back to March 2020, when YES Bank wrote down AT1 bonds worth Rs 8,415 crore as part of its reconstruction. The decision was challenged before the Bombay High Court, which on January 20, 2023, set aside the write-down.YES Bank, the Reserve Bank of India and the Union government subsequently challenged the Bombay High Court order before the Supreme Court. Advt On October 15, 2026, the Supreme Court upheld the Bombay High Court judgment and dismissed the appeals filed by the bank, RBI and the Union government.In its October 16 disclosure, YES Bank said it was examining the judgment in detail, including evaluating all available legal options and suitable steps going forward.The bank also disclosed that reinstatement of the AT1 bond principal could have an approximately 250-basis-point impact on its Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio, including profits, based on its position as of June 30, 2026. Its Tier 1 capital ratio, however, was expected to remain unchanged at 14 per cent on a pro forma basis.YES Bank also said it had access to appropriate capital-management levers and retained flexibility to undertake additional capital actions, as considered appropriate to pursue its growth objectives.On October 24, the bank disclosed that it retained the right to exercise coupon discretion under the terms of the AT1 bonds, as specified in their respective Information Memoranda.The bank said it had received queries from bondholders regarding coupon payments. It specifically referred to its 9 per cent AT1 bonds, which had a coupon payment date of October 18, 2026, under the Information Memorandum dated October 17, 2017. Advt YES Bank said its assessment of the Supreme Court judgment was still underway and that it would communicate its decision, if any, on exercising coupon discretion after completing the evaluation.Trustee seeks reinstatement of bondsOn October 30, YES Bank disclosed that it had communicated with Axis Trustee Services on October 18 and October 24 regarding the Supreme Court judgment.The trustee subsequently issued a letter on October 28 asking the bank to reinstate the AT1 bonds and undertake all necessary corporate actions within three days. It also warned that failure to do so could lead to contempt proceedings before the Supreme Court against the bank, its directors and officers on grounds of alleged wilful disobedience of the judgment. Advt YES Bank said it had responded to the trustee on October 30, reiterating that it acknowledged the Supreme Court judgment and was committed to acting in accordance with the ruling and applicable law.The bank said it was examining the judgment and its implications in detail and would take all necessary steps and measures permissible under applicable law.
