Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Saluja Construction Company Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) CIT(A) Must Examine an Alleged Double Disallowance U/s 40(a)(ia) Even if the Sec 143(1) Intimation Was Not Appealed: ITAT Delhi Tribunal Restores Five Appeals for Adjudication on Merits The Delhi ITAT held that the CIT(A) should have examined the assessee’s claim of double disallowance under section 40(a)(ia) on merits. The company contended that the amount disallowed in reassessment was already included in an amount it had voluntarily added back. The CIT(A) dismissed the grounds because the assessee had not appealed against the earlier section 143(1) intimation. The Tribunal found that this disposed of the issue on a technical ground without examining the claimed overlap. It restored the lead appeal and applied the same directions to four connected appeals. Lead Case Concerned TDS Deducted but Allegedly Not Deposited For AY 2019–20, Saluja Construction filed its original return on 31 October 2019, declaring a loss of ₹3,49,46,337. The Centralised Processing Centre issued an intimation on 4 January 2021, determining income of ₹5,77,87,970. A search under section 132 was conducted on the company and its group entities on 9 June 2022. Following centralisation of the case, a notice under section 148 was issued on 13 January 2023. The company filed a return in response declaring a loss of ₹4,45,03,960. The Assessing Officer relied on search material said to show that TDS had been deducted on substantial payments but had not been deposited. The show-cause notice dated 24 March 2024 proposed a disallowance of ₹3,35,80,482, being 30% of ₹11,19,34,941. The reassessment under section 147 read with section 143(3) was completed on 30 March 2024 at ₹5,77,87,970, the same income as determined in the earlier intimation. Assessee Claimed the Amount Was Already Added Back Before the CIT(A), the company submitted that the disputed ₹3,35,80,482 was already covered by a larger suo motu disallowance of ₹18,32,79,196. It also explained that its return had inadvertently referred to section 40(a)(ia) for a disallowance it said related to section 40(a)(i). According to the company, that description did not change the fact that the disputed amount had already been added back. The CIT(A) recorded the explanation but rejected the interconnected grounds because the disallowance had already been made while processing the return and the assessee had filed no appeal against the intimation. The Tribunal found that the appellate order had left the factual issue unanswered. The question was whether the same amount had already been included in the company’s computation and was being disallowed again. Short Response Period Also Weighed in Favour of Remand The Tribunal noted that the Assessing Officer had given the company only three days to respond to the show-cause notice. It considered this circumstance alongside the absence of adjudication on merits by the CIT(A). At the Tribunal hearing on 28 October 2026, no one appeared for the company or sought an adjournment. After considering the history of adjournments, the Bench proceeded ex parte, heard the Departmental Representative and examined the available record. The Tribunal directed the CIT(A) to give the company due opportunity to submit the evidence supporting its claim of duplication and then decide the matter on merits. It also directed the company to comply with the appellate proceedings. If the company failed to do so, the CIT(A) would be free to proceed and decide the appeal on merits. Common Remand Covered All Five Assessment Years The Tribunal extended its directions to the connected appeals for AYs 2020–21, 2021–22, 2022–23 and 2023–24. The grounds across those years included alleged duplicate disallowances under section 40(a)(ia), claims concerning section 43B, and challenges to additions under sections 68, 69A and 69C. All five appeals were allowed for statistical purposes. The order restored the matters to the CIT(A) for fresh adjudication. Whether the disallowances were duplicated, and whether the other additions could be sustained, remains for determination in those proceedings. Author’s Comments The fact that the processed income and reassessed income were identical does not, by itself, establish whether an individual item was counted twice. The answer requires a reconciliation of the return computation, the section 143(1) adjustments and the reassessment computation. In the lead year, the factual question is whether the payments underlying the ₹3.36 crore disallowance formed part of the ₹18.33 crore add-back already made by the company. The order’s practical significance lies in requiring the first appellate authority to examine that reconciliation. The absence of a separate appeal against the intimation did not answer the duplication claim raised in the reassessment appeal. The remand gives the company an opportunity to establish the overlap transaction by transaction. DR has been heard and material on record has been carefully considered. 5.1 Brief facts of the case are that the assessee had filed its original return for A.Y. 2019-20 on 31.10.2019 declaring loss of Rs. 3,49,46,337/- and intimation u/s 143(1) was issued by CPC on 4.1.2021 determining income of Rs.5,77,87,970/-. Subsequently, a search u/s 132 was conducted on the assessee and its group entities on 09.06.2022 pursuant to which the case was centralized vide order u/s 127 dated 05.09.2022. Subsequently, a notice u/s 148 of the Act was issued on 13.01.2023. The assessee filed return in response to the notice on 16.03.2024 declaring loss of Rs. 4,45,03,960/-. Based on incriminating material gathered during the course of search evidencing non-deposit of TDS deducted on huge payments made by the assessee, the AO issued a show-cause notice dated 24.03.2024 regarding proposed disallowance of Rs. 3,35,80,482/- u/s 40(a)(ia) [being 30% of Rs. 11,19,34,941/-] on account of non-compliance of TDS provisions. As the assessee could not furnish any explanation, the AO completed the assessment vide order u/s 147 r.w.s. 143(3) dated 30.03.2024 at assessed income of Rs. 5,77,87,970/-, the same as was computed u/s 143(1) by the CPC. 5.2 Aggrieved, the assessee preferred an appeal before the CIT(A). Ld. CIT(A), however, dismissed the assessee’s appeal with the following observations: – “5.1.5 I have carefully considered the assessment order, the grounds of appeal, the written submissions of the appellant, and the material available on record. The issues raised in Additional Ground and Grounds No. 2 & 3 pertain to the disallowance of Rs. 3,35,80,482/- made under section 40(a)(ia) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Since these grounds are interconnected and arise from the same set of facts, they are adjudicated together. 5.1.6 In this case, AO has made a disallowance of Rs. 3,35,80,482/- under Section 40(a)(i) of the I Tact,1961. In this regard, the appellant has submitted before me that it has suo moto disallowed an amount of Rs. 18,32,79,196/- which includes the amount of Rs. 3,35,80,482/-. It is the contention of appellant that although it had inadvertently mentioned the disallowance under 40(a)(i) of the Income tax Act,1961 as being under Section 40(a)(ia) of the Income tax Act,1961 only, in return of income, this does not have any revenue impact, the amount of Rs. 3,35,80,482/- disallowed by the AO has been suo moto disallowed by the appellant. 5.1.7 However, I notice that the disallowance was already made u/s 143(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 against which no further appeal was filed by appellant. Consequently, the Additional Ground, Ground No. 2 and Ground No. 3 stand DISMISSED.” Thus, the CIT(A) has not examined the merits of the issue and simply dismissed the appeal on technical issue. Moreover, at the time of issuing show cause notice, the AO has also given only 3 days’ time to the assessee to furnish its response, presumably for the reason that the case was getting time barred. The assessee is aggrieved on this account also. 5.3 After considering the totality of the facts and circumstances of the case, and in the interest of justice, we deem it appropriate to restore the matter to the CIT(A) for fresh adjudication on merits after giving the assessee due opportunity of submitting requisite evidence in support of its contention that the impugned addition being part of suo-moto disallowance made by the assessee, amounts to double addition. The assessee is also directed to make compliance before the CIT(A) failing which the CIT(A) shall be free to proceed and decide the appeal on merits. 6. In the result, appeal of the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes. 7. Since the facts and circumstances in all the other appeals for A.Y. 2020- 21 (ITA No.- 5907/Del/2025), A.Y. 2021-22 (ITA No. – 8425/Del/2025), A.Y. 2022-23 (ITA No. – 5908/Del/2025) & A.Y. 2023-24 (ITA No.33/Del/2026) are similar, the above order shall apply mutatis mutandis to these appeals also. 8. In the result, all the five appeals of the assessee are allowed for statistical purposes.
