Minimum Public Shareholding Rules 2026: Revised IPO Limits and Timelines Summary: The Ministry of Finance, through Notification No. G.S.R. 184(E) dated 13 March 2026, notified the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Amendment Rules, 2026, substituting Rule 19(2)(b) of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957. The amendment introduces a six-tier framework for determining the minimum public offer required when companies seek listing on recognised stock exchanges. The thresholds are linked to post-issue capital calculated at the offer price, with separate requirements for companies valued up to ₹1,600 crore, above ₹1,600 crore to ₹4,000 crore, above ₹4,000 crore to ₹50,000 crore, above ₹50,000 crore to ₹1 lakh crore, above ₹1 lakh crore to ₹5 lakh crore, and above ₹5 lakh crore. Depending on the category, the minimum public offer ranges from 25% to a combination of prescribed monetary values and lower percentage thresholds. The amendment also provides three-year, five-year and ten-year timelines for increasing public shareholding, extends prescribed timelines to eligible existing listed companies, addresses the simultaneous listing of superior voting rights shares, and retains a special 10% public offer requirement for listings in International Financial Services Centres. These changes provide greater flexibility to large issuers while retaining the objective of broader public participation. Table of Contents▸ Legal Background and Applicability Q1. The amendment came into force on 13 March 2026, the date of publication in the Official Gazette. It substitutes Rule 19(2)(b), which prescribes the minimum offer and allotment to the public in terms of an offer document for equity shares or debentures convertible into equity shares. Important distinction: Minimum Public Offer (MPO) refers to the minimum securities required to be offered and allotted to the public at listing. Minimum Public Shareholding (MPS) refers to the prescribed level of public ownership that a listed company must achieve or maintain. The amended Rule 19(2)(b) addresses both the initial public offer and the subsequent timelines for achieving specified public shareholding levels. Q1. Where both a monetary threshold and a percentage threshold are prescribed, both conditions must be satisfied. The public shareholding increases must be undertaken in the manner specified by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). Q2. How Is the Minimum Public Offer Calculated? The prescribed percentage is determined with reference to the company’s post-issue capital calculated at the offer price. For illustration, assume a company has post-issue capital of ₹80,000 crore calculated at its IPO offer price. Applicable category: Above ₹50,000 crore and up to ₹1 lakh crore. Minimum percentage requirement: 8%. Value represented by 8%: ₹6,400 crore. Separate minimum monetary requirement: ₹1,000 crore. Accordingly, an offer of 8%, representing ₹6,400 crore, satisfies both numerical conditions, assuming the relevant securities and offer structure meet the other applicable requirements. Illustration: The figures above are hypothetical and are intended only to explain the calculation mechanism. Q3. What Is the Minimum Public Offer for Companies Above ₹5 Lakh Crore? Under Rule 19(2)(b)(vi), companies with post-issue capital exceeding ₹5 lakh crore are subject to a minimum public offer equivalent to ₹15,000 crore and at least 1% of each relevant class or kind of securities. However, Rule 19(2)(b)(vii) expressly provides that, notwithstanding sub-clause (vi), at least 2.5% of each such class or kind of securities must be offered to the public. Therefore, the 2.5% requirement operates as an overriding minimum percentage. The monetary requirement of ₹15,000 crore must also be considered. Example: If the post-issue capital is ₹8 lakh crore, 2.5% represents ₹20,000 crore. An offer of 2.5% would therefore exceed the ₹15,000 crore monetary threshold. This provision is particularly relevant to very large companies proposing to enter the public markets. Q4. What Are the Timelines for Achieving 25% Minimum Public Shareholding? The timelines vary according to the applicable post-issue capital category. Companies Above ₹1,600 Crore and Up to ₹50,000 Crore Companies falling under sub-clauses (ii) and (iii) must increase public shareholding to at least 25% within three years from the date of listing. Companies Above ₹50,000 Crore and Up to ₹1 Lakh Crore Companies covered by sub-clause (iv) must achieve at least 25% public shareholding within five years from the date of listing. Companies Above ₹1 Lakh Crore For companies covered by sub-clauses (v) and (vi), the timeline depends on public shareholding at listing. Below 15% at listing: Increase public shareholding to at least 15% within five years and at least 25% within ten years. 15% or above at listing: Increase public shareholding to at least 25% within five years. The applicable SEBI framework governing methods of achieving minimum public shareholding must also be followed. Q5. Are Existing Listed Companies Eligible for the Revised Timelines? Yes. The first proviso following sub-clause (vii) extends the prescribed timelines for achieving public shareholding to companies listed on or before the commencement of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Amendment Rules, 2026. Accordingly, existing listed companies may benefit from the revised timelines, subject to the applicable statutory conditions and SEBI requirements. However, this provision should not be interpreted as an automatic waiver of previous violations or penalties. Companies should examine their existing public shareholding, applicable category, earlier compliance position and regulatory obligations before determining the revised compliance deadline. Q6. What Are the Requirements for Superior Voting Rights Shares? The amendment addresses applicant companies that have issued equity shares carrying Superior Voting Rights (SVR) to promoters or founders. Where such a company proposes to list its ordinary equity shares through a public offering, it must also list its SVR equity shares on the same recognised stock exchange along with the ordinary shares offered to the public. This requirement is subject to the applicable SEBI regulations governing such securities. The provision seeks to ensure that the listing of ordinary shares and the corresponding superior voting rights shares is appropriately aligned. Q7. What Are the Minimum Public Offer Requirements for IFSC Listings? The amended Rule 19(2)(b) retains a separate framework for applicant companies seeking listing on a recognised stock exchange in an International Financial Services Centre (IFSC). For such companies, the reference to 25% in sub-clause (i) is replaced by 10%, irrespective of post-issue capital. Further, the requirements under sub-clauses (ii) to (vii) do not apply to such companies. Consequently, the minimum public offer under this provision is 10% for qualifying IFSC listings, subject to other applicable laws and regulatory requirements. Q8. Can Stock Exchanges Impose Penalties for Earlier Non-Compliance? Yes. Rule 19(2)(b)(viii) expressly permits a recognised stock exchange to impose a fine or penalty against a company for non-compliance with public shareholding norms committed before the commencement of the 2026 Amendment Rules. Therefore, the availability of revised timelines does not automatically extinguish liability for past non-compliance. Companies with historical shortfalls should separately evaluate their exposure to regulatory action and the effect of the amended compliance timelines. Q9. What Is the Practical Impact on IPO Planning? The amendment has important implications for companies planning an initial public offering, particularly large issuers. Reduced initial dilution: Certain large companies can list with a smaller initial public offer than would otherwise be required under a uniform 25% threshold. Phased public ownership: Eligible companies have additional time to increase public shareholding after listing. IPO structuring: Companies must evaluate both the monetary and percentage requirements applicable to their capital category. Promoter shareholding: The revised framework may allow promoters to retain a larger initial stake, subject to subsequent dilution requirements. Compliance planning: Listed companies must monitor the applicable milestones and methods permitted by SEBI. Historical defaults: Earlier non-compliance should be evaluated separately because the amendment preserves the possibility of fines or penalties. Q10. Does the Amendment Remove the 25% Public Shareholding Objective? No. The amendment does not generally abolish the 25% public shareholding objective for domestic listings. Instead, it provides a graded framework under which certain companies may make a smaller initial public offer and progressively increase public shareholding within prescribed periods. The special IFSC provision is separately governed by its 10% minimum public offer requirement under Rule 19(2)(b). Conclusion The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Amendment Rules, 2026 introduce a more flexible framework for minimum public offers and the achievement of prescribed public shareholding levels. By linking the initial public offer requirement to post-issue capital calculated at the offer price, the amendment accommodates companies of different sizes while maintaining public participation as a central regulatory objective. The most significant changes concern large companies, particularly those with post-issue capital exceeding ₹1 lakh crore, which may benefit from lower initial public offer percentages and longer timelines for achieving 25% public shareholding. Companies planning an IPO and existing listed companies should assess the revised Rule 19(2)(b), applicable SEBI regulations, public shareholding milestones and any historical compliance exposure before finalising their capital-market strategy.
