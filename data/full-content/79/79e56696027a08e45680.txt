Advt Such dollar sales from state-run banks occurred over multiple trading sessions last ​week, limiting the rupee's fall despite lingering pressures.On ⁠the day, ‌heightened demand to buy dollars at the daily reference ​rate, flows ​linked to corporates' overseas debt repayments and hedging demand ⁠from local importers also troubled the currency, traders ​said."The intensity of RBI interventions has diminished over last ​two-three sessions, so 95.50 could be on the cards sooner rather than later," a trader at a state-run bank said.Elsewhere, the dollar index lingered just shy of the 99 mark while Asian currencies were trading mixed. Stocks in Asia were under pressure as well with ‌the MSCI's gauge of regional stocks down 0.5%.Higher oil prices and elevated bond yields have weighed on investor sentiment, ​setting the stage ​for the series ⁠of central bank meetings in the coming days.The European Central Bank is expected to raise rates later today while the U.S. Federal Reserve and ​the Bank of Japan will deliver policy decisions next week."While higher inflation may warrant tighter policy, additional rate hikes would also increase government borrowing costs at a time when fiscal deficits and debt servicing burdens are already under scrutiny," MUFG said in a note.(Reporting by Jaspreet Kalra; Editing by Harikrishnan Nair and Janane Venkatraman) Reuters Published On Sep 10, 2026 at 10:59 AM IST Telegram Facebook Copy Link Comments What are your thoughts?
