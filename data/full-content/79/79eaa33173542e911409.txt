Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Indu Dhandharia LR of Pankaj Dhandharia Vs ACIT (Delhi High Court) Summary: The Delhi High Court, in Indu Dhandharia LR of Pankaj Dhandharia Vs ACIT & Connected Cases, disposed of seven connected writ petitions concerning the taxation of additional remuneration and discretionary performance rewards received by partners of professional firms. The controversy arose because assessing authorities had included these amounts in the individual income of partners even though, according to the petitioners, the partnership firm had already paid tax on the corresponding amounts. In an earlier interim order dated 1 April 2026 in the petition filed by Rajeev Sawhney, a partner of S. R. Batliboi & Co. LLP, the Court recorded the petitioner’s contention that the Assessing Officer had relied upon the Explanation to Section 10(2A) and Section 28(v) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, without considering the effect of the relevant proviso. Recognising the wider implications for partners of professional firms, the Court had directed the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) to examine the issue and issue a clarification in accordance with law. It had also stayed recovery proceedings arising from the assessment order dated 6 March 2026. At the subsequent hearing, the Income Tax Department informed the Court that the CBDT had issued a letter dated 16 October 2026 pursuant to its earlier directions, addressing the principle of single-point taxation of a firm’s income. According to the Department, the clarification substantially resolved the controversy and redressed the petitioners’ grievances. The petitioners consequently requested the Court to quash the assessment orders, reassessment orders, proceedings and penalty orders involving additions of additional remuneration and discretionary performance rewards in the hands of partners. The Department opposed unconditional quashing, submitting that an enquiry contemplated under paragraph 3 of the CBDT letter was necessary before the relevant assessments could be reconsidered. It therefore requested remand to the respective Assessing Officers for fresh orders in accordance with the CBDT clarification. The High Court, comprising Justice Dinesh Mehta and Justice Aditi Choudhary, accepted the course of fresh examination and set aside the impugned assessment orders, reassessment orders, notices and penalty orders. It directed the Assessing Officers to pass fresh orders in accordance with law after providing the petitioners an opportunity of hearing and conducting the enquiry contemplated under paragraph 3 of the CBDT clarification dated 16 October 2026. The judgment is significant because it requires the tax authorities to reconsider additions concerning partners’ remuneration and discretionary performance rewards in light of the CBDT’s clarification rather than sustaining the impugned orders without such examination. However, the Court did not itself finally determine the taxability of every disputed payment or grant an unconditional exemption. The writ petitions and pending applications were disposed of accordingly. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF DELHI HIGH COURT 1. On 01.04.2026, we had passed following order in W.P.(C) 4248/2026: “1. The petitioner, who is a partner in a Firm S. R. Batliboi & Co. LLP (SRB) has approached this Court by invoking its jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, challenging action of the Assessing Officer (AO), who has added in petitioner’s income, the amount of additional remuneration and discretionary performance reward recovered by him though the partnership firm has already paid tax on such payments. 2. While highlighting that the firm had undeniably paid tax on these amounts, learned counsel argued that the AO has simply relied upon Section 10(2A) Explanation and Section 28(v) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act of 1961’) while completely ignoring the proviso and its effect. 3. Learned counsel further submitted that another writ petition being W.P.(C) 3151/2026 (Annexure P-7) on similar issue is pending consideration before this Court, wherein a detailed interim order has been passed. 4. Having said so, Mr. Kamal Sawhney, learned counsel for the petitioner submitted there are a number of proceedings triggered by the various assessing authorities throughout the country. He added that they are either assessing or reassessing such amounts in the hands of the respective partners in spite of the facts that the firm had offered tax on such amounts. 5. Having regard to the fact that the issue in hands may have larger implications and bearing on various assessees who are partners in different professional firms, we deem it appropriate to direct the CBDT to issue a clarification in this regard. The firm of the petitioner (SRB), shall move a representation giving out precise facts and relevant provisions in this regard before the Chairman, CBDT within a period of two weeks’ from today. 6. The Chairman CBDT or some officer(s) nominated by him shall examine the issue, whereafter the CBDT shall issue a clarification in accordance with law. 7. List this case on 18.05.2026. 8. Meanwhile, the recovery proceedings in furtherance of the impugned assessment order dated 06.03.2026 shall remain stayed till further order.” 2. Mr. Sunil Aggarwal, learned Senior Standing Counsel for the respondents, submitted that pursuant to the directions issued by this Court in the above order dated 01.04.2026, in order to give effect to the principle of single-point taxation of a firm’s income, the Central Board of Direct Taxes has issued a letter dated 16.10.2026, whereby requisite/necessary clarifications have been issued, which substantially resolve the issue and redress the petitioners’ grievance. The letter dated 16.10.2026 issued by the Central Board of Direct Taxes is taken on record. 3. Mr. Kamal Sawhney, learned Counsel for the petitioners, submitted that in light of the above referred letter/Office Memorandum, all the similar assessment orders, re–assessment orders, proceedings and penalty order passed by the Assessing Officers taking the addition of remuneration and discretionary performance reward in the hands of the partners including the ones impugned in the present writ petitions, be quashed and set aside. 4. To this request of Mr. Sawhney, learned Senior Standing Counsel for the respondents submitted that the assessment orders cannot be quashed out-rightly, unless an enquiry in terms of paragraph no.3 of the letter dated 16.10.2026 is conducted. He argued that in case, this Court deems it apposite to set aside the assessment orders/re-assessment orders, then, the matters be remanded to the Assessing Officers for passing fresh orders in light of the Circular so issued by the Central Board of Direct Taxes. 5. In view of what has been noted hereinabove, we dispose of the present writ petitions while setting aside the impugned assessment orders/re-assessment orders/notices/penalty orders and direct the Assessing Officers to pass fresh order(s) in accordance with law while providing an opportunity of hearing to the petitioners and conducting an enquiry in terms paragraph no.3 of the Circular dated 16.10.2026 issued by the Central Board of Direct Taxes. 6. All pending applications also stand disposed of.
