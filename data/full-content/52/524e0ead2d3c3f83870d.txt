Summary : Oasis Network is a proof-of-stake blockchain whose native token is ROSE. Its architecture separates a consensus layer from compute environments called ParaTimes. Sapphire, an Oasis-developed ParaTime, provides an EVM-compatible environment designed to support confidential computation. That separation creates operational distinctions that ordinary token descriptions often miss: ROSE on the consensus ledger, ROSE deposited into Sapphire, and wrapped representations on another network are not the same balance or transfer route. Official project documentation states a capped supply of 10 billion ROSE and identifies transaction fees, staking, delegation and governance as token functions. Those protocol facts do not guarantee security, privacy, liquidity or value. This guide explains the current network identity, Sapphire model, staking and delegation, wallet and bridge controls, and Indian virtual-digital-asset tax records without forecasting price or recommending ROSE. ParaTimes execute applications separately and can use different runtime technologies. Official token-management documentation expressly warns that the consensus layer and each ParaTime have separate ledgers. A user moving ROSE from a consensus account into Sapphire therefore performs a deposit into a different runtime ledger; the destination is not simply another display of the same account. Withdrawals reverse that pathway. Exchanges may support only consensus-layer ROSE, so an EVM-format Sapphire address should not be assumed to be a valid exchange deposit address. Sapphire Confidential EVM: Capabilities and Limits Sapphire is described by Oasis as a confidential EVM-compatible ParaTime deployed on mainnet. It supports Solidity-style smart contracts while using trusted execution technology for confidential computation. Confidentiality can allow contract state or transaction data to be protected from ordinary public inspection, but it is not anonymity and it is not an absolute promise that every application hides every datum. Application design, key handling, gateway selection, wallet behaviour, metadata, developer errors and the trusted-computing assumptions all matter. The official network page also cautions that an RPC endpoint is a point of trust that can rate-limit, censor or attempt a man-in-the-middle attack. A public gateway’s availability does not certify a dApp. Users should verify the Sapphire mainnet chain identifier, the contract address, requested permissions and the application’s own disclosure of what remains public. Testnet is not a production privacy environment, and test tokens or state must not be confused with mainnet value. What “Confidential” Does Not Mean It does not make unlawful transactions lawful or remove tax and recordkeeping duties. It does not guarantee that a front end, wallet, RPC provider or developer has handled data securely. It does not protect a seed phrase disclosed to phishing malware or a support impersonator. It does not make smart-contract code free from economic, authorization or upgrade risks. It does not ensure that an exchange can trace or credit a deposit sent through the wrong ledger. ROSE Supply, Fees, Staking and Delegation Oasis documentation states that ROSE has a capped supply of 10 billion units and nine decimal places. The token pays network gas, supports staking and delegation, and participates in governance and network incentives. A supply ceiling describes protocol issuance; it does not state how much supply is liquid, staked, custodied or available on a particular venue. Token unlocks, delegations, validator behaviour and market access can affect circulating conditions without changing the stated maximum. Delegation assigns ROSE to a validator’s staking pool under the protocol. It is not a bank deposit, does not carry deposit insurance and does not promise a fixed interest rate. Validators can change commission, performance can vary, governance can alter parameters, and an unbonding period can delay availability. A holder should verify validator identity and commission in the current wallet, understand the active and debonding states, retain delegation and reward records, and keep enough liquid ROSE for fees. Self-custody removes reliance on an exchange for withdrawal but transfers the security burden to the holder. Hardware failure is recoverable only if backups work; a lost seed may be irreversible. Centralised custody creates different risks including insolvency, freezes, KYC restrictions and delayed withdrawals. Bridges add a separate contract, validator or custodian and may issue a token that is redeemable only under bridge rules. These risks should be identified separately rather than bundled under a generic statement that blockchain transactions are secure. Indian VDA Tax and Transaction Records for ROSE ROSE is capable of falling within India’s broad virtual-digital-asset definition. Section 115BBH generally taxes income from transfer of a VDA at 30 per cent, plus applicable surcharge and cess. The special rule generally allows only cost of acquisition as a deduction against transfer income and bars set-off or carry-forward of a loss from transfer of a VDA. A sale for rupees, token-for-token exchange, payment for a service or transfer for other consideration can be a taxable transfer even if no bank withdrawal occurs. Section 194S generally requires one per cent tax deduction on consideration for transfer to a resident when its conditions and thresholds are met. The stated thresholds are ₹50,000 for a specified person and ₹10,000 for another payer during the financial year. Consideration wholly or partly in kind has special mechanics. Platform withholding is not the final tax computation: the taxpayer must reconcile consideration, acquisition cost, TDS credit and the legal character of each transfer. Moving ROSE between wallets under the same beneficial ownership is factually different from disposing of it, but the owner should retain evidence linking both addresses. Consensus-to-Sapphire deposits, withdrawals and bridge transactions require similar substance analysis. Delegation rewards can raise a receipt-time income question and a later transfer question; the appropriate treatment depends on control, business facts and the law for the relevant year. A complete file should include exchange orders, bank records, wallet exports, hashes, rewards, fees, INR valuation sources and AIS/Form 26AS reconciliation. Frequently Asked Questions Is ROSE on Sapphire the same ledger balance as consensus ROSE? No. The consensus layer and each ParaTime maintain separate ledgers. Depositing or withdrawing moves value through a documented cross-layer process. Is Sapphire a separate token? No. Sapphire is a confidential EVM-compatible ParaTime and uses ROSE for gas. Applications may issue separate tokens, which require their own contract verification. Does confidential computation make a transaction anonymous? No. Confidentiality depends on the application and system design and does not eliminate metadata, gateway, wallet, legal or disclosure risks. Is 10 billion ROSE a guaranteed circulating-supply figure? No. It is the documented maximum supply. Circulating, staked, locked and custodied amounts are different measures. Does ROSE delegation guarantee a yield? No. Rewards and availability depend on protocol parameters, validator performance, commission and debonding rules, while token value can change sharply. Is a consensus-to-Sapphire deposit automatically taxable in India? Tax depends on beneficial ownership, consideration and transaction substance. A self-controlled internal movement should be documented rather than assumed to be a disposal. Can a ROSE transfer loss offset salary or another VDA gain? Section 115BBH generally restricts set-off and carry-forward of VDA transfer losses. The exact return position should be reviewed professionally. Key Takeaways ROSE is the native token of a network that separates consensus from application ParaTimes. Sapphire is a mainnet confidential EVM ParaTime, not a privacy guarantee for every app or interface. Consensus and Sapphire balances use different ledgers, so network and address verification are essential. Staking and delegation are protocol activities with validator, liquidity and custody risks, not fixed deposits.
