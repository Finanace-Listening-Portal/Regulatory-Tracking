The bank’s deposits grew 27% year on year and 12% sequentially, supported by mobilisation of foreign currency non resident (FCNR(B)) deposits. Loans grew 30% year on year and 12% sequentially, although growth was lower after excluding FCNR(B) deployment. Advt The shift in the balance sheet towards overseas assets also weighed on margins, as these assets earn around 1%, compared with more than 4% on domestic assets. Net interest margin (NIM) contracted about 18 basis points sequentially, while net interest income grew 18% year on year and 4% sequentially. Management expects NIM to moderate further in Q3FY27 before recovering from Q4FY27 as surplus FCNR(B) liquidity is deployed, according to JM Financial’s assessment of the bank’s Q2FY27 results and management commentary.Excluding FCNR(B), loan growth stood at 24% year on year and 7% sequentially, while deposit growth was 17% year on year and 3% sequentially. The overseas loan book nearly tripled to Rs 1.43 trillion following deployment of FCNR(B) funds. Of the USD 18 billion in FCNR(B) deposits, around USD 10 billion was deployed in customer loans, USD 3.6 billion through partner banks and more than USD 4 billion represented customers’ own funds.The growth driversDomestic credit growth was led by business banking and corporate loans, which rose 34% and 31%, respectively, year on year. Growth was supported by working capital demand, investment activity and a shift from bond and foreign currency borrowing towards rupee loans. Retail and rural loans grew 15% year on year and 3% sequentially.Core fee income grew 20% year on year and 7% sequentially, partly aided by processing fees on FCNR(B) backed lending. Core pre provision operating profit rose 25% year on year and 6% sequentially. Management indicated that processing fees related to FCNR(B) lending broadly offset the incremental operating costs of raising wholesale funds. Advt Asset quality improved during the quarter. Gross slippages declined about 24 basis points sequentially, while credit costs fell about 12 basis points to 0.20%, against JM Financial’s estimate of 0.45%. A large corporate recovery helped reduce reported credit costs, partly offset by higher standard provisions on FCNR(B) linked loans. Normalised credit costs are expected to rise to around 50 basis points after adjusting for one off items.Performing corporate loans rated BB and below stood at Rs 35.47 billion, around 0.2% of advances. Standard fund based loans under resolution declined to Rs 11.85 billion from Rs 13.63 billion in the previous quarter. An update on the additional Rs 12.83 billion agricultural provision is expected within two quarters, subject to regulatory remediation. Advt The brokerage marginally revised its earnings per share estimates for FY27 and FY28 but maintained its BUY recommendation and target price of Rs 1,710, valuing the bank at 2.4 times estimated FY28 core price to book value. It expects average return on assets of about 2.3% and return on equity of 17% over FY27 and FY28.The near term focus is on deploying surplus FCNR(B) liquidity, with management expecting margins to recover from Q4FY27 as the funds are put to use. The pace of deployment will be important in determining how quickly the pressure on net interest margins eases.
